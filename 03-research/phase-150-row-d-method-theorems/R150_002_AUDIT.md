## §1. Attacks on T3 (§2 of `R150_001`)

### 1.1 The continuation inference is false (Import 2.3, §2.1; blueprint §1)

Both the blueprint and Import 2.3 write the hypothesis as
`N_𝒫(x) = ax + O(x·exp(−c(log x)^{1/2}))` **"so that `ζ_𝒫` … continues meromorphically to a region
left of `Re s = 1`"**. This implication does not hold. With `u = log x`,

```
∫_1^∞ |N(x) − ax| x^{−σ−1} dx  ≪  ∫_0^∞ exp((1−σ)u − c√u) du,
```

finite for `σ ≥ 1` and **divergent for every `σ < 1`**. So the hypothesis yields exactly: `ζ_𝒫(s) − a/(s−1)`
analytic in `σ > 1`, continuous up to `σ = 1`. Continuation past the line is an **extra** property that
DMV build into their systems by construction (they prescribe `ζ` first and realise it), and its
*width* and *growth* are precisely the quantity the whole Beurling theory trades against the
regularity of `N(x)`. Consequence: **Prop. 2.5 has no proof for BE3 and BE7**, and the phrase "in the
form supplied by the source" (Prop. 2.5, proof, penultimate sentence) is a placeholder, not a step.
`PROVED modulo Import 2.3` is therefore label inflation: the correct label is `CONJECTURAL`.

This propagates: Theorem 2.8's proof requires the DMV system to be a model of BE1–BE8. If BE7 fails
for it — and the systems are named *"with large oscillation"*, which is a warning, not a reassurance —
then T3 is a statement about `{BE1, BE2, BE4, BE5, BE6, BE8}`, a list from which Prop. 2.6 (the dVP
region) is itself no longer derivable, and the theorem loses its target.

### 1.2 The axiom list is honest about dVP, and dishonest by omission about what else is classical

Obs. 2.7's inspection claim (dVP, Landau's trigonometric polynomials `Σa_ν cos νθ ≥ 0`, Mertens
variants use only BE1–BE8) I accept: I attempted and failed to find a classical unconditional
zero-free-region proof that uses anything else. BE2 + BE3 + BE7 + BE8 is genuinely the content of the
`3 + 4cos θ + cos 2θ` argument. **This part of the audit finds nothing.**

What the list omits is that BE1–BE8 has *never been claimed by anyone* to yield a zero-free strip.
`(ZFS_δ)` is not a target of the trigonometric method; the method's known optimal output is
`1 − c/log t`, and that the classical region is optimal in the Beurling class is the standard reading
of DMV. So T3's conclusion, granted its import, is **the known content of DMV restated with an axiom
list attached**. It is not false; it has no bite beyond what DMV already says. `METHOD-CONSTRAINT`
overstates it; `RESTATEMENT (with axiom list)` is the honest label once O-150.1 closes.

### 1.3 Beurling systems with functional equations — the `+` claim is too strong

§2.5 row 1 asserts the failure of the functional equation is `PROVED` "by definition",
on the ground that `𝒩 ⊂ R_{>0}` is a multiplicative semigroup with `𝒩 + 𝒩 ⊄ 𝒩`. Counterexamples
inside the Beurling class:

* **Function-field systems.** Let `K/F_q` be a global function field and `𝒫 = {N𝔭} = {q^{deg 𝔭}}`.
  This is a legitimate discrete Beurling system; `ζ_𝒫 = ζ_K` is rational in `q^{−s}`, satisfies a
  functional equation, and satisfies RH (Weil). It satisfies BE1–BE8. Its `𝒩` is *not* closed under
  `+` either — yet it has `F`. The additive structure that produces the functional equation lives on
  the *field*, not on the multiplicative semigroup `𝒩 ⊂ R_{>0}`, and `𝒩` is only the image of a norm.
* **Arithmetically realised systems.** `𝒫 = {rational primes}` is a Beurling system. So "no Beurling
  system has a functional equation" is refuted by the most familiar example of all.
* **Literature flagged as unread (O-150.3).** Hilberdink–Lapidus ("Beurling zeta functions,
  generalised primes and fractal membranes") and Zhang construct Beurling zetas with prescribed
  analytic behaviour, including self-dual / functional-equation-type structures. I have not read them
  either; I record only that the *possibility* is live, which is enough to withdraw a `PROVED` label.

**What survives.** The corrected statement is: *`F` is not a consequence of BE1–BE8; a Beurling
system is not obliged to have it; DMV exhibit one that (on the import) does not.* That is the true
content and it suffices for Theorem 2.8. Everything in §2.5 must be re-labelled from "fails for
Beurling systems (`PROVED`, structural)" to "not implied by BE1–BE8 (`PROVED`, model-theoretic)".
Rows 2, 4, 5, 6 need the same demotion for the same reason (each says "a *generic* Beurling system
lacks it", which is a statement about the generic member, not about the class).

### 1.4 The falsifier triangle does not close (Thm 2.10, Rmk 2.11)

Three defects, in increasing order of damage.

1. **Different conclusions.** `Ξ_Q` violates the Hodge-index conclusion (Paper 47 Thm 18.10);
   `Ξ_a` violates `Re ρ = ½`; `ζ_𝒫` violates `(ZFS_δ)`. A triangle of *necessary conditions for one
   statement* requires three falsifiers of the **same** statement. As assembled, the figure is three
   unrelated non-implications.
2. **The `U`-vertex is undefined at the `ζ_𝒫` corner.** Remark 2.11 concedes `U` is not defined for
   `ζ_𝒫` ("—"). Then statement 2 of Thm 2.10 establishes `E ⊬ F` and nothing more; the necessity
   claim needs `E ∧ U ⊬ F`, for which no witness is offered.
3. **`E` is not what `Ξ_Q` lacks.** `Z_Q` for `h(−D) > 1` is a finite `C`-combination of `h` Euler
   products (Remark 18.12a, as already noted). So `Ξ_Q` falsifies "`Z` *is* an Euler
   product", not "`Z` is built from Euler products with nonnegative atoms". The independence
   statement is sensitive to which form of `E` is meant, and the document uses two.

**Repair available.** Keep the table as a *summary of three separate falsifiers*, drop the word
"triangle", drop the "jointly non-redundant ⟹ every derivation must invoke each" corollary, and state
each necessity claim separately with its own conclusion. Then all three are `PROVED` and the
aggregate claim disappears — which is the honest outcome.

## §2. Attacks on T4 (§4 of `R150_001`)

### 2.1 Lemma 4.2 — correct, range wrong

The identity is right. Euler–Maclaurin with `g(x) = x^{−iγ}`, `|g'| = |γ|/x`, error `O(1 + |γ|log 2)`;
`|2^{1−iγ}| = 2` so `|2^{1−iγ} − 1| ∈ [1,3]`; main term modulus `= N|2^{1−iγ}−1|/|1−iγ| ≍ N/(1+|γ|)`.
Independent numerical identity check (main term vs. direct summation, `dps = 30`):

| `N` | `γ` | main term | direct sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| `10^4` | `1` | `9805.7238` | `9805.7374` |
| `10^4` | `50` | `599.2158` | `599.2526` |
| `10^4` | `100` | `103.8928` | `103.7980` |

The last row is the attack: at `N = 10^4`, `γ = 100 = N^{1/2}`, the main term is `≈ 104` and the
stated error is `O(1 + |γ|) = O(100)`. **The conclusion `|Σ| ≫ N/(1+|γ|)` is exactly false at the
endpoint of the stated range `|γ| ≤ N^{1/2}`.** Repair: `|γ| ≤ N^{1/2−ε}`, or `|γ| ≤ c N^{1/2}` with
`c` small and the implied constant tracked. Everything downstream only needs `γ = O(1)`, so the
repair is free — but the statement as printed is wrong and would be caught on refereeing.

**A second, conceptual objection to Lemma 4.2's role.** The non-cancellation it exhibits is
**entirely the pole of `ζ` at `s = 1`**, i.e. `Σ_{n ≤ N} n^{−iγ} ≈ N^{1−iγ}/(1−iγ)` is the residue.
It is not a statement about "regimes", and it carries no information about the difficulty of RH. The
contrast is decisive: for a holomorphic cusp form, `Σ_{n ≤ N} λ_f(n) ≪ N^{1/3}` — **power saving at
`γ = 0`, the smooth regime at its most extreme**, unconditional, by the functional equation and
Voronoi (Hecke). So smooth-regime cancellation is not impossible; it is impossible *for a Dirichlet
series with a pole*. §4.1–§4.3 should say this, and saying it removes most of T4's rhetorical force.

### 2.2 Theorem 4.5 — true, and evaded by every real method

The falsifier `a_n = A n^{iγ}` proves the floor for schemes valid for **all** `a` with given norms.
But the object every unconditional method actually bounds is the **centered** sum
`Σ Λ(n)n^{−iγ}w(n/N) − N^{1−iγ}W(1−iγ)`, and `a_n = An^{iγ}` is not in that class. Concretely:

* PNT with the Vinogradov–Korobov error is a **theorem about the centered sum**:
  `ψ(x) − x ≪ x exp(−c(log x)^{3/5}(loglog x)^{−1/5})`, obtained from C3 + contour shift.
* Baker–Harman–Pintz: `ψ(x + x^{0.525}) − ψ(x) ∼ x^{0.525}`. This is a **power-saving cancellation in
  the smooth regime** (`γ = O(1)`; the "phase" is the short-interval cut-off), obtained from zero-density
  estimates plus VK — *not* from a coefficient-blind scheme applied to `Λ`.

So the correct reading of Thm 4.5 is: *a scheme that ignores the arithmetic of `a` cannot beat the
trivial bound.* True, known, and not a constraint on anything anyone does. It should be stated as a
**normalisation lemma**, not as the engine of a method theorem.

### 2.3 The proof of Theorem 4.8 is invalid for three of the seven mechanisms

Theorem 4.8's proof, first sentence: *"C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C7 are coefficient-blind in the sense of
Definition 4.4 — their hypotheses constrain `supp a`, `‖a‖_∞`, `‖a‖_2` and the phase, never the
arithmetic of `a`."* This is false for three entries, and the failure is not cosmetic: Theorem 4.5's
falsifier `a_n = An^{iγ}` must lie in the mechanism's hypothesis class for the floor to apply.

* **C5 (Vinogradov minor arcs).** The hypothesis is `a_n = Λ(n)`. The bound
  `Σ_{n≤N}Λ(n)e(nα) ≪ (Nq^{−1/2} + N^{4/5} + (Nq)^{1/2})log^C N` is *the* arithmetic bound of the
  subject; it is proved by Vaughan's identity, i.e. from the multiplicative structure of `Λ`. It is
  the least coefficient-blind statement in analytic number theory. Theorem 4.5 does not apply.
* **C7 (restriction / Green–Tao, Harper).** Hypotheses are about `Λ` (or a Selberg majorant), again
  arithmetic. Theorem 4.5 does not apply. D.60.6 is in fact cited here rather than Thm 4.5,
  so the proof of Thm 4.8 contradicts §4.4's own treatment.
* **C3 (VK bound for `Σ n^{−it}`).** Hypothesis `a_n ≡ 1`; a one-point class. `a_n = An^{iγ}` is not
  in it. Theorem 4.5 does not apply; §4.4's C3 paragraph argues instead that the *frequency*
  hypothesis fails at fixed `γ`, which is a different and correct argument.

**Consequence:** Theorem 4.8's proof reduces to "§4.4, case by case". That is acceptable — §4.4's
per-mechanism arguments for C1, C2, C3, C5 are correct — but the theorem must be re-proved as a
finite case check, and Theorem 4.5 must be demoted from "the engine" to "the argument for C1, C2, C4".

### 2.4 Theorem 4.7 is false as applied; true only as a tautology

The displayed identity `Σ_m Σ_k a_m b_k χ(mk) = (Σ a_m χ(m))(Σ b_k χ(k))` for completely
multiplicative `χ` is correct **for uncoupled ranges**. In every Vaughan / Heath-Brown / Linnik
decomposition the Type II ranges are **coupled**: `Σ_{mk ≤ N}`, or `m ≍ M`, `k ≍ K`, `MK ≍ N`, with
`mk` confined to a dyadic block. Under the constraint `mk ≤ N` the form does **not** factorise, and
the whole method consists in exploiting exactly that coupling via Cauchy–Schwarz in `m` and the
resulting correlation sums `Σ_k b_k \bar b_{k'} χ(k/k')`-type expressions. Step (3) of Thm 4.7
("evaluating a product of Dirichlet series at a single `s` is the product of the evaluations") is an
identity for *absolutely convergent whole* Dirichlet series in `σ > 1`; applied to `s = 1 + iγ` it is
the trivially true statement `−ζ'/ζ(1+iγ) = (\text{product})`, which says nothing about the
**truncated** sum over `N < n ≤ 2N` that is T4's target. Decoupling the ranges requires Perron, which
reintroduces a `t`-integral of length `T` and lands in C4 at `T ≥ N` — the useful regime, not a wall.

Corollary 4.7a ("the bilinear machinery becomes a mechanism only when the phase does not factorise")
inherits the defect and should be withdrawn with it. Corollary 4.7a is also refuted in the direction
that matters: **Friedlander–Iwaniec** (primes `x² + y⁴`) and **Heath-Brown** (`x³ + 2y³`) obtain
power-saving bilinear cancellation whose input is the arithmetic of a quadratic/cubic ring, at no
"oscillatory frequency" at all. These are arithmetic-not-metric cancellation mechanisms, and their
existence is a direct answer to O-150.4 in the negative sense not originally expected.

### 2.5 Mechanisms outside `𝒞` that act in the smooth regime with power saving

Requested by the brief; this is the list that shows `𝒞` is curated, not complete.

| mechanism | acts at fixed `γ`? | power saving? | in `𝒞`? |

> *For the specific mechanisms C1, C2, C3, C4, C5 applied directly to the uncentered twisted prime

### 2.6 The sharp thresholds

C1 (`θ_0 = 1`), C2 (`θ_0 ≥ 1`), C4 (`T ≥ N`), C5 (`q ∈ [N^ε, N^{1−ε}]`) are correctly computed; I
checked the exponent arithmetic in §4.4 and found no error. C3's `θ_0 = ∞` is a reasonable reading of

C3**: the reason C3 does no better is that VK is the best known zero-free region — an empirical fact
about the state of the art, not a derived threshold. Calling it a "sharp boundary" presents a
literature summary as a theorem. Demote to `CERTIFIED` (already done for the exponent; extend the

### 2.7 Falsifier F4-a (`Ξ_a`) — holds, with one gap

The claim that every mechanism in `𝒞` gives for `N^{−a}Λ_a` exactly what it gives for `Λ` is correct

argument needs partial summation with the weight `n^a`, which is routine but is not written. Minor;

## §3. Attacks on T5 (§5 of `R150_001`)

### 3.1 Definition 5.2 is not vacuously narrow — but it targets a use nobody makes

The brief asks whether "norm-only, valid for all `b`" makes T5 vacuous. It does not: the large sieve
`Σ_{q≤Q}Σ^*_{a(q)}|Σ_{n≤N}b_n e(na/q)|² ≤ (N+Q²)‖b‖_2²`, Montgomery–Vaughan
`∫_0^T|Σ b_n n^{−it}|²dt = (T+O(N))‖b‖_2²`, character orthogonality and Heath-Brown's quadratic large
sieve are all of exactly this shape and all valid for every `b`. Def. 5.2 captures them. Theorem 5.3
is correct.

The damage is elsewhere. **Single-fibre extraction `|S_{f_0}|^{2k} ≤ M_{2k}` is not how orthogonality
is used.** The standard uses are:

1. **Large-value counting (Halász–Montgomery).** From `M_2 ≤ (|ℱ|+L)‖b‖_2²` one deduces
   `#\{f : |S_f| ≥ V\} ≤ (|ℱ|+L)‖b‖_2²/V²`, and combined with a second moment over a *well-spaced*
   subfamily one obtains the classical large-value theorems. These feed zero-density estimates
   `N(σ,T) ≪ T^{3(1−σ)/(2−σ)}log^C T` (Ingham) — **non-trivial arithmetic output from a Parseval-type
   bound**. T5 says nothing about it, because counting is not extraction.
2. **Montgomery–Vaughan hybrid large sieve.** Same: the output is a bound on the number of large
   values in a `(q,t)`-grid, and it produces Linnik-type and density results.
3. **Averaged statements** (Bombieri–Vinogradov, Barban–Davenport–Halberstam) where the target *is*
   the average and no fibre is ever isolated.

So T5's correct scope is: *the particular extraction step used in D.59 cannot work.* That is a true
and useful negative statement about **one construction in this programme**, not a theorem about
moment methods. Label accordingly.

**Heath-Brown's quadratic large sieve specifically** (the brief's question): its statement is norm-only,
so T5 applies to the statement and the single-fibre extraction from it is `≥ L`. It does **not**
escape T5, and it does not give a non-trivial extraction. Where it does give non-trivial arithmetic —
Heath-Brown's `2`-class-group and quadratic-`L`-function moment results — it does so by combining the
sieve with the *arithmetic* of the coefficients (reciprocity, Gauss sums, and a restriction to
`d ≠ □`), i.e. by leaving Def. 5.2. §5.7 anticipates this correctly; this confirms it and

### 3.2 Corollary 5.5 is arithmetically wrong

Stated requirement: `(|ℱ| + kL)·E_{2k}(b) ≤ L^{k+o(1)}`. Stated lower bound, same paragraph:
`E_{2k}(b) = ‖b^{*k}‖_2² ≥ ‖b^{*k}‖_1²/(kL) = ‖b‖_1^{2k}/(kL) = L^{2k−1}/k` for nonnegative `b` with
`‖b‖_1 = L`. Substituting:

```
(|ℱ| + kL)·L^{2k−1}/k  ≥  kL·L^{2k−1}/k  =  L^{2k}   >   L^{k+o(1)}   for every k ≥ 1.
```

So the requirement is **unsatisfiable**, for every `|ℱ|`, and the printed conclusion "forces
`|ℱ| ≤ L^{o(1)}`" does not follow from the displayed inequalities. The repair is a strengthening, not
a weakening — the corollary should read *"no nonnegative `b` admits a non-trivial extraction at any
`k`; only centered `b` can, and then only via square-root cancellation in the `2k`-fold energy"* —
but as printed it is an error and the `PROVED` label must come off until rewritten.

### 3.3 Proposition 5.7 conflates two convolutions and one family type

`S_f² = Σ_m (b*b)_m ψ_f(m)` gives `M_4 ≤ (|ℱ|+2L)‖b*b‖_2²`, with `‖b*b‖_2² = Σ_m|Σ_n b_nb_{m−n}|²`.
The additive energy is `E(b) = Σ_d|Σ_n b_n\bar b_{n+d}|² = ‖b ⋆ \bar b‖_2²`. For real nonnegative `b`
these agree up to reflection; for the centered complex `b = Λ − M_Q` of D.59 they are different
quantities and the identification is not justified in the text. More seriously: for the archimedean
family `ψ_t(n) = n^{−it}` the relevant convolution is the **Dirichlet** convolution, so the invariant
is a multiplicative-energy count, not an additive one. The claimed uniformity of T5 "over the family
type" is exactly where this breaks: the *theorem* (5.3) is uniform, but the *quantitative corollaries*
(5.5–5.7) are not, and §5.6's table silently applies the additive invariant to the archimedean row.

### 3.4 Proposition 5.6 — sharpness claim mixes normalisations

"attained by `b = \bar ψ_{f_0}` when `|ℱ| ≤ L`, and by a `δ`-function when `|ℱ| ≥ L`": with the
standing hypothesis `‖b‖_∞ ≤ 1` and `supp b ⊆ {1,…,L}`, a `δ`-function has `‖b‖_2² = 1`, not `L`, so
it is extremal for a *different* normalisation. The two-sided sharpness claim needs the constraint
stated per case. Minor; the `k = 1` equivalence with D.60.3 (`|ℱ| ≍ Q² = N^{2c}`, `L ≍ N`,
`|ℱ| ≥ L ⟺ c ≥ 1/2`) is correct and is the cleanest thing in §5.

### 3.5 Proposition 5.8 — the "analytic face of the sterility lemma" is an analogy, not an implication

The proof is one sentence: *"Theorem 5.3 is exactly this statement."* It is not: phase-36 Prop. 3.9
is about superadditivity and growth of an abstract index computed from signature data; Thm 5.3 is
about a moment bound and a trivial bound computed from norm data. The common shape ("same data on
both sides ⟹ no contradiction") is a **slogan**, and the document says so honestly in §3, but
`PROVED` on Prop. 5.8 asserts an implication that is not derived. Demote to `ANALOGY` or supply an
actual functor from one setting to the other.
