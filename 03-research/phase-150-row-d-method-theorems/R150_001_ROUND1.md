## 2. T3 — the Beurling theorem: the additive structure of `Z` is necessary

### 2.1 The Beurling class

**Definition 2.1.** A *Beurling system of generalized primes* is a sequence
`𝒫 : 1 < p_1 ≤ p_2 ≤ … → ∞` of reals. Its *generalized integers* `𝒩` is the multiplicative semigroup
generated by `𝒫`, listed with multiplicity as `1 = n_0 ≤ n_1 ≤ …`, with counting functions
`N_𝒫(x) = #\{k : n_k ≤ x\}` and `π_𝒫(x) = #\{j : p_j ≤ x\}`. Set

```
ζ_𝒫(s) = Σ_k n_k^{−s} = Π_j (1 − p_j^{−s})^{−1},        Λ_𝒫 = Σ_{j,m} (log p_j)·δ_{p_j^m},
ψ_𝒫(x) = Σ_{p^m ≤ x} log p,                             −ζ'_𝒫/ζ_𝒫(s) = ∫_1^∞ x^{−s} dΛ_𝒫(x).
```

(One also allows `𝒫` to be a nonnegative measure `dΠ` on `(1,∞)` rather than a discrete sequence;
DMV work in that generality. Nothing below depends on the choice.)

**Import 2.2 (Beurling 1937; `IMPORTED`, statement only).** If
`N_𝒫(x) = ax + O(x(log x)^{−γ})` with `a > 0` and `γ > 3/2`, then `π_𝒫(x) ∼ x/log x`. The exponent
`3/2` is sharp: there are systems with `γ = 3/2` and no PNT (Diamond 1970; Kahane). *Reference: A.
Beurling, Acta Math. 68 (1937), 255–291; H. G. Diamond, Illinois J. Math. 14 (1970), 12–28. I have
not re-read either; the exponent `3/2` and its sharpness are `CERTIFIED`.*

**Import 2.3 (Diamond–Montgomery–Vorhauer 2006; the falsifier class).** *H. G. Diamond, H. L.
Montgomery, U. M. A. Vorhauer, "Beurling primes with large oscillation", Math. Ann. **334** (2006),
1–36, `doi:10.1007/s00208-005-0638-2`.*

> **(DMV-a) `IMPORTED` (shape of the statement).** There exists a Beurling system `𝒫` with
> `N_𝒫(x) = a x + O(x·exp(−c(log x)^{1/2}))` for some `a > 0, c > 0` — so `ζ_𝒫` continues
> meromorphically to a region left of `Re s = 1` with a single simple pole at `s = 1` and the PNT
> holds for `𝒫` — whose zeta function has **infinitely many zeros `β_n + iγ_n` with `β_n → 1`**.
> Consequently `ζ_𝒫` has **no zero-free strip**: for every `δ > 0` there are zeros with `β > 1 − δ`.
>
> **(DMV-b) `CERTIFIED`, not read in the source.** The zeros can be placed so that
> `β_n ≥ 1 − C/log γ_n` for infinitely many `n`, i.e. the de la Vallée Poussin region is attained in
> the Beurling class; equivalently
> `π_𝒫(x) − li(x) = Ω_±(x·exp(−c'(log x)^{1/2}))`, so the classical error term is essentially
> optimal for Beurling systems.

**Label discipline.** Everything T3 needs is **(DMV-a)**, and only in the weak form *"there is a

### 2.2 The axiom list BE

**Definition 2.4 (the Beurling axioms).** Let `Z` be a function of a complex variable given in
`Re s > 1` by a Dirichlet-type series. Consider:

* **BE1 (nonnegative Dirichlet datum).** `Z(s) = Σ_k n_k^{−s}` with `n_k ≥ 1` real, the series
  absolutely convergent for `Re s > 1`; equivalently `Z(s) = ∫_1^∞ x^{−s}dN(x)` with `dN ≥ 0`.
* **BE2 (Euler product with nonnegative atoms).** `Z(s) = Π_j(1 − p_j^{−s})^{−1}` absolutely
  convergent for `Re s > 1`; hence `−Z'/Z(s) = ∫_1^∞x^{−s}dΛ(x)` with `dΛ ≥ 0`, and
  `log Z(s) = Σ_{j,m} m^{−1}p_j^{−ms}` has nonnegative coefficients.
* **BE3 (pole and residue).** `Z` continues meromorphically to a region `Re s > 1 − η(|t|)` (some
  `η > 0`) with a single simple pole at `s = 1`, `Res_{s=1}Z = a > 0`.
* **BE4 (reality).** The coefficients are real and nonnegative, so `Z(s̄) = \overline{Z(s)}` and the
  zeros are symmetric about `R`.
* **BE5 (Chebyshev).** `Λ([1,x]) = ψ(x) ≍ x`.
* **BE6 (no zeros to the right).** `Z(s) ≠ 0` for `Re s > 1`, immediate from BE2.
* **BE7 (growth).** `Z(σ + it) ≪ |t|^{A}` in the continuation region, for some `A`, uniformly for
  `σ ≥ 1 − η(|t|)/2`, `|t| ≥ 2`.
* **BE8 (Landau).** For a Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients the abscissa of convergence
  is a singularity — available from BE1 alone, for `Z` and for every series with nonnegative
  coefficients built from `Z` by BE2 (in particular for `Z(s)^{3}|Z(s+iτ)|^{4}|Z(s+2iτ)|`-type
  products).

**Proposition 2.5 (`PROVED`, modulo Import 2.3).** The DMV system of (DMV-a) satisfies BE1, BE2,
BE4, BE5, BE6, BE8 outright, and BE3, BE7 in the form supplied by the source; and it has no
zero-free strip.

*Proof.* BE1, BE2, BE4, BE6 hold for every Beurling system by construction. BE5 follows from
`N(x) = ax + O(x e^{−c√log x})` by the standard Chebyshev/Mertens argument (which uses only `dΛ ≥ 0`,
`dN ≥ 0` and the density of `𝒩`). BE8 is Landau's theorem, which requires only nonnegativity of the
coefficients. BE3 and BE7 are part of the DMV construction and carry its `CERTIFIED` label. The
final clause is (DMV-a). ∎

**Proposition 2.6 (`RESTATEMENT` + `CLASSICAL`: the classical region is available in the class).**
From BE1–BE8 alone one derives the de la Vallée Poussin region
`Z(σ+it) ≠ 0` for `σ ≥ 1 − c/log(|t|+2)`.

*Proof sketch, to show the axioms suffice.* By BE2, for `σ > 1`,
`log|Z(σ)^3 Z(σ+iτ)^4 Z(σ+2iτ)| = Σ_{j,m}m^{−1}p_j^{−mσ}(3 + 4cos(mτ log p_j) + cos(2mτ log p_j)) ≥ 0`
using `3 + 4cos θ + cos 2θ = 2(1 + cos θ)² ≥ 0` and the nonnegativity of the atoms (BE2). Hence
`|Z(σ)^3Z(σ+iτ)^4Z(σ+2iτ)| ≥ 1`. Feed in the pole (BE3) for the `Z(σ)^3` factor, the growth bound
(BE7) for the other two, and a zero at `β + iτ` for the middle factor; Landau's standard optimisation
gives `1 − β ≫ 1/log τ`. Nothing else is used. ∎

**Observation 2.7 (`CERTIFIED`; this is the inspection clause and it is not a theorem).** Every
classical *unconditional* zero-free-region argument for `ζ` — de la Vallée Poussin; Landau's
nonnegative-trigonometric-polynomial method with any admissible polynomial `Σ a_ν cos νθ ≥ 0`,
`a_ν ≥ 0`, `a_0 < a_1`; the Mertens-type variants; the "`Z(s)Z(s+iτ)`-positivity" arguments — uses
only BE1–BE8. Concretely: it uses nonnegativity of the coefficients of `log Z` (BE2), the pole and
its residue (BE3), reality (BE4), the growth bound (BE7), and Landau/positivity (BE8). It does not
use the functional equation, the integrality of the `n_k`, or any additive structure.

This is a claim about a body of proofs, verified by inspection of the standard ones. It is an
**observation**, not a theorem: there is no formal system here in which "every classical proof" is a
well-defined object. This is the second weak joint of T3.

### 2.3 T3

**Theorem 2.8 (T3, the Beurling constraint; `METHOD-CONSTRAINT`, conditional on Import 2.3).**
*There is no derivation, from BE1–BE8 and the data they mention alone, of the statement*

```
(ZFS_δ)      Z(β + iγ) = 0  ⟹  β ≤ 1 − δ,          for some fixed δ > 0.
```

*In particular there is no such derivation of `(ZFS_δ)` for `ξ`, a fortiori none of RH.*

*Proof.* A derivation from BE1–BE8 is a finite chain of steps each invoking only those axioms and the
objects they name (`N`, `Λ`, `ψ`, `Z`, the pole, the residue, the growth exponent). Such a chain is
valid for **every** structure satisfying the axioms. By Proposition 2.5 the DMV system is such a
structure, and it violates `(ZFS_δ)` for every `δ > 0`. Contradiction. ∎

**Corollary 2.9 (`PROVED`, same conditionality).** Any proof of `(ZFS_δ)` for `ζ` — hence any proof
of RH — must invoke, at some line, a property of `ζ` that **fails for Beurling systems**. It is not
enough to invoke the Euler product with `Λ ≥ 0`, the pole at `1`, meromorphic continuation, growth
bounds, Chebyshev, or Landau positivity: `ζ_𝒫` has all of these.

**Relation to T2 (Paper 47 Thm 18.16).** T2 says a proof of the sign must invoke A7/A8/A9 — the Euler
product's consequences. T3 says invoking A8 (`Λ ≥ 0` on prime powers, with `ψ(x) = O(x)`) is **not
enough**, because A8 is exactly BE2 + BE5, and `ζ_𝒫` satisfies it. T3 therefore sharpens T2 from
"you need the Euler product" to "the Euler product alone, in the form A8, is insufficient". The two
are not in tension: `Ξ_Q` has no A8, `ζ_𝒫` has A8 and nothing else.

### 2.6 The falsifier triangle and its independence

Let `E` = "Euler product with nonnegative von Mangoldt atoms" (axiom A8 of Paper 47 Def. 18.13, =
BE2 + BE5); `F` = "functional equation of Poisson/Tate type" (A1, with the theta/self-duality input);
`U` = "equimodular polar block / unimodular local data" (A3, `|p − ½| = θ_P` constant; for `ζ` this is
`θ_P = ½`, the statement that the polar block of `−Z'/Z` sits at `{0,1}` symmetrically and the local
roots have the modulus the weight prescribes).

| object | `E` | `F` | `U` | violates | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ξ` | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — (conclusion `OPEN` = RH) | Paper 47 Thm 18.14 row 1 |
| `Ξ_Q` (Epstein, principal `Q`, `h(−D) > 1`) | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | Hodge index (Thm 18.10); has real zeros in `(0,1)` (Thm 18.5) | Paper 47 §18.2 |
| `Ξ_a` (`0 < a < ½`) | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | zeros at `Re s = ½ ± a` | Paper 47 Thm 12.4 |
| **`ζ_𝒫` (DMV)** | ✓ | ✗ | — | `(ZFS_δ)` for every `δ` | Import 2.3 |

**Theorem 2.10 (mutual independence of the three axioms; `PROVED`, given the three falsifiers).**
`E`, `F`, `U` are pairwise independent, and none is implied by the conjunction of the other two:

1. `F ⊬ E`: `Ξ_Q` satisfies `F` (and `U`) and not `E` (Paper 47 Thm 18.12: `r_Q` is not
   multiplicative once `h(−D) > 1`; `Z_Q` is a nontrivial `C`-combination of `h ≥ 2` distinct Euler
   products, hence not an Euler product, Remark 18.12a).
2. `E ⊬ F`: `ζ_𝒫` satisfies `E` and not `F` (Prop. 2.5 and §2.5 row 1).
3. `E ∧ F ⊬ U`: `Ξ_a` satisfies both and not `U` (Paper 47 Thm 18.14 row 2: A3 fails since
   `p − ½ ∈ {±(½−a), ±(½+a)}`).

Consequently the three are jointly non-redundant: a derivation of the sign must, by Theorem 2.8,
Theorem 18.16 and Theorem 12.4 respectively, invoke each of `E`, `F`, `U` at some line. ∎

**Remark 2.11 (a triangle, not a chain).** The three falsifiers each remove exactly one vertex and
keep the other two, which is why the picture is a triangle: `Ξ_Q` has `+` and no Euler product,
`ζ_𝒫` has the Euler product and no `+`, `Ξ_a` has both and the wrong local phase data. Note that

## 4. T4 — the smooth-regime theorem

### 4.1 The two regimes

**Definition 4.1.** For a phase `φ(n) = e^{2πi f(n)}` on `N < n ≤ 2N`, call the pair `(f, N)`
*oscillatory* if `f'` varies by `≫ 1` across the range (equivalently `f(2N) − f(N) ≫ N^{θ}` for some
`θ > 0` together with a nondegenerate derivative), and *smooth* otherwise. For `f(n) = −(γ/2π)log n`
one has `f'(x) = −γ/(2πx)`, `f''(x) = γ/(2πx²)`, so the total variation of `f` across `[N,2N]` is
`(γ log 2)/2π` — **independent of `N`**. The regime is therefore governed by `γ` alone: oscillatory
iff `γ → ∞`, and RH is a statement at **fixed `γ`, `N → ∞`** — the smooth regime, at the extreme
`γ = O(1)`.

### 4.2 The untwisted size: the sum does not cancel

**Lemma 4.2 (`PROVED`; an identity check, no numerics).** For `γ ∈ R` and `N ≥ 1`,

```
Σ_{N < n ≤ 2N} n^{−iγ}  =  N^{1−iγ}·(2^{1−iγ} − 1)/(1 − iγ)  +  O(1 + |γ|),
```

and `|2^{1−iγ} − 1| ≥ |2^{1−iγ}| − 1 = 1`. Hence for `|γ| ≤ N^{1/2}` (say),

```
|Σ_{N < n ≤ 2N} n^{−iγ}|  ≫  N/(1 + |γ|).
```

*Proof.* Euler–Maclaurin: `Σ_{N<n≤2N}g(n) = ∫_N^{2N}g + O(|g(N)| + |g(2N)| + ∫_N^{2N}|g'|)` with
`g(x) = x^{−iγ}`, `|g| = 1`, `|g'(x)| = |γ|/x`, so the error is `O(1 + |γ| log 2)`. The integral is
`[x^{1−iγ}/(1−iγ)]_N^{2N}`. For the lower bound on `|2^{1−iγ}−1|`: `|2^{1−iγ}| = 2^{1} = 2`, so the
modulus is at least `1` and the quantity never vanishes (`2^{1−iγ} = 1` would force
`(1−iγ)log 2 ∈ 2πiZ`, impossible as `Re((1−iγ)\log 2) = \log 2 ≠ 0`). ∎

**Corollary 4.3 (`PROVED`; where the content of RH sits).** With `w ∈ C_c^∞`, `supp w ⊂ [1,2]`,
`w ≥ 0`, `\widehat w(0) > 0`, the explicit formula gives

```
Σ_n Λ(n) n^{−iγ} w(n/N)  =  N^{1−iγ}·W(1 − iγ)  −  Σ_ρ N^{ρ−iγ}·W(ρ − iγ)  −  (archimedean),
W(s) := ∫_0^∞ w(u)u^{s−1}du.
```

The main term has modulus `≍ N/(1 + |γ|)` (Lemma 4.2, with `W` in place of the sharp cut-off). RH is
the assertion that the **second** term is `O(N^{1/2+ε})`. So:

> at fixed `γ`, the sum `Σ Λ(n)n^{−iγ}w(n/N)` **is of size `≍ N`**; the entire content of RH lies in
> a secondary term of relative size `N^{−1/2}`.

This is the sentence T4 formalises. Any method that outputs an **upper bound for the whole sum** is,
in the smooth regime, bounding an object of size `≍ N` and therefore cannot output anything below `N`
— let alone isolate the secondary term.

### 4.3 The class `𝒞` and the coefficient-blindness floor

**Definition 4.4 (bound scheme; coefficient-blind).** A *bound scheme* is a map assigning to data
`(N, γ, a = (a_n)_{N<n≤2N})` a bound `B` with `|Σ_n a_n n^{−iγ}| ≤ B`, proved for all `a` in a class
specified by hypotheses. The scheme is **coefficient-blind** if its hypotheses on `a` involve only
`supp a`, `‖a‖_∞`, `‖a‖_2` (and possibly `‖a‖_1`), so that `B = B(N, γ, ‖a‖_∞, ‖a‖_2, ‖a‖_1)`.

**Theorem 4.5 (the floor; `PROVED`).** Let `B` be a coefficient-blind bound scheme, valid for every
`a` supported in `(N,2N]` with `‖a‖_∞ ≤ A`. Then

```
B(N, γ, A, A√N, AN)  ≥  A·N.
```

In particular, normalising as for `Λ` (`A = log 2N`, `‖a‖_2 ≍ (N log N)^{1/2}`, `‖a‖_1 ≍ N log N`),
every coefficient-blind scheme returns `≥ N log N`: **nothing**, uniformly in `γ` and `N`.

*Proof.* Take `a_n := A·n^{iγ}·1_{(N,2N]}(n)`. Then `‖a‖_∞ = A`, `‖a‖_2 = A√(N + O(1))`,
`‖a‖_1 = A(N+O(1))`, and `Σ_n a_n n^{−iγ} = A·#\{N<n≤2N\} = A(N + O(1))`. Validity of the scheme for
this `a` forces `B ≥ A N + O(A)`. ∎

**Remark 4.5a.** The theorem is trivial and that is precisely its point: it is the exact formal
content of "magnitude methods are zero-blind" (corpus conclusion 1; NG-B1's universal reason). The
work in T4 is not this lemma; it is the verification (§4.4) that the mechanisms actually used are
coefficient-blind or reduce to coefficient-blind pieces.

**Definition 4.6 (the class `𝒞`).** `𝒞` is the following explicit, finite list of unconditional
cancellation mechanisms, each with its hypothesis:

* **(C1) Kusmin–Landau.** If `f'` is monotone and `‖f'‖_{R/Z} ≥ λ` on `[N,2N]` then
  `|Σ e(f(n))| ≪ 1/λ`.
* **(C2) van der Corput `k`-th derivative tests / exponent pairs `(κ,λ)`.** If
  `|f^{(k)}| ≍ F/N^{k}` then `Σ e(f(n)) ≪ N(F/N^k)^{1/(2^k−2)} + …`; in exponent-pair form,
  `Σ_{N<n≤2N} n^{−iγ} ≪ (γ/N)^{κ}N^{λ}`.
* **(C3) Vinogradov's mean value theorem / `ℓ²`-decoupling, and the Vinogradov–Korobov bound for
  `Σ_{N<n≤2N}n^{−it}`.** Input: additive energy of the moment curve over `Z ∩ [1,N]`.
* **(C4) Montgomery–Vaughan / Halász–Montgomery mean value theorem.**
  `∫_0^T|Σ_{n≤N}a_n n^{−it}|²dt = (T + O(N))Σ_n|a_n|²`; and the large sieve
  `Σ_{q≤Q}Σ^*_{a(q)}|Σ_{n≤N}a_ne(na/q)|² ≤ (N + Q²)Σ|a_n|²`.
* **(C5) Vinogradov minor-arc bound.** `Σ_{n≤N}Λ(n)e(nα) ≪ (N q^{−1/2} + N^{4/5} + (Nq)^{1/2})\log^{C}N`
  for `|α − a/q| ≤ q^{−2}`, `(a,q)=1`.
* **(C6) Bilinear identities: Vaughan, Heath-Brown, Linnik.** `Λ` written as `O(\log N)` sums of
  Type I (`Σ_{d ≤ U}c_d Σ_{m} g(dm)`) and Type II (`Σ_{U<m≤V}Σ_{k}a_m b_k g(mk)`) terms.
* **(C7) Restriction / additive energy bounds for exponential sums over primes (Green–Tao, Harper).**

This list is the analytic core of the fifteen mechanisms of NG-B1 restricted to the ones that produce
*cancellation in a twisted sum*. **"`𝒞` is the complete list of known unconditional cancellation
mechanisms" is `CERTIFIED`, an observation, not a theorem.** See §4.7.

### 4.4 Mechanism by mechanism: the sharp boundary

Throughout: the target is `Σ_{N<n≤2N}Λ(n)n^{−iγ}w(n/N)` (or its untwisted model, Lemma 4.2), the
trivial bound is `≍ N`, and "the mechanism gives nothing" means "returns `≥ N^{1−o(1)}`".

**(C1) Kusmin–Landau.** `f(x) = −(γ/2π)\log x`, `f'(x) = −γ/(2πx)`, monotone. For `γ ≤ N` one has
`‖f'‖ = |γ|/(2πx) ≍ γ/N`, so C1 gives `≪ N/γ`. Compare Lemma 4.2: the **truth** is `≍ N/(1+γ)`.
So C1 is sharp and gives exactly the truth; it is not a cancellation statement at all below `γ ≍ N`,
because there is no cancellation below `γ ≍ N`. **Boundary: `θ_0 = 1` (`γ ≍ N`).** `PROVED`.

**(C2) van der Corput / exponent pairs.** Second-derivative test with `|f''| ≍ γ/N²`:
`≪ N(γ/N²)^{1/2} + (γ/N²)^{−1/2} = γ^{1/2} + N γ^{−1/2}`. For `γ ≤ N` the second term is `≥ N^{1/2}`
and for fixed `γ` it is `≍ N`: nothing. Exponent pair `(κ,λ)`: `(γ/N)^{κ}N^{λ}` beats `N` iff
`γ^{κ} < N^{1+κ−λ}`; with the trivial pair `(0,1)` this is vacuous, and with any `κ > 0` it requires
`γ > N^{(1+κ−λ)/κ}`, which for the classical pairs (`λ − κ ≤ 1/2`) means `γ ≥ N^{1/(2κ)} ≫ N`.
**Boundary: `θ_0 ≥ 1`.** `PROVED` (arithmetic of exponents only).

**(C3) Vinogradov / decoupling / VK.** These are used with `N ≪ exp(c(\log t)^{2/3})`, i.e. with
the **length exponentially smaller than any power of the frequency**; the output is
`|ζ(1+it)| ≪ (\log t)^{2/3}` and the region `1 − c/(\log t)^{2/3}(\log\log t)^{1/3}`. At fixed `γ`
the hypothesis `frequency ≫ length^{θ}` fails for every `θ > 0`, and there is no statement. Moreover
(§2.5) the input is an additive-energy count on `Z ∩ [1,N]` — a magnitude statement about the
integers, transported to the zeros only through `|ζ|` (closure plan §3.3). **Boundary: `θ_0 = ∞`
(frequency larger than every power of the length).** `CERTIFIED` for the exponent `2/3`;
`PROVED` for the statement that the hypothesis fails at fixed `γ`.

**(C4) Mean value theorems.** Montgomery–Vaughan gives
`∫_0^T|Σ_{n≤N}a_nn^{−it}|²dt = (T + O(N))‖a‖_2²`, which is an asymptotic — hence a bound better than
`T·‖a‖_2²` — only when `T ≥ N`. The large sieve is the additive avatar: `(N + Q²)‖a‖_2²` is
`≍ Q²‖a‖_2²` only for `Q ≥ √N`. **At fixed `γ` there is no `t`-average at all**, and pointwise C4
gives, by positivity of the integrand, nothing: `|Σ a_n n^{−iγ}|² ≤ ` (no bound). Extracting a
pointwise bound from the mean value requires dividing by the measure of a `t`-neighbourhood, which
returns `T^{−1}(T + N)‖a‖_2² ≥ ‖a‖_2² ≍ N\log N` — the trivial bound again. **Boundary: `T ≥ N`
(`θ_0 = 1`).** `PROVED`. This is the archimedean mean-value wall of closure plan §9.3, and it is the
`|ℱ| ≥ L` regime of T5 (§5) in archimedean clothing.

**(C5) Vinogradov minor arcs.** The phase here is additive, `e(nα)`, not `n^{−iγ}`; the analogue of
"fixed frequency" is `α = 0` (or `α = a/q` with `q = O(1)`), which is a **major** arc: the bound is
`≫ N/φ(q)` and the mechanism returns `ψ(N)` itself. The mechanism is nontrivial exactly on
`N^{ε} ≤ q ≤ N^{1−ε}`, i.e. when the frequency has height a positive power of the length.
**Boundary: `θ_0 = ε > 0`, and the fixed-frequency point is on the wrong side of it.** `PROVED`
(the major-arc asymptotic is classical).

**(C6) Bilinear identities — the structural reason.** This is the one mechanism in `𝒞` that is *not*
coefficient-blind, so Theorem 4.5 does not apply and a separate argument is needed.

**Theorem 4.7 (`PROVED`; a completely multiplicative phase factorises every bilinear form).** Let
`χ(n) := n^{−iγ}`, which is completely multiplicative: `χ(mk) = χ(m)χ(k)`. Then for every Type II
term of a Vaughan/Heath-Brown/Linnik decomposition,

```
Σ_{m}Σ_{k} a_m b_k χ(mk)  =  (Σ_m a_m χ(m))·(Σ_k b_k χ(k)),
```

a **rank-one** form. Consequently:

1. Cauchy–Schwarz on the bilinear form, `|Σ_{m,k}a_mb_kχ(mk)| ≤ ‖a‖_2(Σ_m|Σ_k b_k χ(mk)|²)^{1/2}`,
   is an **equality up to the factorisation**, and the inner double sum is
   `‖a‖_2²·|Σ_k b_kχ(k)|²`-shaped: the off-diagonal terms which the method exists to exploit are
   `Σ_{m}χ(m)\overline{χ(m')} = Σ_m (m/m')^{−iγ}`, i.e. copies of the *same* smooth-regime sum. No
   new cancellation is created.
2. Type I terms `Σ_{d≤U}c_d Σ_{m ≤ N/d}χ(dm)` become `Σ_{d≤U}c_dχ(d)·Σ_{m≤N/d}χ(m)`, and the inner
   sum is `≍ (N/d)/(1+|γ|)` by Lemma 4.2 — no cancellation in `m`. With `c_d = μ(d)` the outer sum
   is the truncation of `Σ_d μ(d)d^{−1−iγ} = 1/ζ(1+iγ)`, and the error of that truncation is exactly
   a PNT-strength statement about `μ`: **circular**.
3. Hence every Vaughan/Heath-Brown decomposition of `Σ Λ(n)n^{−iγ}` returns a linear combination of
   products of Dirichlet polynomials evaluated at the single point `s = iγ`, i.e. it returns
   `−ζ'/ζ`-type data at `1 + iγ` — the smooth-regime object itself, not a bound on it.

*Proof.* (1) and (2) are the displayed factorisation plus Lemma 4.2; (3) is the observation that the
Vaughan identity is an identity between Dirichlet series (`−ζ'/ζ = F(s)... ` with `F` a truncated
`μ`-polynomial) and that evaluating a product of Dirichlet series at a single `s` is the product of
the evaluations. ∎

**Corollary 4.7a (`PROVED`).** The bilinear machinery becomes a cancellation mechanism only when the
phase **does not factorise over the bilinear decomposition** — i.e. for `e(n α)` with `α` irrational
(where `e((mk)α)` is not `e(mα)e(kα)`), for Dirichlet characters against an additive phase, or for
`n^{−it}` averaged over a range of `t` of length `≥ N` (C4). At a fixed point `s = 1 + iγ` of a
completely multiplicative twist, it is an identity, not an inequality.

**(C7) Restriction / additive energy.** `R149_001` Thm D.60.6 (`PROVED` there, cited here, not
rebuilt): the Green–Tao restriction exponent `∫_0^1|F|^r ≍ N^{r−1}` is **attained by the major arcs
alone**, i.e. by the `μ(q)/φ(q)` singular-series main terms, hence carries no information about the
zeros. The centered version is open and equivalent to ATRH (D.60.6a). **Boundary: the mechanism is
saturated at the trivial/major-arc exponent.** `CITED`.

### 4.5 T4

**Theorem 4.8 (T4, the smooth-regime constraint; `METHOD-CONSTRAINT` relative to `𝒞`).**
*Let `γ` be fixed (more generally `|γ| ≤ N^{o(1)}`) and let `B` be any bound for
`Σ_{N<n≤2N}Λ(n)n^{−iγ}w(n/N)` obtained by a finite composition of the mechanisms C1–C7, where each
mechanism is applied within its stated hypothesis. Then `B ≥ N^{1−o(1)}`.*

*Proof.* C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C7 are coefficient-blind in the sense of Definition 4.4 — their
hypotheses constrain `supp a`, `‖a‖_∞`, `‖a‖_2` and the phase, never the arithmetic of `a` — so by
Theorem 4.5 each returns `≥ N^{1−o(1)}` for the stated normalisation; §4.4 in addition exhibits, for
each, the sharp frequency threshold below which even its own hypothesis is void. C6 is not
coefficient-blind, but by Theorem 4.7 it is, for the completely multiplicative phase `n^{−iγ}`, an
identity reducing the sum to products of Dirichlet polynomials at `s = 1 + iγ`, each factor of which
is again a smooth-regime sum to which C1–C5, C7 (hence Theorem 4.5) apply. A finite composition of
maps each of which returns `≥ N^{1−o(1)}` on its arguments, applied to a quantity that is itself
`≍ N` by Corollary 4.3, returns `≥ N^{1−o(1)}`. ∎

**Remark 4.8a (what the theorem is and is not).** T4 is a theorem **relative to the list `𝒞`**, in
exactly the sense that T2 is a theorem relative to A1–A9. It is not, and cannot be made into, a
statement about all conceivable methods; there is no formal system in which that is a proposition.
Its force comes from two things that *are* theorems: Lemma 4.2/Corollary 4.3 (the object is not
small — this is unconditional and complete) and Theorem 4.5 (size-only hypotheses have a floor —
unconditional and complete). What is `CERTIFIED` is only the claim that C1–C7 is the list.

### 4.6 Corollaries

**Corollary 4.9 (`PROVED` relative to `𝒞`; NG-B1 and MW-2 acquire a mechanism).** The universal
reason recorded in NG-B1 — "arithmetic information from `Re s > 1` does not propagate into the
strip" — is, for the cancellation entries of that list (large sieve, Korobov–Vinogradov, Turán
powers, Montgomery pair correlation), the statement of T4: at `Re s = 1 + iγ` with `γ` fixed the
relevant sum is of size `≍ N` and the zeros live in a secondary term of relative size `N^{−1/2}`
which no mechanism in `𝒞` isolates. **This is not a restatement of NG-B1**: NG-B1 records failure,
T4 supplies the common cause and the sharp threshold `θ_0` per mechanism.

**Corollary 4.10 (`RESTATEMENT`; zero-free regions are exactly what the oscillatory regime yields).**
Every known unconditional zero-free region has the form `1 − c/(\log t)^{a}` with `a ≥ 2/3`, and is
obtained by running a mechanism of `𝒞` at frequency `t` against lengths `N ≪ t^{o(1)}` — the deep
oscillatory regime. Vinogradov–Korobov (`a = 2/3`) is the sharp instance. A zero-free **strip**
`β ≤ 1 − δ` is a statement at every frequency, including `γ = O(1)`, hence requires a mechanism
outside `𝒞`. *(Consistency check with T3: DMV shows that even a full zero-free strip cannot come from

**Corollary 4.11 (`PROVED`; three known walls are instances).**
(i) The additive-pencil wall `c = 1/2` (D.60.3: `H(1,c)` is a theorem exactly for `c ∈ [1/2,1)`, where

### 4.7 Falsifiers for T4

**Falsifier F4-a (`Ξ_a`; `PROVED`).** Let `Λ_a(n) := Λ(n)(n^{a} + n^{−a})`, `0 < a < ½`, the
coefficients of `−Ξ_a'/Ξ_a` (Paper 47 Prop. 12.6). Then:

* `Λ_a ≥ 0`, `‖Λ_a‖_∞ ≍ N^{a}\log N`, `‖Λ_a‖_2² ≍ N^{1+2a}\log N` on `(N,2N]`. Every mechanism in
  `𝒞` applied to `N^{−a}Λ_a` returns **exactly the bounds it returns for `Λ`** — the hypotheses are
  size hypotheses (C1–C5, C7) and `Λ_a` is `n^{a}Λ(n)(1 + n^{−2a})`, a smooth reweighting; C6
  applies verbatim since `Λ_a = Λ ⋆ (\text{the two shifts})` and the phase still factorises.
* But `Ξ_a` has zeros on `Re s = ½ ± a` (Paper 47 Thm 12.4), so the secondary term in the analogue of
  Corollary 4.3 genuinely has size `N^{1/2+a}`, not `N^{1/2}`.

Hence `𝒞` cannot distinguish `Λ` from `Λ_a`, while the conclusion sought (`β ≤ ½`) is true for one
and false for the other. **`𝒞` cannot prove RH.** This is the T4 form of the `Ξ_a`-indifference
already recorded as D.63.1.

**Falsifier F4-b (Beurling `Λ_𝒫`; `PROVED` modulo Import 2.3).** For a DMV system, `Λ_𝒫 ≥ 0` with
`ψ_𝒫(x) ≍ x`; the coefficient-blind mechanisms C1, C2, C4 (the `t`-mean-value form), C7 apply with
the same exponents; and `ζ_𝒫` has zeros with `β → 1`, so the secondary term has size `N^{β}` with
`β` arbitrarily close to `1`. (C3, C5, C6 are unavailable in the Beurling class — §2.5 — which only
strengthens the point.)

**Falsifier F4-c (the Davenport–Heilbronn gate; `CITED`).** Phase-119 README, "the candid gate": any
argument built only from the functional equation, the explicit formula and mean values of Dirichlet
polynomials applies verbatim to `f_{DH}`, whose RH-analogue is false. C4 is precisely "mean values of
Dirichlet polynomials". T4 and the DH gate are consistent and mutually reinforcing.

### 4.8 The weakest point of T4

**The class `𝒞` is hand-curated.** The honest content of T4 is: (a) an unconditional identity
(Lemma 4.2/Cor. 4.3) saying the smooth-regime sum is not small; (b) an unconditional floor
(Thm 4.5) for size-only hypotheses; (c) a verification, mechanism by mechanism, that C1–C7 are
size-only or factorise. Step (c) is a finite check and is correct; the claim that C1–C7 **exhausts**
the unconditional cancellation toolbox is an observation (`CERTIFIED`). A second, subtler joint:
Theorem 4.7 is a theorem about the *standard* use of bilinear identities against a completely
multiplicative twist; a future use that first breaks the multiplicativity of the phase (e.g. by
inserting an additive character, as in the circle method) escapes it — and that is exactly what the
Farey pencil of phase 149 does, where the wall is instead D.60.3, i.e. C4.

## 5. T5 — the no-extraction theorem

### 5.1 Setting and the definition of "Parseval-type"

**Definition 5.1 (family moment method).** Data: a finite index set `ℱ` ("family"), unimodular
characters `ψ_f : \{1,…,L\} → C`, `|ψ_f(n)| = 1`, coefficients `b = (b_n)_{n ≤ L}`, fibre sums
`S_f := Σ_{n ≤ L} b_n ψ_f(n)`, moments `M_{2k} := Σ_{f∈ℱ}|S_f|^{2k}`, and the **extraction**
`|S_{f_0}|^{2k} ≤ M_{2k}` for a distinguished `f_0 ∈ ℱ`. (D.59 is the case `ℱ = \mathcal F_Q`,
`ψ_{a/q}(n) = e(na/q)`, `f_0 = 0`, `L ≍ N`, `|ℱ| ≍ Q²`; the archimedean case is
`ℱ = [0,T]`, `ψ_t(n) = n^{−it}`; the character case is `ℱ = \{χ \bmod q\}`.)

**Definition 5.2 (Parseval-type bound).** A moment bound `M_{2k} ≤ 𝔅` is **Parseval-type** if `𝔅` is
a function of `(|ℱ|, L, k, ‖b‖_2, ‖b‖_∞, ‖b‖_1)` only, and the bound is asserted for **every** `b`
with those norms. Equivalently: it is derived from an `L²`-orthogonality relation on `ℱ` (large sieve
on Farey points; Montgomery–Vaughan MVT on `[0,T]`; Heath-Brown's quadratic large sieve; Ramanujan-sum
or character orthogonality) followed by the trivial evaluation of the resulting convolution norms.

Every bound of the canonical shape `M_2 ≤ (|ℱ| + L)‖b‖_2²`, and its `2k` version obtained by writing
`S_f^{k} = Σ_m (b^{*k})_m ψ_f(m)` and applying the same orthogonality to `b^{*k}`, is Parseval-type.

### 5.2 T5

**Theorem 5.3 (T5, no extraction; `PROVED`).** *Let `𝔅` be Parseval-type in the sense of
Definition 5.2, valid for all `b` supported in `\{1,…,L\}` with `‖b‖_∞ ≤ 1`, and suppose the family
contains a point `f_0` with `ψ_{f_0} ≡ 1` (or, more generally, any `f_0` and the family is closed
under the twist `b_n ↦ \overline{ψ_{f_0}(n)}b_n`). Then the extracted bound satisfies*

```
𝔅^{1/2k}  ≥  L        for every k ≥ 1,
```

*i.e. the extraction yields at best `|S_{f_0}| ≤ L`, the trivial bound. This holds uniformly in `k`,
in `|ℱ|`, and in the family type.*

*Proof.* Take `b_n := \overline{ψ_{f_0}(n)}` for `n ≤ L`. Then `‖b‖_∞ = 1`, `‖b‖_2 = √L`,
`‖b‖_1 = L` — the extremal values for the given support and sup-norm — and
`S_{f_0} = Σ_{n≤L}|ψ_{f_0}(n)|² = L`. Since `𝔅` is valid for this `b` and
`M_{2k} ≥ |S_{f_0}|^{2k} = L^{2k}`, we get `𝔅 ≥ L^{2k}`. ∎

**Theorem 5.4 (the quantitative form; `PROVED`).** For the canonical Parseval shape, with
`‖b‖_∞ ≤ 1` and `supp b ⊆ \{1,…,L\}`:

```
M_{2k} = Σ_{f}|Σ_m (b^{*k})_m ψ_f(m)|²  ≤  (|ℱ| + kL)·‖b^{*k}‖_2²,
‖b^{*k}‖_2² = Σ_{m ≤ kL} |(b^{*k})_m|²  ≤  kL·L^{2k−2}  =  k L^{2k−1}   (trivially, since |(b^{*k})_m| ≤ L^{k−1}),
```
hence
```
|S_{f_0}|  ≤  M_{2k}^{1/2k}  ≤  ( k(|ℱ| + kL)L^{2k−1} )^{1/2k}  =  L·( k(|ℱ| + kL)/L )^{1/2k}  ≥  L·k^{1/k}  ≥  L.
```

*The last inequality uses `|ℱ| + kL ≥ kL`.* So the Parseval bound with the trivial evaluation of
`‖b^{*k}‖_2` **never** beats the trivial bound, for any `k`, any `|ℱ|`, any family. ∎

**Corollary 5.5 (`PROVED`; where the only possible gain lies).** In the chain of Theorem 5.4 the
single step that can be improved is the trivial evaluation `‖b^{*k}‖_2² ≤ kL^{2k−1}`. Writing
`E_{2k}(b) := ‖b^{*k}‖_2² = Σ_{d}|𝒞_{2k}(d)|²`-type `2k`-fold additive energy, a non-trivial fibre
bound `|S_{f_0}| ≤ L^{1/2+o(1)}` requires

```
(|ℱ| + kL)·E_{2k}(b)  ≤  L^{k + o(1)}.
```

Since `E_{2k}(b) ≥ |(b^{*k})_{\text{typical}}|²·(\#\text{support}) ≥ L^{k−1}` for `b` of size `1`
(Cauchy–Schwarz: `‖b^{*k}‖_2² ≥ ‖b^{*k}‖_1²/(kL) = ‖b‖_1^{2k}/(kL) = L^{2k−1}/k` for nonnegative `b`),
the requirement forces `|ℱ| ≤ L^{o(1)}` for nonnegative `b` — and for centered `b` it forces
**square-root cancellation in the `2k`-fold energy**, which is a statement about the arithmetic of
`b`, not about orthogonality. **This is precisely (P149) / D.62.1, and, at `k = 2`, the
Hardy–Littlewood pair-correlation conjecture on average with square-root error.** `PROVED`.

### 5.3 The equivalence at `k = 1` and `k = 2`, exactly

**Proposition 5.6 (`k = 1`; `PROVED`).** With `M_2 ≤ (|ℱ| + L)‖b‖_2²` and `‖b‖_2² ≤ L`:
`|S_{f_0}| ≤ (L(|ℱ| + L))^{1/2}`. This is `< L` **never**, and it equals `L·(1 + |ℱ|/L)^{1/2}`. The
orthogonality bound is *sharp* (attained by `b = \overline{ψ_{f_0}}` when `|ℱ| ≤ L`, and by a
`δ`-function when `|ℱ| ≥ L`), so no improvement of the constant helps. A non-trivial extraction at
`k = 1` requires `M_2 ≪ |ℱ|·L^{1+o(1)}` in the regime `|ℱ| < L`; but in that regime the canonical
bound is `(|ℱ|+L)‖b‖_2² ≍ L‖b‖_2²`, i.e. the `L`-term dominates and the bound carries no `|ℱ|`-gain.
**The hypothesis has the required shape exactly where it is useless.** This is D.60.3, stated without
reference to primes: there `|ℱ| ≍ Q² = N^{2c}`, `L ≍ N`, and `|ℱ| ≥ L ⟺ c ≥ 1/2`.

**Proposition 5.7 (`k = 2`; `PROVED`).** `M_4 ≤ (|ℱ| + 2L)·E(b)` with
`E(b) := Σ_d|Σ_n b_n\overline{b_{n+d}}|²` the additive energy. A non-trivial extraction
`|S_{f_0}| ≤ L^{1/2+o(1)}` requires `(|ℱ| + 2L)E(b) ≤ L^{2+o(1)}`, i.e.

```
E(b) ≤ L^{2+o(1)}/max(|ℱ|, L).
```

For `b = Λ − M_Q` centered as in D.59 this is the assertion that the pair correlations
`𝒞_2(d) = Σ_n b_n\overline{b_{n+d}}` exhibit square-root cancellation on average over `d` — the
Hardy–Littlewood `k`-tuple statement with power-saving error. **Not an orthogonality statement**;
`OPEN` (and identified by the R149 audit as (P149)'s true identity).

### 5.4 T5 is the analytic face of the sterility lemma

**Proposition 5.8 (`PROVED`, one direction).** Phase-36 Doc 106 Prop. 3.9 (sterility lemma) says: if
the superadditivity of the index and its growth bound are computed from the same signature data,
no contradiction can be derived. The analytic instance: if the moment bound `𝔅` and the extraction
target `L` are both computed from the data `(|ℱ|, L, ‖b‖_2, ‖b‖_∞)` — the moment by orthogonality, the
target by the trivial bound — then, since the same data admit the extremal `b = \overline{ψ_{f_0}}`
realising `|S_{f_0}| = L` (Theorem 5.3), the bound cannot be `< L`. I.e. *no independent input, no
contradiction.*

*Proof.* Theorem 5.3 is exactly this statement. ∎

The converse direction (that every sterile abstract product gives a Parseval-type analytic bound) is
**not** claimed and is not true in that generality.

### 5.5 Falsifier for T5

**Falsifier F5 (`PROVED`).** `b_n ≡ 1` on `\{1,…,L\}` (equivalently the uncentered `Λ` suitably
normalised), `f_0` the trivial point (`a/q = 0`; `t = 0`; `χ = χ_0`). Every hypothesis of every
Parseval-type bound is satisfied, all inequalities in Theorem 5.4 are equalities up to constants, and
`S_{f_0} = L` exactly. The fibre at the trivial point equals the trivial bound. **No Parseval-type
bound can do better, because this object is in its domain of validity.**

This is also the reason the centering by `M_Q` in D.59 is indispensable and yet insufficient: it
removes `b ≡ 1` from the *intended* application but not from the *hypothesis class* of the large
sieve, which is stated for all `b`.

### 5.7 The weakest point of T5

**T5 is a theorem, and a trivial one; its force is entirely in Definition 5.2.** Everything turns on
"a function of the norms only, valid for all `b`". A bound that is *stated* for all `b` but whose
proof uses arithmetic of `b` (for instance a large sieve with a Selberg-sieve majorant, or a
character sum bound using Weil), is not Parseval-type, and T5 says nothing about it. The claim that
**every published moment bound in the additive/archimedean/character families is Parseval-type in this
sense** is an observation (`CERTIFIED`), not part of the theorem. The one class where I know this to
be delicate is Heath-Brown's quadratic large sieve, whose proof does use reciprocity; its *statement*
is nonetheless of the norm-only shape, so T5 applies to the statement.
