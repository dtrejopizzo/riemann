## 3. Interlude: what T4 became

Round 1's T4 said "the smooth regime forbids cancellation". That is **refuted** by the auditor's
example: `Σ_{n ≤ N} λ_f(n) ≪ N^{1/3}` for a holomorphic cusp form is an unconditional power saving at
`γ = 0`, the smooth regime at its most extreme. The regime is not the obstruction. §4.6 proves what
the obstruction is: the Dirichlet series of `Λ` and of `μ` has poles **at the zeros of `ζ`**, and for
such a sequence a power-saving partial-sum bound is *equivalent* to a zero bound — which is why no
unconditional mechanism supplies one. For a sequence whose Dirichlet series is *entire* at those
points, power saving is available and carries no zero information at all. This is T4's replacement
content and it is the only new mathematics in this round.

### 4.1 Lemma 4.2, range corrected

> **Lemma 4.2′ (`PROVED`).** For `γ ∈ R` and `N ≥ 1`,
> ```
> Σ_{N < n ≤ 2N} n^{−iγ}  =  N^{1−iγ}(2^{1−iγ} − 1)/(1 − iγ)  +  O(1 + |γ|),
> ```
> with an absolute implied constant. Moreover `1 ≤ |2^{1−iγ} − 1| ≤ 3`. Consequently, for every fixed
> `ε > 0` and `|γ| ≤ N^{1/2−ε}` and `N ≥ N_0(ε)`,
> ```
> |Σ_{N < n ≤ 2N} n^{−iγ}|  ≫_ε  N/(1 + |γ|).
> ```
>
> *Proof.* Euler–Maclaurin with `g(x) = x^{−iγ}`: `Σ_{N<n≤2N} g(n) = ∫_N^{2N}g(x)dx + O(|g(N)| +
> |g(2N)| + ∫_N^{2N}|g'(x)|dx)`. Here `|g| = 1` and `|g'(x)| = |γ|/x`, so the error is
> `O(1 + |γ|\log 2)`. The integral is `[x^{1−iγ}/(1−iγ)]_N^{2N} = N^{1−iγ}(2^{1−iγ}−1)/(1−iγ)`.
> Since `|2^{1−iγ}| = 2`, the triangle inequality gives `1 ≤ |2^{1−iγ}−1| ≤ 3`. Hence the main term
> has modulus `≍ N/(1+|γ|)`. The error `O(1+|γ|)` is smaller than a fixed multiple of `N/(1+|γ|)`
> precisely when `(1+|γ|)² ≪ N`, i.e. `|γ| ≪ N^{1/2}` with a **small** constant; taking
> `|γ| ≤ N^{1/2−ε}` makes the ratio `≪ N^{−2ε}` and the lower bound follows. ∎
>
> **The round-1 range `|γ| ≤ N^{1/2}` is wrong at its endpoint** (there main term and error budget
> are both `≍ N^{1/2}`) and is withdrawn. Everything downstream uses only `γ = O(1)`, so the repair
> costs nothing.

### 4.2 The floor for coefficient-blind schemes (retained, re-scoped)

**Definition 4.4 (unchanged).** A bound scheme is **coefficient-blind** if its hypotheses on `a`
involve only `supp a`, `‖a‖_∞`, `‖a‖_2`, `‖a‖_1`, so that `B = B(N, γ, ‖a‖_∞, ‖a‖_2, ‖a‖_1)` and the

**Theorem 4.5 (`PROVED`; a normalisation lemma, not an engine).** For a coefficient-blind scheme
valid for all `a` supported in `(N,2N]` with `‖a‖_∞ ≤ A`: `B(N,γ,A,A√N,AN) ≥ A·N`. *Proof:*
`a_n := A n^{iγ}1_{(N,2N]}` realises the extremal norms and `Σ_n a_n n^{−iγ} = A(N + O(1))`. ∎

**Scope, as the auditor demands.** The falsifier `a_n = An^{iγ}` lies in the hypothesis class only of
schemes valid for **all** `a` with given norms. It is *not* in the class of any scheme applied to the
**centered** object `Σ Λ(n)n^{−iγ}w(n/N) − N^{1−iγ}W(1−iγ)`; and centering is what every real
application does. Theorem 4.5 is therefore a **normalisation lemma** — "a scheme that ignores the
arithmetic of `a` cannot beat the trivial bound" — and it is not a constraint on anything anyone
does. Round 1 called it "the engine of T4"; that is withdrawn.

### 4.4 Theorem 4.7 and Corollary 4.7a: WITHDRAWN

Round 1's Theorem 4.7 asserted that a completely multiplicative phase factorises every Type II form,
`Σ_mΣ_k a_m b_k χ(mk) = (Σ a_mχ(m))(Σ b_kχ(k))`. The identity is correct **only for uncoupled
ranges**. In every Vaughan / Heath-Brown / Linnik decomposition the ranges are **coupled** —
`Σ_{mk ≤ N}`, or `m ≍ M`, `k ≍ K`, `MK ≍ N` with `mk` confined to a dyadic block — and under the
coupling the form does not factorise; exploiting the coupling (Cauchy–Schwarz in `m`, then
correlation sums in `k`) is the entire method. Decoupling requires Perron, i.e. a `t`-integral of
length `≥ N`, which is C4 in its **useful** regime, not a wall. Step (3) of the round-1 proof
("evaluating a product of Dirichlet series at one `s` is the product of the evaluations") is an
identity for absolutely convergent whole Dirichlet series in `σ > 1`; applied at `s = 1+iγ` it is the
true and empty statement `−ζ'/ζ(1+iγ) = \prod(\cdots)`, which says nothing about the truncated sum
over `N < n ≤ 2N`.

**Theorem 4.7 and Corollary 4.7a are withdrawn.** Corollary 4.7a is additionally refuted in the
direction that matters: **Friedlander–Iwaniec** (primes `x²+y⁴`) and **Heath-Brown** (`x³+2y³`)
obtain power-saving bilinear cancellation whose input is the arithmetic of a quadratic/cubic ring, at
no oscillatory frequency at all. Arithmetic-not-metric bilinear cancellation exists. This answers
O-150.4 in the negative sense round 1 did not expect, and it is recorded as such.

### 4.6 The real obstruction: polar-at-zeros versus entire-at-zeros

This subsection replaces T4's withdrawn rhetorical content with a proof. It is the auditor's key
point, made precise and proved.

**Definition 4.11.** Let `a = (a_n)_{n≥1}` be complex with `a_n ≪_ε n^{ε}`, `D_a(s) := Σ_n a_n n^{−s}`
(absolutely convergent in `Re s > 1`), `A(x) := Σ_{n ≤ x} a_n`, and let `m(s)` be a fixed meromorphic
"main term" (for us: a finite sum of poles in `Re s ≥ 1`). Fix `θ ∈ [½, 1)`. Say that `a` is:

* **polar-at-zeros** (for `ζ`) if `D_a` continues meromorphically to `Re s > 0` and, for every
  non-trivial zero `ρ` of `ζ`, `D_a` has a pole at `s = ρ`. *Examples:* `a_n = Λ(n)`, `D_a = −ζ'/ζ`,
  pole at each `ρ` of residue `−m_ρ`; `a_n = μ(n)`, `D_a = 1/ζ`, pole at each `ρ` of order `m_ρ`.
* **entire-at-zeros** if `D_a` continues to an entire function of finite order on `Re s > θ`
  (holomorphic there, no poles). *Example:* `a_n = λ_f(n)` for a holomorphic Hecke cusp form,
  `D_a = L(f, s + \tfrac{k-1}{2})` entire.

> **Theorem 4.12 (`PROVED`; polar class: a partial-sum bound *is* a zero bound).** Let `a` be
> polar-at-zeros with `D_a = −ζ'/ζ` (the case `a = Λ`, `A(x) = ψ(x)`, `m(s) = 1/(s−1)`), and let
> `θ ∈ [½,1)`. Then
> ```
> ψ(x) − x  ≪_ε  x^{θ+ε}   for all x ≥ 2 and all ε > 0     ⟺     β ≤ θ for every zero β + iγ of ζ.
> ```
> *Proof.* (⇐) Perron with the explicit formula: `ψ(x) − x = −Σ_ρ x^{ρ}/ρ − \log 2π − ½\log(1−x^{−2})`
> in the standard truncated form; if `β ≤ θ` for all `ρ`, the sum is `≪ x^{θ}\log^2 x`. (⇒) Landau's
> theorem on the abscissa, in the Mellin form: for `Re s > 1`,
> ```
> ∫_1^∞ (ψ(x) − x) x^{−s−1} dx  =  −\frac{1}{s}\frac{ζ'}{ζ}(s)  −  \frac{1}{s−1}.
> ```
> If `ψ(x) − x ≪_ε x^{θ+ε}`, the integral converges absolutely and defines a holomorphic function in
> `Re s > θ`. By uniqueness of analytic continuation, `−(1/s)(ζ'/ζ)(s) − 1/(s−1)` is holomorphic in
> `Re s > θ`. But at a zero `ρ` with `Re ρ = β > θ` the left member of that expression has a pole of
> residue `−m_ρ/ρ ≠ 0`, and `1/(s−1)` is regular there. Contradiction. Hence `β ≤ θ`. ∎
>
> **Corollary 4.12a.** Writing `β_max := \sup\{Re ρ\}`, the statement "`ψ(x) − x ≪_ε x^{θ+ε}` for all
> `x`" is **equivalent** to `β_max ≤ θ`. The same argument with `D_a = 1/ζ` gives
> `M(x) = Σ_{n≤x}μ(n) ≪_ε x^{θ+ε} ⟺ β_max ≤ θ`. In particular a power-saving bound for the partial
> sums of a polar-at-zeros sequence is *exactly* as hard as the corresponding zero bound: it is not
> merely sufficient, it is equivalent.

> **Theorem 4.13 (`PROVED`; entire class: a partial-sum bound carries no zero information).** Let `a`
> be entire-at-zeros on `Re s > θ` with `A(x) ≪_ε x^{θ+ε}`. Let `S ⊂ \{s : Re s > θ\}` be any finite
> set of points. Then there is a sequence `b` with
> ```
> B(x) := Σ_{n ≤ x} b_n ≪_{ε,S} x^{θ+ε},        D_b(s) = D_a(s)·P(s),
> ```
> where `P` is a Dirichlet polynomial whose zero set contains `S`. Consequently the hypothesis
> "`A(x) ≪ x^{θ+ε}`" is compatible with **any** prescribed zero configuration of `D_a` in
> `Re s > θ`, and therefore implies no statement whatever about the location of zeros.
>
> *Proof.* For `s_0 ∈ C` put `P_{s_0}(s) := 1 − 2^{s_0}·2^{−s}`, a Dirichlet polynomial with
> coefficients `c_1 = 1`, `c_2 = −2^{s_0}`, whose zeros are exactly `s = s_0 + 2πik/\log 2`, `k ∈ Z`;
> in particular `P_{s_0}(s_0) = 0`. Let `P := \prod_{s_0 ∈ S}P_{s_0} = Σ_{d ≤ D}c_d d^{−s}`, a finite
> Dirichlet polynomial (supported on `d = 2^j`, `j ≤ |S|`). Set `b := a * c` (Dirichlet convolution),
> so `D_b = D_a·P` in `Re s > 1` and by continuation wherever both sides are defined. Then
> ```
> B(x) = Σ_{d ≤ D} c_d A(x/d) ≪_ε (Σ_{d ≤ D}|c_d| d^{−θ−ε})·x^{θ+ε} ≪_{ε,S} x^{θ+ε},
> ```
> the same power saving, with a constant depending only on `S` and `ε`. The zeros of `D_b` are those
> of `D_a` together with those of `P`, which contain `S`. Since `S` was arbitrary, no constraint on
> the zero set follows from the bound. ∎
>
> **Corollary 4.13a (the auditor's example, explained).** `Σ_{n ≤ N}λ_f(n) ≪ N^{1/3}` (Hecke, via the
> functional equation and Voronoi summation) is an unconditional power saving at `γ = 0` — the smooth
> regime at its most extreme. By Theorem 4.13 it implies nothing about the zeros of `L(f,s)`. There
> is no contradiction with Theorem 4.12 and none with T4: **`λ_f` is entire-at-zeros, `Λ` and `μ` are
> polar-at-zeros.**

**Consequence for T4, stated plainly.** The obstruction in the smooth regime is **not** the regime.
It is that the sequence RH is about is polar-at-zeros, so by Theorem 4.12 any power-saving bound for
its partial sums is *equivalent to* the zero bound — the method must therefore contain the full
strength of the conclusion, and cannot be a size-only or phase-only estimate. Smooth-regime
cancellation as such is routine (Theorem 4.13's class). Round 1's sentence "the smooth regime forbids
cancellation" is **withdrawn** and replaced by the two theorems above.

## 5. T5, repaired

### 5.1 Scope, stated exactly (repair 9)

**T5 constrains one operation: the extraction of a single fibre from a moment bound,
`|S_{f_0}|^{2k} ≤ M_{2k}`.** It is a true and useful negative statement about the D.59 construction
in this programme. It is **not** a theorem about moment methods, and the audit's §3.1 is accepted:
the standard, productive use of the same Parseval input is **large-value counting**, which T5 does
not touch:

1. **Halász–Montgomery.** From `M_2 ≤ (|ℱ|+L)‖b‖_2²` one deduces
   `#\{f : |S_f| ≥ V\} ≤ (|ℱ|+L)‖b‖_2²/V²`; combined with a second moment over a well-spaced
   subfamily this gives the classical large-value theorems, which feed zero-density estimates
   `N(σ,T) ≪ T^{3(1−σ)/(2−σ)}\log^C T` (Ingham) and their modern successors (Huxley; Guth–Maynard).
   **This is non-trivial arithmetic output from a Parseval-type bound.** Counting is not extraction,
   and T5 says nothing about it.
2. **Montgomery–Vaughan hybrid large sieve.** Same shape: the output bounds the number of large
   values in a `(q,t)`-grid, and produces Linnik-type and density results.
3. **Averaged statements** (Bombieri–Vinogradov, Barban–Davenport–Halberstam) where the target *is*
   the average and no fibre is isolated.

Definitions 5.1, 5.2 (family moment method; Parseval-type = the bound is a function of
`(|ℱ|, L, k, ‖b‖_2, ‖b‖_∞, ‖b‖_1)` alone and is asserted for **every** `b` with those norms) stand
unchanged; they correctly capture the large sieve, Montgomery–Vaughan, character orthogonality and
Heath-Brown's quadratic large sieve *as statements*.

> **Proposition 5.5′ (`PROVED`).** Let `b ≥ 0` be supported in `\{1,…,L\}` with `‖b‖_∞ ≤ 1` and

## 9. What may go to Paper 47 §18 (revised; ≤ 1 page)

The audit's verdict on paper use is accepted. **T3 must not appear until O-150.1 closes.** Theorems
4.7, 4.8 and Cor. 5.5 must not appear in their round-1 form; 4.7 and 5.5 are withdrawn outright.
What is defensible now, as §18.3 "Two method observations and a classification":

> **§18.3.1 (where the content of RH sits).** For `γ` fixed and `|γ| ≤ N^{1/2−ε}`,
> `Σ_{N<n≤2N}n^{−iγ} = N^{1−iγ}(2^{1−iγ}−1)/(1−iγ) + O(1+|γ|)` has modulus `≫ N/(1+|γ|)`: the
> twisted prime sum does not cancel, and by the explicit formula RH is a statement about a secondary
> term of relative size `N^{−1/2}`. The non-cancellation is the pole of `ζ` at `s = 1`, not a feature
> of the regime — smooth-regime cancellation is routine elsewhere: `Σ_{n ≤ N}λ_f(n) ≪ N^{1/3}` for a
> holomorphic cusp form, unconditionally, by the functional equation and Voronoi summation.
>
> **§18.3.2 (polar-at-zeros versus entire-at-zeros).** Call a coefficient sequence `a` with
> `a_n ≪ n^{ε}` *polar-at-zeros* if `Σ a_n n^{−s}` has a pole at every non-trivial zero of `ζ`
> (so `Λ` and `μ` are, with `−ζ'/ζ` and `1/ζ`), and *entire-at-zeros* if it continues holomorphically
> to `Re s > θ` (so `λ_f` is). Then: **(i)** for `a = Λ`, `ψ(x) − x ≪_ε x^{θ+ε}` for all `x` is
> *equivalent* to `β ≤ θ` for every zero — one direction by the explicit formula, the other by
> Landau's theorem applied to `∫_1^∞(ψ(x)−x)x^{−s−1}dx = −(1/s)(ζ'/ζ)(s) − 1/(s−1)`; the same holds
> for `μ` and `1/ζ`. **(ii)** for an entire-at-zeros `a` with `A(x) ≪ x^{θ+ε}`, and any finite
> `S ⊂ \{Re s > θ\}`, the convolution of `a` with the Dirichlet polynomial
> `\prod_{s_0∈S}(1 − 2^{s_0}2^{−s})` has the same power saving and zeros at every point of `S`;
> so the bound implies nothing about zero location. **Consequence:** a power-saving partial-sum bound
> is informative about zeros exactly for polar-at-zeros sequences, and there it is equivalent to the
> zero bound and therefore as hard. This is the precise sense in which size-only and phase-only
> methods do not reach the sign.
>
> **§18.3.3 (no single-fibre extraction from orthogonality).** Let `ℱ` be a family of unimodular
> characters on `\{1,…,L\}` and `M_{2k} ≤ 𝔅(|ℱ|, L, k, ‖b‖_2, ‖b‖_∞)` a moment bound valid for all
> `b` with those norms — the shape produced by `L²`-orthogonality (large sieve, Montgomery–Vaughan,
> character orthogonality, Heath-Brown's quadratic large sieve). Then `𝔅^{1/2k} ≥ L`: the extraction
> `|S_{f_0}|^{2k} ≤ M_{2k}` gives at best the trivial bound, uniformly in `k` and in the family type;
> take `b_n = \overline{ψ_{f_0}(n)}`. The only improvable step is the evaluation of `‖b^{*k}‖_2²`,
> and for nonnegative `b` the required inequality is unsatisfiable at every `k`, so only centered `b`
> can help, via square-root cancellation in the `2k`-fold energy — a statement about the arithmetic
> of `b`, not about orthogonality. **Scope:** this constrains single-fibre extraction only. The
> productive use of the same input is large-value counting (Halász–Montgomery; the hybrid large
> sieve), which yields zero-density estimates and is untouched by the above.
>
