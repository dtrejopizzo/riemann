## 2. Statement and conventions

Let u_i>0 and F_i>0, i=1,...,n. No monotonicity, concavity, or differentiable underlying function is needed. Set

    K_ij=(F_i+F_j)/(u_i+u_j),
    R_i=u_i/F_i, S_i=u_iF_i=u_i²/R_i,
    (A_R)_ij=(R_j−R_i)/(u_i+u_j),
    (A_S)_ij=(S_j−S_i)/(u_i+u_j).

The diagonals of A_R,A_S are zero. Use the convention Pf([[0,a],[-a,0]])=a and Pf of the empty matrix equal to1. For an antisymmetric matrix A of size n, define

    P_n(A)=Pf(A),                         n even,
    P_n(A)=Pf([[A,e],[-e^t,0]]),           n odd,

where e is the vector of n ones and the border is placed at the END. Then, for ALL n>=1,

    det K=P_n(A_R)P_n(A_S)/(product_i R_i).       (ALL-ORDERS)

The product and its sign do not depend on the chosen order of the nodes: permuting them changes both Pfaffians by the same sign. Coincident nodes do not harm the algebraic identity either. To interpret an application with F as a function, naturally its coincident values must coincide.

## 3. Two displacement identities

Write D=diag(u_i), E=diag(R_i), r=(R_i), u=De, A=A_R and T=EKE. Entry by entry one verifies

    T=DA+r e^t,
    E A_S E=DAD+r u^t−u r^t.                     (DISPLACEMENT)

In the first, (DA+r e^t)_ij=u_i(R_j−R_i)/(u_i+u_j)+R_i=(u_iR_j+u_jR_i)/(u_i+u_j). In the second, multiplying by u_i+u_j reduces the equality to

    R_i u_j²−R_j u_i²
      =u_i u_j(R_j−R_i)+(R_i u_j−u_iR_j)(u_i+u_j).

Both correctly include the diagonals. Moreover det T=(product R_i)² det K.

## 4. Elementary Pfaffian lemmas

We use Pf(A)²=det A, the congruence Pf(QAQ^t)=det(Q)Pf(A) and expansion along a row. These classical properties also follow directly from the definition by matchings.

### 4.1 Rank-two update

If V is even antisymmetric and invertible, and a,b are vectors, then

    Pf(V+a b^t−b a^t)=Pf(V)[1+b^t V^(-1)a].        (UPDATE)

A brief justification that fixes the sign: the Pfaffian of V+t(ab^t−ba^t) is affine in t. In the multilinear expansion, two copies of the decomposable form a∧b cancel. Its value at0 is Pf(V). Differentiating the square of the Pfaffian at0, using the determinant, gives for its linear coefficient

    Pf(V)·tr[V^(-1)(ab^t−ba^t)]/2
       =Pf(V)b^t V^(-1)a.

This proves UPDATE, not only its square.

### 4.2 Odd cofactors

For A antisymmetric of odd size n, define

    v_i=(−1)^(i+1)Pf(A with row and column i deleted).

The Laplace expansion proves

    A v=0,
    Pf([[A,b],[-b^t,0]])=b^t v,
    adj(A)=v v^t.                                 (ODD-COFACTORS)

For the last equality, first consider rank n−1. The adjugate is symmetric, its columns belong to the one-dimensional kernel generated by v, and its diagonals are the squares v_i² by the even identity. This fixes adj(A)=vv^t. If the rank is smaller, all minors of size n−1 and all v_i vanish. Thus the equality also holds on that boundary, including n=1 with empty Pf equal to1.

### 4.3 Border that eliminates an update

For any odd antisymmetric V and vectors r,u,

    Pf([[V+r u^t−u r^t,r],[-r^t,0]])
       =Pf([[V,r],[-r^t,0]]).                      (BORDER)

Indeed the matrix on the left is Q[[V,r],[-r^t,0]]Q^t with Q=[[I,u],[0,1]], of determinant1. This congruence fixes the sign of the border and requires no inverse.

## 5. Proof in even order

Suppose first A invertible. The first DISPLACEMENT identity and the rank-one determinant lemma give

    det T=det D·det A·[1+e^t A^(-1)D^(-1)r].

Set V=DAD. UPDATE, with a=r,b=u, and the second DISPLACEMENT identity give

    Pf(E A_S E)
       =Pf(DAD)[1+u^t(DAD)^(-1)r]
       =det D·Pf(A)[1+e^t A^(-1)D^(-1)r].

Since det A=Pf(A)², it follows that

    det T=Pf(A)Pf(E A_S E)
         =(product R_i)Pf(A_R)Pf(A_S).

Dividing by (product R_i)² proves ALL-ORDERS in even size.

Data with singular A are not excluded. Multiplying by the nonzero denominators turns the identity into a polynomial equality; it holds on the generic set where A is invertible and by continuity on the whole domain. For distinct positive nodes, that set is dense: the choice R_i=u_i gives the Schur matrix, whose Pfaffian is product_(i<j)(u_j−u_i)/(u_i+u_j), nonzero (classical Schur formula, Okada cited in§1, Proposition2.2, equation(2.7)). Afterwards, coincident nodes are obtained by continuity. It is an algebraic extension step, not an additional nondegeneracy hypothesis.

## 6. Proof in odd order

Now det A=0. The rank-one determinant lemma, valid via the adjugate even if DA is singular, and ODD-COFACTORS give

    det T=e^t adj(DA)r
         =det D·e^t adj(A)D^(-1)r
         =det D·(e^t v)(v^t D^(-1)r).              (ODD-DET)

The first scalar factor is P_n(A_R). Apply BORDER to V=DAD and border the second DISPLACEMENT identity with r. One obtains

    Pf([[E A_S E,r],[-r^t,0]])
       =Pf([[DAD,r],[-r^t,0]]).

The congruence diag(E,1) on the left-hand side and diag(D,1) on the right gives

    (product R_i)P_n(A_S)
       =det D·Pf([[A,D^(-1)r],[-r^tD^(-1),0]])
       =det D·v^tD^(-1)r.

Substituting into ODD-DET produces

    det T=(product R_i)P_n(A_R)P_n(A_S).

Dividing by (product R_i)² proves odd ALL-ORDERS, including the singular cases and n=1. No additional u_i factors are introduced in the final formula.

## Appendix — Classical Rolle hierarchy and Taylor determinants

Status of the appendix: **identities and abstract sufficient criterion PROVED; discharge for real R,S CONDITIONAL/OPEN; audit of the appendix passed.** A classical identity is not presented as a new sign inequality.

### A. Pfaffians as interpolation determinants

For 2k distinct ordered positive nodes, x_i=u_i² and f_i=f(x_i), the classical specialization of [Ishikawa–Okada–Tagawa–Zeng, Theorem1.1(b)](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0411280) is

    Pf((f_j−f_i)/(u_i+u_j))
      =sigma_k det[1,x,...,x^(k−1),f,xf,...,x^(k−1)f]
                  /product_(i<j)(u_i+u_j),
    sigma_k=(−1)^(k(k−1)/2).                         (INTERPOLATION-EVEN)

One takes p=q=r=s=0 in the theorem, variables x_i=u_i², a_i=f_i and b_i=u_i. The second generalized determinant has columns of even powers of u followed by the odd ones; reordering them produces sigma_k and the usual Vandermonde. This cancels the corresponding part of the Vandermonde in u². The formula is not new.

For 2k−1 nodes, the border of ones of083 satisfies

    P_(2k−1)(A_f)
      =sigma_k det[1,x,...,x^(k−1),f,xf,...,x^(k−2)f]
                  /product_(i<j)(u_i+u_j).          (INTERPOLATION-ODD)

At k=1 the block with f is empty. The odd formula follows from the even one by adding a last node u_2k→infinity and freely assigning f_2k=u_2k. The entries of its last antisymmetric column tend to1. In the determinant the last column f x^(k−1) dominates, of degree2k−1 in u_2k, exactly the degree of the new factors of the denominator. Its cofactor has positive sign because it occupies the last row and column.

### B. A sufficient hierarchy, with all hypotheses explicit

Fix k and an interval of squares I. Suppose f∈C^(2k−1)(I). Define

    H_j(x)=(d/dx)^k[x^j f(x)], j=0,...,k−1,
    D_(k,j)(x)=W(H_0,...,H_(j−1))(x), j=1,...,k.

Here W is the Wronskian with rows of derivatives of order0,...,j−1. If ALL the D_(k,j) are nonzero on I, the system H_0,...,H_(k−1) is an extended Chebyshev system. The justification is weighted Rolle: dividing by H_0, which does not vanish, and differentiating eliminates the first function. The remaining Wronskians are the previous ones divided by nonzero powers of H_0. Repeating the procedure reduces the zero bound by one at each stage. Thus a nontrivial combination of j initial functions has at most j−1 zeros counted with multiplicities.

If P_(k−1)+fQ_(k−1) had2k distinct zeros, differentiating k times would give a combination of the k functions H_j with at least k zeros, a contradiction. If Q were zero from the start, the polynomial could not have that many zeros. Hence the determinant of INTERPOLATION-EVEN does not vanish.

For INTERPOLATION-ODD it suffices that D_(k,j)≠0 for j=1,...,k−1: a combination P_(k−1)+fQ_(k−2) with2k−1 zeros would give at least k−1 zeros after differentiating k times, against the reduced system of dimension k−1. At k=1 the odd claim is directly P_1=1.

The sign is also fixed, not only the absence of zeros. The Wronskian of the original system has a triangular polynomial block, and its determinant is

    [product_(m=0)^(k−1)m!] D_(k,k),       even case,
    [product_(m=0)^(k−1)m!] D_(k,k−1),     odd case k>=2.

The ordered chamber of nodes is connected and the confluent limit divides by a positive Vandermonde and positive factorials. Hence the sign of the evaluation determinant is that of the final D indicated. In particular,

    sign D_(k,k)=sigma_k and D_(k,j)≠0 ALL j<=k
       ⇒ Pf(A_f)>0 on ALL sets of2k nodes;

    sign D_(k,k−1)=sigma_k and D_(k,j)≠0 ALL j<k
       ⇒ P_(2k−1)(A_f)>0 on ALL sets of2k−1 nodes.

It is a global sufficient criterion on the interval: checking it at one point or for a single k does not prove the rest.

### C. The Wronskians are Hankel matrices of normalized Taylor coefficients

Translating the basis x^c to (t−x)^c when evaluating at x is a triangular transformation of determinant1. From Leibniz one obtains exactly

    D_(k,j)=det_((l,c)=0,...,j−1)
                [(k+l)!/(k+l−c)! · f^(k+l−c)].

Set a_m(x)=f^(m)(x)/m!, m>=1. Factoring (k+l)! out of each row and reversing the columns gives

    D_(k,j)=(−1)^(j(j−1)/2)
        ·[product_(l=0)^(j−1)(k+l)!]
        ·det_((l,c)=0,...,j−1)[a_(k−j+1+l+c)(x)].    (TAYLOR-HANKEL)

For example,

    D_(2,1)=f'',
    D_(2,2)=3f''²−2f'f'''=−T_f,
    D_(3,2)=4f'''²−3f''f^(4).

This recovers the concrete criterion of084 in size four. It does not extrapolate its inequality T_f>0 to the next row of the hierarchy.

For the full hierarchy suppose f smooth of all orders. A more structured sufficient condition would be that, for each x, the numbers

    mu_m(x)=(−1)^m a_(m+1)(x), m>=0,

were moments of a positive measure on [0,infinity), with all their shifted moment determinants strictly positive. In that case TAYLOR-HANKEL would give

    sign D_(k,j)=(−1)^[jk−j(j+1)/2].

For j=k and j=k−1 this sign is sigma_k, so both parities would be covered. Only the algebraic sufficiency is declared; it is not claimed that these sequences of the theta quotient are positive moments.

Additional classical antecedent: the matrices of normalized derivatives starting at a_1 appear in the Dobsch–Donoghue criterion. See [Heinävaara, Local characterizations for the matrix monotonicity and convexity of fixed order](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07516). The abstract Hankel hierarchy is not presented as a new positivity principle.

### D. Specific obligation for the real Phi

The real coordinates are quotients, not transforms of a single positive density:

    R(x)=u/M(u)=Z(u)/Z_J(u),
    S(x)=uM(u)=u² Z_J(u)/Z(u), x=u²,
    Z_J(u)=Z'(u)/u.

The positive densities J and K of076–078 contribute exact structure to the denominator and to certain defects. Their positivity does NOT prove that the normalized coefficients of R or S are a moment sequence, nor the Hankel minors of TAYLOR-HANKEL. The missing step is a signed representation or a recurrence preserving those minors for BOTH quotients and unbounded k.

No such recurrence is available here for the real Phi. This hierarchy makes explicit the datum an induction would require, but does not replace it; the global sign and the higher Pfaffians remain OPEN.
