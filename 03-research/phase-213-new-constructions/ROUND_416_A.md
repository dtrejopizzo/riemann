# The second dyadic difference already changes sign if there is an exterior zero

Let E be the dilation EF(s)=F(4s), with F(s)=D(√s)/√s. The first contrast G=(I−E)F is B(√s)/(2√s) by 414. For any s₀>0, if there is an exterior zero, the sequence ((I−E)²F(4^k s₀)) with integer k takes both signs. The claim concerns the fixed dilation, different from the ordinary differentiation of 415.

Indeed, G is strictly positive by 414. It tends to zero at both ends: F has a finite limit at zero and limit zero at infinity by the integral representation of 415 and the summable majorant 2Σd²/(γ²−1/4). Hence the values of G on the orbit go up at some step and go down at another. More precisely, at the small end (I−E)²F(s)=9s F'(0)+O(s²), with F'(0)<0: the differentiated integrand is −Re[(γ+iu)⁻⁴], strictly negative for 0≤u≤1/2, γ≥2, since 4 arctan(1/4)<π/2. The two derivatives are dominated by convergent series even at s=0.

Hence it would suffice to prove G(4s)≤G(s) only on an orbit approaching zero to exclude every displacement (a subsequence tending to zero of points satisfying the inequality suffices). The expansion shows the necessary precision: one must determine the sign of the term linear in s. In the coordinates of 412 its magnitude is a positive moment of the displacements. This observation rules out building a dyadic tower with signs inherited from 414; it does not yet provide an arithmetic estimate of that moment.

**Exact identification with 410.** Set h(w)=log(X(w)/P(w)). By 412, F(s)=∫₋₁¹[h(√s y)−c]dy. The function h is even and analytic near zero, hence F'(0)=h''(0)/3. By the normalization X(0)=P(0)=1 and parity, h''(0)=V₀−Σ2/γ². Hence the coefficient 9F'(0) is exactly −3 times the deficit of 410. In particular, trying to estimate this coefficient directly reproduces the same arithmetic obligation, with identical weight and multiplicity. More generally, [sⁿ]F=2[w²ⁿ]h/(2n+1) for n≥1. No finite linear combination of dilations introduces additional local data: for Σ bⱼF(cⱼs) the coefficient of order n only acquires the multiplier Σ bⱼ cⱼⁿ.
