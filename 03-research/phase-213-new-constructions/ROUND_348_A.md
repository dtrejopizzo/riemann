# Disjoint pairing of mass comparable to the whole window

For each large X there exists a set of disjoint pairs of odd squarefree integers, independent of the center x, with the following property. On X≤x≤2X, h=X^(−δ), H=hX and fixed 0<δ≤1/16, its mass in absolute value in the full observable C_h is ≫H. Its joint balance costs O(X^(7/16)log X)=o(√H) at each center. Since the total weight in modulus is O(H), a positive fraction is covered, with non-optimized constant.

**Reserving primes.** Choose fixed κ>0 small enough, less than 1/100. Take Z powers of 16 in [X^(1/4−κ), X^(1/4)]. There are ≍_κ log X scales. To each one assign the primes of [Z/3,3Z]; these intervals are disjoint. Let T be the union of all those primes. By Mertens their sum of reciprocals is at most log((1/4)/(1/4−κ))+o(1), using the outer interval with the constant factors 1/3 and 3. This bound can be made smaller than any prescribed positive constant.

**Many pairs per scale.** Put Y=X/Z, J=⌊Y^(1/4)⌋. In [Y,2Y] the odd squarefree integers occupy c₂Y+o(Y), c₂>0. There is a partition into intervals of length J (except the ends) in which the sum of the absolute imbalances between both signs of odd μ is o(Y). Indeed, the integral mean of the imbalance over the initial points is o(J) by 329–333: the maximum over progressions includes the odd integers and the exceptional set has proportion o(1). Averaging the partitions translated by a real shift uniform on [0,J] gives the result, with O(J) endpoints. The estimates are uniform over the indicated scales as the minimum Y tends to infinity. Erasing from the base factors all multiples of primes of T costs ≤2YΣ_{p∈T}1/p points. Before erasing one could form (c₂Y−o(Y))/2 pairs of opposite signs in the cells; each erased point destroys at most one pair. Choose κ so as to keep ≫Y disjoint pairs (a,b), each with |b−a|≤J. [Maximal estimate for Möbius](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05770v1)

**Disjointness across all scales.** For each pair and each prime t assigned to its scale select (at,bt). Each product has exactly one prime factor belonging to T, t itself, with multiplicity one. Recovering t determines the scale and the base factor determines the pair. There are no double uses. Both coefficients in C_h are those of μ on odd integers and have opposite signs.

**Mass and error.** For each a and x there is an interval of t of length ≍hZ, inside [Z/3,3Z], where η(log(x/(at))/h) exceeds a positive constant. The location is between (1/2−o(1))Z and 2Z. For κ<1/100 one has hZ≫Z^(0.73) in the indicated range of δ. The theorem on primes in short intervals gives ≫hZ/log X primes uniformly in each interval. Hence each scale contributes ≫H/log X, and all together ≫H. [Guth and Maynard, primes in short intervals](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20552v1)

On the other hand, for each pair of bases only O(1+hZ) integers t activate one of the pulses: the relative displacement between a and b is O(J/Y)≪h. Each difference of weights costs O_η(J/(Yh)). With O(Y) pairs, the cost per scale is O(ZJ)=O(X^(1/4)Z^(3/4))=O(X^(7/16)). Summing the scales keeps the cost below √H even for δ=1/16. No cancellation between pairs is needed for this estimate.

**Scope.** 347 controlled a family with decreasing density. Here the pairs cover a positive proportion at each center and their errors are already summed. The proof gives neither coverage close to one nor control of the complement, which includes all odd products without a reserved prime and the other coefficients of C_h. It has not been proved that iterating the construction preserves the distribution hypotheses on the residue.
