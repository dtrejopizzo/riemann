# Majorization only at prime samples with x-dependent splittings

Let η be fixed in (0,1/2]. For the mixtures of 100 with η≤y≤1−η there exist B>1,c>0 such that, for all x large enough, even choosing a different mixture for each x, if h(x/p)≥log(x/p) at the primes x/B≤p≤x, then

    Σ_{x/B≤p≤x} log p [h(x/p)−log(x/p)] ≥ c x.

**Proof.** Take R,ε from 100, 1<d<min(2,1/(1−η)), B=dR. Write λ_x=x⁻¹Σ log p δ_(x/p) restricted to [1,B]. By PNT its discrepancy over all intervals with respect to dt/t² is o(1) uniformly (θ(z)=z+o(x) uniformly in [x/B,x], and the endpoints cost O(log x/x)). If the conclusion failed there would be a sequence with normalized excess tending to zero and ∫h dλ_x bounded. For each u≤R, the kernel equals one on [u,du] for all allowed splittings. By discrepancy λ_x([u,du])≥(1−1/d)/(2R) uniformly for large x. By Tonelli the mass of parents with u≤R is uniformly bounded. Each kernel has O(R) jumps in [1,R], so h and |h−log| have uniformly bounded variation and sup norm there. By integration by parts, ∫_[1,R]|h−log| dλ_x differs from the weighted continuous integral by o(1). The former tends to zero by majorization at the samples and by the assumed total excess. The latter is ≥ε by 100. Contradiction.

**Why the size of the parts matters.** If arbitrarily extreme splittings are allowed, for each fixed x one can interpolate EXACTLY any list of prescribed non-negative values at the points x/p, p≤x. For the sample p place a parent at u=x/p. When evaluating at x/q, the argument is p/q. At q=p the carry equals one for all 0<y<1. At each q≠p, p/q is not an integer: for positive y small enough one has floor(yp/q)=0 and floor((1−y)p/q)=floor(p/q). The list is finite, so the same small y can be chosen for all pairs. Give each parent the prescribed weight. This also explains why a continuous obstruction alone does not decide the discrete question. The interpolant uses the primes themselves and does not make their sum cheaper; it is not a certificate of the sign.
