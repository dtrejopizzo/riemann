# Arbitrarily distant crossings and representation with primes x^o(1)

**Status: PROVED, with independent review of inclusion-exclusion, the differential equation and the diagonal choice of primes.**

Let W(y) be the signed union of 386. For each fixed non-integer real u>1, setting b=L/u one has

    W(exp b)=−JxE/b² · ω'(u) + o_u(xE/L²),

where ω is the Buchstab function. The error is additive even at zeros of the derivative. For every ε>0 and every sufficiently large x there is an exact representation of the pulse S with prime factors ≤x^ε and a single weighted terminal factor as in 391. The terminal prime can be chosen as p(x)=x^o(1). This existential conclusion does not specify a rate of decrease of the exponent.

**General profile.** Let k=floor u. By the Gaussian tail, the terms with k+1 factors >exp b cost O_u(x exp(−c_u L²)) even with marks of any order ≤k. Apply inclusion-exclusion for the indicator of at least one factor. The binomial mark of order j extracts j primes with sign (−1)^j and factor 1/j! over the orderings. Multiplying by (−1)^(j+1) leaves the minus sign at all orders. Collisions of marked primes with each other or with the cofactor cost O_u(x L^(k+2) exp(−b)) by the sum over multiples of p² for n≤x²; for n>x² the Gaussian is used with multiplicity polynomial in log n. The j PNT substitutions of 391 cost O_u(x L^(k+2)exp(−c_u√L)), by integration by parts with |f|+|f'|≪exp v and reciprocal mass O(L) of each other factor. Thus, up to o_u(x/L²), the union is

    −x ∫ exp(−v) f(v) H(L−v) dv,
    H(D)=Σ_{j=1}^k (1/j!) (1_[b,∞)(w)/w)^{*j}(D),

using the frozen-sieve cofactor of 384. In a neighborhood of L of radius c_u L the density is H(D)=ω(D/b)/b. Indeed, in the normalized variable t=D/b the sum of all convolutions is locally finite and coincides there with the j≤k. If the normalized sum is called ω, the symmetry of the summands of t gives tω(t)=1+∫₁^(t−1)ω(s)ds for t≥2 and tω(t)=1 for 1≤t≤2. This is the Buchstab equation. By steps it is smooth outside the integers; in the stated neighborhood |H''|≪_u L^(−3). Globally the truncated sum is O_u(1/b) by the simplex bound of 381. Taylor and the moments zero and −JE of 384 give the profile with the stated sign.

**The derivative takes both signs in every tail.** If ω' were eventually ≥0, ω would be non-decreasing and the equation uω'(u)=ω(u−1)−ω(u) would give ω'≤0 one unit later. The case ≤0 is symmetric. A constant tail propagates backwards by the same equation down to (1,2), contradicting ω(u)=1/u. Hence for every M>2 one can choose non-integer u₁,u₂>M with derivatives of opposite sign; the continuity of the derivative on (2,∞) is used. Only the two profiles at fixed parameters are needed; no uniformity between them is claimed.

**Arithmetic consequence.** For M>1/ε the two values of W at those endpoints have opposite signs for all sufficiently large x. Varying the threshold between them there are two contiguous states enclosing zero or a state where zero is attained. As in 391, interpolating the retained masses is equivalent to weighting a single terminal factor 1−λp^(−s), 0≤λ≤1, in the finite product of the primes between z and p. The result equals S exactly. The prime is below x^ε (in case of a plateau the cut in the band, or its last prime, suffices). To choose p=x^o(1), apply this successively to ε=1/m with increasing validity thresholds tending to infinity. It is also possible to choose the smallest prime admitting such an interpolation, since the set of candidates is finite below the first cut obtained.

**Review record.** At each order j, the sign of the extraction is(−1)^j and that of inclusion-exclusion is(−1)^(j+1): their product is always−1. The sum H therefore carries all positive convolutions, not an additional alternation. For the terms with more than k primes, the error of truncating the binomial identity is bounded by a fixed power of log n and is still absorbed by the tail n≥y^(k+1)>x^(1+c_u). Multiplying the normalized sum of convolutions by t gives exactly1+∫₁^(t−1)ω(s)ds; differentiation gives tω'=ω(t−1)−ω(t) for t>2. Eventual monotonicity forces a constant tail and the equation propagates it towards(1,2), contradicting1/t. This proves both signs in every tail without needing an asymptotic formula for its amplitude.

The arithmetic selection uses only two fixed non-integer parameters for each ε: their errors may depend on those parameters and are not summed over a growing family. To obtain p=x^o(1), choose strictly increasing validity thresholds X_m, with X_m→∞ and, if needed, X_m≥exp(m²), and use the representation for ε=1/m on X_m≤x<X_(m+1). Then m→∞ and p≤x^(1/m); the interpolation identity remains exact at each scale. No rate is extracted from the pointwise limits, nor is it claimed that the profile error is uniform as u→∞. Collisions of marks cost xL^(k+2)exp(−b), negligible for each fixed u although that error need not be below square root; the final representation keeps the exact terminal weight and does not round it.

**Limit of the conclusion.** The weight is chosen with W, which requires information about integers with primes larger than p. It is not a formula for computing S from data of primes ≤p alone. The sign of the weighted finite sum remains open.
