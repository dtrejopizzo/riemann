# A centre that annihilates the main term in all quotients

The oscillation of 058 belongs to the representative with log(d/L). The freedom of constant in 056 allows changing the representative of the whole form before cutting. Use in the two symmetric logarithms of H the additional subtraction

    b₀=log 2+1/4+η'(1/2)/η(1/2).

The upper criterion O(L) for the new representative is equivalent to the same global sign. Indeed for q>1 the inversion of Λ(q) does not change: Σ_{d|q}μ(d)=0. The small-period argument of 055–056 admits the shifted coefficients. If during the extension to all q the term q=1 appears, its row costs o(1) by 054. One can work with the diagonal included: the new computation below gives o(L) on it.

**Simultaneous cancellation.** With u=t√r/L, r=ab, d=at,m=bt in a reduced quotient a/b of 057, the profile after removing the cutoffs becomes f(u)=(log u−b₀)F(u). It is still smooth, of bounded variation and rapidly decreasing at zero. Now ∫f=0: 057 gives C_diag, and the same Mellin identity before differentiating gives C_unit=∫F/ζ(2)=V̂(1/2)η(1/2)²/ζ(2)>0. The quotient C_diag/C_unit is exactly b₀. This simultaneously annihilates the main term of each slope; no constants have been chosen according to the quotient. For r≤log x the proof of 057 now gives a sum of absolute values of **totals per slope** O_N(L/log^N x) for each N. Thus the signed excess of 058 is avoided in this representative.

**Polynomial block.** After removing the cutoffs in a slope, the uniform estimate for squarefree coprimes of 057 and the variation of f give O(τ(r)√L r^(−1/4)) per slope, since the integral term is zero. Summing the absolute values of those totals for r≤R one obtains

    O(√L Σ_{r≤R} τ(r)² r^(−1/4)) = O(√L R^(3/4+o(1))).

For R=L^(2/3−ε) and fixed 0<ε<2/3 this is o(L). There are two ways to guarantee the removal of the cutoffs:

1. With the original K=C₀L(log x)² and D=cL/(log x)², restrict to 1/2≤a/b≤2. The differences of the cutoffs only intervene at u≤C max(c,1/C₀)/(log x)², as in 057, now because of the bounded ratio. In this zone the sum over w and each variant with U are O(u^(−C') exp(−c'/√u)). To see this use the partition of 055 in w=ij at size ij comparable to u⁻²; the larger variable has size ≥c/u and its derivatives have the order-two constants of 047, also for the factor U. Alternating and optimising the order gives the stated estimate. With C₀ large and c small the error is smaller than any **prescribed** power of 1/x in that zone. It suffices to beat the polynomial cost of all slopes, samples and logarithms. Thus with the original ranges this bounded-aspect block is controlled.
2. To admit **all** quotients with r≤R take K=L^(4/3) and D=cL^(2/3), with c fixed and sufficiently small. It is another choice of the cutoff of the same criterion, with individual indices now between constants times L^(2/3) and L^(4/3). In 054 the exponential factor for the rows q≤L is even smaller. In 055 the box proof for periods d≤D gains (d/K)^(N/2) while the complete quadrature gains (d/y)^N. Both ratios are negative powers of L and a sufficiently large fixed order covers the sum of the D coefficients in 056. The high part of the mean and the small rows remain negligible by the same estimates. If dw<eK and V(dmw/y)≠0, m≥4a₀L²/(eK)>D by the choice of c, with a₀=1/16. This again justifies the symmetrisation. In any slope with r≤R, d,m lie between Lu/√r and Lu√r. If a lower limit fails or some U differs from one on the support of V, then u≤C√r L^(−1/3)≤C L^(−ε/2), using w≤4B/u². The decay O_N(u^N) of the sum over w now suffices even after summing polynomial costs in L. One obtains the bound o(L) for the sum of the absolute values of all totals per slope of the block.

In particular, with the second choice all pairs with sf(dm)≤x^(1/3−ε') can be excluded from the form for each fixed 0<ε'<1/3. The terms have been summed with their sign within each slope before taking absolute values. The widening of the individual ranges is the cost of the second option. Neither this bound nor the annihilation of the mean term yet gives the upper bound on the remaining slopes.
