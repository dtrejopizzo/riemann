# A sparse defect invisible on the real axis and in any finite list of moments at one

168 lost real regularity at β. For any η>0, prefix and integer M≥0, one can keep simultaneously the frequency guarantees of 168, regularity of the Mellin defect on the whole real axis σ≥1/2, its derivatives 0 to M exactly zero at s=1, and zero natural mean of the additive divisor defect. Even so the sparse ceiling of 166 fails. Regularity is understood locally at each real point, without a strip of width uniform in height.

**Deterministic rounding.** Take β=2/3 and τ>0 distinct from every ordinate of a zero of ζ and such that B(β+iτ)≠0. For p>P very large set a_p=(log p)p^(β−1)cos(τ log p), of modulus less than one. For each of the positive and negative parts accumulate their magnitudes in increasing order of p. At each step assign the increment of the floor of that accumulated sum; subtract the two sequences of increments to obtain b_p∈{−1,0,1}. Only the floor corresponding to the sign of a_p can change. Every partial sum of b_p−a_p from the cut has modulus ≤2. Hence Σ(b_p−a_p)p^(−s) converges locally uniformly on Re s>0 by Abel. Moreover Σ_{P<p≤y}|b_p|≤Σ_{P<p≤y}|a_p|. For every fixed j the sum Σ |b_p|(log p)^j/p tends to zero as P goes to infinity: use Abel with the decreasing weight beyond P and the absolute convergence of Σ (log p)^(j+1)p^(β−2). In particular the sum of reciprocals of the modified primes is arbitrarily small.

**Finite calibration.** Before fixing P choose M+2 distinct auxiliary primes, larger than the prefix and with sum of reciprocals as small as desired. Their columns can be chosen independent in the rows (−log p)^j/p, 0≤j≤M, and 1/(p−1). Indeed a relation valid for all sufficiently large primes would have, after multiplying by p, the form Q(log p)+c p/(p−1)=0. Passing to infinity Q would be constant and then c=Q=0. Choose P larger than all the auxiliaries. Solve the real system for the auxiliary b_p that annihilates all those sums, including the contribution of the rounded tail. The auxiliary b_p tend to zero as P grows because the matrix is fixed; take |b_p|≤1. At the other primes put b_p=0. One obtains that L(s)=Σ b_p p^(−s) has zero jet up to M at 1 and Σ b_p/(p−1)=0.

**Divisors and singularity.** Use weights log p+b_p for each p^k, positive by choosing auxiliary and tail primes larger than 3. The original defect is g(n)=Σ b_p v_p(n). The mean of g(n) up to N tends to Σ b_p/(p−1)=0 by dominated convergence on expanding the floors. With the same proof as 168, the mean of 2^|g| is ≤∏_{b_p≠0}(1+1/(p−2))<1+η. Since the auxiliary coefficients are real, the frequency of g≠0 is controlled directly by the union of the multiples of the modified primes, of proportion ≤Σ_{b_p≠0}1/p<η in each prefix.

On Re s>1, L(s) differs from [D(s+1−β+iτ)+D(s+1−β−iτ)]/2 only by a function holomorphic on Re s>0, by the rounding and the finite changes. The shifted arguments have real part >1/2 near each σ≥1/2. The choice of τ avoids poles on that whole axis. At s=β+iτ a residue 1/2 does remain by the argument of 167. The transform of the modified prime sum is B(s)[D(s)+L(s)]; D is regular there by the choice of τ, and the Gaussian factor of 166 does not vanish either. The ceiling would imply holomorphy there, a contradiction.

**Route decision.** The modulation appeared in R167_102 and in 213-167. The combination with sparse rounding, calibration of moments and exactness of the identity on each prefix remedies the explicit real defect of 168. This rules out adding to the sieve bounds only checks of real regularity, a prescribed number of exact moments and absence of mean bias of the divisor error. A proof using the pointwise identity for the original weights remains open. It is not claimed that the model preserves the functional equation of ζ or its exact identity at all integers.
