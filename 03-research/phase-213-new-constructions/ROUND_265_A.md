# The correction from proper powers of a binomial, even for decreasing splits

With c(m)=⌊n/m⌋−⌊k/m⌋−⌊(n−k)/m⌋, define B₁(n,k)=Σ_p(log p)c(p), which pays only the first level of each prime. For any δ>0, uniformly in n^(16/19+δ)≤k≤n/2, one has without RH

    log binom(n,k) − B₁(n,k)
        = |ζ(1/2)| [√k+√(n−k)−√n] + o(√k).

Thus one can pass from the exact factorial mass to the mass of simple primes with an explicit correction on the same scale as the sign. Even if k/n→0 in this regime, the correction is equivalent to |ζ(1/2)|√k. For k/n bounded below the expression with the three square roots persists. The first level counts the carry at m=p; it is not necessarily the logarithm of the radical of the binomial, since a prime may enter through a carry only at higher levels.

**Extraction of the squares.** The difference is the exact sum over proper powers. Those of exponent ≥3 cost O(n^(1/3)log² n)=o(√k) by elementary counting. For the squares one compares Σ_p(log p)c(p²) with ∫₀^∞ c(u²)du. Up to P=ε√k the cost of both sides is O(P) by c≤1 and θ(P)≤2P. Fix 0<ε<1 while n grows.

Above P, the kernel is the sum of the indicators of (( (n−k)/j )^(1/2),(n/j)^(1/2)] minus those of (0,(k/j)^(1/2)], cut off at u>P. There are at most n/P² intervals of the first type. Writing y=√(n/j), the length before cutting lies between yk/(2n) and yk/n. For y≥P the ratio of that length to y^(5/8) tends uniformly to infinity: it is ≫_ε k^(19/16)/n. The PNT in short intervals of 238 approximates the mass of each uncut interval by its length with uniform relative error o(1), since y≥P→∞. The sum of those lengths is ≤(√n−√(n−k)) Σ_(j≤n/P²)j^(−1/2) ≤2k/P. The total cost of those errors is o_ε(√k). The intervals crossing P number O(1+k/P²), with y≤√2 P; on their pieces the ordinary PNT is applied at both endpoints with error o(P) per piece. The surviving intervals of the second type number ≤k/P², with endpoints between P and √k, and also cost o_ε(√k) in errors. Then letting ε↓0 proves the replacement by the integral, without assumptions on the independence of the fractional parts.

Substituting v=u² in that integral leaves half the Mellin transform of the carry at s=1/2. The formula of 259 gives exactly ζ(1/2)(√n−√k−√(n−k)), which is positive. The margin is genuinely of order √k over the whole range, since √n−√(n−k)≤(√2−1)√k for k≤n/2.

**Why some control on the split must be imposed.** For each fixed integer k≥1 there is a positive density (depending on k) of upper integers n at which ALL carries at proper powers are zero. For p≤k choose A_p with p^(A_p)>k and impose n≡k modulo Q=∏_(p≤k)p^(A_p). The k numbers n−i, 0≤i<k, then have exactly the same valuations at those primes as k−i. Moreover, for each level p^a up to p^(A_p) the floors give zero carry by divisibility of n−k, and above that level the interval (n−k,n] contains no multiples. For p>k exclude the k residues n≡i mod p², 0≤i<k. Up to a cutoff Y the density follows from the Chinese remainder theorem. The tail in n≤X costs at most kΣ_(Y<p≤√X)(X/(Qp²)+1); after dividing by X and letting X→∞, Y→∞, it tends to zero. The constructed set has density Q^(−1)∏_(p>k)(1−k/p²)>0. Taking successively k→∞ and choosing n as large as needed proves that the square-root formula does not hold uniformly over all splits with k→∞.

**Relation to the goal.** For a row in the proved range, e(n,k) of 262 exceeds B₁(n,k) by the above explicit correction plus o(√k), since the Stirling remainder is O(log n). This measures a genuine favorable contribution before combining rows. It gives no control of the error when summing many signed rows with growing variation. 260 treated the mass fraction in the octave to leading order; 257 separated squares for a smooth weight. Here the jumps of the carry are resolved on a uniform range of splits, and splits are exhibited that prevent extending the conclusion without restrictions.
