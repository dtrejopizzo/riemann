# How much exactness of the divisor identity the energy criterion requires

For any η>0 and prescribed prefix there are positive weights on the true prime powers that satisfy the untwisted identity Σ_{d|n}Λ(d)=log n for at least a fraction 1−η of EACH prefix of integers, and nevertheless fail the energy criterion of 166. Even the exponential mean of the error is small uniformly in the end of the prefix.

**Construction.** Fix 1/2<β<1, for example β=2/3. In each block [2^k,2^(k+1)) from k₀ on choose the first floor(2^(βk)/k) primes. PNT guarantees availability for all large k. Denote the set by L. Its mass Σ_{p∈L}1/p is at most Σ_{k≥k₀}2^(−(1−β)k)/k and can be made arbitrarily small. Add exactly one to Λ(p^j) for all p∈L and j≥1, leaving the rest untouched. On the primes the maximal relative error in the tail is 1/log p. The defect in the original divisor identity is exactly g(n)=Σ_{p∈L}v_p(n), a nonnegative completely additive integer function.

For each integer N, by expanding 2^g over divisors with positive coefficients and floor(N/d)≤N/d,
    (1/N) Σ_{n≤N}2^g(n) ≤ ∏_{p∈L}(1+Σ_{j≥1}2^(j−1)/p^j)=∏_{p∈L}(1+1/(p−2)).
k₀ was chosen so that the product is less than 1+η. Since 2^g−1≥1 when g≠0, the number of failures up to N is ≤ηN, simultaneously for all N. The argument also bounds the proportion with g≥m by η/(2^m−1).

**Failure of the ceiling.** The Mellin transform of the sum over primes of 162 gains B(s)L(s), L(s)=Σ_{p∈L}p^(−s). This series is holomorphic on Re s>β and diverges to +∞ as real s tends to β from the right: each block contributes ≫1/k at β. At β=2/3, B(β)≠0 by the formula of 160 (ζ(2β)>0, ζ(β+1/4)<0 and P(β)≠0). The original factor B(s)D(s) is regular at that real point, since D is the negative logarithmic derivative of ζ minus higher powers and ζ does not vanish there. The Gaussian multiplier is nonzero. A holomorphic continuation of the modified transform to Re s>1/2 would be incompatible with that divergence by meromorphic continuation from Re s>1 to Re s>β. By 166, the ceiling on the sparse blocks fails. The prime weights remain positive, comparable, asymptotic to log p, and preserve the prime number theorem and the pair sieve.

**Elementary boundary.** For a completely additive defect, if g(n)=0 on a set of density one, then g is identically zero. For each fixed prime p one can choose an n with g(n)=g(pn)=0, because both sets have density one (the failures among pn up to pN are o(N)). By additivity g(p)=0; factorization concludes. Thus a loss of fixed positive density, however small, and a loss of zero density have different consequences in this class. The models depend on η; no single one satisfying all tolerances is presented.

**Cross-check and conclusion.** R167_102 and 213-167 preserved sieve bounds. The model of this memo moreover preserves the ORIGINAL identity on almost all integers of each prefix and an exponential control of the defect. The spectral failure used here is real; the regularity of the zeta model on the whole real segment is not preserved. This rules out simply replacing the identity by a fixed-frequency certificate of hits. The exact identity does distinguish the real arithmetic, but converting it into the energy bound is still missing.
