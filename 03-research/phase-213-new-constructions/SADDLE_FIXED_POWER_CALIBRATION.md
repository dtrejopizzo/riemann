# Which abscissa a fixed cut at powers of log x requires

For the sum S* of 368, write L=log x and fix A≥2, z=L^A, b=log z. The real point α of K'(α)=0 satisfies

    α=1−1/A+(log A+o(1))/b,
    K(α)=(1−1/A)L+(1+log A+o(1))L/b.

The estimates are for fixed A. Indeed, for σ=1−1/A+c/b and bounded c, partial summation of the prime number theorem gives
Σ_{p≤z}(log p)p^(−σ)∼z^(1−σ)/(1−σ)∼Ae^(−c)L.
The higher powers contribute O_A(log² z) to the derivative because σ≥1/2−O(1/b). The sign of K' at c=log A±ε and K''>0 locate its unique root. The same partial summation, with the additional weight 1/log p, gives log E_z(α)∼L/b. This gives the second formula. The case A=2 recovers 366–368.

Hence, at any logarithmic cut of fixed power A>2 the shift to the saddle passes at asymptotic distance 1/A to the left of 1. A zero-free strip of width decreasing with height leaves the possible poles of 1/ζ in that shift uncontrolled. This identifies a precise obligation of this contour deformation. It does not establish that every sign proof must satisfy it.
