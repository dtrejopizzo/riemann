# The near block is positive even at logarithmic distance

With f_v and S of 162, let N_X(H) be the integrated contribution of the ordered pairs p≠q with |p−q|≤H. There are constants C₀,c,C>0 such that for large X and

    C₀ log X ≤ H ≤ X/log X

one has unconditionally c XH ≤ N_X(H) ≤ C XH log X. In particular with H=C₀ log X the near block already contributes at least a constant times X log X, the scale of the full diagonal. The statement is aggregate, without requiring the existence of pairs with a particular gap.

**Control of the negative products.** The f_v are Lipschitz with a common constant. Indeed the derivatives of K in the logarithmic variable satisfy the same superpolynomial bound at infinity as K: in the inversion of 160 it suffices to insert s and shift to any fixed negative line. This controls the derivative of K(v/t) down to t=0; at the other end there is compact support and smoothness. For every pair with negative product, |f_v(p/X)f_v(q/X)|≤|f_v(p/X)−f_v(q/X)|²/4≤C(H/X)². Only p,q≤C'X matter. The total weight of all near pairs in that compact set is O(XH log X), using Chebyshev in p and the fact that there are O(H) possible integers q for each p. Thus the negative products cost at most O(H³ log X/X), which is o(XH) uniformly in the indicated range. The same count with the sup norm gives the ceiling of N_X(H).

**Enough pairs of equal sign.** Since K is not zero, by continuity there is a fixed rectangle J×I inside (1,2)×(0,∞) where f_v(t) has constant sign and |f_v(t)|≥b>0. For each v∈J use the primes in XI and divide XI into L≤C_I X/H intervals of length ≤H. By PNT the total number M of primes there is ≥c_I X/log X. If M_i are the counts of the cells, the distinct ordered pairs within a single cell are at least M²/L−M by Cauchy–Schwarz. Taking C₀ large enough, M/L≥2 over the whole range. The weights log p log q are ≫(log X)² in XI. On integrating over J, these positive products alone contribute ≫XH. Subtracting all the previous negative cost proves the lower bound. PNT is used on an interval of length proportional to X; the internal distribution may be arbitrary.

**Consequence for the global proof.** Choose for example H=(log X)². The sign is equivalent to the integrated contribution of the pairs with |p−q|>H being ≤0, eventually in X=2^j. Under RH the total off-diagonal is −D_X+O(X) by 162; removing N_X(H)>0 makes it negative. Conversely, if the distant part is ≤0, ∫₁² S(Xv)²dv≤D_X+N_X(H)=O(X(log X)³). The Mellin argument by blocks of 162 still works with any fixed power of the logarithm. More generally it holds for every H=X^{o(1)} in the indicated range. This removes from the obligation all pairs with gaps up to H without estimating any gap individually. The negativity of the distant block of 162 was proved only for H=ηX fixed in relative scale; it does not transfer to this H, which decreases in relative scale.

**Cross-check.** 162 uses double PNT on a fixed macroscopic band; its weak convergence does not give the lower bound at logarithmic distance. The cell count and the two-sided bound for products of opposite sign allow reaching here down to a constant times the mean spacing. The negativity of the logarithmic complement remains open.
