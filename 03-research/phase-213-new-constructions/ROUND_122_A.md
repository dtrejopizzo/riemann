# Detection with fixed maximal frequency for each lower exponent

For each 0≤η<Δ there exists a finite B_η such that, in every sufficiently large window, the even Dirichlet cosines of frequency ≤B_η contain a primitive vector with quotient ≤−exp(2ηT). The value B_η is not assumed known nor uniform as Δ is approached. In particular 119 and 120 hold with N=ceil(T log log(T+4)) instead of ceil(T³). Any slower divergent factor in place of log log(T+4) also works: the proof only uses that every fixed frequency is eventually included and the polynomial ceiling of 119. The number of modes at fixed maximal frequency still grows linearly with T.

**Detection before cutting.** Choose a zero z=ρ−1/2=b+iγ with b>η and 0<v<1 such that vb>η. Let g be the real centered Gaussian density of variance cT. Apply P=−∂²+1/4 to the sum of its translates centered at ±(vT+u), u bounded. Its two-sided transform is
     
    Φ(z)=2(1/4−z²)exp(cT z²/2)cosh((vT+u)z).

In the series Σρ Φ(z)², expand 4cosh² into two exponentials and a constant. By the symmetry z↦−z the two exponential sums coincide. The constant has negative rate because |γ|>14. For the growing exponential the rates per unit of T are f(z)=2vb+c(b²−γ²). The chosen zero gives f>2η when c is small enough. The maximum exists by the penalty in height. Up to conjugates and multiplicities, two functions of c can only coincide at one point (if they have the same intercept and slope, b and |γ| coincide). Choose a generic c among the sufficiently small values. A single dominant conjugate pair remains in the growing sum, with a fixed gap from the rest. The tails with coefficient polynomial in γ are controlled by reserving one unit of T in the Gaussian exponential, uniformly for bounded u. Varying u in [0,π/|γ_*|] the phase 2uγ_* goes once around: the leading term, including the phase of its coefficient, can be made real negative. Hence ΣΦ(z)²≤−c₁ exp(T max f) for all large T and some choice of u per window.

**Cutoff and approximation.** Use a smooth even cutoff equal to one up to |t|≤T−2 and zero from |t|≥T−1 on, with uniform derivatives. Reducing c in advance, the L¹ norms of the removed part and of its first two derivatives with weight exp(|t|/2) are O(exp(−5T)), uniformly for bounded |u|: in the tails the distance to the center is ≥(1−v)T−O(1), and completing the square in the Gaussian exponent gives any fixed negative rate by reducing c. This condition can be imposed before the generic choice: u≤π/14. Thus the change in the transform in the strip is O(exp(−5T)/(1+γ²)) by integrations by parts. The ideal transform admits there the bound O(poly(T)exp(T)/(1+γ²)) by the explicit formula above (c<1). By summability the change in the series of squares is o(1); for the cut test that series is the Weil energy. The error of the primitive moment is O(exp(−5T)), since Φ(±1/2)=0.

For the cosine expansion of the cut test, each unnormalized coefficient at frequency a_j equals its transform at ia_j divided by T. It is the sum of the ideal coefficient O((1+a_j²)exp(−cTa_j²/2)/T) and O(exp(−5T)/(T(1+a_j²))). Choose B_η large enough, for example c B_η²/4>5. By Parseval with weight 1+a_j² the tail of frequencies above B_η has H¹ norm O(exp(−4T)); in the second bound one sums (1+a_j²)⁻¹ over a grid of step π/T. The moment correction by the first mode of 119 costs at most a polynomial factor times the H¹ error plus the previous moment residue. By 081 the energy error is o(1), since the H¹ norm of the starting test is bounded and the non-archimedean part costs O(exp T). The L² norm of the final vector is bounded above and its negative energy guarantees it is nonzero. Normalizing and absorbing constants via max f>2η gives the statement. For the ceiling of the rate in the declared spaces the argument of 119 applies directly.

**Scope.** 115 uses a Gaussian to average a series and 119 truncates a fixed Gevrey bump. Here the variance of the physical test itself grows linearly with the window: it attenuates the high ordinates and allows simultaneously an exponentially precise cutoff in time and in frequency. A subexponential bound of the matrices or of the arithmetic data of 120 remains to be proved; the dimension reduction does not prove that ceiling.
