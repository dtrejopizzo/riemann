# Frequency of the sign changes of the critical moment

Let N(Z,B) be the number of sign alternations of H(x)=Σ_{r≤x}μ(r)/√r−1/ζ(1/2) on [Z,B], omitting zero values. For B=Z^(1+h), h>0 fixed, the lower limit of N(Z,B)/log(B/Z) is at least γ₁/π, with γ₁ the smallest positive ordinate of a nontrivial zero. More generally, if there exists a zero whose real part strictly exceeds those of all zeros of height |Im ρ|≤G, that lower limit is at least G/π. For the empty set the first claim suffices. In particular:

* If all zeros up to height G are on the critical line and there were one off it, the lower logarithmic density of the crossings would be at least G/π.
* If Θ is not attained, the quotient tends to infinity.

Thus a ceiling strictly less than G/π along an unbounded sequence of these bands, together with the indicated verification of zeros, would suffice to exclude a failure of the sign. Such a ceiling has not been proved.

**A single smoothing for many centers.** Write T=log Z, t=log x, U(t)=H(exp t) extended by zero for t<0. Fix 0<e<h/2. U is cut to the interval [T,(1+h)T] and convolved with the Gaussian of variance cT, centered at vT with 1+e≤v≤1+h−e. For c>0 small enough depending on e,h, replacing U by the cut one costs O(exp(−3T)) uniformly: by Cauchy–Schwarz the error is ≪exp(((1+h)/2+c/4−e²/(4c))T). The same c is used at all centers.

As in 142, use the fixed segment of Re s=3/4 and shift to −2, with the factor exp(T(vs+cs²/2)). Horizontally and on the right tails the rate can be made <−1 by a sufficiently large fixed height, uniformly in v; the left side has rate ≤−2v+2c<−1. The rate of a pole z=b+iγ, b=Reρ−1/2, is vb+c(b²−γ²)/2. The maximum is >−1/2 uniformly by taking c small, through a fixed pole with b≥0. For the claim with G one can additionally make a pole with real part strictly larger than those of the finite set below G exceed the rates of all those poles on the whole interval of v. This uses the positive gap between real parts, not a bound on their residues.

Only finitely many conjugate pairs can attain the maximum, uniformly in v, by the quadratic penalty in height. Two distinct rates are lines in v; if they coincide identically, the poles correspond to the same conjugate pair. Removing neighborhoods of arbitrarily small total length around the tie points leaves compact segments with a single dominant pair and a uniform gap. On each of them, the first term of the smoothing is

    2 Re[ exp(T(vz+cz²/2)) T^(m−1) C (v+cz)^(m−1) ],

with m the multiplicity and C≠0 constant. The lower-degree terms of the same pole cost O(1/T) relative to the envelope for m≥2, and the other poles have exponential saving. The envelope exceeds the error of the contours and of the cutoff. The argument of the complex term has derivative Tγ+O(1) in v, since Re(v+cz)>0. On a segment of length l this gives at least T|γ|l/π−O(1) real alternations by choosing phases that are multiples of π. Concatenating segments loses at most one alternation per junction. All dominant heights exceed G in the refined case and are always ≥γ₁.

**Transfer of the alternations.** The Gaussian convolution of compactly supported real data cannot have more sign changes than the data. Here there is a finite proof: after removing a positive factor, the function of the position of the center is the two-sided Laplace transform of U cut by a positive Gaussian weight. If the data have k alternations, choose a point a of the first transition. Differentiating the transform after multiplying it by exp(−as) is equivalent to multiplying the data by t−a, reducing its number of alternations by one. Rolle and induction bound by k the number of distinct real zeros of the transform; the one-sign case is strict except for null data. The rescaling of the center parameter is positive. The alternation count then passes to the original H inside the band. Dividing by hT, letting the excluded length tend to zero and then e proves the bounds. For non-attained Θ, every finite set of heights lies below some zero, so the claim applies to every fixed G.

**Scope and antecedents.** 115 separates rates of zeros; 142 used a modulated Gaussian to guarantee at least one crossing per band. Here an interval of centers is swept without modulation and the count is preserved by the variation-diminishing property. It does not imply that every interval of fixed ratio contains a crossing; a counting bound does not control the largest gap. The pending term of 141 still has no arithmetic bound.
