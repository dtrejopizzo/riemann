# Equal mass, equal gap and opposite signs without primality

In the model that allows distinct integers with their exact weight log k, there exist two prefixes of equal length, product and last element, with the same next element, in strict crossing, whose margins B have opposite signs and modulus ≫H/log H. It serves to test what additional information 094 requires.

For large N and M between 3.2N/log N and 3.5N/log N (rounding), use u=N+1,...,N+M. List A includes u and 6u; list B, 2u and 3u. Within each list all are distinct by separation of bands. The common mass is H₀=Σ log(6u²). Add to both the integer m=floor(H₀+log H₀)−1 as last element of the prefix and designate m+4 as the next one. One has H₀/N between 6.4+o(1) and 7+o(1), hence m>6(N+M). Moreover H=H₀+log m satisfies 1+o(1)≤H−m≤2+o(1), so the crossing is strict.

The exact difference of Q and of B is 2M log 2 log 3, by expansion of each pair. To centre the signs, let D(M) be the average of the two margins. In both prefixes Q/H=log N+(log 6)/2+o(1) uniformly: the weighted average of the logarithms of the two members of a pair differs from their simple average by O(1/log N), and the last element has relative weight O(log N/N). Thus D/H=log(e√6 N/H)+o(1), positive at the lower end and negative at the upper one. Between contiguous values of M, H increases by O(log N) and both Q change by O(log²N), including the replacement of the last element. Hence |D(M+1)−D(M)|=O(log²N). At the first sign change |D|=O(log²N)=o(M). Each margin differs from D by ±M log 2 log 3, as desired.

The exact equality of products exploits composite factorizations. Unique factorization prevents that exchange between two distinct sets of primes. The model does not reproduce the arithmetic of ζ nor prove that using unique factorization suffices for the sign.
