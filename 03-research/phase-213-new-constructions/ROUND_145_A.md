# A full Liouville average with unconditional sign and a variance frontier

Use U(u)=exp(−u/2)L(exp u) of 144. For fixed c>0 set I_c(T)=∫φ_{cT}(u−T)U(u)du, T=log x. With c=1.25·10^(−25), as T→∞ one has unconditionally

    I_c(T)=a + 2 exp(−c γ₁² T/2) Re(R₁ exp(iγ₁T)) + O(exp(−2·10^(−23)T)),
    a=1/ζ(1/2)<0,   R₁=ζ(1+2iγ₁)/[(1/2+iγ₁)ζ'(1/2+iγ₁)].

The same expansion holds when cutting the upper integral at T+3√c T, giving a finite Liouville sum with positive weights. It is a sign of the full average for every sufficiently large scale, without assuming RH. No effective value of the first scale is specified.

**Certification with the data already used in P46.** By Z1 of paper 46, the first pair is simple, with 14.13≤γ₁≤14.14 and no other zeros of height ≤21. By Z2 all zeros up to 3·10¹² are on the line. The transform R(s) is that of 144. Start at Re s=.54, where R is bounded (the reciprocal series of the denominator converges absolutely). Choose a fixed height G between 2.99·10¹² and 3·10¹² that is not the ordinate of a zero. The real exponent at s=σ+it is T[σ+c(σ²−t²)/2]. On the tails of the right line and on the horizontals of the rectangle down to σ=−1/4 the exponent per unit T is less than −.01; this is already checked by .54−c(2.99·10¹²)²/2<−.018. The left side costs O(exp(−.24T)). The Gaussian and the boundedness on the fixed contour justify the integrated errors, with margin in the exponents. Only the real pole at zero and poles on the shifted imaginary axis are crossed. The first gives a; the first pair gives the second written term. The remaining ones have rate ≤−c·21²/2<−2.7·10^(−23) and are finitely many in this rectangle; their polynomial factors from possible multiplicities are absorbed in the announced error. The removed physical tail costs ≤exp(T/2+cT/8) Prob(N(0,1)>(3−√c/2)√T) by the elementary bound for U. It also stays within the error.

**Frontier of averages with linear variance.** For z=ρ−1/2=b+iγ define

    c*=sup_{b>0} 2b/(γ²−b²),

with value zero if the set is empty. A positive value is attained: outside a finite set the ratios tend to zero. If c>c*, I_c(T)→a exponentially. If 0<c<c*, I_c(T) takes both signs on intervals of bounded length in T for T large (the length may depend on c). Hence c* is the infimum of the positive coefficients with eventual negativity, without needing to decide the frontier value.

Indeed the rate of a pole with b>0 is b−(c/2)(γ²−b²), and its residue is not cancelled by ζ(1+2z) by convergence of the Euler product. For the general contour take the left side −d with d<min(1/4,1/c), chosen without poles on it, and a fixed height so large that the horizontals and right tails have strictly negative rate. The poles with −d<b≤0 and γ≠0 have negative rate. If c>c*, all others also have negative rate and only the bias remains. If c<c*, there are poles with positive rate; all maximizers are included when choosing the height. Extract the largest exponential and within it the largest degree in T from multiplicity. The leader is a nonzero real trigonometric polynomial of mean zero: the frequencies are the imaginary parts of z+cz²/2, nonzero, and in the half-plane b>0 the map is injective by factorization of the difference. The leading coefficient of each pole contains (1+cz)^(m−1)≠0. The trigonometric function has positive and negative values with margin and, by recurrence of the translates of a finite trigonometric polynomial, both reappear with bounded gaps. The normalized remainder tends to zero. This general statement refers to the untruncated averages; the finite cutoff above is justified for the chosen numerical c.

**Scope.** Z2 implies c*≤1/((3·10¹²)²−1/4). Proving c*=0 would amount to excluding every zero off the line. The average of 144 has V/T→0 and lies outside the positive region of fixed coefficients certified here. The Gaussian base and the separation of rates are in 115/122/143/144. What is added is the threshold for this arithmetic average, the treatment of ties and the unconditional expansion with the sign and the first oscillatory term.
