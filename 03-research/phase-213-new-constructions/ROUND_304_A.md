# The first row admits balanced cutoffs with arbitrarily large cofactors

Let J(z)=Σ_(m≤z)b₀(m)(1/m−1/z), z≥1. J takes both signs arbitrarily far out. Consequently, for each fixed H there are finite H<A<B such that, for all sufficiently large x, some y∈[x/B,x/A] leaves |R₀(x,y)|≤C_B. The cutoff near x/3 of 303 was only the first of an unbounded sequence of macroscopic scales with this property. No growth rate is given for the positions of the sign changes.

**Real factorization.** The series D(w) of row zero has product ∏_(p≡2 mod 3)(1−2p^(−w))², including 2. For each N extract the N factors (L(nw,χ)/ζ(nw))^(a_n), with

    n a_n = Σ_(d|n; d odd) μ(d) 2^(n/d).

At an inert prime the identity of formal series follows by taking logarithms: the coefficient of degree m requires Σ_(n|m; m/n odd) n a_n=2^m. The exponents are positive: writing n=2^k l with l odd, the numerator counts words of minimal period l over an alphabet of 2^(2^k) letters. They are also integers: by induction the residual inert factor after the lower degrees has integer coefficients and is identically 1 modulo 2 (each quotient (1−z^j)/(1+z^j) and the target have that property). The coefficient of degree n is eliminated with an integer power since the linear term in z^n of the quotient is −2.

At split primes the quotients equal 1. The factor at 3 is corrected by the inverse of the finite product of (1−3^(−nw))^(a_n), with no singularity in Re w>0. The Euler remainder converges locally in Re w>1/(N+1), since at large primes it starts at degree N+1; the finitely many small ones may give zeros but have no poles there. For each real positive w, ζ has no positive real zeros (by the eta series on (0,1)), and L(w,χ)>0 by grouping into pairs of residues 1,2. Thus D is holomorphic near the whole positive half-line, and D(1/n)=0 for all n≥1 by the pole of ζ at 1 and a_n>0. Absence of complex zeros is not required.

**Oscillation.** The Mellin transform ∫₁^∞ J(z)z^(−1−s)dz coincides for Re s>0 with D(1+s)/(s(s+1)). Suppose J has eventual sign. After removing a compact interval, Landau for the transform of a function of fixed sign forces convergence for each real s>−1: on that interval the continuation is regular, even at 0 because of the at least double zero of D at 1. The Mellin transform would have infinitely many real zeros s=1/n−1, n≥2. This is impossible with a finite number of sign changes. Indeed, multiplying the Mellin transform by c^s and differentiating inserts the weight log(c/z), in addition to a positive factor c^s. Choosing c at a sign change reduces their number by one. Rolle preserves infinitely many zeros in the open interval. Iterating (applying each time to the Mellin transform with the new weight, without its prefactor) leads to the Mellin transform of a function of fixed sign that is not identically zero. The derivatives converge on each compact subinterval by the preceding absolute convergence. J is affine in 1/z between integers, so it has only finitely many changes on each compact set and is positive just after 1. The contradiction proves the claim.

**Passage to the exact cutoff.** Take A,B of opposite signs. At each fixed endpoint z=A,B the single-prime computation of 303 gives R₀(x,x/z)∼−2x J(z)/log x: the cofactors m≤B are fixed, and all primes >x/B appear with exponent one and are unique for x>B². The jumps when moving y cost ≤4Σ_(m≤B)|b₀(m)|. Hence at a crossing there is a cutoff with the stated error. For each fixed pair the signs persist by continuity in some band of frequencies near zero; no common band for infinitely many pairs is claimed.

In the first row one can even choose y=o(x) with |R₀|≤x^o(1) for all large x: take successive pairs with A_j→∞, and delay the use of pair j until the statement holds and C_(B_j)≤x^(1/j). This diagonalization does not provide y≤x^(1−δ) for any fixed δ. The use of Landau to force oscillation has antecedents in the index; here the real singularities are excluded by the above factorization, and it is the **real zeros at all integer reciprocals** that force the result.
