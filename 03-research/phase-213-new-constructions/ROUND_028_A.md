# Transfer of a negative test to the other parity by translations

**Lemma of the remote crossing.** Let F be real, smooth, primitive and compactly supported. For C(D)=a(F,V_DF) one has

    liminf_{D→∞} C(D) ≤ 0 ≤ limsup_{D→∞} C(D).

Infinite extreme limits are allowed. Let c=c_F, w the diameter of the support. For D>w, P47 thm:band gives C(D) as the sum of an archimedean term O_F(exp(−5D/2)) and of −Σ Λ(n)c(log n−D)/√n. In a neighbourhood of w the continuity of C is used, applying the exponential estimate only from w+1 on. The arithmetic sum grows at most like O_F((1+D)exp(D/2)), by support and Λ(n)≤log n. Thus the one-sided transform of C converges absolutely for Re z>1/2. Extending the indicated arithmetic term to all D≥0 only changes the remaining part on a compact set. For n with log n>w, the change v=log n−D gives the full integral of c(v)exp(zv) times n^(−z). The finitely many exceptions are corrected by entire functions. Consequently the transform is

    E(z) − P(z) S(z+1/2),    P(z)=∫ c(v)exp(zv)dv,    S(s)=Σ Λ(n)n^(−s),

with E holomorphic for Re z>−1, more than enough here. The interchanges in the region of convergence are absolute. P(1/2)=A(F)B(F)=0.

For Re s>1, the convolution Σ_{d|n}Λ(d)=log n gives S=−Z'/Z in a neighbourhood of each real s>1, with Z(s)=Σ n^(−s). Put p(s)=1−2^(1−s) and η(s)=Σ_{j≥1}[(2j−1)^(−s)−(2j)^(−s)]. The grouped series is holomorphic in Re s>0 by local normal convergence (integrate s x^(−s−1) on each pair), and strictly positive at each real s>0. Since η=pZ for Re s>1, the expression −η'/η+p'/p continues S in neighbourhoods of each positive real point except the simple pole at s=1. The factor P annihilates that pole. This gives local holomorphic continuation of the transform along the whole real half-axis z≥0, including its origin.

If C were ≥ε>0 eventually, integrate the positive tail from a suitable D₀. Removing the compact piece preserves that continuation. Its real abscissa of convergence σ lies between 0 and 1/2. A transform of a positive function cannot be holomorphic at σ: take a disc of continuation centred at σ, choose x>σ inside and close enough to the centre so that the Taylor expansion at x reaches x−h<σ with h>0. At x the coefficients of the expansion to the left are ∫ D^k exp(−xD)C(D)dD/k! over the tail. By monotone convergence the finite value of the Taylor series would give convergence of the integral at x−h, a contradiction. The identity with the continuation for x>σ follows by uniqueness from the initial region of absolute convergence. Applying the same to −C excludes the other strict separation. This proves the lemma.

**Application.** If F has definite parity and q=a(F)<0, there are arbitrarily large separations D with C(D)>q. Then V_{−D/2}F−V_{D/2}F has opposite parity, zero moments and energy 2(q−C(D))<0. Every negative test can be taken real and of definite parity by orthogonal decomposition of the form. It suffices to work with smooth tests: mollifying with a real even compact kernel preserves the zero moments and the parity and converges in form norm on a slightly larger common window. Hence the global sign on all primitive even tests of all windows would imply the full sign; the same holds for the odd sector. This does not allow discarding a parity on a fixed window, nor replacing the whole even sector by the plateau subfamily of 027. PROVED after independent mathematical audit.

**Amplification to a plane.** By continuity of the crossing, there exists a sequence D_j→∞ with C(D_j)→0: if its modulus stayed separated from zero on a tail, it would have fixed sign there. For q<0 and large j the two centred translates have Gram matrix with diagonal q and crossing C(D_j), with eigenvalues q±C(D_j)<0. For a profile of definite parity, the sum and the difference give simultaneously negative tests of both parities in that plane. This reviewed corollary requires a single scalar crossing. Simultaneous control of the crossings of a family remains open.

## Several crossings with a single mixture and exact limit of the inference

For two finite families of real smooth compactly supported primitive profiles F_i,G_j, form the matrix C(D) with entries a(F_i,V_DG_j), p entries in total. For each R and ε>0 there are at most p+1 separations D_b≥R and coefficients θ_b≥0 summing to one with ||Σ_b θ_b C(D_b)||_Frob<ε. It is a single mixture for all entries.

Indeed, in the Laplace computation of the lemma replace c_F(v) by the cross-correlation of the two profiles, or its reflection according to the direction of the shift. It is compactly supported and its integral against exp(v/2) factors into a moment of each profile, zero in both directions. For large D there is only one orientation of the comb; the corrections from small contacts remain entire. For the archimedean part, the separated kernel formula of P47 gives exponential decay, sufficient for regularity on the nonnegative real axis. By linearity, every real combination of the entries has that same regularity and bounded exponential growth. The Taylor argument of the lemma prevents it from being uniformly above a positive number on a tail. If 0 lay outside the closed convex hull of the matrices in D≥R, the metric projection of 0 onto that hull would give a strictly separating real functional with such a positive bound. Approximating 0 within the hull and applying Carathéodory in dimension p proves the statement. Regarding the index, the antecedent after 212-84 already identifies the cost of the energy of the average.

The loss in passing from one separation to a mixture is effective for the Laplace argument. As an auxiliary model on real profiles take the bilinear form −Re(conj(F̂(1))Ĝ(1)). It is translation invariant and its negative index is at most two by its factorization into real and imaginary parts. There is a real smooth primitive F with F̂(1)≠0: apply (1/4−∂²) to a positive even bump supported where cos t>0. The crossings against translates of F and of V_{π/2}F are, up to sign and the same nonzero constant, cos D and sin D. Their Laplace transforms are regular along the whole real half-axis including 0. The vector of both crossings has constant positive norm for all D, although it can be annihilated by a mixture of two translates separated by π. For this mixture the averaged profile has zero Fourier coefficient at frequency 1 and zero energy. This model only checks the logical limitation of deducing simultaneous decoupling or index amplification from Laplace regularity. It is not the arithmetic comb.
