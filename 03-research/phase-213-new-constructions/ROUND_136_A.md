# The anchoring with multiplicative signs and the sector of two real primes

Let Q_f(x)=Σ_{a,b≤D,a≠b} f(a)f(b){x/(ab)}. The anchoring x∈[D,D²] of 134 is kept. For each D fix f on the squarefree integers multiplicatively through prime signs, and set f=0 on the others. Even fixing the signs at −1 for all p≤(log D)², when taking independent equiprobable signs at the remaining primes one has, for fixed ε,A>0 and D large enough,

    Prob(max_{D≤u≤v≤D²}|Q_f(v)−Q_f(u)|>D^(1+ε)) ≤ D^(−A).

The probability refers to a law conditioned on each D. This result keeps the origin and multiplicativity on coprimes, two restrictions that were simultaneously absent in the falsifier of 135. It does not give the bound for the single vector with all prime signs negative.

**Proof of the typical size.** For squarefree a,b group by the squarefree part t of ab. With a=gr,b=gs, rs=t and (r,s)=1, there are at most τ(t)D/√t ordered pairs: each choice of r leaves g≤D/max(r,s). For fixed x this also bounds the coefficient of the sign monomial of t. When fixing the signs of p≤Y=(log D)² write t=us where u only has primes >Y and s only primes ≤Y. The modulus of the new coefficient of u is ≤D τ(u)/√u times ∏_{p≤Y}(1+2/√p). This product is D^o(1) by Chebyshev and partial summation. It includes the constant coefficient. Orthogonality of the monomials gives ||Q_f(x)||₂≤D^(1+o(1)), since over squarefree u Σ τ(u)²/u≤∏_{p≤D²}(1+4/p)≪log^C(2D) by the classical harmonic bound for primes. Everything is uniform in x.

The maximal multilinear degree is O(log D/loglog D) since a product of k distinct primes is at least k! and t≤D². The Bonami inequality on the Boolean cube gives ||Q_f(x)||_p≤(p−1)^(degree/2)||Q_f(x)||₂=D^(1+o_p(1)) for each fixed p≥2. Markov and a union bound over the integers up to D² give the stated probability by taking p large enough depending on ε,A, reserving a constant factor to pass from values to differences. Between integers the slope has modulus ≤(Σ_{a≤D}1/a)², so real endpoints are also covered.

**What does happen with the true coefficients.** In the expansion by t *before fixing signs*, for f=μ the sign of the whole sector is (−1)^ω(t). At x=D², the pairs with exactly two distinct primes in t make a positive contribution ≫D²/log²D. Indeed it suffices to take a,b distinct primes in [.80D,.81D]: there are ≫D²/log²D pairs by PNT and D²/(ab) lies between 1.52 and 1.57, so each fractional part exceeds 1/2. All other terms of the sector have the same sign. At x=D the absolute value of any subsum is ≤D(Σ_{a≤D}1/a)². Hence the same sector by itself has a positive increment ≫D²/log²D from D to D², even along the crossings of 134. A sum of absolute bounds by number of primes in t cannot pay for the target. The criterion needs compensation between even and odd sectors; the random theorem controls the correlations when the large prime signs may vary.

**Cross-check.** 135 averages shifts; here the only randomization is arithmetic and the window is fixed. 050 and 060 already group products by squarefree part in other remainders. The pair count and hypercontractivity are used to assess specifically the anchored maximum of 134; no novelty of the probabilistic tools is claimed.
