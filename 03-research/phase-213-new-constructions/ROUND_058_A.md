# The cutoff by size of the quotients gives excesses of both signs

In 057 the reduced slopes a/b were grouped by divisibility ab|P. Here the ordering ab≤R is put to the test. For x=j⁴, R=log x, the **signed** contribution to the criterion of 056 of all pairs with sf(dm)≤R is Ω₊ and Ω₋ of √x log log x. This remains true when excluding d=m. It is an obstruction to cutting the quadratic form; it is not a negative direction of the original form. In 052 a different product was cut, before the inversion that produced the two Möbius indices of 056.

**From the slopes to the coefficient.** Put

    a(r)=μ(r) 2^ω(r) ∏_{p|r} p/(p+1),    A(R)=Σ_{r≤R}a(r)/√r.

For squarefree r there are 2^ω(r) ordered slopes of reduced product r. The individual proof of 057, with the same control in the small-u zone and the same density errors, is summed over all r≤R: the number of slopes is O(R²), all have r≤log x, and the order of decay of the kernel can be chosen in advance. Hence the sum with diagonal is C_diag√x A(R)+O_N(√x/log^N x) for each fixed N. It also holds uniformly up to R≤C log x for each fixed C. In the sum without diagonal the term r=1 is subtracted, with the same precision. The constant C_diag is positive by 057.

**Transform and oscillation.** For Re z>1, F(z)=Σ a(r)r^(−z) has local factor 1−[2p/(p+1)]p^(−z). Thus

    F(z)=H(z)/[ζ(z)²ζ(2z)].

The local factor of H divides the previous one by (1−p^(−z))²(1−p^(−2z)). It differs from one by O(p^(−1−σ)+p^(−3σ)) for σ=Re z>1/3, locally uniformly. On Re z≥1/2 no factor vanishes, since 2√p/(p+1)<1. The convergent product preserves that property. The Laplace transform of A(exp t) on t≥0 is G(s)=F(1/2+s)/s first on Re s>1/2, by integrating the sum. It is regular on the real axis s>0 and at s=0: the zero of 1/ζ(1+2s) compensates the divisor s and ζ(1/2)≠0.

We use here classical results about ζ, without assuming RH. A nonreal zero ρ=1/2+iγ on the critical line gives a pole of order 2m at iγ, where m is its multiplicity; ζ(1+2iγ) is finite and nonzero. Let b≠0 be the leading coefficient of the pole. If A(exp t)≤Ct eventually, for C>0, the transform of Ct−A(exp t), after removing a compact piece, is that of a positive function. As in 038, Landau and regularity on the positive real axis imply convergence on Re s>0. Its absolute value at σ+iγ does not exceed the value at σ, which is C/σ²+O(1). If the order of the pole is two, this requires C≥|b|. If it is larger, it is impossible for every fixed C. Apply also to Ct+A(exp t). One deduces sequences with A(R)≥c log R and with A(R)≤−c log R, for some c>0. The mesh R=4log j ends up running through all intervals [n,n+1) because it is increasing, unbounded and its increments tend to zero. This transfers both sequences to the fourth powers.

**A fixed average of the cutoff does not suffice either.** More generally replace A(exp t) by ∫ψ(v)A(exp(t−v))dv with any real ψ, smooth, of compact support and not identically zero, extending A(exp t)=0 for t<0. The transform is multiplied, up to an entire correction from a finite piece, by P(s)=∫ψ(v)exp(−sv)dv. It is a nonzero entire function of exponential type and has O(T) zeros with multiplicity in |s|≤T (Jensen at a centre where P does not vanish). By the classical Riemann–von Mangoldt formula and symmetry with respect to the critical line, the zeros of ζ with Re ρ≥1/2 and 0<Im ρ≤T sum to ≫T log T with multiplicity. For each of them, at ρ−1/2, the unsmoothed transform has a pole of order 2m. Avoiding all interior poles and reducing all boundary ones to order at most one would require P to contribute at least m zeros there, counting multiplicities. This is impossible for large T. Therefore an interior pole or one of order at least two on the boundary survives. An interior pole already contradicts every one-sided bound Ct by Landau; on the boundary the previous comparison applies. Consequently Ω of both signs persists at the same level.

On the mesh, this last statement uses that the smoothed average has t-derivative of order O(exp((1/2+o(1))t)), by differentiation over ψ and the elementary bound 2^ω(r)≤r^o(1). For t=log(4log j) the increments are O(1/(j log j)), so the oscillation lost when rounding tends to zero. The approximation by slopes of 057 is uniform on the fixed support of the average.

**Conclusion for the sign.** The grouping sf(dm)|P of 057 and the grouping sf(dm)≤log x produce different behaviours. The second cannot be controlled separately with a uniform upper bound of order √x, not even after smoothing the radius with a fixed profile of compact support in its logarithm. Compensation with the remaining slopes would be needed. The tool is the classical Landau oscillation, already present in 028/038; the object to which it is applied here is the series of effective coefficients of 057. The analytic argument is a diagnosis of this route, not a proof of the global sign.
