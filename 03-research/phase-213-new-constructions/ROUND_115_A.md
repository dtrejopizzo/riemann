# Detection in each power interval without a maximum of real parts

Let Δ=sup Reρ−1/2>0. For each 0≤η<Δ, in EVERY sufficiently large window T one has M_A(T)≥exp(2ηT) and M_W(T)≥exp(2ηT), with the notation of phase 159 G2_B5_R5. It is not required that Δ be attained. Hence the existence of a sequence T_j→∞ with M_A(T_j)=O(exp(2ηT_j)) implies Δ≤η, also for the full form. This resolves the sequential converse of §2.3 of that memo at the level of strictly smaller exponents. A positive constant at the extreme exponent and an upper bound for the full floor are not proved.

**Elementary localization lemma.** Let J(t)=Σ_z a_z exp(zt) be real for real t, a_z≠0 grouped by z, Σ|a_z|<∞, |b|=|Re z|≤1/2, no frequency γ=Im z zero and only finitely many z per bounded frequency interval. If some b>d≥0, then for all 0<δ<1 and all T large enough there are points of both signs in [(1−δ)T,(1+δ)T] with |J|>exp(dT).

Take a Gaussian t with mean T and variance cT, with c>0 small depending on δ. Its modulated average I_τ=E[J(t) exp(−iτt)] sums terms of modulus |a_z|exp(T f_τ(z)), where

    f_τ(z)=b+(c/2)[b²−(γ−τ)²].

Exchanging sum and integral is valid by |J| and the absolute majorant (Σ|a_z|)exp(|t|/2). The maximum f₀ exists since it decays quadratically in height. If max f₀≥d, choose τ close to the ordinate of a maximizer: centering it gains cγ²/2>0. If max f₀<d, choose τ close to the ordinate of the z with b>d. In both cases there remains an open set with max f_τ>max(d,max f₀). For c<1 two exponents with the same frequency have distinct rates (b+cb²/2 is increasing); if the frequencies differ, there is at most one tie value of τ. Choose a generic τ in the open set. The maximum f is unique and separated from the second by a positive gap, since outside a finite band the rates tend to −∞. Hence |I_τ|≥(|a_*|/2)exp(fT) eventually, while |I₀|≤(Σ|a_z|)exp(T max f₀).

On the tail |t−T|>δT the integral of exp(|t|/2) is O(exp(−T)) taking c small enough before choosing τ: Cauchy–Schwarz and the normal tail give exponent ≤1/2+c/4−δ²/(4c). Multiply the Gaussian density by 1±cos(τt+φ), choosing the phase φ to obtain averages I₀±|I_τ|. These are non-negative weights of integral ≤2. After removing the tails, the two averages keep opposite signs and moduli ≫exp(fT), which proves the lemma.

**Application to both forms.** Choose η<d<Δ and δ small with d/(1+δ)>η.
