# An exact node at the detecting scale and good blocks at extremes of the prime count

Let U(t)=exp(−t)S(t) with the boxes of 240. The limiting mean is H(1), by the prime number theorem. The following two results give existence statements for this same sum.

**One node.** If B>0, for every continuous step d(t)>0 there are arbitrarily large starting points with S(t+d(t))−exp(d(t))S(t)=0. In particular it holds for d(t)=exp(−(log t)²)/t², taking floor(t²) nodes in 242 (the proof and the limit are identical). If B=0 the explicit formula of 240 already gives differences of size ≤exp(t/2+o(t)) at all those nodes. Thus, unconditionally, there is at least one good node in arbitrarily distant blocks of the detecting scale.

Indeed, when B>0 the function A=S−exp(t)H(1) takes both signs arbitrarily far out. Its one-sided Laplace transform coincides, up to entire prefix terms, with H(s)P(s)−H(1)/(s−1), where P(s)=Σ_p(log p)p^(−s). In Re s>1/2, P differs from −ζ'/ζ by a holomorphic series of prime powers. The transform is regular at every real point s>1/2 (including 1) and has a pole at each zero to the right, since H never vanishes there. If A had an eventual sign, Landau would impose a real singularity at the abscissa of convergence, which lies between the real part of any of those poles and 1. This proves the oscillation. If U(t+d(t))−U(t) had constant sign for all large t, iterating t↦t+d(t) would produce from each starting point a monotone sequence of values of U with limit H(1): the arguments tend to infinity by continuity and positivity of the step. All initial values would have to lie on the same side of H(1), a contradiction. Continuity gives the stated zeros.

**A whole block.** There are κ>0 and arbitrarily large starting points t such that, for h=exp(−t/4+κ√t) and N=ceil(t²), ALL the differences of 242 with step δ=h/N have modulus ≤C exp(t/2), unnormalised. The physical length is asymptotic to X^(3/4)exp(κ√log X). In the case B>0, the crossings already proved and Rolle give starting points with U'(t)=0. The classical PNT bound |θ(x)−x|≤Cx exp(−c√log x) implies |U''(u)|≤C exp(−c'√u): write U−H(1)=exp(−u)∫W(log x−u)d(θ(x)−x), differentiate twice and integrate by parts in x. The derivatives of the weight up to order three are bounded and x runs over [exp(u),exp(u+1/10)]. Thus on all of [t,t+h], |U'|≤Ch exp(−c'√t). The unnormalised differences are exp(t+(k+1)δ) times increments of U and cost ≤C exp(t−c'√t)h²/N. It suffices to choose 2κ<c'. In the case B=0 the same estimate by parts for U' uses θ(x)−x=O(√x log²x) under RH and gives, at every starting point, a cost ≤C exp(t/2)t²δ, smaller than required.

**Distance to closure.** The first result constructs one node at the correct scale; 242 asks for of the order of t/log²t in the same block. The second obtains all nodes by reducing the length. The arguments use respectively a return equality of the count and a second-order tangency; they do not guarantee multiple simultaneous returns on the mesh of 242. Rounds 237–238 worked with another observable and another energy, so their numerical lengths are not comparable as one and the same bound.
