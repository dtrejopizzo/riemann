# An explicit frequency and the first order that detects each zero

Take the character and the self-convolution b_t of 292 and fix t=0. For a nonnegative integer k, c_k(n)=[t^(2k)] b_t(n), the Taylor coefficient, and C_k(x)=Σ_(n≤x)c_k(n). The global sign is equivalent to the one-sided ceilings

    C_k(x) ≤ O_(k,ε)(x^(1/2+ε)) for each fixed k and each ε>0.

No uniformity in k is required. The row k=0 alone already detects every exterior zero of ζ whose multiplicity exceeds its multiplicity in L(s,χ). More precisely, if their respective multiplicities are r>0 and m≥0, the first k producing a pole is max(0,m−r+1). In particular rows 0 and 1 would suffice if m≤r at every common exterior zero. This comparison of multiplicities is not established.

**Local order.** Use the factorization of 292 in a neighborhood of (s,t)=(ρ,0), with cos(t log p) continued holomorphically. There G_t(s)² is a unit: at t=0 it could vanish only if p^(Re ρ)=2 for a prime p≡2 mod 3, impossible for 1/2<Re ρ<1 (p=2 gives 1 and p≥5 gives less than 1/2). The remaining correction factors do not vanish. With h=s−ρ, the full numerator is written (h²+t²)^m U(h,t) with U a holomorphic unit, even in t. For k≤m, its coefficient of order 2k has exact order 2(m−k) in h: the minimal term is binom(m,k)U(0,0)h^(2(m−k)). The denominator has order 2r. This proves the formula even when both zeros are multiple. On the real line s>1/2 the quotient of each row is holomorphic, including s=1. A one-sided ceiling prevents any pole to its right by the same Landau argument of 292 applied to the partial sum of the row. If there is an exterior zero, some finite row contradicts the ceiling.

**Under RH.** Fix σ₀>1/2 and take a complex circle |t|=d with 2d<σ₀−1/2. The local tail of G² after cancelling the linear term is O(p^(−2σ₀+2d)), uniformly summable, and the finite factors are bounded in the strip of integration. The two shifted L-functions are evaluated at real part ≥σ₀−d>1/2; the integrated bound for the product via the second moment and Cauchy–Schwarz of 292 remains uniform on the circle. The estimate under RH for the reciprocal of ζ applies in the same strip as before. Cauchy's formula for extracting the coefficient costs d^(−2k), fixed. Shifting the smoothed Mellin integral as in 292 even gives |C_k(x)|≪_(k,ε)x^(1/2+ε).

**Arithmetic support.** At a prime with extended χ equal to +1 (including 3), u_p=1−cos(t log p) starts at order two. The factor of b_t(p^e) starts at order 2e for e=1,2. Hence c_k vanishes if the sum of the exponents of those primes exceeds k; it still vanishes on multiples of cubes. For k=0 only primes p≡2 mod 3 remain, with local values −4 and 4 at p and p². Each row thus admits an explicit sum over integers with a bounded number of factors from the other class. The reduction identifies which zeros will remain invisible when a finite number of rows is proved; the ceiling is not yet obtained even for the first.
