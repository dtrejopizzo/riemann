# Unweighted Chebyshev oscillations at sparse nodes

Fix any integer k≥2, Θ=sup Reρ. If Θ>1/2, at the nodes x=m^k the two parts (θ(x)−x)_± have upper exponent Θ. More precisely, for each sign and all sufficiently small δ>0 there are arbitrarily distant dyadic bands [R,2R] with ≫ R^(1/k−3δ) nodes of that sign and deviation ≥R^(Θ−2δ). The bands may be different for the two signs.

Thus it suffices to prove, for a fixed k and a single sign, (θ(m^k)−m^k)_± ≪ε (m^k)^(1/2+ε) for all ε>0 in order to close the global sign. The pointwise ceiling can be replaced by the same ceiling for the mean over the nodes of each dyadic band. Under RH both bounds follow from PNT with error O(√x log²x). This criterion uses the raw values of θ; 039 uses a weighted sum of Λ and a tail constant, and 125 uses matrices of autocorrelations.

**Continuation of the rounded measure.** Push dψ−dv on [1,∞) to the right endpoint r(v) of the cell [m^k,(m+1)^k) containing v. For Re s>1, the Mellin transform of the pushed measure is −ζ'/ζ(s)−1/(s−1)+E(s). The error E is holomorphic in Re s>Θ−1/(2k). To see this, apply the local estimate of 125 to q=r(v)^(−s)−v^(−s) on [R,2R). On compact sets of s, for σ=Re s one can use A=C R^(−σ−1/k), J=C R^(1/k), with piecewise slope O(AJ) in log(v/R); the same argument works for complex functions. The local bound is O(polylog(R)(R^(Θ−σ−1/(2k))+R^(−σ))). It guarantees normal convergence there since Θ−1/(2k)>0. The non-real poles to the right of that line survive and the positive real axis in that region is regular, including s=1. It is not necessary that a zero with real part exactly Θ exist.

**Why both signs appear many times.** The pushed cumulative function D(x) is ψ(m^k−)−m^k+1 on [m^k,(m+1)^k). Its integral against x^(−s−1) is the above transform divided by s, initially by parts for Re s>1. Replacing D by θ(m^k)−m^k adds O(√x log²x), holomorphic after integrating in Re s>1/2. Call this last step function d(x). The non-negative integrals of d_+ and d_− against x^(−s−1) both have abscissa of convergence exactly Θ. Indeed, they are at most Θ by the classical PNT bound with exponent Θ+ε. If both were smaller, their difference would be holomorphic in some half-plane with boundary to the left of Θ, contradicting the poles with real part arbitrarily close to Θ. If only one were smaller, its holomorphy near the real point Θ and the regularity there of the difference would continue the other across its real abscissa, contradicting Landau for a non-negative function.

Therefore, on arbitrarily distant dyadic bands the integral mean of each part is ≥R^(Θ−δ), since an eventual bound below that threshold would force a smaller abscissa. The cells over the band have length O_k(R^(1−1/k)). The nodes with deviation less than R^(Θ−2δ) contribute o(R^(1+Θ−δ)) to the integral. Each remaining node contributes O(R^(1−1/k+Θ+δ)) by the pointwise ceiling. There are at least ≫ R^(1/k−2δ) remaining nodes, discounting at most one cell at the left edge. This implies the statement with margin 3δ. The claim controls an abundance exponent along subsequences, not a uniform positive density.
