# Smooth-divisor threshold for removing terms at root cost

In the simple-prime term P_h of 353 fix 1<A≤2, y=(log X)^A and H=hX. For H≥1 remove all pairs (p,m) such that m has a squarefree divisor, with primes ≤y, of size at least H^(A/2). The **pointwise absolute** cost of the whole removal on X≤x≤2X is O_(A,ε)(√H X^ε) for each ε>0. In particular it suffices to control the balance of the remaining pairs in the norm of 353. For A=3/2, for example, all cofactors with a divisor of this type ≥H^(3/4) can be removed, even if they have other arbitrarily large factors.

**Proof.** In each removed product n=pm there exists a squarefree smooth divisor d in [H^(A/2)/y,H^(A/2)]: multiply primes of the admissible divisor until reaching the threshold and, if it is exceeded, take the immediately preceding product. For H=1 d=1 works. Since d≤H, the interval of the pulse contains O(H/d) multiples. For each n the sum of weights in modulus over all possible p costs ≤log(2X). For 0<α<1/A, Rankin gives

    Σ_{d smooth, sf, d≥H^(A/2)/y} 1/d
       ≤ (y/H^(A/2))^α ∏_{p≤y}(1+p^(−1+α)).

The logarithm of the product is O_α(y^α)=o(log X), even using the sum over all integers. The factor y^α log X is also X^o(1). The bound is therefore H^(1−Aα/2) X^o(1); choose α close enough to 1/A depending on ε, using H≤X. For 0<H<1, without specific truncation, the norm of any subsum is O(√H log X): there are O(1) samples per center, weight per n ≤log(2X), and the integral of the number of samples in dx/x is O(hX). This covers all widths of 353. The removed term need not be small compared with √H: the budget is proved with the loss X^ε admitted by the criterion.

**Optimality of the exponent for a proof by moduli.** With the same A, any θ<A/2 in place of A/2 can exceed the root by a power, already at h=1. Choose max(θ,0)<β<A/2. Take k=⌊β log X/log y⌋ and d as a product of k distinct primes of [y/2,y]. Each d=X^(β+o(1)) exceeds X^θ. There are X^(β(1−1/A)+o(1)) such products: the interval contains ≍y/log y primes, and the logarithm of the binomial coefficient is k(log((y/log y)/k)+O(1)). For each d add a prime q in [1.1X/d,1.2X/d]. By the prime number theorem there are X^(1−β+o(1)) choices uniformly; q is the only prime >y of n=qd. Hence one obtains X^(1−β/A+o(1)) distinct arguments in [1.1X,1.2X], all inside the pulse for 1.2X≤x≤2X, except irrelevant endpoints. The pair p=q,m=d is admissible and has weight log q. Even when gathering the removed representations of a single n, all have the same sign since n is squarefree. The exponent 1−β/A is greater than 1/2. The optimality refers to the absolute cost uniform over all widths; it is not an obstacle to cancellation between distinct products.

**Relation with 309 and 355.** 309 identifies the scale log²X in a different Euler product norm. 355 detects an intermediate divisor to save logarithms with a larger smoothness threshold. Here varying the smoothness with A allows removing a whole sector of the simple primes at the root budget, with optimal frontier for this count. The obligation on all cofactors whose product of distinct small primes is below the threshold remains.
