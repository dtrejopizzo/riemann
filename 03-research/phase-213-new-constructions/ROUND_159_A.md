# A flat weight that beats the Gaussian prefix barrier and keeps detection

In the average of 155 replace the Gaussian exp(−πt²) by the following weight, positive on its support. Let U_j be independent uniform on [−j^(−2),j^(−2)], B=Σ_j U_j, A=Σ j^(−2), w(t)=Pr(B≥log t). It is one for t≤exp(−A) and zero for t≥exp(A). For any fixed d in (0,1/4] the ceiling O(√x) of the centred average with this weight is still equivalent to the sign. Now the error of replacement by integral plus constant, summed in modulus over the WHOLE prefix r≤x/(K log² x), is O(1) with fixed K sufficiently large. This beats x^(5/6) for sufficiently large x.

**Detection.** The Mellin transform is W(s)=E exp(sB)/s on Re s>0, i.e. the product of sinh(s/j²)/(s/j²) divided by s. Each factor has only imaginary zeros and the product converges locally with tail 1+O_s(Σ j^(−4)); W does not vanish on Re s>0. In fixed vertical strips it decays faster than any power by integration by parts in the transform of the compact density of B. In 155 H is replaced by W(s)ζ(2s). With L_d and L_0 recomputed as residues with that factor, the same contour and Landau proof applies literally on Re s≥1/4. Thus the change of weight preserves detection at all orders of multiplicity.

**Quantified regularity.** The k-th derivative of the distribution function of B is bounded in sup norm by C^k(k!)²: differentiate the first k−1 uniform densities in the sense of measures (total norms j²), use the sup norm k²/2 of the next density and convolve the remaining probabilities. The derivatives are continuous by smoothing with more factors. Composing with log t keeps that bound on the fixed transition interval. Indeed in the composition formula divided by N!, for k outer derivatives the ordered compositions of N into k parts give at most C^N binom(N−1,k−1) terms after using |(log t)^(l)|/l!≤C^l on the interval; the outer factor is ≤C^k k!. This gives C₁^N(N!)² after summing and multiplying by N!.

**Quadrature.** Fix 0<d≤1/4 and y=x/r. In the sum Σ m^(−d)w(m/y) the integral term is y^(1−d)W(1−d). For g(t)=t^(−d)(w(t)−1), vanishing near zero, one has ||g^(N)||₁≤C^N(N!)² for N≥2. On the transition use Leibniz and the previous estimate. In the tail it is the derivative of −t^(−d), integrable with that bound. Euler–Maclaurin gives

    |Σ m^(−d)w(m/y) − y^(1−d)W(1−d) − ζ(d)|
        ≤ y^(1−d−N) · 2ζ(N)/(2π)^N · ||g^(N)||₁.

To justify the use with non-integrable g, truncate the sum and the integral of m^(−d)(w(m/y)−1) at an integer L. The difference of the sum and the integral of m^(−d) up to L tends to ζ(d); the boundary terms of g at L/y tend to zero. At d=0 the same inequality for the weight w is obtained directly with Euler–Maclaurin: the boundary term at zero is −1/2=ζ(0) and the derivatives there are zero. Take N of order κ√y with κ small and use N!≤N^N. For y≥1 one gets C y exp(−c√y), for both weights d and 0. For y≥K log² x the bound is O(x^(−2)) on choosing K large; Σ_{r≤x}b(r)=O(x) by 157. This even proves o(1) for the total error of both prefixes.

**Cross-check and decision.** 055 and 061 already used Gevrey regularity to truncate arithmetic sums, and 129–134 used smoothing of dilations. The direct antecedent is 047: it already combines convolution of uniforms, Gevrey regularity of order two and detection without zeros. What is specific here is the quadrature of the singular weight m^(−d) with its constant ζ(d) inside criterion 155. With this the two-sided obstruction of 158 is confined to the Gaussian choice. The integrals and constants on the prefix still contain b(r); controlling their centring and the region x/(K log² x)<r≤exp(A)x is still necessary. The quadrature bound is unconditional and gives no global ceiling.
