# What mass alone loses between two fourth powers

**Obstruction for the data of 123.** There is a single positive density, between 1/2 and 3/2, with exactly the Lebesgue mass on each cell [m⁴,(m+1)⁴), whose quadratic discrepancy on primitive even unit vectors grows like exp(T/2). It holds even in the Dirichlet spaces of 122. This confirms the need for additional information if one intends to deduce the bound from mass alone and positivity. The model is a measure in the prime variable; it lacks the multiplicative structure of Λ.

Take density 3/2 on the first half of each cell and 1/2 on the second, and call D its difference from Lebesgue on [1,∞). With the notation f(v)=v^(−1/2)c_F(log v) of 123, for every real even unit vector with derivative norm ≤B one has

    ∫ f dD = (3/8) Φ_F(1/4)² + O((1+B²)(1+T)),
    Φ_F(s)=∫exp(st)F(t)dt.

Indeed, the first centered moment of D on a cell of length h and left endpoint l is −h²/8. Taylor gives −h²f'(l)/8 with remainder O(h³ sup|f''|). Replacing the leading term by −(1/2)∫cell v^(3/4) f'(v)dv costs O(h³ sup|f''| + h l^(1/2) sup|f'|), since h=4l^(3/4)+O(l^(1/2)). The bounds of 123 and |f'|≪(1+B)v^(−3/2) make the total error O((1+B²)(1+T)) when summing over dyadic bands (in each band of scale R there are O(R^(1/4)) cells). Integration by parts leaves (1/2)f(1)+(3/8)∫₀∞exp(u/4)c_F(u)du. The integral over the negative half-line with that weight is O(1) by |c_F|≤1. The two-sided integral factors as Φ_F(1/4)² by parity.

**Primitive test.** Choose a smooth even nonzero function ψ≥0 supported in (−1,1), set g=(−∂²+1/4)ψ, and add two copies of g centered at ±(T−2). After normalizing, the vector F has Φ_F(1/2)=0, bounded H¹ norm and |Φ_F(1/4)| comparable to exp(T/4), since Φ_g(1/4)=(3/16)Φ_ψ(1/4)>0. For the passage to finitely many modes, project spectrally both F and the pair of copies of ψ at the same positions. Their L² errors are O(B_cut^−1) by Parseval of the derivative, and their H¹ norms stay bounded. The moment error at fixed s>0 is O_s(exp(sT)/B_cut) by Cauchy–Schwarz. The second projected vector has moment at 1/2 comparable to exp(T/2), so subtracting a multiple O(1/B_cut) suffices to correct exactly the moment of the first. The lower bound at 1/4 and a norm comparable to one are preserved by taking B_cut→∞ as in 122. For the block (X^a,X] with fixed a<1 one can use a sequence X with X^a a cell node, avoiding the lower partial cell. The upper endpoint requires no cutoff because f=0 above X. The portion up to X^a along that sequence costs O((1+B²)(1+T)+ (1+B)X^(a/4)) by the same expansion before integrating by parts.

**Precise scope.** This particular bias induces a dominant rank-one term. Imposing the additional moment Φ_F(1/4)=0 removes it. That does not remove the first moment of the cells of the real primes, whose bias may change between cells. Antecedents: 104_90/92/101 already exclude general arguments by positivity and PNT; R166_702 alters prime positions. Here all masses on the mesh of 123 are equalized and the defect is computed on the hyperplane of 122 itself.
