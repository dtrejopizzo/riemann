# Positivity for up to 32 arbitrary real poles over the whole admissible range

For any list of 1≤n≤32 rates v_j>1/2, even repeated, and any nonzero complex polynomial of degree <n, the causal inverse of F(s)=P(s)/D(s), D(s)=∏(s+v_j), has W(F)>0. No bound on rates, separation or sum of reciprocals is required. In particular the case of five arbitrary powers of phase 211 is closed. The verified height G=3·10¹² and Z3 of paper 47 are used. No common L² margin at infinite order is obtained.

**Exact representation.** Write z=ρ−1/2. The Hadamard factorization of the centred, even ξ gives M(v)=Σ_z 1/(v−z) with pairing z,−z (the linear term of the centred factorization disappears by parity). By the symmetry and partial fractions, K(u,v)=(M(u)+M(v))/(u+v)=Σ_z [(u−z)(v+z)]⁻¹ with absolutely convergent sum. By 001, for any complex combination of exponentials the value of the form is therefore Σ_z F(−z) overline(F(conj z)). Powers of denominators are obtained by divided differences or differentiation, locally dominated by C/(1+γ²) in this identity; the real poles of F lie outside the strip. The identity also follows by polarization in the partial-fraction basis. Each summand can be written as F(iγ−δ) overline(F(iγ+δ)), with δ=Re z and γ the ordinate with sign changed. |δ|≤1/2. It suffices to consider real parts. Up to G both factors coincide by Z2.

**Counting in a high band.** On each half-axis of absolute heights >G use bands (A,A+H], H=10⁹. For every subinterval of that band of length h≥100, its count (open on the left, closed on the right) is ≥h log(A)/10; the total count is ≤H log(A)/4. Indeed, for t∈[A,A+H], the main derivative of Z3, log(t/(2π))/(2π), lies between 0.14 log A and 0.17 log A. Each endpoint error 0.137 log t+0.443 log log t+1.588 is ≤log A for A≥G, using t≤1.001A. Subtracting two errors costs ≤2 log A. Reflection of the ordinates allows the same count on the negative band.

**Variation of the numerator.** For each sign of the height put p(t)=P(±it) and B=max_[A,A+H]|p|>0. By Markov's inequality on an interval and its iteration, sup|p^(j)|≤b^j B, b=2·31²/H<2·10⁻⁶. It also holds with complex coefficients, rotating the phase of the derivative at the point and taking the real part. Taylor in δ gives |P(±it±δ)−P(±it)|≤(exp(b/2)−1)B<1.1·10⁻⁶ B. Next to a maximizer there is an interval of length h=H/(8·31²)>100 where |p|≥3B/4: choose toward the available side of length ≥H/2. This includes maxima occurring at the edges.

**Denominator and comparison.** For |γ|=t≥G the factors of D(±it)/D(±it±δ) are (1+e_j)⁻¹ with |e_j|≤1/(2G). The product differs from one in modulus by ≤(1−1/(2G))⁻³²−1<10⁻¹⁰ (geometric series of the inverses). Consequently both shifted evaluations of F differ from F(±it) by at most 1.2·10⁻⁶ B/|D(±it)|. Multiplying, the real part of each summand is ≥(|p(t)|²−3·10⁻⁶B²)/|D(±it)|². Within the band the modulus of D varies at most by the factor exp(32H/G)<1.02 because |d log|v_j±it|/dt|≤1/t. Call D_min its minimum there. Summing the positive terms over the preceding subinterval and paying the error over the whole band one obtains the lower bound

    (B/D_min)² H log A [ (3/4)²/(1.02²·10·8·31²) − 3·10⁻⁶/4 ] > 0.

The final inequality is strict by rational arithmetic. Each high band has strictly positive sum, without claiming a term-by-term sign. The lower verified bands only add squares. Absolute convergence allows gathering everything and concludes the proof.

**Difference and limit.** The unit count of 175 and the use of verified zeros are antecedents. Here one works in the numerator of the rational transform over bands of ordinates, exploiting that its degree is bounded, and the real denominator has uniform relative variation even with arbitrary rates. This avoids extrapolating the concavity of 085 or running the Schur tower. The method explicitly uses the degree in the Markov bounds and in the length of the good subinterval; the fixed constants do not work for all orders. The global sign and that of new windows do not follow from this argument.

**Exact obstruction to passing to unbounded degree band by band.** For a finite band that contained a zero off the line, the set of evaluation points is finite, closed under the exchange between the two abscissae ±δ at the same ordinate (equal multiplicities by symmetry). Choose values 1 and −1 for a single pair of nonzero abscissae and zero at all other distinct points of the band. Fix v=1, choose n at least equal to the number of distinct points and, with D=(s+1)^n, interpolate P at those points so that F=P/D takes the prescribed values. The degree is <n. Thus the band sum is strictly negative, even without counting the rest of the form. If all zeros of the band were critical, the sum would be nonnegative in any degree. Hence, proving separate positivity of each high band at all degrees would require proving that each contains only critical zeros; this is not obtained by uniform optimization of Markov constants nor by the ordinate count alone. This local falsifier does not give a negative datum for the global sum.
