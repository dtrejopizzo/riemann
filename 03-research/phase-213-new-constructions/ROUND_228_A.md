# An average of squares in the arithmetic variable measures the absolute horizontal displacement

Let X=exp(2N), g_N(t)=exp(−t²/(4N))/√(4πN), and dν_N(x)=g_N(log x)(dψ(x)−dx) restricted to [1,X]. Define the accumulated deficit P_N(r)=ν_N([1,min(r,X)]) and

    V_N = (1/2)∫₁^∞ |P_N(r)|² dr/r² + (1/2)|∫₁^X dν_N(x)/x|².

For B=sup_ρ |Re ρ−1/2| one obtains the ordinary limit log(V_N)/(2N)→B². Under RH moreover V_N→0. If RH fails, V_N grows exponentially for all large N, even if no zero attains B. All prime powers enter with their authentic weight. The observable does not need to compute any ordinate nor integrate the POWER rate. A subexponential ceiling along a subsequence would suffice.

**Exact transformation.** Let A_N(T)=∫ x^(−1/2) cos(T log x)dν_N(x). Integrate it squared with the probability weight dλ(T)=dT/[2π(T²+1/4)]. Its transform is exp(−|t|/2). By the product of cosines, the kernel after including (xy)^(−1/2) is exactly (1/max(x,y)+1/(xy))/2. The identity 1/max(x,y)=∫_(max(x,y))^∞ dr/r² and Fubini for finite measures give ∫|A_N|²dλ=V_N, with the tail r>X included. The second summand of V_N is O(1) uniformly: ∫ g_N(log x)dψ(x)/x≤C by Chebyshev and partial summation, and the continuous integral satisfies the same.

**Explicit formula.** For completed zeta set δ=Re ρ−1/2, γ=Im ρ and
    b_N(T)=Σ_ρ exp(−N(γ−iδ−T)²).
By the symmetries of the zeros this sum is real and coincides with the even test obtained by averaging the translations ±T. Its inverse transform is g_N(t)cos(Tt). In the convention of 208 the explicit formula gives b_N=−2A_N+O(log(2+|T|)/√N). In detail: the poles contribute h(i/2)+h(−i/2)=2∫_ℝ exp(t/2)g_N(t)cos(Tt)dt for the even test h. On t≥0 the continuous term is the one subtracted in A_N, and t<0 costs O(N^(−1/2)). For t≥2N the product exp(t/2) exp(−t²/(4N)) is ≤exp(−(t−2N)/2). The continuous tail and the prime tail cost O(N^(−1/2)), the latter grouping by unit intervals of log x and using ψ(y)≤Cy. The archimedean density is O(log(2+|τ|)), so its integral against the translated Gaussians is O(log(2+|T|)/√N). The error has L²(dλ) norm of order N^(−1/2).

**Ceiling and floor.** The unit counting of zeros gives |b_N(T)|≤C log(2+|T|) exp(NB²), on summing exp(−(γ−T)²) for N≥1. Under RH there is pointwise convergence to zero off the ordinates and C log(2+|T|) dominates in L²(dλ). Around the first ordinate, b_N≥exp(−1) for |T−γ₁|≤1/√N under RH because all terms are positive; the explicit error there tends uniformly to zero. Thus V_N≳N^(−1/2) under RH, which gives rate zero from both sides.

Off RH, fix 0<b<B and a zero with |δ|>b. Near its ordinate some parabola δ²−(γ−T)² exceeds b² with margin. Only finitely many pairs (γ,|δ|) compete for the maximum in that neighbourhood; distant heights stay uniformly below. Two distinct parabolas cross at most once, and for the same height and distinct |δ| they never cross. Choose a compact subinterval of positive length where a single pair dominates with gap η>0 and minimal exponent >b². The dominant term is 2m exp(N[δ²−(γ−T)²]) cos(2Nδ(γ−T)), with |δ|>0. The others add up, uniformly, to at most a constant times the dominant exponential multiplied by exp(−(N−1)η), using the unit count for the first-moment factor. On a fraction of the interval bounded from below the cosine has modulus ≥1/2, for all large N. The weight dλ is positive there. One deduces liminf log(V_N)/(2N)≥b²; let b↑B. The height of the interval may depend on b, never on N.

**Comparison and limit.** 145 already used Gaussians and residue rates, and 208 recovered B by taking suprema over rates. 210 gives a quadratic average with the rate of the relative displacement. Here the Gaussian translation and the specific choice of the Cauchy weight eliminate the frequency integral exactly in favour of squares of an accumulated sum, with a single limit for absolute B. The square makes V_N≥0 trivially. What would decide Weil positivity is the growth CEILING of V_N, which this identity does not prove from arithmetic.
