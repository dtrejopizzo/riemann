# Scattered deletions and the cost of inverting the barycentre

The weight of 247 satisfies k(z)=1/3−3(z−2/3)². If deleting primes before q is allowed, a logarithmic mass of order √q located near 2q/3 can exceed the margin of the squares. The effect admits a simultaneous construction at infinitely many scales, even forbidding deletions of consecutive primes.

**Example with the true primes.** At q=99991, exactly 30 deletions are needed to reach C(q)<0. Any set of 29 leaves C(q)>2.64. There are 30 primes between 67247 and 67547 inclusive whose removal leaves C(q)<−1.06. It is obtained with the integers of verify_247.py: for each prime form the interval [l_p,u_p], the product of the two integer bounds for log p by (q−p)(3p−q), reordering the endpoints by sign. From the sum of lower endpoints subtract the 29 largest upper endpoints. For the negative bound subtract from the sum of upper endpoints the 30 largest lower endpoints. On dividing by 10^20 q² the two results lie respectively in (2.6434,2.6435) and (−1.0608,−1.0607).

**A single deletion with unconditional oscillation.** For any ε>0 one can choose a set D of primes, without touching any prescribed prefix, with these properties:

* The weighted count of 247 restricted to the primes outside D takes both signs arbitrarily far out.
* On every sequence of intervals containing a number of primes tending to infinity, the fraction removed tends to zero.
* The multiplicative factor P_D(s)=∏_(r∈D)(1−r^(−s)) satisfies sup_(Re s≥1/2)|log P_D(s)|<ε with the branch defined by the logarithm series. It is holomorphic and nonzero in the open half-plane and continuous and nonzero in the closed one. The Euler product of the remaining system preserves the zeros of ζ in Re s>1/2, with multiplicities.

Take X_j=2^(2^j), starting at a large j. In |r−2X_j/3|≤X_j/log X_j there are N_j≫X_j/log²X_j primes by 238. In the ordered list choose those with indices i floor(N_j/m_j), i=1,...,m_j, with m_j=ceil(1.1√X_j/log X_j). The gaps in indices between successive deletions tend to infinity even when passing from one band to the next. Outside a finite prefix, an interval with n primes contains at most 1+n/K deletions for each fixed separation K admissible there. Letting K grow proves the second claim. The accumulated deleted mass up to x is O(√x), by the separation X_(j+1)=X_j² and the cost O(√X_j) of each band. On the other hand Σ_D 1/√r≪Σ 1/log X_j, with arbitrarily small tail, which proves the claim on the Euler factor through the absolute series of log(1−z).

If RH fails, the two-sided excursions of 249 of order greater than √x survive the cost O(√x). Under RH, C(X_j)/√X_j≤4/15+0.094+o(1). The deletions of the band itself are worth (1.1/3+o(1))√X_j, the earlier ones cost o(√X_j) in modulus and the later ones do not intervene. Thus the new sign is negative at X_j. At 3X_j all primes removed so far have k(r/(3X_j))≤0 and the genuine sum is already positive. This gives the two signs. By contrast, under RH any deletion of accumulated mass o(√x) preserves the eventual sign by 247 and |k|≤1.

**Scope and antecedent.** R163_102, section 4, already records which zeros an Euler product with scattered deletions preserves. The additional step is to invert this barycentre at infinitely many scales with zero relative density of deletions even on moving intervals. The Euler factor can be uniformly close to one, but Σ_D(log r)/√r diverges: each band contributes an amount tending to 1.1√(3/2). The construction therefore does not preserve control of the logarithmic derivative on the boundary. Nor is it a counterexample for the intact primes. It rules out a justification of the sign based solely on the properties of relative abundance and stability of the product preserved here.
