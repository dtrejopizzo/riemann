# Iteration of the narrowing with sufficiently high critical zeros

**Status: PROVED, with independent review of the stability lemma, the start and the scope of the iteration.**

The operation of 407 admits an infinite sequence of distinct critical pairs for which **each finite stage** preserves a positive kernel strictly decreasing on v>0. RH is not required. The height threshold may depend on the kernel of each stage.

**Stability lemma.** Let f be even, smooth, with f>0, f'(v)<0 for v>0, f''(0)<0. Assume that for each j≥0, (−1)^j f^(j)(v)>0 for v sufficiently large (the threshold depends on j), and that each derivative decays doubly exponentially. For a real zero γ of its Fourier transform define Tγ f as in 407. If γ is sufficiently large, Tγ f satisfies the same properties.

On the half-line, for each j, two integrations by parts give

    (Tγ f)^(j)(v) = f^(j)(v) − γ^(−1)∫₀^∞ f^(j+2)(v+t) sin(γt)dt.

The difference in uniform norm is ≤‖f^(j+2)‖₁/γ. The even extension is smooth by the differential equation of 407 and the zero-derivative condition at the origin. For fixed large R, first keep the strictly negative curvature on [0,r] with small r>0, then the strictly negative first derivative on [r,R], by the previous bounds and compactness. On [R,∞) choose R such that −f'>0 and f''>0 there: the half-period argument applied to −f' gives −(Tγ f)'>0 without any height requirement. The same proof for (−1)^j f^(j), using also the derivative of order j+1, gives the eventual sign of every transformed derivative. The sine representation and the decay of the derivatives preserve the tail bounds at each fixed step. Positivity on the whole axis by 407.

**Start for Φ.** The monotonicity is that of 407. In the theta formula, differentiating j times each term is exp(v/2−r) P_j(r), r=πn²exp(2v), with P₀=4r²−6r and P_(j+1)=(1/2−2r)P_j+2rP'_j. Its leading coefficient is 4(−2)^j; this gives an eventual sign simultaneously in n and the doubly exponential tail for each j. At the origin P₂=16r⁴−112r³+165r²−(75/2)r. The term n=1 is less than −16 (substitution with 3.14159<π<3.14160). For n≥2, P₂(r)≤18r⁴; the sum of these majorants times exp(−r) is less than 2: the first is <1.6 and the successive ratio is ≤(3/2)^8 exp(−5π)<0.001. Thus Φ''(0)<0.

**Review record.** The uniform bound for the derivative j uses the complete L¹ norm of f^(j+2) and is valid on the whole half-line. To keep monotonicity near0 the approximation of the second derivative is needed: f''≤−c on[0,r], followed by(Tγf)'(0)=0. On[r,R] the first derivative is approximated with a compact margin; on the tail half-periods are applied to−f', positive and strictly decreasing by f''>0. For eachj only this last argument is repeated beyond an R_j, using simultaneously the signs of f^(j) and f^(j+1); a single initial height controlling all orders on compact sets is not required. The integrable tails justify both integrations by parts and the differential equation propagates the even smoothness at0.

The recurrence forP_j reproduces P₂=16r⁴−112r³+165r²−(75/2)r and its leading coefficient4(−2)^j. For n≥2, r≥4π and the majorant18r⁴ has successive ratio≤(3/2)^8exp(−5π)<0.001; the first term<1.6 gives tail<2. Together with the term n=1<−16 it fixes Φ''(0)<0 with margin. Thus the start does not depend on a conjecture about the curvature. Each new critical height is chosen after the threshold of the kernel already constructed and excluding the previous pairs; Hardy guarantees enough zeros for each stage. No threshold uniform in the number of steps is extracted.

**Choice and scope.** By Hardy's theorem there are critical zeros of arbitrarily large height. Each division removes only the chosen pair, so another zero above the threshold of the lemma and the previously used heights can be chosen. At each finite step 407 gives the same negative equivalent: the additional multiplier at the saddle tends to one and the cost of the poles on the line is at most a fixed power of b. The variance after k steps is V₀−Σ_{j≤k}2/γ_j². It is not proved that it is possible to remove the critical zeros in increasing order nor that this variance tends to zero. In fact, if the first zero γ₁ is reserved, its characteristic function keeps vanishing at γ₁ after each step, so that, for the measure normalized by its constant mass, 1=E(1−cos(γ₁v))≤γ₁²E(v²)/2. This sequence keeps a positive floor of variance and does not justify a pointwise limit for the sign.
