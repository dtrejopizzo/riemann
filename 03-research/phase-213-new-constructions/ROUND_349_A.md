# A single pairing leaves a sparse residue and preserves coverage at each center

Work directly with S_h of 337, X≤x≤2X, h=X^(−δ), H=Xh, δ=1/j, j≥16. There exist a translated partition into cells of length J=⌊X^(9/20)⌋ and a pairing of integers with opposite signs of μ within each cell such that:

1. O_A(X/(log X)^A) squarefree integers in [X/2,3X] remain unpaired, for each fixed A.
2. The signed balance of all pairs is O_η(J)=o(√H) at each center. At each center the pairs cover absolute mass ≫H. Except for a set of centers of measure O_A(X/(log X)^A), the unpaired mass is O_A(H/(log X)^A).
3. The second-moment bound of 337 is equivalent to the same bound on the residues alone. They can be replaced by a single integer coefficient per cell, equal to its exact imbalance.

**Choosing the mesh.** Partition [X/2,3X] by a translate of the grid of step J, with two truncated boundary cells. Minimize the sum of |Σ_cell μ|. The average over translations in [0,J] is equivalent, for whole cells, to integrating the imbalances over the initial positions and dividing by J. By 329 on a bounded number of dyadic blocks, this average is O_A(X/(log X)^A) for every fixed A; the ends cost O(J). The same minimizer serves for all exponents. In each cell pair as many opposite signs as possible. The residual quantity is exactly the imbalance in modulus. [Möbius estimate in short intervals used in 329](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05770v1)

**Cost of moving mass.** Two samples of a cell meeting the support of the pulse have difference of weights O_η(J/H), by differentiation with respect to the real integer. The activated cells contain O(H+J) samples in total. Hence the balance O(J) uniformly. The same computation allows placing all the signed mass at the center of each cell, with weight equal to Σ_cell μ. The second moment with budget H X^ε is preserved in both directions by the triangle inequality in L²(dx/x), since J=o(√H). Integrate the residual absolute mass with respect to dx: each residual integer costs O_η(H) in the integral. Using the global cardinality bound with exponent 2A+2 and Markov gives the announced exceptional estimate. The absolute mass of all squarefree integers in the pulse is ≍H uniformly by elementary counting, since H≫√X.

**Coverage even at exceptional centers.** In 348 the pairs reaching this range have distance in integers ≤O(ZY^(1/4))=O(X^(7/16)), writing Y=X/Z as there. Denote this last bound by D. In a given pulse, the pairs of 348 crossing some edge of the grid contribute at most O_η((H/J+1)(D+1))=o(H), since they are disjoint and their members are at distance ≤D from an edge. The other pairs of 348 provide mass ≫H in individual cells. The maximum pairing of the cell uses at least as many integers as those internal pairs. To compare their weights, replace each weight by the minimum on the cell: the aggregate error costs O_η(J) by the same variation as before. This proves the uniform lower coverage for the same pairing that minimizes the global residue.

**Scope.** The need to iterate over complements of reserved primes is eliminated. The concrete residue is described by the imbalances of cells and preserves the difficulty of the criterion. Sparsity with logarithmic savings, even to every fixed order, does not prove a quadratic cost O(H X^ε) for those imbalances. The exceptional centers remain included in the equivalence, they have never been discarded from the integral.
