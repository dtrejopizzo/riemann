# A fixed multiplicative falsifier with arbitrarily small inversion defect

Given η>0 and an initial threshold, there exists a single multiplicative f, equal to zero on non-squarefree integers, with prime signs ±1 and equal to μ up to that threshold, for which

    Σ_{n>1} |(f*1)(n)|/n < η.

For infinitely many D one has simultaneously |Σ_{n≤D}f(n)/n|≤1/D and an off-diagonal increment of Q_f, in [D,D²], of modulus ≫D²/log²D. Here Q_f is exactly the observable of 136. The exact inversion identity of μ remains an essential distinction. This construction contrasts both with the typical random model of 136 and with the non-multiplicative example of 135.

**One stage.** The initial base of the stage is μ with finitely many prime signs already altered, all >5. At size D very large compared with the previous alterations, its harmonic moment H_base(D) is o(1/log D). Indeed, the base is μ*h, where for each altered prime p the factor of h is (1+p^(−s))/(1−p^(−s)). The finite product converges absolutely on every line Re s>0. Use H_μ(t)≪_A(log(2t))^(−A) by the quantitative PNT and partial summation with Σμ(n)/n=0. In H_base(D)=Σ_{k≤D} h(k)H_μ(D/k)/k, separate k≤√D and the rest, which costs O(D^(−1/4)Σh(k)/√k) using boundedness of H_μ. The constants may depend on the stage.

Prepare three disjoint groups of high primes, near .96D, and three disjoint reserves of low primes, near .18D. It suffices to split the ordered primes of each interval into three cyclic groups. Intervals of fixed positive relative widths are used, first a very narrow low one and then a sufficiently narrower high one. By PNT each high group has ≍D/log D elements; the harmonic size available in each low reserve, divided by 15, strictly exceeds twice the corresponding high harmonic size. The intervals are also chosen to satisfy the geometric separation indicated below.

Flipping a high prime changes the coefficient at p by +2 and no other coefficient up to D. A low prime changes the values at kp by 2μ(k), k≤5, since the primes up to 5 keep their original sign and p>D/6. In the harmonic moment this contributes 2H_5/p=−1/(15p), by H_5=−1/30. No index ≤D contains two of the eligible primes. These changes are additive and their supports disjoint.

Flip the first high group mandatorily and add consecutive low primes from its reserve until the total moment crosses zero. This is possible because H_base(D)=o(1/log D) and there is strict margin in both directions. Choose the option closest to zero, before or after the last step. The error stays below .2/D, since half the step is 1/(30p) and p is near .18D. For the two remaining groups repeat the procedure with target zero for the **increment** of moment of each group; keep each already balanced group optional. The four combinations have final moment of modulus <.6/D.

**Mixed difference.** Let e₁,e₂ be the two optional changes in the coefficients up to D. The alternating difference of the four off-diagonal quadratic forms is exactly 2Σ_{a,b}e₁(a)e₂(b){x/(ab)}, since the supports are disjoint. Choose u<v which in units of D² enclose a small neighborhood of .96². Between u and v all high-high products contribute their first multiple. No multiple of a high-low or low-low product enters: at the centers, the ratios .96/.18=16/3 and (.96/.18)²=256/9 are away from the integers. The factors k≤5 of the low indices only produce integer multiples. There is a bounded number of relevant multiples, so the separation persists when choosing sufficiently small neighborhoods of the three centers. Consequently the floor part of the mixed difference jumps by 8 for each pair of optional high primes. The mixed drift is 2(v−u)(Σ e₁(a)/a)(Σ e₂(b)/b)=O(1). By the triangle inequality one of the four choices has an increment of Q_f of modulus ≫D²/log²D. That choice is fixed and one passes to the next stage.

**A single model across all stages.** Choose each D so large that all new primes exceed the previous D and the initial threshold, in addition to the estimates of the stage holding. The cost Σ1/p of all eligible primes is O(1/log D) by PNT. A summable geometric budget of arbitrarily small sum can be imposed. Future stages preserve all values up to each previous D, which are the only ones used by Q_f. Let S be the union of the flipped primes. The convolution defect f*1 vanishes at every n with a prime divisor outside S and equals 2^ω(n) if all its prime divisors are in S. Hence Σ_{n>1}|(f*1)(n)|/n=∏_{p∈S}(1+2/(p−1))−1, less than η by adjusting the budget. Also the proportion of integers ≤x where f may differ from μ is bounded by Σ_{p∈S}1/p uniformly in x.

**Scope.** The conclusion invalidates transferring the anchored bound through multiplicativity, initial agreement, small harmonic moment and small harmonic norm of the inversion defect alone. 135 did not preserve multiplicativity and 136 kept the small signs under a different law for each D. R162_801 already analyzes the sensitivity of a factorial identity in another contact problem. Here a single model is exhibited and the excess occurs in the quadratic observable of 134. No excess for μ nor a new upper bound for its off-diagonal term has been proved.
