# Sampling the balance at frequencies where the smooth block is exactly zero

With the notation of 292–297, let J=[1.50,1.51], L=log x. For each prime p with 13≤x/p²≤15, solve S_t(x,p)=0 in J if there is a solution. Each prime gives at most one. Collect the distinct frequencies in a set E. For e∈E let w_e be the length of the set of points of J closer to e than to the other nodes (ties do not affect the length). Define R_e as the sum of b_e(n) over n≤x with a prime factor larger than a prime p giving that node. Then, unconditionally and for each fixed A,

    ∫_J (B_t(x))₊ dt = Σ_(e∈E) w_e (R_e)₊ + O_A(√x/log^A x).

The weights are built using only the smooth block. At each node its sum vanishes exactly; if two primes give the same node the value of R_e is the same. By 292, bounding the positive sum on the right by O_ε(x^(1/2+ε)) for all ε is equivalent to the global sign. The necessity argument of 292 works in the interior of J: the exceptional set for each pole is discrete or countable, and the localized smooth weight that detects the pole can be placed there. The direction under RH is uniform over the whole original band.

**Coverage.** Temporarily add the endpoints of J. For each t, between the real cutoffs √(x/15) and √(x/13) the block changes from negative to positive with extreme values of modulus ≫x/L² by 295 and the strict signs of 297. The first prime p making it nonnegative belongs to that range and leaves an excess ≤C√x/L by 296. For that same prime, now fixed, the derivative over all of J is ≥c x/L² by 297. Going back in frequency by up to δ=C' L/√x one therefore reaches a root or the left endpoint of J. In particular every t at distance >δ from the left endpoint has a root at distance ≤δ. Applied to t=1.5+2δ, this gives a nearby root also for the initial strip: the set E alone already covers all of J with radius ≤3δ for large x. Consequently each w_e is O(L/√x) and there are of the order of √x/L distinct nodes (upper bound by the number of primes in the interval).

**Sampling error.** By the differentiated bound for the full partial sum in 297, sup_J |∂_t B_t(x)|≪_M x/L^M for any fixed M. The positive part is 1-Lipschitz as a function of the balance. Replacing each cell by its node gives integrated error ≪_M δ x/L^M; choose M=A+1. At the node B_e(x)=R_e by definition. The construction allows the direct study of an arithmetic sum over the complement with weights determined before evaluating that complement. One still needs to control its positive values at square-root scale; the density of the nodes alone does not imply that control.
