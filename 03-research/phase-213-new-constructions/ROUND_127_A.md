# Eventual sign of the signed mean of primorials at fourth powers

For integer M and X=M⁴, set A(M)=M⁻¹Σ_{M<m≤2M}(θ(m⁴)−m⁴). The global sign of P46 is equivalent to A(M)≤0 for all M large enough. Under the sign one even obtains

    | A(M)/M² + 7/3 | ≤ (9/4)(2+γ_E−log(4π)) + o(1).

The conclusion allows excesses and deficits to compensate and has no unknown constant in the threshold. The idea of the bias from squares and its domination over the zeros already appears in selection 092 for a continuous logarithmic weight. The additional operation verified here is the transfer to the block of exactly M unsmoothed values with their equal integer weights. This is a criterion; no arithmetic proof of A(M)≤0 is available.

**Transfer.** Let E(v)=ψ(v)−v. The difference between the discrete average of E(m⁴) and ∫₁² E(Xt⁴)dt is obtained by writing E(v)=E(X)+∫_(X,v]dE and exchanging the order in both averages. On (X,16X] the resulting kernel q is the difference between M⁻¹ times the number of integers m∈(M,2M] with m≥v^(1/4) and the length of [v^(1/4)/M,2]. Outside the block it is zero. Its amplitude is ≤1/M, it has O(M) jumps and in logarithmic coordinate its piecewise derivative is O(1). The value at each jump is taken from the left, as the inclusive sample requires. Thus 125, on four dyadic bands with A=1/M and J=O(M), gives error O(log^C(2X)(1+X^(Θ−1/8))). The dominant proper powers satisfy ψ(v)−θ(v)=(1+o(1))√v by PNT on the squares, uniformly in this block. In both averages their contribution is (7/3+o(1))√X, by ∫₁²t²dt=7/3.

**On the line.** Under RH the continuous average of E(Xt⁴) is, up to O(log^C(2X)), the sum of −X^ρ (2^(1+4ρ)−1)/(ρ(1+4ρ)) over zeros with multiplicity. One can integrate the formula truncated at height comparable to X² as in 125: the pointwise error is polylogarithmic and the integrated tail is absolutely convergent and negligible. Since |1+4ρ|≥4|ρ| and |2^(1+4ρ)|=8, the sum in modulus is ≤(9/4)√X Σ|ρ|⁻². The sum is 2+γ_E−log(4π)<0.047 under RH by the identity of 092. The transfer loses o(√X). The announced margin follows.

**Off the line.** If Θ>1/2, an eventual ceiling A(M)≤0 would imply that G(X)=∫₁² E(Xt⁴)dt is bounded above by Oε(X^(b+ε)) at X=M⁴, with b=max(1/2,Θ−1/8). The extension to all large real X costs Oε(X^(Θ+ε−1/4)): write G(Y⁴)=Y⁻¹∫_Y^(2Y)E(u⁴)du, replace Y by its integer part in the lengths and the denominator and use |E(v)|≪ε v^(Θ+ε). The one-sided Mellin transform of G on [1,∞) is that of E multiplied by ∫₁² t^(4s)dt, plus an entire term from compact endpoints. This factor does not vanish for Re s>0. The Mellin transform of E is (−ζ'/ζ(s)−s/(s−1))/s for Re s>1; it is regular on the positive real axis and has poles at the nontrivial zeros. Choose b<d<Θ and ε small enough. Landau applied to CX^d−G(X), eventually non-negative, rules out poles in Re s>d: its abscissa is ≤Θ and a real abscissa greater than d would be a real singular point, where the transform is regular. There is a zero with real part greater than d. This contradicts the ceiling and proves the converse.

**Equivalent arithmetic target.** If P(n)=∏_{p≤n}p, one requires ∏_{M<m≤2M}P(m⁴) ≤ exp(Σ_{M<m≤2M}m⁴) eventually. The exact identification by logarithms provides no estimate of that product.
