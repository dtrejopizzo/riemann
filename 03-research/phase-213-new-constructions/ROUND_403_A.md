# Unconditional separation of the negative term and of the poles at growing height

**Status: PROVED, with independent review of the safe edges, the residues and the unconditional saddle.**

Let α,K be those of 368, F=1/ζ and T₀=exp(K(α))/(|ζ(1/2)|√(2πK''(α))). For all large x one can choose σ∈[α,α+1/L] and H∈[2√L,2√L+1] with

    S(x)=R(x)−(1+o(1))T₀,
    R(x)=Σ_{Re ρ>σ, |Im ρ|<H} Res_{s=ρ} x^s exp((s−1/2)²/2) E_z(s)/ζ(s).

Each distinct pole is counted once with its complete residue, whatever its multiplicity. The additive approximation is uniform on the safe edges described below. Neither RH nor separation between zeros is assumed. 364–366 treated fixed height and growing diffusion, and 368 computes the saddle under RH. Here the pulse and the cut are exactly those of 368, and the saddle term survives with its sign even if poles are crossed.

**Safe edges.** Exclude from the interval of abscissae the neighborhoods of radius L^(−2) of Re ρ for |Im ρ|≤2√L+4. By the classical zero count, the lost length is O(L^(−3/2)log L)<1/L. Choose H in the stated interval at distance ≥L^(−2) from all ordinates and their opposites by the same count. On these edges F is bounded by exp(O(log² L)) for |t|≥4 and σ≤Re s≤1+1/L where applicable. Indeed the standard local formula for the logarithmic derivative, ζ'/ζ(s)=Σ_{|γ−t|≤1}1/(s−ρ)+O(log(2+|t|)) in the band, integrated horizontally from 2+it and taking real parts, gives log|ζ(s)|≥−O(log(2+|t|)log L). The logarithm of the moduli is integrated by difference of endpoints, with a limit in t if the segment meets a zero. There are O(log(2+|t|)) summands and each final distance is ≥L^(−2). At bounded heights away from the zeros compactness is used, and F is regular at 1; near any fixed zero the same margin gives a power of L.

**Shift.** From c=1+1/L exactly the stated set of poles is crossed. The horizontals and the tails at that height are negligible by exp(−2L); in the rectangle |E_z(s)|≤E_z(σ)=exp(O(L/log z)). The only pole of ζ at 1 gives a zero of F. The residues are conjugate in pairs. On the vertical σ, K(σ)=K(α)+O((log z)/L), K'(σ)=O(log z) and K''(σ)∼K''(α)≍L log z. Thus the linear term at the scale 1/√K'' is o(1). On |t|≤1/log z the local argument of 368 holds unconditionally because F is regular in a fixed neighborhood of 1/2; the domination comes from the high primes as in 367. Between 1/log z and H the loss ≫L/(log z)³ of 367 dominates O(log² L); increasing the height by one keeps the same short-PNT margins. One obtains for this integral (1+o(1))exp(K(α))/(ζ(1/2)√(2πK''(α))), strictly negative. The computation does not bound the individual residues via 1/ζ'(ρ).

**Consequence.** RH is equivalent to R(x)≤T₀(x)/2 eventually for a choice of safe edges: under RH R is empty, and that inequality would give S<0 by 368. The factor 1/2 can be replaced by any fixed non-negative constant less than one. The new content is that the sign and amplitude of the background term are proved without assuming the absence of poles; the one-sided inequality for the sum of residues remains open.

**Review record.** The local formula used for ζ'/ζ and its integrated version are found in [Titchmarsh–Heath-Brown, theorem9.6(A)–(B), pp.217–218](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/EM18/TitchmarshZeta.pdf#page=113). The identity of logarithms of moduli extends by continuity when the horizontal segment passes through a zero; the integral of the modulus of1/(s−ρ) is not bounded. The O(log|t|) local zeros are counted with multiplicity, and each final distance≥L^(−2) contributes at most O(log L), giving the ceiling exp(O(log²L)) forF without mutual separation. The height margin+4 covers all zeros of the local window|γ−t|≤1 at the chosen edges.

The exclusion of abscissae consumes O(sqrt L log L·L^(−2))=o(1/L), and that of heights consumes o(1); hence both edges exist simultaneously. Only the ceiling ofF on the edges is required, not in the interior containing poles. When shifting, S is the sum of residues plus the left vertical; E_z has its poles on Re s=0 and the pole ofζ at1 is a zero ofF, so there are no extra terms. The vertical segment is truncated atH: absence of poles at greater heights on that abscissa is not presupposed.

In the local window near t=0, F is bounded in a fixed neighborhood of1/2 and so is its derivative, withoutRH. The displacement σ−α≤1/L changes K by O(b/L)=o(1), and K'(σ)/sqrt(K''(σ))=O(sqrt(b/L))=o(1). In the local tail that bound onF is used, not the global ceiling exp(O(log²L)); on the intermediate range the lossL/b³ does dominate that ceiling. This proves the error o(T₀) uniform on the safe edges. Each residue is the complete one of its pole, so the claim and its one-sided criterion require neither simple zeros nor individual bounds on1/ζ'(ρ).
