# A quartet above the height can cost (1+o(1))exp T

For each fixed 0<κ<1 and sufficiently large T, put G=exp(κ exp T). There is a modification of the real completed function ξ that preserves the functional equation, all ordinates and multiplicities up to G, and changes the zero count at any height by at most a constant, but whose spectral form on [−T,T] has a unit direction of value ≤−(1−κ+o(1))exp T. It is a counterexample to using only those data. The modified function need not satisfy the arithmetic formula of the paper with its exact prime comb.

**Profile.** Fix δ=1/2−1/T² and put h(t)=1_[−T,T](t)sinh(δt)/D, where
    D²=∫_{−T}^T sinh²(δt)dt=sinh(2δT)/(2δ)−T.
One has 2D²=(1+o(1))exp T. The L¹, sup and total variation norms of h extended by zero are O(1) for large T: before normalizing each is O(exp(δT)), and D is comparable to exp(δT). Hence |ĥ(v)|≪1/(1+|v|) and the logarithmic energy is finite. Choose f_Γ(t)=exp(−iΓt)h(t), in the convention Φ(s)=∫ f(t)exp((s−1/2)t)dt.

**A height with controlled cost in the true form.** For Γ∈[2G,3G] the archimedean term of W_T(f_Γ) is ≤log G+O(1), since m(v±Γ)≤log G+C+log(2+|v|) and Plancherel normalizes the main term. The average in Γ of the comb contribution has modulus ≤2A_T/(G log 2): each shift of h has overlap of modulus ≤1 and the average of the phase at distance log n costs ≤2/(G log n). The pole of the true form costs O(exp(T)/G²) uniformly, by integration by parts for h(t)exp(±t/2), whose variations including the edge jumps are O(exp(T/2)). Hence there is Γ in that band with W_T(f_Γ)≤log G+O(1)+O(exp(T)/G), using A_T=O(exp T). The computation uses only the exact formula for ζ and does not assume RH. The profile with jumps belongs to the domain; the spectral formula admits smooth approximation, or roughEF of the paper is used.

**Finite change.** Choose two distinct pairs of critical zeros of ξ with heights γ_a,γ_b>G (there are infinitely many by Hardy). In the variable z=s−1/2 multiply ξ by
    [((z−δ)²+Γ²)((z+δ)²+Γ²)] / [(z²+γ_a²)(z²+γ_b²)].
The singularities of the denominator are removable in the product. The resulting function is entire of the same order, real and even in z. The new zeros remain in the strip and above G; one copy of each indicated zero is removed. The two new zeros at ordinate Γ contribute together 2 Re[Φ(1/2+δ+iΓ) conj Φ(1/2−δ+iΓ)]=−2D²: the two integrals are D and −D by oddness. At −Γ, the same integrals with frequency phase 2Γ are O(exp(δT)/Γ) by total variation. The two zeros at that ordinate thus cost O(exp(2δT)/Γ²). The removed critical pairs contributed nonnegative squares. Adding to the true form gives the announced inequality. Since the form is Hermitian and real, a complex negative direction also gives a real one by separating real and imaginary parts.

**Comparison with 177.** Its sufficient coefficient 1 for ζ comes from an arithmetic norm. Here a single unverified quartet can produce a loss with that same coefficient, while the archimedean cost of the modulated test is at most log G+O(1). The scales coincide, but the hypotheses are different: this is not a proof that verification up to exp(exp T) is necessary for ζ. The models depend on T. What the result excludes is lowering the coefficient below 1 by invoking only functional symmetry, strip geometry, counting with tolerance O(1) and exact agreement with ζ up to finite height.

**Antecedents.** Intervention by quartets and preservation of the functional equation are in prop:shape-twist of paper 46 and in R162_2501b; the perturbative curvature of a quartet is in R7-01. The comb norm of size exp T is in 081. The specific computation here is the coefficient 1 at the joint scale of window and height, using the odd edge profile and the average of the true arithmetic formula before modifying the spectrum.
