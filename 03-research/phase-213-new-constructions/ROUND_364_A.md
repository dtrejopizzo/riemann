# Which zero region a diffusive Gaussian with moving sieve detects

For fixed a>0, v=log x, use in Q of 363 the weight g_x(u)=exp(u/2) exp(−u²/(2av))/√(2πav). Write Φ_a(s)=s+a(s−1/2)²/2. The bound |Q(x;z)|≪_(a,ε) x^(1/2+ε) for each ε>0 and all cuts (log x)²≤z≤4(log x)² **is equivalent** to the absence of zeros ρ of ζ with Re ρ>1/2 and Re Φ_a(ρ)>1/2. In particular:

* With G=3·10¹² the bound holds unconditionally for each a≥1/(G²−1/4), at every large scale x, by the rigorous verification up to G.
* The family of bounds for any positive sequence a→0 is equivalent to RH. A fixed variance multiplied by log x only detects the indicated parabolic region; the bound of 363 for a fixed Gaussian is not deduced.

[Height verified by Platt and Trudgian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09765)

**Contour.** The two-sided Mellin integral is ∫ x^Φ_a(s) D_z(s) ds/(2πi) over c=1+1/log x, since the transform of the weight is exp(av(s−1/2)²/2). To the right of the critical line D_z is meromorphic, regular at 1, and its only poles are the zeros of ζ, as in 361. On any compact set of Re s≥1/2+η avoiding them, |D_z|=x^o(1) uniformly in the corridor. Indeed, the finite Euler factor and its inverse cost at most exp(O_η(z^(1/2−η))) for small fixed η, and the polynomial of exterior primes of 361 is polylogarithmic. On small fixed circles to the right of 1/2 these bounds also bound the Laurent coefficients of fixed order.

Take a fixed height T with 1+a(1/4−T²)/2≤1/2, avoiding ordinates of poles off the critical line. On the horizontals Re Φ_a increases with Re s for Re s≥1/2 and does not exceed 1/2+O_a(1/log x). On the tails |t|>T on c, use |D_z|≪log² x and the Gaussian decay from the endpoint: its cost is x^(1/2+o(1)). On the left vertical σ=1/2+η the cost is x^(1/2+η+aη²/2+o(1)). Choose η small avoiding poles. When shifting the contour, each crossed pole contributes x^Φ_a(ρ) times a polynomial in log x of degree m, where m is the multiplicity of the zero. The coefficients are x^o(1) in modulus. If no zero violates the condition, this gives the requested bound by making η small depending on ε.

**Inverse detection.** If a zero violates the condition, choose T large enough and η small enough to cross it and leave the ceiling of the vertical strictly below its exponent. Freeze z=2(log X)² while x runs over [X,eX]; this respects the corridor. The pole contributions are now a linear combination of functions exp(Φ_a(ρ)t)t^j in t=log(x/X)∈[0,1], with a finite family independent of X. They are linearly independent, since Φ_a is injective in Re s>1/2 (the other symmetric root lies on the other side) and the exponents are distinct. Each coefficient is bounded by a fixed constant times the sup norm of the combination, by equivalence of norms. For the chosen zero the coefficient of the top degree m has modulus equal to a positive constant times X^ReΦ_a(ρ)|E_z(ρ)|: the leading coefficient of 361 without W and the factor Φ_a'(ρ)^m/m!, nonzero, enter. The finite product has modulus ≥X^(−o(1)). That lower bound for the norm dominates the contour errors and refutes the ceiling for some ε>0. Freezing the cut is essential in this step.

**Proved range.** For β∈[1/2,1], Re Φ_a(β+iγ)≤1+a(1/4−γ²)/2. When a≥1/(G²−1/4), every ordinate above G is below the threshold and there are no zeros off the line up to G. For the family a→0, each zero with β>1/2 would end up violating the condition because Re Φ_a(ρ)→β.

**Antecedent and limit.** 142 already studies the pole rates with variance proportional to log x. Here the prime cut depends on x and the estimate and its inverse are uniform in the corridor of the full product of 360. The verified branch uses a previously known height; it enlarges neither that height nor the support window of paper 47.
