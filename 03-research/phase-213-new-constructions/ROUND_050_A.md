# Products with small squarefree part

Let sf(n) be the unique squarefree s with n=s r². This function can differ from rad(n). In the bilinear criterion of 049 we can eliminate, at absolute cost O_ε(√x), all pairs with sf(km)≤x^(1/4−ε) for each fixed 0<ε<1/4. All perfect powers can also be eliminated. The estimate preserves the precision required for the sign and sums absolute values before grouping pairs with the same product.

**Choice of the compensation.** Keep b_d=−(μ*a)(d) for d≤L=⌊√x⌋, exactly as in 049. On L<d≤2L use only primes, with b_p=λ log p and λ=−(Σ_{d≤L}b_d/d)/(Σ_{L<p≤2L}log p/p). The denominator tends to log 2 and the numerator to zero by the identity of 049 and the PNT. The argument for the progressions of 049 applies with the same bound Σ|b_d|/d=O(log x). By the formula for μ*a, the remaining coefficient is a sum of rows of weights O(log p) with p^e|k and p^e>L/2: for the double term one further requires 2p^e|k, and for the compensation e=1. Each (p,e) gives rise to at most three rows. The absolute value of the total coefficient is O(log k) by Σ_{p^e|k}log p=log k.

**Counting.** Write S=x^(1/4−ε), so that √L S=L^(1−2ε+o(1)). On the support of V and U from 049, km=s r² is comparable to L² and k lies between L and C L(log x)². Only squarefree m count. Then m|sr, h=sr/m is an integer, k=rh, r≤C L/√s and h≤C √s(log x)². We will use H=C√S(log x)² with a sufficient constant. For each s,r there are at most τ(sr)=L^{o(1)} choices of h, by the elementary bound on the number of divisors; it is uniform because sr≤C L√S.

* For p not dividing s and p>H, the prime does not divide h either. The row p^e|k forces p^e|r. Since p^e>L/2, one obtains s≤C' and r/p^e≤C'. Moreover h|sr/p^e. Therefore for each p^e there are O(1) pairs in this case, with constants independent of L. All have p^e≤C L. Chebyshev gives Σ_{p^e≤CL}log p=O(L), sufficient even when summing all absolute values.
* For p not dividing s and p≤H, p^e|s r² requires p^ceil(e/2)|r, and that divisor exceeds √(L/2). For each p,e,s there are at most C√L/√s values of r. Summing over s≤S and p≤H and using O(log L) exponents, the divisor bound and the weight log p costs at most √L S L^{o(1)}.
* For p|s write s=pv. From p^e|s r² one obtains p^ceil((e−1)/2)|r, a divisor larger than √(L/(2p)). There are at most C√L/√v values of r for each p,e,v. Summing v≤S/p and then p≤S costs O(√L Σ_{p≤S}√(S/p))=O(√L S), before factors L^{o(1)} from h,e and weights.

The last two bounds are o(L) and the first O(L). This proves the claim, including the bounded factor V and 0≤U≤1. The squares are already included. For perfect powers of exponent at least three there are O(x^(1/3)log x) possible products of size O(x); the absolute sum over their divisors costs at most x^(1/3+o(1)) by the coefficient bound. Thus both sets can be removed at once.

**Scope.** In 044 proper powers were removed from the index carrying Λ in the original expression. Here the product km carries μ(m) and divisibility rows in k, and all pairs of the set are controlled before any cancellation. This does not authorise removing all rows p^e with e≥2: many of their products have large sf(km). The signed deficit on the complement remains open.
