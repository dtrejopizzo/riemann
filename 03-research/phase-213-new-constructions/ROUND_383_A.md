# The real zero of the parity weight near one

**Status: PROVED, with independent review of the branch jump, the constants and the uniformity of the contour.**

Let U(r)=Σ_{P^−(n)>L²} μ(n) r^ω(n) g(log(x/n)), with L=log x, h=log L and E=∏_{p≤L²}(1−1/p)^−1. For x sufficiently large, U has a unique zero r_x in [1−h^(−2),1+h^(−2)]. There, uniformly,

    U'(r)=(1+o(1)) J x E/L²,     J=exp(1/8).

Consequently r_x−1=−U(1)L²/(JxE)·(1+o(1)), even as an identity with both sides zero if U(1)=0. One has |r_x−1|≪exp(−cL/h) by 377. The goal of 368 is equivalent to proving r_x>1 eventually. The index records the Selberg–Delange mechanism and its limits (P7/P9 and R162_2802); the deduction here is that of the displacement of a real zero for the exact pulse and cut, without attributing a sign to the endpoint by continuity.

**Contour.** The series on Re s>1 has product D_r=ζ(s)^(−r) H_r(s), with H_r equal to the product of (1−p^(−s))^(−r) for p≤L² and of (1−rp^(−s))/(1−p^(−s))^r for p>L². This second product converges absolutely for Re s>0.9 and there its logarithm and its derivative in r are O(1). Shift the Mellin integral with factor x^s exp((s−1/2)²/2) as in 377 to 1−d, d=c₀/h, height 2√L, opening a slit from 1−d to 1. For real r in the interval considered, D_r and ∂_r D_r on the edges cost exp(O(h²)), apart from the Gaussian factor: the branch of log ζ is controlled by the discs of 370 for |Im s|≥3 and at small heights by separating the pole. On the left edge at small heights the distance to the pole is ≥d. The small product and its derivative cost exp(O(log h)) by p^d≤exp(2c₀). The right tails are also negligible by absolute convergence and the Gaussian (the product with absolute weights and its derivative are polynomial in L). The exterior errors for U and U' cost O(x exp(−cL/h)).

**Jump.** Set B_r(s)=((s−1)ζ(s))^(−r)H_r(s) exp((s−1/2)²/2), regular in a neighborhood of the slit. Adding the two sides of the branch of (s−1)^r leaves for U(r) the term

    −x sin(πr)/π ∫₀ᵈ exp(−Lv) v^r B_r(1−v) dv.

The infinitesimal circle at 1 also vanishes after differentiating. On the whole segment, ∂_r log B_r=O(log h+1) and ∂_s log B_r=O(h), by Σ_{p≤L²} log p/p≪h and p^d=O(1). Moreover B_1(1)=JE. Uniformly for v≤h²/L one has B_r(1−v)=(1+o(1))JE, since |r−1|≤h^(−2). For the rest B_r(1−v)≪JE suffices, by integrating the same derivatives and dh=O(1). Thus the integral equals (1+o(1))JE/L² by the change w=Lv, Γ(1+r)→1 and L^(1−r)→1. Its derivative in r is bounded by O(h JE/L²), using the absolute moment of log v. Differentiating the jump, −cos(πr)=1+o(1) while |sin(πr)|h=o(1); the equivalent for U' follows. Differentiation of the contour is legitimate by uniform convergence at fixed x and the above bounds. Now 377 and the mean value theorem give opposite signs at the endpoints, uniqueness and the displacement formula.

**Review record.** The Hankel orientation gives −sin(πr)/π, in agreement with 1/Γ(−r)=−Γ(1+r)sin(πr)/π; therefore when differentiating near1 the leading sign is −cos(πr)>0. The small circle costs O_x(ε^(1+r)(1+|log ε|)) also after differentiating and vanishes uniformly on the interval of r. On the real part of the slit, ((s−1)ζ(s)) and all factors of H_r are positive for large x; B_r admits a regular, real positive branch. Its logarithmic derivatives have the stated costs O(log h) and O(h), without a factor growing like L. In particular |r−1|log L≤1/h→0 justifies L^(1−r)→1 uniformly, while ∂_r of the integral introduces at most O(h). The exterior error of U' is negligible relative to JxE/L². Gaussian convergence is uniform in r on compact sets at fixed x, legitimizing the differentiation of the sum and of the contour. Finally the maximal displacement h^(−2) produces a variation ≍xE/(L²h²), much larger than |U(1)|; this guarantees opposite signs at the endpoints and uniqueness, and the mean value theorem gives the relative formula even when U(1)=0. The polynomial factors L²/E of the derivative floor are absorbed by reducing the constant c in the exponential ceiling for |r_x−1|.

The positive derivative is proved unconditionally. Its magnitude determines the sensitivity to the per-prime weight. Which side of one the zero falls on requires an additional estimate.
