# The height cost uses the norm of the compressed comb

For each fixed κ>1 and sufficiently large T, verification of the zeros up to G=exp(κ exp T) implies strict positivity of W_T on its whole domain. Thus in 176 the sufficient coefficient 4 is improved to 1. Equivalently in the direction of use: for fixed ε>0 and sufficiently large G, verifying up to G suffices for 0<T≤log log G−ε. It is a conditional asymptotic law. No new window is evaluated with the G known in this round.

**Exact norm at first order.** On the interval [−U,U] call Π_U the sum of the right and left compressed translations by log n, each of weight Λ(n)/√n. This convention gives the complete comb of (form) of the paper, with multiplier 2Λ(n)cos(τ log n)/√n before compressing. For h(t)=cosh(t/2), L(y)=Σ_{n≤y}Λ(n)/n, ψ(y)=Σ_{n≤y}Λ(n), and y_±=exp(U±t), one obtains almost everywhere
    Π_U h/h = [e^(t/2)L(y_+)+e^(−t/2)ψ(y_+)
                +e^(−t/2)L(y_−)+e^(t/2)ψ(y_−)]/[e^(t/2)+e^(−t/2)].
The L terms cost O(U+1) by Chebyshev. Replacing ψ(y) by y gives exactly exp U in the other two. The replacement costs o(exp U) uniformly in t: given ε>0, for y≥Yε the error is ≤εy by PNT, while for y<Yε each weighted error in the quotient costs O(Yε), fixed. By the Schur test for a symmetric kernel with positive weights the norm in L² is ≤sup Π_U h/h (it also follows by applying 2ab≤a²+b² with the weights h in each translation). The Rayleigh quotient of h gives the lower bound via the infimum of the same ratio. In short ||Π_U||=(1+o(1))exp U. The factor 4 of 176 came from taking the mass of the complete translations even at the ends of the interval.

**Transfer to the form.** Fix 1<r<κ and put R=exp(r exp T), m the first even integer ≥4000TR, k a fixed integer with 2k(κ−r)>r, H=100km T², u(τ)=1−sinc(τ/H)^(2k), φ=1−u^m. Define ĝ=u^(m/2)f̂. The factor is a polynomial in sinc, and g is supported in [−U,U], U=T+km/H=T+1/(100T²). The logarithmic domain is preserved. Let μ be the atomic extension with continuous correction of 172, exact on |t|<2U+1. By linearity in the measure and the identity of the form on both windows,
    W_T(f)=W_U(g)+∫φ|f̂|² dμ.
Here one uses that the filter is real and even on the real axis and that m is even. In particular the bound of the compressed comb can be applied directly to g, avoiding a pointwise bound for the arithmetic extension.

By the definition of W in the paper and Plancherel,
    W_U(g) ≥ (1/(2π))∫ [m_arch(τ)−||Π_U||] u^m |f̂|² dτ − 2|A(g)B(g)|.
For |τ|≥R the bracket exceeds 2 for large T because log R=r exp T and ||Π_U||=(1+o(1))exp T. On the whole line it is bounded below by −O(exp T). Reserve half of its value on |τ|>G/2. The uncertainty argument of 175 gives η≥exp(−1800(1+TR)); using a=kR²/(3H²), u≤a on |τ|≤R, u≥2a on |τ|≥2R as in 176. The integrated contribution after the reserve is at least a^m[2^mη−O(exp T)]||f||², with the parenthesis greater than two for large T.

The two polar moments of g are those of f multiplied by u(i/2)^(m/2) by entire continuation and parity. By Cauchy–Schwarz 2|A(g)B(g)|≤O(exp T)|u(i/2)|^m||f||². For fixed k, |u(i/2)|=O_k(H^(−2)), so its ratio to a is O_k(R^(−2)). This allows paying the polar term with o(a^m)||f||².

**Closing the conditional.** The atomic term of φμ is positive. For the continuous one use exactly the spectral cutoff of 176 with U in place of T and N=floor(G/200). On |τ|≤G/2 the cost is exp(−cG), against a^m≥exp(−O_k(TR log T)). The latter exponent in modulus is o(G) by r<κ. On |τ|>G/2 the ceiling of |φC| is O((1+U)exp(U)log(3+G)m(2H/G)^(2k)), whose logarithm is ≤[r−2k(κ−r)+o(1)]exp T. It tends to zero and is absorbed in the pointwise reserve of the bracket multiplied by u^m/(2π), since there φ=o(1). A strictly positive margin uniform in f remains. Inclusion of supports extends the conclusion to smaller windows.

**Cross-check.** 081 already proved the same asymptotics of the norm with the same cosh weight and PNT. The application to the square filter and its polar moments is the contribution of 177. The paper already compresses the comb and contains local bounds on its norm (prop:coer-prime); 176 already gave the doubly exponential law using A_T. The specific step consists in letting the filter act as a square on a test still of finite support and paying separately for its two Tate moments. The Rayleigh computation also explains why improving only the absolute norm of the comb cannot lower its leading coefficient 1. This is not a lower bound for the verification height needed for the sign.
