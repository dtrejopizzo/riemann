# The first critical zero can be removed before narrowing again

**Status: PROVED, Arb certificate reproduced and tails independently reviewed.**

Let γ be the first positive critical ordinate and f=TγΦ the kernel of 407. It is certified that f''<0 on [0,1/16] and f'<0 on [1/16,∞). In particular f is strictly decreasing on the whole positive half-line and satisfies the stability hypotheses of 408. This proves the low-height case that 408 left out.

**Reproducible verification.** Run python3 verify_409.py with python-flint. Arb encloses γ via acb.zeta_zero(1). For j=1,2 integrate γ∫ Φ^(j)(v+t)sin(γt)dt over 0≤t≤2 with ball arithmetic, by interval sums of step 1/1024. Each cell of v has the same width and hence the interval of v+t has double width. 64 curvature cells and 448 slope cells were verified. Endpoints are included by overlap. The local computation gave PASS at 96 bits.

**Analytic tails of the certificate.** For 0≤v+t≤5/2, the term n of the two derivatives is exp((v+t)/2−r)P_j(r) with the polynomials of 408 and r=πn²exp(2(v+t)). For n≥5, |P_j(r)|≤200r⁴, exp((v+t)/2)<4 and r⁴≤exp(r/2); hence the tail is ≤800Σ_{n≥5}exp(−πn²/2)<10^(−12). For v≥1/2, r≥πe>8, and the explicit polynomials give P₁<0, P₂>0. Thus the half-period argument of 407 applied to −Φ' proves f'<0 there. For t≥2 and 0≤v≤1/2, the absolute integral of Φ' or Φ'' is, respectively, Φ(v+2) or −Φ'(v+2); this uses the same signs. Both quantities times γ cost <10^(−25) by the previous bound with r≥πn²exp(4) and exp((v+2)/2)<4. The certificate adds that margin before comparing with zero. Finally f'(0)=0 by 407, so the verified curvature also ensures negative slope for 0<v<1/16.

**Consequence.** Now 407 can be applied to f with the second critical zero: the kernel obtained after removing the first two pairs is positive, preserves the negative average and its variance is V₀−2/γ₁²−2/γ₂². It has not been certified that this second kernel remains decreasing. For a chain of arbitrary length starting with the first one, 408 can be used and sufficiently large heights chosen afterwards.
