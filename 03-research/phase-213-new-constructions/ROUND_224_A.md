# A change of order eliminates the instability of 221 for a family that still detects the whole sign

In the samples of 217 keep s_k=−√k+ik and replace the order k² by N=jk with j a fixed positive integer. Write C_(k,j)=∫₃ᴺ A_N(u)u^(s_k−1)du. Then

    lim_(k→∞) log(1+|C_(k,j)|)/k = max(0, Θ−d_j),
    d_j=arctan(1/(2j))−j log(1+1/(4j²)) >0,    d_j∼1/(4j).

The same formula holds for C_(k,j) built with ANY other count whose accumulated difference from the authentic prime count is O_ε(x^(1/2+ε)) for every ε>0. In particular it suffices to prove a subexponential bound along some subsequence for each j in an unbounded set; the subsequences may depend on j. An alteration from 221 can impose exp(k) on the sample of order k² and still preserves ALL these linear-order rates.

**Detection versus damping.** The exact Mellin identity of 214 is used with N=jk and s=s_k, with the same Z(s_k) of 217. The error in replacing the spectral integral by −2C_(k,j) is o(1): on [3,N] and u>N the bounds of 217 only require N→∞; for u<3 the factor (45/196)^(N−1) and 2N>√k ensure the same control. In the beta factor, Stirling in N±s/2 gives

    log |K_N(s_k)|/k → j[log(1+y²)−2y arctan y],    y=1/(2j).

For example one can start from s=ik (Stirling formula of 214) and move the real part by O(√k): for the two log-gammas the total cost is O(√k log k)=o(k) by uniform Stirling, since |arg(N±s/2)| is bounded away from π/2. The bracketed expression is negative for y>0, by its derivative −2 arctan y. From 217, when Θ>0 one has log|Z(s_k)|/k→Θ without cancellation along any subsequence. If Θ=0, the sum is bounded. When Θ−d_j>0 the signal dominates the error o(1); when Θ−d_j≤0 the upper bound and the trivial floor log(1+|C|)≥0 suffice. The expansion in small y gives the equivalent of d_j.

**Stability at linear order.** If D(x) is the accumulated difference of weights, take |D(x)|≤C_ε x^(1/2+ε) with 0<ε<1/2. In the integration by parts of 222 choose a single contour of height ε, also in the boundary term. The factor exp(εL) of the error cancels with exp(−εL) of the contour. For all N≥4 the contribution is bounded by C_ε poly(N) exp(N m_3(ε)), with m_3(ε)=log M_ε(3)=O(ε²). The weighting in u of s_k does not increase this ceiling. For N=jk in fixed ratio to k this gives limsup log(1+|ΔC_(k,j)|)/k≤j m_3(ε). Letting ε↓0 at fixed j proves subexponential stability and transfers the limit to the model. The order of limits matters: 221 rules out this reasoning for the universal schedule j=k.

**Version with a single adapted order.** Given the concrete count and model, for each integer j≥1 there is K_j such that k≥K_j implies |ΔC_(k,j)|≤exp(k/j). Increase the K_j so that they grow strictly and K_j≥j. On K_j≤k<K_(j+1) use N(k)=jk. The error remains subexponential and N/k→∞, N≤k². At these orders log|K_N(s_k)|=o(k): the symmetric Stirling expansion in z=s_k/2 has term O(k²/N), remainder O(k³/N²) and normalisation O(log N). Thus the ordinary limit of log(1+|C|)/k is again exactly Θ, also for the perturbed model. The K_j depend on the comparison and this diagonalisation does not by itself offer effective thresholds. The version for all fixed j has explicit orders and avoids that dependence.

**Verifiable arithmetic example.** One can use a model supported only on integer squares: move each mass log p of the interval [n²,(n+1)²) to the point n². At a cut inside that cell the accumulated difference is at most (2n+1)log((n+1)²)=O(√x log(2x)), simply by the number of available integers. One can even replace each log p by log(n²) for n≥2; the additional accumulated error is O(√x) on summing O(1/n) over O(n) integers per cell. The first primes only change a constant. Thus the numbers of primes per interval between consecutive squares suffice to build samples with the same linear rates. For measures supported on squares, A_N is read as the truncated integral of the same weight against the measure; the estimate by parts does not use that its atoms are primes. This identification does not give a uniform ceiling on the samples of those counts.

**Relation with the corpus.** 214 localised frequencies t proportional to the order using energy and 217 isolated a single extreme value with the moving weight. 221–223 discovered a real instability for the quadratic schedule. Here the schedule is modified, quantifying the price d_j and proving the transfer at the same time. The preservation of Mellin singularities to the right of 1/2 under counting differences O_ε(x^(1/2+ε)) has a classical antecedent (219); it is not presented as a new principle about zeros. The proved stability is for these concrete finite samples and their ordinary limits. It has not been proved that those limits are zero for arbitrarily large j.
