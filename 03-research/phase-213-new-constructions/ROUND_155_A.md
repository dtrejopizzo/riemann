# How much is lost by averaging the complete count over divisors

With V of 154 put A_d(x)=Σ_{m≥1}m^(−d)V(x/m), 0≤d≤1/4. The average without tilt is computed without primes: by Σ_{q|r}Λ(q)=log r, it is Σ_{r,n}log r exp(−π(rn²/x)²). A weight m^(−d) arbitrarily close to the original weight recovers global detection, even if some distinct zeros cancel. The critical scale constant after subtracting the computable average can be taken proportional to d under RH.

**Precise centring.** Write H(s)=π^(−s/2)Γ(s/2)ζ(2s)/2 and
    F_d(s)=H(s)(−ζ'/ζ)(s)ζ(s+d).
Let L_d(x) be the sum of the residues of x^s F_d(s) at the distinct points of the set {1,1−d,1/2}. For d=0 one uses the residue of the double pole at 1, which contains log x. Work with the centred difference
    D_d(x)=A_d(x)−L_d(x)−A_0(x)+L_0(x).
The functions are defined by real data at the three points and by convergent sums, without zero ordinates. For any fixed d in (0,1/4], a ceiling D_d(x)≤C_d √x for all large x is equivalent to the global sign. Under RH one has the stronger estimate |D_d(x)|≤C d√x simultaneously for x≥1 and 0<d≤1/4.

**Direct.** Mellin on Re s>1 gives F_d. For d=0 the product is −Hζ', regular at the nontrivial zeros. Under RH move the difference of integrands to Re s=1/4. After the real residues the terms −mρ H(ρ)ζ(ρ+d)x^ρ remain. On the critical line ζ(ρ)=0; the fundamental theorem of calculus bounds |ζ(ρ+d)| by d times the supremum of |ζ'| on the segment. This derivative grows at most polynomially in height in the fixed strip by Cauchy. The gamma factor and the count by bands make the sum of coefficients O(d). On the shifted line ζ(s+d)−ζ(s) likewise costs O(d) times a power of 1+|Im s|. The horizontal distance to the critical zeros is 1/4, so the logarithmic derivative grows at most polynomially. The integral costs O(d x^(1/4)). For the shift the horizontals separated from the ordinates of 151 are valid. The constants of the two estimates are independent of d, even as the two main poles approach each other.

**Converse.** Assuming a zero to the right, choose at the same ordinate a zero ρ of largest real part, which exists by discreteness and compactness on that horizontal. Then ζ(ρ+d) is nonzero, even when ρ+d leaves the critical strip. The residue −mρ H(ρ)ζ(ρ+d) is nonzero. For the unilateral Mellin transform on x≥1 the omitted piece of A_d on (0,1) gives an entire function by Gaussian decay. Subtracting L_d removes the real principal parts, also in the double case, and F_0 contributes no nonreal poles to the right of 1/2. If D_d≤C_d√x eventually, Landau applied to C_d√x−D_d excludes poles in Re s>1/2 since there the transform is regular on the whole real axis. Contradiction.

**Route decision.** Factorial averages and their inversion were already tried in 129–134; the suppression of poles by a factor ξ appears in 150. Here the loss from using exactly the elementary divisor weight is quantified: the point d=0 gives D_0 identically zero, while each positive d keeps at least the rightmost zero of each ordinate. The estimate at that isolated point does not extend by continuity with a constant uniform in x. The unconditional bound for a positive d remains open; iterating the elementary identity at d=0 is not enough.
