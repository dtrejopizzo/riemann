# Prime ceiling certified on a continuous interval and comparison with the filter

With the weight of 109 one has E(x)<0 for ALL real x in [2,999983]. All primes up to floor(10⁶ exp(11/25)) are sieved and the signs at the 78498 prime centers up to 10⁶ are certified with ball arithmetic at 128 bits. The convexity between centers of 109 extends the bound to the closed intervals between them. The last prime center is 999983. RH is not used.

The [verifier](verify_111.py) evaluates the two prefixes of 110, the indices with certified floors and the identity 2√r E(q)=qZ−(R−rL+log q), where Z=r(s+log(q)/q−h)−(l−h), h=11/25. All sign intervals were strict. The filter selected 3130 centers. In 267 of them the excess Z was positive (last one 55871) although E(q)<0: discarding the mass term does change finite decisions. At q=720829 both centered reciprocal moments are positive and Z<0; at q=951497 both are negative and Z<0. Hence requiring simultaneously l≥h and s+log(q)/q≤h loses real cases. These examples do not determine the eventual behavior of the two halves. The certified interval concerns only the local ceiling of 109, not a Weil window for every test.
