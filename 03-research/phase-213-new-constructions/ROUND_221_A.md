# Counting data up to x^(1/2+o(1)) allow samples to be programmed in a perturbation on the same primes

For any sequence of complex targets z(k) with |z(k)|≤exp(k), there exist integers k_j→∞ and positive weights λ_p=1+o(1) on the authentic PRIMES with the following properties. In C_k of 217 replace log p by λ_p log p, without changing the kernel or the cut. Then

    C_(k_j)(λ)=z(k_j) exactly,
    |Σ_(p≤y) (λ_p−1)log p| = O_ε(y^(1/2+ε)) for each ε>0.

The mass in each modified block is preserved exactly and the whole accumulated perturbation vanishes between blocks. The ceiling concerns the DIFFERENCE between the two counts, without presupposing a von Koch bound for θ(y)−y. With the same subsequence one can produce, separately, targets zero and exp(k), choosing the indices simultaneously for both constructions. This result does not modify the authentic weights of ζ nor establish a density of excursions. It determines which data do not suffice to justify an estimate of C_k by comparison with a nearby prime measure.

**Capacity of a block.** Fix 0<a<1/2, β=1/2+a, m=log M_a(3), v=∂_a log M_a(3), N=k², X=exp(Nv). Use K,θ,x=x_a from 212. On three disjoint intervals of log p=Nv+d, of the same small width h>0, with starting points d_j=2πj/(3x), j=0,1,2, allow constant perturbations X^(β−1)b_j with |b_j|≤1. The mass datum of each column, divided by X^β, tends to ∫ exp(d)dd over its interval, by PNT.

Define I_k as in 218, J=3/[v(a−ix)], S=3^(−√k−1) exp(Nm)/N^(3/2). For the complex output δC of the block, rotate by 3^(−ik), divide by S, and multiply the imaginary coordinate by k. The two rows of the resulting real matrix, per column of b_j, are, with error o(1) uniform over the three columns,

    ∫ exp((1/2−a)d) Re[K exp(iNθ+ixd) J] dd,
    ∫ exp((1/2−a)d) Re[K exp(iNθ+ixd) J²/3] dd.

To check the factors, set u=3+r/N in I_k. Extract 3^(−√k−1+ik) exp(Nm−a log p)/N^(3/2). The real profile of 212 at rate 3, with the exact scaling of 216, contains exp(−avr/3) and the phase xr v/3. The factor (u/3)^(−√k−1) tends to one. For the rotated real part the remaining exponential contributes cos(k log(u/3))→1; for the amplified imaginary part k sin(k log(u/3))→r/3 and its modulus is ≤r/3. The kernel is real before the rotation, so the o(1) of the profile is not multiplied by k in this second row. The envelopes of 212 dominate by C(1+r)exp(−c r), uniformly for compact d. The cut includes each fixed r because v<m'(1/2)<2log M₁(3), where m' is understood as the derivative of the logarithm of M with respect to a. The integrals in r give J and J²/3. Multiplying by p^(−1/2)X^(β−1) and summing log p with PNT cancels all powers of X. The PNT step is uniform in the phase Nθ by linearity in sine and cosine.

**Three independent real controls.** Divide the columns by their positive normalised masses. Up to a common nonzero complex factor, the points of their last two rows are the image of exp((−1/2−a)d_j) exp(ixd_j) under rotation by Nθ and the real map z↦(Re(Jz),Re(J²z/3)). This map is invertible since J is not real. The factor of the width h is common and nonzero when h is chosen small. The three positive radii on directions at 120 degrees form a triangle with the origin in its interior. Its area stays bounded away from zero under all rotations followed by the fixed map. Thus, on adding the mass row, the exact three-by-three matrix has inverse of bounded norm independently of sufficiently large k (for fixed a). Imposing zero mass, the block can contribute any complex value of modulus ≤c_a S/k. This radius grows like exp(k²m−O_a(√k+log k)), with m>0.

**Construction on disjoint blocks.** At step j fix a_j=1/(j+3) and take k as large as needed to apply the bounds above FOR THAT a_j. Place X above the maximal cut of all previous k. The authentic C_k costs exp(O(k)) by 217; for the finite number of already altered primes, the contribution is bounded as k tends to infinity by the envelope of 211 in the integral of 217. Therefore the capacity S/k exceeds the required difference relative to z(k). Solve the exact system, with target mass zero. The weights remain positive and future blocks do not alter any already fixed datum. On a block the accumulated change of count is O_j(X^β) by Chebyshev and returns to zero when the block closes. Choose k furthermore so large that the constant of this bound is absorbed by X^(a_j), which gives a bound y^(1/2+2a_j) on that block. Since a_j→0, this proves the ceiling for each ε. All relative weights tend to one.

**Difference from 167 and 219.** 167 uses an oscillating bias in the weights and 219 preserves two boundary data of the cell at scale k/3; neither of those arguments programs the complex global sample of 217. Here the second coordinate is used, smaller by a factor k but linearly independent, in the distant block log p≈k²v. The falsifier proves sensitivity of the complete sample along a subsequence. It is not a falsifier of the exact Euler-product identities of ζ nor does it control samples in consecutive blocks of the criterion of 220.
