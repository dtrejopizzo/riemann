# Only descending crossings can host a maximum violating the prime ceiling

With S,H_n,Q_n of 092–093, it suffices to prove B_n≥0 for all sufficiently large n satisfying strictly p_n<H_n<p_(n+1). The other prefixes can be omitted simultaneously in this proof. If the sign fails, at these same crossings B_n takes negative values without lower bound.

Indeed, set F(x)=S(x)−x. If the sign fails, J of 088 admits no constant one-sided bound, on either side, on a tail: apply Landau to constant ±J as in 088. By 092 there are arbitrarily late values of F/√x of both signs and of arbitrarily large moduli. For each M>0 one can therefore choose three points in order with F negative, then F>M, then F negative, all as late as desired. On the compact interval so formed the maximum is interior and exceeds M. Between primes F'=H_n/x−1, decreasing; at a prime the right derivative strictly exceeds the left one. A local maximum is impossible there, even if one of the one-sided derivatives is zero. Hence the maximum is exactly at H_n inside the gap and equals −B_n. The other direction is 093. This proof keeps the absolute excess M, without claiming that its size normalized by the root of the point where the maximum is attained is preserved.

**Crossings for a root-scale tolerance.** For any prescribed C≥0 apply the same argument to F_C=F−C√x. Its derivative in the gap has the sign of H_n−x−C√x/2; the jump at the primes is still positive. The only candidate is y_n=[(sqrt(C²/4+4H_n)−C/2)/2]². The sign is equivalent to checking H_n log y_n−Q_n≤y_n+C√y_n only if p_n<y_n<p_(n+1), eventually. Under RH, 092 gives F<0 eventually. If it fails, F_C has positive maxima without bound. The case C>0 locates a violation of the normalized ceiling at its own shifted crossings.

Using a crossing requires later negative points: it would not suffice to look for local maxima of a continuous function that could be positive and increasing on a whole tail. That possibility is excluded here by the two-sided oscillation argument. What remains is to control the accumulated value at the crossings. Their frequency and density among the primes have not been estimated.
