# A single sum over primes suffices, and its logarithmic scale is necessary in both directions

With U from 069 and the original average of lengths 1/[n(n+1)] from 047, let
     
    J(x)=Σ_{p prime} (log p) U(x/p).

The global sign is equivalent to J(j⁴)≤C j² log(j⁴) eventually. It is also equivalent to the same inequality for −J. Unconditionally, J(j⁴)/(j² log(j⁴)) has strictly positive limit superior and strictly negative limit inferior, infinite limits allowed. The goal is finiteness of the bound, not that the quotient tend to zero.

**One-sided converse.** Put L(s)=ζ'(s)/ζ(s) and T(s)=Σ_{p,k≥2}(log p)p^(−ks). For Re s>1, Ĵ=Û(−L−T). As in 060 and 069, Û=A L+B with A,B holomorphic in Re s>1/2 and rapidly decaying in height on closed strips around the critical line; this last statement also holds on the line. Indeed the factors ζ(4s),ζ(6s) are bounded there, the differences are finite, V̂ is rapidly decreasing and the compact correction of 060 has the same decay. A does not vanish for 1/2<Re s<1. T is holomorphic in that half-plane. Every zero of ζ in the open strip to the right of 1/2 produces in Ĵ a double pole with coefficient −A(ρ)mult(ρ)². On the real axis s>1/2 there is no singularity: at 1, Û has a double zero by 072. J and its first two logarithmic derivatives are O(x log x) by 069 and the prime sum of (log p)/p. Interpolating an upper bound between fourth powers costs O(√x log x) as in 073. By Landau, applying the bound to either of ±J, all those double poles are excluded and the sign holds by the bridge of 060.

**Necessity.** Under the sign, all nontrivial zeros lie on the critical line. First include all powers, with transform −LÛ. At s=1 no pole remains. On the line Re s=1/2+δ, 0<δ<1/10, the classical local formula for the logarithmic derivative of ζ gives a sum of 1/(s−ρ) over |Im ρ−Im s|≤1 plus O(log(2+|Im s|)), counting multiplicities. There are O(log(2+|t|)) zeros per interval of fixed length. On each unit interval at height n, Cauchy–Schwarz and the integral of 1/(δ²+t²) therefore give
    
    ∫ |L(1/2+δ+it)|² dt ≪ δ^(−1) log²(2+|n|).

This includes every n, enlarging the constant at bounded heights. The decay of A,B gives an integral norm of −LÛ of order O(1/δ). The Mellin shift from Re s>1 is valid by the local bound and that decay (for fixed δ); at the crossing of the real axis use the cancellation at 1. Setting δ=1/log x gives O(√x log x). Removing the composite powers costs O(√x log x) by 073 and the two-sided bound of U under the sign. Thus J satisfies even the required two-sided bound.

**Why waiting for a small improvement of the order in one direction does not suffice either.** Let ρ=1/2+iγ be the classical zero with 14<γ<15 (the first ordinate also appears in paper 17). A(ρ)≠0: the transform of the average of 047 on the imaginary axis vanishes only at nonzero multiples of 4π, by its uniform factors and local convergence of the product. The hat factor of 060 is nonzero there, as are the other factors of A. Near ρ and 1/2, T(s)=−L(2s)+R(s), with R holomorphic in Re s>1/3: subtract the even powers with exponent ≥2 and the odd exponents ≥3 remain. ζ(1+2iγ)≠0. Hence Ĵ has at ρ a double pole with nonzero coefficient d, and at 1/2 at most a simple pole.

Suppose the normalized limit superior on the samples for J or −J is ≤0. The converse above implies the sign. Under it, the first two logarithmic derivatives of U are O(√x): shift the Mellin integral of s^kÛ, k≤2, to Re s=0.45, collecting the simple poles of L on the critical line. The residues are absolutely summable by the rapid decay and the local zero count; on the horizontal sides choose heights at distance ≫1/log²(2+|t|) from their ordinates by the same count. The series on the shifted line is integrable by the local formula for L. Thus the second logarithmic derivative of J is O(√x Σ_{p≤Cx} log p/√p)=O(x) by Chebyshev. Linear interpolation (positive coefficients) now costs O(√x), negligible against √x log x.

For each ε>0 we then have ±J(x)≤ε√x log x on a tail. The transform of the nonnegative difference there satisfies |F(σ+iγ)|≤F(σ) for σ>1/2 by the two-sided bound already proved. Multiplying by (σ−1/2)² and letting σ↓1/2 gives |d|≤ε: the majorant has a double pole with coefficient ε at 1/2 and is regular at ρ; the lower cutoff only adds entire functions. Contradiction. Applying this to both signs proves the statement on the limits.

**Cross-check.** The index already discards using Selberg or the positivity of Λ*Λ as a sign mechanism (TB-3). Here no sign is deduced from those coefficients: it is shown that the second term of the difference of 073, isolated, has an equivalent one-sided bound and an optimal scale. The equivalence proof uses classical tools on zeros and the earlier bridge. It remains to prove the uniform bound for J without assuming the sign.
