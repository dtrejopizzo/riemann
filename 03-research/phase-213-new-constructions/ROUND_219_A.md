# What centring and separately controlling the endpoints of 218 demands

Fix x=exp(k/3), 0≤h≤1/3 and E(y)=θ(y)−y. On the cell (x,xe^h] define the contribution of 218 minus its continuous density,

    S_k(h)=∫_(x,xe^h] y^(−1/2) I_k(log y) dE(y).

The coefficient I_k and the factor F_k are those of 218. There are constants independent of large k such that

    sup_h |S_k(h)| ≍ |F_k| x^(−1/2) sup_h |E(xe^h)−E(x)|.

In particular requiring sup_h|S_k(h)|≤exp(o(k)) on ALL large cells is equivalent to RH. Separating the two centred parts of 218 while requiring stability under each shift h reproduces exactly the classical prime-counting obstacle. This does not invalidate the criterion of 217, which only asks for a subsequence for the complete combination.

**Uniform inversion on the cell.** Write Q_k(d)=exp(−d/2) I_k(k/3+d)/F_k. The uniform profile of 218 implies convergence to H exp(−(1/2+3i)d) and in particular inf|Q_k|≥c>0. The variation must be controlled, since uniform convergence alone does not allow the Stieltjes integral to be inverted. In the definition of I_k, at fixed u the indicator 1_{k/3+d≤log X_(k²)(u)} has at most one jump in d. By 211, the supremum and the variation of its product with c_(k²)(k/3+d;u) cost ≤Cu(1+u)/k on the cell. Integrating this bound against u^(−√k−1)du over [3,k²] gives O(|F_k|) for large k. The variation of Q_k is bounded, and so is that of its inverse by the lower bound. Q_k is continuous: at each d the cut equality occurs at at most one u because M₁ is strictly decreasing; apply dominated convergence in u. For any finite complex measure ν on the cell, integration by parts gives sup_h |∫_(0,h] Q_k dν|≤(sup|Q_k|+Var Q_k) sup_h |ν((0,h])|. Apply the same to Q_k⁻¹ and Q_k dν for the reverse inequality. Take ν the transport of dE by d=log(y/x). This establishes the two bounds with exact normalisation.

**Classical scope.** log|F_k|=o(k). If the S_k have the required ceiling, the increments of E on each cell are O_ε(x^(1/2+ε)) for every ε>0. Consecutive cells cover the half-axis and the geometric sum of their ceilings gives E(y)=O_ε(y^(1/2+ε)). The converse is immediate. Under RH the classical von Koch bound for ψ applies, cited in R166_802, and ψ−θ=O(√y log² y). In the reverse direction, the ceiling for each ε gives by partial summation that −ζ'/ζ(s)−s/(s−1) extends holomorphically to Re s>1/2 via s∫₁^∞(ψ(y)−y)y^(−s−1)dy. By symmetry this implies RH. The equivalence is an identification of an already travelled route, not a proof of the ceiling.

**Checking only the right endpoint loses information.** Here is an exact perturbation that preserves both the value S_k(1/3) and the prime mass of each cell. For 1/2<β<1 divide the logarithmic cell into four equal subintervals. In each I_j multiply the weight log p of each prime by 1+x^(β−1)b_j. Choose a real vector b of maximum norm one in the kernel of the three rows formed by the sums of log p, and by the real and imaginary parts of the sums of (log p)p^(−1/2)I_k(log p), one column per subinterval. It exists by dimension. Do this on each sufficiently large cell. The weights remain positive on the same primes and their relative variation tends to zero. The three constraints preserve both data exactly, even though the perturbations change from cell to cell. On the subinterval with |b_j|=1 the change of mass is comparable to x^β by PNT. At one of its endpoints the accumulated difference between the prime and perturbed measures has modulus ≥c x^β, despite vanishing at both edges of the complete cell. Therefore the single-endpoint complex measurement and the total mass do not allow the interior profile to be recovered uniformly in this class of positive measures. This does not construct a counterexample on the authentic weights of ζ, nor does it necessarily preserve the complete C_k. 167 also perturbs positive weights; here the invisibility conditions for both boundary data are exact.

**Decision.** Do not attempt to close 217 by applying von Koch under the name of uniform local control of the centred blocks. A closure via the single endpoint would need to exploit relations between cells or an additional property of the authentic weights; the boundary data above allow variations of order x^β.
