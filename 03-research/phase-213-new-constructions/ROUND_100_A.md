# A conservative cut for scale-dependent splittings

Let ν be positive on (u,y), u≥1, 0<y<1, with finite mass where u≤R for each R. Arbitrary dependence between u and y is allowed. Write h(t)=∫ K_y(t/u)dν with the carry of 099. There exist finite R and ε>0 independent of ν such that

    ∫₁ᴿ |h(t)−log t|dt/t² ≥ ε.

**Cut.** Each splitting contributes one unit of measure at u and subtracts units at u/y and u/(1−y). The resulting signed measure σ is locally finite. The weighted accumulation C_h(X)=∫_[1,X]dσ(v)/v is non-negative: before the parent nothing enters, afterwards only parts of 1/u have been subtracted, which add up exactly to 1/u. For G(t)=σ([1,t]), h(t)=Σ_{n≤t} G(t/n). By inversion G(t)=Σ_{n≤t} μ(n)h(t/n). Integration by parts gives C_h(X)=G(X)/X+∫₁ˣ G(t)dt/t², including any initial atom.

**Profile forced by the target.** For h=log on t≥1 (zero below), G is continuous, G(1)=0 and G'(t)=M(t)/t almost everywhere, where M is the Möbius sum. Hence C_log(X)=∫₁ˣ M(t)dt/t². For Re s>0 its Mellin transform with respect to dX/X is 1/[s(s+1)ζ(1+s)], by integration and absolute convergence. This function is regular on the whole real segment s>−1: at zero the pole of ζ cancels s. However there is a pole at s=ρ−1 for the first critical zero. If C_log were eventually non-negative, Landau (removing a compact interval only adds an entire function) would force abscissa ≤−1, impossible by that pole. The same holds for −C_log. By continuity there is a compact interval I on which C_log is strictly negative.

**Stable separation.** Integrate C_h over I in dX. In terms of h this is a bounded linear functional on L¹([1,R],dt/t²), R=max I: in G only n≤R occur and after t=n v each integral of |h(t/n)| with t in [1,R] is bounded by a finite constant times the stated norm. For the term G(X)/X the same argument holds after integrating in X. The functional gives value ≥0 for every admissible h and a strictly negative value for log. Its gap divided by its norm gives ε. For a fixed pointwise majorization on [1,R], PNT turns this loss into an excess of order x in the prime band [x/R,x]: h is of bounded variation on the compact set by local finiteness of the parents and the uniform bound on the jumps of each kernel.

**Scope.** The constant and the band here are existential. 099 required a scale-independent splitting; here conservation of sizes is used instead of factoring their Mellin average. The same cut works for splittings into any prescribed finite number of positive parts summing to one. An estimate that compensates errors of opposite signs in the prime sum is not excluded.
