## CM multiplication polynomial from its principal part

For the nonzero CM endomorphism alpha of norm N used in conj_sgStar, f is odd, has simple zero at zero with f'(0)=1, and has zero set the lattice. The function H(z)=f(alpha z)^2/f(z)^(2N) is even. Its quasi-period multipliers cancel by alpha*conj(alpha)=N, so it is elliptic with its only possible pole modulo the lattice at zero. Its leading Laurent term is alpha² z^(-2(N-1)). Since wp(z)=z^-2+O(z²), subtract alpha² wp^(N-1), then successively the multiple of each lower power required to remove the current even pole. The holomorphic elliptic remainder is constant. This proves H=P(wp) and identifies the degree N-1 and leading coefficient alpha², including N=1.

For an intermediate field M containing alpha, all coefficients of f, and g2,g3, the Laurent divisions above are over M: f/z has invertible constant 1 and the Laurent series for wp is over Q(g2,g3). Every subtraction therefore takes place over M, including the final constant. This replaces the coefficientwise linear retraction in EndSer.descend for this normalized application. ## Direct calculation of the unshifted CM normalization

Write f(z)=z+a z³+O(z⁵), and let H be the elliptic quotient in the preceding entry. On E=C/Lambda put S=ker[alpha] minus the origin. The kernel has N elements by the lattice index (the real determinant of multiplication by alpha is its norm). Then H=alpha² times the product over u in S of (wp(z)-wp(u)). Indeed each pair u,-u of distinct points contributes a double zero at both points; a nonzero point of order two occurs once and wp is ramified there, giving a double zero as well. These are all zeros of the numerator off the origin, since the zeros of f are simple by its quasi-period law and f'(0)=1. Both sides have pole order 2(N-1) at the origin and the same leading coefficient. Their ratio is constant on the elliptic curve, equal to one by this normalization. The empty product covers N=1.

Comparing the next Laurent coefficients yields 2a(alpha²-N) = -sum_{u in S} wp(u). Here wp has zero constant Laurent coefficient. Since alpha²=-N, the divisor is nonzero. ## Direct proof of the pointwise Smith-factor inequality

FinalD.lean:14–17 combines LieMat and LieRoots. Here is a finite polynomial proof.
