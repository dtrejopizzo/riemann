# Reduction to indices below an arbitrary power with local observation

Fix 0<δ<1, N=x^δ, h=x^(1−δ/2) and D=x^o(1) as in 317. Choose φ real smooth with compact support in (0,1), integral one and moments 1 to r vanishing, with (r+1)δ/2>1/2. It exists as a combination of a smooth function of integral one and its first r derivatives, adjusting the moments in triangular order. Let ψ be smooth, zero up to 1 and one from 2 on. Define

    J(x,t)=Σ_(D<n<2N) g(n)(1−ψ(n/N)) ∫φ(u) C_t((x+uh)/n) du.

RH is equivalent to the integral over 1≤t≤2 of (−J(x,t))₊ being O_ε(x^(1/2+ε)) for every ε>0 and every sufficiently large x. A fixed choice of δ in that interval suffices, however small. The retained arguments are ≥x^(1−δ)/2, inside windows of relative width x^(−δ/2). It is a criterion for a smoothed balance with a weight that may change sign, not an estimate of the unsmoothed balance at each x.

**Disappearance of the tail.** Write T(x,t)=∫φ(u) C_t(x+uh)du. Freeze D,q,g when integrating in u. By 317 the full mean with g has a root ceiling. The term n=1 is T. We will show that the stretch with ψ(n/N) costs O_A(x^(−A)) for every fixed A. On exchanging coefficients, to each m≤2x/N corresponds the sampling with g of the function in v

    F_m(v)=ψ(v/N) ∫_((mv−x)/h)^∞ φ(u)du.

This function is smooth with compact support in v>0. For j≥1 its j-th derivative has L¹ norm ≪_j min(N,h/m)^(1−j). The pure terms live in a transition of length O(N) or O(h/m); the mixed ones live in their intersection and are bounded by Leibniz. Throughout the stretch min(N,h/m)≫x^(δ/2). Since g=1*a, the sampling is Σ_d a(d)Σ_l F_m(dl). The integral term of Poisson vanishes by Σ_d a(d)/d=0. For j≥2, the remaining modes cost ≪_j ||F_m^(j)||₁ Σ_d |a(d)|d^(j−1), by integration by parts and convergence of Σ k^(−j). The d are ≤qD and Σ|a(d)|≤(1+q)D, so for fixed j that last sum is x^o(1). Now sum |c_t(m)|≪_ε m^ε and take j large enough depending on δ,A. This gives the saving of every order, uniformly for 0≤t≤2. Consequently T(x,t)+J(x,t)=O_ε(x^(1/2+ε)).

**Local smoothing preserves the exterior poles.** Under RH the modulus ceiling for T holds by 317, even though φ has both signs. For the converse, the one-sided Mellin transform of T differs from that of C_t by a function holomorphic in Re s>1−(r+1)δ/2. To see this integrate initially in Re s>1 from a large fixed scale. For each u change to y=x+u x^(1−δ/2). Write x=y v(z), z=u y^(−δ/2), where v+z v^(1−δ/2)=1 and v(0)=1. By local analytic inversion, the weight x^(−1−s)dx is y^(−1−s)dy times a factor analytic in z, equal to one at zero, with Taylor expansion and remainder uniform for s in compact sets. The differences at the lower endpoint are entire in s. After integrating in u one gets 1+O_s(y^(−(r+1)δ/2)), and |C_t(y)|≪_ε y^(1+ε) gives the claimed domain, uniformly in frequency on compact sets of s. Given an exterior zero choose the smooth positive average in t that detects the pole of 317. The average of T preserves the pole and is regular on the real axis s>1/2. A ceiling for the integral of T₊ prevents it by Landau as in 318. The uniform approximation between T and −J allows using directly the ceiling for (−J)₊.

**Cross-check.** Round 049 already uses smoothness and cancellation of the integral mode to cut a bilinear sum down to factors near the root. Here the observed scale fraction tends to zero as a power, the object is the ordinary summatory function C_t of 317 observed in that window, and the criterion preserves poles via vanishing moments in the nonlinear change of scale. No novelty is attributed to Poisson summation. The one-sided ceiling of the band D<n<2x^δ is missing; the passage to δ=0 is not justified.
