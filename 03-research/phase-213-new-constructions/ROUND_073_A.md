# The cost of all composite prime powers in a recurrence for the full criterion

Let U be the equivalent representative of 069 and 072. Write Φ(x)=Σ_{n≥1}U(x/n) and μ*Φ(x)=Σ μ(n)Φ(x/n)=U(x), by finite inversion at fixed x thanks to the lower support. Φ can be built without U: its Mellin transform in Re s>1 is
     
    2 H(s)ζ(4s)ζ(6s) P(s),    H(s)=[I'(2s)−b₀I(2s)]/ζ(s),
    P(s)=(1−4^(1/3−s))(1−4^(1/4−s)).

Here H is the entire function of 068, expressible directly with η, η', V̂ and V̂'. The pole at 1/4 cancels. By shifting the contour to any fixed negative real line one obtains Φ(x)=c x^(1/6)+O_N(x^(−N)) for large x (possible residue of ζ(6s)); the same argument holds for logarithmic derivatives with the corresponding residue. As before, Φ vanishes below a threshold v₀>0.

**A single pending estimate.** Form the difference
     
    E(x)=Σ_n μ(n) log(x/n) Φ(x/n) − Σ_{p prime} log p Σ_n μ(n)Φ(x/(pn)).

The global sign is equivalent to E(j⁴)≤C j² log(j⁴) for every sufficiently large j. In the two-sided version, the implication towards the bound for U admits a proof by induction with limiting factor 1/2.

Indeed, divisor inversion gives μ(n)log n=−(μ*Λ)(n), by differentiating 1/ζ or by factorization. Separating from Λ the powers of exponent one leaves the exact recurrence
    
    E(x)=(log x) U(x)+Σ_{p, k≥2} (log p) U(x/p^k).

At root scale, the sum of the weights of the composite powers with p^k≤x/v₀ is
     
    Σ_{p,k≥2} (log p) p^(−k/2) = (1/2)log x+O(1)    [p^k≤x/v₀].

Indeed for k=2 use Σ_{p≤z}(log p)/p=log z+O(1), and for k≥3 the full series converges. Under the sign U has a two-sided root bound and this bounds E two-sidedly by O(√x log x).

Conversely, under a continuous two-sided bound for E, solve for U in the recurrence. For large x the contribution of the smaller scales is at most (1/2+o(1)) times their supremum normalized by the root, after dividing by √x log x. All smaller arguments are ≤x/4. Choosing x₀ so that the factor is ≤3/4, covering first a compact interval, and then extending over intervals of ratio four gives a finite global supremum. The factor 1/2 is the effective mass of this argument with absolute values.

For the converse from a single inequality at j⁴ we use the bridge of 060. The first two logarithmic derivatives of E and E are O(x log x) at infinity by the recurrence and Σ_{p,k≥2} log p/p^k<∞. Linear interpolation in log x between samples (distance O(x^(−1/4))) adds O(√x log x) to the upper bound. In Re s>1 one has
     
    Ê(s)=−Û'(s)+Û(s) Σ_{p,k≥2} (log p)p^(−ks).

The series converges normally in Re s>1/2. The expression is regular on the real axis of that half-plane by 069. An eventual upper bound on the continuum forces holomorphy of Ê on the whole half-plane by Landau applied to C√x log x−E on a tail. A pole of Û of order d there would give a pole of order d+1 of Ê, impossible to cancel by the second term. Thus Û is holomorphic in the half-plane and 069 applies. The two-sided induction alone does not justify the one-sided converse.

**Why change the split between kernel and coefficients.** Keeping K of 069 as kernel gives the arithmetic factor ζ(6s)/[ζ(s)ζ(2s)ζ(3s)] when summing all rows. In the same logarithmic identity the coefficient of log p for p^k goes from 1 to 1+2·1_{2|k}+3·1_{3|k}−6·1_{6|k}. For k=2 it is 3 and the normalized cost of the composite powers tends to 3/2, which does not close that induction in modulus. The split with μ preserves U and brings that cost below one.

**Scope.** Logarithmic differentiation of the Möbius series is classical. 072 separated a large prime with unit coefficient; here the identity groups all primes and leaves a contractive contribution of the composite powers in the two-sided proof. The estimate on the difference E, including the primes of exponent one, remains open. The contraction does not control its source term.

**Signed cost and cancellation of the main term.** Under RH the sum of all composite powers in the recurrence costs O(√x), uniformly. For k≥3 the absolute series of this note suffices. For k=2 cut at Z=√x (if v₀<1, the terms with √x<p≤√(x/v₀) cost O(√x) by Chebyshev and boundedness of U on the corresponding compact set). In p≤Z insert the expansion of 075 with remainder O((x/p²)^0.45). By Chebyshev the remainder costs O(x^0.45 Z^0.1)=O(√x). For each γ≠0 one has

    |Σ_(p≤Z) (log p) p^(−1−2iγ)| ≪ 1+|γ|+1/|γ|.

Indeed under RH θ(t)=t+O(√t log²(2t)); integrating the error by parts against t^(−1−2iγ) costs O(1+|γ|) by a convergent integral, and the main integral costs O(1/|γ|). Summing against the rapidly decreasing residues of 075 gives the result. It remains true that the absolute mass of the weights grows like (log x)/2.

In particular E(x)=(log x)U(x)+O(√x) under RH. Let K(x)=Σ μ(n) log(x/n)Φ(x/n)=E(x)+J(x). The dyadic profile of 075 then gives

    K(x)/(√x log x) = Σ_γ (1−m_γ)b_γ x^(iγ)+o(1).

Thus the main cancellation of this arithmetic sum leaves only the multiple zeros visible through the weight. The local check does not presuppose RH: if Û=Aζ'/ζ+B near a right zero ρ of multiplicity m, the transform of K has double-pole coefficient (1−m)Res_ρ Û, from −Û' plus the multiplier −ζ'/ζ of the full convolution. Indeed K=E+J contains the full convolution with Λ. At a simple zero the double term disappears but a simple pole may remain. No unconditional ceiling for U is deduced from that cancellation.

**Remaining residue and a proof with two scales of the same weight.** Put Q=Φ̂=ζÛ and K=μ*(log(·)Φ). Then K̂=−Q'/ζ by differentiating the single weight before inversion. At a simple right zero ρ, Q(ρ)=ζ'(ρ) Res_ρ Û≠0 (074). The remaining residue of K̂ is exactly −Q'(ρ)/ζ'(ρ); it vanishes if and only if Q'(ρ)=0. For multiplicity m≥2, Q has order m−1 and its derivative order m−2, so that K̂ keeps a double pole.

The possible coincidence Q'(ρ)=0 is resolved for detection with two explicit weights. For d>0 take Φ_d(x)=Φ(x/d), K_d=μ*(log(·)Φ_d). The numerator of K̂_d is d^(−s)[(log d)Q−Q']. Comparing d=1 and d=2 excludes that the two residues vanish at the same simple right zero. In particular, at least one of the residues of K̂ and of 2^s K̂₂ has modulus ≥(log 2)|Res_ρ Û|/2. This gives a guarantee uniform in the ordinate and requires no global simplicity hypothesis.

In arithmetic coordinates the operation is exactly K₂(2x)−K(x)=(log 2)U(x), summing term by term. Thus any proof by subtraction of two two-sided estimates at root scale already has to control U at that scale. The cancellation of 073 remains of interest for identifying which multiplicities dominate, but doubling the weight to recover the simple zeros does not reduce the global obligation. There are no two proved root estimates here.

**Large composite powers: unconditional reduction with error smaller than root.** Fix B>0 and Y=(log x)^B. Sum only over p^k≥x/Y, k≥2, and denote the sum by C_B(x), with the same weights (log p)U(x/p^k). For each A>0 one has without RH

    C_B(x)/√x = (1/2) ∫₀^Y U(v)v^(−3/2)dv + O_(A,B)((log x)^(−A)).

For squares, replace dθ(t) by dt in t≥Z=√(x/Y). By 069, |U(x/t²)|≪x/t² and its derivative in t is O(x/t³); the integral ends at t≤√(x/v₀). Using the unconditional PNT with error O_N(t/log^N t), including the one-sided endpoint θ(Z−), gives by parts an error O_N(x/(Z log^N Z)). Since log Z is comparable to log x and √Y=(log x)^(B/2), the announced precision follows. Substituting v=x/t² in the continuous integral gives the factor 1/2. For k≥3, the weighted count up to t of these powers is O(t^(1/3)log(2t)) by Chebyshev and k≤log₂t. Partial summation and the bound |U(x/p^k)|≪x/p^k give O(x(x/Y)^(−2/3)log x), also absorbed. Both errors are uniform for large x.

The remaining integral has an exact interpretation. Let I(Y)=∫₀^Y U(v)v^(−3/2)dv. Its Mellin transform is Û(s+1/2)/s in Re s>1/2 by Fubini. It is regular on the real axis s>0, and a right zero produces a pole at s=ρ−1/2 by 074. If I were bounded above, Landau applied to a constant minus I would exclude such a pole. Under RH, the expansion of 075 on v≥1 makes I bounded: integrating each v^(iγ−1) costs ≤2/|γ| and the remainder O(v^(−1.05)) is integrable. A ceiling for I at Y=(4 log j)^B suffices, since |I'(Y)|≪Y^(−1/2) and the error up to the adjacent sample tends to zero. Thus, for any fixed positive B, a ceiling O(√x) for C_B alone at fourth powers is already equivalent to the sign. This is an obstruction to treating the tail of large powers separately: its discrepancy against the density is indeed resolved without RH; its continuous term retains the whole criterion.

**Localization of the Möbius indices of the primitive.** The centering of 068 gives H(1/2)=0, so Q(1/2)=Φ̂(1/2)=0 by the initial formula of this note. The integral defining this moment converges absolutely. Hence for Ψ(z)=∫₀^z Φ(t)t^(−3/2)dt one has Ψ(z)=−3c z^(−1/3)+O_N(z^(−N)) to any order at infinity, and Ψ=0 below v₀. Changing v=nt in the finite sum defining I(Y) gives exactly I(Y)=Σ μ(n)n^(−1/2)Ψ(Y/n).

Let q=2^(−1/3) and W(z)=Ψ(z)−qΨ(z/2). The computed tail term cancels. W is rapidly decreasing and has the same lower threshold. Hence, for each fixed 0<ε<1 and A>0,

    I(Y)−qI(Y/2) = Σ_(Y^(1−ε)<n≤Y/v₀) μ(n)n^(−1/2) W(Y/n) + O_(A,ε)(Y^(−A)).

Indeed the omitted terms cost in absolute value O_N(Y^(−N) (Y^(1−ε))^(N+1/2)), which gives the announced error choosing N large. A uniform ceiling of the localized sum for large real Y implies that of I by geometric iteration with q<1, absorbing the initial compact interval. Under RH I is two-sidedly bounded, which gives the converse. Thus every fixed sublinear power of small indices is removed without RH at negligible cost for this filtered primitive. The cancellation of the pole at 1/6 by dilations is akin to 069; here the exact null moment at 1/2 and the positive contraction needed to preserve the one-sided ceiling also intervene. The Möbius sum with n between Y^(1−ε) and Y/v₀ persists.

**What happens when grouping all small primes before taking moduli.** Let z=log Y, R the product of the primes up to z and W_R(u)=Σ_(d|R) μ(d)d^(−1/2)W(u/d). Decompose each squarefree integer as dm, d|R and (m,R)=1. The full filtered sum of the previous section becomes exactly Σ_((m,R)=1) μ(m)m^(−1/2)W_R(Y/m). Even after making all those internal cancellations, the cost of summing the moduli of the groups is

    Σ_((m,R)=1) μ²(m)m^(−1/2)|W_R(Y/m)| ≫ √Y/(log log Y)².

To see this, the count of squarefree integers coprime to R up to t≥1 is κ_R t+O(2^π(z)√t), where κ_R=ζ(2)^(−1)∏_(p|R)(1+1/p)^(−1). This is obtained by expanding μ²(m)=Σ_(a²|m)μ(a) and using inclusion-exclusion for the cofactor coprime to R. Both W_R and its first logarithmic derivative are bounded by O(∏_(p|R)(1+p^(−1/2)))=Y^o(1) by the divisor expansion and the bounds for W. Since π(log Y)=o(log Y), partial summation with m^(−1/2)|W_R(Y/m)| gives

    sum of moduli = κ_R √Y ∫₀^∞ |W_R(u)|u^(−3/2)du + O(Y^o(1)).

The derivative in m costs O(Y^o(1)m^(−3/2)) almost everywhere; integrating up to Y/v₀ introduces only log Y, and adding the continuous interval 0<m<1 costs Y^o(1). To bound the integral below choose a fixed real t with Ŵ(1/2+it)≠0, which exists by Fourier uniqueness and W not identically zero (W=0 would imply Ψ=0 by iteration and then Φ=0). The transform of W_R at that point is Ŵ(1/2+it)∏_(p|R)(1−p^(−1−it)). Its modulus is ≥|Ŵ(1/2+it)|∏_(p|R)(1−1/p), and is dominated by the integral of moduli. Mertens gives both κ_R and this last product ≫1/log z. This proves the bound. The same obstruction includes grouping by odd part alone taking R=2 fixed, with cost of order √Y. Control of moduli between groups thus loses much more than the required constant ceiling, even though the number of primes treated within each group grows with Y.

**Signed balance of the prime cofactor for the moving primorial.** In the grouping above, z=log Y, let P_R be the contribution of prime m, m>z, including its sign μ(m)=−1. With q=2^(−1/3), D=Û''(1)>0 from 072 and L=log Y, one has unconditionally

    P_R ∼ D(1−q/√2) [∏_(p≤z)(1−1/p)] √Y/L².

Indeed Ŵ(s)=(1−q2^(−s))Q(s+1/2)/s, with Q=ζÛ. By 072, Q(1)=0, Q'(1)=D/2. Thus Ŵ(1/2)=0 and Ŵ'(1/2)=C=D(1−q/√2)>0. For large X, by PNT and partial summation with this fixed weight,

    −Σ_p p^(−1/2) W(X/p) = √X [C/(log X)² + O(1/(log X)³)].

To justify the error, p<√X costs O(X^(−1)) by rapid decay to sufficient order, and in p≥√X the PNT with any logarithmic power of saving allows replacing the sum by the density dt/log t: by parts one uses that W and its logarithmic derivative decay rapidly. In the resulting integral with u=X/t expand 1/(log X−log u) to first order; the constant moment vanishes and the logarithmic moment is −Ŵ'(1/2). The outer sign reverses it.

Expand W_R over d|R. For d≤√Y the formula applies with X=Y/d; excluding p≤z costs O(Y^(−1)) in total by rapid decay and Σ_(d|R)d^(−1/2)=Y^o(1). After extracting √Y, the error of replacing (log(Y/d))^(−2) by L^(−2) is O(L^(−3)Σ_(d|R)(1+log d)/d)=O(L^(−3)(log(2z))²), by Euler products and Mertens. For d>√Y use the absolute bound O(√X) for the prime sum (it already holds summing over all integers by support and boundedness). The weight ∑_(d|R,d>√Y)1/d is O(exp(−L/(2log z))(log z)^O(1)) by Rankin with exponent 1/log z and the product of (1+p^(−1+1/log z)). This also allows completing the signed product of the small d. The errors are smaller than the main term since z=L.

The total balance I(Y)−qI(Y/2) is o(√Y/L^A) for each fixed A by the quantitative PNT for U of 072, integrating over v≤√Y and √Y<v≤Y separately. Thus the remaining cofactors have asymptotic balance −P_R. The moment mechanism of 072 is a direct antecedent; here the sectors are the prime cofactor after grouping ALL divisors of the moving primorial, and its complement. The first signed term and its compensation are obtained, without yet controlling the joint error at constant scale.

**Filter towards larger scales.** For F(Y)=I(Y)−qI(Y/2), take TF(Y)=F(Y)−2^(−1/2)F(2Y). This orientation preserves the one-sided ceiling: iterating towards infinity the final term 2^(−k/2)F(2^kY) tends to zero for each Y by F(X)=o(√X), already proved without RH. Thus TF≤M on a tail implies F≤M/(1−2^(−1/2)) there. Under RH F is two-sidedly bounded. For each fixed order r the same argument holds step by step for T^r because finite combinations remain o(√X).

The weight before grouping with the same R=∏_(p≤log Y)p in all terms is now W_r=(1−2^(−1/2)D₂)^rW, with D₂W(u)=W(2u). Its Mellin transform is (1−2^(s−1/2))^r Ŵ(s); the first nonzero moment at s=1/2 is the derivative of order r+1, with value (r+1)! (−log 2)^r C. The expansion of the prime density of the previous paragraph to order r+1 gives, for the prime cofactor of the new weight, the equivalent

    (r+1)! (log 2)^r C ∏_(p≤log Y)(1−1/p) √Y/(log Y)^(r+2).

Indeed the sign of the prime sum is minus and the logarithmic moment of degree r+1 introduces (−1)^(r+1). The errors are estimated for fixed r with one more absolute moment, giving after extracting √Y the error O_r((log log Y)^2/(log Y)^(r+3)) by the same cut in d. The lower threshold of the weight becomes v₀/2^r. It is essential to regroup at the same R; applying T directly to the equivalent with moving R does not justify this formula.

This construction unconditionally cancels any FIXED number of density terms while preserving the criterion. Each fixed order leaves a divergent positive prime balance. Passing to an order growing with Y would require both errors uniform in r and a uniform one-sided inversion; the proved iteration only gives the factor (1−2^(−1/2))^(−r).

**An infinite filter with bounded positive inverse.** Take d_j=exp(j⁴) for j≥1, and T_j=Id−d_j^(−1/2)D_(d_j), where D_dF(Y)=F(dY). In this construction the loss of the one-sided inversion is bounded by the absolute constant ∏_(j≥1)(1−exp(−j⁴/2))^(−1). For F of the previous section there exists unconditionally G=(∏ T_j)F given by the series over finite subsets S, with term (−1)^|S| exp(−n/2) F(exp(n)Y), n=Σ_(j∈S)j⁴. Moreover

    F(Y)=Σ_(n≥0) p₄(n) exp(−n/2) G(exp(n)Y),

where p₄(n) counts partitions with parts j⁴, allowing repetitions. Hence a constant ceiling for G on a half-line suffices, and under RH G is two-sidedly bounded by the product of (1+exp(−j⁴/2)) and the norm of F.

**Convergence without RH.** From 072 and integration over (0,√X) and (√X,X) one has |F(X)|≪√X exp(−c√log X) for large X, reducing c if needed. For 0<t≤1, log Σ p₄(n)e^(−tn)=Σ_(k≥1)k^(−1)Σ_(j≥1) exp(−tkj⁴)≪t^(−1/4) by comparison with the integral in j. With t=n^(−4/5) this gives p₄(n)≤exp(C n^(1/5)). The absolute series over subsets is dominated coefficientwise by p₄. The double series of the composition with the inverse also converges absolutely: the product of the two majorants has coefficients ≤(n+1)exp(2C n^(1/5)), absorbed by exp(−c√n) for fixed Y. Thus one can regroup by n and the formal product cancels exactly.

For Re s<1/2, the multiplier of the dilations is M(s)=∏_(j≥1)(1−exp((s−1/2)j⁴)), normally convergent on compact sets and zero-free there. On the real axis s↑1/2 one has M(s)=O_r((1/2−s)^r) for each r, by the first r factors and because the rest lie in [0,1]. This gives vanishing of all orders by approximation **from the left**. It does not establish absolutely convergent moments at the boundary: in each factor the weighted absolute norm at s=1/2 is 2. Nor does it allow applying term by term the equivalent of the prime cofactor of fixed order. The uniform inversion is settled for the full arithmetic sum; the independent estimate G≤C remains open.

**Test of the filter on a positive density and loss of inversion in the limit.** Take F₀(Y)=√Y/(log Y)², Y>1, as an exact scalar model. For steps a_j=log d_j and L=log Y, the product of the first r filters applied to F₀ is

    √Y ∫₀^∞ t exp(−Lt) ∏_(j≤r)(1−exp(−a_j t)) dt.

It comes from the Laplace integral of L^(−2). The integrands decrease and are dominated by t exp(−Lt). For the steps j⁴ of the previous filter, the limit product is strictly positive for each t>0. The integral restricted to any interval [b,c] with 0<b<c<1/2 gives a lower bound a constant times Y^(1/2−c). Since the trivial majorant is √Y/L², the logarithmic exponent of the result is exactly 1/2. Flatness of all orders does not even reduce the power exponent of this model, although it does save all powers of the logarithm by integration.

There is an alternative that does bound the model, but reveals a defect when passing to the limit. Choose a_j=2 log j+4 log log j for j≥3. The sum Σ exp(−a_j/2)=Σ1/(j log²j) converges, so the finite inverses have uniformly bounded masses. For each t<1/2 one has Σ exp(−a_j t)=∞, and the infinite product in the integral is zero. Consequently the limit output lies between zero and √Y∫_(1/2)^∞ t exp(−Lt)dt=1/(2L)+1/L². Nevertheless F₀(Y) diverges: the one-sided inversion is not transmitted by mere pointwise convergence of the outputs. Even in the PNT growth class this is seen with F_α(Y)=Y^α, 0<α<1/2: the finite outputs are Y^α∏_(j≤r)(1−exp(−(1/2−α)a_j)), tend to zero and the input is unbounded. Injectivity is lost in that class; the mass bound only serves to recover the datum before the limit or under additional uniformity hypotheses on the future scales. It is a counterexample to the proposal of densifying the filter while keeping only the finite-mass inversion. It does not identify F₀ or F_α with the actual arithmetic sum.

**Independent review of the extensions: PROVED with the stated scopes.** The signed cost O(√x) of powers under RH uses an absolutely summable series of modes: the cost of each squares mode is O(1+|γ|+1/|γ|), absorbed by the decay of b_γ. The profile of K carries (1−m_γ)b_γ, because the residue b_γ of Û already includes one multiplicity and the prime convolution contributes the other. For Q=ζÛ, −Q'/ζ has residue −Q'(ρ)/ζ'(ρ) at a simple zero and double coefficient (1−m)ResÛ at a multiple one. The two dilated weights avoid simultaneous cancellation, but K₂(2x)−K(x)=(log2)U(x) exactly: this rules out a gain by subtraction of two two-sided estimates, not every possible joint one-sided inequality.

In the tail C_B, replacing θ by its density includes the boundary θ(Z−), even when Z is prime. Its normalized error is O_N((logx)^(B/2−N)); that of exponents ≥3 is O(x^(−1/6)(logx)^(2B/3+1)), smaller than any fixed negative power of the logarithm. The change v=x/t² fixes the factor 1/2. The separation between samples Y_j=(4logj)^B costs O((logj)^(B/2−1)/j)=o(1) using |I'|≤CY^(−1/2), so the one-sided criterion also holds at fourth powers. The primitive has Mellin transform Û(s+1/2)/s: the difficulty does not disappear when evaluating only polylogarithmic arguments.

The moment Q(1/2)=0 converges absolutely by Φ(t)=ct^(1/6)+O_N(t^(−N)). Thus Ψ(z)=−3cz^(−1/3)+O_N(z^(−N)) and q=2^(−1/3) cancels the tail exactly. The omitted cost of indices n≤Y^(1−ε) is O_N(Y^(−Nε+(1−ε)/2)), which gives any O(Y^(−A)). The inversion of I−qI(·/2) preserves upper ceilings because 0<q<1. The antecedent is the same principle of pole cancellation by dilation of 069; the content here is the normalization of the primitive and the range of indices, not a bound on the remaining balance.

For the moving primorial, the error of the squarefree coprime count is 2^π(z)√t and the norm of the weight and its derivative is Y^o(1). Partial summation produces error Y^o(1), smaller than √Y/(logz)². The fixed Mellin frequency need not be an ordinate of a zero: its product has modulus ≥∏(1−1/p). For the prime sector, Ŵ'(1/2)=D(1−q/√2), since Q'(1)=D/2 and the divisor s equals 1/2. The expansion of 1/(logX−logu) and the sign μ(p)=−1 give the positive main term. After regrouping at the same R, the relative error is O((logz)³/L), with L=logY, z=L; excluding p≤z and d>√Y is negligible. The moment mechanisms are those of 072; the moduli between groups remain too large and the two sectors compensate without constant precision.

For the finite filter towards larger scales, 2^(−k/2)F(2^kY)→0 justifies the one-sided inversion, and the first moment of the weight of order r is (r+1)!(-log2)^rC. The sign of the moment and the outer sign of the prime sum leave the positive coefficient (r+1)!(log2)^rC. R is kept fixed when regrouping and r fixed when estimating; the conditioning of the converse grows like (1−1/sqrt2)^(−r). No uniformity in growing r is obtained.

In the infinite filter j⁴, the bound p₄(n)≤exp(Cn^(1/5)) and the real ceiling |F(e^nY)|exp(−n/2)≤C_Yexp(−c sqrt n) make the double composition with the inverse absolutely convergent as well. Only afterwards are the formal coefficients cancelled and the exact identity obtained. The product M(s) is the symbol of the dilations in Re s<1/2; it is not claimed that the Mellin transform of F converges unconditionally in that strip nor that there are absolute moments at the boundary. For the model F₀, the product j⁴ is positive at each t>0 and keeps exponent 1/2 despite saving any power of the logarithm. In the denser family, the outputs of F_α tend exactly to 0 because Σexp(−(1/2−α)a_j)=∞; F_α does satisfy the PNT growth ceiling. This refutes inversion after a mere pointwise limit even with finite inverse masses, but it is not a falsifier of the arithmetic identity of U nor a proved ceiling for its filtered output.

**Response of the dense filter to a nonzero ordinate.** Let f(Y)=Y^(α+iγ) with 0<α<1/2 and γ real nonzero, and put w=1/2−α−iγ. The partial filters with steps a_j=2log j+4log log j multiply f by B_r=∏_(3≤j≤r)(1−exp(−w a_j)). For each such mode, liminf |B_r|=0 and limsup |B_r|=∞. Hence the limit that annihilated the real powers does not even define a finite complex output on these oscillatory witnesses.

Indeed, let D_r=r^(1−2Re w)(log r)^(−4Re w), which diverges. Expand log|1−z|=−Re z+O(|z|²) in the tail. The quadratic sum is o(D_r), since Σ|exp(−w a_j)|=O(D_r) and the individual moduli tend to zero. For the linear sum one has

    Σ_(3≤j≤r) j^(−2w)(log j)^(−4w)
        = r^(1−2w)(log r)^(−4w)/(1−2w) + o(D_r).

Passing to the integral costs O_w(1) because the derivative is absolutely integrable on [3,∞); differentiating the main term leaves an error integrable up to r of size O_w(1+D_r/log r), by integration by parts or real comparison. For a row with exceptional ℓ only the frequencies of that unique p that realize multiples of the reduced ratio and moreover p|b need to be retained. All remaining residues admit the budget O(1/δ) already paid in the proof of the list, even summed over those same rows. The sum of the retained terms remains open.

**Independent review of uniqueness and exclusion by characters: PROVED.** Uniformly in the list, A=O(H), P,Q≤H^ν and the indices n,m,n',m' are O(H^C). Eliminating A gives a nonzero form in two logarithms of distinct primes with positive integer coefficients. Matveev separates it by exp(−O(H^(2ν)log(2H))), while the majorant is δH^C=exp(−H/2+o(H)); since ν<1/2 they are incompatible for large X. If p=q, the same elimination leaves an integer of modulus less than one and forces exactly n'm=nm', not just proximity of ratios. This proves uniformity and uniqueness for the original broad list.

The characteristic equation of the periodic coefficients has roots exp(±iπ/3). Each character multiplied by exp(−ijτA) produces a geometric sum whose denominator can only be small if τA is near ±π/3 modulo 2π. With τ=πn/(3P), this is precisely equivalent to nA/P near 6ℤ±1. There is no additional resonance in the other four classes. Since n≡±1 modulo six, the reduced numerators and denominators remain coprime to six. The residues outside this unique prime and ratio admit C/δ, with uniform constant; their absolute sum and their weights are paid by the budget of the list, also when restricted to exceptional rows. No saving is obtained for the retained resonant sum.

**Payment by the integer difference of powers.** For each real exceptional pair write a/d for the reduced ratio m/n, g=|ℓ^d−p^a|. At the full ceiling M one can remove the contribution of all pairs with

    ℓ^(d−1)/g ≤ exp((1/2−ε)H),

for any fixed 0<ε<1/2. In particular all integer ratios (d=1) are removed and, without additional information on g, all d with (d−1)log ℓ≤(1/2−ε)H.

Indeed, for each witness of that ratio write n=hd, m=ha. By the certified proximity one has |d log ℓ−a log p|≤δ log p=o(1), hence max(ℓ^d,p^a)≤2ℓ^d. The integral of 1/t between both powers gives |d log ℓ−a log p|≥g/(2ℓ^d), and g≥1 by unique factorization. The partial sum of the resonant character costs O(log p/(h|d log ℓ−a log p|)), uniformly in its length, by the geometric formula. The other character and the residues unrelated to the ratio are already paid. Thus, even discarding the improvement 1/h, the multiplier after including the reciprocal weight 1/ℓ of the marking is O((log p)ℓ^(d−1)/g). The sum is over at most exp(O(H/log H)) primes ℓ, each with a single ratio; for each b the moduli of ALL residues have sum exp(o(H)). The cost of the weights 1/b is exp(o(H)). Including the marking exp(−H/2+o(H)) relative to √X one obtains exp(−εH+o(H)). The argument keeps the budget log b≤H^ν. For each fixed ε only pairs with d≥5 and g<ℓ^(d−1)exp(−(1/2−ε)H) remain, taking 0<ε<1/2. No bound on g eliminating those pairs has been given.

**Independent review of the payment by integer powers: PROVED.** Put Δ=d log ℓ−a log p. For each witness n=hd, m=ha, one has h|Δ|≤δlog p. In the resonant character its geometric denominator is comparable to h|Δ|/log p, uniformly over all retained multiples; the conjugate character is separated by a fixed angle and costs O(1). The inequality |Δ|≥g/(2ℓ^d) comes from integrating 1/t between the two integers and uses only their already certified proximity. No factor of ℓ is lost when combining it: the reciprocal weight of the marking is 1/ℓ, leaving exactly ℓ^(d−1)/g.

1/h can be discarded because the absolute sum of ALL residues of the kernel of b is already bounded by exp(o(H)); no additional multiplicity is added for the frequencies realizing that ratio. Summing the common list of exp(O(H/log H)) primes and the weights of b still costs exp(o(H)). Hence the written condition leaves exp(−εH+o(H)) and pays the full contribution of those pairs. With 0<ε<1/2, d=1 satisfies the condition by g≥1. Among reduced denominators coprime to six, the next ones are ≥5. This eliminates the indicated class, without claiming a uniform bound on g for the remaining pairs.

**Separation of the unpaid primes in a class of numerators.** Fix 0<ε<1/4 and consider only the pairs surviving the payment by integer difference. Put c=1/2−ε, ξ=p^(a/d) and U=exp(H/2+H/log H). By the previous criterion g<ℓ^(d−1)exp(−cH). The factorization of ℓ^d−ξ^d contains the factor ℓ−ξ and a positive cofactor at least ℓ^(d−1). Consequently |ℓ−ξ|<exp(−cH). ℓ is recovered as the integer nearest to ξ, with uniform absolute error even if d grows.

Fix p,d and a class a modulo d. For two candidates with a₂>a₁ write t=(a₂−a₁)/d≥1, so that ξ₂=p^t ξ₁. The integers ℓ₂ and p^t ℓ₁ are distinct because the second is composite and the first prime. Hence 1≤|ℓ₂−p^tℓ₁|<(1+p^t)exp(−cH), which forces p^t>exp(cH)−1. For large H there are at most TWO candidates in each class: three would give ξ₃>(exp(cH)−1)²ξ₁>U+1, since 2c>1/2 and ξ₁>1. Thus the remaining pairs number at most O(exp(H^ν) H^(8+2ν)) over the whole ceiling, summing d classes for d≪H^(4+ν) and primes p≤exp(H^ν). In particular the logarithm of their cardinality is ≤H^ν+O(log H), better than O(H/log H) for the broad list. This only counts candidates after the payment; a weighted bound of those surviving is still needed.

**Independent review of the separation and the count: PROVED.** The cofactor of ℓ−ξ is Σ_(j=0)^(d−1)ℓ^(d−1−j)ξ^j≥ℓ^(d−1), with no constant depending on d. This gives the indicated absolute error and a unique integer candidate for each ξ. In a class a modulo d, the ratio between two ξ is the integer power p^t; p^tℓ₁ is composite for t≥1, so its distance to the prime ℓ₂ is at least one. Two jumps would require ξ₃>(exp(cH)−1)², which exceeds U+1 for large H because 2c−1/2=1/2−2ε>0 and H/log H=o(H).

The reduced denominators satisfy d≤n≪H⁴log p≪H^(4+ν), uniformly in the list. There are d classes per denominator and at most two candidates per class. Summing Σ_(d≤CH^(4+ν))d=O(H^(8+2ν)) and the primes p≤exp(H^ν) verifies the global cardinality and its logarithm. The coprimality-with-six restrictions can only reduce it. This count does not estimate the multiplier or the weighted mass of the survivors and does not allow omitting their contribution.

**Asymptotic extinction for fixed p and d (application of Ridout).** Fix 0<ε<1/4, a witness prime p and a reduced denominator d≥5. For sufficiently large X no unpaid pairs with those parameters remain at the full ceiling. This holds simultaneously for any fixed finite catalogue of pairs (p,d).

Indeed, write a=td+r with 0<r<d. The number α=p^(r/d) is an irrational algebraic number: its p-valuation, if it were rational, would have to be an integer. Put q=p^t. Each survivor requires |ℓ−qα|<exp(−cH), c=1/2−ε, 0<ε<1/4. Since q≤U+1≤exp(H) eventually, it follows that |α−ℓ/q|<q^(−1−c) for q>1. The denominator is a power of p and the fraction is reduced. Ridout's theorem for denominators with prime factors in a finite set gives only finitely many such fractions for fixed α,p,c. There are only d−1 possible classes r. Since ℓ>exp(H^κ), no numerator of that finite set can keep appearing as X grows. The case q=1 is likewise excluded by ℓ→∞ and fixed α. Hence any sequence of survivors with X→∞ must leave every finite set of pairs (p,d). The argument gives no effective threshold and no uniformity for variable p or d. [D. Ridout, Mathematika 4 (1957), 125–131](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300001182).

**Independent review of the Ridout specialization: PROVED.** The reduction (a,d)=1 with d≥5 excludes r=0, so p^(r/d) is not rational. Since α>1 and |ℓ−qα|<1, one has q≤ℓ+1≤U+1≤exp H; hence exp(−cH)/q≤q^(−1−c). Moreover ℓ≠p, by the ranges of the list, and the fraction ℓ/p^t is reduced. In Ridout's p-adic formulation with S={p}, the factor |ℓq|_p is q^(−1); the resulting inequality has exponent 2+c and admits only finitely many fractions. The data of the [original publication](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300001182) and the specialized formulation in [Bugeaud (2011), p. 37, equation (2.2)](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.749.pdf) were verified.

For fixed ε,p,d there are finitely many classes r and, in each, finitely many allowed fractions. Their numerators are fixed, while ℓ>exp(H^κ) tends to infinity. This eliminates the whole catalogue and also covers q=1 directly. Taking the maximum of finitely many thresholds allows a fixed finite catalogue of pairs, but not a catalogue growing with X. The conclusion requires escaping every finite set of pairs (p,d), without claiming that p and d must both diverge separately. No effective threshold or uniform bound paying the remaining variable pairs is obtained.

**Separation exponent independent of X.** Fix 0<σ<1. Up to the full ceiling, all resonances satisfying g=|ℓ^d−p^a|≥ℓ^(d−2+σ) together cost o(√X), under the same budget on b. The arithmetic condition involves only the two powers. It is not presupposed that it always holds.

For each multiple of the reduced ratio combine the length ceiling N≪exp(H) and the phase bound ≪(log p)ℓ^d/g. After extracting the weight 1/ℓ, the cost is ≪(1+log p) min(exp(H)/ℓ,ℓ^(1−σ)). The maximum of this minimum for ℓ≥1 is attained at ℓ=exp(H/(2−σ)) and equals exp(H(1−σ)/(2−σ)). The marking contributes exp(−H/2+o(H)); the number of candidates, the residues and the sum of weights of b jointly cost exp(o(H)). There remains therefore a relative saving exp(−σ H/(2(2−σ))+o(H)). There is no independence assumption between p and ℓ. The same maximization for σ=0 gives only net exponent zero, a concrete limitation of these two majorants.

Thus, for any fixed σ in (0,1), the pending analysis can be restricted to g<ℓ^(d−2+σ), simultaneously with the earlier restrictions. This amounts to requiring a relative error smaller than ℓ^(−2+σ) when comparing p^a with ℓ^d; it is a necessary condition to escape this payment and does not prove the existence of persistent pairs.

**Independent review of the exponent and the minimum: PROVED.** The resonant character has simultaneously the ceilings CN and C(log p)ℓ^d/g, uniform over the multiples of the ratio. Multiplying by the weight 1/ℓ and using the hypothesis on g gives C(1+log p)min(exp H/ℓ,ℓ^(1−σ)). For ℓ≥1 the first branch is decreasing and the second increasing; they are equal exactly at exp(H/(2−σ)), with value exp(H(1−σ)/(2−σ)). Subtracting H/2 for the marking produces −σH/(2(2−σ)), with correct sign and constant.

The factor log p, the cardinality of the list, the absolute sum of the residues and the reciprocal weights of b are jointly exp(o(H)) under the earlier budget. Thus the improvement is strict for each fixed σ>0, without assuming uniformity as σ tends to zero. At σ=0 the maximum is exp(H/2) and these two majorants leave only exponent zero; this proves neither existence nor size of unpaid pairs. The final relative condition is exactly g/ℓ^d<ℓ^(−2+σ), and not a hypothesis on the independence of the two primes.

**Recovery of the exact threshold via the decay of the residues.** Fix max(1,4ν)<β<2. In the sector d/log p≥H^β the condition g≥ℓ^(d−2) does suffice for joint cost o(√X) at the full ceiling. More generally one can allow g≥ℓ^(d−2)exp(−c₁√(d/log p)), with a sufficiently small absolute constant c₁>0 for the fixed kernel.

First remove the pairs already paid by integer difference, with ε=1/8. For the rest, the per-class count gives exp(H^ν+O(log H)) candidates. For each multiple of the reduced ratio the modulus of the frequency is πhd/(3log p), h≥1. In the gamma computation of the residues reserve half the decay exp(−c√|τ|) before summing: one obtains Σ|r_τ| over these multiples ≤exp(O(H^(2ν)log(2H))−c₂√(d/log p)), uniformly in b,p,d, with fixed c₂>0. Indeed, the other half of the decay absorbs the same polynomial of degree O((2+log b)²) and changes only the constant in the gamma budget. The reservation is valid without cancellation between the multiples.

Put R=ℓ^(d−2)/g. The geometric mean of the two ceilings of the multiplier after the weight 1/ℓ gives min(N/ℓ,C(log p)ℓ R)≤C exp(H/2)√((log p)R). The marking exp(−H/2+O(√H log(2H))) cancels the factor exp(H/2). If log R≤c₁√(d/log p), choose c₁≤c₂. The sum over all candidates and weights 1/b then costs, relative to √X, at most exp(O((√H+H^(2ν))log(2H))−(c₂/2)H^(β/2)), which tends to zero. The terms already paid earlier only add o(1). The case of fixed d falls outside this frequency gain when p varies.

**Independent review of the exponential reservation and the threshold: PROVED.** Before the gamma sum, each residue has a majorant exp(−c√|τ|) times a polynomial of degree O((2+log b)²), with the height factors already included in the earlier budget. At the multiples of the ratio |τ|≥πd/(3log p) holds. Splitting the exponential into two halves allows extracting exp(−c₂√(d/log p)); summing the other half against the same polynomial changes only the constant of the majorant exp(O(H^(2ν)log(2H))). The pole count and the sum of residues remain uniform. No bound is extracted from a conditionally convergent series and no cancellation between multiples is assumed.

After the weight 1/ℓ, the product of the two ceilings is O(N(log p)R), so their geometric mean gives exactly exp(H/2)√((log p)R). The root of R consumes at most c₁/2 of the frequency saving; with c₁≤c₂ at least c₂/2 remains. The cardinality of candidates, the weights of b and √log p are absorbed in the written losses. Since β/2>1/2 and β/2>2ν, the negative term dominates their log H factors and proves o(√X). The result keeps the budget on b and the high-frequency condition; by this proof it does not extend to fixed d nor eliminate the pending low-frequency pairs.

**Obstruction to imposing critical separation without a frequency restriction.** For EACH fixed pair of primes p<ℓ there exist infinitely many positive reduced pairs a,d, both coprime to six, for which |ℓ^d−p^a|/ℓ^(d−2) tends to zero along the sequence. Exact example: p=2, ℓ=3, a=11 and d=7 give |2187−2048|=139<243=3^5.

Proof. The ratio x=log ℓ/log p is irrational by unique factorization. For consecutive convergents A_j/D_j, A_(j+1)/D_(j+1) of x, the matrix of their two columns (numerator, denominator) has determinant ±1. Hence there are coefficients u,v in {1,...,6} whose combination of columns is (1,1) modulo six. Let a',d' be its two coordinates. By the convergent errors |d'x−a'|≤6(|D_j x−A_j|+|D_(j+1)x−A_(j+1)|)→0. The common divisor of a',d' is that of u,v by unimodularity and hence ≤6. After reducing, a,d coprime to six remain, d→∞ and |dx−a|→0. Thus |ℓ^d−p^a|/ℓ^(d−2)=ℓ²|1−exp((a−dx)log p)|→0. This eliminates universal critical separation as a lemma for powers without a cut. It does not prove that those approximants survive the simultaneous conditions on X, the height of the poles and the residues. Precisely those controls are essential in any use of that separation.

**Independent review of the obstruction without cut: PROVED.** The determinant ±1 makes the matrix invertible also modulo six, so the two indicated positive bounded representatives u,v exist. The unimodular transformation exactly preserves the greatest common divisor: gcd(a',d')=gcd(u,v)≤6. Since a' and d' are both 1 modulo six, that divisor is coprime to six; after dividing by it, the two coordinates remain units modulo six. Moreover d'≥D_j+D_(j+1) tends to infinity and the divisor is bounded, so d→∞. The convergent error bound and the final exponential identity are uniform with respect to the bounded choices of u,v. The numerical example satisfies gcd(11,7)=1, both units modulo six, and 139<243.

The result refutes a universal critical gap at all exponents of a fixed prime pair. It does not fix d, does not make ℓ grow and does not guarantee simultaneously the height and scale restrictions of the exceptional list. Hence it does not contradict the Ridout extinction for fixed p,d nor constitute a counterexample to the pending arithmetic estimate.

**Uniqueness when combining all scales, and jump of denominators.** Fix the parameters ν<κ<1−ν of the list, ν>0. For every sufficiently large ℓ, and for each p, there is at most one reduced ratio a/d that can enter the list for SOME admissible scale X. If moreover κ>2ν, even the prime p is unique over the union of all those scales.

Indeed, A=log ℓ satisfies H^κ<A≤H/2+H/log H. Hence A≤H≤A^(1/κ) eventually. For any witness p one has P=log p≤A^(ν/κ), and its two original integers m,n and the reduced a,d are ≤A^C for fixed C depending on κ,ν, increasing C to absorb constants. Moreover, for x=A/P the phase condition gives |dx−a|≤exp(−H/2+H/log H)≤exp(−A/3) for large A. Two distinct fractions within this budget, even from different scales, would generate a nonzero integer determinant of modulus ≤2 A^C exp(−A/3)<1. By Legendre's criterion the only possibility is an ordinary convergent of x, since |x−a/d|<1/(2d²). The next denominator Q satisfies Q+d>1/|dx−a|≥exp(A/3), by the exact formula for the convergent error. In particular there can be no further reduced witness within the polynomial budget.

For two distinct primes, eliminate A between the two relations |dA−aP|≤P exp(−A/3). The nonzero form in the two logarithms has coefficients ≤A^(2C), modulus ≤exp(−A/3+O(log A)), and by Matveev modulus ≥exp(−O(A^(2ν/κ)log A)). They are incompatible if κ>2ν. Thus, for 0<ν<1/3 one can choose 2ν<κ<1−ν and assign to each ℓ a unique prime and a unique ratio for all scales of the selected intermediate interval. The jump of denominators gives a finite filter for each pair; by itself it does not bound the contribution of the convergent that passes it.

**Independent review of the union of scales: PROVED.** The membership condition implies A≤H≤A^(1/κ) for every admissible scale, and hence P≤A^(ν/κ). All original and reduced indices lie within a single budget A^C, with C depending only on the fixed parameters, and δ≤exp(−A/3). For fixed p, the determinant of two distinct ratios would have integer modulus between zero and 2A^Cexp(−A/3)<1, a contradiction. The exponential bound also satisfies Legendre's criterion for d≤A^C. The convergent error inequality 1/(Q+d)<|dx−a| implies Q+d>1/|dx−a|≥exp(A/3), with the written orientation.

For two distinct p, the form in log p and log q is nonzero and its coefficients are polynomial in A. Matveev's separation is exp(−O(A^(2ν/κ)log A)); if κ>2ν, its exponent is o(A), incompatible with the majorant exp(−A/3+O(log A)). Uniformity refers to all p and scales satisfying this budget; X is not fixed before comparing. This gives the unique second prime for ν<1/3 with the indicated choice, without paying the contribution of the single possible convergent.
