# A single sign criterion for a short Liouville average

For T≥2 set j=floor(log₂ T), V=j log(j+2) and φ_V the centered normal density of variance V. Write L(x)=Σ_{n≤x}λ(n) and U(u)=exp(−u/2)L(exp u), with U=0 for u<0. Define the finite arithmetic datum

    B(T)=∫₀^{T+3√(TV)} φ_V(u−T) U(u) du.

The global sign of P47 is equivalent to B(T)≤0 for all sufficiently large real T. Under RH one has B(T)→1/ζ(1/2)<0. If there is a zero off the critical line, B(T)>0 at some point of every sufficiently distant [2^j,2^(j+1)]. The integral is a finite sum of λ(n) with positive coefficients, integrating from log n to the upper limit. The typical deviation in log x is √V; for x=exp T it corresponds to a multiplicative factor exp(√V)=(log x)^o(1). Not all terms at more than one typical deviation are discarded.

**Bias under RH.** Let I_V(T) be the same average with infinite upper limit. The truncation error is o(1) uniformly: by |U(u)|≤exp(u/2) and the exponentially tilted normal tail it costs at most exp(T/2+V/8) Prob(N(0,1)>3√T−√V/2). By Gaussian inversion as in 142, I_V(T) is the vertical integral of R(s)exp(Ts+Vs²/2), divided by 2πi, with

    R(s)=ζ(1+2s)/[(s+1/2)ζ(1/2+s)].

Under RH one can start from σ=1/T, since L(x)≪ε x^(1/2+ε) for each ε>0 by 138. For |t|≥2 one has there |1/ζ(1/2+σ+it)|≤exp(C log(2+|t|)log T) for T large. Indeed integrate horizontally the classical local formula ζ'/ζ(w)=Σ_{|γ−t|≤1}(w−ρ)^(−1)+O(log(2+|t|)) from Re w=2: under RH each integrated term costs O(log T) in real part and there are O(log(2+|t|)) of them with multiplicity. At bounded heights the same local factorization is used. Moreover |ζ(1+2s)|≤ζ(1+2/T)=O(T). Since V/log T→∞, the Gaussian exp(−Vt²/2) makes the integral at those heights o(1). For |t|≤2 shift the segment to −1/4; there are no nontrivial zeros at those heights. The left side is O(exp(−T/4+V/32)); the horizontal ones give o(1) by the factor exp(−2V), using Ts≤1 in real part and V=o(T). Only the simple pole s=0 is picked up, with residue 1/ζ(1/2). This allows multiple zeros without using a series of 1/ζ'(ρ).

**Survival of a failure under the short average.** Set N=2^j and consider first an average of variance cN, c>0 fixed small, at centers vN in [1.4N,1.6N]. If some zero has b=Reρ−1/2>0, choose c so small that the pole z=ρ−1/2 has rate vb+c(b²−γ²)/2>0 on the whole interval. The numerator ζ(1+2z) does not vanish when b>0. The possible poles with b≤0 in a fixed contour do not dominate: for b in [−1/4,0] their rate is ≤0. Shift from 3/4 to −1/4 in a rectangle of large fixed height, adjusting the left edge slightly within (−1/3,−1/5) if it contains a pole. The bounds of 142 for the tails and horizontals and the negative rate of the left edge are uniform in v. The real pole s=0 contributes only a constant. As in 143, on some closed subinterval of positive length there is a single dominant conjugate pair, separated by a gap. The phase goes around ≍N times and the multiplicity factor has nonzero leading coefficient. Hence for each large j there is a center vN whose average of variance cN is positive and even ≥exp(dN) for some fixed d>0.

By exact composition of Gaussians, that wide average is the average of I_V(t) with a Gaussian centered at vN of variance cN−V, where V=j log(j+2) stays constant over the *whole* convolution. Within [N,2N] it is precisely the variance prescribed by the definition, except for the right endpoint of measure zero. Outside the band one uses |I_V(t)|≤exp(t/2+V/8). Its integral there costs O(exp(−N)) by reducing c at the start: the minimal distance of the center to the edge is 0.4N, and the tilt shifts the mean by at most cN/2. If all B(t) were ≤0 in the band, the I_V(t) there would be at most o(1) by the uniform truncation. This contradicts the bound for the wide average. The proof of the converse only needs V=o(N) and constancy on bands; the proof under RH additionally uses V/log T→∞.

**Scope.** 092 and 127 use the bias of squares for prime averages without tolerance. 141 computes the bias of a Möbius block and 142–143 locate poles via Gaussians of variance proportional to log x. Here the bias is kept in the full Liouville average under a single prescription of much smaller width, and detection is transferred through the Gaussian semigroup. Proving the eventual inequality arithmetically without RH remains to be done.
