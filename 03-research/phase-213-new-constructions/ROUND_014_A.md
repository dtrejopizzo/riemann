# Two translations with arbitrary coefficients and a radius common to all k

Antecedents 213-10 to 013. To overcome the relative loss when two atoms almost cancel, we count only the contacts with the head jumps while the two copies of the tail overlap. The independent selection enabled this case. The O symbols and the constants in the proof are uniform in large k and in the indicated separation.

**Statement.** There exists δ>0 such that for all k≥3 and 0<h≤δ the physical matrix of F_k,V_hF_k is positive definite, with real or complex coefficients. The claim also covers any two positions at separation h by common translation.

By translation invariance, reality and symmetry, the matrix has equal diagonals and real crossing. Its eigenvalues are the energies of F_k±V_hF_k divided by two. For the plus sign 213-13-A suffices. For the minus sign, if h≥w=log(1+1/k), the tails of both atoms are disjoint up to endpoints and the squared norm of their difference is 2d², d=log(4/3). The second inequality of 213-13-A proves positivity for all large k, uniformly in that range.

Now suppose 0<h<w. Split F_k−V_hF_k into head difference A and tail difference D, both in the logarithmic variable. The original tail is d exp(t/2) on [log k,log(k+1)). On its first strip of length h only the original copy contributes. On the two extreme strips its amplitude is O(√k) and on the overlap it is O(h√k). Hence

    d²kh ≤ ||D||² ≤ Ckh.

Its support has length ≤2w. After translating it to a fixed window, the floor of 213-10-A gives a(D)≥||D||² for all large k. The head rests on a fixed window and ||A||²≤Ch: the strips [log j,log j+h], j=1,2,3, have amplitude O(1), and outside them A has amplitude O(h). Thus a(A)≥−Ch by the lower bound of the symbol and the bounded comb on that window.

**Comb crossing.** On the three strips call A₁ the restriction of A and A₀ the rest. For A₁ and D, each active n satisfies that log n falls in one of three intervals of length ≤w+2h centred at bounded distance from log k. In the variable n each interval has length O(k(w+2h))=O(1). The O(1) integers it contains have weight O(log k/√k). Cauchy–Schwarz gives for the total crossing of A₁ with D a bound C(log k/√k)√h√(kh)=Ch log k. For A₀ one has ||A₀||≤Ch, and all contacts lie in a band of n comparable to k whose total mass is O(√k) by Chebyshev (012-A). Its crossing with D is O(kh√h). Both terms are o(kh), uniformly for h<w.

**Archimedean crossing.** In physical coordinates let A=Ua and D=Ub. For separated supports x<y the off-diagonal kernel is −(1/2)[1/(y−x)+1/(y+x)] (212-75, formula of the cells before integrating, obtained from the physical kernel). For rational endpoints this formula is also obtained by refinement to a common mesh: an integer dilation turns the intervals into unions of cells, both sides scale by the same factor and 212-75 is applied by bilinearity. For the translations used here exp(h) is approximated by positive rationals. The translations converge in the Fourier norm with weight 1+|m|, and the kernel integral converges by separation of supports and L¹ convergence of the physical functions. The supports here are in x∈[1,3 exp h] and y∈[k,(k+1)exp h]. The kernel is O(1/k). The total length of the physical head strips is O(h), with amplitude O(1), and on the rest the amplitude is O(h), so ||a||₁=O(h). The physical support of the tail has length O(1) and its amplitude is bounded, so ||b||₁=O(1). The crossing is O(h/k), also o(kh).

Consequently a(A+D)≥d²kh−o(kh)>0 uniformly for large k and 0<h<w. One threshold is chosen for both ranges of h. Reducing the radius by means of the finitely many positive radii of 213-10-A for k below that threshold yields the claim. For h=0 the span is a single positive line. Mixtures of three or more signed coefficients are not covered by this diagonalization. Status PROVED after independent review.
