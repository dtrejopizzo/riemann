# How many frequency-independent cutoffs allow the block to be balanced

Let S and the interval I be as in round 296, L=log x, N the number of primes in I. Among all lists of D cutoffs in I chosen before t is known, the smallest possible value of the integral over [1,2] of min_y |S_t(x,y)| is comparable to x/(D L²), for large x and 1≤D≤N. The constants are independent of D. This quantifies exactly the cost of discretizing the adapted cutoff. The one-sided criterion for the total balance of 292 remains open.

**Lower bound.** The certificate verify_297.py proves for 1.5≤t≤1.51 that K_t(13)>1/1000, K_t(15)<−1/1000 and that its derivative in t exceeds 1/20 for 13≤z≤15. The cells in z end at each integer and the new weight is zero on entry. Outside this rectangle, on the rest of I for those frequencies, the exact monotonicity of 296 and the expansion at the endpoints give |S|≥c x/L².

For each fixed z in [13,15], the t-derivative of the exact block has the uniform expansion 8x(∂_t K_t(z))/L²+O(x/L³). The finite identity of 295 is applied to the coefficients differentiated by the product rule. In detail, the derivative of the correcting factor of 294 is still absolutely summable with weight n^(−3/4), since the local linear term is still zero and the rest costs O((log p)p^(−3/2)). Differentiating a twisted L factor introduces log n and leaves partial sums O(log²(2x)) by parts. The hyperbola method of 294 therefore gives the same bound of every logarithmic order for the partial sums of ∂_t b. The moments of orders zero and one still vanish because of the factor ζ^(−2). The absolute harmonic norm up to x costs at most a logarithmic power by the product rule on the bounded local factors (a differentiated factor costs O(log p)). Thus the one-prime term with the derivative on b is O(x/L³) by the li expansion of 294. A derivative on u_p introduces the weight χ(p)log p sin(t log p), with sum O_R(v/log^R v) up to v by the PNT in classes modulo 3 and partial summation. In the one-prime term this suppresses the contribution to any logarithmic order, since p≥√(x/15). In the ordered pairs one first sums the differentiated prime over its vertical section, losing at most a logarithmic power on summing the other weights. The diagonal and the squared term cost O(√x log x). Only the derivative on the cofactor m≤15 of the two-prime term retains the main area of 295. These estimates justify the expansion without differentiating its remainder.

Consequently, for each fixed interior cutoff, the measure within that frequency interval where |S|≤h is ≤C h L²/x by the mean value theorem. For the exterior cutoffs the measure is zero if h<c x/L². Taking h=c' x/(D L²) with c' sufficiently small, the union of the D exceptions occupies less than half of the interval of length 1/100. Integrating over the remaining half proves the lower bound.

**Upper construction.** Group the N primes in order into D consecutive groups of sizes at most ceil(N/D) and choose the last of each group. At each frequency the first zero crossing of 296 lies in some group. From the prime preceding the crossing to the end of the group there are at most ceil(N/D) jumps, each O(√x/L); the value at the chosen endpoint lies between zero and that bound times the number of jumps. The uniform estimate implies the integrated one since N is comparable to √x/L by the PNT. In particular, to retain an average error O(√x/L) one needs, and it suffices to have, of the order of √x/L cutoffs. The conclusion refers to I and to the absolute value of the smooth block; it does not impose that cost on a direct estimate of the balance between blocks.
