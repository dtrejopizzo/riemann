# Uniform separation of head and tail for mixtures of bounded total variation

Antecedents 213-10-A, 213-11-A, 213-12-B and the quadrature 106_47 §2. The additional operation controls the variation of the **quadrature test** by moving the variation to the head and using only the mass of the tail. We keep the self-energy of the tail. This admits measures with atoms depending on k. The targeted cross-check with 106_118 distinguishes this use of the variation from an absolute bound on discrepancies of growing variation.

With the notation of 012-B, let μ be a complex measure on [0,δ] of TV norm ≤1. Finite combinations are included. We write Y_k=U(A_k+D_k), with A the mixture of the limiting head. For some fixed δ>0 and uniformly in μ,
    
    a(Y_k)=a(UA)+a(UD_k)+o(1),
    a(Y_k)≥c||UA||²+||UD_k||²−ε_k,    ε_k→0, c>0.

Here the limit is k→∞. For arbitrary TV norm the error is multiplied by ||μ||_TV².

**Quadrature.** A physical mixture is integrated with dμ(s)exp(−s/2) and argument exp(−s)x. The two head indicators therefore have, after mixing, sup norm and total variation in x bounded by a constant depending on δ, also for A_k. For G_k(y)=k D_k(ky), each dilated cell has length exp(s)/k in y. By integration against |μ| one obtains ||G_k||₁≤|d|exp(δ/2), with support in [1,2exp δ]. For large k the physical head and tail supports are disjoint and ordered. The mixed term of the comb is 2 Re k⁻¹ Σ Λ(n) Φ_k(n/k), with

    Φ_k(v)=v⁻¹ ∫G_k(y) conjugate(A_k(y/v))dy.

Its support lies in a common compact set [l,L]⊂(0,∞), chosen with room to spare. On that compact set v↦y/v is monotone. The variation of v↦A_k(y/v) is bounded by the variation of A_k, and multiplying by 1/v adds a uniform bound through its supremum. Integrating against |G_k| gives uniformly bounded variation for Φ_k. This function is continuous by continuity of dilations in L¹ of G_k and boundedness of A_k. Summation by parts bounds the difference between the normalized sum and ∫Φ_k by C sup_{l≤v≤L}|ψ(kv)/k−v|, which tends to zero by PNT. The continuous term is (∫conjugate(A_k(x))dx/x)(∫G_k(y)dy). The first factor is O(1/k): that of A vanishes exactly and the difference of coefficients is O(log(1+1/k)). Thus the full arithmetic crossing is o(1) uniformly.

**Archimedean part.** In logarithmic coordinates, UA_k is a combination of mixtures of two fixed compactly supported profiles of bounded variation, with uniformly bounded coefficients. The multiplier m(D) applied to each profile belongs to L² by 213-10-A. On [log k,∞), the L² norms of all its translations by s∈[0,δ] tend uniformly to zero by integrability of their squares. By Minkowski the same bound holds for m(D)UA_k. On the other hand ||UD_k||₂≤|d| by the norm of the original cell and the isometry of logarithmic translations. Its support starts at log k, so its archimedean crossing is o(1). Replacing a(UA_k) by a(UA) costs O(1/k) uniformly: the mixtures of the two profiles have bounded form norm on a fixed window and the coefficients differ by O(1/k).

**Floors and scope.** For the head use the radius and the coercive bound of 012-B. The bound extends from finite sums to measures by approximation of the vector integral of translations continuous in form norm. For the tail, fit its support by translation into a fixed window of length 2. Choose δ sufficiently small in the short-support floor of 010-A for that window. The length δ+log(1+1/k) then satisfies that floor for all large k. This gives a(UD_k)≥||UD_k||², with no moment condition. In this argument each piece lies in the domain by integration of translations of BV profiles on a finite window for each k.

Therefore, for any η>0, the mixtures with ||UA||≥η||μ||_TV have strictly positive energy for all k sufficiently large depending on η and δ, uniformly over those measures. A sequence of nonpositive energies with ||μ||_TV=1 and k→∞ would force ||UA|| and ||UD_k|| both to tend to zero. The inequality leaves the sign open precisely at that cancellation scale. Status PROVED after independent mathematical review, including the following corollary.

**Uniform radius on the cone of nonnegative coefficients.** For positive μ the ordinary physical moment of A is (b−a)∫exp(s/2)dμ(s). Its absolute value is at least d||μ||_TV. The support of A has length at most 3exp δ and ||UA||₂=||A||₂, so the previous criterion holds with η=d/(3exp δ)^(1/2). The threshold K is common to the cone. For the finitely many 3≤k<K take the minimum of their radii in 213-10-A and of the δ already obtained. Each coercive bound of those profiles passes to measures in the same way. A nonzero mixture of a positive measure and a nonzero profile does not vanish either: the product of their entire transforms is not identically zero. Hence there exists δ*>0 valid for all k≥3 and every nonzero positive measure on [0,δ*]. The full energy of each mixture is strictly positive.
