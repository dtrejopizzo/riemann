# Transfer at the critical scale of the inversion defect

Let f be real, |f(n)|≤1, g=f*1, and assume that g and its convolution inverse have absolutely convergent series with weight n^(−b) for all b>1/2. It suffices, in particular, that Σ |g(n)−δ₁(n)|/√n<1 by the Neumann series. For the anchored datum of **full floors**

    S_D(x)=Σ_{a,b≤D} f(a)f(b) floor(x/(ab))

the global sign is equivalent to the oscillations on [D,D²] being bounded by D^(1+o(1)) along some sequence D→∞. Under RH one even has sup_{x≤D²,x≥1}|S_D(x)|≤D^(1+o(1)). If Θ>1/2, the exponent of the oscillation with respect to D is 2Θ at every sufficiently large D, in the sense of a logarithmic limit. Here no crossings are chosen. At the crossings |Σ_{a≤D}f(a)/a|≤1/D of 134 the same equivalence holds for the off-diagonal fractional parts, provided an unbounded sequence of such crossings is available.

**Identity and cost of the truncation.** Write M_f(x)=Σ_{a≤x} f(a), A(x)=Σ_{a≤x}(μ*g*g)(a), f_D=f·1_{a≤D}. The identity f=μ*g and the fact that (f−f_D)*(f−f_D)*1 has no support below (D+1)² give

    S_D(x)=2Σ_{k≤x}g(k) M_f(min(D,x/k)) − A(x),     1≤x≤D².

If Θ>1/2 fix 1/2<b<Θ. The classical bound M₀(x)≪ε x^(Θ+ε) and absolute convolution bound M_f and A by the same order. The boundary sum in the identity is Oε(||g||_b x^b D^(Θ+ε−b)), using min(D,x/k)^(Θ+ε)≤D^(Θ+ε−b)(x/k)^b. On the whole band this costs Oε(D^(Θ+b+ε)), of exponent strictly smaller than 2Θ. Under RH take b>1/2 arbitrarily close to 1/2 and apply the same argument with ceiling x^b for M_f,A. This gives O_b(D^(2b)) for S_D. The relation with the total fractional part is S_D(x)=xH_f(D)²−Q_full(x). At the crossings the drift costs ≤1 per band and removing the diagonal of Q costs ≤D in increments.

**Transferring an excursion without knowing its position.** Let h=(g*g)^(−1); then M₀(x)=Σ h(k)A(x/k). By 133 the oscillation of M₀ on [D^(3/2),D²] has exponent 2Θ (the h of the notation of 133 is 1/3 here). Fix 0<κ<1/2. For any pair of points of that interval, the tail k>D^κ costs Oε(D^(2(Θ+ε)−κ(Θ+ε−b))||h||_b), using the bound for A. Choose ε small enough depending on κ and Θ−b: the tail has rate smaller than 2Θ. For k≤D^κ both arguments of A lie in [D,D²]. The cost of the short coefficients is ≤D^(κ b)||h||_b. Consequently the oscillation of A on [D,D²] has lower exponent at least 2Θ−κb. Letting κ→0 one obtains 2Θ. The boundary sum above does not alter this rate in S_D. This proves the converse even along an arbitrary sequence of D.

**Explicit example.** The Liouville function f(n)=(−1)^Ω(n) is completely multiplicative and satisfies g(n)=1 if n is a square and zero otherwise. The inverse of g equals μ(m) at n=m² and zero elsewhere. Both norms are finite for each b>1/2, although that of g diverges exactly at b=1/2. Thus the criterion applies to the signs λ(ab), with the integer squares as the only inversion defect. The classical equivalence between RH and the ceiling for Liouville sums is an antecedent; here the controlled datum would be the oscillation of the double floor sum along a sequence of anchored windows.

**Why the threshold is sharp as a uniform condition.** For each 1/2<σ≤1 and η>0 one can modify 137 so that Σ_{n>1}|(f*1)(n)|/n^σ<η and still obtain off-diagonal increments ≫D^(2σ)/log⁴D at infinitely many crossings. At each stage, the base with finitely many alterations has harmonic Mellin transform equal to 1/[sζ(1+s)] multiplied by a finite product (1+p^(−1−s))/(1−p^(−1−s)). This product is holomorphic and nonzero in Re s>−1. By Landau as in 134, the base admits arbitrarily large crossings with |H_base(D)|≤1/D. At those D select only floor(D^σ/log²D) high primes per group of 137; availability by PNT is sufficient. The low reserves suffice to compensate them and to adjust the base at the cost of one step as in 137. The mixed difference keeps ≫D^(2σ)/log⁴D jumps. The cost per stage in Σ_{p altered}p^(−σ) is O(1/log²D), since O(D^σ/log²D) low primes are needed; the D are chosen so large that their sum is arbitrarily small. The product for the norm of the defect is now ∏(1+2/(p^σ−1))−1. At the crossings these excesses also pass to the full floor, because D and the unit drift are smaller. The falsifier proves that a single norm to the right of 1/2 does not by itself give the root bound. It does not contradict the convergence hypothesis for *every* b>1/2.
