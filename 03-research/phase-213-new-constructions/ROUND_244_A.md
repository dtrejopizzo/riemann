# How many small nodes an extreme line with zeros would admit

Keep h=exp(−log²t), δ=h/t² and the floor(t²) nodes of 243. Write b=1/2+B. If B>0 and there is a zero with real part b, there is a fixed integer m such that, for each ε>0 and all sufficiently large t, at most m normalised differences V_k are ≤exp((B−ε)t). If only one conjugate pair lies on that line, m=1 suffices, even if other zeros approach the line arbitrarily closely. Thus, in that scenario the returns of 243 produce exactly one small node along a sequence and no distant block contains two. If there are L distinct zeros on the line, L finite, m=L−1 suffices.

**Local derivatives.** Set g_t(v)=[S(t+v+δ)−exp(δ)S(t+v)]/[δ exp(bt)], keeping δ fixed in the differentiation. For each fixed order j, on 0≤v≤h,
    
    g_t^(j)(v)=−Σ_{Re ρ=b} H(ρ)(ρ−1)ρ^j exp(i Imρ t) exp(ρv)+o(1).

The main mean disappears. The spectral identity of 240 also holds for the differentiated weights by differentiation of the smoothed explicit formula; the prime powers cost O_j(exp(t/2)poly(t)) even with any fixed number of derivatives of the weight. The divided difference is bounded by applying the fundamental theorem to the increment of the rescaled count as in 243 (for the error even the cost 1/δ=exp(o(t)) would suffice). At the zeros the multiplier after division by δ has modulus ≤C|ρ−1| and converges to ρ−1. The absolute moments of H are finite at every order. Dominated convergence eliminates all zeros with Re ρ<b and gives the uniform o(1), without rate or spectral gap. In particular the derivatives of each fixed order are uniformly bounded.

**A finite combination of derivatives has modulus bounded away from zero.** Fix ρ₀ on the extreme line. For G>|ρ₀| take the polynomial P_G(z)=∏(z−ρ)/(ρ₀−ρ) over the other distinct zeros of that line with |Im ρ|≤G. In the case of a finite line the complete product is used directly. In the infinite case its degree M is O(G^q log⁵G), q<1, by Ingham at any σ between 1/2 and b. Each denominator is bounded away from zero by a constant depending on ρ₀ and for |Im z|>G, Re z=b, one has |P_G(z)|≤(C|z|)^M. On that tail, M log(C|z|)=o(|z|/log²|z|) uniformly as G grows. The near-exponential decay of H from 240 and the unit count imply that Σ_tail |H(ρ)(ρ−1)P_G(ρ)|→0. Choose G once so that this sum costs less than half the modulus of the coefficient of ρ₀, counting multiplicity. By the derivative formula at v=0, |P_G(d/dv)g_t(0)| stays bounded away from zero for large t. Therefore some derivative between 0 and m=deg P_G has modulus ≥c>0, uniformly in the starting point.

**Why m+1 small nodes are impossible.** At such nodes |g_t|≤exp(−εt)/δ. For each j≤m, use any j+1 of them. The divided difference of order j costs at most (j+1)exp(−εt)δ^(−j−1)=o(1). Since g_t is real, at some point of the interval that difference is g_t^(j)/j!. The next derivative is uniformly bounded and h→0, hence g_t^(j)(0)=o(1) simultaneously for all j≤m along any violating sequence. This contradicts the bound of the differential polynomial.

**Consequence and limit.** If along a subsequence there is a number tending to infinity of nodes with V_k≤exp(o(t)), every failure of RH with attained supremum is excluded. For a single extreme pair two nodes suffice. It is not presumed that such a pair exists, that the supremum is attained, nor that the primes supply those nodes. The case of a supremum without maximum does not allow all modes to be discarded after normalising by exp(bt) and keeping one with fixed coefficient.
