# Up to 72 000 poles and sufficient height quadratic in the order

For every causal rational block of n≤72000 real poles −v_j, v_j>1/2 with arbitrary multiplicities, one has W(f)≥exp(−5000000)||f||₂² for all complex numerators of degree <n. More generally the high bands of the true sum over zeros have positive contribution from height max(3·10¹²,150(2n+8)²) for every order n and nonzero datum. In 202 the height grew cubically. The verified height Z2 is kept in the unconditional result for 72000.

**Relative approximation of the denominator on a proportional band.** Split each half-axis of ordinates of modulus >G=3·10¹² into successive bands I=(A,4A/3], H=A/3. Treat each sign separately. With c=±7iA/6 and D(s)=∏(s+v_j), expand 1/D at c on the disc of radius 7A/12. On that disc |1/D|≤2^n/|D(c)| because |c+v_j|≥7A/6. All points s=±it'+δ with t'∈I and |δ|≤1/2 have |s−c|≤A/6+1/2; the ratio to the radius is ≤0.287 and |D(s)|≤1.144^n |D(c)|. If Q is the Taylor polynomial up to degree n+8 inclusive, Cauchy with geometric remainder gives

    |D(s)Q(s)−1| ≤ 2.288^n (0.287)^(n+9)/(1−0.287) < 0.0001.

Therefore U=P Q is a polynomial of degree <2n+8 that relatively approximates F=P/D both on the band and at all points where the zeros are evaluated. This construction is repeated per band only to estimate the exact sum of the same F; no analytic test is glued together from different polynomials.

**Sampling and shift.** The proof of 202 via Legendre and integration by parts remains valid with t=(2n+8)²/H, l=0.14−6t, h=0.17+6t. Indeed for A≤x≤4A/3 the remainder of Z3 is still ≤log A and the main derivative lies between 0.14 log A and 0.17 log A for A≥G, directly from their formulas in P46. The sampling includes multiplicities and the disjoint half-band convention of 201. If X is the vector U(±iγ) on this band of absolute heights and X_± its two versions shifted by the abscissae of the zeros, one has

    ||X_±−X||₂ ≤ √(h/l)(exp(t)−1)||X||₂.

This time the estimate is applied to the complete polynomial U. The real denominator may vary by a large factor between the endpoints of I. That factor does not enter the relative error. For n≤72000 and A≥G one has t≤0.020739, l>0.0155, h<0.295 and √(h/l)(exp(t)−1)<0.092. Since |U−F|≤10⁻⁴|F| at each point, the two vectors of true values of F at the shifted ordinates are at norm distance from X at most [0.092+(1.092)10⁻⁴/(1−10⁻⁴)]||X||<0.1||X||. The sum of the real parts of their conjugate products dominates 0.79||X||². By the lower sampling inequality and the approximation on the real ordinate axis, this gives more than 0.3∫_I |F(±ix)|²dx (0.79·l·log G·(1−10⁻⁴)²>0.3). For every n the condition A≥150(2n+8)² implies t≤0.02, so the announced height suffices to control the upper contribution, with the same constants.

**Floor in norm.** Ordinates up to G contribute squares. To compare the low energy of F with the first band use the final argument of 202, now on I=[G,4G/3] applied to the original numerator P. The Legendre coordinate of [−G,G] has modulus ≤13 (1+4G/H); by recurrence each Legendre polynomial normalized at 1 has modulus ≤27^k there. The maximal denominator on I divided by its minimum on [−G,G] is ≤(9·10¹²)^n since v_j>1/2. Therefore the ratio low energy/high energy is ≤6n²(27·9·10¹²)^(2n), with logarithm <4800000 for n≤72000. By Plancherel, the floor 0.3 on the high energy implies the announced floor. Polynomial approximation is a classical instrument; here it allows bands whose length is a fixed fraction of the height and keeps the evaluations off the line.

**Limit.** For unbounded dimension, Z2 does not automatically cover the whole region below the quadratic height. The interpolation obstruction on a fixed band of 201 remains in force. The radius of the global window in the compact domain does not change.
