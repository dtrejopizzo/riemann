# Almost total coverage at all centers using two moments

In the window of 337 put h=X^(−δ), H=Xh, fixed 0<δ≤1/16. There exists a selection of disjoint pairs of opposite signs of μ, independent of the center, that leaves uncovered absolute mass O_A(H/(log X)^A) for every fixed A **at each center** X≤x≤2X. Its total signed balance is O_η(1+X^(4/3)/H)=o(√H) also at each center. The selection only requires values of μ in each cell separately. Hence in the equivalence of 337 one can keep only the unselected set without loss in the budget.

**Distribution in longer cells.** Use consecutive integer cells of length J=⌊X^(2/3)⌋ without truncating them at the edge of the window. Only cells in [X/2,3X] matter for X large enough. The estimate for μ in *all* intervals with exponent 5/8 and fixed margin gives, for each prefix of a cell, an imbalance O_A(J/(log X)^A). Indeed up to length X^(13/20) the trivial ceiling is used and after that the theorem with fixed margin less than 13/20−5/8. The same estimate and the count of squarefree integers with error O(√X) imply that in each outer quarter of the cell there are ≫J instances of each sign. [Matomäki, Shao, Tao and Teräväinen, Higher uniformity I, constant case](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03754)

**Balancing position as well as quantity.** In each cell first erase the excess of the majority sign, taking as many samples as the modulus of the sum of μ. As many positive as negative signs remain. The signed first moment, with positions measured from the start, costs O_A(J²/(log X)^A) before erasing, by partial summation over the prefixes. After the erasure that bound still holds. If the moment exceeds J in modulus, remove two samples of opposite signs: a positive one from the upper quarter and a negative one from the lower when the moment is positive, and swap the roles when it is negative. Each removal preserves the zero sum and corrects the moment by between J/2−O(1) and J in the needed direction. Stop upon reaching modulus ≤J. O_A(1+J/(log X)^A) removals are used, fewer than the reserves ≫J of the quarters. The decisions can be fixed, for example, by choosing the smallest available indices in each admissible set. Now pair arbitrarily the two retained signs in each cell. The erased terms constitute the residue. This same selection satisfies simultaneously the bounds for all fixed A.

**Balance for the pulse.** In each cell touching the support one has |W'_x|≪_η H^(−1) and |W''_x|≪_η H^(−2), by the chain rule at n comparable to X. Taylor at the start of the cell: the constant term vanishes; the linear one costs O(J/H) by the correction of the first moment; the aggregate remainder in that cell costs O(J³/H²). This controls the joint balance and admits cancellation between pairs of the same cell. There are O(1+H/J) active cells; hence the cost O(1+J²/H). The exponent 1/3+δ is below (1−δ)/2 for δ≤1/16. In the same cells only O_A(H/(log X)^A) terms were erased and the weights are bounded. The total squarefree mass in the pulse is ≍H by the elementary count. The triangle inequality in the norm of 337 justifies the equivalence for the residue.

**What changes relative to 349.** There are no exceptional centers in the mass bound nor is a reserved prime required. The gain comes from choosing real samples from both ends to correct the linear moment, which allows cells long enough for a uniform arithmetic estimate. The use of two moments in quadrature already appears in 123; here disjoint integers with the exact signs of μ are selected. The absolute ceiling of the residue, although uniform, still exceeds the required root precision. No power saving for its balance has been proved.
