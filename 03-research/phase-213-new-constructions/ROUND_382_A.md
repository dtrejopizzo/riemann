# Uniform comparison of sectors on both sides of the maximum of the main profile

**Status: PROVED, with independent review of the uniform step, the limit law and the holomorphic continuation.**

Fix 0<a<1 and A>0, L=log x, b=L^a, B=log(L/b), h=log L. For 1≤d≤AB, with the definitions of 381,

    S_d=(−1)^d (1+o(1)) J x P(0) B^(d−1)/[(d−1)! L] · F((d−1)/B).

The error is uniform. F(λ) is the probability of [0,1] for the law on [0,∞) with Laplace transform exp(λ∫₀¹(exp(−st)−1)dt/t). It is positive and continuous on each compact set of λ≥0. In particular |S_(d+1)/S_d|=(1+o(1))B/d uniformly up to AB (enlarge A to include d+1). The ratio crosses from greater than one to less than one outside any fixed relative band around B. No monotonicity inside that band is deduced.

**The probabilistic factor.** In the simplex of 381 set n=d−1. Exactly, L H_d(L) n!/B^n is the probability of ΣX_i≤1−b/L, where the independent X_i have density 1/(Bt) on [b/L,1]. Along any sequence n/B→λ, the transform of the sum is (1+B^(−1)∫_{b/L}¹(exp(−st)−1)dt/t)^n, which converges to the stated one. The laws are tight by the bounded first moment; uniqueness of the transform characterizes the limit ν. For λ>0 differentiating the transform gives the identity of measures tν(dt)=λ(ν*1_[0,1]dt)(dt), with absolutely continuous right-hand side, so 1 is not an atom. For λ=0 the limit is mass at zero. The same convergence proof shows continuity of F. For a uniform floor require all X_i≤r=1/(8(A+1)): this condition has probability (1+log r/B)^n bounded below and, conditioned on it, the sum has expectation ≤nr/(B+log r)<1/3 for L large. Markov keeps positive mass below 1−b/L. By compactness this gives the formula for H_d(L) with uniform relative error o(1).

**Signed sum with growing dimension.** In the fixed-simplex continuation of 381 now take |D−L|≤L/(10h). There each factor (D−db)/(b+(D−db)u_i) has modulus at most (1+O(1/h)) times the real factor at L, uniformly in u_i∈[0,1], since db=o(L). Multiplying O_A(h) factors and dividing by D, one obtains |H_d(D)|≪_A H_d(L). This avoids an exponential constant in d in the control of derivatives. Truncating log m≤L/(40h) by Rankin costs exp(−L/(40hb)+O(h)) relative to x in absolute value for the arithmetic mass, by uniqueness of the unordered representation. For the truncated sum the errors of the d PNT substitutions and the diagonals of 381 cost at most x exp(O_A(h²)−c√b): there are d substitutions and ≤d² pairs of positions, and each remaining factor costs O(L) in reciprocal mass. The Gaussian truncations cost x exp(−cL+O_A(h²)); for the integrals truncated in primes the support has D≤2Ld and the untruncated density is ≤(1+log(2Ld/b))^(d−1)/D for D≥db. All those errors are negligible, since H_d(L)≥exp(−O_A(h)) by the probabilistic floor and the factorial bound.

Apply the Taylor expansion of 380 with radius L/(10h). On log m≤L/(40h), |u|≤√L, the argument falls in the half disc. The cost of the absolute remainder relative to H_d(L) is O_{A,N}(L^C(10hb/L)^N); choose N fixed and large according to a. To complete the polynomial terms, Rankin with margin 1/(2b) dominates any power of L. The non-constant signed terms are, relative to H_d(L)P(0), of order O_{A,i}((10hb/L)^i), by the same moments of P. The main term J is preserved. This justifies the joint limit, without interchanging an infinite series with pointwise equivalents.

**Review record.** For d=1 the empty sum is0 and its probability is exactly1, compatible with F(0)=1. For λ>0 the identity of measures implies absence of atoms at all t>0; for λ=0 the atom is at0 and not at the threshold1. Thus weak convergence also controls the moving threshold 1−b/L. By extracting subsequences with n/B in the compact set[0,A], this convergence and the probabilistic floor make the relative error uniform. The conditioning X_i≤r has probability (1+log r/B)^n≥c_A>0 and total mean<1/3, including small n; no constant is lost as d increases.

In the complex continuation each factor is (D−db)/(b+(D−db)u_i). Its quotient relative to the real factor at L differs from1 by O(1/h) uniformly even at u_i=0; multiplying n=O_A(h) factors costs only exp(O_A(1)). Division by D does not alter that uniformity. The floor H_d(L)≥exp(−O_A(h)) incorporates both the1/L and B^n/n!, and P(0)≥L^(−1) eventually. Hence the errors x exp(O_A(h²)−c sqrt b), x exp(−cL/(hb)+O_A(h²)) and the Gaussian truncations are all negligible relative to xH_d(L)P(0), for each fixed a<1. The fixed choice of N can absorb the factor L^C and P(0)^(−1), since N(1−a)>C+1 suffices with fixed margin. Finally F is uniformly continuous and bounded away from0 on[0,A+1]; hence F(d/B)/F((d−1)/B)=1+o(1) uniformly, justifying the ratio of sectors and the crossing outside a fixed relative band.

The estimate decides the sign of each sector at every fixed multiple of B, even beyond the zone of largest mass. The sectoral relative error o(1) is still not enough to decide the alternating balance to square-root precision. No uniformity in a→1 nor in growing A is claimed.
