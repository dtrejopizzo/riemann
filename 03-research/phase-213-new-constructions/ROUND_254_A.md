# Exact frontier of all orders and size of the first negative order

For F=KP from 253 define

    S_* = 1/2 + inf_{ρ=1/2+b+iγ, b>0} (b²+γ²)/(2b),

with infinity if there are no such zeros. If it is finite the infimum is attained, since b≤1/2 and |γ|→∞ outside finite sets. For 1/2<s≤S_* ALL alternating derivatives of F are strictly positive, including the finite endpoint. For s>S_* there are infinitely many orders of each sign. In particular there is no indeterminate interval between positivity at all orders and the appearance of negatives. If S_* is finite and ν(s) is the first strictly negative order, there are positive constants c,C and δ₀ (depending on the spectrum) with

    c/δ ≤ ν(S_*+δ) ≤ C/δ,       0<δ<δ₀.

**Endpoint included.** For s≥G², the condition s≤S_* is equivalent to |s−ρ|≥s−1/2 for all zeros, by the identity of squares |s−ρ|²−(s−1/2)²=b²+γ²−2b(s−1/2). In the case b≤0 it always holds. Z2 gives S_*≥G² according to 253. Repeat the proof of 253 with the anchor s instead of G²: for n≤30s the argument of the two integrals is identical and for n≥30s the zero budget only uses the distance inequality. For values below G², 253 suffices.

**Beyond.** In the disc centred at s of radius s−1/2 the only type of singularity of F is simple poles at zeros of ζ, since P is −ζ'/ζ plus a holomorphic series there, and K(1)=0. Each residue is −mult(ρ)K(ρ)≠0. If the disc contains any, those at minimal distance form a finite set of conjugate pairs with distinct nonreal angles. Subtract those poles and apply Cauchy on a circle slightly larger than the minimal distance, still inside the disc and without other poles. For the alternating derivatives divided by n!, after scaling by the minimal distance to the power n+1, there remains a real trigonometric polynomial in n with mean zero plus an o(1). The coefficients are the residues and the frequencies the signed arguments of s−ρ. The positive mean square and recurrence give both signs with margin at arbitrarily large orders. This does not presuppose a unique winning pair.

**Why the first negative is delayed.** Set s₀=S_* finite, R=s−1/2 and r_ρ(s)=R/|s−ρ|. For the zeros touching the boundary at s₀, a finite nonempty set, log r_ρ(s₀+δ)=κ_ρ δ+O(δ²), κ_ρ=b/(s₀−1/2)²>0. The others have r_ρ≤r₀<1 uniformly near s₀: the tail tends uniformly to zero and the remaining set is discrete. In particular all r_ρ≤exp(Kδ) with some fixed K.

In the budgets of 253 for n≥30s, the only change is that the cost of the zeros of J_1 becomes ≤0.095 exp(Kδ(n+1)). If δ(n+1)≤c₁ and c₁ is sufficiently small a positive margin still remains. For n≤30s the low-order proof holds for all s≥G². This gives the lower bound for ν.

For the upper bound use the normalised moments R^(n+1)(−1)^nF^(n)(s)/n!. Uniformly near s₀, as n→∞ there remain the term 4/15 and the contributions of the pairs that touched the boundary, with error o(1). This is obtained from 248: the other zeros cost O(r₀^n), the terms of J_2 have ratio strictly less than one (uniformly here by |γ|>14, compactness and decay in the tail). The bounded terms and the odd powers are now estimated in absolute value: replace the ceiling of 253 for odd R by three times it, using |k|≤1 and θ≤2x. All decay exponentially in n, up to polynomial factors.

Take n=floor(A/δ) with fixed A. The maximal magnitude M of the coefficients of the pairs at that order tends to max |mult(ρ)K(ρ)|exp(κ_ρ A). It can be made arbitrarily large by choosing A. After normalising by M, over any fixed number of steps j≥0 the moduli vary by o(1), and the frequencies in j tend to the distinct angles of the boundary at s₀. There are finite L and η>0 such that every real polynomial of these pairs with maximal coefficient modulus one takes some value ≤−η in 0≤j≤L, even absorbing initial phases into the coefficients. Indeed the Cesàro mean is zero and the mean square is the sum of squares of the coefficients (distinct frequencies modulo 2π), so a sequence of that type cannot be always nonnegative. Compactness of the set of coefficients and continuity give uniform L and η. Choose A with ηM>2 for small δ. One of the orders n,...,n+L is negative despite the main term 4/15. The announced upper bound follows.

**Distinction from 145.** The Gaussian variance frontier of 145 already used spectral competition with a height penalty. Here the included endpoint is decided for ALL orders and it is computed how the first order that would detect a violation diverges. The result explains how a check of finite orders can lose the sign on crossing the frontier. It does not exclude that S_* is finite nor does it provide numerical detection constants without knowing the zeros that would touch the frontier.
