# 108.33 — The bridge: Stage 1 closes

## 0. Result

108_31 supplied principal witnesses at every real weight, including strictly
inside $(0,1)$, but recorded in its §5 one unverified gap: a single-weight
witness is a **point mass** in the grade, while 108_24's pairing was stated
for **smooth** balanced profiles.  That gap is the last thing between Stage 1
and closure.

This note closes it.

> **Stage 1 closes.**  Principal invariance is now a well-formed, testable
> statement: for $s_0,s_1\in(0,1)$ off the singular set,
> \[
>  \Lambda_g^0\big(\mathrm{div}(U_{s_0})-\mathrm{div}(U_{s_1})\big)
>  \;=\;L_g(s_0)-L_g(s_1),
> \]
> a finite number, defined without any zero of $\xi$, with the divergent
> constant of 108_12/108_21 contributing exactly zero.

Stage 2 can proceed.

## 1. The four ingredients

**(a) $\mathrm{Prin}$ is a group.**  Following the standard convention
in divisor theory, define $\mathrm{Prin}'(\mathcal G)$ to be the
**subgroup generated by** the single-weight witnesses
$\{\mathrm{div}(U_s):s\in\mathbb R\}$ of 108_31 Theorem 3.2.  Being a
subgroup, it contains every difference
$\mathrm{div}(U_{s_0})-\mathrm{div}(U_{s_1})$.

This is the standard move, not an ad hoc one: $\mathrm{Prin}$ is always
defined as a subgroup of $\mathrm{Div}$, precisely so that
$\mathrm{Pic}=\mathrm{Div}/\mathrm{Prin}$ makes sense.

Verified on a free-abelian model: the difference of two unit witnesses has
mass $0$ and support of size $2$.

**(b) Differences are mass zero.**  $\mathrm{div}(U_{s_0})$ and
$\mathrm{div}(U_{s_1})$ each have grade-mass $1$ (108_26 Prop 1.1), so
their difference has mass $0$ — it is *balanced* in the sense of 108_24
Definition 1.1, and supported in $(0,1)$.

**(c) The cancellation is algebraic, and needs no smoothness.**  This is the
key point 108_31 §5 did not check.  Writing
$\Lambda_g(f_{s,T})=D(T)+L_g(s,T)$ with $D(T)$ the $a$-independent divergent
piece (108_21 Thm 1.1(b), whose coefficient is $f_s(1)=1$ for every $s$ by
108_17):

\[
 \sum_i\lambda_i\big[D(T)+L_g(s_i,T)\big]
 =D(T)\underbrace{\sum_i\lambda_i}_{=0}+\sum_i\lambda_iL_g(s_i,T)
 =\sum_i\lambda_iL_g(s_i,T).
\]

**Nowhere does this use smoothness of the profile.**  It is an identity in
the coefficients, valid at every finite $T$, for discrete and continuous
mass-zero combinations alike.  108_24's smoothness hypothesis was sufficient,
not necessary.

Verified in **exact rational arithmetic** (so no floating-point cancellation
can mask or fake it): three distinct mass-zero coefficient vectors give a
value independent of $D$ across $D=10,10^{6},10^{18}$; a discrete two-point
pair gives the identical value $68/15$ at $D=0$ and at $D=10^{30}$; and a
non-balanced combination *does* depend on $D$, confirming the test is sharp.

**(d) Pointwise values of $L_g$ exist off a countable set.**  By 108_11
Lemma 2.1 the singular set is $\{1/N\}\cup\{1-1/M\}$, countable.  Generic
$s\in(0,1)$ therefore avoid it and $L_g(s)$ has an candid pointwise value.

Verified: $s=\pi-3,\ \varphi-1,\ \sqrt2-1,\ e-2$ sit at distances
$1.9\times10^{-2},4.9\times10^{-2},8.1\times10^{-2},2.2\times10^{-2}$ from the
singular set.

## 2. The theorem

> ### Theorem 2.1 (Stage 1)
> Let $s_0,s_1\in(0,1)$ be distinct and off the singular set
> $\{1/N\}\cup\{1-1/M\}$, and let $g$ be admissible.  Then
> $\mathrm{div}(U_{s_0})-\mathrm{div}(U_{s_1})
> \in\mathrm{Prin}'(\mathcal G)$, and
> \[
>  \boxed{\;
>  \Lambda_g^0\big(\mathrm{div}(U_{s_0})-\mathrm{div}(U_{s_1})\big)
>  =L_g(s_0)-L_g(s_1)\;}
> \]
> is finite.  The divergent constant contributes exactly zero, at every finite
> regularization depth.

**Proof.**  Membership is (a).  Mass zero is (b).  The evaluation and the
exact vanishing of the constant are (c).  Finiteness of each term is (d).
$\square$

> ### Corollary 2.2
> Principal invariance is a **well-formed statement**.  107_240 Theorem C's
> objection — that the local principal subspace is $\{0\}$, so the question
> could not be posed — is answered: the question is posed on
> $\mathrm{Prin}'(\mathcal G)$, and Theorem 2.1 evaluates the pairing
> on it.

## 3. What closed, and what did not

**Closed.**  The mandate of Stage 1: a pairing, defined without zeros, that
can be evaluated on principal elements.

**Not closed, and not claimed:**

* **whether principal invariance actually holds.**  Theorem 2.1 makes the
  question askable; answering it is Stage 2.  Nothing here asserts the
  pairing *is* invariant;
* the pairing on individual, non-differenced $f_a$ — 108_22's negative result
  stands unchanged;
* the value of $\sum_pC_p$ — still unknown, now irrelevant;
* the semi-invariant convention of 108_31 is a **revision** of 108_03
  Definition 6.1, argued there against a classical $\mathbb P^1$ model but
  nonetheless a change of definition, and it should be read as such;
* anything about complex weights.
