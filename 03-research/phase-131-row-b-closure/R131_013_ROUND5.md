## 2. M1/LF3: the non-sequitur, withdrawn; the stronger statement, proved

**Accepted without reservation.** "`Q_fin ≤ 0` ⟺ RH" is a biconditional between
two propositions of fixed truth value; comparing two fixed quadratic forms
cannot refute it. Round 4's proof showed only that the forms are *distinct*.
Withdrawn as **§9bis W19**.

Replaced by two statements:

**(a) `PROVED`** — the two forms differ by `∫|ĥ|²ω̂_∞ dτ/2π` with `ω̂_∞`
indefinite (`≈5.372>0` at `τ=0` by Paper 43 (14.2); `→−∞` by (8bis.8)), hence
they are **distinct**. This is exactly what the round-4 argument gives, and it
is all (iv)(a) now claims.

**(b) `PROVED`, Theorem 8bis.4a — the audit's stronger statement.** I checked
each step the the round record flagged before writing it.

* *The symbol at `τ=0`.* On the principal component `θ_p = 0`, so
  `ζ_p = p^{−1/2} ∈ (0,1)` is real and
  `ω̂_fin(0) = Σ_{p∈S_f}(log p)·2p^{−1/2}/(1−p^{−1/2}) > 0` strictly, with
  `|ω̂_fin| ≤ A_S` (8bis.8b). Numerics block (E) tabulates it.
* *A primitive test exists, and it is explicit.* `Π = −∂_t²+¼` **automatically**
  primitivizes: `λ_∓(Γ_{Πg}) = ∫(−g''+g/4)e^{±s/2}ds = 0` by two integrations
  by parts, since `∂_s²e^{±s/2} = ¼e^{±s/2}` and `g` has compact support. So
  `g_T := Π(φ(·/T))` is primitive for **every** real even `φ ∈ C_c^∞`. (This is
  why Paper 45 Thm 10.4's operator is *the* primitivizer, and it makes the
  the round's worry about "two linear conditions" vacuous.)
* *Concentration, with `ĥ = |ĝ|²` not compactly supported — the the round's
  explicit caution.* Handled by **decay**, not by support:
  `ĝ_T(τ) = (τ²+¼)Tφ̂(Tτ)`, so after `σ = Tτ` the tail is
  `T∫_{|σ|>Tδ}(σ²/T²+¼)²|φ̂(σ)|²dσ/2π`, and for `T ≥ 1` the integrand is
  `≤ (σ⁴+1)|φ̂(σ)|²`, which is Schwartz; hence `M_tail(T)/M_bulk(T) → 0` with
  `M_bulk ≍ T`. Then `Q_fin(g_T) ≥ c·M_bulk − A_S·M_tail > 0` for large `T`.
* *Is the colimit limit legitimate for that test?* **No — and I say so.**
  Rem 8bis.4b(1): the proof uses Plancherel against the **bounded** chart
  symbol; in the colimit `ω̂_fin^∞` is unbounded (`ω̂_fin^∞(0) = +∞`, numerics
  block (E)) and the two scales — the concentration width `≍1/T` and the
  symbol's variation scale `≍1/log p_max` — become comparable. **The colimit
  version is `OPEN` and is not asserted.** Per the the round's instruction I
  would have stated (a) only had this failed; it does not fail at a chart, so
  (b) is stated at a chart with its scope in the statement.

**Rewording, as instructed.** §0, the banner, §10's round-5 block and the
README now say "…**not** the Frobenius Gram form" citing **(iii)** — Weil's
criterion on the radial family, in the colimit — and the corrected **(iv)**;
**never** a proof by symbol comparison. Rem 8bis.4b(3) states the residual
logic explicitly: what is proved is that the left-hand side of the
biconditional is **false at every finite chart**, while (iii) is a **colimit**
statement, so the two quantifiers differ and no claim about RH follows.

## 3. M2/LF2 and M3/LF1: the quarantined converse

**M2, error 1 (primitivization).** Accepted. A single mesh bump near
`log n ≈ L/2` has `λ_−`-weight `≍ gap_n·n^{1/2} ≍ e^{−3L/4}` against
`λ_−(e) = O(1)`, so the coefficient would be `≍ e^{3L/4}` — round 4's
`O(e^{−L})` is wrong by `≍ e^{5L/4}`. **W21.** *Repair, written out with every
estimate:* tests are primitive **by construction** (`Π`), and the Riemann-sum
approximant is corrected along `w_1, w_2`, the Riemann sums of `e^{±s/2}η`,
whose `λ`-weights are exponentially large — (8bis.13d): `λ_−(w_1) ≍ e^L`,
`λ_+(w_1) ≍ L`, `λ_−(w_2) ≍ L`, `λ_+(w_2) ≍ e^{−L/2}`, determinant `≍ e^{L/2}`;
Cramer with (8bis.13e) gives `a = O(e^{−L})`, `b = O(Le^{−L/2})`, and (8bis.13f)
the correction's **sup norm** `O(Le^{−L/2})` — the same size as `‖e‖_∞`, so
every estimate survives.

*One correction to the review's prescription, reported as instructed.* The review
wrote the correction is "`O(Le^{−L/2})` **in `C¹`**". It is not: a single mesh
bump has `‖·‖_{C¹} ≍ e^{L/2}`, so `w_1` has `C¹` norm `≍ e^{L}` and
`‖a w_1‖_{C¹} ≍ L`. The size that **matters** is the **sup** norm, because in
Step 3 the derivative always falls on the smooth factor `g̃_L` (Young:
`‖ẽ⋆g̃_L^†‖_{C¹} ≤ ‖ẽ‖_∞‖g̃_L'‖_1`). With that reading the review's `a ≍ Le^{−L}`,
`b ≍ Le^{−L/4}` and my `a = O(e^{−L})`, `b = O(Le^{−L/2})` agree in the only
place they are used, namely `‖correction‖_∞ = O(Le^{−L/2})`.

**M2, error 2 (the import).** Accepted: for non-primitive `g_L` the polar term
is `|ĝ_L(±i/2)|² ≍ L²e^L`, which **dominates** `e^{2|β−1/2|L}` since
`2|β−1/2| < 1`. **W20.** Repair: `Π` makes the tests primitive, the Tate packet
is then **absent** (Paper 45 Thm 8.1: it is `β_T(M,M)`, `M = 0`), and the
import is restated with **no Tate clause** and renamed **(Wgr)** (M6(b)).
`Π` multiplies a zero's contribution by `|ρ_0(1−ρ_0)|² ≠ 0` and preserves
support, so the main term survives.

**M3/LF1 (the partition of unity).** Accepted, and the counterexample is
reproduced in the script: a mass-one bump of width `0.1` gives
`|Σ_kρ(x−k) − 1|` up to `7.29`. **W22.** Repair: `ρ ∈ C_c^∞([−1,1])` is now a
genuine smooth **partition of unity**, `Σ_kρ(x−k) ≡ 1`, and the **pointwise**

## 5. Claims of the round-5 instruction that I refuted or corrected

1. **"the correction … hence `O(Le^{−L/2})` in `C¹`" (item 3, from LF2).**
   **Refuted.** *Counterexample:* a single mesh basis element
   `ρ((·−log n)/gap_n)` has `‖·‖_∞ = ‖ρ‖_∞ ≍ 1` but `‖·‖_{C¹} ≍ 1/gap_n ≍ e^{L}`
   near `log n ≈ L`; so `w_1`, being a sum of such with coefficients `≍ e^{L/2}`,
   has `C¹` norm `≍ e^{3L/2}` and `‖a w_1‖_{C¹} ≍ Le^{L/2}`, **not**
   `O(Le^{−L/2})`. The estimate is true and is what the proof needs in the
   **sup** norm, for the structural reason that in Step 3 the derivative falls
   on the smooth factor. Written into the paper as (8bis.13f) with the note.

2. **"the N-linear diagonal divergence … in the convention Thm 8bis.1 adopts
   the same diagonal → 0. Fix ONE convention" (item 5).** Accepted as a
   finding, **but the instruction's framing that one convention settles it is
   not sustainable**, and I report why rather than pick a convention that makes
   a headline true. *Counterexample to "fix one convention and the statement is
   fine":* in the bare convention the Gram diagonal `→ 0`; in the ♭ convention
   it `→ −∞` **geometrically**, not linearly (the linear bracket is multiplied
   by `V_N^{-1}`, which grows geometrically). **So "linear divergence in `N`"
   is true in *neither* convention as a statement about the Gram diagonal** —
   it is a statement about `T_∞` of the smeared idempotent with bare indicators
   and unit height, and nothing else. I therefore (i) fixed the bare convention
   as instructed, (ii) displayed **both** values side by side with the
   conversion factor as the instruction permits, and (iii) **removed every
   rate from every headline**, keeping only the convention-free statement
   (Rem 8bis.1c). Numerics block (F) shows both columns.

3. **"(b) if the audit's stronger statement holds, prove it … a primitive
   radial profile with `ĥ` concentrated near `τ = 0`" (item 2).** Proved — but
   the instruction's parenthetical "`ĝ(∓i/2) = 0` is two linear conditions" is
   **not how it is done**, and doing it that way would repeat the M2 error: the
   two conditions are *unbounded* functionals on a concentrated test, and
   enforcing them by perturbation destroys the concentration. The correct route
   is `Π`, which makes the conditions **automatic** (`λ_∓(Γ_{Πg}) ≡ 0` for
   every `g`) at zero cost to concentration, since `ĝ_T(τ) = (τ²+¼)ĝ_{0,T}(τ)`.
   And the colimit step the instruction asks me to verify **does not go
   through**; I state (b) at a fixed chart and record the colimit as `OPEN`, as
   the instruction directs in that case.

4. **"Lemma 8bis.5 … with a correct constant" (item 6).** There is **no**
   correct explicit constant of the round-4 shape: the displayed `4` requires
   `ψ(X) ≤ X`, which fails for infinitely many `X` (Littlewood). The repair is
   to write an unspecified absolute `C_0` and label the import; an explicit
   constant would require an explicit Chebyshev constant, which the paper does
   not need and does not claim.
