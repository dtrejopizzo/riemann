## 1. The FATAL finding: accepted in full, repaired, not softened

**F1 (audit) = the review's confirmation.** Round 3's Corollary 8bis.1a said
the Gram form of the Frobenius family *is* the Weil kernel. **It is false**,
and the refutation is internal: apply (3ter.9) with `r = m/n`. The archimedean
part of the off-diagonal entry carries

    ∏_{s ∈ S_f∖(P(m)∪P(n))} 1[ s^N | (n−m) ] ,

which vanishes for all large `N` whenever `S_f ⊋ P(m)∪P(n)` — equivalently,
`supp Frob_{m/n,F} ∩ Im j_∞ = ∅` (Cor 3ter.10b). I accept this without
reservation; my own round-3 report had already recorded the mechanism as "a
fourth correction" and §8bis was then written as if it did not apply. That is
my error, not the audit's overreach.

**What replaces it** (§8bis.1, fully rewritten):

| **(8bis.2a)** | the off-diagonal **archimedean** part, displayed with its arithmetic indicator, vanishing for large `N` |
| **Thm 8bis.1b** | the **diagonal**, computed (F2): no shells; unit packets at `p\|n` vanish; unit packets at `r∤n` are `−(log r)[(r−2)/(r−1)+(N−1)]` **exactly**, linear in `N`; archimedean `−log(1/ε)+O(1)` |

I verified the unit-packet computation independently before writing it. With
`du` the probability Haar measure of `Z_r^×`: `vol(1+r^jZ_r) = 1/((r−1)r^{j−1})`
for `j ≥ 1`, hence `vol{v_r(1−u) = j} = r^{−j}` for `j ≥ 1` and `(r−2)/(r−1)`
for `j = 0`, summing to `1`; the integrand `|1−u|_r^{−1} = r^{j}` is constant
on each stratum; so `∫_{Z_r^×∖(1+r^NZ_r)}|1−u|_r^{−1}du = (r−2)/(r−1) + (N−1)`.
The `r = 2` case (`Z_2^× = 1+2Z_2`, first term `0`) is a consistency check and
passes. The archimedean `−log(1/ε)+O(1)` is derived from (8.5∞) by splitting at
`t = ε`, using `K(t) = 1/(2t)+O(1)` and `φ(t/ε) − e^{−t/2} = O((t/ε)²)+O(t)`.

**F2** (diagonal never computed): repaired by Thm 8bis.1b. **F3** (missing

## 2. Claims of the round-4 instruction that I REFUTED, with the counterexample

The the round's message and the review's L1/L3 contain three claims I could not
prove as stated. In each case I wrote the corrected statement and said so in
the paper.

**R1. "Chart-invariance of the ruling pairing forces `c_p = log p`."**
(Instruction L1: "examine whether chart-invariance ... forces every place to
carry the same residue".) **It does not, and cannot.** *Counterexample/reason,
written as Remark 8bis.8b:* Paper 43 Theorem 9.7b(c) computes the **total**
Tate residue of `T̂_S` as `∓i(|S_f|+1)` — one `∓i` per place. That number is
**manifestly chart-dependent**: adding a place changes it. Chart-invariance of
`T_∞` (Thm 8A.7c(e)) is a statement about values on compact windows of bounded
radius; a residue is read off the symbol, and no statement of Paper 43 makes
the total residue chart-independent. So no chart-invariance argument can pin
the per-place value. The instruction anticipated this ("If it does NOT close,
say so exactly"), and I have. **What I proved instead** (Thm 8bis.8): the local
Tate residues are `c_p/log p` (finite) and `1` (archimedean), so they agree at
all places **iff** `c_p = log p`; this is a **uniqueness theorem given a named
requirement** — universality of the Tate residue per place — which is exactly
the requirement Paper 43 already uses when it reads the total residue as a
count `|S_f|+1`. Cor 8bis.8a then states Λ(p^k) = log p as the Haar volume of
`supp Frob_{p^k}` in that unique normalization. Weil's normalization is cited
as `CLASSICAL` context only, used nowhere.

**R2. "Weil positivity on Dirichlet-polynomial profiles ⇒ RH, with a single
`ε`."** (L3 as posed, and Def 8bis.3's constant-width class.) **The
single-`ε` version fails**, and I state exactly why (Remark 8bis.7a(2)). Over
the window `[L/2, L]` the gaps `gap_n = log(1+1/n)` vary by a factor `e^{L/2}`.
With `ε = min gap_n ≍ e^{−L}` the bumps near `log n ≈ L/2` (spacing
`≍ e^{−L/2} ≫ ε`) do not overlap, `g_D` is a comb of spikes, and
`‖g−g_D‖_∞ ≍ ‖g‖_∞` — not `O(e^{−L/2})`. With `ε = max gap_n ≍ e^{−L/2}` the
bumps near `log n ≈ L` overlap `≍ e^{L/2}` at a time and the partition of unity
is destroyed. **What I proved instead** (Thm 8bis.7): the converse **does**
close for **per-`n` widths** `ε_n ≍ gap_n`, modulo the named classical import
**(W)** (Weil's growth estimate at a hypothetical off-line zero). The two
supporting lemmas are proved here: Lemma 8bis.5 (`|T(Γ_h)| ≤ 4‖h‖_∞e^{R/2} +
C‖h‖_{C¹}` for `supp h ⊆ [−R,R]`, from Chebyshev plus the (8.5∞)
regularization) and Lemma 8bis.6 (the mollified Riemann sum, error
`O(e^{−L/2}‖g‖_{C¹})`). The cross terms are `O(Le^{−L/4})` against a main term
`≍ L²e^{2|β−1/2|L}`. So the fixed-window obstruction of round 3 is **not
repaired — it is circumvented**, by letting the window move to `+∞` *and* the
widths track the local gaps; the constant-width class stays `OPEN`
(Thm 8bis.4(v)).

**R3. "The interface is `I_∂ ≤ 0` on primitive combinations of smeared
Frobenii ⟺ RH."** (Round-3 claim; and the instruction's phrasing "Hodge index
on primitive combinations of smeared Frobenii ⇔ RH" survives in the
Frobenius-side sentence.) **False for the pure Frobenius family**, and I prove
a **negative theorem** rather than merely dropping the claim
(Thm 8bis.4(iv)): the Frobenius kernel is `W_fin` alone, and the missing
archimedean symbol `ω̂_∞(τ) = log π − Re ψ(1/4+iτ/2)` is **indefinite** — it is
`≈ 5.372 > 0` at `τ = 0` (Paper 43 (14.2)) and `→ −∞` as `|τ| → ∞` (8bis.8).
Two quadratic forms whose symbols differ by an indefinite function are not
equivalent for definiteness on an infinite-dimensional space. Hence the
sentence "the object whose Hodge index is RH is the Frobenius Gram form" is
killed and replaced by "…is the Gram form of `Frob ⋆ Δ^rad`", exactly as
instructed. The equivalence itself (8bis.10), stated on the **radial** family,
is `CLASSICAL`, unconditional, has **no** density question (the profile class
is all of `C_c^∞(R)`), and — as the instruction requires me to say in so many
words — **it is Weil's criterion carried by row A's trace, and it is not new**.
The renaming test is passed only because the new content is Thm 8bis.1e, the
factorization through the Frobenius family.

*(A fourth, smaller correction: the instruction's "compute
`T(Δ^{(n)} ⋆ ρ_ε ⋆ ρ_ε^† ⋆ 1_{U_N})`" is stated with a mass-normalized bump; I
state Thm 8bis.1b for a **unit-height** bump `φ_ε(0) = 1`, because with mass
normalization the archimedean term is `−ε^{−1}log(1/ε)`-type and the finite
unit packets acquire the same `ε^{−1}`, which obscures the `N`-linearity that
is the point. Both normalizations give divergence; the unit-height one
separates the two mechanisms cleanly.)*
