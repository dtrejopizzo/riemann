## 1. Danskin and Theorem 2.3

**1.1 The formula.** One must distinguish what each part requires.

- (2.1) is 𝔞^{a′}(g*_a) ≥ λ_A(a′) plus affinity in a. It holds **with no hypothesis** on the minimizer; it only requires g*_a to be
  admissible. It is the only thing the memo uses afterward.
- Differentiability (Prop. 2.1) requires three things:
  - (i) the infimum is attained;
  - (ii) the set of minimizers is compact;
  - (iii) the minimizer is unique (mod sign).

  If there is no uniqueness, Danskin gives only the directional derivative λ′(a; b) = min_{g∈M}(−⟨b, s(g)⟩), which is concave in b,
  not linear.
- In the certified basis μ = 15, (i)–(iii) are reasonable: finite dimension, and LEMMA_I3 reports an O(1) gap.
- **But that λ_A is the one of the truncated class**, and it is ≥ λ_A in P_T. For the full P_T, (i) and (ii) are not proved: the
  problem is infinite-dimensional and compactness depends on the archimedean part being coercive outside the near-kernel.
- **Correct label:** Prop. 2.1 PROVED for the μ-truncated class, CONDITIONAL (existence and uniqueness) for P_T. (2.1) PROVED in general.
- Prop. 2.2 inherits the same and additionally requires a uniform gap. "Standard Rayleigh–Schrödinger" is correct with a gap. Without
  a gap it is ARGUED.

**1.2 "Concavity only gives upper bounds."** Correct, and complete in this sense:

- a concave function is bounded below only via chords (convex combinations of points with known value);
- the tangent (Danskin, 2.1) and the second-order term bound from above.

One precision is missing. The chords need not lie on the comb line: any family {a_i} with Λ in its convex hull and known λ_A(a_i)
works. That case is exactly the registered "convex decomposition" (index l. 212), so the exception to Theorem 2.3 is correctly
identified. **Theorem 2.3: CONFIRMED.**

## 2. Object III — the ladder

**(a) The sign part is REF-D.**

- Λ(n) ≥ 0 and c_{g*}(log n) > 0 for n = 2..14 (the thirteen JSON values are positive).
- Therefore W^{(N)} is **non-increasing** in N, and W^{(N)} ≥ W^{(14)} = W_T(g*).
- "0 ≤ W^{(N)} ∀N" is equivalent to W_T(g*) ≥ 0, the Mori datum, already CITED.
- **L-III.1(ii) is tautological: it holds without informing anything.** The memo's logical order ("final positivity propagates
  contact by contact") is inverted: it is the final positivity that implies the partial ones.
- An ascending "step lemma" (§4.6) would need to prove W^{(N−1)} ≥ v_N c(log N); at the last step that **is** W* ≥ 0. Label: REF-D.

**(b) The domination is a ratio of contacts, and is ill-defined.**

- W^{(N)} = W* + Σ_{n>N} v_n c(log n).
- With superexponential decay and W* ≈ 10⁻⁷⁰ negligible, W^{(N)} = v_{N+1}c(log(N+1))·(1 + O(10⁻²)) if Λ(N+1) ≠ 0. Then
  **q_N ≈ 1 automatically**: it is arithmetic of a tail dominated by its first term.
- **Technical flaw:** with Λ(N+1) = 0 (N = 5, 9, 11, 13) the denominator of q_N is 0 and (L-esc) is undefined. It must be replaced
  with "the next contact with Λ ≠ 0".
- Even corrected, q_N is a functional of the profile of g* = h* (Theorem X; AUDIT_CONSTRAINTS restriction 11, W-lin). It cannot
  separate combs.

**(c) The ladder is the edge profile of g*: it is (α).** Fit of a line over the thirteen values:

| log₁₀c / (log n)² | −2.49 | −2.74 | −3.02 | −3.31 | −3.61 | −3.91 | −4.25 | −4.59 | −4.97 | −5.40 | −5.90 | −6.50 | −7.35 |

- The sequence is **smooth and monotone in x = log n**. The composites (4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14) fall on the same curve as the
  primes, with no jump at all.
- The slope steepens near 2T = log 15 (log₁₀c ≈ −51 at distance 0.069 from the edge). It is the profile of a prolate g* (c = 30π)
  nearly vanishing near ±T; c_{g*} is an analytic function of x sampled at x = log n.
