## 1. Upper-triangular total differential

Let

\[
 (P_1\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d_P}P_0),\qquad
 (C_1\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d_C}C_0)                 \tag{1.1}
\]

be two two-term real or complex Hilbert complexes.  Think of `d_C` as the
multiplicatively compatible divisor-incidence differential of `R170_866`,
whose kernel is the line generated by the exact arithmetic anchor
`e_Lambda=(Lambda,1)`.  Let

\[
 L_1:P_1\to C_1,\qquad L_0:P_0\to C_0                 \tag{1.2}
\]

be the proposed Euler--Poisson transmission maps.  On the direct sums define

\[
 D_1=\begin{pmatrix}d_P&0\\ L_1&d_C\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 D_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\ L_0&0\end{pmatrix},          \tag{1.3}
\]

where the displayed grading only records the part relevant to the composite.
Equivalently, in standard mapping-cone notation, the total differential has
off-diagonal component `L`.

### Theorem 1 (chain-gate identity). `PROVED`

The square of the total differential has a single possibly nonzero
off-diagonal block,

\[
 \boxed{D_{\rm tot}^{,2}\big|_{P_1}
       =d_C L_1-L_0d_P.}                                \tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
 D_{\rm tot}^{,2}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d_C L_1=L_0d_P.                                         \tag{1.5}
\]

**Proof.**  Apply the two successive block differentials to `f in P_1`.
The `C_0` component is `d_C L_1f-L_0d_Pf`, with the sign dictated by the
mapping-cone grading.  All diagonal squares vanish because (1.1) are
complexes.  This proves (1.4)--(1.5). `QED`

The sign convention in (1.4) is immaterial: changing the suspension changes
both occurrences of `L` and leaves the chain-map equation unchanged.

### Proposition 2 (nilpotence is not a free sign). `PROVED`

Defining `L_1` so that (2.3) holds does not imply (1.5).  Conversely, defining
`L_0` on `ran d_P` by the equation in (1.5) makes nilpotence tautological and
places no restriction on `beta_phys` over `ker d_P`.

**Proof.**  Equation (1.4) says that the failure of nilpotence is exactly the
chain-map defect.  Prescribing one scalar observation of `d_CL_1F` cannot make
that operator vanish.  In the converse direction, (1.5) determines `L_0d_P`
only on `ran d_P`; on `ker d_P` its right side is zero and the assertion to be
proved is precisely `d_CL_1F=0`.  Thus extension from the range does not
control the radical. `QED`

## 6. The affine factor-swap alternative is cohomologically trivial

Let `J^2=I` on a real vector space `V`.  An affine action of the order-two
group has the form

\[
 T(v)=Jv+\Gamma,
 \qquad T^2=I,
\]

and hence its cocycle satisfies

\[
 (I+J)\Gamma=0.                                          \tag{6.1}
\]

### Theorem 3 (order-two cocycles are coboundaries). `PROVED`

Every `Gamma` satisfying (6.1) has

\[
 \Gamma=(I-J)h,\qquad h=\Gamma/2.                        \tag{6.2}
\]

After the translation `v'=v-h`, the affine involution becomes the linear
involution `v' -> Jv'`.

**Proof.**  Equation (6.1) gives `J Gamma=-Gamma`, so
`(I-J)(Gamma/2)=Gamma`.  Substitution into `T` proves the translated formula.
`QED`

Thus the covariant cocycle `Gamma_{L,S}` isolated in `R170_876` cannot itself
be a new sign-bearing invariant: over the real numbers it is always removable
by recentering the ambient affine space.  Such a recentering helps row D only
if it also preserves the physical fiber `A_L`, the first-contact radical and
the Tate normalization.  `R170_876(0.3)--(0.4)` proves that the raw swap does
not preserve `A_L`; translating by half its defect changes the physical
operator.  Requiring that it not change the operator is precisely an
additional intertwining law, not a consequence of (6.1).

This closes the pure affine-symmetry branch.  The common-radical chain gate
`(CG1)--(CG2)` is the remaining oriented mechanism in this route.
