## 1. Correction to the earlier source audit

The long primary source, Haran, *New foundations for geometry*, Section
13.2, proves two genuine normal-form statements for an individual oriented
tree:

1. every oriented tree has a unique `1`-reduced tree;
2. every oriented tree has a unique `lhd`-reduced tree, obtained by removing
   adjacent vertices with the same orientation.

For a reduced tree, the orientations alternate and are determined by the
root orientation.  Thus the individual-tree part of the normal-form problem
is not open and must not be included in H7-NF.

However, an element of the positive arithmetic plane is not one tree.  It is
bilateral data

\[
 ([F_1];[\bar F_x]_{x\in X};\sigma),                                  \tag{1.1}
\]

where `sigma` bijects the two leaf sets, modulo isomorphism, the two tree
reductions and **consistent commutativity**.  The source defines the latter
equivalence by finite chains of moves.  It does not choose a unique reduced
representative of (1.1) modulo those chains.

This distinction reconciles the source with `a74`--`a75`: those files give a
faithful analytic normal form on a one-sided signed read-once sector, while
the source supplies the local tree reductions used before the bilateral cut
quotient.

## 2. What changes when integers are introduced

Let

\[
 P=\Upsilon\otimes_{\mathbb F}\mathbb F\{\pm1\}                       \tag{2.1}
\]

be the signed positive presentation.  Haran gives a surjection

\[
 \pi:P\twoheadrightarrow
 A=\mathcal G(\mathbb Z)
 \otimes_{\mathbb F\{\pm1\}}\mathcal G(\mathbb Z),                    \tag{2.2}
\]

and states that its kernel is the equivalence ideal

\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm cancel}=E((x_0,x_1))                                 \tag{2.3}
\]

generated by the left and right cancellation elements.  This is an exact
generators-and-relations statement, but it does not assert that (2.3) is
root-closed under multiplication by a prime.

For every operation set define the colon equivalence relation

\[
 (\mathcal E_{\rm cancel}:p)
 =\{(F,G):(pF,pG)\in\mathcal E_{\rm cancel}\}.                         \tag{2.4}
\]

Because `E_cancel` is compatible with every generalized-ring operation,
(2.4) is the exact preimage congruence of multiplication by `p` on the
quotient.

### Theorem 2.1 (cancellation-purity criterion)

On a fixed arity and chart, multiplication by `p` on `A` is injective if and
only if

\[
 (\mathcal E_{\rm cancel}:p)=\mathcal E_{\rm cancel}.                  \tag{2.5}
\]

### Proof

The classes of `F,G` have the same `p`-multiple precisely when the pair
`(pF,pG)` lies in the kernel congruence.  Equality of the original classes is
precisely `(F,G) in E_cancel`.  Thus injectivity and (2.5) are equivalent.
QED.

This is the source-level version of the saturation equivalence in `a64`.

## 3. Why positive uniqueness alone is insufficient

Even when a presentation object has unique representatives, a quotient by a
generated relation can create prime torsion if the relation is not pure.  In
the additive model

\[
 \mathbb Z/(p\mathbb Z),                                                \tag{3.1}
\]

the ambient group has a unique integer normal form, but `p*[1]=0` in the
quotient.  The failure is exactly

\[
 (p\mathbb Z:p)=\mathbb Z\ne p\mathbb Z.                              \tag{3.2}

Therefore the unique `1`/alternating reductions of Section 13.2 do not by
themselves prove (2.5).  One needs a theorem about how cancellation moves
interact with bilateral leaf bijections, contexts and consistent-cut moves.

Nor may one simply call (2.2) a free abelian group completion: the operation
sets are generalized-ring sets, and consistent commutativity can move a cut
through a bilateral graph.  A freeness or purity statement is exactly what
must be proved, not an available premise.

## 4. Sector already proved pure

The analytic maps of `a74`--`a75` show that the restriction of
`E_cancel` to every signed one-sided read-once family is `p`-root-closed for
every prime.  Indeed, equality after multiplication by `p` maps to equality
of real functions; real cancellation followed by signed Hessian
reconstruction recovers the tree.

Consequently any failure of (2.5) must use structure absent from that sector:

1. a repeated/contracted use of an input;
2. a genuinely nontrivial tree on both sides of the leaf bijection; or
3. a consistent-commutativity/cut chain that cannot be represented inside a
   one-sided read-once tree.

## 5. The exact remaining PRIME-REG theorem

Replace the overly broad old normal-form gate by

> **H7-CANCEL-PURE.** For every prime `p`, every arity, every affine member
> of a cofinal pro-cover and every subsequent central localization, the
> cancellation equivalence ideal (2.3) satisfies (2.5).

By `a71`, central localization creates no new collision once this holds on
the affine presentation.  By `a75`, only the repeated/bilateral cut sector
remains.  Thus H7-CANCEL-PURE is equivalent to the remaining H7-RF-BICUT
problem, not an additional gate.

A proof can take either of two exact forms:

1. construct a faithful target on the bilateral quotient in which `p`
   cancels; or
2. prove directly that every finite cancellation/cut derivation of
   `pF~pG` can be divided by `p`.

The source provides neither theorem.  H7-PRIME-REG and the completed lattice
therefore remain open, but their unresolved content is now isolated from the
positive individual-tree normal forms that are already proved.

`a_77` further proves that all admissible cuts of one fixed expanded network
are connected by consistent-commutativity moves and that cancellation inside
one fixed parallel bundle is prime-pure.  Thus H7-CANCEL-PURE is sharpened to
H7-CORE-CONFLUENCE: only overlaps where cancellation triggers pruning or
contraction and changes the network core remain.

**Later scope correction (`a_81`).** Fixed-incidence local moves do not
generate every multiplication/contraction context image of `x_0,x_1`.  The
binary `K2,2` grid is locally irreducible but contextually zero.  Hence the
full residual gate is H7-MACRO-CONTEXT-NF/SAT, and the preceding local
sharpening is not a replacement for the colon criterion (2.5).
