## 1. The degree-product form on the presentation lattice

Use the all-prime presentation lattice of `a112`, written

\[
 \mathfrak D_{\rm pr}=\mathfrak D_1\oplus\mathfrak D_2.                 \tag{1.1}
\]

For `x=(m_(p,1),m_(p,2))`, define the two ruling degrees

\[
 d_i(x)=\sum_pm_{p,i}\log p.                                           \tag{1.2}
\]

Put `c=(2 log 3)^(-1)` and define

\[
 B_{\rm RR}^{\rm cand}(x,y)
 =c\bigl(d_1(x)d_2(y)+d_1(y)d_2(x)\bigr).                              \tag{1.3}
\]

This is a symmetric bilinear form, each ruling is isotropic, and

\[
 \frac12B_{\rm RR}^{\rm cand}(x,x)=c\,d_1(x)d_2(x).                    \tag{1.4}
\]

Thus (1.4) is exactly the continuous code coefficient of `a53`.  On the
rank-two lattice generated by `D_(p,1),D_(q,2)`, the matrix is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&c\log p\log q\\
 c\log p\log q&0
 \end{pmatrix},                                                        \tag{1.5}
\]

which has signature `(1,1)`.

## 2. Descent criterion

Recall the completed Picard realization

\[
 \rho:\mathfrak D_{\rm pr}\longrightarrow
 \mathrm{Pic}_{\rm cmp}(Y^{\rm reg}).                            \tag{2.1}
\]

A bilinear form on `rho(mathfrak D_pr)` pulls back to (1.3) if and only if
`ker rho` lies in the radical of (1.3).

### Theorem 2.1 (RR descent iff anti-diagonal faithfulness)

The following are equivalent:

1. `B_RR^cand` descends to a bilinear form on `rho(mathfrak D_pr)`;
2. `rho` is injective;
3. the prime anti-diagonal map `delta_pr` of `a112` is injective.

### Proof

The equivalence of (2) and (3) is Proposition 3.1 of `a112`.  If `rho` is
injective, descent is tautological.

Conversely, take a nonzero `x in ker rho`.  By `a112` it has the unique form

\[
 x=\sum_pa_p(e_{p,1}-e_{p,2}).                                        \tag{2.2}
\]

Put

\[
 A=\sum_pa_p\log p.                                                    \tag{2.3}
\]

Unique factorization implies `A!=0`: otherwise `product p^(a_p)=1`, forcing
every `a_p=0`.  For any prime `r`, take `y=e_(r,2)`.  Then

\[
 B_{\rm RR}^{\rm cand}(x,y)=cA\log r\ne0.                              \tag{2.4}
\]

Thus `x` is not radical, so (1.3) cannot descend through a nontrivial kernel.
QED.

This is stronger than saying the anti-diagonal is aesthetically desirable:
it is logically necessary for the degree-product RR form even on the
prime-generated sector.

## 3. Principal invariance and what it does not prove

The numerical form (1.3) depends only on the two completed Picard degrees.
Therefore, once descent is proved, it is invariant under principal changes
of the curve representatives.  Its difference from the local contact block
is exactly the forced complementary value in `a115`.

However, three implications must not be reversed:

1. writing (1.3) does not prove `delta_pr` injective;
2. anti-diagonal injectivity would make the formula well-defined, but would
   not construct the generalized excess/Green complex realizing it;
3. the quadratic code lower bound does not prove that the global selective
   section invariant has (1.4) as its full leading coefficient.

Thus the live chain is now

\[

 \delta_{\rm pr}\text{ faithfulness}
 \longrightarrow B_{\rm RR}^{\rm cand}\text{ descends}
 \longrightarrow\text{H7-REG-EXCESS-RR + H7-SEL-RR/EXACT}.             \tag{3.1}
\]

## 4. Status

`a116` closes the algebraic descent **criterion** and constructs the forced
form on the presentation lattice.  It does not close the anti-diagonal
theorem, geometric intersection, selective Riemann--Roch, gauge, row A or
RH.

**Reflected-square correction (`a122`).**  Bare H7-U3/LD applies to the
literal Cech square only.  Since `Y^reg->Y` is induced by a quotient of chart
rings, restriction can kill Picard classes.  The sufficient route after
reflection is H7-U3 + H7-LD + H7-REFL-PIC, or the direct ruling product
formula H7-RULING-PF.
