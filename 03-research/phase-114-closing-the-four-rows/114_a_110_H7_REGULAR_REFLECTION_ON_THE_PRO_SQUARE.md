## 1. Functoriality of the regular reflection

For any commutative involutive `F`-ring `A` carrying two signed integer
rulings, define `Reg_Z(A)=A/E_reg(A)` exactly as in `a109`, using all equality
kernels of maps from `A` to simultaneous Z-regular targets.

### Theorem 1.1

`Reg_Z` is a functor and a left adjoint to the inclusion of the full
subcategory of simultaneous Z-regular objects.  It is idempotent.

### Proof

For a map `f:A->B` and any map `g:B->T` to a regular target, `g f` is one of
the maps used to define `E_reg(A)`.  Hence

\[
 (f\times f)E_{\rm reg}(A)\subseteq E_{\rm reg}(B),             \tag{1.1}
\]

so `f` descends uniquely.  The factorization theorem of `a109`, natural in
the target, is precisely the adjunction.  If `A` is already regular its
identity map occurs among the defining maps, so `E_reg(A)` is diagonal and
the unit is an isomorphism.  QED.

In particular `Reg_Z` preserves all colimits computed followed by reflection.

## 2. Compatibility with central localization

Let `S` be a central multiplicative system in `A_(1,1)`.  There is a
canonical isomorphism in the regular category

\[
 \mathrm{Reg}_{\mathbb Z}(S^{-1}A)
 \simeq
 \mathrm{Reg}_{\mathbb Z}
 \left(S^{-1}\mathrm{Reg}_{\mathbb Z}(A)\right).          \tag{2.1}
\]

The unreflected central localization on the right is already Z-regular, so
(2.1) becomes

\[
 \mathrm{Reg}_{\mathbb Z}(S^{-1}A)
 \simeq S^{-1}\mathrm{Reg}_{\mathbb Z}(A).                \tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Maps from either side to a regular target are maps `A->T` killing
`E_reg(A)` and sending `S` to units.  The two universal properties coincide.
For the last statement, localization is the filtered colimit of central
multiplication maps.  Multiplication by a nonzero integer commutes with this
system; filtered colimits of abelian groups preserve the injective sequence
`0->M --n--> M`.  Thus every operation set remains Z-torsion-free.  QED.

## 3. The repaired scheme and pro-system

Let `Y={Y_(N,M)}` be Haran's literal pro-square.  Choose its standard affine
cover

\[
 Y_{N,M}=\bigcup_\alpha\mathrm{Spec}\,A_\alpha.             \tag{3.1}
\]

Replace each chart by `Spec Reg_Z(A_alpha)`.  On a standard central overlap
`Spec S^{-1}A_alpha`, equation (2.2) identifies its reflection with
`Spec S^{-1}Reg_Z(A_alpha)`.  Hence the reflected affine charts have the
same transition isomorphisms needed for gluing.

### Proposition 3.1 (H7-REG-SHEAF)

The reflected charts glue to commutative involutive `F`-ring schemes
`Y^reg_(N,M)` and form a functorial pro-system.  Every nonzero integer in
either ruling acts injectively on every arity, every smaller open and every
later pro-level.  There is a canonical morphism

\[
 Y^{\rm reg}_{N,M}\longrightarrow Y_{N,M}.                    \tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Equation (2.2) makes the reflected affine gluing independent of the chosen
standard cover.  Restriction and pro-transition maps descend by Theorem 1.1
and respect overlaps by naturality.  A stalk is a central localization, or
equivalently a filtered colimit, of regular operation groups; exactness
preserves injectivity of multiplication by every integer.  A local section
killed by `n` has every germ zero and is therefore zero.  The quotient maps
`A_alpha->Reg_Z(A_alpha)` give the displayed scheme morphism
contravariantly.  QED.

Write

\[
 Y^{\rm reg}=\{Y^{\rm reg}_{N,M}\}_{N,M}.                      \tag{3.3}
\]

This is a new pro-scheme mapping to the literal square.  Its underlying
topological stages are the spectra of the reflected quotients; they are
**not** asserted to equal the old stages.  Both projections and the diagonal
descend, with the same retraction identities, by `a109`.

## 4. Completed lattices generated by prime denominators

For every prime `p`, Proposition 3.1 places its two ruling images in Haran's
Section-11 denominator system on `Y^reg`.  Therefore the local multiplier
`1/p` is defined in the fraction sheaf and gives a completed inverse lattice

\[
 \mathcal L_{p,i}^{\rm reg}=p_i^*\mathcal L_p,qquad i=1,2.     \tag{4.1}
\]

For

\[
 n=\prod_p p^{v_p(n)},
\]

put

\[
 \mathcal L_{n,i}^{\rm reg}
 =\bigotimes_p(\mathcal L_{p,i}^{\rm reg})^{\otimes v_p(n)}.   \tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (prime-generated completed lattice)

The lattices (4.2) exist on the repaired pro-square, are faithful in `n`,
and satisfy

\[
 \mathcal L_{m,i}^{\rm reg}\otimes
 \mathcal L_{n,i}^{\rm reg}simeq
 \mathcal L_{mn,i}^{\rm reg}.                                 \tag{4.3}
\]

Their unit torsors are the unconditional `Pic_tor` labels of `a66`, now
realized inside the completed fraction-lattice category.

### Proof

The only nonunit denominators in (4.2) are the finitely many primes dividing
`n`; Proposition 3.1 proves their Section-11 admissibility with all required
quantifiers.  Tensoring local generators adds valuations, giving (4.3).
Diagonal pullback recovers the ordinary curve lattice `L_n`, whose degree
`sum_p v_p(n) log p=log n` separates `n`; hence faithfulness.  The transition
cocycles are unchanged from their `Pic_tor` images.  QED.

The regular prime quotient of `a109` also gives

\[
 \deg_\Delta(V^{\rm reg}_{p,i})=\log p,                         \tag{4.4}
\]

so completed lattice degree and literal diagonal contact agree on the prime
generators.

## 5. Exact remaining scope

This closes H7-REG-SHEAF and the **prime-generated** instance of
H7-REG-DIV.  It does not yet compute the entire completed Picard group or
prove arbitrary Section-11 denominators from the curve remain regular.  It
also does not construct:

1. an intersection pairing between arbitrary repaired divisors;
2. dynamic cycles `Gamma_n` and their convolution;
3. the selective section moment/Riemann--Roch package;
4. the canonical Green gauge.

Those are H7-REG-INTER, H7-DYNAMIC-LIFT, H7-SEL-MOM/RR/EXACT and
H7-REG-GAUGE.  G-7, a4-strong and row A remain open.  Nothing here asserts
RH.

radicals in finite presentations, exactness of integer action under bounded
direct systems/localizations, valuation-additive lattice laws, faithfulness
and prime-contact degrees.  The sheaf/pro statements are the categorical
proofs above, not conclusions from finite enumeration.
