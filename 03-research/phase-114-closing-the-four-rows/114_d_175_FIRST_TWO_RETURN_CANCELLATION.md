# D.175 — First two return cancellations

## Verdict

The exact reference harmonic lift makes the first return vector a defect
difference.  In normalized source coordinates put

\[
 T=R_0^{\dagger/2}Y_0^*Y_0R_0^{\dagger/2},
 \qquad D=I-T.                                      \tag{0.1}
\]

Let

\[
 r=X_0^*X_E,qquad l=Y_0^*Y_E,qquad H=R_0^\dagger r,
 \qquad S=S_E,                                      \tag{0.2}
\]

and define

\[
 h=R_0^{1/2}HS^{\dagger/2},qquad
 q=R_0^{\dagger/2}(r-l)S^{\dagger/2}.              \tag{0.3}
\]

Then the source vector of the normalized boundary load is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 u:=R_0^{\dagger/2}Y_0^*y=Dh-q.}                    \tag{0.4}
\]

Here (q) is the complete primitive old--born cross of
(-B_{\rm nuc}): it contains the full Gamma place, the two Tate
projections and every (p^j).  Its coherent continuous Dirichlet part is
zero by the two primitive moments, so its arithmetic component is the
centered discrepancy (E_N=W_N-M_N), with the exact endpoint Volterra
pieces retained.

The first two nontrivial return moments are therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 c_1=(Dh-q)^*(Dh-q),qquad
 c_2=(Dh-q)^*T(Dh-q),}                               \tag{0.5}
\]

and satisfy the positive cancellation

\[
 \boxed{c_1-c_2=(Dh-q)^*D(Dh-q)\ge0.}               \tag{0.6}
\]

More generally, the whole part of the return series generated by (Dh)
telescopes.  With the usual supported-range convention,

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{k\ge1}c_k
 =\left(D^{1/2}h-D^{\dagger/2}q\right)^*
  \left(D^{1/2}h-D^{\dagger/2}q\right).}            \tag{0.7}
\]

Thus no decay estimate is required for the harmonic-lift contribution.
The only non-telescoping datum is

\[
 \boxed{q^*D^\dagger q,}                             \tag{0.8}
\]

plus its explicitly controlled cross with (h).  This is a smaller and
more arithmetic target than estimating the raw load (y) in the output
defect.  It also identifies where Tate centering must be applied at every
return: to (q), before any inverse or absolute value.

## 1. Exact derivation of the defect difference

The normalized born load of D.170 is

\[
 y=(Y_E-Y_0H)S^{\dagger/2}.                           \tag{1.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
 R_0^{\dagger/2}Y_0^*y
 =R_0^{\dagger/2}(l-Y_0^*Y_0H)S^{\dagger/2}.        \tag{1.2}
\]

Since (r=R_0H) on the supported range and
(Y_0^*Y_0=R_0^{1/2}TR_0^{1/2}),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 l-Y_0^*Y_0H
 &=r-(r-l)-(R_0-(R_0-Y_0^*Y_0))H\\
 &=(R_0-Y_0^*Y_0)H-(r-l).                            \tag{1.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Multiplying (1.3) by the two normalizing factors gives (0.4).
No positivity of the enlarged cell and no inverse of (D) is used.

## 2. The first two moments

D.172 gives, for (k\ge1),

\[
 c_k=u^*T^{k-1}u.                                    \tag{2.1}
\]

Substitution of (0.4) gives (0.5).  Because (D=I-T) commutes with
(T),

\[
 c_k-c_{k+1}=u^*T^{k-1}Du
 =\|D^{1/2}T^{(k-1)/2}u\|^2.                        \tag{2.2}
\]

The case (k=1) is (0.6).  This is the cancellation pattern requested
from the first two returns: successive returns lose exactly one positive
old-cell defect factor.

For a finite partial sum,

\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{m}c_k
 =u^*(I-T^m)D^\dagger u.                             \tag{2.3}
\]

Monotone convergence gives

\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}c_k=\|D^{\dagger/2}u\|^2.              \tag{2.4}
\]

Using (u=Dh-q) in (2.4) proves (0.7), provided (q) has no unsupported
component; otherwise both sides are infinite in the extended positive
cone.  In expanded form,

\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}c_k
 =h^*Dh-2\operatorname {Re}(h^*q)+q^*D^\dagger q.   \tag{2.5}
\]

If (q=0), the entire infinite return series is simply (h^*Dh).  Hence
the apparent repeated harmonic feedback is not a source of divergence.

## 3. Exact A--B--C content of the cross (q)

Before normalization,

\[
 r-l=X_0^*X_E-Y_0^*Y_E.                              \tag{3.1}
\]

For old primitive (F) and born primitive (e), (3.1) evaluates as

\[
 \langle X_0F,X_Ee\rangle-
 \langle Y_0F,Y_Ee\rangle
 =-B_{\rm nuc}(F,e).                                 \tag{3.2}
\]

Term by term, the finite part of (3.2) is

\[
 -\sum_{p^j\le N}{\log p\over p^{j/2}}
 \left(\langle F,S_{j\log p}e\rangle+
       \langle S_{j\log p}F,e\rangle\right),        \tag{3.3}
\]

and the archimedean part is the complete digamma/Gamma screw together
with the (\beta) and (Q_{1/2}) channels.  Equation (3.3) includes all
prime powers and no mixed integer, because its coefficient is
(\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n).

On the two-moment primitive space the continuous Chebyshev synthesis

\[
 M_N(\tau)={N^{1/2-i\tau}-1\over1/2-i\tau}           \tag{3.4}
\]

pairs only with the two functions (e^{\pm t/2}) and hence vanishes,
apart from the explicit endpoint Volterra pieces already present in the
exact support truncation.  Thus the nonlocal arithmetic coefficient in
(q) is

\[
 E_N(\tau)=
 \sum_{n\le N}{\Lambda(n)\over\sqrt n}e^{-i\tau\log n}
 -M_N(\tau),                                         \tag{3.5}
\]

not the uncentered (W_N).  Taking absolute values before forming (q)
would erase precisely the cancellation (3.4)--(3.5).

## 4. Reduced summable target

Equations (0.7)--(0.8) show that it is enough to construct

\[
 q=D^{1/2}a                                      \tag{4.1}
\]

with an estimate on (a) which fits the remaining born-cell budget.  This
is still a Douglas problem, but now its input is the centered completed
cross (3.2), rather than the raw load (y).

Equivalently, if (E_D) is the spectral measure of (D), the exact missing
measure is

\[
 \nu_N^q(B)=q^*E_D(B)q,
 \qquad q^*D^\dagger q=\int_{(0,1]}d^{-1}\,d\nu_N^q(d). \tag{4.2}
\]

A summable return bound can now be sought for

\[
 q^*T^kq,                                             \tag{4.3}
\]

where every occurrence of (q) is already Tate-centered and every
occurrence of (T) retains the exact reference inverse (R_0^\dagger).
This is the correct object for a centered large-sieve estimate.
