# D.33 — Blaschke defect and the semilocal Hodge target

## 1. Purpose

D.32 proves the exact pullback of `B_nuc` but also proves that its Hodge sign
cannot be obtained by applying D.31 separately at each prime.  This note
identifies the canonical local meaning of the D.31 Szego vectors and gives a
source-defined semilocal target in which the finite and Gamma terms are one
scattering defect.

## 2. The prime contact is a Blaschke defect

For `0<r<1`, let

\[
 b_r(z)=\frac{z-r}{1-rz},\qquad
 h_r(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-r^2}}{1-rz}.                   \tag{2.1}
\]

The function `b_r` is an inner function and its model space

\[
 K_{b_r}=H^2\ominus b_rH^2                              \tag{2.2}
\]

is one dimensional, generated by the normalized defect vector `h_r`.
For `z=e^{i theta}` one has the standard identity, verified directly by
differentiation,

\[
 \frac1i\frac d{d\theta}\log b_r(e^{i\theta})
 =\frac{1-r^2}{|1-re^{i\theta}|^2}
 =P_r(e^{i\theta}).                                  \tag{2.3}
\]

The trivial Tate winding is the inner function `z`; its phase derivative is
one.  Therefore the unitary local scattering ratio

\[
 v_r(z)=\frac{b_r(z)}z
       =\frac{1-rz^{-1}}{1-rz}                       \tag{2.4}
\]

satisfies

\[
 \frac1i\frac d{d\theta}\log v_r(e^{i\theta})
 =P_r(e^{i\theta})-1.                                \tag{2.5}
\]

For a prime `p`, put `r=p^(-1/2)` and `theta=tau log p`.  Then

\[
 \frac1i\frac d{d\tau}\log v_p(\tau)
 =\log p\bigl(P_{p^{-1/2}}(e^{i\tau\log p})-1\bigr). \tag{2.6}
\]

The Fourier multiplier on the right is exactly the complete `p^k` summand
of D.32(3.3).  Thus:

* `h_p` is not an auxiliary interpolation vector; it is the defect vector of
  the local Frobenius Blaschke characteristic;
* the positive Poisson norm is the `b_p` defect;
* the negative torsor norm is the trivial winding `z`;
* logarithmic differentiation produces every power `p^k` with coefficient
  `log(p)p^(-k/2)`.

This explains both the exact success and the rank limitation of D.31: it
models the one-dimensional defect of the local characteristic, not the
whole translation representation on test functions.

## 3. Gamma is the continuous scattering defect

Put `a=1/4` and

\[
 u_\infty(\tau)=
 \pi^{i\tau}\frac{\Gamma(a-i\tau/2)}{\Gamma(a+i\tau/2)}.             \tag{3.1}
\]

For real `tau`, this has modulus one.  Direct logarithmic differentiation
gives

\[
 \frac1i\frac d{d\tau}\log u_\infty(\tau)
 =\log\pi-\operatorname{Re}\psi(a+i\tau/2)
 =m_\infty(\tau).                                    \tag{3.2}
\]

The even Hardy oscillator of D.22 is the positive-energy realization of
this scattering character, and D.17 gives its renormalized phase derivative
as `m_0||F||^2-||partial_infinity F||^2`.

## 4. The finite semilocal phase identity

For a finite set `P` of primes define

\[
 \Theta_P(\tau)=u_\infty(\tau)\prod_{p\in P}v_p(\tau).               \tag{4.1}
\]

There is a useful signed model-space factorization.  Put

\[
 B_P(\tau)=\prod_{p\in P}b_{p^{-1/2}}
                  (e^{i\tau\log p}),\qquad
 Z_P(\tau)=\exp\left(i\tau\sum_{p\in P}\log p\right).               \tag{4.2}
\]

Both `B_P` and `Z_P` are inner functions of the upper half-plane and

\[
 \prod_{p\in P}v_p(\tau)=\frac{B_P(\tau)}{Z_P(\tau)}.                \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus the finite-place form is exactly the difference between the phase
density of a finite Blaschke model space and that of the trivial Tate
winding.  This is the scalar scattering counterpart of the two norms in
D.32(3.6).

It is unitary on the real axis and is built entirely from the local
characteristics of A--B and the Gamma boundary.  Equations (2.6) and (3.2)
give the exact identity

\[
 \frac1i\frac d{d\tau}\log\Theta_P(\tau)
 =m_\infty(\tau)+
  \sum_{p\in P}\log p\bigl(P_{p^{-1/2}}
     (e^{i\tau\log p})-1\bigr).                     \tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently the stabilized quadratic form of D.32 is the phase-derivative
form of the semilocal scattering product:

\[
 B_{P}(F,F)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 \frac1i\frac d{d\tau}\log\Theta_P(\tau)
 |\widehat F(\tau)|^2\,d\tau .                      \tag{4.5}
\]

Formula (4.5) is an identity, not a positivity argument.  A unitary
scattering function need not have a phase derivative of constant sign;
indeed the Gamma phase alone does not.  Local innerness in (4.2) also gives
no model-space inclusion: neither `B_P` nor `Z_P` divides the other, and the
Gamma ratio is a renormalized unitary scattering factor rather than a
bounded inner multiplier with the required divisibility.

## 5. Correct two-jet semilocal quotient

Let `Q>1`, take

\[
 P_Q=\{p:p<Q\},\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}F\subset[-\tfrac12\log Q,
                                \tfrac12\log Q].       \tag{5.1}
\]

The two primitive jets on the A--C test representation are the Tate
characters

\[
 M_-(F)=M_+(F)=0.                                    \tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently, they are the cokernel coordinates of the compact-potential
operator `D^2-1/4`: integration by parts gives
`M_+((D^2-1/4)u)=M_-((D^2-1/4)u)=0`, and the Green kernel proves the converse
for compactly supported functions with (5.2).  The local model spaces
`K_(b_p)`, the trivial winding spaces and the Gamma oscillator now assemble
into the semilocal scattering correspondence determined by
`Theta_(P_Q)`.  Identifying these two Tate cokernel coordinates with the two
boundary maps in an adelic Poisson realization must be part of the
semilocal comparison; it is not silently assumed here.

The required Hodge object is therefore the Sonin/Poisson quotient of this
**assembled** correspondence by the two boundary jets, not the direct sum
of the local one-dimensional defect spaces.  On that quotient the desired
theorem is a source statement:

\[
 \|\mathbf S_QF\|\le\|\mathbf B_QF\|
 \quad(F\text{ satisfying }(5.1)-(5.2)).             \tag{5.3}
\]

Together with D.32, (5.3) is exactly the primitive Hodge inequality.

## 6. What the Blaschke reformulation proves and what it does not

It proves:

1. the intrinsic origin of the D.31 Szego vectors as local model-space
   defects;
2. exact inclusion of every `p^k` by logarithmic differentiation;
3. exact assembly with Gamma into a single semilocal unitary phase;
4. exact identification of the two primitive jets;
5. compatibility of the finite cutoffs with the support exhaustion.

It does not yet prove (5.3).  Innerness of each local `b_p` does not imply
the sign of the derivative of the quotient `b_p/z`, and a direct sum of
local model-space inequalities is ruled out by D.32(6.3).  The new theorem
needed is a two-boundary contractivity theorem for the assembled semilocal
Poisson/Sonin quotient, uniform in `Q`.  Establishing it from Poisson
duality, rather than from the already evaluated form (4.5), is the active
row-D task.
