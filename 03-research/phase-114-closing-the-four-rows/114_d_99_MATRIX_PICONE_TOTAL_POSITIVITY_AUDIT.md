# D.99 — Matrix Picone and total-positivity audit

## Status

A scalar positive supersolution controls the bottom eigenvalue of a
Dirichlet operator.  Row D is a codimension-two statement and would require
a two-function/matrix ground-state representation which identifies the two
Tate jets with the two low modes and orders the remaining spectrum above
the exact mass `m_T`.

The required hypotheses are not supplied by the prime--Gamma jump
operator.  The Tate span is not invariant after cutoff and Gamma
completion.  Moreover the jump kernels are not totally positive or
variation diminishing: a prime translation contains a negative `2x2`
minor, and the Gamma jump density is strictly log-convex, which also gives
negative ordered minors.  Hence nonlocal jumps do not admit the Sturm
oscillation argument that would turn two moment constraints into a
third-eigenvalue bound.

The exact matrix replacement is a rank-two boundary majorant.  Its
existence is equivalent to positivity of the primitive compression, hence
to row D.  A matrix Picone factorization obtained by solving that majorant
is circular unless an independent A--B--C geometry constructs it.

No RH statement or desired sign is used.  The paper is not modified.

## 1. Scalar Picone identity and its spectral level

For a finite symmetric weighted graph, let

\[
 (Lf)_x=\sum_yw_{xy}(f_x-f_y),\qquad w_{xy}=w_{yx}\ge0.    \tag{1.1}
\]

If `h_x>0`, direct expansion gives the graph Picone identity

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \langle f,Lf\rangle
 -\sum_x{(Lh)_x\over h_x}|f_x|^2
 ={1\over2}\sum_{x,y}w_{xy}h_xh_y
 \left|{f_x\over h_x}-{f_y\over h_y}\right|^2\ge0.       \tag{1.2}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Thus a positive supersolution `Lh>=m h` proves `L>=mI` on the **whole**
space.  It controls the ground level.  Row D asks instead for

\[
 P_{\ker M}(L-mI)P_{\ker M}\ge0,                         \tag{1.3}
\]

while `L-mI` is allowed to have two low directions.  A scalar Picone
identity cannot distinguish those two directions.

For a positivity-improving heat semigroup, every positive heat-kernel
mixture has nonzero overlap with the positive ground state.  Its Barta or
Picone bound therefore controls the first level, not the constrained third
level.  Making the mixture orthogonal to both low modes requires signed
coefficients and destroys the positive supersolution hypothesis.

## 2. Necessary two-mode invariance

Let

\[
 h_-(t)=e^{-t/2},\qquad h_+(t)=e^{t/2},qquad
 E_T=\mathrm{span}\,\{h_-,h_+\}\subset L^2(I_T).      \tag{2.1}
\]

For ordinary min--max/Sturm reasoning to identify
`ker M_T=E_T^perp` with the complement of the two low modes, at minimum

\[
 L_TE_T\subset E_T                                      \tag{2.2}
\]

must hold.  In the translation-invariant interior, an individual symmetric
jump acts formally by

\[
 (2I-S_a-S_{-a})h_\pm
 =(2-2\cosh(a/2))h_\pm.                                  \tag{2.3}
\]

But this formal eigenvalue is negative and the vectors are not in the
global `L^2` domain.  Zero extension on `I_T` adds boundary layers outside
the span.  The Gamma analytic energy has its first pole at the Tate
exponents, so the completed action is a residue/boundary operation rather
than an invariant two-dimensional eigenspace.

The defect is already visible in the minimal path model of D.98.  With

\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \quad h_+=(1/2,1,2)^T,
 \quad h_-=(2,1,1/2)^T,                                  \tag{2.4}
\]

one has

\[
 Lh_+=(-1/2,-1/2,1)^T,
 \qquad\det[h_+,h_-,Lh_+]=-21/8\ne0.                     \tag{2.5}
\]

Thus exponential moment vectors do not generically span spectral modes,
even for a local positive graph.

## 3. Prime translations break total positivity

Variation-diminishing oscillation theory for a one-dimensional local
Sturm operator is based on total positivity of its Green/heat kernel.  The
raw prime jump at distance `a` has ordered discrete kernel

\[
 K_a(i,j)=1_{|i-j|=1}                                    \tag{3.1}
\]

on the ordered lattice generated by that jump.  On rows and columns
`(0,1)`,

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}K_a(0,0)&K_a(0,1)\\
                 K_a(1,0)&K_a(1,1)
 \end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad\det=-1.                                          \tag{3.2}
\]

Hence the jump kernel is not even totally positive of order two.  On the
full arithmetic translation group, a `log p` jump is nonlocal relative to
the points generated by the other primes and by the Gamma continuum; it
cannot be reinterpreted as a nearest-neighbour birth--death edge in the
common order.

The Markov heat semigroup remains positivity preserving.  Positivity
preserving is much weaker than variation diminishing and does not order
the third eigenvalue by nodal count.

## 4. Gamma kernel also fails TP2

The off-diagonal Gamma jump density is

\[
 w_\infty(r)={e^{-r/2}\over1-e^{-2r}},\qquad r>0.          \tag{4.1}
\]

Its logarithm satisfies

\[
 {d^2\over dr^2}\log w_\infty(r)
 ={4e^{-2r}\over(1-e^{-2r})^2}>0.                         \tag{4.2}
\]

Thus it is strictly log-convex.  Choose ordered rows `0<epsilon` and
ordered columns `R<R+epsilon`, with `R>epsilon`.  The corresponding minor
of the translation kernel `w_infinity(|x-y|)` is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{R,\epsilon}
 &=w_\infty(R)^2
   -w_\infty(R-\epsilon)w_\infty(R+\epsilon)\\
 &<0                                                       \tag{4.3}
 \end{aligned}
\]

by strict log-convexity.  Therefore the Gamma Cauchy--Stieltjes mixture is
not TP2 as a kernel in the ordered logarithmic coordinate.  Although each
single Ornstein--Uhlenbeck exponential kernel has special positivity, the
positive mixture in (4.1) does not preserve the determinants needed for a
Sturm oscillation theorem.

Consequently neither the discrete finite places nor the archimedean jump
kernel provides an oscillation-diminishing mechanism which would force
`h_-`, `h_+` to be the first two modes.

## 5. Exact matrix Picone replacement

Let `Q=L-mI` and let `M:H->C^2` be the two-moment map.  The desired
statement is

\[
 P_{\ker M}QP_{\ker M}\ge0.                              \tag{5.1}
\]

In finite dimension, and in the closed-form setting with the usual range
conditions, the projection/Finsler lemma gives the equivalent existence of
a Hermitian boundary matrix `R` such that

\[
 \boxed{Q+M^*RM\ge0.}                                    \tag{5.2}
\]

One direction is immediate by restriction to `ker M`.  Conversely,
decompose `H=ker M direct-sum (ker M)^perp` and use the Schur complement;
the positive primitive block admits a finite boundary majorant precisely
when the off-diagonal block satisfies the corresponding range condition.
This is the two-channel form of the boundary capacity theorem in D.71.

If (5.2) holds, its positive square root gives a matrix
ground-state/Picone factorization.  But constructing `R` by completing the
square uses positivity of the primitive block.  For the actual completed
form, the naturally supplied Tate matrix is the crossed matrix `C`, and

\[
 Q+M^*CM=QW.                                              \tag{5.3}
\]

Showing that (5.3), or another source-defined `R`, is positive is exactly
the Pick/Weil problem of D.97.

## 6. Why a two-function nodal theorem is unavailable

For a local second-order Sturm operator, a Chebyshev pair and total
positivity can identify a codimension-two moment kernel with the subspace
above the first two eigenmodes.  Here:

1. the Tate pair is Chebyshev as a pair of functions because
   `h_+/h_-=e^t` is strictly monotone;
2. it is not an invariant spectral pair by (2.5) and the Gamma pole;
3. the jump kernels fail TP2 by (3.2) and (4.3).

The first property alone does not imply spectral ordering for a nonlocal
jump operator.  Any theorem claiming that the two moments remove exactly
the two low modes would need a new arithmetic total-positivity structure
different from the order kernel of `L_T`.

## 7. Outcome

Scalar and matrix Picone approaches leave the same exact alternatives:

* a scalar positive supersolution proves the wrong spectral level;
* a two-function transform requires invariance and oscillation ordering,
  both absent from the raw operator;
* a rank-two majorant is equivalent to the primitive inequality.

The next viable route must therefore exploit an algebraic structure which
can enforce a two-negative-square index without total positivity--for
example, a Pontryagin-space index theorem showing that the completed
prime--Gamma colligation has negative index exactly two, with those two
directions exhausted by the Tate residues.  Such an index theorem must be
proved from the source colligation; assuming the index of the Weil form
would be circular.
