# D.78 — Triangular transport of the Fourier--Poisson cross block

## Status

D.77 identifies the exact discrepancy between the semilocal corner trace
and a supported leakage: the cross block `Q P_hat P`.  This note attempts to
transport that block into the support space by a triangular Halmos/graph
transform whose entries depend only on the two projections.

The transport equation has a canonical minimal solution on each fixed
window, but it cannot give the required trace.  Moving the off-diagonal
corner into the negative support block necessarily creates a new diagonal
Schur term of the opposite sign.  For Fourier windows the minimal angle
operator is moreover unbounded in the directed limit.  A full Halmos
rotation remains unitary, but rotates out of the position-support subspace.

On the primitive correspondence a test-dependent Riccati solution exists
with the required contractive sign exactly when the row-D form is already
nonpositive.  Thus neither the triangular fold nor the Riccati transform is
an independent proof.  No RH, zero divisor, or operator defined from the
spectral sign of `B_nuc` is used.  The paper is not modified.

## 1. Two-projection blocks

Let `P` be the position projection, `Q=I-P`, `U` the semilocal
Fourier--Poisson unitary and

\[
 \widehat P=U^*PU.
\]

Relative to `H=PH direct-sum QH`, write

\[
 \widehat P=
 \begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\beta^*&\delta\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1.1}
\]

The projection equations are

\[
 \alpha^2+\beta\beta^*=\alpha,
 \qquad
 \beta^*\beta+\delta^2=\delta,
 \qquad
 \alpha\beta+\beta\delta=\beta.                            \tag{1.2}
\]

Put

\[
 D=\alpha-I_{PH}=-(QUP)^*(QUP)\le0.                         \tag{1.3}
\]

The self-adjoint part of the corner operator from D.77 is

\[
 H_{\rm cor}
 ={1\over2}\bigl((\widehat PP-P)+(P\widehat P-P)\bigr)
 =\begin{pmatrix}D&\beta/2\\\beta^*/2&0\end{pmatrix}.       \tag{1.4}
\]

The trace of the skew-adjoint part vanishes under the Hermitian
polarization.  Hence (1.4) is the operator which a Hermitian supported
correction must reproduce.

## 2. The triangular supported fold

Let

\[
 T_X=P+XQ:H\longrightarrow PH,
 \qquad X:QH\longrightarrow PH.                             \tag{2.1}
\]

It is supported by construction.  Its leakage through the support block is

\[
 T_X^*DT_X
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 D&DX\\X^*D&X^*DX
 \end{pmatrix}\le0.                                        \tag{2.2}
\]

Matching the off-diagonal blocks of (1.4) requires

\[
 DX={\beta\over2}.                                          \tag{2.3}
\]

But matching the bottom block additionally requires

\[
 X^*DX=0.                                                   \tag{2.4}
\]

Since `D<=0`, (2.4) implies `(-D)^(1/2)X=0`, hence `DX=0`.
Together with (2.3) this forces

\[
 \beta=0.                                                   \tag{2.5}
\]

The Fourier--Poisson cross block has infinite rank, so (2.5) is false.

> **Theorem 2.1 (triangular transport no-go).**  If
> `Q P_hat P` is nonzero, no triangular map into the position-support
> subspace transports the Hermitian corner exactly.  The obstruction is not
> existence of a solution of the off-diagonal equation; it is the unavoidable
> diagonal cost `X*DX` generated by that solution.

The same proof covers any finite or countable family of supported
corrections: a sum of supported leakage forms is still nonpositive, and a
zero sum of their `QQ` diagonal blocks forces every transported cross block
to vanish.

## 3. Minimal solution and the positive Schur remainder

Equation (2.3) has a bounded solution only if

\[
 \mathrm{Ran}\,\beta\subseteq\mathrm{Ran}\,D.     \tag{3.1}
\]

At a fixed regularized window, its minimal solution is

\[
 X_0={1\over2}D^\dagger\beta.                               \tag{3.2}
\]

Completing the square gives, on the supported range of `D`,

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}D&\beta/2\\\beta^*/2&0\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}I&X_0\\0&I\end{pmatrix}^{\!*}
 \begin{pmatrix}D&0\\0&S\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}I&X_0\\0&I\end{pmatrix},                  \tag{3.3}
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{
 S=-{1\over4}\beta^*D^\dagger\beta\ge0.}                  \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the cross block is equivalent to a negative supported block **plus a
positive Schur channel**.  Deleting `S` changes the corner trace; realizing
it by another supported leakage is impossible because such leakages are
negative.

If (3.1) fails, there is not even a closed triangular solution.  The
Moore--Penrose range obstruction of D.74 reappears at the level of the
two-projection angle.

## 4. Exact angle growth for Fourier windows

From (1.2),

\[
 \beta\beta^*=\alpha(I-\alpha).                             \tag{4.1}
\]

Since `D=-(I-alpha)`, the minimal solution satisfies

\[
 X_0X_0^*
 ={1\over4}\alpha(I-\alpha)^\dagger                         \tag{4.2}
\]

on the generic part.  Hence an angle eigenvalue `lambda in (0,1)` of
`alpha=P P_hat P` contributes

\[
 \|X_0\|_\lambda^2={\lambda\over4(1-\lambda)}.              \tag{4.3}
\]

For time--frequency limiting on expanding intervals, the largest prolate
eigenvalues tend to `1`.  Therefore

\[
 \|X_{0,T}\|\longrightarrow\infty                           \tag{4.4}
\]

along the support exhaustion.  At each fixed window the generic solution
can be bounded, but there is no uniformly bounded directed triangular
correction.

This is also a no-go for a bounded Volterra solution.  A Volterra kernel on
the compact support interval is bounded/compact in the required graph norm,
whereas (4.3) forces arbitrarily large singular values.  More basically,
the Fourier cross kernel couples both endpoints and is not triangular for
either support order; the two primitive exponentials cancel two tails, not
the continuum of Fourier angles.

## 5. Full Halmos rotation

Two projections do possess a canonical Halmos decomposition.  On the
generic angle fiber one may write

\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \widehat P=
 \begin{pmatrix}C^2&CS\\CS&S^2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad C^2+S^2=1.                                         \tag{5.1}
\]

The rotation

\[
 R_H=\begin{pmatrix}C&-S\\S&C\end{pmatrix}                 \tag{5.2}
\]

is unitary and satisfies `R_H P R_H*=P_hat`.  It is defined solely from
`P,U` and therefore is noncircular.

However

\[
 R_H(PH)=\widehat P H,                                      \tag{5.3}
\]

not `PH`.  It rotates position-supported vectors into Fourier-supported
vectors.  Composing it with `P` returns to the contraction `C`, loses the
cross block `S`, and recreates the positive Schur remainder (3.4).  Hence the
full Halmos unitary transports the corner only by abandoning the support
condition required for a negative leakage.

The negative spectral subspace of `P_hat-P` gives another canonical graph,
but it has the same defect: it is not the position-support subspace.  Mapping
it back to `PH` uses the angle operator (4.2).

## 6. Riccati equation on the primitive correspondence

For the full phase operator

\[
 A_U=\widehat P-P
 =\begin{pmatrix}D&\beta\\\beta^*&\delta\end{pmatrix},       \tag{6.1}
\]

an invariant graph `Graph(Y)`, `Y:PH->QH`, satisfies the operator Riccati
equation

\[
 \beta^*+\delta Y-YD-Y\beta Y=0.                            \tag{6.2}
\]

The canonical maximal negative graph can be obtained from the negative
spectral projection of `A_U`.  That construction is source-defined from
`P,U`, but it does not solve the trace problem: a nonpositive vector need
not lie in this particular invariant graph, and the primitive A--B--C range
has not been shown to be invariant.

Let `R_T` denote the rigged primitive realization built from the compact
potential of D.75 and the adelic features of D.76.  Define

\[
 C_{0,T}=PR_T,
 \qquad C_{1,T}=PUR_T.                                      \tag{6.3}
\]

Then

\[
 R_T^*(\widehat P-P)R_T
 =C_{1,T}^*C_{1,T}-C_{0,T}^*C_{0,T}.                         \tag{6.4}
\]

The Douglas lemma gives the exact equivalence

\[
 \boxed{
 R_T^*(\widehat P-P)R_T\le0
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \exists\text{ a contraction }K_T\text{ with }
 C_{1,T}=K_TC_{0,T}.}                                       \tag{6.5}
\]

The usual kernel inclusion is part of this factorization statement.  After
an exact trace-exact comparison of the corrected lift, the left side is the
primitive row-D inequality.

> **Theorem 6.1 (contractive graph gate).**  A contractive graph for the
> pair `(PR_T,PUR_T)` exists if and only if its phase pullback is
> nonpositive.  Solving the ambient Riccati equation (6.2) is unconditional
> but does not imply this source factorization.  Conversely, a Douglas
> contraction need not solve (6.2) unless an additional invariance theorem
> is proved.

A test-dependent correction could be chosen for an individual vector whose
quadratic value is already nonpositive.  Selecting one continuously and
functorially for the whole primitive source is exactly (6.5), so it cannot
be used as an independent definition of the missing sign.

## 7. Why support order does not remove the Schur channel

The compact potential `W_TF` from D.75 has a genuine interval support order.
One may try a causal correction

\[
 (Xg)(t)=\int_{-T}^tK(t,s)g(s)\,ds.                          \tag{7.1}
\]

But exact cross transport still imposes (2.3).  Equations (4.1)--(4.3) are
unitary invariants of the pair `(P,P_hat)` and are unaffected by the kernel
presentation of `X`.  Consequently any causal solution has the same
positive Schur remainder and the same cofinal norm blow-up.

The primitive conditions do make `W_TF` vanish outside the convex hull of
the source support.  They do not change the angle spectrum of
`P P_hat P`.  Thus Volterra order solves the **vector support** problem but
not the **Fourier cross-block** problem.

## 8. Verdict

The cross block can be transported off-diagonally on each regularized
window, but not as a pure supported leakage.  Its minimal triangular
transport creates the positive Schur channel (3.4), and its norm diverges in
the support exhaustion.  The full Halmos rotation is bounded and canonical
but abandons position support.

The remaining graph/Riccati landing condition is exactly the primitive
row-D inequality.  Therefore D.78 rules out an uncorrected triangular,
Halmos or Volterra repair of D.77.  A successful construction must add new
geometric data whose own positive channel cancels (3.4) before compression;
it cannot be obtained from the two projections and primitivity alone.  Row D
is not declared closed.
