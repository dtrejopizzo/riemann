# E101.096 - Holomorphic one-level inversion and conjugation topology no-go

## 1. Decision

The finite Guinand--Weil dictionary can be polarized into an exact matrix
one-level identity with linear zero multiplicity.  This is useful
infrastructure, but it is not an uncontracted divisor current and it does not
transport the parity-Gram contraction.

There are two exact obstructions.

At every finite test level, an off-real zero quartet has the same complete
holomorphic moment matrix as a finite real signed measure supported on the
real axis.  Hence no finite one-level matrix, even before scalar contraction,
can distinguish those two objects without an additional positivity or
variation condition.

Cofinally, there is an explicit net of even real signed measures supported on
the real axis which converges to an off-real quartet against every entire
test.  Its total variation diverges.  Every parity-Gram functional vanishes
on the net but is positive on the limiting quartet.  Therefore parity-Gram
contraction is discontinuous in the topology generated by holomorphic
one-level tests.

The missing extension is

```text
holomorphic one-level moments
 -> a Radon or distributional divisor current sensitive to conjugation
 -> parity-Gram contraction.                                (1.1)
```

The extension in `(1.1)` is exactly `TRUE-DIVISOR-IDENT`.  If finite real
divisors converge to the `Xi` divisor in a support-sensitive topology, the
limit is supported on the real axis and RH is already proved.  Thus the word
`identify` in `(1.1)` contains the full force-bearing step.

## 2. Abstract one-level dictionary

Let the centered divisor be

```text
mu_Xi=sum_(rho in Z_Xi)m_rho delta_rho.                (2.1)
```

At a fixed finite level, polarization of a scalar one-level test produces a
matrix-valued entire function

```text
G_(L,N)(z)=(G_(ab)(z))_(1<=a,b<=d).                    (2.2)
```

The forward dictionary has the form

```text
Q_(L,N)
 =integral_C G_(L,N)(z)d mu_Xi(z)
 =sum_rho m_rho G_(L,N)(rho),                          (2.3)
```

entry by entry.  In the relevant symmetrized parity sector, the entries are
even and real-type:

```text
G_(ab)(-z)=G_(ab)(z),
G_(ab)(conj(z))=conj(G_(ab)(z)).                       (2.4)
```

Equation `(2.3)` has three important properties:

```text
one spectral level is used;
multiplicity m_rho is linear;
the zero labels have already been summed.              (2.5)
```

The exact quotient on the source side concerns efficiency and injectivity of
the finite source parametrization.  It does not assert injectivity of the map

```text
mu -> integral G_(L,N)dmu                              (2.6)
```

on spectral divisors or signed measures.

## 3. Finite holomorphic indistinguishability

Let `V` be a finite-dimensional real vector space of entire functions such
that

```text
H(-z)=H(z),
H(conj(z))=conj(H(z))                                  (3.1)
```

for every `H in V`.  In particular, `H(t)` is real for `t in R`.

Fix

```text
p=x+iy,
x>0,
y!=0,                                                  (3.2)
```

and define the quartet functional

```text
ell_p(H)
 :=H(p)+H(conj(p))+H(-p)+H(-conj(p)).                  (3.3)
```

By `(3.1)`, `ell_p` is a real linear functional on `V`.

### Theorem 3.1 - Exact real signed representation

There exist real points `t_1,...,t_r` and real coefficients
`a_1,...,a_r`, with

```text
r<=dim_R(V),                                           (3.4)
```

such that

```text
ell_p(H)=sum_(j=1)^r a_j H(t_j)                        (3.5)
```

for every `H in V`.  Equivalently, the finite even real signed measure

```text
nu_V
 :=sum_(j=1)^r(a_j/2)[delta_(t_j)+delta_(-t_j)]        (3.6)
```

has exactly the same `V`-moments as the off-real quartet measure

```text
mu_p
 :=delta_p+delta_(conj(p))+delta_(-p)+delta_(-conj(p)). (3.7)
```

### Proof

For `t in R`, let

```text
ev_t(H)=H(t).                                          (3.8)
```

We claim that the real span of `{ev_t:t in R}` is all of `V^*`.  Otherwise
its annihilator in `V` would contain a nonzero `H`.  That function would
satisfy

```text
H(t)=0  for every t in R.                              (3.9)
```

The identity theorem would give `H=0`, a contradiction.  Since `V^*` is
finite-dimensional, a basis can be selected from at most `dim_R(V)` real
evaluations.  Expressing `ell_p` in that basis proves `(3.5)`.

Evenness of every `H in V` gives

```text
integral H dnu_V=sum_j a_j H(t_j)=ell_p(H),             (3.10)
```

which proves `(3.6)`--`(3.7)`. `QED`

### Corollary 3.2 - Matrix indistinguishability

Let `V_(L,N)` be the real span of the even real-type entries in `(2.2)`,
together with any finite collection of previously fixed holomorphic markers.
Then

```text
integral G_(L,N)dmu_p
 =integral G_(L,N)dnu_(V_(L,N))                        (3.11)
```

entry by entry.

Thus the complete finite polarized matrix is not an injective encoding of
support in the complex plane.

The coefficients in `(3.6)` need not be positive.  The theorem does not say
that every off-real quartet has a positive real representing measure.  It
says exactly that holomorphic finite-level data alone cannot supply the
missing positivity, tightness or variation control.  Any such additional
condition is a new hypothesis and must be audited for RH-strength.

If nonsymmetric markers are included, the same dual-space proof gives a real
signed representation on the real axis after closing the real test space
under reflection and conjugation.  Evenness of the representing measure must
not be asserted unless the test sector itself is even.

## 4. Explicit cofinal holomorphic approximation of an off-real quartet

The finite theorem has a cofinal counterpart which displays the missing
topology.

For `h>0` and an integer `k>=0`, define the forward-difference measure

```text
D_(h,x)^(k)
 :=h^(-k)sum_(j=0)^k(-1)^(k-j)binom(k,j)delta_(x+jh).
                                                               (4.1)
```

Let `R` denote reflection `t->-t` and `R_#` its push-forward on measures.
For `M>=0`, put

```text
nu_(M,h)
 :=2sum_(r=0)^M [(-1)^r y^(2r)/(2r)!]
   [D_(h,x)^(2r)+R_#D_(h,x)^(2r)].                    (4.2)
```

Every `nu_(M,h)` is a finite even real signed measure supported on the real
axis.

### Theorem 4.1 - Entire-test convergence

For every entire function `H`,

```text
lim_(M->infinity) lim_(h->0)
 integral_R H(t)dnu_(M,h)(t)

 =H(p)+H(conj(p))+H(-p)+H(-conj(p)).                  (4.3)
```

Moreover, there is a net of measures of the form `(4.2)` which converges to
`mu_p` against every entire test simultaneously.

### Proof

The standard finite-difference identity gives, for fixed `k`,

```text
lim_(h->0) integral H dD_(h,x)^(k)=H^(k)(x).           (4.4)
```

Hence for fixed `M`, the inner limit of `(4.3)` is

```text
2sum_(r=0)^M [(-1)^r y^(2r)/(2r)!]
 [H^(2r)(x)+H^(2r)(-x)].                               (4.5)
```

Taylor expansion of an entire function gives

```text
H(x+iy)+H(x-iy)
 =2sum_(r>=0)(-1)^r y^(2r)H^(2r)(x)/(2r)!,             (4.6)

H(-x+iy)+H(-x-iy)
 =2sum_(r>=0)(-1)^r y^(2r)H^(2r)(-x)/(2r)!.            (4.7)
```

Taking `M->infinity` proves `(4.3)`.

For the simultaneous statement, direct the pairs `(F,epsilon)` by inclusion
of finite subsets `F` of the space of entire functions and by decreasing
`epsilon>0`.  First choose `M` so that the Taylor tails in `(4.6)`--`(4.7)`
are below `epsilon/2` for every `H in F`.  Then choose `h` so that all finite
differences through order `2M` are within the remaining error for every
`H in F`.  The resulting net converges pointwise on the whole space of entire
tests. `QED`

### Variation explosion

For fixed `M>=1`, the point `x+2Mh` occurs in the highest-order forward
difference and in no lower-order term.  Its coefficient in `(4.2)` has
absolute value

```text
2 |y|^(2M)/[(2M)! h^(2M)].                             (4.8)
```

The reflected endpoint has the same absolute coefficient.  Therefore

```text
||nu_(M,h)||_TV
 >=4 |y|^(2M)/[(2M)! h^(2M)] ->infinity                (4.9)
```

as `h->0`.  The analytic continuation in `(4.3)` is purchased by unbounded
signed variation.

## 5. Parity-Gram contraction is discontinuous

Let `Phi:C->R` be continuous, even and invariant under conjugation.  Assume

```text
Phi(t)=0  for every t in R,
Phi(p)>0.                                              (5.1)
```

A separating parity-Gram coefficient `g_Q` from E101.093 has precisely these
properties at a detected quartet.

Since every `nu_(M,h)` is supported on the real axis,

```text
integral Phi dnu_(M,h)=0                               (5.2)
```

for all `M,h`.  On the other hand,

```text
integral Phi dmu_p=4Phi(p)>0.                          (5.3)
```

Theorem 4.1 and `(5.2)`--`(5.3)` prove:

```text
the map mu -> integral Phi dmu
is not continuous in the topology generated by all entire tests.    (5.4)
```

This is stronger than failure at one fixed finite dictionary.  Even the full
holomorphic moment topology does not permit parity contraction to pass
through an unbounded signed approximating family.

## 6. The topology required by a true divisor bridge

The obstruction disappears only in a topology which detects transverse
support.

### Proposition 6.1 - Stability of real support

Let `(T_j)` be distributions on `C` with

```text
supp(T_j) subset R.                                    (6.1)
```

If `T_j->T` in `D'(C)`, then

```text
supp(T) subset R.                                      (6.2)
```

The same conclusion holds for vague convergence of Radon measures.

### Proof

Let `varphi` be a smooth compactly supported test whose support is disjoint
from `R`.  Then

```text
T_j(varphi)=0                                          (6.3)
```

for every `j`.  Passing to the distributional limit gives `T(varphi)=0`.
Such tests characterize the complement of the support, proving `(6.2)`.
The Radon statement is the same argument with compactly supported continuous
tests. `QED`

### Corollary 6.2 - Support-sensitive divisor convergence has RH force

Suppose finite real spectral divisors converge in `D'(C)` or vaguely as Radon
measures to `mu_Xi`.  Proposition 6.1 gives

```text
supp(mu_Xi) subset R.                                  (6.4)
```

Hence every zero of `Xi` is real and `Omega7` follows.

Thus distributional or Radon convergence is an admissible statement of
`TRUE-DIVISOR-IDENT`, but it is already a theorem of RH strength.  The
topology is not a technical afterthought.

## 7. Multiplicity and all-pairs audit

Let

```text
mu=sum_rho m_rho delta_rho.                            (7.1)
```

The available operations have different multiplicity laws.

### 7.1 One trace

```text
integral f(z)g(z)dmu(z)
 =sum_rho m_rho f(rho)g(rho).                          (7.2)
```

This is same-zero and linear in `m_rho`, but the product is holomorphic.  It
does not produce `g(conj(rho))` or a Hermitian parity Gram.

### 7.2 Product of two traces

```text
[integral f dmu][integral g dmu]
 =sum_(rho,sigma)m_rho m_sigma f(rho)g(sigma).          (7.3)
```

This contains all pairs and quadratic multiplicity.

### 7.3 Diagonal or conjugate-graph restriction after tensoring

The product measure `mu tensor mu` assigns mass `m_rho^2` to the diagonal
atom `(rho,rho)`.  A formal restriction to a conjugate graph likewise gives
quadratic mass unless the divisor is first recovered and a new graph measure
is constructed with prescribed linear weights.

### 7.4 Recover first, then push forward

If the divisor is already known, the measure

```text
(id,conj)_#mu                                          (7.4)
```

on the conjugate graph has mass `m_rho`.  But constructing `(7.4)` requires
the complete divisor and the antiholomorphic support map.  It is the desired
inversion, not a consequence of the one-level aggregate.

Consequently:

```text
linear multiplicity + same-zero holomorphic product    available;
linear multiplicity + conjugate same-zero product      TRUE-DIVISOR-IDENT;
unprojected tensor product                              all-pairs and m^2.
                                                               (7.5)
```

## 8. Poincare--Lelong does not provide a finite arithmetic bypass

For the centered entire function,

```text
mu_Xi=(1/(2 pi))Delta log|Xi(z)|                       (8.1)
```

as a distribution on `C`.  This is an exact divisor current with linear
multiplicity and no all-pairs contamination.

It begins with the target function `Xi`, however, rather than identifying a
limit of finite Gamma--Euler divisors.  Contracting `(8.1)` with every
separating parity Gram gives

```text
integral g_Q dmu_Xi=0 for every Q
 <=> supp(mu_Xi) subset R
 <=> RH
 <=> Omega7.                                           (8.2)
```

Thus Poincare--Lelong supplies the canonical target current but not the
missing source-to-target bridge.

## 9. Projective-limit interpretation

As `(L,N)` varies, the finite matrices determine compatible restrictions of a
linear functional to finite-dimensional holomorphic test spaces.  At most,
this produces an element of a projective limit

```text
inverse_limit_(L,N) V_(L,N)^*.                         (9.1)
```

The object `(9.1)` is the restriction of a Weil distribution to holomorphic
tests.  To obtain a divisor measure on `C`, one must extend it to a
support-sensitive class such as

```text
C_c^infinity(C),
or a controlled mixed algebra containing z^a conj(z)^b.              (9.2)
```

Theorem 4.1 shows that this extension is not continuous or unique without a
variation, positivity or tightness condition.  Proposition 6.1 shows that a
successful extension preserving the real supports of the finite divisors
already proves RH.

This is the exact meaning of the statement

```text
take the limit of the uncontracted moments and then contract.         (9.3)
```

The force-bearing content is hidden in the phrase `take the limit` unless
the topology in `(9.2)` and its source estimate are proved explicitly.

## 10. Relation to previous no-go results

E101.081--E101.083 established that a one-level aggregate does not produce a
conjugate graph, that products create all-pairs terms, and that tensor powers
alter multiplicity.  The present result strengthens those conclusions in two
directions:

```text
finite:
  an off-real quartet and a real signed measure can have exactly the same
  complete polarized matrix at every fixed level;

cofinal:
  real-supported signed measures can converge to the quartet against every
  entire test while parity-Gram contraction remains discontinuous.   (10.1)
```

This does not rule out a source theorem with positive tight measures and
uniform local variation.  It identifies precisely what such a theorem must
add, and Proposition 6.1 shows why that addition is force-bearing.

## 11. Literature and novelty boundary

The finite one-level dictionary and fixed-parameter archimedean tail belong
to

```text
A. Groskin,
A finite Guinand--Weil dictionary and archimedean tail order for the
truncated Weil quadratic form,
arXiv:2607.02828.                                      (11.1)
```

That result is a forward dictionary.  It does not assert recovery of the
individual zero divisor or continuity of an antiholomorphic contraction.

The following ingredients are classical and are not novelty claims:

```text
the identity theorem;
finite-dimensional dual spanning by point evaluations;
Taylor expansion and finite-difference approximation of derivatives;
Poincare--Lelong;
stability of support under distributional convergence.               (11.2)
```

The program-specific contribution is their combination with the polarized
finite dictionary and the parity-Gram target:

```text
exact finite signed real representation of an off-real quartet;
explicit cofinal variation-exploding real approximation;
discontinuity of the parity-Gram contraction;
localization of the required extension at TRUE-DIVISOR-IDENT.         (11.3)
```

No claim is made that the underlying general functional-analytic principles
are new.

## 12. Stop rules

```text
1. Do not call a one-level moment matrix an uncontracted divisor current.

2. Do not infer spectral support from finitely many holomorphic moments.

3. Do not multiply two one-level traces and discard the all-pairs terms.

4. Do not restrict a tensor square to the conjugate graph while retaining
   linear multiplicity without first constructing the divisor measure.

5. Do not commute parity-Gram contraction with a holomorphic-moment limit.

6. Any positive-measure escape must prove tightness and local variation in a
   topology which rejects the net (4.2).

7. Any mixed-moment escape must derive the antiholomorphic coordinate from
   Gamma--Euler source data and fail at a named identity for the plant.

8. Poincare--Lelong may define the target current, but it may not be used as
   the finite source identification.                                  (12.1)
```

## 13. Revised work order

The Groskin route is retained only as forward infrastructure:

```text

 -> exact finite holomorphic moment matrix
 -> source consistency checks.                          (13.1)
```

It is frozen as an inversion bypass.  A live successor must prove one of:

```text
RADON-DIVISOR-IDENT:
  locally bounded variation and vague convergence of the finite divisor
  measures to mu_Xi;

MIXED-MOMENT-IDENT:
  source estimates for a determining class containing conjugate variables,
  with linear multiplicity and no all-pairs leakage;

GROUND-MODEL-IDENT:
  the weighted transform estimate of E101.095, which bypasses moment
  inversion by proving compact-open characteristic convergence directly.
                                                               (13.2)
```

Each item in `(13.2)` is a realization of the same discriminator, not a
separate easy module.
