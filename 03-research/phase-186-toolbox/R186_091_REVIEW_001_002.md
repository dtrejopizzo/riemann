## R186_001

### MAJOR-1 (§4): the open distribution lemma is false as written, and the correct version closes it

**The error.** Let F ∈ 𝒫_T, so that F̂(i/2) = F̂(−i/2) = 0 (these are the constraints A and B). Set
Φ(z) = F̂(z)·F̂^#(z), with F̂^#(z) = conj F̂(z̄). At z = i/2 both factors vanish:
* F̂(i/2) = 0;
* F̂^#(i/2) = conj F̂(−i/2) = 0.

So Φ has a zero of order ≥ 2 at i/2, and the same at −i/2. The conditions Φ(±i/2) = 0 and
Φ'(±i/2) = 0 are linear, so they pass to convex sums and to locally uniform limits.
Hence

 𝒦_T ⊂ {Φ ≥ 0 on ℝ, entire of type ≤ 2T, in L¹, with zeros of order ≥ 2 at ±i/2}.

The inclusion proposed in the document ("Φ(±i/2) = 0") is too large. A Φ with simple zeros
at ±i/2 is **not** in 𝒦_T. The "distribution lemma" is false for simple zeros.

**The correct version.** Let Φ ≥ 0, of type 2T, in L¹, with double zeros at ±i/2. By Kreĭn–Akhiezer
we have Φ = EE^#, with E of type T in L²(ℝ). The non-real zeros distribute into conjugate pairs
(i/2, −i/2), and here there are at least two such pairs. One can place i/2 in E from one pair and −i/2 from the
other. After multiplying by e^{icz}, Paley–Wiener gives E = F̂ with F ∈ L²(I_T) and A(F) = B(F) = 0. This is
exactly the "immediate" case that the document itself points out.

**Consequences.**
* The set {|F̂|² : F ∈ 𝒫_T} is already convex: a sum of two of them is again of the same type.
 There is no need to take the convex hull.
* The map Φ ↦ (a, c) is linear, because a = (1/2π)∫mΦ and c_n = (1/2π)∫cos(s log n)Φ. Hence
 𝒞_T = conv 𝒞_T: **the joint numerical range is convex**.
* So the remark following Prop. 2 ("for ≥ 3 forms 𝒞_T is not convex in general",
 invisible to E4) is false in this case (MINOR), and Prop. 2 (density matrices) is redundant.
* Two detail checks remain:
 - the reduction to real F (even Φ) goes through prop:finite(ii), because the form is the same for complex F;
 - the form domain requires ∫mΦ < ∞, and this condition is preserved.

**Label.** Row 7: "OPEN" becomes **PROVED (corrected)**:

 𝒦_T = {Φ ≥ 0, entire of type ≤ 2T, in L¹, with double zeros at ±i/2, ∫(1+|m|)Φ < ∞}.

This is the Fejér–Riesz–Kreĭn theorem for PW_T with two prescribed zeros. It is CITED + one line.

### MAJOR-2 (§4): the L¹ closure is incompatible with an unbounded σ_v

The symbol σ_v = m − Σ v_n cos(s log n) satisfies m(s) ~ log|s| → +∞, so Φ ↦ ∫σ_vΦ is not continuous
in L¹. Fatou only gives **lower** semicontinuity of ∫(σ_v + C)Φ. That does not preserve "≥ 0" when passing to
a limit:
* Let Φ have ∫σΦ < 0, and let Φ_k = Φ + ε_k·(a mass centered at s_k → ∞), with ε_k m(s_k) → c.
* Then ∫σΦ_k ≥ 0, while Φ_k → Φ in L¹.

Hence "⟹" is false with conv̄^{L¹}, even though it is correct with conv without closure. Fix: close in
L¹((1+|m|)ds), or do not close. Thanks to MAJOR-1, the family {|F̂|²} itself suffices.

### Novelty of the Paley–Wiener–Tate cone reformulation

**It is not substantially new.** The identity 𝔞 = (1/2π)∫|F̂|²σ_v is in R185_006 §1 and is the
content of prop:finite and eq:Aform. "v ∈ 𝒱_T ⟺ ∫σ_vΦ ≥ 0 on 𝒦_T" is that identity, read
linearly in Φ. The only genuine contribution, which the document does not see, is that of MAJOR-1:
* the cone is {|F̂|² : F ∈ 𝒫_T}, already convex, with **double** zeros at ±i/2;
* hence 𝒞_T is convex.

Relative to Theorem Z (certificate = explicit formula on flat-top tests), the cone is the
dual formulation of the same data. It adds no new degree of freedom.

## R186_002

### Theorem 1.3: direction of the inequalities (verified)

* **First inequality.** It is [CC] Thm 1, eq. (4), literally: g ∈ C_c^∞ supported in
 [2^{−1/2}, 2^{1/2}], ĝ(i/2) = ĝ(0) = 0, and S the Sonin projection on L²(ℝ)^{ev}. The direction
 is W_∞ ≥ Tr(ϑSϑ*), which is correct.
* **Second inequality.**
 - [CM] Cor 2.2: every eigenfunction of a negative eigenvalue is in the Sonin space.
 - [CM] Thm 1.6(iv): the spectrum is discrete.
 - Hence Ran 1_{(−∞,0)}(W_sa) = the closed sum of the negative eigenspaces ⊂ Son.
 - For two orthogonal projections, an inclusion of ranges gives P ≤ Q. Thus 1_{<0} ≤ Π_Son, and
 conjugating, ϑ1_{<0}ϑ* ≤ ϑΠϑ*, of finite trace. The direction is correct.
 - Parity: [CM] works on L²(ℝ) and [CC] on L²(ℝ)^{ev}. W_sa commutes with parity (it commutes
 with F, and F² is parity), so the even restriction of 1_{<0} is contained in Son^{ev}.
 The document should say so.
 - Normalization: λ = 1 and F_{e_ℝ} are consistent, because (2πλ)²x² corresponds to e^{2πix}.
* **MINOR.** "Π_Son − 1_{<0} = Π_E with E finite-dimensional by [CM]" is false as a citation. [CM]
 literally says "we do not claim … there could be only finitely many exceptions." That E is
 finite-dimensional is not proved: it should be labeled CONJ./numerical.

**Novelty.** **It is not new.** It is a one-line corollary: [CC] Thm 1 weakened through
1_{<0} ≤ Π_Son, and it is strictly weaker than [CC] Thm 1. The reading of §1.3 and the "first true
case" of Conjecture P_S are relabelings of [CC]. Label: "PROVED (immediate corollary of
CITED)", not an original theorem.

### Lemma 2.1 (F_S = (−1)^{N_S})

* In [CCM], N is the grading of the filtration span{p(𝔰)ξ : deg p ≤ n}. For S = {∞},
 span{𝔰^jξ : j ≤ n} = span{h_0, h_2, …, h_{2n}}, and F h_{2n} = (−1)^n h_{2n}. This is consistent with
 W_λ = −𝔰² + 2πλ²(4N+1) − ¼.
* The pieces of the lemma are correct:
 - the sign F(H+½)F^{−1} = −(H+½), because F x∂_x F^{−1} = −∂_x x;
 - (a) and (b).
* **MINOR.** The proof of (c) is invalid as written. "F_S acts by (−1)^n on 𝔰^nξ and
 leaves E_{n−1} invariant" is not enough, because on E_{n−1} it acts with mixed signs. The
 correct proof: dm_S is even, so the orthogonal polynomial p_n has parity (−1)^n, and then
 F_S p_n(𝔰)ξ_S = p_n(−𝔰)ξ_S = (−1)^n p_n(𝔰)ξ_S.
* For F_S = (−1)^{N_S} to hold on all of L²(X_S)^{K_S} one additionally needs ξ_S to be cyclic, which
 gives the determination of §2.6(i). The document should say so.

### §2.2–2.3 (verified)

* η*θ = 1 gives θ^{−1} = η* and θ* = η^{−1}, so (θWθ^{−1})* = ηWη^{−1}.

* "No transport introduces the primes" is PROVED for unitary transports or ones by similarity

### §2.4 (recomputed)

* dm_{∞,2} ∝ |Γ(¼ + is/2)|² |1 − 2^{−½−is}|^{−2} ds.
* The Poisson kernel with q = 2^{−1/2} gives Σ_k q^{|k|} e^{iks log 2}/(1−q²).
* Barnes, with t = s/2: ∫|Γ(¼ + is/2)|² e^{ius} ds = 4πΓ(½)(2cosh u)^{−1/2}.
* The k term is a translation u ↦ u + k log 2, and (2cosh(u + k log 2))^{−1/2} = (2^k e^u + 2^{−k}e^{−u})^{−1/2}.
* The weight of the k term is 2^{−|k|/2}·2^{−|k|/2} = 2^{−|k|}.

It matches the document. PROVED.

### §2.5 (verified, with its scope)

* The factor of the quotient is a meromorphic, non-constant, periodic function in s, of period
 2π/log 2. It has infinitely many poles, and multiplying it by the Γ part, which is rational, does not cancel them.
 Hence it is not rational.
* **MINOR.** The obstruction holds only for Pearson on the lattice s ↦ s+2i. With the lattice
 2πi/log 2 the Γ part fails, as stated. "There is no finite-order Laguerre–Freud" is not proved
 in general, and the label must say so.
