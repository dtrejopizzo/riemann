## 1. Lemma 1 — PNT-window identity

**Derivation reproduced step by step.** (i) eq:Aform: 𝔞 = C₀Φ(0) + Lev_K(Φ) − Σv_nΦ(log n). (ii) lem:K: K = 2e^{−u/2} + K₁,
K₁ = 2Σ_{k≥1}e^{−(2k+½)u} ≥ 0. (iii) Lev_{2e^{−u/2}}(Φ) = Φ(0)·∫₀^∞2e^{−u/2}du − 2∫₀^{2T}Φe^{−u/2} = **4Φ(0)** − 2∫Φe^{−u/2}:
**the "4" is the Φ(0) coefficient of the k = 0 term of the Lévy energy, not "the price of the pole"** (Obs. 2 of the memo is
a mistaken reading: the pole does not appear in 𝔞_T; the Tate relation is only used to flip the sign of ∫Φe^{−u/2}).
(iv) Only ∫_ℝΦcosh(u/2) = 0 (= Re(AB̄), thm:A) is used; the u·sinh relation does not enter. (v) x = e^u: 2∫₀^{2T}Φe^{u/2}du =
2∫₁^{e^{2T}}x^{−1/2}Φ(log x)dx and Σv_nΦ(log n) = 2∫x^{−1/2}Φ(log x)dψ(x); boundary x = e^{2T}: Φ(2T) = 0 (support of the
autocorrelation), n = 1 does not jump. Constants correct.

## 4. Lemma 2 (Bochner of e^{−s_T}, Feynman–Kac)

Correct and **PROVED**, but standard: m − C₀ = ∫(1−cos τu)K du is a Lévy–Khintchine exponent (thm:S gives it
even completely Bernstein; ∫min(1,u²)K < ∞ because K ~ 1/u at 0), hence conditionally negative definite and e^{−t(m−C₀)}
is positive definite ∀t (Schoenberg); e^{−C₀} > 0 is a constant. The comb Σv_n cos(τ log n) is of positive type
(positive combination of cosines) and e^{v cos θ} = I₀(v) + 2Σ_{k≥1}I_k(v)cos kθ (Jacobi–Anger; the memo omits the factor 2:
MINOR) with coefficients > 0; a finite product of PD functions is PD (Schur). h_T = e^{−C₀}p₁ ∗ (positive atomic measure on
±log of the N_T-smooth numbers) ≥ 0; p₁ exists as an L² density (e^{−ψ_Lévy} ≍ 1/|τ| ∈ L², not L¹: MINOR, "smooth comb" is an
abuse of language). The formula λ_min(𝔞_T|L²(I_T)) = C₀ − 𝒜_T + λ₁^{Dir}(ν_T; I_T) is an identity (−v_nΦ(log n) = v_n(Φ(0) −
Φ(log n)) − v_nΦ(0)) and the reading as a Dirichlet form of the killed process is already in Paper 47 (proof of cor:mono);
the identification λ₁^{Dir} = survival exponent is standard for symmetric semigroups (CITED). **PROVED,
not heuristic; new content: none except absorbing the atoms into the Lévy measure.** Jensen NO-GO: ARGUED, acceptable.
