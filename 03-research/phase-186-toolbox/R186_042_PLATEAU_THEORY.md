## 0. Verdict

1. **The bundle's "plateau" is not a time-band plateau (NUMERICAL, robust in M).** The time-band operator of the negative set, S_U = R_T 1_U(D) R_T, **has no eigenvalue near 1** in any window computed: λ₀(S_U) = 0.78, 0.86, 0.84, 0.88, 0.80, 0.82, 0.76 at T = 0.62, log 2, 0.75, ½log5, 0.85, 0.9, 1.0 (resolved with M up to 513), against 1 − λ₀ = 10⁻⁵ … 10⁻¹⁶ for a single interval of the same measure. Reason (§3.2): each negative component has Shannon number N_i = T|J_i|/π ∈ [0.1, 0.95] (mean 0.33–0.44): the negative set of σ_T is a **sea of sub-Shannon intervals** at scale π/T, not a band. Hence 1 − β_max = 1.7·10⁻¹¹ is not a plateau number of any time-band operator: it is a cancellation between two O(0.2) quantities (R186_040 §2.2) in a regime where every concentration asymptotics (Slepian, Fuchs, Widom, Landau–Widom, Bonami–Jaming–Karoui, Karnik–Romberg–Davenport) lies outside its hypotheses. **The box "asymptotics theory for the plateau of a bundle of two time-band operators" is a poorly posed goal: that theory is not what is missing, something else is (§5).**
2. **Clean formulation (§2, PROVED).** (i) The β_j of the bundle are the values of β at which the inertia of a **single** truncated Wiener–Hopf operator changes, W_T(σ₊ − σ₋/β)|𝒫_T (Thm 2.1); 1 − β_max = ε*/(1+ε*) with ε* = sup{ε : 𝔞_T ≥ ε·Neg_T on 𝒫_T} (dilation margin of the symbol). (ii) In the eigenbasis (φ_k, ν_k) of Neg_T, the β_j are the eigenvalues of the bundle (diag ν, P) with P_{kl} = (1/2π)∫_{Ω₊}σ₊ φ̂_k φ̂_l — the overlap matrix of Widom's weighted prolates (Thm 2.2; verified to relative 10⁻⁴ at 1 − β_max = 1.7·10⁻¹¹). (iii) For two disjoint bands with constant weights and a gap, β_j = (s₋/s₊)·tan²φ_j **[corrected, C-W2: cot²φ_j, φ_j the angle to ran Q₋; note after Thm 2.3]** with φ_j the principal angles between V = Q_{Ω₋∪Ω₊}L²(I_T) and PW(Ω₋) (Thm 2.3, Halmos); without a gap, β_j = (s₋/s₊)λ_j(S_U)/(1 − λ_j(S_U)) exactly (Cor. 2.4; verified). (iv) **β_max is NOT a function of the angles** (Prop. 2.5): with the same U and the same λ_j(S_U), the constant-weight/real models give β_max = 0.79, 0.53, 3.21 and 1 − 1.7·10⁻¹¹ (½log5). (v) Lemma 2.6 (CITED Nazarov 1993 / Jaming 2007): 1 − λ₀(S_V) ≥ C⁻¹e^{−4CT|V⁺|} for every V of finite measure — a non-asymptotic plateau bound valid for arbitrary unions, which upgrades the ARGUED point (iv) of Lemma 2.1 of R186_039 to CITED (with J ≤ N_Sh(1 + O(log N_Sh)) instead of 2N_Sh).
3. **First lemma (§3, PROVED):** in the model with a single negative interval and constant σ₊, 1 − β_max = [s₊ − (s₊+s₋)λ₀(c)]/[s₊(1 − λ₀(c))] exactly, and by Fuchs/Slepian 1 − β_max ∼ −(s₋/s₊)(4√π)⁻¹c^{−1/2}e^{2c} → −∞: **the model gives the wrong sign** for all large c; it never reproduces either the staircase (in the model β_j > 1 for j < N_Sh − O(log c)) or the 2π²N/ln N form. The refined models with the real negative set (§3.3, seven variants) do not either: with constant σ₊ the bundle is trivially positive (1 − β ≈ 0.5), with constant σ₋ it is negative (3–6 directions), with per-component levels 1 − β_max ∈ [−0.11, +0.04]. **The sign lies in the remainder in every model; it is fixed only by the full profiles σ₊ and σ₋ together (§5).**
4. **Question (Gauss quadrature, §4): NO-GO with argument and counterexample.** The Gauss remainder has a sign tied to f^{(2N)} only for **positive** measures; σ_T dτ/2π is signed, and moreover (|F̂|²)^{(2N)} oscillates in τ. In the band-limited framework (Kreĭn/de Branges) the quadrature is exact with no remainder term; the "remainder" λ_A‖F‖² is the object itself. PROVED counterexample: R186_041's Lemma 1 does not use the sign of λ; for the symbols σ′ of prop:negtrace(v) (λ_A(σ′) < 0) the nodal measure exists with w_x ≥ 0 and real nodes, and the "Gauss error" is negative. The e^{−cN log N} law of Gauss quadrature for entire functions is the regime N ≫ type; here the node density equals the type at T* (critical regime), which is Zhu's regime, not Gauss's. **The exact point where the sign enters the quadrature reading is: λ_A = Σ_{γ>T*}|ĥ*(γ)|²·(1+o(1)) (R186_040) — positivity of the terms requires γ real (CONDITIONAL, zero side); the nodes of ĥ* above T* are not zeros.**

**Contact with the sign: none.** Everything computed has β_max < 1 in the real object; the models with β_max > 1 are models, not ζ. No claim bounds 1 − β_max from below.

**CITED verdict.** (a) The only result with relative error o(1) in 1 − λ_n is Fuchs/Slepian/Widom 1964: **one** interval (with a regular weight in Widom), **fixed index**. (b) For unions of intervals there is no plateau asymptotics (R183_031, R186_039 §2.3(b) already noted this); only Nazarov's bound exists (Lemma 2.6), with a universal constant in the exponent. (d) What this memo shows (§3.2) is that **(a)–(c) are irrelevant to 1 − β_max**: the real object is not at the plateau.

## 2. Clean formulation of the bundle

Framework (R186_034 Thm 2.1, PROVED): 𝔞_T = Pos_T − Neg_T on 𝒫_T, Pos_T = R_Tσ₊(D)R_T, Neg_T = R_Tσ₋(D)R_T, σ = σ₊ − σ₋ with σ₊σ₋ = 0, U := {σ_T < 0} (finite union of intervals, prop:band), Neg_T trace class; β₁ ≥ β₂ ≥ … eigenvalues of the bundle Neg u = β Pos u on 𝒫_T; λ_A > 0 ⟺ β₁ < 1; 1 − β₁ ∈ [λ_A/Pos(h*), λ_A/c_P].

**Theorem 2.1 (the bundle as the inertia family of a single operator; PROVED).** For β > 0 let σ_β := σ₊ − σ₋/β and W_T(σ_β) := R_Tσ_β(D)R_T|𝒫_T. Then (i) n₋(W_T(σ_β)) = #{j : β_j > β} (finite for each β, because Neg is trace class and Pos ≥ c_P > 0); (ii) β ↦ n₋(W_T(σ_β)) is non-increasing, with jumps exactly at the β_j; (iii) 1 − β₁ = ε*/(1 + ε*), ε* := sup{ε ≥ 0 : 𝔞_T ≥ ε·Neg_T on 𝒫_T}. *Proof.* W_T(σ_β) = Pos − Neg/β; by Sylvester with Pos^{1/2}: n₋(Pos − Neg/β) = n₋(1 − B/β) = #{β_j > β}, B = Pos^{−1/2}Neg Pos^{−1/2}. (iii): 𝔞 ≥ εNeg ⟺ Pos ≥ (1+ε)Neg ⟺ β₁ ≤ 1/(1+ε). ∎
Reading: "the plateau theory of the bundle" = the theory of the **last inertia crossing** of the single-operator truncated Wiener–Hopf family with symbol σ₊ − σ₋/β; it is an **extreme-eigenvalue** problem (λ_min(W_T(σ_β)) = 0), not a counting one. The Szegő/Widom/LW theorems control the count with error o(log T), which is why they do not localize β₁ (R186_039 §2.3 NO-GO, now stated in the family).

Then {β_j} = the eigenvalues of the bundle (Π diag(ν) Π, Π P Π) on ran Π, and 1 − β₁ = min_{a ∈ ran Π} (a*Pa − Σν_k|a_k|²)/(a*Pa). *Proof.* Unitary change of basis. ∎ NUMERICAL (script, ½log5, M = 40): 1 − β₁ = 1.7190·10⁻¹¹ by Thm 2.2 against 1.7191·10⁻¹¹ directly. ## 4. The question: is the Gauss quadrature error positive by structure?

Framework (R186_041 Lemma 1, PROVED on V_X): (1/2π)∫(σ_T − λ)|F̂|² = Σ_{x∈X}w_x|F̂(x)|² for F ∈ V_X, w_x ≥ 0, X = real zeros of ĥ*; below T* the nodes are the zeros γ_k with weight 1 (NUMERICAL). Quadrature reading: for F ∈ V_X, (1/2π)∫σ_T|F̂|² − Σ_x w_x|F̂(x)|² = λ_A‖F‖²; in particular λ_A = (1/2π)∫σ_T|ĥ*|² − 0.

**Proposition 4.1 (NO-GO for the sign by Gauss structure; PROVED).**
(a) *Classical remainder.* ∫f dμ − Σw_if(x_i) = f^{(2N)}(ξ)/(2N)!·∫p_N²dμ requires μ ≥ 0 (so that ∫p_N²·f[x₁,x₁,…,x_N,x_N,·]dμ admits the mean value theorem) and f^{(2N)} of constant sign. Here μ = σ_T dτ/2π is signed on U (measure |U⁺| = 6.9 … 23 with N_Sh up to 24), and for f = |F̂|² with F̂ of type T the higher-order derivatives oscillate (f^{(2N)} changes sign at every scale π/T). Neither hypothesis holds; the theorem does not apply.
(b) *Band-limited quadrature.* In the Kreĭn–de Branges framework (de Branges 1968, Thm 22: {K(x,·) : x ∈ X} orthogonal for X the zeros of a phase function), quadrature with nodes at the zeros of an entire function of type T is **exact** on the space of type 2T for positive measures: there is no remainder term with a derivative. R186_041's "remainder" λ_A‖F‖² is not a Gauss remainder but the difference between two measures representing the same form: the Lebesgue measure weighted by σ_T (signed) and the nodal one (positive). That this difference is ≥ 0 on V_X is the definition of λ_A ≥ 0.
(c) *Counterexample.* R186_041's Lemma 1 uses only: λ = min 𝔞_T|𝒫_T simple, h* with real simple zeros, and orthogonality (𝔞 − λ)(H_x, H_y) = 0. Nothing uses the sign of λ. For a symbol σ′ from prop:negtrace(v) (same U, same σ₋, σ₊′ ≤ σ₊ such that λ_A(σ′) < 0; e.g. M3′ or M23′ of §3.3, with 1 − β_max = −0.08, −0.03), under (H) for σ′ the nodal measure exists with w_x ≥ 0 and real nodes, the quadrature is exact on V_X, and the "error" is λ_A(σ′)‖F‖² < 0. **The sign is not structural within the class of Gauss–Kreĭn quadratures of truncated Toeplitz forms.**
(d) *Where the sign enters in the quadrature reading (exact).* By lem:roughEF, the zero measure Σ_ρ also represents σ_T dτ/2π on all of 𝒫_T (with terms Φ(ρ)Φ(1−ρ)). The two rules (zeros; nodal) coincide below T* (nodes = zeros, w = 1, R186_041), hence λ_A = 𝔞_T(h*) = Σ_{γ>T*}|ĥ*(γ)|² + (terms from non-real ρ, if any) − 0: R186_040 §2.3 (85–91% in (T*, 10T*)). The positivity of each term requires γ real: **CONDITIONAL (zero side)**, exactly the inheritance of R186_040 §4. Above T* the nodes of ĥ* are **not** zeros (R186_041: 40.925 vs 40.919, weights 2.5–3.2) because the zero density (1/2π)log(γ/2π) exceeds the node density T/π of an entire function of type T there; i.e. below T* the nodal quadrature is subcritical (interpolation possible) and above T* supercritical.
(e) *Regime of the asymptotics.* The e^{−cN log N} law for the Gauss error on entire integrands is the regime N ≫ type (e.g. Gauss–Legendre: error ≍ (eτ/4N)^{2N} for type τ). Here N = N(T*) and the type 2T are **at critical density** at T* by construction of T* (m(T*) = 2T, R186_040 §1.3): the error is of critical-sampling/LW-drop type (what Zhu heuristically captures as 2π²N/ln N), not of Gauss type. NUMERICAL: an N log N law would make −ln λ*·ln N/N grow like log²N (×3 between N = 30 and N ≈ 400), incompatible with Zhu's 20.0–20.3 plateau in Table 3 (CITED; for λ_W). The staircase of increasing 5–7 steps (R186_040 §3.2) matches neither Gauss's (steps 2 log(N/c) growing like log N: at N = 4 → 8 the step would rise by 1.4, 0.7 is observed) nor a single interval's (decreasing log(8c/(j+1))); it remains a record.
**Point verdict:** NO-GO for "the Gauss error is positive by structure"; the nodal quadrature is an exact rewriting (degree 0, as R186_041 already said) that relocates the sign to "the zeros above T* are real," without changing it. The interval model neither confirms it nor can: there the error is negative (§3.1, 3.3).

## 5. Honesty: does the model inherit the margin? Where does ζ enter?

**5.1 Inheritance.** The models of §3 are computed **from σ alone** (not using λ_A): that is why they can give a negative sign. The real object (REAL in the tables) is obtained by diagonalizing the bundle, and every fine description (head, nodes, Thm 2.2) inherits it. The PROVED statements of this memo (Thm 2.1–2.3, Cor. 2.4, Prop. 2.5, Lemma 2.6, Lemma 3.1, Prop. 4.1) are structural, sign-free, and one of them (Lemma 2.6) improves the tail; none bounds the head.
**5.2 Where ζ enters.** The coefficients v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n (n < e^{2T}) determine σ_T entirely, hence simultaneously (a) the location of U, (b) the profile σ₋ on U, (c) the profile σ₊ outside. The tests of §3.3 fix (a) and one of (b), (c): fixing (a)+(b) and making (c) constant, the bundle is comfortably positive (M2: 1 − β = 0.47); fixing (a)+(c) and making (b) constant, it is negative (M3: −2.2); with per-component levels on both (M23′) it stays within 3% of balance, with a negative sign. **The sign is neither in the location of the negative intervals nor in either profile alone: it lies in the fine relation σ₊ ↔ σ₋ across ∂U** (where σ vanishes linearly and the head concentrates its spectral mass: centroids 7–14 at ½log5, R186_040 §1.2(b), exactly at the crossings 1.98, 4.24, 6.46, 8.74, 11.29).
