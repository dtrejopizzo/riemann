## 3. Closure of ρ1′ (task 3)

Notation: τ_loc = topology of uniform convergence on compacta of ℂ; 𝒦_C := {K ∈ 𝒦 : sup_ℝ|K| ≤ C}; Δ := Δ_max.

**Lemma A (compactness of 𝒦_C). PROVED (on CITED Paley–Wiener–Schwartz, Plancherel–Pólya, Montel).** (i) Every
K ∈ 𝒦_C is entire of exponential type ≤ Δ with |K(x+iy)| ≤ C e^{Δ|y|}. (ii) 𝒦_C is τ_loc-compact and metrizable.
*Proof.* (i) K bounded continuous with Ǩ supported in [−Δ,Δ] is entire of type ≤ Δ (PW–S); bounded on ℝ and of
type ≤ Δ ⟹ |K(x+iy)| ≤ ‖K‖_∞e^{Δ|y|} (Plancherel–Pólya / Phragmén–Lindelöf in each half-plane). (ii) By (i) the
family is uniformly bounded on every strip |Im z| ≤ Y, hence normal (Montel); a limit K_∞ is entire with
|K_∞(z)| ≤ Ce^{Δ|Im z|}, hence of type ≤ Δ and bounded on ℝ, hence Ǩ_∞ ⊂ [−Δ,Δ] (PW–S), K_∞ ≥ 0 on ℝ (pointwise
limit), K_∞ continuous: K_∞ ∈ 𝒦_C. Closed and normal ⟹ compact; metrizable by the exhaustion by compacta. ∎

**Lemma B (continuity of the functionals). PROVED.** Let G = F̂ with F ∈ C_c^∞ (Paper 46 witness; for finite-type
witnesses with G ∈ L² see the note in audit 1) and Σ a multiset of zeros with |Im t_ρ| ≤ ½ and N(T) ≪ T log T. On
𝒦_C, the linear functionals K ↦ Tail_K(λ) (λ ≥ 0 fixed), Z_K(Σ), 𝔄_K, Q^{hyp}_K are τ_loc-continuous.
*Proof.* Each is an absolutely convergent sum or integral of G(t)²K(t) against a fixed weight, with |K| ≤ Ce^{Δ/2}
on the strip and Σ_ρ|G(t_ρ)|² < ∞, ∫|G|²(1 + log(2+|t|))dt < ∞ (G ≪_N (1+|t|)^{−N} on strips):
Tail_K = Σ_{X≤n<Xe^Δ}Λ(n)n^{−½}Ψ̌(log n) with Ψ̌(x) = (2π)^{−1}∫G²K e^{itx}dt; Z_K = Σ_ρG(t_ρ)²K(t_ρ); 𝔄_K = polar
term (evaluation of G²K at ±i/2, continuous in τ_loc) + ∫G²K·Re(Γ′/Γ)(¼+it/2)dt + finite sum over primes n < X;
Q^{hyp}_K = 4Re G(t_q)²K(t_q). τ_loc convergence ⟹ pointwise on the strip, and dominated convergence concludes. ∎
(Normalization of 𝔄_K: to be checked against def:W/eq:nuw before any integration, as a rule; here only linearity and
absolute convergence of 𝔄_K are used, as in R185_003 Prop 3(c).)

**Theorem C (the ba(𝔉) functionals factor through a test). PROVED (on CITED: ba(𝔉) = C(β𝔉)*).** Let 𝔉 ⊂ 𝒦_C and
μ ∈ ba(𝔉) (finitely additive, bounded; e.g. a Banach limit along K_n ∈ 𝔉). Let 𝔉̄ be the τ_loc-closure of 𝔉
(compact by Lemma A). Then there exists a Radon measure μ̃ on 𝔉̄, with μ̃ ≥ 0 if μ ≥ 0 and ‖μ̃‖ ≤ ‖μ‖, such that
 μ(φ∘ι) = ∫_{𝔉̄} φ dμ̃ for every φ ∈ C(𝔉̄), ι: 𝔉 ↪ 𝔉̄;
and K_μ := ∫_{𝔉̄} K dμ̃(K) satisfies K_μ ∈ 𝒦_{C‖μ‖} (if μ ≥ 0) and
 Tail_{K_μ} = μ(Tail), Z_{K_μ}(Σ) = μ(Z(Σ)), 𝔄_{K_μ} = μ(𝔄), Q^{hyp}_{K_μ} = μ(Q^{hyp}).
*Proof.* Identify μ with a Radon measure on the Stone–Čech compactification β𝔉 of discrete 𝔉 (ba(𝔉) = ℓ^∞(𝔉)* =
C(β𝔉)*). By the universal property, ι extends to a continuous ι̃: β𝔉 → 𝔉̄ (𝔉̄ compact Hausdorff); μ̃ := ι̃_*μ is
Radon and μ(φ∘ι) = ∫φ dμ̃ for φ ∈ C(𝔉̄). K_μ(z) := ∫K(z)dμ̃(K) converges uniformly on compacta
(|K(z)| ≤ Ce^{Δ|Im z|}), hence is entire with |K_μ(z)| ≤ C‖μ‖e^{Δ|Im z|}, bounded on ℝ, of type ≤ Δ, ≥ 0 on ℝ if
μ ≥ 0: K_μ ∈ 𝒦_{C‖μ‖}. For ℓ ∈ {Tail, Z, 𝔄, Q^{hyp}}: ℓ ∈ C(𝔉̄) by Lemma B, so μ(ℓ∘ι) = ∫ℓ dμ̃; and
ℓ(K_μ) = ∫ℓ(K)dμ̃ by Fubini (ℓ is an absolutely convergent sum/integral of K against a fixed weight, with uniform
majorant C e^{Δ/2}|G|²·weight). ∎

*Reading.* A Banach limit along K_n ∈ 𝒦_C is a μ̃ concentrated on the τ_loc accumulation points of (K_n), and
Z_{K_μ} = lim_Banach Z_{K_n}: the infinite sum over ρ **does** commute with μ, because Z is continuous on the
compact set 𝔉̄. The objection of R185_092 M2 ("an infinite sum over ρ does not commute with a merely finitely
additive μ") can only occur if 𝔉 is not sup-bounded (mass leakage of K toward |t| → ∞ or toward large values,
where compactness fails). With Theorem C, Prop 3 of R185_003 holds for ba(𝔉) and Thm 2 reduces the exclusion to
**one** test K_μ ∈ 𝒦: the ba subcase of ρ1′ is closed.

*Normalization.* The inequalities of (1.1) are homogeneous in K; if 𝔉 is closed under scaling and S_K is
1-homogeneous, WLOG sup_𝔉‖K‖_∞ = 1. If 𝔉 is not sup-bounded with S_K bounded, the normalization K_n/‖K_n‖ sends
S_{K_n}/‖K_n‖ → 0: in the limit the information about the remainder becomes R_{K_∞} ≥ 0 and the inequality may stop
being strict (R185_003 Obs 1.3). That degenerate case remains in ρ1‴ (§4).

**Lemma D (closure of 𝓡 + C̄ with lower bounds). PROVED.** Let 𝔉 be arbitrary, C̄ ⊂ ℝ^𝔉 the closed cone generated
by v(γ), and 𝓡 ⊂ ℝ^𝔉 closed (product topology) with inf_{r∈𝓡} r_K =: −S_K > −∞ for each K ∈ 𝔉 (coupled or not;
no upper bound). Then 𝓡 + C̄ is closed. If 𝓡 is convex, 𝓡 + C̄ is convex.
*Proof.* K ≥ 0 on ℝ ⟹ v(γ) ∈ ∏_K[0,∞) ⟹ C̄ ⊂ ∏_K[0,∞) (closed orthant). Let r_α + c_α → w be a net, r_α ∈ 𝓡,
c_α ∈ C̄. View c_α in the compact set [0,∞]^𝔉 (Tychonoff): a subnet converges to c ∈ [0,∞]^𝔉. For each K,
r_{α,K} + c_{α,K} → w_K and r_{α,K} ≥ −S_K give c_{α,K} ≤ w_K + S_K + 1 eventually, so c_K ≤ w_K + S_K + 1 < ∞:
c ∈ ℝ^𝔉 and the subnet converges to c in the product topology of ℝ^𝔉; C̄ closed ⟹ c ∈ C̄. Then
r_α = (r_α + c_α) − c_α → w − c along the subnet and 𝓡 closed ⟹ w − c ∈ 𝓡. Hence w ∈ 𝓡 + C̄. Convexity: sum of
convex sets. ∎

**Theorem E (circularity in (M2) with non-compact coupling). PROVED.** Let 𝓡 be closed convex with per-coordinate
lower bounds (Lemma D), G fixed, 𝔉 ⊂ 𝒦 arbitrary, and suppose Sh(B) ∩ W₂ = ∅ with W₂ = w₀ − 𝓡 − C̄ (exclusion of
the relaxed set). Then there exist K₁,…,K_m ∈ 𝔉 and a ∈ ℝ^m with K* := Σa_iK_i ≥ 0 on ℝ (hence K* ∈ 𝒦) such that
 Tail_{K*}(Λ_ζ) > 𝔄_{K*} − Q^{hyp}_{K*} + S_𝓡(a), S_𝓡(a) := sup_{r∈𝓡}(−a·r) < ∞,
equivalently Z_{K*}(Σ_ζ) < Q^{hyp}_{K*} − S_𝓡(a).
*Proof.* W₂ is closed convex (Lemma D) and t_ζ ∈ Sh(B) ∖ W₂. ℝ^𝔉 with the product topology is locally convex
Hausdorff with finite-support dual (R185_003 Lemma 5, CITED); strict separation gives a of finite support with
a·t_ζ > sup_{W₂}a·w = a·w₀ + S_𝓡(a) + sup_{o∈C̄}(−a·o). Finiteness of the last term ⟹ a·o ≥ 0 ∀o ∈ C̄ ⟹ K_a ≥ 0
on ℝ (R185_001 Lemma 4, using the isolated zeros of G) and it equals 0; finiteness of S_𝓡(a) is automatic.
Linearity of Tail, 𝔄, Q, Z in K concludes. ∎

*Scope.* Replaces in R185_003 Thm 2 and in BR N0.5 the hypothesis "𝓡 compact" with "𝓡 with per-coordinate lower
bounds". Includes: (M1) (𝓡 = ∏[−S_K,∞)); (M1) + any convex couplings (R_{K₁} + R_{K₂} ≥ −S, remainder cones, etc.);
𝓡 not bounded above. As soon as a proof in 𝔐 has **some** lower bound on the remainder in each test it uses (and
tests without a bound are discarded as in R185_003 Obs 1.2, which does not change Sh or the exclusion if they do
not enter 𝓡), the exclusion factors through a single admissible test. That is the content of ρ1′ "non-compact
couplings": closed.

**Example F (the hypothesis of Lemma D cannot be dropped). PROVED.** 𝔉 = {K₁, K₂} with K₂(γ₀) = 0 < K₁(γ₀),
G(γ₀) ≠ 0 (K₂ with a real double zero; exists in 𝒦). Then (1,0) ∈ C̄. Let 𝓡 = {(r₁,r₂) : r₂ ≥ e^{−r₁}}, closed
convex, with inf r₂ = 0 but inf r₁ = −∞. Since C̄ ⊂ [0,∞)² and (1,0) ∈ C̄: {r₂ > 0} = 𝓡 + ℝ₊(1,0) ⊂ 𝓡 + C̄ ⊂
𝓡 + [0,∞)² = {r₂ > 0}. So 𝓡 + C̄ = {r₂ > 0} is not closed and W₂ = {t : t₂ < w₀,₂} is open: t_ζ ∈ cl W₂ ∖ W₂ is
possible (t_{ζ,2} = w₀,₂). ∎
*Reading.* The coupling says "the remainder at K₂ is strictly positive but as small as wanted, in exchange for an
arbitrarily negative remainder at K₁": no lower bound at K₁. In this example the exclusion Sh(B) ∩ W₂ = ∅ is
exactly Tail_{K₂}(λ) ≥ w₀,₂ ∀λ ⊨ B: **it still factors through a test**, with a non-strict inequality.

**Proposition G (finite dimension without closure). PROVED.** Let 𝔉 be finite, 𝓡 any closed convex set, and
Sh(B) ∩ W₂ = ∅ with t_ζ ∈ cl W₂ (the case R185_091 M1 leaves outside Thm 5(b)). Then there exists a ∈ ℝ^𝔉 ∖ {0}
with K_a ≥ 0 on ℝ and
 Tail_{K_a}(Λ_ζ) ≥ 𝔄_{K_a} − Q^{hyp}_{K_a} + S_𝓡(a), i.e. Z_{K_a}(Σ_ζ) ≤ Q^{hyp}_{K_a} − S_𝓡(a).
*Proof.* In ℝ^m, for W₂ convex, int(cl W₂) = int W₂; t_ζ ∈ cl W₂ ∖ W₂ is a boundary point of cl W₂, and every
boundary point of a convex set in ℝ^m has a supporting hyperplane: a ≠ 0 with a·t_ζ ≥ a·w ∀w ∈ W₂. Finiteness of
sup_{W₂}a·w ⟹ a·o ≥ 0 on C̄ ⟹ K_a ≥ 0 (Lemma 4 of R185_001) and S_𝓡(a) < ∞. ∎
*Reading.* If the quartet is present and the (M2) information is true, a·Z(Σ_ζ) = a·Q^{hyp} + a·R + a·O ≥
a·Q^{hyp} − S_𝓡(a), and the exclusion forces the equality a·R = −S_𝓡(a), a·O = 0: it is not "false" but "possible
only in the measure-zero boundary case", and it remains a zero statement. Repairs the gap of R185_001 Thm 5(b)
except for strictness.

## 4. What remains of ρ1′: residue ρ1‴ (OPEN, narrow)

ρ1‴ := {(M2) with 𝔉 infinite, 𝓡 with no lower bound in some test and coupled such that 𝓡 + C̄ is not closed, with
t_ζ ∈ cl W₂ ∖ W₂} ∪ {ba(𝔉) with 𝔉 not sup-bounded}. In the first set, in finite dimension Prop G factors through a
test (non-strict); in infinite dimension a boundary point of a convex set with empty interior may not be a support
point (standard CITED), and there ρ1‴ is genuine. It remains a zero statement by R185_003 Thm 6∞ (CITED). Honest
models of the other quartets (cones generated by u(t) = (4Re G(t)²K(t))_K, t off the line) are homogeneous: if
they are bounded below in one coordinate they are ≥ 0 there; if not, it is through quartets where the test changes
sign, and the coupling of Example F (exponential) is not conic. It is not claimed that ρ1‴ is empty.

## 5. Does it move BR? (task 4) Honestly: it reaffirms it

## 6. Explicit falsifiers

- **Of Theorem C:** a sequence K_n ∈ 𝒦 with sup_n‖K_n‖_∞ < ∞ and a Banach limit L such that
 L(Z_{K_n}(Σ)) ≠ Z_{K_∞}(Σ) for every τ_loc accumulation point K_∞ — impossible by Lemma A + Lemma B (Z
 continuous on the compact set). Falsifier of the proof: a K ∈ 𝒦 bounded on ℝ that is not entire of type ≤ Δ
 (against PW–S) or a Σ with Σ_ρ|G(t_ρ)|² = ∞ (against N(T) ≪ T log T).
- **Of Lemma D:** 𝓡 closed with inf_𝓡 r_K > −∞ ∀K and a net r_α + c_α → w ∉ 𝓡 + C̄; the proof shows this requires
 c_{α,K} → ∞ in some coordinate, contradicting the lower bound. Example F shows that without the lower bound it
 does occur.
- **Of Theorem E:** an exclusion of W₂ with 𝓡 having lower bounds that does not factor through any finite
 combination K* ≥ 0.
- **Of ρ1‴ (in the route sense):** a scheme in 𝔐 whose information about the remainder is coupled, with no lower
 bound in some test, with 𝔉 infinite and t_ζ ∈ cl W₂ ∖ W₂ not a support point. No file in the corpus constructs
 one (check in §1).
