## 1. Lemma 0 and the trilemma (attack 1)

**1.1 The statement is correct and exact.** For k(t,s) = κ₁(t−s) + κ₂(t+s) and F ∈ L²(I_T), the form only sees κ_i|[−2T,2T]
because c_F and the "Hankel autocorrelation" ∫F(t)F(u−t)dt are supported on [−2T,2T]. The blindness is exact, not
asymptotic: c_F(log n) ≡ 0 for log n ≥ 2T (including the boundary, c_F(±2T) = 0). For distributional κ_i one must say the
form is defined by duality with c_F ∈ C_c(ℝ) (c_F is continuous for F ∈ L²): the atoms exactly at ±2T do not
contribute; it is correct but unwritten. MINOR.

**1.2 Zero novelty.** R165_202 1.1 ("No jumps," PROVED): "every comb term with log n ∈ [2T, 2T′) vanishes on F … the
jumps of σ_T at 2T = log n are invisible to the forms," and W_T = W_∞|L²(I_T). Lemma 0 is 1.1 plus the remark that
the Hankel kernels also truncate. The memo does not cite 1.1 when checking Lemma 0 (it does cite 1.4–1.8). MINOR (attribution).

**1.3 "Polar (Hankel)" is false.** A(F)B(F) = ∫∫e^{(t−s)/2}F(t)F(s) is **Toeplitz** (a function of t−s), not Hankel. Hankel
would be A(F)² = ∫∫e^{(t+s)/2}FF, which does not appear in 𝔞_T. Nothing in 𝔞_T is Hankel; the Hankel part of the lemma
is decorative. MINOR (factual error with no consequences).

**1.4 The trilemma is not exhaustive. MAJOR.** The memo deduces "S2 ⟹ one of three": (i) degree ≠ 2 in F with effective
support > 2T, (ii) zero side, (iii) dynamics in T. But Lemma 0 only covers **quadratic forms with a Toeplitz+Hankel kernel**.
Left out, and the memo acknowledges this only halfway:
- (b) general quadratic kernels k(t,s) (e.g. F ↦ ⟨LF, LF⟩_{𝔞_{T′}} with L linear of growing support, or
 convolution with a fixed kernel of positive support): the memo closes these **by citation** to W-lin (R186_026 Thm 3.3 /
 R186_031). That is not Lemma 0; the trilemma is a tetralemma with one branch closed by another tool, whose scope ("fixed
 basis with coefficients in Λ") does not match "general kernel": a kernel k depending on F (nonlinear) escapes W-lin.
- **degree 0 in F** (S1 explicitly admits it: "degree 0: phase of G at t_q, harmonic measure of the node set,"
 R186_032 §4). A functional of the node set X of ĥ*, e.g. Σ_{n>e^{2T}} Λ(n)n^{−1/2}Σ_{x∈X}w(x)cos(x log n), is
 **exactly what S2 asks for** ("Λ|[X,∞) at the node frequencies of F̂") and is neither (i) (not degree ≥ 4), nor (ii)
 (not the zero side of ζ), nor (iii) (T fixed). Seed (γ) itself is degree 0 (via ω) and the memo classifies it as
 "(iii)+(i)," which is inconsistent with its own table ("S1: degree 0").
Conclusion: Lemma 0 is a **classification tool for Toeplitz/Hankel quadratic forms**, not a reduction of
S2. The phrase "S2 ⟹ one of three" is a reading (ARGUED), not a theorem. Rewrite: "every S2 object that is a quadratic
Toeplitz/Hankel form in F is trivial; the remaining candidates are degree ≥ 4, degree 0 (nodes), general kernel (W-lin by
citation), the zero side, or dynamics in T."

## 2. Lemma α.1 and the reading "fold = nodal lattice" (attack 2)

**2.1 Formula. PROVED, standard.** Integration by parts: ĥ(x) = [h(−T)e^{ixT} − h(T)e^{−ixT}]/(ix) + (ix)^{−1}∫h′e^{−ixt};
the second term is o(1/x) by Riemann–Lebesgue with h′ ∈ L¹. Correct. W^{1,1}(I_T) (h absolutely continuous on the
closed interval, h′ ∈ L¹) is **sufficient**; it is the first-order asymptotic of the transform of a function with jumps at
±T (classical: Cartwright/Paley–Wiener, any text on transforms of compactly supported functions). It is not new.
(ii) (|h(T)| = |h(−T)| if G has infinitely many real zeros): correct; note that it **does not need real-rootedness**, only
a sequence of real zeros x_k → ∞ (which Cartwright gives under full support, R165_202 1.7). Hypothesis overbuilt.
MINOR. The formula is an "o(1)" uniform in nothing: there is no error bound; for h*_T it is not even known that the trace exists.

**2.2 Application to h*_T: CONDITIONAL, not PROVED.** The memo admits this in "Scope" (H^{log} with no trace, OPEN) but §0,
the table and the README 186 (round 17–18, line R186_038) write "Lemma α.1 PROVED: the fold is the amplitude of the
asymptotic nodal lattice." For **consequence (i)** (fold of λ_A′ = coefficient of cos(2T_n x)) to be a statement about λ_A,
all the hypotheses R186_110 §1.4 already demanded for Lemma 2 are needed, and the memo omits them: (REG),
h* with a trace at ±T_n, **λ_A simple in a neighborhood of T_n (1.10 OPEN)** and **continuity of T ↦ h*_T in form norm
(1.5 ARGUED)**; without them the fold is a jump of −𝒱_T(h*_T)/T, not of λ_A′, and eigenvalue crossings produce folds with no
prime. MAJOR (label in §0/README; the body is honest).

**2.3 Is h*_T ∈ W^{1,1}(I_T)? Does it have jumps at ±T?** OPEN in the record (R165_202 1.5 "boundary behaviour … not
established"; R186_004 "Hadamard formula OPEN: the eigenfunction may be singular at the boundary"). There is also a
**tension** the memo does not flag: 1.5 (ARGUED) says that where λ_A strictly decreases, Dh* ∉ 𝒟_T (boundary
singularity). That is compatible with a finite jump (h*(±T) ≠ 0) but also with h* ~ (T−t)^{−1/4} (no trace), and in that
case c_{h*}′(2T⁻) = ∞ and the "fold" is −∞ (λ_A continuous but with slope −∞ to the right of T_n), which does **not
contradict lem:contA** (modulus C/log(1/h), not Lipschitz). So the alternative "finite jump / no trace" remains open and
α.1–α.2 cover only the first branch. The memo's analytic falsifier (|h*(T)| ≠ |h*(−T)|) is vacuous: the parity of h*
(J-invariant form, simple λ) already gives h*(−T) = ±h*(T) without α.1.

**2.4 "Fold = amplitude of the asymptotic nodal lattice": a reading, not a theorem.** The lattice kπ/T (or (k+½)π/T) is
universal (depends only on T and parity); the "amplitude" is 2h(T)². Saying the prime "couples via the lattice amplitude"
is just rewriting −2v_nh(T)h(−T) = v_n·(coefficient of cos 2Tx). It is algebra, PROVED as an identity and empty as
content: no individual node enters, as the memo itself concludes. Consistent with R162_407 Prop 4.3 ("the sign
of c_F(log n) near the boundary is ε·F(T′−)F(−T′+) … and not arithmetic"): same fact, 2026-09. Novelty: the phrase.

**2.5 α.2.** (i) PROVED (one-sided continuity; it is R165_202 1.2 to first order with a, b constants). (ii) CONDITIONAL with
the full hypotheses of 2.2 (the memo lists only "(REG) and λ_A simple"). (iii) CONDITIONAL. The reading "on the even
sector the primes can only worsen the slope" is correct under (ii) and is the differential form of R165_202 1.2
(q_n negative on symmetric boundary profiles). MINOR (incomplete hypotheses).

## 3. Lemma α.3: Grönwall at the minimizer (attack 3)

**3.1 (a) needs absolute continuity of λ_A. MAJOR.** The memo moves from "λ_A′ ≥ −cλ_A on J" (derivative where it exists) to
λ_A(T₁) ≥ λ_A(T₀)exp(−∫c). Grönwall requires λ_A to be **absolutely continuous** on J; the record gives: λ_A monotone
non-increasing (lem:monoT), continuous (lem:contA, with modulus C/log(1/h) in R165_202 1.3), and D⁺λ ≤ −𝒱/T ≤ D₋λ (1.4). A
continuous monotone function can have a singular part (λ′ = 0 a.e. and still descend to 0): the a.e. bound does not control
it. Absolute continuity would follow from a **uniform** bound sup_{T∈J}𝒱_T(h*_T) < ∞ (Danskin: λ locally Lipschitz),
which is (REG) uniform on J and is not in the record. SIGN-REAUDIT §3 states the abstract lemma with "q_r locally
absolutely continuous" as an explicit hypothesis; the memo omits it. Relabel (a): CONDITIONAL ((REG) uniform +
simplicity + continuity of the minimizer + AC of λ_A).

**3.2 (b): deduced, not assumed.** From λ_A(T) ≥ λ_A(T₀)e^{−∫c} and thm:M(b) (λ_A(T) ≤ exp(−e^{(2−ε)T}), Paper 48,
CITED from the record, not refereed) one gets ∫_{T₀}^T c ≥ e^{(2−ε)T} − log(1/λ_A(T₀)). Correct arithmetic. It is **thm:M(b)
rewritten** as "−(log λ_A)′ has average ≥ e^{(2−ε)T}": the super-exponential behavior is a consequence of thm:M, not a
premise, and adds no information (c_min(T) := 𝒱_T(h*_T)/(Tλ_A(T)) is by definition the one optimal c). With only Paper 46
thm:margin (limsup λ_A ≤ 0 and the explicit bound ℬ(T,k)) the conclusion would be ∫c ≥ log(λ_A(T₀)/ℬ(T,k)), much weaker: the
label "super-exponential" depends on Paper 48, as the memo says. CITED, correct.

**3.3 (c) has contradictory hypotheses. MAJOR (vacuous twice over).** Hypotheses: λ_A(T_c) = 0, λ_A > 0 to the
left, and (a) "holds up to T_c with finite c." If c ∈ L¹([T₀,T_c]) and λ_A is AC, (a) gives λ_A(T_c) ≥ λ_A(T₀)e^{−∫c} > 0:
contradicting λ_A(T_c) = 0. So either c ∉ L¹ near T_c (in which case 𝒱 → 0 does not follow from (a): T_cλ_A can
tend to anything with c → ∞, λ_A → 0) or the hypothesis is vacuous. Either way the conclusion 𝒱_{T_c}(h*_{T_c})
= 0 **does not follow from (a)**. What is in the record, independently of Grönwall, is R165_202 1.5 (ARGUED) "𝒱 = 0 ⟺
Dh* ∈ 𝒟_T ⟹ h*(±T) = 0," and R162_407 Cor 3.3 (under (REG): (LB) to o(κ′) ⟺ 𝒱 ≤ 0 on N_{T_*}). The memo cites 1.5 and then
says "by α.1 this is equivalent to h*(±T_c) = 0": α.1 does not intervene (h(T)² + h(−T)² = 0 ⟺ h(±T) = 0 is trivial) and
1.5 only gives one direction (Dh* ∈ 𝒟 ⟹ 𝒱 = 0 and zero trace), ARGUED. Withdraw (c) or rewrite it as "if 𝒱_T(h*_T) → 0 as
T → T_c⁻ then …," which is 1.5 and nothing more.

**3.4 "f_t shares h*_T and 𝒱_T for T < T₁": PROVED (not ARGUED), with the correct citation.** R163_901 Thm F item 3 (PROVED;
standard explicit formula CITED) gives W^{f_t}_T = W^ζ_T **exactly** for T ≤ ½log λ₁ =: T₁: same form ⟹ same
𝒫_T, same λ_A, same minimizer (if simple) and same 𝒱_T (which only uses m, the comb n < e^{2T}, and the polar term). What is
ARGUED in R186_110 is **Cor F** (properties of the witness for f_t: (A-loc), balance); here only item 4 of Thm F is used
(finite contact T_c(f_t) ≤ T₁ + ε, PROVED). The memo cites "Theorem 3(a) of R186_036," which in turn depends on Thm F: the
chain is sound, the label is PROVED. No objection to the substance of the "escape": (a) on [T₀,T₁) is identical for ζ and
f_t, and what distinguishes ζ is a statement of size λ_A on [T₁, ∞). Correct and already said in R186_004 Prop 2.1 ("Grönwall
would need to bound ∫λ′, the same problem with one fewer scalar") and in I16.

**3.5 Finite-range Grönwall [½log5, T₁]: a nonempty statement?** A precise answer, which the memo does not give:
- (α) With AC of λ_A, "there exists c ∈ L¹([T₀,T₁]) with 𝒱_T(h*_T) ≤ Tc(T)λ_A(T)" ⟺ "λ_A > 0 on [T₀,T₁]." (⟸: if λ_A > 0 on
 the compact set, log λ_A is AC and c := −(log λ_A)′ ∈ L¹ works. ⟹: Grönwall.) That is, the finite-range Grönwall **is**
 the finite-range target, not a tool: nonempty statement, zero content. Any proof of the relative virial bound on [T₀,T₁]
 is a proof of D on [T₀,T₁] by definition; and the required relative precision is 𝒱/λ_A with λ_A ≤
 exp(−e^{(2−ε)T}) (thm:M(b)) for large T, or ≥ 2.5·10⁻¹² only at T₀ = ½log5 (thm:log5A).
- (β) Version with **independent** input: an absolute bound 𝒱_T(h*_T) ≤ V (not relative to λ_A). Then λ_A(T) ≥

 δ ≈ T₀λ_A(T₀)/V ≈ 0.8·2.5·10⁻¹²/V. Is V attainable with certified data? The archimedean virial (1/2π)∫τm′|ĥ*|² ≤
 M₁‖h*‖² = M₁ (M₁ = sup|τm′|, lem:contT) is O(1) PROVED; the comb virial Σ v_n log n·c′_{h*}(log n) needs (REG)
 and a bound on |c′_{h*}| that does not exist without a trace (OPEN); the polar term is O(1) if A, B, Ȧ, Ḃ are (yes, by
 ‖h*‖ = 1). Even with V = O(1) PROVED, δ ≈ 10⁻¹²: **strictly worse than R162_407 Thm 2.1** (λ_A(T_*+δ) ≥ −κ₀(δ) with
 κ₀ ≍ 1/log(1/δ), linear transport with no virial), which is already vacuous at that scale (δ ~ exp(−C/λ_A(T₀))). And note
 that (β) **is not Grönwall** (it is the additive bound of 1.4 integrated): the multiplicative form λ_A′ ≥ −cλ_A does not

- (γ) Conclusion: there is no nonempty Grönwall statement for finite range. The needed bound 𝒱_T(h*_T)/λ_A(T) ∈

 This agrees with R186_004 Prop 2.1 ("cannot be stronger than bounding Q(½log3) directly") and quantifies it.

## 4. Lemma γ.1: "the sign is polar" (attack 4)

**4.1 What γ.1 is.** With g(u) = φ(u)cos(ωu) (no weight), Weil's explicit formula gives Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−1/2}g(log n) =
∫g(u)(e^{u/2} + e^{−u/2})du − Σ_ρ ĝ(t_ρ) + archimedean; with supp φ ⊂ (2T, 2T+Δ) the polar term is ≍ e^{T} and the zeros on
the line enter via φ̃(γ ± ω) with no weight. **It is the PNT with the n^{−iω} twist at fixed ω** (Landau;
Σ_{n≤x}Λ(n)n^{−iω} ~ x^{1−iω}/(1−iω)), in smoothed form: PROVED-CITED, zero novelty. The memo writes in the proof "test
u ↦ e^{−u/2}φ(u)cos(ωu)," which would make the prime side Σ Λ(n)n^{−1}φ cos, **not** ½𝔖_γ; the stated formula is that of
the unweighted test. MINOR (inconsistency in the proof; the statement is correct).

**4.2 The O(1) of the zeros on the line: correct and unconditional.** Σ_γ|φ̃(γ−ω)| with |φ̃(t)| ≤ C_kΔ(1+Δ|t|)^{−k}
(φ ∈ C_c^k) and N(t) ≪ t log t converges and is uniform in T (translating supp φ only changes φ̃ by a phase). There
is no "log T" unless ω grows with T; ω = s*(T) is O(1) (plateau, R186_103 NUMERICAL). The "log T" in the statement is spurious.
MINOR. No hypothesis "zeros on the line O(1)" distinct from the density N(t) (CITED) is used: γ.1 does not depend on
where the zeros are for its line term; **it depends on zeros off the line** in the leading term.

**4.3 The relative order e^{−T(1−2δ_max)} is NOT proved. MAJOR.** The memo writes "δ < ½ for every zero (I2 of
R163_401, PROVED-CITED)" and concludes relative order ≤ e^{−T(1−2δ_max)}. (I2) is δ_q < ½ **for each** zero (β < 1,
Hadamard–dlVP). For e^{−T(1−2δ_max)} to be o(1), one needs **sup_ρ δ_ρ < ½**, i.e. Θ := sup Re ρ < 1, which is
**OPEN** (it is "quasi-RH"). What is CITED is the zero-free region: β ≤ 1 − c/log γ (dlVP) or 1 −
c/(log γ)^{2/3}(log log γ)^{1/3} (VK). With that, and the decay of φ̃ in γ, a single zero's term is e^{2Tδ_ρ}|φ̃(γ_ρ ±
ω)| ≤ e^{T}·e^{−2Tc/log γ}·(Δγ)^{−k}; splitting at γ ≤ e^{√T} and γ > e^{√T} one gets relative order **e^{−c′√T}** (dlVP)
or e^{−c′T^{3/5}(log T)^{−1/5}} (VK): o(1), so the **qualitative conclusion** ("the sign is decided by the polar term for
large T") is PROVED-CITED, but the written exponent is false as an unconditional statement. Fix: "relative order
e^{−T(1−2Θ′)} if Θ < 1 (OPEN), and e^{−c√T} unconditionally."

**4.4 Is "the sign is polar" PROVED-CITED from R166_802 Thm 3?** Not via Thm 3: Thm 3 is about the exponent Θ − ½ of the
fluctuation A_γ(Y) over unbounded Y, and it states that frequency γ_q **does not isolate** the quartet. γ.1 does not use
Thm 3; it uses the PNT (zero-free region). The correct citation is "Landau / Hadamard–dlVP, CITED"; Thm 3 enters only in
the *reading* ("the remainder o(e^T) has exponent Θ − ½, so controlling the remainder to exponent < δ_q is zero

zero-free region) what remains is Σ_{n}Λ(n)n^{−1/2}cos(ω log n)φ(log n) = polar − Σ_ρ(…), a **sign-free identity**: the
dominance of the polar term is precisely the statement "there are no zeros at β ≥ 1," and at that level the "polar sign" is
