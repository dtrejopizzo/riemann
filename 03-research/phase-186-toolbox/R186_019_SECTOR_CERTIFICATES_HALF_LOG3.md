## 1. Block decomposition (PROVED)

Let ℓ = log2, T = ½log3 and I_T = (−T, T). The blocks are:
- J = (−T, T−ℓ) = (−0.549, −0.144);
- J′ = J + ℓ = (ℓ−T, T) = (0.144, 0.549);
- M = (T−ℓ, ℓ−T) = (−0.144, 0.144).

Their lengths are |J| = 2T−ℓ = log(3/2) = 0.405 and |M| = 2ℓ−2T = log(4/3) = 0.288. This matches the definition of J, M, J′ in R186_004 §1 and §3. The partition is I_T = J ⊔ M ⊔ J′, up to points.

**Lemma 1.1.** For x ∈ I_T:
- x+ℓ ∈ I_T ⟺ x ∈ J;
- x−ℓ ∈ I_T ⟺ x ∈ J′;
- if x ∈ M, neither is in I_T.

Consequently, b_2(h) = ∫_J h(x)h(x+ℓ)dx. The prime-2 term, −√2 ℓ·b_2(h), only sees the paired block L²(J) ⊕ L²(J′), and there it acts as the swap τ: (f on J, g on J′) ↦ (g(·+ℓ), f(·−ℓ)). On L²(M) it acts as zero.

*Proof.* x+ℓ < T ⟺ x < T−ℓ, and x+ℓ > −T whenever x > −T. The case x−ℓ is symmetric. ∎

**Decomposition.** We have L²(I_T) = H_s ⊕ H_a ⊕ L²(M), with
H_± = {h = (φ ± φ(·−ℓ))/√2 : φ ∈ L²(J)}, of norm ‖h‖ = ‖φ‖. The spaces H_s and H_a are the ±1 eigenspaces of τ.

On H_±, with h = (φ ± φ(·−ℓ))/√2, we have b_2(h) = ±½‖φ‖² = ±½‖h‖². Hence the prime term equals −√2ℓ·b_2(h) = ∓(√2ℓ/2)‖h‖² = ∓0.490‖h‖² (PROVED).

The archimedean part is **not** diagonal in this decomposition. By the symbol identity of R185_006 (PROVED for T ≤ ½log3), the full form is
W(h₁, h₂) = (1/2π)∫θ ĥ₁ \overline{ĥ₂} ds, with θ = m − √2ℓ cos(sℓ).

**Proposition 1.2 (PROVED; reformulates R186_004 Prop 3.1 with the cross terms).** For φ, ψ ∈ L²(J) and χ ∈ L²(M):
- W(h_±(φ), h_±(φ)) = (1/2π)∫θ·2cos²(sℓ/2) or θ·2sin²(sℓ/2), times |φ̂|² (with the ½ factor from the normalization ‖h‖ = ‖φ‖);
- W(h_+(φ), h_−(ψ)) = (1/2π)∫θ·(i sin(sℓ))·φ̂ψ̂̄ ds, which is not zero;
- W(h_±(φ), χ) = (1/2π)∫θ·(1 ± e^{−isℓ})/√2·φ̂χ̂̄ ds, which is not zero.

The last two are the couplings. The prime part does not contribute to W(h_±, χ) (Lemma 1.1). It also does not contribute to W(h_+, h_−), because τ is diagonal. So the couplings are **purely archimedean**.

**Tate.** The functional is ĥ(±i/2) = ∫h e^{±x/2} dx. On h_±(φ) it equals φ̂(±i/2)(1 ± e^{±ℓ/2})/√2. The factor does not vanish, so the sectoral Tate condition is equivalent to φ̂(±i/2) = 0, as in R186_004 Prop 3.1. The exact factors depend on the convention for ĥ: R186_004 writes 1 ± 2^{∓1/2} and here one gets 1 ± 2^{±1/2}. Neither vanishes, and the conclusion does not change.

The global constraints are the two conditions Σ_blocks ĥ_B(±i/2) = 0.

## 3. Coupling and localization of the near-kernel (NUMERICAL except 3.1)

**Composition of the near-kernel v (λ = 9.4·10⁻⁴), in ‖·‖²:** H_s 0.369, H_a 0.067 and M 0.564. Its projection onto E (defined below) is 0.805.

**Experiments removing couplings**, with global Tate and min of W:
- all removed (block-diagonal): **−0.637**;
- without s–M (norm 1.21): −0.707;
- without a–M (norm 0.79): **+0.085**;
- without s–a (norm 0.06): −0.012.

So the s–M coupling **sustains** positivity. The a–M one consumes it, ~0.084, which is approximately gap_∞ from R185_006. The s–a one is negligible.

**The block-Schur test fails.** ‖B_sM‖² ≈ 1.46 > 0.594·1.428 = 0.85. That block-Schur test cannot prove anything (NO-GO for the "margins + small coupling" route).

**Theorem 3.1 (exact reduction to a 4×4 secular problem; algebra PROVED, entries NUMERICAL).** Let 𝒱 ⊂ 𝒫_T be the direct sum of the three blocks with their sectoral Tate; it has codimension 6 in L²(I_T). Let E := 𝒫_T ⊖ 𝒱, of dimension 6 − 2 = 4. E is generated by the functions
e_B^± := e^{±x/2}·1_B (B = J, M, J′, in the coordinates H_s, H_a, M),
subject to the two global Tate relations. If W|_𝒱 ≥ c > 0, then

 W ≥ 0 over 𝒫_T ⟺ S := G_EE − G_E𝒱 (G_𝒱𝒱)^{−1} G_𝒱E ⪰ 0 (4×4 matrix),

and λ_min(W|𝒫_T) ≥ min(c, λ_min(S))·(angle constant) > 0 if S ≻ 0.

*Proof.* This is the standard Schur complement for the bounded form W over 𝒫_T = 𝒱 ⊕ E. The identification of E comes from the fact that, within each block, the orthogonal complement of the kernel of the two functionals ∫h e^{±x/2} is span{e^{±x/2}|_B}. ∎

**Values (NUMERICAL, stable in N):**
- G_EE is a 4×4 matrix over explicit elementary functions. Its eigenvalues are 0.1139, 0.2218, 1.4481 and 1.4820, with min 0.114.
- c = 0.5734 and ‖G_𝒱E‖ ≈ 0.91.
- The eigenvalues of S are **1.17·10⁻³**, 3.54·10⁻², 1.1855 and 1.2499.
- The critical vector of S is distributed as: 0.23 (H_s), 0.08 (H_a), 0.69 (M).

The cancellation is G_EE ≈ 0.114 against a correction of 0.113: **1%**. The 9.4·10⁻⁴ of W is λ_min of the full form. The 1.17·10⁻³ is λ_min(S), and the angle between 𝒱 and E reconciles them.

## 4. The remaining exact lemma (OPEN)

For an analytic proof at ½log3 the following is needed:

**(L1)** W|_𝒱 ≥ c > 0, with explicit c. The margin is 0.57, robust. It is a problem of three short intervals with their own Tate, with no fine cancellation.

**(L2)** The 4×4 secular problem: S ⪰ δ·I with δ > 0. This is equivalent to a certified upper bound on the four quadratic forms ⟨G_𝒱𝒱^{−1} g, g⟩ for g = W(·, e_B^±)|_𝒱, with relative error < 1%.

(L2) is not "small" in precision: it requires 1% accuracy in a resolvent. It is small in dimension. It is a dimension-4 version of the dimension-≤47 secular problem of R185_006 Thm 3.2, with the boundary vectors identified as **e^{±x/2} per block**. The natural approach is interval arithmetic: Galerkin with N ≈ 12 per block, plus the tail bound of R185_093 applied to G_𝒱𝒱, whose gap 0.57 makes the tail cheap.
