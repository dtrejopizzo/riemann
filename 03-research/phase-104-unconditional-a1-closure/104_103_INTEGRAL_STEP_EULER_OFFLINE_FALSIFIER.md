# 104_103 — Integer-step Euler falsifier with an off-line zero

## Result

This document asks whether the information not yet used by the energy of
`104_93` — integrality of the counting function's jumps, gaps, and exact
multiplicative composition — by itself excludes an excursion

\[
 {x^\beta\over\log x}\cos(\gamma\log x+\phi),
 \qquad {1\over2}<\beta<1.                                  \tag{1}
\]

The answer is **no**, even while preserving considerably more than in the
falsifier of `104_101`.

**Theorem A (step Euler system with a prescribed zero).** Let

\[
 \rho=\beta+i\gamma,
 \qquad {1\over2}<\beta<1,
 \qquad \gamma\ne0,                                        \tag{2}
\]

and let $W\ge1$ be a fixed integer. There exists a sequence
$a_n\in\{0,1\}$, supported on $(n,W)=1$, whose counting function

\[
 P(x)=\sum_{n\le x}a_n                                      \tag{3}
\]

satisfies

\[
 P(x)=\mathrm{Li}(x)
      -2\Re\!\int_{X_0}^x{t^{\rho-1}\over\log t}\,dt+O(1)
      =\mathrm{Li}(x)
       -2\Re{x^\rho\over\rho\log x}
       +O\!\left({x^\beta\over\log ^2x}\right).           \tag{4}
\]

The consecutive gaps of the support are $O_{W,\rho}(\log x)$. In
particular the system satisfies PNT and any fixed Vinogradov–Korobov-type
envelope.

Declare each $n$ with $a_n=1$ an abstract prime generator of norm
$n$, and form the free commutative monoid generated by them. Its Euler
zeta is

\[
 Z_{\mathcal P}(s)=\prod_{a_n=1}(1-n^{-s})^{-1},
 \qquad \Re s>1.                                           \tag{5}
\]

Then (5) admits local meromorphic continuation to neighborhoods of
$1,\rho,\bar\rho$, has a simple pole at $1$, and simple zeros at
$\rho$ and $\bar\rho$. Its Mangoldt weights are real and non-negative:

\[
 \Lambda_{\mathcal P}(m)
 =\sum_{\substack{a_n=1,\ k\ge1\\n^k=m}}\log n\ge0,
 \qquad
 -{Z_{\mathcal P}'\over Z_{\mathcal P}}(s)
 =\sum_{m\ge2}{\Lambda_{\mathcal P}(m)\over m^s}.          \tag{6}
\]

The composition is exact. If

\[
 Z_{\mathcal P}(s)=\sum_{m\ge1}{g(m)\over m^s},
 \qquad g(m)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},                            \tag{7}
\]

then, coefficient by coefficient,

\[
 \boxed{\quad g(m)\log m
 =\sum_{d\mid m}g(m/d)\Lambda_{\mathcal P}(d).\quad}        \tag{8}
\]

**Theorem B (exact rigidity of the ordinary norm).** For any free system
with integer generator norms, if the norm map to the ordinary monoid
$\mathbb N$ is injective, then its generator counting function satisfies

\[
 P_{\mathcal Q}(x)\le\pi(x)\qquad(x\ge2).                  \tag{8a}
\]

If the norm map is moreover surjective, the generators are exactly the
ordinary primes. Therefore any integer Euler deformation that preserves
**bijective** composition is impossible: the system is rigidly pinned
down. Norm collisions are the explicit escape used in Theorem A; in
general, any nontrivial deformation must lose injectivity or
surjectivity of the norm. Theorem B does not state which of the two
losses is forced in another model.

Finally, the corresponding prime energy violates the target of
`104_93` at a polynomial rate:

\[
 \sum_{m\le N}{\{P(m)-\mathrm{Li}(m)\}^2\over m(m+1)}
 \asymp_{W,\rho}{N^{2\beta-1}\over\log ^2N}.               \tag{9}
\]

**Exact boundary.** The theorem preserves $0$–$1$ jumps, integer
norms, logarithmic gaps, an arbitrary fixed wheel, PNT/VK, positive
Mangoldt weights, the Euler product, and unique factorization in the
abstract monoid. What it does not preserve is that the norm map be the
literal irreducible factorization of $\mathbb N$: a generator can have a
composite norm, and two distinct generalized integers can have the same
norm. Requiring the generators to be exactly the ordinary irreducibles
leaves, as in `104_101`, the *singleton* of the ordinary primes.

Thus, an exterior excursion is not incompatible with integrality,
step structure, gaps, or an abstract positive Euler product. For the
ordinary primes it can only be excluded using a property that
distinguishes their literal multiplicative embedding in $\mathbb N$.
That full property is, again, the prime sequence itself; the present
falsifier neither proves nor refutes (9) for the ordinary primes, A1, or RH.

## 1. Construction of the $0$–$1$ staircase

First choose two integers coprime to $W$, $q_1,q_2>1$, and declare
$q_1,q_2,q_1q_2$ generators in the finite prefix. This installs the
norm collision

\[
 N([q_1q_2])=N([q_1][q_2])=q_1q_2,                         \tag{9a}
\]

Also leave out of the prefix some ordinary prime $q_0$ distinct from
those three norms; this also makes the norm non-surjective. These finite
decisions affect no asymptotics nor any singularity in $\Re s>0$. Now
choose $X_0>q_1q_2$ large enough that, for $x\ge X_0$,

\[
 2x^{\beta-1}\le{1\over2},
 \qquad {3\over2\log x}< {1\over W}.                       \tag{10}
\]

Define the increasing real function

\[
 F(x)=\int_{X_0}^x
 {1-t^{\rho-1}-t^{\bar\rho-1}\over\log t}\,dt
 =\int_{X_0}^x
 {1-2t^{\beta-1}\cos(\gamma\log t)\over\log t}\,dt.      \tag{11}
\]

By (10),

\[
 {1\over2\log x}\le F'(x)\le {3\over2\log x}.            \tag{12}
\]

Enumerate the integers greater than $X_0$ and coprime to $W$:
$r_0<r_1<\cdots$. Since $r_j-r_{j-1}\le W$, raising $X_0$
if necessary, (12) gives

\[
 0<F(r_j)-F(r_{j-1})<1.                                   \tag{13}
\]

Set

\[
 a_{r_j}=\lfloor F(r_j)\rfloor-\lfloor F(r_{j-1})\rfloor,
 \qquad a_n=0\quad(n\ne r_j),                              \tag{14}
\]

and absorb the finite prefix into a constant. Equation (13) proves
$a_n\in\{0,1\}$. If $r(x)$ is the last candidate not exceeding
$x$, telescoping (14) gives

\[
 P(x)=\lfloor F(r(x))\rfloor+C=F(x)+O_W(1).                \tag{15}
\]

The last equality uses $0\le x-r(x)<W$ and (12).

For the gaps, take $y=x+8\log x+W$. For $x$ large,
$\log y\le2\log x$, and by (12)

\[
 F(y-W)-F(x)\ge {y-W-x\over2\log y}\ge2.                 \tag{16}
\]

There is a candidate between $y-W$ and $y$, and the floor of $F$ must
have jumped before $y$. This proves the $O(\log x)$ gap.

Complex integration by parts gives

\[
 \int_{X_0}^x{t^{\rho-1}\over\log t}\,dt
 ={x^\rho\over\rho\log x}
 +O_\rho\!\left({x^\beta\over\log ^2x}\right),           \tag{17}
\]

and (4) follows from (11), (15), and the definition of $\mathrm{Li}$,
with a base constant absorbed. For every $C>0$,

\[
 {x^\beta\over\log x}
 =o\!\left(xe^{-C(\log x)^{3/5}(\log\log x)^{-1/5}}\right),\tag{18}
\]

which justifies the PNT/VK claim.

## 2. Local continuation and the exterior zero

Let the prime zeta function be

\[
 \mathfrak p(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}{a_n\over n^s}
 =\int_{X_0^-}^{\infty}x^{-s}\,dP(x),
 \qquad \Re s>1.                                          \tag{19}
\]

Write $D(x)=P(x)-F(x)$. By (15), $D=O(1)$, and Stieltjes integration
by parts shows that

\[
 \int_{X_0^-}^{\infty}x^{-s}\,dD(x)
 =H_0(s)+s\int_{X_0}^{\infty}D(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx,             \tag{20}
\]

where the boundary term $H_0(s)$ is an entire function. The expression is
holomorphic in $\Re s>0$. With $L=\log X_0$, initially set

\[
 \mathscr E_L(z)=\int_L^\infty{e^{-zt}\over t}\,dt,
 \qquad \Re z>0.                                          \tag{21}
\]

Equations (11), (19), and the change $x=e^t$ give

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak p(s)=
 \mathscr E_L(s-1)-\mathscr E_L(s-\rho)
 -\mathscr E_L(s-\bar\rho)+H(s),}                         \tag{22}
\]

where $H$ is holomorphic in $\Re s>0$. Locally at $z=0$,

\[
 \mathscr E_L(z)=-\log z+h_L(z),                           \tag{23}
\]

with $h_L$ holomorphic. Hence, on small disjoint discs,

\[
 \mathfrak p(s)=
 \begin{cases}
  -\log(s-1)+H_1(s),&s\sim1,\\
  \phantom{-}\log(s-\rho)+H_\rho(s),&s\sim\rho,\\
  \phantom{-}\log(s-\bar\rho)+H_{\bar\rho}(s),&s\sim\bar\rho,
 \end{cases}                                               \tag{24}
\]

with the three remainders holomorphic.

In $\Re s>1$, the logarithm of the product (5) is

\[
 \log Z_{\mathcal P}(s)=
 \sum_{k\ge1}{\mathfrak p(ks)\over k}.                    \tag{25}
\]

Since $\beta>1/2$, for $s$ in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
$\rho$, all the terms $k\ge2$ of (25) are given by absolutely
convergent series and form a holomorphic function. From (24),

\[
 \log Z_{\mathcal P}(s)=\log(s-\rho)+K_\rho(s),
 \qquad
 Z_{\mathcal P}(s)=(s-\rho)e^{K_\rho(s)}.                 \tag{26}
\]

The coefficient of the logarithm is the integer $+1$, so its
monodromy disappears upon exponentiating: (26) is a single-valued
holomorphic continuation, not merely a formal branch. This proves the
simple zero. The conjugate argument proves the zero at $\bar\rho$, and
the integer coefficient $-1$ of (24) at $1$ proves, after
exponentiating, the single-valued simple pole.

## 3. Positive Mangoldt, exact composition, and norm rigidity

Expanding each geometric factor of (5) produces (7), where $g(m)$
counts, with multiplicity, the abstract factorizations whose norm is
$m$. Each coefficient is a non-negative integer. Differentiating (25) in
$\Re s>1$ produces (6). Multiplying

\[
 -Z_{\mathcal P}'(s)=
 Z_{\mathcal P}(s)\sum_{d\ge2}{\Lambda_{\mathcal P}(d)\over d^s}
                                                               \tag{27}
\]

and comparing the coefficient of $m^{-s}$ gives (8), since the
left-hand side is $g(m)\log m$. No analytic continuation is used in this
identity.

To prove Theorem B, factor each generator norm $q\le x$ into ordinary
primes and denote by

\[
 v_q=(v_p(q))_{p\le x}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\pi(x)}          \tag{27a}
\]

its exponent vector. If these vectors were linearly dependent over
$\mathbb Q$, clearing denominators and separating the positive and
negative parts would give two distinct products of generators with the
same norm. This contradicts injectivity. The $v_q$ are therefore linearly
independent, and their number does not exceed the dimension $\pi(x)$;
this proves (8a).

If the norm is also surjective and a generator had composite norm
$ab$ with $a,b>1$, surjectivity would lift $a,b$ to two generalized
integers. Their product and the generator would be distinct elements
with equal norm, against injectivity. Every generator norm is then an
ordinary prime. Conversely, every ordinary prime must be the norm of
some element; its free factorization can only have one generator, which
by injectivity is unique. This recovers exactly the ordinary prime
system.

The corresponding Chebyshev function also directly exhibits the
planted zero. By Stieltjes summation and (11),

\[
 \vartheta_{\mathcal P}(x)
 :=\sum_{n\le x}a_n\log n
 =x-{x^\rho\over\rho}-{x^{\bar\rho}\over\bar\rho}+O(\log x).
                                                               \tag{28}
\]

The powers $k\ge2$ contribute $O(\sqrt x\log^2x)$, so

\[
 \psi_{\mathcal P}(x)
 =x-2\Re{x^\rho\over\rho}+O(\sqrt x\log^2x).              \tag{29}
\]

The exterior oscillation, the zero of (26), and the positive weights of
(6) are therefore three faces of the same exact system.

## 4. Polynomial energy

From (4) and (17), uniformly for $x$ large,

\[
 P(x)-\mathrm{Li}(x)
 =-{2x^\beta\over|\rho|\log x}
   \cos(\gamma\log x-\arg\rho)
  +O_\rho\!\left({x^\beta\over\log^2x}\right).            \tag{30}
\]

The upper bound of (9) is immediate. For the lower bound, each interval
of fixed length in the variable $u=\log x$ contains a subinterval of
fixed length where the cosine in (30) has modulus at least $1/2$. On the
last such subinterval contained in
$[\log N-C_\gamma,\log N]$, the weight
$e^{(2\beta-1)u}/u^2$ is comparable to its value at $u=\log N$.
The sum–integral comparison gives

\[
 \sum_{m\le N}{m^{2\beta-2}\over\log^2m}
 \cos^2(\gamma\log m-\arg\rho)
 \asymp_\rho {N^{2\beta-1}\over\log^2N}.                  \tag{31}
\]

The error in (30) is smaller by a factor of $\log N$, and (9) follows.

## 5. What it decides and what it does not

`104_101` already builds a $0$–$1$ sequence with a fixed wheel,
logarithmic gaps, and polynomial energy. Theorem A adds two constraints
not simultaneously present there:

1. an exact Euler product with non-negative integer coefficients;
2. non-negative Mangoldt weights and the exact composition (8), while
   a concrete exterior zero is prescribed.

The construction is not a repetition of Hurwitz, Nyman–Beurling, Weil
positivity, or a continuous model of $\psi$. Nor is bibliographic
novelty claimed for the generalized-primes philosophy; the new content
**within this phase** is the explicit falsifier linking (4), (6), (8), (9),
and (26).

The result rules out one specific inference:

\[
 \substack{0\text{-}1\text{ jumps}+\text{gaps}+\text{PNT/VK}
 +\text{positive Euler}+\text{abstract composition}}
 \quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{subpolynomial energy}.                              \tag{32}
\]

It does not rule out an identity that uses the fact that $n$ is
irreducible in the **ordinary** monoid $(\mathbb N,\cdot)$. But that
condition admits no deformation: it determines $a_n=\mathbf1_{\mathbb P}(n)$
coordinate by coordinate. Proving the energy on that singleton remains
exactly the open goal.
