# 104_21 — Global compound-Poisson gate and total positivity

**Role.** Subject the surviving global gate of `104_20` to the natural
positive structures of the complete multiplicative convolution:
independence over primes, compound-Poisson representation, PF/TP,
log-concavity, decreasing variation, and representation by a positive
Laplace measure.

**Verdict.** The normalized arithmetic law is indeed compound-Poisson and
admits an exact stochastic coupling as \(u\) varies. However, its kernel on
the exponent lattice fails PF2 with a rational two-prime minor, and the
**real completed** cocycle fails complete monotonicity with an explicitly
negative inverse-Laplace density before the first arithmetic atom. Hence the
sign of \(g_{n,\varepsilon,c}\) cannot be obtained via a global positive
measure, PF\(_\infty\), TP, or the stochastic order of this law. This
neither refutes the sign of \(g_{n,\varepsilon,c}\) nor proves A1 or RH.

## 1. Normalized arithmetic law

Fix \(0<u<\varepsilon\), \(s_0=1+\varepsilon\), and normalize the
coefficients of `104_19`:

\[
 \mathbb P(N=m)
 ={b_u(m)m^{-1-\varepsilon}\over A_u(1+\varepsilon)^2},
 \qquad
 A_u(s)={\zeta(s-u)\over\zeta(s)}.                 \tag{1}
\]

Since \(b_u\) is multiplicative, if

\[
 N=\prod_p p^{K_p},                                \tag{2}
\]

the exponents \(K_p\) are independent. For

\[
 r=p^{-1-\varepsilon},\qquad Q=p^u,\qquad
 R=Qr=p^{-1-(\varepsilon-u)},                      \tag{3}
\]

the normalized local generating function is

\[
 \boxed{
 G_p(z)=\mathbb E z^{K_p}
 =\left({1-R\over1-r}\right)^2
  \left({1-rz\over1-Rz}\right)^2.}                \tag{4}
\]

Each square-root factor is a probability generating function. Indeed,
\(K_p=X_{p,1}+X_{p,2}\), with independent copies of

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
  \mathbb P(X_p=0)&={1-R\over1-r},\\
  \mathbb P(X_p=k)&={1-R\over1-r}(R-r)R^{k-1},
       \qquad k\ge1.
 \end{aligned}}                                    \tag{5}
\]

The mass in (5) sums exactly to one. This is a zero-inflated geometric,
not an approximation.

## 2. Global compound-Poisson identity

Taking logarithms in (4),

\[
 \log G_p(z)
 =2\sum_{j\ge1}{R^j-r^j\over j}(z^j-1).           \tag{6}
\]

Hence \(X=\log N\) is a compound-Poisson variable over the additive
semigroup generated by the \(\log p\), with Lévy measure

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_A
 =2\sum_p\sum_{j\ge1}
 {p^{-j(1+\varepsilon-u)}-p^{-j(1+\varepsilon)}\over j}
 \delta_{j\log p}.}                                \tag{7}
\]

The mass series of (7) converges for \(u<\varepsilon\). Formula (7) is
the product of operators over **all** primes: interactions between distinct
primes arise when exponentiating their sum; they are not discarded tower by
tower.

Moreover, if \(0<u_1<u_2<\varepsilon\), then

\[
 \nu_A(u_2)-\nu_A(u_1)\ge0.                        \tag{8}
\]

Hence one can couple

\[
 X(u_2)=X(u_1)+Z_{u_1,u_2},                        \tag{9}
\]

where the increment \(Z_{u_1,u_2}\ge0\) is compound-Poisson and
independent. The arithmetic law grows in convolution order and, in
particular, in stochastic order. This fact orders only monotone functions
(and their convex refinements); the Laguerre functions in the gate change
sign and do not belong to those classes.

The Mecke identity associated with (7), for every function for which both
sides converge, is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E[X\varphi(X)]
 =\int_0^\infty y\,\mathbb E[\varphi(X+y)]\,\nu_A(dy).} \tag{10}
\]

This is the concrete global successor of the local square: it keeps all
the other primes inside the expectation on the right. It has no sign for an
oscillatory Laguerre test function.

## 3. PF2 already fails at a single prime coordinate

Let

\[
 a_k=b_u(p^k)r^k\qquad(k\ge0).                     \tag{11}
\]

Using the exact formula of `104_19`,

\[
 \boxed{a_1^2-a_0a_2=r^2(Q-1)(Q-3).}              \tag{12}
\]

Consequently, for \(1<Q<3\), the sequence is neither log-concave nor PF2.
In particular it is not PF\(_\infty\), and the Euler product cannot be
justified as a decreasing-variation operator via local TP factors.

There is an entirely rational witness already involving two primes. Take

\[
 u=1,\qquad\varepsilon=2,\qquad c={1\over2},       \tag{13}
\]

and write \(w(m)=b_1(m)m^{-3}\). Then

\[
 w(3)={4\over27},\qquad
 w(6)={1\over27},\qquad
 w(12)={5\over432}.                                \tag{14}
\]

The PF2 minor on the fiber \(3\cdot2^k\) equals

\[
 \boxed{
 \det\begin{pmatrix}w(6)&w(12)\\w(3)&w(6)\end{pmatrix}
 =w(6)^2-w(3)w(12)=-{1\over2916}<0.}               \tag{15}
\]

The normalization in (1) multiplies (15) by a positive constant and does
not change its sign. The witness does not replace the arithmetic with an
abstract divisor: it uses the real primes \(2\) and \(3\).

## 4. Lévy measures of the Gamma and polar channels

The normalized Gamma ratio is the Laplace transform of

\[
 \eta=-{1\over2}\log V,
 \qquad
 V\sim {\rm Beta}\left({3+\varepsilon-u\over2},{u\over2}\right).
                                                               \tag{16}
\]

Its Lévy measure is

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_K(x)\,dx
 ={e^{-(3+\varepsilon-u)x}-e^{-(3+\varepsilon)x}
   \over x(1-e^{-2x})}\,dx.}                       \tag{17}
\]

By contrast, the normalized polar factor is the inverse of the Laplace
transform of \(P_1+P_2\), where the \(P_i\) are independent and

\[
 \mathbb P(P_i=0)=1-c,\qquad
 \mathbb P(P_i\in dx)=c(\varepsilon-u)e^{-(\varepsilon-u)x}\,dx.
                                                               \tag{18}
\]

The Lévy measure of that sum is

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_P(x)\,dx
 =2{e^{-(\varepsilon-u)x}-e^{-\varepsilon x}\over x}\,dx.}   \tag{19}
\]

Hence the logarithm of the normalized completed cocycle has the **signed**
Lévy measure

\[
 \boxed{\nu_{\mathcal S}=\nu_A+\nu_K-\nu_P.}       \tag{20}
\]

The subtraction in (19) already warns that (9) does not automatically
transfer to the completed cocycle. The next section proves something
stronger: not even another representation of its transform by a positive
measure exists.

## 5. Global counterexample to complete monotonicity

With the rational parameters (13), the Beta variable in (16) has
\(V\sim{\rm Beta}(2,1/2)\), and the normalized density of \(\eta\) is

\[
 f(x)={3\over2}{e^{-4x}\over\sqrt{1-e^{-2x}}},
 \qquad x>0.                                       \tag{21}
\]

Before normalizing by its value at \(t=0\), the inverse transform of the
polar factor is

\[
 \delta_0-2e^{-2x}\,dx+xe^{-2x}\,dx.              \tag{22}
\]

The absolutely continuous part of the convolution of (21) and (22) is

\[
 h(x)=f(x)-2\int_0^xe^{-2y}f(x-y)\,dy
          +\int_0^xye^{-2y}f(x-y)\,dy.             \tag{23}
\]

Setting \(Q=\sqrt{1-e^{-2x}}\), the two integrals are computed exactly,
with no approximation:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^xe^{-2y}f(x-y)\,dy
   &={3\over2}e^{-2x}Q,\\
 \int_0^xye^{-2y}f(x-y)\,dy
   &={3\over2}e^{-2x}(\mathrm{artanh}\,Q-Q).
 \end{aligned}                                     \tag{24}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 h(x)={3\over2}e^{-2x}
 \left({1\over Q}-4Q+\mathrm{artanh}\,Q\right).}          \tag{25}
\]

At the exact point \(x_0=\frac12\log2\), one has \(Q=1/\sqrt2\) and

\[
 {1\over Q}-4Q+\mathrm{artanh}\,Q
 =-\sqrt2+\mathrm{artanh}(1/\sqrt2)<0.       \tag{26}
\]

The last inequality is elementary and strict:

\[
 \mathrm{artanh}\,q
 =q\int_0^1{ds\over1-q^2s^2}
 <2q=\sqrt2,
 \qquad q={1\over\sqrt2}.                         \tag{27}
\]

Now restore the global arithmetic factor (1). Its law has positive mass at
\(X=0\) and no support on \(0<X<\log2\). Since
\(0<x_0<\log2\), the inverse-Laplace density of the **complete** cocycle at
\(x_0\) equals \(h(x_0)\) multiplied by the mass at zero and by positive
normalization constants. By (26), it is strictly negative.

Uniqueness of the Laplace transform for finite signed measures prevents
replacing this inverse by another positive measure. By Bernstein's
theorem,

\[
 \boxed{
 t\longmapsto{\mathcal S_1(3+t)\over\mathcal S_1(3)}
 \quad\hbox{is not completely monotone for }t\ge0.}          \tag{28}
\]

This is a witness on the Euler product and the real Gamma and polar
factors, not on an off-line model.

## 6. Exact scope and successor

The above results close the following routes:

1. PF\(_\infty\), TP, and decreasing variation obtained by factoring the
   product across prime coordinates: killed by (12)–(15).
2. Representing the completed cocycle as the transform of a positive
   probability and applying stochastic order: killed by (28).
3. Directly transferring the increasing coupling (9) to the Laguerre
   coefficients: the polar factor is a deconvolution, and the oscillatory
   test does not preserve that order.

It has not been proved that \(g_{n,\varepsilon,c}\) has a positive,
negative, or changing sign. In particular, (28) does not imply that any
of the gate's coefficients has the wrong sign.

The most concrete remaining global successor is (10): a Stein–Mecke
identity applied **after** keeping Gamma, the pole, and the expectation
over all the other primes. To supply a new bound it would have to prove a
special signed inequality for the completed Laguerre tests. Applying
absolute value to (10) returns the already-recorded loss, and replacing
those tests with monotone functions returns to a class that does not
contain A1. No such inequality is claimed here.
