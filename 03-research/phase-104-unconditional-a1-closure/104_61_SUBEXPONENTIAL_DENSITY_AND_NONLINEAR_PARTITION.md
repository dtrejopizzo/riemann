# 104_61 — Subexponential density criterion and nonlinear arithmetic partition

**Status.** This document again reduces the quantifier needed to
prove RH. The quartic margin \(4\lambda _n>A_n\) is not necessary: a
polynomial lower bound suffices —even the constant bound \(\lambda_n\ge-1\)—
outside an exception of zero logarithmic density, or on consecutive
blocks of unbounded length.

Two nonlinear observables are also constructed. The main one is a
bounded Fermi–Dirac-type partition: it detects a positive density of
excursions without requiring a bound at every degree. The second is an
unbounded exponential partition that admits an especially simple
arithmetic factorization, but which does impose coefficient-by-coefficient
control and is hence a stronger auxiliary criterion. Both jointly keep the
pole, the archimedean factor, and all the weights \(\Lambda(m)\) together
before applying the nonlinearity. The identities are exact; the upper
bounds equivalent to RH are not proved.

---

## 1. Single input: the dominant modes

We will use Theorem 3.1 of `104_56`. If RH is false, there are constants

\[
 R>1,\qquad c>0,\qquad n_0\ge1                         \tag{1}
\]

and a set \(D\subset\mathbb N\) that is syndetic and has natural
density \(d>0\), such that

\[
 \boxed{\lambda_n\le-cR^n\qquad(n\in D,\ n\ge n_0).} \tag{2}
\]

The proof of (2) does not assume a random distribution of phases. It
starts from the finite number of exterior modes of maximal modulus and
from the returns of a rotation on the compact group generated by their
phases. By partial summation, \(D\)
also has logarithmic density \(d\).

Under RH, on the other hand, Li's criterion gives

\[
 \lambda_n\ge0\qquad(n\ge1).                       \tag{3}
\]

Everything that follows is a consequence of the dichotomy (2)--(3).

---

## 2. A criterion with any subexponential barrier

Let \(b=(b_n)_{n\ge1}\) be a nonnegative sequence such that

\[
 \boxed{\log(1+b_n)=o(n).}                         \tag{4}
\]

This includes \(b_n=1\), \(b_n=Cn^B\) and
\(b_n=\exp(o(n))\).

**Theorem 2.1 (weak-barrier criterion).** For every sequence \(b\)
satisfying (4), the following are equivalent:

1. RH;
2. there exists \(E\subset\mathbb N\), of zero upper logarithmic density,
   such that

   \[
    \lambda_n\ge-b_n                              \tag{5}
   \]

   for all sufficiently large \(n\) outside \(E\);
3. there exist consecutive integer intervals \(I_j\), with
   \(|I_j|\to\infty\), on which (5) holds at every index.

**Proof.** RH implies (5) with \(E=\varnothing\), by (3), and allows
taking arbitrarily long intervals.

Now suppose RH is false. Set \(r=\log R>0\). From (4), for all
sufficiently large \(n\),

\[
 b_n\le e^{rn/2}<cR^n.                             \tag{6}
\]

The second inequality only requires raising the threshold to absorb \(c\).
By (2), (5) fails at every sufficiently large element of \(D\).
Since \(D\) has logarithmic density \(d>0\), it cannot be contained,
except for finitely many elements, in a set \(E\) of zero upper
logarithmic density. This contradicts 2.

Since \(D\) is syndetic, there is \(L\) such that every interval of \(L\)
sufficiently large integers contains an element of \(D\). (5) fails at
that element. Hence the lengths of the good blocks are bounded,
contradicting 3. \(\square\)

In particular:

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathrm{RH}
 \iff \lambda_n\ge-1\text{ on consecutive blocks of unbounded length}.}
                                                               \tag{7}
\]

And, for any \(C>0\), \(B\ge0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathrm{RH}
 \iff \lambda_n\ge-Cn^B
 \text{ outside an exception of zero logarithmic density}.} \tag{8}
\]

These conditions are strictly weaker, as statements about an arbitrary
sequence, than \(4\lambda_n>A_n\). They become equivalent to RH
only through the exponential rigidity of the dominant modes of the Li
coefficients.

An intrinsic form of (8) is

\[
 \mathrm{RH}\iff
 \exists E,\ \overline\delta_{\log}(E)=0:\quad
 \limsup_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\notin E}}
 {1\over n}\log\bigl(1+\lambda_n^-\bigr)=0,
 \qquad \lambda_n^-:=\max\{-\lambda_n,0\}.          \tag{9}
\]

## 3. Nonlinear logistic detector

Define

\[
 x_n(b):=(-\lambda_n-b_n)_+,
 \qquad
 \Phi(x):={x\over1+x},                              \tag{10}
\]

and, with \(H_X=\sum_{n\le X}1/n\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak M_b(X):={1\over H_X}
 \sum_{n\le X}{1\over n}\Phi\!\left(x_n(b)\right).} \tag{11}
\]

This is not an Abel average of the generating function. It first applies
a nonlinear barrier to the complete coefficient and only then averages
in the **degree** \(n\).

**Theorem 3.1 (bounded average criterion).** For every \(b\) satisfying
(4),

\[
 \boxed{\mathrm{RH}\iff \mathfrak M_b(X)\longrightarrow0.}   \tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Under RH, \(x_n(b)=0\) for all \(n\), so (11) is
identically zero. If RH is false, then (2) and (6) give
\(x_n(b)\to\infty\) along \(D\). Hence
\(\Phi(x_n(b))\to1\) on \(D\). Since \(D\) has logarithmic density
\(d\),

\[
 \liminf_{X\to\infty}\mathfrak M_b(X)\ge d>0.       \tag{13}
\]

This contradicts the right-hand side of (12). \(\square\)

Detector (11) only asks to exclude a positive logarithmic proportion of
subexponentially deep excursions. It does not attempt to recover the
sign of each coefficient.

### 3.1 Bounded Fermi–Dirac partition

Suppose further that \(b_n\to\infty\), and fix \(t>0\). Define

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak F_{t,b}(X):={1\over H_X}\sum_{n\le X}{1\over n}
 {1\over1+\exp\!\bigl(t(\lambda_n+b_n)\bigr)}.}     \tag{13a}
\]

**Theorem 3.2 (bounded partition).** For every nonnegative barrier
satisfying (4) with \(b_n\to\infty\), and for any \(t>0\),

\[
 \boxed{\mathrm{RH}\iff\mathfrak F_{t,b}(X)\longrightarrow0.} \tag{13b}
\]

**Proof.** Under RH, \(\lambda_n\ge0\), so the bounded term of
(13a) is at most \((1+e^{tb_n})^{-1}\to0\). Its logarithmic average
tends to zero. If RH is false, (2) and (4) give
\(\lambda_n+b_n\to-\infty\) on \(D\). The term then tends to one
on a set of logarithmic density \(d\), and

\[
 \liminf_{X\to\infty}\mathfrak F_{t,b}(X)\ge d>0.  \tag{13c}
\]

This formulation is smooth and, unlike the exponential partition of the
next section, is bounded by one. Hence an isolated spike cannot replace a
positive density of spikes. The concrete choice
\(b_n=\log(n+1)\) already satisfies all the hypotheses.

The relaxation is genuine for arbitrary sequences. For example, define

\[
 a_n=n\quad(n\ne2^k),\qquad a_{2^k}=-e^{2^k}.     \tag{13d}
\]

Substituting \(\lambda_n\) by \(a_n\) in (13a), the exceptional indices
contribute to the unnormalized sum at most
\(\sum_k2^{-k}<\infty\), and the remaining ones contribute a convergent
series. So the Fermi average tends to zero, even though (13d) has
infinitely many exponential negative excursions. What excludes those
excursions for the Li coefficients is not the detector by itself, but
Theorem 3.1 of `104_56`: under \(\neg\mathrm{RH}\) they cannot be
sparse, but must contain a set of positive logarithmic density.

## 4. Auxiliary exponential criterion

For \(t>0\), set

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal Z_t(X):=\sum_{n\le X}{e^{-t\lambda_n}\over n}.}     \tag{14}
\]

**Theorem 4.1 (partition criterion).** The following are equivalent:

1. RH;
2. for every \(t>0\) and every \(X\ge1\),
   \(\mathcal Z_t(X)\le H_X\);
3. there exists \(t>0\) such that

   \[
    \boxed{\mathcal Z_t(X)=O_t(\log X).}             \tag{15}
   \]

**Proof.** Equation (3) implies
\(e^{-t\lambda_n}\le1\), so 1 implies 2, and 2 implies 3. If RH is
false, choose a sequence \(n_j\to\infty\) in \(D\). By (2),

\[
 \mathcal Z_t(n_j)\ge{1\over n_j}
 \exp\!\left(tcR^{n_j}\right),                    \tag{16}
\]

which contradicts (15) for every \(t>0\). \(\square\)

Unlike (11) and (13a), (14) is not a bounded detector: it amplifies a
single negative excursion. Indeed, (15) implies, for each degree,

\[
 {e^{-t\lambda_n}\over n}\ll_t\log n,
 \qquad
 \lambda_n\ge-{1\over t}
 \bigl(\log n+\log\log n+O_t(1)\bigr).             \tag{16a}
\]

Hence (14)--(15) **do not exploit** the density relaxation: they
constitute a coefficient-by-coefficient criterion, albeit weaker than
positivity. Their advantage is purely algebraic: they admit the exact
factorization of the next section.

## 5. Exact arithmetic form before the Abel limit

For \(\varepsilon>0\), define

\[
 p_n(\varepsilon)
 :=n\sum_{k=1}^n{n-1\choose k-1}
 {(-1)^{k-1}\over k\varepsilon^k},                 \tag{17}
\]

\[
 \mathscr E_{n,\varepsilon}
 :=p_n(\varepsilon)
 -\sum_{m\ge2}{\Lambda(m)\over m^{1+\varepsilon}}
 L_{n-1}^{(1)}(\log m).                            \tag{18}
\]

The prime–Laguerre ledger identity gives

\[
 \lambda_n=A_n+\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \mathscr E_{n,\varepsilon}.                       \tag{19}
\]

The new letter is deliberate. In `104_30`,
\(P_{n,\varepsilon}\) denotes the opposite difference
\(\sum_m\Lambda(m)m^{-1-\varepsilon}L_{n-1}^{(1)}(\log m)
-p_n(\varepsilon)\), whose limit is \(A_n-\lambda_n\). Hence
\(\mathscr E_{n,\varepsilon}=-P_{n,\varepsilon}^{\text{`104_30`}}\);
(19) coincides exactly with `104_28`--`104_30` and introduces no second
sign convention for \(P\).

For \(\varepsilon>0\), the series of (18) converges absolutely. Since the
outer sum is finite, (14) and (19) imply the rigorous identity

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal Z_t(X)
 &=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \sum_{n\le X}{e^{-t(A_n+p_n(\varepsilon))}\over n}
 \exp\!\left(
 t\sum_{m\ge2}{\Lambda(m)\over m^{1+\varepsilon}}
 L_{n-1}^{(1)}(\log m)\right)\\
 &=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \sum_{n\le X}{e^{-t(A_n+p_n(\varepsilon))}\over n}
 \prod_{m\ge2}
 \exp\!\left(
 {t\Lambda(m)\over m^{1+\varepsilon}}
 L_{n-1}^{(1)}(\log m)\right).
\end{aligned}}                                                   \tag{20}
\]

The second line is an absolutely convergent product for each
\(\varepsilon>0\). All of its factors are positive real numbers, even
though their exponents change sign with the Laguerre kernel.

This representation is effectively truncable. Indeed,

\[
 L_{n-1}^{(1)}(u)=
 \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k+1}{(-u)^k\over k!}
\]

gives, for \(u\ge0\),

\[
 |L_{n-1}^{(1)}(u)|
 \le 3\,2^n(1+u)^{n-1}.                            \tag{20a}
\]

Indeed, each \(u^k/k!\le(1+u)^{n-1}\) for
\(0\le k\le n-1\), and
\(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k+1}=2^n-1\); the factor \(3\) of (20a) is
therefore slack and not a hidden asymptotic constant.

Using \(\Lambda(m)\le\log m\), for every integer
\(M\ge e^n\) the tail of the exponent satisfies

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{m>M}{\Lambda(m)\over m^{1+\varepsilon}}
 |L_{n-1}^{(1)}(\log m)|
 \le3\,2^n\int_{\log M}^{\infty}
 e^{-\varepsilon u}u(1+u)^{n-1}\,du.}              \tag{20b}
\]

To close the integral test, set
\(f(x)=x^{-1-\varepsilon}\log x(1+\log x)^{n-1}\). If
\(u=\log x\ge n\), then

\[
 {d\over du}\log f(e^u)
 ={1\over u}+{n-1\over1+u}-(1+\varepsilon)
 \le-\varepsilon<0,                                \tag{20b'}
\]

because \(u^{-1}+(n-1)/(1+u)\le1\) is equivalent to
\(u(u-n+1)\ge1\). Hence
\(\sum_{m>M}f(m)\le\int_M^\infty f(x)\,dx\); the change
\(x=e^u\) produces exactly (20b), with no term omitted. The
right-hand side is a finite combination of incomplete gammas and
effectively tends to zero. Thus, for
finite \(X,\varepsilon,M\), (20) admits interval arithmetic with an
explicit tail; the non-uniformity lies solely in
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

The bounded partition (13a) has the parallel regulated representation

\[
\boxed{
 \mathfrak F_{t,b}(X)
 =\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}{1\over H_X}
 \sum_{n\le X}{1\over n}
 \left[1+e^{t(A_n+b_n+p_n(\varepsilon))}
 \prod_{m\ge2}\exp\!\left(
 -{t\Lambda(m)\over m^{1+\varepsilon}}
 L_{n-1}^{(1)}(\log m)\right)\right]^{-1}.}        \tag{20c}
\]

For each \(\varepsilon>0\), the product of (20c) converges absolutely
in the sense \(\sum_m|\log(\text{factor}_m)|<\infty\); the tail of its
logarithm is bounded by \(t\) times (20b). Passing to the limit is
legitimate because the outer sum is finite and the logistic function is
continuous. This is the representation that preserves the density
content of `104_56`: directly proving that (20c) tends to zero would
prove RH without producing an individual bound for each \(\lambda_n\).

Equations (20) and (20c) are the exact arithmetic bridges:

* they keep the pole \(p_n(\varepsilon)\), the archimedean block \(A_n\),
  the primes, and all the prime powers together until after forming the
  observable;
* they take no absolute values of the kernel;
* they create multiplicative interactions among prime powers before
  averaging in \(n\);
* they fall outside the additive-polynomial classifier of `104_53` and
  outside the linear Abel average of `104_17`.

By Theorem 3.2, the front that exploits the density consists in directly
proving from (20c), for a fixed \(t>0\) and e.g.
\(b_n=\log(n+1)\),

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak F_{t,b}(X)\longrightarrow0.}       \tag{20d}
\]

As a stronger auxiliary criterion, Theorem 4.1 allows attempting in (20)

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal Z_t(X)\ll_t\log X.}               \tag{21}
\]

Statements (20d) and (21) are unconditionally equivalent to RH; the
representations prove neither.

## 6. Three immediate bridges that fail

### 6.1 Jensen has the wrong direction

The function \(x\mapsto e^{-tx}\) is convex. Jensen gives a **lower
bound**

\[
 {1\over H_X}\sum_{n\le X}{e^{-t\lambda_n}\over n}
 \ge
 \exp\!\left(-{t\over H_X}\sum_{n\le X}{\lambda_n\over n}\right), \tag{22}
\]

not the upper bound (21). Controlling a linear average of \(\lambda_n\)
does not control the partition.

### 6.2 Separating pole and prime powers destroys the limit

For fixed \(n\),

\[
 L_{n-1}^{(1)}(u)
 ={(-1)^{n-1}\over(n-1)!}u^{n-1}+O_n(u^{n-2}).      \tag{23}
\]

PNT and partial summation give

\[
 \sum_{m\ge2}{\Lambda(m)\over m^{1+\varepsilon}}
 \left|L_{n-1}^{(1)}(\log m)\right|
 \asymp_n\varepsilon^{-n}\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).     \tag{24}
\]

Hence a factor-by-factor majorization, or one via the absolute value of
the exponent of (20), costs

\[
 \exp(c_nt\varepsilon^{-n}),                       \tag{25}
\]

while the paired limit of (20) is finite. The cancellation between
\(p_n(\varepsilon)\) and the Mangoldt sum must be preserved within the
exponent; it cannot be recovered after (25).

### 6.3 Prior Abel positivity does not control the partition

The rational falsifier of `104_17`,

\[
 \rho={1+2i\over5},\qquad w=2i,                    \tag{26}
\]

has quadruple coefficients

\[
 Q_n=4-2\mathrm{Re}(w^n+w^{-n}),             \tag{27}
\]

and its rational Abel germ is positive at every \(0<q<1\). However,

\[
 Q_{4k}=4-2(2^{4k}+2^{-4k})<-16k^2=-(4k)^2         \tag{28}
\]

for every \(k\ge1\). In particular, the (14)-version of the quadruple has
doubly exponential spikes on a class of density \(1/4\). So the
positive radial information of `104_17` cannot imply (21), not even
after allowing a zero-density exception. The bounded partition also
detects the falsifier: for every \(b_n\to\infty\) with
\(\log(1+b_n)=o(n)\), the term of (13a), with \(\lambda_n\) replaced
by \(Q_n\), tends to one on \(n\equiv0\pmod4\) and to zero on the other three
classes. Consequently its logarithmic average tends to \(1/4\), not to
zero. Abel positivity also does not imply (20d).

The strict inequality of (28) is elementary: if
\(F(k)=2\,16^k-4-16k^2\), then \(F(1)=12>0\) and

\[
 F(k+1)-F(k)=30\,16^k-16(2k+1)>0\qquad(k\ge1).
\]

Moreover \(Q_{4k}<4-2\,16^k=-16k^2-F(k)\).

The three points above are no-gos for concrete transfers. They are not
an impossibility theorem for a new inequality applied directly
to the paired functional of (20c).

## 7. Result and new front

**Proved:**

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
 \mathrm{RH}
 \iff \lambda_n\ge-b_n
 \text{ outside an exception of zero logarithmic density},\\
 \mathrm{RH}
 \iff \lambda_n\ge-b_n
 \text{ on consecutive blocks of unbounded length},\\
 \mathrm{RH}
 \iff \mathfrak M_b(X)\to0,\\
 \mathrm{RH}
 \iff \mathfrak F_{t,b}(X)\to0
 \quad(b_n\to\infty),\\
 \mathrm{RH}
 \iff \mathcal Z_t(X)\ll_t\log X\quad\text{for some }t>0,
\end{gathered}}
\tag{29}
\]

for every barrier \(b_n\) satisfying \(\log(1+b_n)=o(n)\).

**Arithmetic connection proved:** the exact partitions (20) and (20c), with
all channels paired.

**Not proved:** bound (21), the limits (12) or (20d) for the real
zeta coefficients, A1, or RH.

The main target does not ask for positivity, proportional coercivity, nor
coefficient-by-coefficient control. It asks for the vanishing of the
bounded prime–pole partition (20c), a logarithmic density bound in the
degree. The exponential partition (20)--(21) remains a stronger
algebraic auxiliary and should not be confused with that relaxation.
Neither one escapes the discarded generators; both still carry RH
strength and need a new arithmetic idea.
