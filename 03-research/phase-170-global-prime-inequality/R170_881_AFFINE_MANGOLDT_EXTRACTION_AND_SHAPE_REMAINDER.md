## 0. Result

The moment law of R170_880 extracts exactly `Lambda(k)` from the
affine component of any exterior test. It also shows precisely
what it does not control.

For `k>1`, define

\[
 \mathcal M_{T,k}[\varphi]
 :=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak m_{T,t}(k)\varphi(t)\,dt.      \tag{0.1}
\]

For any scalars `a,c`,

\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal M_{T,k}[\varphi]
 =2T\,a\Lambda(k)
  +\mathcal R_{T,k}[\varphi;a,c],}                         \tag{0.2}
\]

where

\[
 \mathcal R_{T,k}[\varphi;a,c]
 :=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak m_{T,t}(k)
       \bigl(\varphi(t)-at-c\bigr)\,dt.                   \tag{0.3}
\]

The constants disappear by the zero mass and the slope produces
Mangoldt. Hence the exterior residue of R170_853 would close via
R170_880 only if PC/minimality jointly cancels or signs the shape
residues (0.3) for the physical tests generated by `z_F`.

## 1. Proof

Expand

\[
 \varphi(t)=at+c+\bigl(\varphi(t)-at-c\bigr).              \tag{1.1}
\]

R170_880 gives, for `k>1`,

\[
 \int\mathfrak m_{T,t}(k)\,dt=0,
 \qquad
 \int t\mathfrak m_{T,t}(k)\,dt=2T\Lambda(k).             \tag{1.2}
\]

Inserting (1.1) in (0.1) proves (0.2)--(0.3).

## 2. The first moment does not determine the physical pairing

The function `m_T,.(k)` is a compactly supported step function,
not a combination of distributions that only observe mass and the first
moment. When it is not identically zero, there exists a smooth,
compactly supported function `psi` such that

\[
 \int\mathfrak m_{T,t}(k)\psi(t)\,dt\ne0,                 \tag{2.1}
\]

even choosing `a=c=0`. For example, take `psi>=0` supported inside
a subinterval where `m_T,.(k)` has constant nonzero sign.

Hence (0.2) does not allow replacing a general test by its affine
part. The first-contact exterior is also not fixed by the
interior equation: R170_853 and the boundary-augmented freedom of R170_869 allow
modifying the exterior class while keeping the same interior radical
datum. Hence the moment identity, in isolation, does not prove `beta_phys=0`.

## 3. Typed application to the Wronskian

Suppose the literal route expansion of the Wronskian and the polar
term produces, for each `k`, a physical test `varphi_F,k(t)` such that

\[
 \mathfrak W_{\rm ext}(F)+\mathfrak P_{\rm pol}(F)
 =\sum_k{k^{-1/2}}\mathcal M_{T,k}[\varphi_{F,k}].          \tag{3.1}
\]

This disintegration is exactly the typing test left open in
R170_880; it is not assumed here to already hold. If it is proved, (0.2) gives

\[
\boxed{
 \mathfrak W_{\rm ext}+\mathfrak P_{\rm pol}
 =2T\sum_k{\Lambda(k)\over\sqrt k}a_{F,k}
  +\sum_k{k^{-1/2}}
       \mathcal R_{T,k}[\varphi_{F,k};a_{F,k},c_{F,k}].}    \tag{3.2}
\]

The first sum is the Mangoldt channel that must match the prime
virial for the choice of slopes `a_F,k` induced by the antidiagonal
derivation. The second sum is the **exterior shape residue**.

A sufficient new law can take either of these two forms:

\[
 \sum_k{k^{-1/2}}\mathcal R_{T,k}=0,                       \tag{3.3}
\]

or an inequality with the sign needed to absorb the already-positive
Wronskian energy. Equation (3.3) must use the first-contact equation
and the Gamma/polar port; it does not follow from Möbius alone.

## 4. Why this is a strict reduction

Before, the exterior residue was represented by an abstract
alternating form. R170_878--880 successively fix:

1. the predictor `q` as a Möbius exterior loop;
2. its coefficients as partial sums over divisors;
3. the affine component of those sums as exact `Lambda(k)`.

After verifying the corrected finite version of (3.1), all remaining
freedom includes both the quotient of the exterior tests by
`span{1,t}` and the divisor tail `n>N`. The quotient is not zero by
algebra, and the tail does not vanish by interior stabilization either.
Confusing either of them with zero would amount to assuming the sought law.
