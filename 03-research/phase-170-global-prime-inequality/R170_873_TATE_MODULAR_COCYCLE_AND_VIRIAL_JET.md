# R170_873 — The Tate modular cocycle and the virial jet

> **Novelty audit.** The term-by-term identities of this record are
> correct, but the jet they produce is not a new class: R170_845 already
> computes it as a moving Tate connection and R161_036/041 as the
> covariant jet of the two orientations. The contribution of this record is
> the exact dictionary between those jets and the arithmetic swap of
> R170_871. It does not prove a new vanishing. The pending equality is
> not the operator defect `d_N,T^Tate` of R170_845(4.1), retracted
> by R170_846, but the scalar `Delta_N,T^Tate` computed in R170_852 and
> its gauge-invariant class `W_K+Theta_ext` of R170_859.

## 0. Result

The asymmetry between the two discrete collars of R161_009 is not an
accidental coefficient. For each active prime power, with

\[
u_n=\log n,\qquad a_n=\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n},
\]

the two weights \(\Lambda(n)\) and \(\Lambda(n)/n\) are the
\(s=1/2\) endpoints of the bilateral cocycle

\[
\mathbf c_n(s)=a_n(e^{su_n},e^{-su_n}).                    \tag{0.1}
\]

Swapping the two orientations is exactly \(s\mapsto-s\).
Its derivative at the balanced point is

\[
\mathbf c_n'(0)
 =\frac{\Lambda(n)\log n}{\sqrt n}(1,-1).                  \tag{0.2}
\]

Hence the arithmetic coefficient of the virial should not be added to
the Tate port: it is its **antisymmetric modular jet**. This exactly
identifies the odd cycle of R170_871 with the virial jet already built in
R170_841/845.

The result does not yet prove the sign. It reduces the missing piece to a
precise statement: minimality must control the transversal derivative
of the full Tate port under the same cocycle, including the Gamma
collar. Evaluating only the endpoints \(s=\pm1/2\) again loses the jet.

## 1. Discrete identity

Let \(J(x,y)=(y,x)\). Then

\[
J\mathbf c_n(s)=\mathbf c_n(-s),                            \tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
\mathbf c_n(1/2)=(\Lambda(n),\Lambda(n)/n),                \tag{1.2}
\]

which are literally the weights of the two reflected collars of
R161_009(2.4). Moreover

\[
\frac{\mathbf c_n(s)-J\mathbf c_n(s)}{2s}
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{\Lambda(n)\log n}{\sqrt n}(1,-1).                   \tag{1.3}
\]

All these equalities are term by term. For \(n<e^{2T}\) the sum
is finite, so differentiating and summing with any linear collar of
\(F\) commute with no convergence hypothesis.

## 2. The generator and the physical scale

On the two-dimensional fiber write

\[
A_n=\begin{pmatrix}u_n&0\\0&-u_n\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathbf c_n(s)=e^{sA_n}\mathbf c_n(0).                     \tag{2.1}
\]

One has

\[
JA_nJ=-A_n,
 \qquad
 \mathbf c_n'(0)=A_n\mathbf c_n(0).                         \tag{2.2}
\]

The factor \(\log n\) thus appears as the generator of the deformation that
swaps the two Tate characters. The factor \(n^{-1/2}\) is the balanced vector
fixed by \(J\). This separation simultaneously explains:

1. why the central face of R170_871 has weight
   \(\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n\);
2. why its Tate endpoints have weights \(\Lambda(n)\) and
   \(\Lambda(n)/n\);
3. why the relevant first variation contains
   \(\Lambda(n)\log n/\sqrt n\), the virial weight.

There is no inequality here: \(A_n\) has opposite signs. Positivity
could only appear by pairing this jet with the first-contact vector in a
common form.

## 3. Full collar

Let \(R_{n,+}(F),R_{n,-}(F)\) be the pair of truncated boundary
functionals left after the change of variables of R161_009(2.2). Define the
deformed discrete port

\[
\mathcal J_{T}^{\rm ar}(s;F)
 =\sum_{2\le n<e^{2T}} a_n
   \big(e^{su_n}R_{n,+}(F),e^{-su_n}R_{n,-}(F)\big).         \tag{3.1}
\]

With the simultaneous reflection of \(F\), the two functionals swap;
hence (1.1) extends to the port. Its derivative at zero is

\[
\partial_s\mathcal J_T^{\rm ar}(0;F)
 =\sum_{2\le n<e^{2T}}
   \frac{\Lambda(n)\log n}{\sqrt n}
   \big(R_{n,+}(F),-R_{n,-}(F)\big).                         \tag{3.2}
\]

The same differentiation applies to the continuous collar of
R161_009(3.2): the factors \(e^{\pm su}\) produce \(\pm u\). Thus, if
\(\mathcal J_T^\Gamma(s;F)\) denotes that deformation, the full port

\[
\mathcal J_T^{\rm full}(s;F)
 =\mathcal J_T^{\rm ar}(s;F)+\mathcal J_T^\Gamma(s;F)       \tag{3.3}
\]

has as derivative the arithmetic--archimedean virial collar, with both
terms generated by a single operation. The statement is a weight and
collar functional identity; it does not by itself identify that
derivative with a positive form.

## 4. Consequence for the remaining door

R170_869 isolated

\[
\beta_{\rm phys}(F)=W_K(F)+\Theta_{\rm ext}(F).             \tag{4.1}
\]

R170_870 showed that a quadratic energy cannot determine this linear
class without an odd coupling. R170_871 supplied the swap, but
R170_872 verified that its flow law was already the R161 concomitant.
Equations (3.2)--(3.3) make precise the coupling that was missing in
that reading:

\[
\boxed{\text{odd exterior cycle}
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \partial_s\mathcal J_T^{\rm full}(0;F).}                   \tag{4.2}
\]

A sufficient law to close the door would be the following identity,
which remains **open**:

\[
\beta_{\rm phys}(F)
 =\mathfrak B_T\!\left(
       ZF,\partial_s\mathcal J_T^{\rm full}(0;F)
   \right),                                                 \tag{4.3}
\]

where \(\mathfrak B_T\) is the mixed block of a single positive
form and the second argument is also zero by the differentiated
first-contact equation. If both vanishings were proved within that
form, Cauchy--Schwarz would give \(\beta_{\rm phys}(F)=0\), which is exactly
the way out allowed by R170_870.

The acceptance condition is strong: (4.3) must come out of the literal
expansion of PC-ren with Tate in the position of R161_064(1.1). Defining
\(\mathfrak B_T\) from \(\beta_{\rm phys}\), or moving the Tate
projection across the synthesis, would be circular.
