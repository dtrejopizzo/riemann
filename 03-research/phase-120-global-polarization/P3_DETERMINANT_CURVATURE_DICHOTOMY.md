# Phase 120 — determinant-curvature dichotomy

## Status

This note tests a new implementation of the global square: obtain it as the
second fundamental form of a positive Hermitian line generated by the finite
Euler--Gamma determinant.  The calculation gives an exact rigidity theorem.
The metric with the required Green connection is flat; every canonical
positive-curvature regularization changes that connection.  Thus curvature
positivity of the determinant line cannot by itself close row (d).

## 1. Source determinant and required connection

On the zero-free half-plane \(\Re s>0\), let

\[
 D_S(s)=\pi^{-s}\Gamma(1/4+s/2)^2
        \prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-1/2-s})^{-2}.            \tag{1.1}
\]

It is constructed from the local factors and has no reference to a zeta zero.
The proved one-port identity is

\[
 \partial_s\log D_S(s)=Z_S(s),
 \qquad \Re Z_S(-i\tau)=A_S(\tau).                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Therefore a Hermitian metric \(h_S\) on the trivial determinant line has the
exact required normal connection at the boundary only if

\[
 \frac12\partial_x\log h_S(x+i y)\big|_{x=0}
       = A_S(-y).                                    \tag{1.3}
\]

## 2. The flat metric gives the exact coefficient

The canonical multiplicative metric

\[
 h_S^{\rm flat}=|D_S|^2                              \tag{2.1}
\]

satisfies (1.3) exactly.  But, away from its divisor,

\[
 -\partial\bar\partial\log h_S^{\rm flat}=0.        \tag{2.2}
\]

Hence its Chern connection recovers the signed Weil multiplier while its
curvature supplies no positive bulk and no second-fundamental-form square.
This is the differential-geometric version of the zero-residual commutator:
the connection is exact, but the desired Hilbert norm is absent.

## 3. Positive curvature changes the Green coefficient

Consider the canonical projective regularization obtained by embedding the
line as the graph of \(\sqrt\lambda D_S\) in a flat two-plane:

\[
 h_{S,\lambda}^{\rm FS}=1+\lambda|D_S|^2,
 \qquad \lambda>0.                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

Its curvature is semipositive (up to the fixed Chern-sign convention), with
positive density

\[
 \partial\bar\partial\log h_{S,\lambda}^{\rm FS}
 =\frac{\lambda|D_S'|^2}
        {(1+\lambda|D_S|^2)^2}\,ds\wedge d\bar s.  \tag{3.2}
\]

However its boundary connection is

\[
 \frac12\partial_x\log h_{S,\lambda}^{\rm FS}
 =q_{S,\lambda}\,\Re\frac{D_S'}{D_S},
 \quad
 q_{S,\lambda}=\frac{\lambda|D_S|^2}
                     {1+\lambda|D_S|^2}.             \tag{3.3}
\]

Since \(0<q_{S,\lambda}<1\), equation (3.3) is not the forced Green
coefficient (1.2) for any finite \(\lambda\).

## 4. Rigidity for radial determinant metrics

### Theorem 4.1

Let \(h_S=\Phi(|D_S|^2)\), where
\(\Phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)\) is \(C^2\) and independent of \(S\).
Suppose its Chern connection agrees with the
forced connection for every value of (D_S):

\[
 \frac12\partial_x\log h_S
 =\Re(D_S'/D_S).                                     \tag{4.1}
\]

Then \(\Phi(r)=Cr\) and the metric is flat.

#### Proof

Put \(r=|D_S|^2\).  Since

\[
 \frac12\partial_x\log\Phi(r)
 =\frac{r\Phi'(r)}{\Phi(r)}
   \Re(D_S'/D_S),                                    \tag{4.2}
\]

identity (4.1), as an identity independent of the local determinant values,
forces \(r\Phi'(r)=\Phi(r)\).  Its positive solutions are
\(\Phi(r)=Cr\).  Then \(\log h_S=\log C+\log|D_S|^2\) is harmonic off the
divisor, so its curvature vanishes.  QED.

The conclusion also applies to finite direct-sum graph embeddings: their
metrics are positive sums \(\sum_jc_j|D_S|^{2m_j}\).  Exact connection weight
one for all determinant values forces a single exponent \(m_j=1\), hence the
flat case.

## 5. Divisor escape is forbidden here

One might retain (2.1) and use its distributional curvature, supported on the
zero divisor of a completed determinant.  At finite \(S\), (1.1) has no such
divisor in the source half-plane.  After cofinal continuation, the new
curvature atoms are precisely the zeros of the completed zeta function.
Selecting a positive complex orientation for those atoms is the residue
polarization gate already isolated in phase 106; it imports the zero geometry
that row (d) is meant to control.

## 6. Verdict

- exact Euler--Gamma boundary connection from \(|D_S|^2\): **PROVED**;
- curvature of that metric off the divisor: **ZERO**;
- positive Fubini--Study curvature: **PROVED**, but with the wrong Green
  coefficient \(q_{S,\lambda}A_S\);
- rigidity of every radial determinant metric with exact coefficient:
  **PROVED**;
- determinant-curvature route without additional non-radial data:
  **CLOSED NEGATIVELY**.

The calculation identifies what a surviving geometry must add: a non-radial
horizontal distribution or boundary quotient whose metric is not a function
of \(|D_S|^2\), while its connection still differentiates to (1.2).  That
additional datum is exactly where a genuine global polarization theorem must
live.
