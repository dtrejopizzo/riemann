# Phase 120 — non-idempotent Gorenstein fusion algebra gate

## Status

The matroid no-go identifies a missing algebraic type: fusion must be
non-idempotent.  This note constructs the minimal replacement and determines
exactly what it supplies and what remains open.  It is new to the corpus, but
does not yet close row (d).

## 1. The faithful finite fusion algebra

Fix a finite prime set \(P\) and depth \(K\).  The coefficient-one Frobenius
law is represented faithfully by

\[
 R_{P,K}=\mathbb R[x_p:p\in P]/(x_p^{K+1}:p\in P),    \tag{1.1}
\]

with

\[
 \Gamma_{p^j}\longmapsto x_p^j,
 \qquad x_p^i x_p^j=x_p^{i+j}                        \tag{1.2}
\]

whenever \(i+j\le K\).  Unlike flat join, multiplication is not idempotent;
unlike configuration/Fock descent, there is one commutative monomial per
integer and no permutation quotient.

The ring is an Artinian complete intersection with one-dimensional socle
generated by

\[
 \omega_{P,K}=\prod_{p\in P}x_p^K.                   \tag{1.3}
\]

The coefficient of \(\omega_{P,K}\) defines a Poincare-duality trace.  For
positive \(c_p\), the element

\[
 \ell=\sum_{p\in P}c_px_p                            \tag{1.4}
\]

is a strong-Lefschetz element: the standard tensor product of the
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) strings
\(\mathbb R[x_p]/(x_p^{K+1})\) gives the Lefschetz decomposition.  Thus this
carrier has a genuine finite Hodge--Riemann mechanism and exact faithful
fusion.

## 2. Why the naive trace is the wrong trace

The arithmetic contact requires

\[
 \tau_{\rm cont}(x_p^{i+j})=\log p,
 \qquad
 \tau_{\rm cont}(x_p^ix_q^j)=0\quad(p\ne q).          \tag{2.1}
\]

This is not the socle trace of (1.1).  It is the evaluation character on each
prime tower, followed by the place projection.  Its Gram is exactly

\[
 \bigoplus_{p\in P}(\log p)\mathbf1_p\mathbf1_p^t,   \tag{2.2}
\]

so it is degenerate and has one positive direction per place.  It cannot be
the Poincare-duality pairing of an Artinian Gorenstein algebra when
\(|P|>1\).

The socle trace has the opposite defect: it is nondegenerate and HR, but it
pairs a monomial only with its complementary multi-degree.  It does not give
the constant same-place contact (2.1), the central metric, or the forced
Gamma/Green term.

## 3. Minimal new datum

A successful non-idempotent carrier would need two traces on the same fusion
algebra:

1. the singular contact trace \(\tau_{\rm cont}\), already fixed by row (b);
2. a source-defined Gorenstein trace \(\tau_{\rm glob}\) with HR, whose
   difference from \(\tau_{\rm cont}\) is the forced archimedean Green
   functional.

The exact desired identity is

\[
 \tau_{\rm glob}(D_fD_g)=B_{\rm nuc}(f,g),            \tag{3.1}
\]

after the two Tate degrees are included.  Merely choosing
\(\tau_{\rm glob}\) to satisfy (3.1) is circular.  The new mathematical task is
to derive it as a residue/socle trace from a canonical completion of (1.1).

## 4. Cofinal structure

The inclusions in prime set are literal:

\[
 R_{P,K}\hookrightarrow R_{P',K},\qquad P\subset P'. \tag{4.1}
\]

Depth inclusions are not literal ring maps between the truncated quotients:
\(R_{P,K}\to R_{P,K+1}\) would send the zero relation \(x_p^{K+1}=0\) to a
nonzero element.  The correct cofinal object is the polynomial algebra

\[
 R_P=\mathbb R[x_p:p\in P],                           \tag{4.2}
\]

with \(R_{P,K}\) a quotient, not a subobject.  Hence finite HR signatures do
not pass by the pointwise-inclusion lemma.  One needs a projective-limit
duality or a single infinite Gorenstein functional on a completed power-series
algebra.  This is a new version of T2, now exposed algebraically rather than
spectrally.

### Theorem 4.1 — incompatibility of finite socle traces

Already for one variable, no depth transition can preserve the faithful
polynomial labels and the normalized Gorenstein traces.  Put

\[
 A_K=\mathbb R[x]/(x^{K+1}),
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}_K(x^j)=\mathbf1_{j=K}.      \tag{4.3}
\]

On the common polynomial span, compatibility would require
\(\operatorname{tr}_{K+1}(x^K)=\operatorname{tr}_K(x^K)\).  The left side is
zero and the right side is one.  Thus no compatible trace exists.

The quotient map \(A_{K+1}\twoheadrightarrow A_K\) does exist.  Pulling
\(\operatorname{tr}_K\) back along it gives a functional on \(A_{K+1}\), but
its radical contains the new socle direction \(x^{K+1}\), so it is not a
Poincare-duality trace and does not carry HR at depth \(K+1\).  Rescaling the
traces cannot repair the zero-versus-nonzero conflict.

Therefore the finite complete-intersection HR theorem cannot be the source of
the cofinal sign.  Any successful residue must be defined once on the infinite
completion and have its own infinite-dimensional polarization theorem; it
cannot be obtained as a compatible limit of the finite socle pairings.

## 5. Counterexample firewall

For zeta there is one variable \(x_p\) per place.  The shifted pair requires
two variables \(x_{p,+},x_{p,-}\) with weights \(p^{\pm a}\), so it fails the
indecomposable-one-generator clause.  Dedekind zeta has one variable for each
prime ideal and remains inside the construction, correctly predicting that a
successful global trace would prove GRH for number fields.

## 6. Verdict and exact next target

The Gorenstein fusion algebra solves two problems that killed the matroid
candidate:

* faithful additive iteration;
* coefficient-one commutative multiplication without factorial descent.

It does **not** solve the decisive trace comparison, and Theorem 4.1 prevents
transporting its finite HR theorem to infinite depth.  The only remaining
version is no longer a vague global square:

\[
 \boxed{\text{construct a canonical infinite socle/residue functional }
 \tau_{\rm glob}\text{ on the completed fusion algebra}}               \tag{6.1}
\]

such that its local singular part is (2.1), its archimedean residue is the
quarter-Gamma determinant, and it obeys an intrinsic infinite polarization
theorem before the comparison with row (c).  Its finite restrictions cannot
be the ordinary socle traces.  The truncated Gorenstein route is therefore
**closed negatively**; only a source-defined infinite residue/potential remains
as a logically distinct candidate.
