## 2. Squares in the literature the corpus never entered

**The single most direct hit in the entire sector**: Connes–Consani's *Weil Positivity and Trace
Formula — the Archimedean Place* (2006.13771) explicitly names, in their own words, "**an
intersection theory of divisors (on the square of the Scaling Site)**" as the missing geometric
object behind their positivity argument — the trace of the Schwartz kernel `k(x,x)` is read as
"the intersection with the diagonal," and the plane is split into a small square `Δ` (where the
archimedean obstruction to positivity concentrates) and a large square `Σ`. This is a **literal,
named square** — `(Scaling Site) × (Scaling Site)` — with an explicit diagonal-intersection
reading, produced by the same authors whose curve-only Riemann–Roch (2205.01391) the corpus already
cites via `NO-GO-LIST.md` MW-5. The corpus's MW-5 entry cites only the *scaling-site* Riemann–Roch
result (real-valued `χ(D)=deg(D)∈R`) and never engages this later paper's square/diagonal
formulation at all — meaning the corpus's own "missing object" diagnosis (a positive intersection
form via a cohomology `H¹` with Frobenius) is stated by Connes–Consani themselves, independently,
about a literal square, and marked by them as unfinished ("still in development… not yet in shape").

The second hit is **Cartwright's *Combinatorial Tropical Surfaces*** (1506.02023): an
intersection pairing on Néron–Severi classes of an intrinsically 2-dimensional tropical complex,
proved non-degenerate with **at most one positive eigenvalue** — a literal Hodge index theorem —
plus a tropical Noether's formula tying `deg td_2` to `χ`. This is not `Spec Z̄ × Spec Z̄`; it is a
combinatorial ∆-complex built directly as 2-dimensional data, and the paper itself flags that
positive self-intersection is not automatic there (unlike for smooth projective surfaces) —
i.e., even in the tropical category the "positivity needs a hypothesis" problem recurs.

Complementing it, Haran's *Geometry over F1* (§10, "Arithmetical surface, and new commutative
rings") is the only Connes–Consani-adjacent source that explicitly writes down a genuine
**product/square** object over `Spec Z̄`:

> "We have now new geometric objects, such as the Arithmetical-plane associated with a number
> field K: `Spec Ō_K ∏_{Spec F{μ_K}} Spec Ō_K` … `Spec Z̄ ∏_{Spec F{±1}} Spec Z̄` … Note that the
> pro-objects … contain the ordinary affine open and dense sub-scheme `Spec(O_K ⊗_{F{μ_K}} O_K)`."

Haran stops at scheme construction — no `H^0`, no "second cohomology," no Riemann–Roch is stated
for this arithmetical plane, and the paper's own bibliography (Arakelov, *Intersection theory of
divisors on an arithmetic surface*) is cited but not developed further. **No source in this
sector actually builds sections/cohomology on `Spec Z̄ × Spec Z̄`** with a dimension, growth, or
duality statement; the closest analogues (Cartwright's tropical surfaces, Adiprasito–Huh–Katz-style
Hodge theory for matroids in 2007.07826) are on combinatorial/tropical stand-ins, not on the
arithmetic square itself.

Neither Connes–Consani's own Riemann–Roch (2205.01391, curve only, logarithmic/integer growth) nor
their Jacobian paper (2602.15941, Picard/Jacobian monoid, no h⁰) nor the absolute-geometry/FF paper
(2606.06604, moduli of points) ever leaves the curve `Spec Z̄`. **The corpus's own conclusion in
`NO-GO-LIST.md` §MW-5 that "Connes–Consani's own proved Riemann–Roch on the scaling site has
real-valued, not even integral, dimensions" understates the situation for 2205.01391 specifically:
that paper's dimensions ARE integer-valued** (the paper explicitly distinguishes itself from the
real-valued log-theta approach it attributes to Bost/Lang) **but they grow only logarithmically**,
confirming independently — from the primary source itself — the corpus's Thm 13.6 diagnosis that
lattice/Γ-module counting gives at most logarithmic growth, never the needed quadratic one.

## 3. Ideas not used by the corpus (source vs corpus, both quoted)

- **Deninger's Thm 5.1** (`W_rat(O(X)) ≅ Corr(X,A)`) is used by the corpus as its "best row-(a)
  candidate" (`AUDIT_CONSOLIDATED.md` line 97-99), but the corpus's own follow-up (`107_242` Thm
  4.1, cited in `NO-GO-LIST.md` NG-107) found the bridge "annihilates the p-adic transverse
  direction" it needed. Reading the source directly confirms *why* independently: the paper never
  develops a positivity/unimodularity argument for its Frobenius/Verschiebung structure at all —
  it stays at the level of ring/sheaf isomorphisms and explicitly flags that stronger descent
  conditions ("to remedy the defects…") are unexplored. The corpus used the theorem statement but
  never had, and the source never supplies, the missing quantitative ingredient.
- **The tropical Hodge index theorem (Cartwright 1506.02023) and tropical Hodge–Riemann relations
  (2007.07826) are absent from the corpus's own literature engagement.** `AUDIT_CONSOLIDATED.md`
  and `NO-GO-LIST.md` discuss Connes–Consani, Deninger, Haran repeatedly but never cite Cartwright,
  Adiprasito–Huh–Katz, or the tropical-surfaces intersection-pairing result, despite this being the
  one source in the whole 00-references corpus that proves a genuine, sign-definite 2-dimensional
  Hodge index theorem on a combinatorial stand-in object. This is a real candidate the corpus's
  own map never entered.
- Haran's explicit "Arithmetical-plane" construction (`Spec Z̄ ∏ Spec Z̄` over `F{±1}`) is likewise
  never engaged by the corpus's own conclusions — `AUDIT_CONSOLIDATED.md`/`NO-GO-LIST.md` treat
  Haran only via the general F1-geometry framing, not this specific §10 product construction.

## 4. Unimodularity of local roots / Frobenius partial-isometry

Across **every** source inspected in this pass — Connes–Consani's Riemann–Roch, Jacobian, and
absolute-geometry papers, the Weil-positivity/prolate/Suzuki cluster, Haran, Deninger's rational
Witt vectors, and all three tropical papers — **no source uses unimodularity of local roots, or a
partial-isometry/unitarity property of a Frobenius-type operator, in a positivity argument.**
Haran's only "partial isometry" is a bare definitional device for morphisms in the category `F_R`
(§2), unconnected to Frobenius or positivity. The Weil-positivity paper (2006.13771) and the
prolate-operator paper (2310.18423) use *unitary* operators throughout (the Fourier–inversion
unitary `u`, the grading `γ`, canonical-form isomorphisms `θ_S`) to reach canonical (Jacobi-matrix)
form, but never as a unimodularity-of-Frobenius-roots positivity mechanism — they compress by
orthogonal projections `P, P_Λ` instead, and the archimedean obstruction to positivity is
explicitly unresolved (§2 above). This is a genuine gap in the literature this sector surveys: the
specific mechanism the corpus's row-D construction needs (`Frob_n ⋆ Frob_n^† = Δ^{(n)}`) has no
precedent here, confirming from the source side that Row D's h⁰ construction is not recoverable
by importing an existing argument from this literature — it must be built from scratch.
