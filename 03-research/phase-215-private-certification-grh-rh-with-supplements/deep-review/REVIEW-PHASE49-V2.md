# Coefficient δ_g of P49: sufficient consequences and literal scope

Corrected version reviewed on 28-11-2026. P49 changed only FJ; the strong phase assertions at 5471–5473 and 5569–5572 remain. This supplement does not modify the manuscript.

## Verdict

**(c) for the literal classification of the phase** as a root of unity times ONE value of υ and the prescribed index: that stronger formula is not certified here. **(a) for the two necessary consequences**, by means of the following supplementary proof: the modulus at each conjugate is exactly I_g^(-1/2), and, if g is integral above l, δ_g is an l-adic unit. These consequences suffice for `pk:conj(i)` and `pk:std(S1)` and do not need the individual phase to be classified. No circular dependence on Short Forms or on auxcore has been found.

The definition of the operators is taken to be the explicit formula P3/D1–D3 of the paper (538–625), including its corrections of character and of Weil index. The proof does not identify an arbitrary operator satisfying only the Heisenberg relations with the normalised operator: that distinction is essential.

## Sufficient lemma

For the normalised finite representation of P49 and a self-dual lattice L, one writes the expansion of the vacuum line

`ω(g)v_L = δ_g Σ_(r∈gL/(L∩gL)) ζ_r ρ°(r)v_L`,

with ζ_r roots of unity and the vectors of the sum orthonormal. Let I_g be the number of terms.

1. δ_g is algebraic and for every σ∈Aut(C), `|σδ_g|²=1/I_g`.
2. If g stabilises the local lattice above l, then δ_g is a unit for every embedding C→C_l. The representatives r can be chosen integral above l.

The expansion and the equality of the moduli of its coordinates are consequences of the Heisenberg relations and the vacuum line (geometric part of W4). What is proved below is the arithmetic of the common coefficient, which is **not** deduced from complex unitarity alone.

## Proof of the modulus of every conjugate

### 1. Field of coefficients and conjugation

Let A be the field generated by roots of unity, square roots of positive rationals and the values of υ at finite ideles. Each value of υ is algebraic and all its conjugates have modulus one. Indeed, for an idele a one chooses h with `a^h=z u_f`, z∈E× and u_f a unit; the non-ramification of υ and its infinity type give

`υ(a)^h=|N_(E/K)(z)|/N_(E/K)(z)`.

The denominator belongs to the imaginary quadratic field K and the numerator is the square root of its rational norm. Both for embeddings that fix K and for those that conjugate it, the quotient has modulus one. Hence each root υ(a) has all its conjugates of modulus one. This uses only the character, the finite ideal class group and its infinity type; it uses neither theta nor the conclusion we wish to prove.

For each generator a of A, `σ(bar a)=overline(σa)`: this is immediate for roots of unity and rational square roots; for υ(a), one uses `bar(υ(a))=υ(a)^(-1)` and the modulus one of ALL its conjugates just proved. It is extended to the whole of A by the field operations.

### 2. Arithmetic operators in Schrödinger

Rao's normalisation expresses the operators by means of linear changes with factor the square root of the modulus, multiplication by quadratic characters and partial Fourier transforms; it respects orthogonal sums. This was collated in Lemma3.2, Theorem3.5 and Proposition3.7 of the [original article of Rao](https://msp.org/pjm/1993/157-2/pjm-v157-n2-p09-p.pdf), especially pp.351–358. The self-dual Haar measures on a p-adic field give volumes that are powers of p and their square roots.

Here comes the additional arithmetic argument, not an inference from mere unitarity. For a finite number of Schwartz–Bruhat functions and their images under those operators, one chooses a common open subgroup of constancy and a common compact support. All the coefficients and integrals needed are then **finite sums**. The phases are roots of unity; the volumes and normalisation factors belong to Q(p^(1/2)). Hence all the coefficients lie in A. The corrections P3 are a value of υ times a power of a Weil index, which is a root of unity; they remain in A.

The identities of scalar products of a finite number of those vectors are finite algebraic identities in A. Since σ commutes with conjugation on A and fixes the rational volumes (the possible sign of a square root does not affect its square), applying σ to the Gram identity preserves the identity exactly. Thus the conjugated operators preserve the norm on these test spaces. σ has not been applied to an infinite integral, nor has it been supposed that every unitary complex operator is unitary after conjugating its coefficients.

### 3. Non-split spaces and vacuum basis

In the doubled space one chooses symplectic polarisations compatible with the sum W⊕W⁻. The change of polarisation is obtained by the same arithmetic generators of Rao. D2 defines, without an additional free scalar,

`ω_(W⊕W⁻)(g,1)=A_g⊗1`.

The coefficients of A_g are recovered by taking in the second factor an indicator of norm one: they are coefficients of the doubled operator between products of indicators. The preceding algebraic Gram reasoning applies to them and preserves the norm of A_g after σ. This covers the non-split case without choosing a phase by abstract uniqueness.

The basis of translates of a vacuum line can be constructed arithmetically: the Heisenberg invariance equations in a finite quotient fix the non-zero coefficients up to a common constant, and their ratios are roots of unity. One of them is chosen equal to 1 and one normalises by the square root of the rational measure of its support. The coefficients of these vectors also lie in A. On conjugating, they remain orthonormal, because their Gram matrix is finite with entries in A.

The vector ω(g)v_L has norm one after any σ by 2–3. Its I_g coordinates are still `σδ_g` times roots of unity. Hence

`1=Σ_r |σδ_g·σζ_r|²=I_g |σδ_g|²`.

Only a finite number of places contribute to the adelic vector, so that the same argument gives the global statement. The case of a vacuum chosen with another common phase does not change δ_g: that phase appears in both members of the expansion. The convention ρ° only multiplies the ratios by roots of unity.

## Proof of integrality at l

An embedding into C_l is fixed. At a place of residual characteristic p≠l, the generators of Rao and their inverses preserve the module of Schwartz functions with values in O_(C_l): the substitutions preserve values, the phases are roots of unity and the denominators of Fourier and of volume are powers of p. The normalisers p^(k/2) are also l-adic units. The local values of υ supported outside l are l-adic units: in the preceding ideal class relation z is a unit above l, and so are the numerator and denominator of υ(a)^h.

At a place above l, g stabilises L; its action on the vacuum line is a smooth character of the compact stabiliser. The vector is smooth, so that this character has open kernel, finite image and value a root of unity. No Fourier formula with denominators l is needed in this case.

The vacuum bases at places p≠l have normalisers that are powers of p and phases that are roots of unity, so that they are integral bases for this computation. The doubling argument uses changes of polarisation with denominators powers of p and is preserved. Consequently ω(g) and its inverse preserve the relevant integral lattice. The vector v_L is primitive; so is its image. All the non-zero coordinates of the image differ by roots of unity; if their common coefficient had positive valuation, the image would not be primitive. Hence `v_l(δ_g)=0`.

Moreover `(gL/(L∩gL))_l=0` when g stabilises L above l; representatives r whose component above l is zero are chosen. This also proves the integrality condition of the translations used in S1. The argument covers l=2: at the places p=2 the stabiliser is used, and at the other places the Fourier denominators are 2-adic units.

## Applications and absence of circularity

- **P49:5470–5476 (`pk:conj`)** needs only `|σδ_g|=I_g^(-1/2)` to compare the norms of the semilinear commutator. The lemma provides it with exact equality, without an extra constant. The continuity of the character of the commutator and the surjectivity of the semilinear operator were checked separately in THETA/CJ05a; they are not replaced by Schur without hypotheses.
- **P49:5567–5574 (`pk:std(S1)`)** needs only that the coefficient be a unit at l and that the representatives r be integral above l. The second part of the lemma provides this, without demanding the specific idele det(u) of the strong formula.
- **Short Forms** uses S1 to transport standard data and `pk:conj` for the exact archimedean modulus; both consequences are covered. The CM constants are not altered.
- **Auxcore of 53** inherits integrality of the jets and bounds for all their conjugates through those inputs of 49. The sufficient phase correction adds no factor of rank, index or twist; N₀ and the bounds exp(x^99 b²), exp(2x^201 b²) remain the same. This does not decide the exchange of characters of 53, reviewed independently.

The logical order is: character υ and explicit normalisation of Rao → arithmetic of coefficients/Gram → conjugate modulus and integrality → pk:conj/S1 → Short Forms and its applications. At no step are Short Forms, auxcore, GRH or the Galois unitarity that one is trying to establish presupposed.

## What remains to be incorporated into the text

For a literally self-sufficient proof, replace the two invocations of the strong phase by the sufficient lemma and its proof, or prove the strong formula separately. **The supplement closes the necessary consequences; it does not certify that additional classification, nor does it assert that it is already written in the corrected manuscript.** The complete attribution of the formula P3 to Kudla was not collated again with a full copy of his article: here the formula P3 is taken as the explicit normalisation defined in P49, and the Rao component is collated directly. That bibliographic limitation is not concealed as a verification of Kudla's original.

## Additional check: classical splitting and fixed vacuum

**Precision of scope that takes precedence over an unconditional reading of the initial verdict:** the preceding arithmetic supplement proves the consequences of δ_g for the explicitly normalised operators P3/D1–D3. To use it as a certificate of the whole chain one must verify in addition that P3 is the classical splitting with those conventions. Defining β(g)r(g) does not by itself prove the representation law. That exact identification remains **(c)** in this review because of the limit of sources specified below; it is not replaced by an assertion of unitarity.

### Primary sources actually consulted

The [original of Pan2001](https://msp.org/pjm/2001/199-1/pjm-v199-n1-p10-p.pdf), §§3.2–3.4 and 6.1, and the [notes of Kudla1996](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/skudla/castle.pdf) were opened. Pan compares the Schrödinger model with the lattice model by means of a factor α and the splitting by means of β; the product αβ is a character of the stabiliser, not automatically one. His hypotheses are odd residual characteristic. The 1996 notes develop the orthogonal/symplectic case and do not provide the explicit collation of the unitary case needed here.

The official PDF of Kudla1994 was attempted by DOI, publisher link and direct download; the response was the [subscription page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02773003), not the complete article. That original is not presented as read. Pan §3.2 confirms the doubling procedure of D2. This settles the foundation of that construction conditionally on the splitting of the split space; it does not by itself collate the exact factor of P3.

### The places above 2 are indeed covered for the data of the series

K=Q(√−d) and d≡7(mod8). Then −d≡1(mod8) is a square in Q₂; for each place v of E⁺ above 2, the polynomial of √−d already splits over E⁺_v. Since E=E⁺(√−d), E⊗_(E⁺)E⁺_v≅E⁺_v×E⁺_v. Hence **all the places above 2 of these data are split**, and D3 applies, not the part of Pan that excludes 2.

D3 is a representation by multiplication and direct substitution. On the O_E-stable lattices used by the chain, its components are A⊕A^∨. If g stabilises the lattice, g_wA=A, so that |det g_w|=1; since υ_w is unramified, υ_w(det g_w)=1. The indicator of A is fixed exactly. The normalisation a_wO_w at the primes where α is not a unit does not modify this argument: g_w preserves A and the corresponding indicator. Thus there is no residual dyadic obligation for these data, although W4 is not certified here for every arbitrary symplectic lattice that its formulation may allow.

### What Pan contributes to the vacuum at non-split odd places

For a unimodular Hermitian lattice, the model of the self-dual lattice has a vacuum and the stabiliser acts on that line by αβ. In Pan §3.3, the restriction to SU of the character αβ is trivial when one of the quotients of lattices is zero, as happens in the unimodular case. It suffices to check a subgroup that surjects onto the determinant: an orthogonal line of unit norm of the unimodular basis and its U(1). The existence of that line uses the unramified extension and the odd characteristic, and once it is extracted orthogonal induction applies.

The rank 1 computation can be checked in Pan §6.1 without declaring a character trivial by unitarity. In the notation there, t=x+δy has norm 1 and the self-dual lattice corresponds to even parity of `ord(ψ₀)+ord(%)`. If t−1 is a unit, α=1 and the unramified character χ equals 1 at (t−1)δ. If t≡1, the norm relation implies `ord(t−1)=ord(y)` and `ord(x−1)=2ord(y)`. In the formula for α of §6.1, the Hilbert symbols of the unramified extension and the Weil index reduce to `(-1)^ord(y)` because the preceding parity is even. The factor β is `χ((t−1)δ)=(-1)^ord(y)`. Their product is 1. The changes of sign and the factor 2 between the additive characters considered have valuation 0 here, and do not alter this computation. The case t=1 is immediate.

This proves that **the splitting of Kudla as read in Pan fixes the unimodular vacuum exactly**. The reduction of SU and the line of the determinant uses the compatibility under orthogonal sums of the normalised model. It is not deduced that any splitting of the same cover does so: twisting by a character of the determinant can change the result.

### Exact obligation remaining for the certificate

It remains to collate or prove the identity of P3: for the skew-Hermitian space `jmath·〈,〉` and the Hermitian line of norm 1, the Rao cocycle is cancelled exactly by

`β_Y(g)=υ(x(g)) γ_F(η∘RV)^(-j(g))`,

with `η(t)=ψ_K(t/2)`, `ψ_K(t)=ψ_k(2t)`, the definition of x(g) and the passage to left actions of the manuscript. The precise theorem invoked is Kudla1994, Theorem3.1 case 3₊, with (1.11)–(1.14); the conventions of RV and the Weil index must also be verified, not only the existence of some splitting. The publisher's abstract page and the O/Sp case of castle.pdf are not enough for this. Pan confirms compatible results and allows the preceding check of the vacuum, but it was not used as though it reproduced all those conventions of Theorem 3.1.
