## Second batch: closure of two analytic steps

### CJ05a — Character of the commutator: closed

The continuity detail can be proved without imposing continuity on the abstract semilinear operator. Once D is an automorphism of the smooth space and D rho(y) D^−1=chi(y)rho(y), take v non-zero. Both v and Dv have a common compact open subgroup H on which rho(h) fixes them. For h in H, the identity applied to v gives chi(h)=1. Thus chi is continuous. The additive group A_f/H is torsion, so the values of chi are roots of unity. Adelic self-duality represents chi as a commutator with rho(y_g), and Schur gives D=c_g rho(y_g). The surjectivity used here is obtained from the construction of Lambda_sigma: B is invertible and the rational approximants of B gamma reach all the classes modulo an open stabiliser of F0_sigma. Its translates generate the whole smooth space. There is no counterexample of a discontinuous character in this situation. CJ05b, the algebraic phase of delta_g, remains pending.

### CJ06 — Theta identity and norm: closed with explicit expansion

The Schrödinger model gives a direct check for arbitrary Schwartz–Bruhat phi and psi, including the translates of the Gaussian. Write W=X+Y with both subspaces rational isotropic and theta(phi)=sum_{a in X(k)}phi(a). For x in X(k), y in Y(k), the factor beta(x,y) is one. Hence the sum of the matrix coefficients is computed by integrating conjugate(psi(t)) phi(t+x) [t,y] and summing first in y by adelic Poisson. Self-duality transforms that last sum into the comb distribution of X(k), with constant one. There remains sum_{a,x} conjugate(psi(a)) phi(a+x), which is conjugate(theta(psi)) theta(phi). The matrix-coefficient function of two Schwartz functions is Schwartz on W(A); the rational sum converges absolutely. The application of Poisson is understood distributionally on Schwartz functions, without exchanging a non-absolutely convergent sum of characters with an ordinary integral.

For the norm, theta(rho(x+y)phi) is, up to a factor of modulus one, the Fourier series sum_a phi(a+x)[a,y]. Integrating its squared modulus over Y(A)/Y(k) kills a different from b; integrating afterwards over X(A)/X(k) unfolds sum_a |phi(a+x)|² to the integral of |phi|² over X(A). The self-dual measures give volume one to both quotients. This proves exactly the normalisation of 5478 and extends the identity to the translated vectors used in 5483. No uncontrolled constant remains here.

### CJ05b — Concrete alternative route for the conjugate modulus; verification still in progress

There is a route that avoids having to identify individually the root of unity of delta_g. In the Schrödinger model use indicator functions of cosets with norm one. The Rao coefficients are integrals of quadratic characters: for two locally constant functions with compact support they reduce to finite sums of roots of unity times rational volumes and their square roots. Kudla's correction adds Weil indices (roots of unity) and values of upsilon. In the non-split case, the coefficient of A_g is recovered from that of A_g tensor 1 by evaluating the second factor on an indicator of norm one, in the doubled space. In that field every conjugation fixing K commutes with complex conjugation: for upsilon this follows from CJ03 and bar(v)=v^−1; for the other generators it is immediate. The lattice vectors, expressed in the Schrödinger model by means of the Heisenberg relations, have coefficients that are roots of unity times volume normalisers.
