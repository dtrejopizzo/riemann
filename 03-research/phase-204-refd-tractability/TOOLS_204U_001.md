## 1. Lemma LCB with constants

### 1.1 What H1PLUS needs
Proposition H, direction ⇒: if W^ev_{T₀} is not ⪰ 0, then H1⁺ fails for some T. H1PLUS derived this from LCB:
«n₋(𝔄_ev(T)) ≥ 2 for large T if there is a hyperbolic quadruple», via boundary tests (§2.3(b)–(c), ARGUED).

For Prop H this weaker statement suffices, with no mention of zeros:

**Lemma LCB′.** Let G ∈ 𝒟^ev_{T₀} be real, even, with W_{T₀}(G) < 0. Then there exists T₁ ≥ T₀ with n₋(𝔄_ev(T)) ≥ 2 for all
T ≥ T₁.

Conventions (P46, verified):
- eq:W: W_T = 𝔞_T + 2Re(A B̄), and prop:WT: W^ev = 𝔄_ev + 2ℓ², with ℓ(F) = ⟨F, χ_ev⟩. In the even sector A = B = ℓ.
- H1PLUS (1.4) writes Z_T := W^ev_T. Here W is used.
- χ_T := cosh(t/2)1_{I_T}, ℓ(χ_T) = ‖χ_T‖² = T + sinh T.

### 1.2 Inputs
- **(a) Extension by zero (PROVED, lem:monoT).** For T ≥ T₀, G ∈ 𝒟_T, and W_T(G), 𝔄_T(G), ℓ(G) do not depend on T.
- **(b) Core (PROVED, H1PLUS §1.4).** C_c^∞(I_{T₀})^ev is a core of the closed form W^ev_{T₀}. Hence, without
  loss, G ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀}) and W(G) < 0.
- **(c) Polarized lem:roughEF (PROVED).** For F₁, F₂ ∈ {G, χ_T}, W_T(F₁,F₂) = Σ_ρ Φ_{F₁}(ρ)Φ_{F₂}(ρ). In the even sector
  Φ_F(1−ρ) = Φ_F(ρ). The hypothesis Σ|Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)| < ∞ holds for G ± χ_T by the bounds in (d); the identity is applied
  to G + χ_T and to G − χ_T and subtracted.
- **(d) Bounds (PROVED + CITED).**
  - Φ_{χ_T}(s) = sinh(sT)/s + sinh((s−1)T)/(s−1).
  - For ρ = σ + iγ with 0 < σ < 1 (CITED, Hadamard–de la Vallée Poussin):
    |Φ_{χ_T}(ρ)| ≤ (e^{σT} + e^{(1−σ)T})/|γ|. Hence e^{−T}|Φ_{χ_T}(ρ)| ≤ 2/|γ|, and it tends to 0 term by term.
  - G ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀}): integrating by parts twice, |Φ_G(ρ)| ≤ e^{T₀/2}‖G″‖₁/|ρ−½|².
  - Σ_ρ |γ|^{−2} < ∞ (CITED).

### 1.3 Proof (PROVED)
Notation: z₁₁ = W(G) < 0, z₁₂ = W_T(G, χ_T), z₂₂ = W_T(χ_T), l₁ = ℓ(G), l₂ = ℓ(χ_T).

**Step 1: limits of the entries.** By dominated convergence with (d):
- e^{−2T}|z₂₂| ≤ Σ_ρ e^{−2T}|Φ_{χ_T}(ρ)|² → 0. Dominant: 4/γ².
- e^{−T}|z₁₂| ≤ Σ_ρ |Φ_G(ρ)|·e^{−T}|Φ_{χ_T}(ρ)| → 0. Dominant: 2|Φ_G(ρ)|/|γ|, summable.

Since l₂ ~ e^T/2, one obtains **z₂₂ = o(l₂²) and z₁₂ = o(l₂)**.

**Step 2: the 2×2 block.** The matrix of 𝔄_ev = W − 2ℓℓᵀ on {G, χ_T} is
[[z₁₁ − 2l₁², z₁₂ − 2l₁l₂], [·, z₂₂ − 2l₂²]]. Expanding, the terms 4l₁²l₂² cancel:

  det / l₂² = −2z₁₁ + ε(T),
  |ε(T)| ≤ |z₁₁|·|z₂₂|/l₂² + z₁₂²/l₂² + 4|l₁|·|z₁₂|/l₂ + 2l₁²·|z₂₂|/l₂².   (1.1)

By Step 1, ε(T) → 0. The diagonal entry is z₁₁ − 2l₁² < 0 and det > 0 as soon as |ε(T)| < 2|z₁₁|. In that case 𝔄_ev
is negative definite on a plane, and min–max (I4 of LEMMA_AINF) gives n₋(𝔄_ev(T)) ≥ 2.

**Explicit threshold.** T₁ is any T from which the right-hand side of (1.1) is < 2|z₁₁| = 2|W(G)|.
- The five numbers z₁₁, l₁, l₂, z₁₂, z₂₂ are explicit.
- The two zero tails are effectively bounded with any explicit zero-free region (CITED, e.g.
  Mossinghoff–Trudgian) and with explicit N(T) (Trudgian).
- No numerical T₁ was computed for a concrete G. It is not needed for Prop H.

Monotonicity: n₋(𝔄_ev(T)) is non-decreasing in T (extension by zero, (a)). In any case (1.1) holds for all T ≥ T₁. ∎

**Remarks.**
1. The proof sees no quadruples or boundary tests. The mechanism is rank-one arithmetic: χ_T has 𝔄(χ_T) ≈ −2ℓ(χ_T)²,
   and the coupling with G is almost entirely polar (−2l₁l₂). The determinant leaves exactly −2W(G)·l₂².
2. This is a Schur identity: the excess of 𝔄 over W on the plane is absorbed by χ_T, and what remains is the sign of W
   at G.
3. The CITED inputs (0 < Re ρ < 1, Σ|ρ|^{−2} < ∞) are classical and unconditional. They are only used for the qualitative
   growth bound of W_T(χ_T). A zero-free version follows from eq:Aform plus the PNT (CITED): 𝔄_T(χ_T) = −2l₂²(1 + o(1)),
   because the leading term of ψ(x) in the comb reproduces −2ℓ² and E(x) = o(x) gives o(e^{2T}). It was not written up; the
   route via (c) is shorter.

### 1.4 Consequence: Proposition H — PROVED

- **⇐** (H1_TRACT/H1PLUS): W^ev_T ⪰ 0 gives n₋(𝔄_ev) ≤ 1 by the sandwich (2.1).
- **⇒**: if W^ev_{T₀} is not ⪰ 0, LCB′ gives n₋(𝔄_ev(T)) ≥ 2 for T ≥ T₁.

### 1.5 The strong version (H1PLUS §2.3) and its failed step — OPEN
Strong statement: «n₋(𝔄_ev(T)) → 1 + N_hip».

- **Step that fails: (b) together with (c).** A boundary test F_T, modulated at the frequency γ_q of quadruple q, also
  receives the contribution 4e^{2β′a}|ψ̂|²·(cos/sin) from every other quadruple q′.
  - If there are quadruples with β′ > β_q at arbitrary heights, the polynomial decay of ψ̂ (ψ of compact support) does not
    compensate e^{2(β′−β_q)T} uniformly.
  - The sign of each term rotates with aγ′.
  - Without a zero-density bound near Re s = 1 that dominates that sum (the Ingham/Huxley ones do not do so
    uniformly in T), (b)'s «Z_T(F_T) ≤ (C′ − 4ce^{2β_qT})‖F_T‖²» is not proved.
- **(c) is not necessary.** LCB′ solves the polar-direction problem using χ_T as a vector, not by imposing ℓ = 0.
- **What LCB′ does give by induction** (ARGUED, not written up): n₋(𝔄_ev(T)) ≥ 1 + n₋(W^ev_{T₀}) for large T.
  The same Schur computation is applied to a negative subspace U of W of dimension k plus χ_T. The U × U block is fixed; the
  coupling with χ_T is −2ℓ(U)l₂ + o(l₂). The Schur complement is −2·l₂²·(1 − ℓ_Uᵀ(W_U − 2ℓ_Uℓ_Uᵀ)^{−1}ℓ_U·…); the

## 3. Upper bound of A∞

### 3.1 Why the direct Dirichlet argument does not close (exact obstacle)
- On the complement of the first N Dirichlet sines, ‖F′‖² ≥ (π(N+1)/2T)²‖F‖². But:
  - (i) that complement is not in H¹₀, and the form 𝔪 lives in 𝒟_T, with a log weight, not in H¹;
  - (ii) m grows only like log τ, so there is no minorant m ≥ a + bτ² with b > 0. By (I1), φ is concave and lies
    ABOVE its chords, never above a parabola on all of [0, ∞).
- The basis that diagonalizes τ² does not control the logarithmic tail from below. That is why the upper bound needs an
  operator whose basis diagonalizes a band indicator, i.e. the prolates.

### 3.2 Theorem A∞-sup (PROVED from two CITED inputs)
Let t₁ > t₀, μ := m(t₁) > 0, δ := μ/(μ − C₀) ∈ (0, ½), c := Tt₁. Then

  n₋(𝔪_T) ≤ ⌈2c/π⌉ + (2/π²)·log(100c/π + 25) [corrected by BIBLIO_CHECK_001; the memo used to say 50c/π]·log(5/(δ(1−δ))) + 7.   (3.1)

*Proof.*
- **Step minorant.** m is even and increasing on [0, ∞) (lem:mbounds(i)), so m ≥ C₀ on |τ| < t₁ and m ≥ μ on
  |τ| ≥ t₁. Hence 𝔪_T(F) ≥ μ‖F‖² − (μ − C₀)⟨A_cF, F⟩, with A_c = P_T Q_{t₁} P_T, the time–band limiting operator
  on I_T × [−t₁, t₁]. Its eigenvalues are the λ_k(c) of the prolates with parameter c = Tt₁.
- **Min–max.** On the complement of the eigenvectors with λ_k > δ, ⟨A_cF, F⟩ ≤ δ‖F‖². There
  𝔪_T ≥ (μ − (μ − C₀)δ)‖F‖² = 0. Hence n₋(𝔪_T) ≤ #{k : λ_k(c) > δ}.
- **Two CITED inputs.**
  - Landau, «On the density of phase-space expansions», IEEE Trans. IT 39 (1993): λ_{⌈2c/π⌉}(c) ≤ ½. Hence
    #{λ_k > ½} ≤ ⌈2c/π⌉.
  - Karnik–Romberg–Davenport, «Improved bounds for the eigenvalues of prolate spheroidal wave functions and discrete
    prolate spheroidal sequences», ACHA 55 (2021): #{ε < λ_k < 1 − ε} ≤ (2/π²)log(100c/π + 25) [corrected by BIBLIO_CHECK_001; the memo used to say 50c/π]log(5/(ε(1−ε))) + 7.
  - **This theorem's constants must be verified against the source before carrying them into P46.** The form
    log c·log(1/ε) is the known one.
- Add the two counts. ∎

**Choice t₁ = t₀ + 1/T.**
- μ ≥ 2t₀φ′(t₁²)/T. Numerically μ ≈ 0.159/T and δ ≈ 0.0297/T.
- Also ⌈2Tt₁/π⌉ ≤ ⌈2Tt₀/π⌉ + 1.
- Result: **n₋(𝔪_T) ≤ ⌈2Tt₀/π⌉ + 8 + (2/π²)·log(16Tt₁ + 25)·log(170T)**, with T ≥ 1 and NUMERICAL-certifiable constants
  (`ub_A_inf.out`).

Excess over ⌈2Tt₀/π⌉:

| T | 0.55 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 10 | 100 | 1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| excess | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 17 | 26 | 35 |

With A∞: **⌈2Tt₀/π⌉ − 1 ≤ n₋(𝔪_T) ≤ ⌈2Tt₀/π⌉ + O(log²T)**, PROVED modulo the CITED constants.

By sector: A_c commutes with reflection. The prolate eigenvalues alternate parity, so each sector receives

### 3.3 The exact O(1) — OPEN
- **Galerkin** gives n₋ = N(T) in 5 windows (LEMMA_AINF §6).
- **Obstacle.** The step minorant loses m's structure near t₀. There, m(τ) ≈ m′(t₀)(τ − t₀) crosses zero
  transversally. The prolates' plunge region (≍ log c eigenvalues between δ and 1 − δ) is exactly where m ≈ 0.
- **What would be needed for O(1).** For smooth symbols with a transversal crossing, the count of negative
  eigenvalues of P_T m(D) P_T is expected to be (T/π)|{m < 0}| + O(1). The log terms appear with discontinuous symbols, and
  the step is one. A proof would require a second-order Szegő asymptotic with the interval's boundary term for a symbol with
  a logarithmic tail (Widom; Landau–Widom with smooth symbol). It is not CITED in that form: OPEN.
- **(1) + (3) do not give O(1)**: they give O(log²T).
