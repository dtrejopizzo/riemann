## 2. Blueprint claims refuted, with the counterexample

Six.  Two are substantive; four are corrections that leave the conclusion
standing but change the proof or the citation.

### 2.1 `z(F) = M(Γ_F)` (blueprint §1, clause C.2) — **FALSE**

The blueprint's clause C.2 says the ruling coordinate is `z(F) = M(Γ_F)`, and
the previous Paper 45 defined `z(F) := (M_−(F),M_+(F)) = (Φ_F(0),Φ_F(1))`
(old (8.1)).

**Counterexample.**  Paper 44 Lemma 3bis.6a states
`λ_∓(Γ_h) = ∫_R h(s)e^{±s/2}ds`, so `λ_−(Γ_F) = ∫F e^{s/2} = Φ_F(1)` and
`λ_+(Γ_F) = Φ_F(0)`.  Hence `M(Γ_F) = (λ_−,λ_+) = (Φ_F(1),Φ_F(0))`, the
**swap** of `z(F)`.  Take any real `F ∈ C_c^∞` with `Φ_F(0) = 1`,
`Φ_F(1) = 2` (available by Paper 43 Theorem 9.1's surjectivity argument):
`z(F) = (1,2)` while `M(Γ_F) = (2,1)`.  The script's check (5f) exhibits the
two vectors numerically for an explicit `F`:
`z(F) = (0.490619, 0.533332)`, `M(Γ_F) = (0.533332, 0.490619)`.

**Corrected statement** (paper, Remark 8.2): `M(Γ_F) = σ(z(F))`, `σ` the swap;
and `β_T(σz,σw) = β_T(z,w)`, so Theorem 8.1(i),(ii),(iii) and every later use
survive unchanged (script check (5g)).  What is genuinely corrected is the
*naming of the rulings*: `λ_+(Γ_F) = Φ_F(0) = M_−(F)`, so `e_+` — the limit of
Paper 43's spread family `F_n`, which has `λ_+(F_n) = 1` — pairs with the
`s = 0` pole, **not** the `s = 1` pole, contrary to the old paper's implicit
reading.  This also confirms Paper 44 Thm 3ter.14's
`M(Frob_n) = (n^{1/2},n^{−1/2})`, which is `(λ_-,λ_+)` in that order.

### 2.2 Blueprint §2.2's mechanism for the polar packet — **FALSE**

The blueprint says: *"the finite-place residues sum to a divergence that is
exactly the archimedean growth, leaving coefficient 1"*, and asks that Mertens
be the quarantined step of the reconciliation.

**Refutation, two independent counts.**

(a) *There is no archimedean divergence to cancel against.*  In the theorem the
blueprint points at — Paper 43 Theorem 10.8b, the only place where the value
`1` is produced — Step 3 bounds the archimedean contribution by
`(1−e^{−2a})^{−1}e^{−(n+m)a}`, which tends to `0` **exponentially**.  The
value `1` comes entirely from the finite places, from the **main term**
`\log x` of Mertens integrated against `−φ'_{n,m}`.  So the archimedean place
is negligible there, not a compensating divergence.

(b) *Nothing needs reconciling.*  This is R132.000's supplement, which I
verified and proved: with `s = ½+iτ`, `A(s) := a(s) + Σ_p f_p(s) =
½\log π − ½ψ(s/2) − ζ'/ζ(s) = −ξ'/ξ(s) + 1/s + 1/(s−1)`, and the functional
equation gives `A(s) + A(1−s) ≡ 0` (paper Theorem 9bis.2; script check (2a),
worst residual `3.9·10^{−31}` over six off-real points).  The **colimit symbol
has no Tate pole**.  The chart residue `|S_f|+1` diverges because
`Σ_{p∈S_f}f_p(s) → −ζ'/ζ(s)` only on `Re s > 1`: each `f_p` has residue `1` at
`s = 0` (script (3b), all `1.000`), while the analytic continuation of the sum
is regular there.  Summation over primes and analytic continuation **do not
commute at `s = 0`** — paper Corollary 9bis.3, script (3c),(3d): chart total
`6.000` for `|S_f| = 5`, colimit `Res_{s=0}[A(s)+A(1−s)] = 9·10^{−33}`.

**What survives.**  Mertens is used once, in Theorem 9bis.4(ii), through
Paper 43 Theorem 10.8b with its `CLASSICAL` label, and only to say that the
hyperbolic `β_T` is the restriction of `I_∞` to the ruling plane rather than an
external decoration.  §11 records a deletion test: removing that one statement
removes nothing else.  And its true subject is the **pole at `s = 1`**:
`Σ_{p≤x}\log p/(p−1) \sim \log x` mimics `−ζ'/ζ(σ) \sim 1/(σ−1)` (paper
Thm 9bis.4(iii); script (3e)), which is a different point of the `s`-plane from
the residue count at `s = 0`.

### 2.3 Blueprint §2.1, bullet 3 — off by a factor of 2

The blueprint says each prime term `∫_{(c)}g(s)n^{−s}ds`, shifted to
`Re s = ½`, gives `Λ(n)n^{−1/2}⟨(S_{\log n}+S_{−\log n})F,F⟩`.

**It gives half of that.**  `\frac1{2πi}∫_{L_{1/2}}g(s)n^{−s}ds =
n^{−1/2}h(−\log n) = n^{−1/2}⟨S_{−\log n}F,F⟩`.  The other half comes from the
`L_{1−c}` line through `ξ'/ξ(1−s) = −ξ'/ξ(s)`, which doubles the `L_c`
integral (paper Lemma 9.3, (9.5)), and the two halves coincide **only because
`h = F⋆F^†` is even, i.e. only because `F` is real**.  Both facts are now
explicit hypotheses of Lemma 9.1 and are used by name in Theorem 9.4(c).

### 2.4 The termwise split of the blueprint's contour combination — invalid

The blueprint writes the zero sum as
`\frac1{2πi}[∫_{(c)} − ∫_{(1−c)}]g\,ξ'/ξ\,ds` and then says "on `Re s = c`
expand `ξ'/ξ = 1/s + 1/(s−1) − ½\log π + ½ψ(s/2) + ζ'/ζ`".  Taken literally
that is a split of the **bracket**, and it destroys the answer: applied to the
entire, symmetric summand `−½\log π`, the bracket
`[∫_{L_c}−∫_{L_{1−c}}]` annihilates it (Lemma 9.3 with `A(s) = A(1−s)`
constant), so the `\log π` of (1.7) would be lost.  Worse, the pairing
`A_Γ(s) − A_Γ(1−s)` has a pole at `s = 1` inside the shift region, so a naive
symmetrised handling of the Gamma term is not available either.

**Corrected route** (paper Theorem 9.4): apply (9.5) **first**, obtaining
`Σ_ρ g(ρ) = \frac1{πi}∫_{L_c}g\,ξ'/ξ\,ds` on the single line `L_c`, and expand
**there**.  Recorded in the paper as Remark 9.5(2).

### 2.5 Blueprint §4: "Cor 6.2's conditionality (now unconditional via Paper 44
Thm 3ter.12)" — wrong theorem

Theorem 3ter.12 is a statement about the **Frobenius objects**
(`supp Frob_n` meets no fixed-point stratum for composite `n`), proved before
any trace is computed.  It is not a statement about the radial family, which
is what the old Corollary 6.2 was about.  The unconditionality of the prime
packet on the radial sector comes from Paper 44 **Theorem 3bis.7**, whose
proof observes that the finite part of `T(Γ_h)` runs over the valuation shells
`p^kZ_p^×`, whose moduli are exactly the prime powers.  The paper cites both
and says which is which (Corollary 5.2), because the blueprint conflates them
and Paper 44 §10's ledger is careful not to.

### 2.6 Blueprint §2.5's display — needs the profile sign

The blueprint writes `I_A(Γ_F,Γ_G) = ∫\hat F\overline{\hat G}\,\hat T_S\,dτ/2π`.
By Paper 44 Def. 3bis.6, `Γ_h(w) = h(−\log|w|_S)`, so
`\widehat{Γ_h}(τ) = \hat h(−τ)`, not `\hat h(τ)` (paper Lemma 1.1).  The
displayed formula is nevertheless **true**, because `\hat T_S` is even on
`𝔠_0`; but the evenness is a fact that has to be proved (it comes from
`θ_p = 0` on the principal component) and is now carried explicitly in
Theorem 12.2's proof.  Recorded as a needed correction, not a refutation.

### 2.7 One claim of the **previous Paper 45**, refuted on the declared carrier

Not a blueprint claim, but the largest single deletion: the old Theorem 4.2,
"the two halves of the Weil weight live at different places — `n^{−1/2}` is
archimedean and `\log p` is finite".  On the declared carrier **both factors
are local data of the same finite place `p`**: `p^{−k/2}` is the value of
`τ_p = |x|_p^{1/2}/|1−x|_p` on the shell `p^kZ_p^×` (Paper 43 Lemma 8.2(i))
and `\log p` is that shell's `d^*x`-volume (Paper 44 (3bis.8)).  The old claim
was an artefact of the withdrawn carrier's normalized representative
`h_S(n) = (1/n;1,1,\dots)`, which D128 `C6` had already flagged as a gauge
choice.  Paper 45 Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.2; ledger W45.4.

## 3. R132.000's supplement: verified, and where it was sharpened

I verified every clause of the supplement and used it as the spine of §9bis.
Three notes.

1. `Ŵ_p(τ) = f_p(s) + f_p(1−s)` needs the identification
   `ζ_p = p^{−1/2}\overline{η_p} = p^{−(1−s)}` on the principal component,
   which requires `θ_p = \arg∏_{q≠p}κ_q(p) = 0` there — true on `𝔠_0` and
   **false** on a ramified component, where `Ŵ_p ≡ −c(κ_p)\log p` is constant
   and **entire** (Paper 43 Thm 9.7b(a), last sentence).  So Theorem 9bis.2 is
   a statement about the principal component only, and the paper says so in
   Lemma 9bis.1's preamble.  This matters: the Tate poles exist on `𝔠_0` and
   nowhere else.
2. The supplement says "residue `−1` at `s = 1`".  Precisely: `f_p` has **no**
   pole at `s = 1` (its poles are at `s ∈ (2πi/\log p)Z`); the pole at `s = 1`
   of `Ŵ_p` comes from the reflected summand `f_p(1−s)`, with residue `−1`.
   Written that way in Lemma 9bis.1(iii).
3. The sign check the supplement asks for goes through: `T_∞(Γ_{F⋆F^†}) =
   2Φ_F(0)Φ_F(1) − Σ_ρΦ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`, matching P123.036's `q = −B_nuc` and
   Paper 44 (8bis.14e) `I_∂ = −Q` on the primitive sector (paper Lemma 1.3;
   script checks (5i),(5j), the latter converging to `1.4·10^{−9}` at 200
   zeros).

## 4. The four places I would press, as the auditor

**P1.  Theorem 9bis.2 is an identity between *formal* colimits, and the paper
must not let it masquerade as a statement about `T_∞`.**  `A(s)` is defined as
`a(s) + Σ_p f_p(s)`, convergent only on `Re s > 1`, and then *continued*.  But
`T_∞` is defined on `D_A^∞` by a **finite** sum at each test (Paper 43
Thm 8A.7c(b)), and its "symbol" is not a function at all (Theorem 9.6′,
`Ŵ_fin^∞(0) = +∞`).  So `T̂_∞^{mer}` is a *name for the continuation of the
Euler product*, not for a Fourier transform of `T_∞`.  The paper says this in
Corollary 9bis.3 — the two operations do not commute — but an auditor should
check that no later sentence uses `T̂_∞^{mer} ≡ 0` as if it were a statement
about the trace.  **My own answer:** nothing does; §9bis is used only by
§14.2's clause C.4 row and by Remark 9bis.5.  But the *rhetoric* of §0.1
item (3) is the weakest point in the paper, and I would tighten it before
granting anything on it.

**P2.  Theorem 9.6's chart clause, and whether `T_∞` is the right object for a
comparison theorem at all.**  (9.9) is a colimit statement and is **false** at
a finite chart outside the window `R(Γ_h) < \log ℓ_{next}(S)`.  Paper 43
§14.0a says every clause of row A is discharged at a **fixed finite chart**,
and that `𝓝_∞ = 0` is not claimed on the colimit.  So row C's central theorem
lives on an object where row A proves nothing about the radical, and
Theorem 12.2 (`𝓝_∩ = 0`) is a *chart* theorem.  An auditor should ask: is the
comparison theorem then a theorem about the declared carrier, or about an
additional structure on it?  **My own answer:** about the carrier *plus its
chart system*, which Paper 43 §14.0a declares to be part of the structure —
and the window statement 8A.7c(e) makes the two agree on an exhausting family
of tests.  But the answer is a reading of §14.0a, not a theorem, and I would
press it.

**P3.  The `C10` fix at (10.1) depends on `s_0² ∉ R`, and I did not verify
`106_11`'s variable.**  The fix is: prescribing the target value by real
`(a,b) ↦ a + b s_0²` is an `R²→C` isomorphism iff `1, s_0²` are `R`-linearly
independent, i.e. iff `s_0` is neither real nor purely imaginary.  I argued
that this holds because `ρ_0` is off the critical line **and** off the real
axis.  That argument is correct *given* that `106_11`'s variable `z` is the
centred one in which the critical line is `Re z = 0` and a real zero would be
`Im z = 0`; I read (20)–(21) and the surrounding construction but I did **not**
trace the change of variable from `ρ` to `z` through the whole of that note.
If the centring differs, the degenerate case `s_0² ∈ R` must be excluded by
hand.  **This is the one place in the paper where I am relying on a reading of
an external repository note rather than on a proof I wrote.**  I flag it here
rather than let an auditor find it.

**P4.  Theorem 12.2 versus Theorem 9.8: is the radial statement really
stronger, or did I smuggle in `D_A`?**  The proof takes `G = Γ_g` radial and
concludes `\hat h\,\hat T_S = 0` a.e. on `𝔠_0` from density of
`\{\hat g : g ∈ C_c^∞(R)\}` in the Schwartz space on `R`.  That is right, but
it uses that `\widehat{Γ_h}` is carried by `𝔠_0` **and** that `\hat T_S`
restricted to `𝔠_0` is not identically zero — the latter from Paper 43 (14.2),
which is a computation at `τ = 0` on the **principal** component with all
`κ_q = 1`.  An auditor should check that (14.2) is indeed a statement about
`𝔠_0` and not about the sup over all components (Paper 43 Theorem 9.7(c)
defines `m := \sup_{Ĉ_S}\hat T`, over **all** components, and (14.2) bounds
`m` from below by the value **at** `𝔠_0` — which is what Theorem 12.2 needs,
so the direction is right).  I checked this and it holds; but the two `m`'s
are easy to confuse and the paper leans on the distinction.
