## 2. Where I went beyond the brief, and one place where I disagree

### 2.1 Theorem 9bis.2′ is stated at **both** Tate points, not only at `s = 0`

`M3` asks for `Res_{s=0}A = 1` against `Res_{s=0}A_S = |S_f|+1`.  While
proving it I computed the other Tate point and found the **mirror** statement,
which the audit does not mention and which makes the section symmetric:

    Res_{s=1} A_S = 0   for EVERY finite chart,     Res_{s=1} A = 1 .

Reason: `a(1) = ½log π − ½ψ(½)` is finite and `f_p(1) = (log p)/(p−1)` is
finite, and `f_p`'s poles are at `s ∈ (2πi/log p)Z`, which never meets `s = 1`.
So a finite chart has **no pole at all** at `s = 1`; the pole is **created in
the limit**, by the pole of `ζ` at `s = 1`.

This is worth having for three reasons.  (i) It completes "the polar packet is
the pair of Tate poles with coefficient `1` each": one pole is archimedean in
origin (`s = 0`, where `−½ψ(s/2) = 1/s + O(1)`), the other is finite-place in
origin (`s = 1`, where `−ζ'/ζ(s) = 1/(s−1) + O(1)`), which is exactly the
`e_+`/`e_−` asymmetry of Remark 8.2.  (ii) It makes the two failures of
exchange **different and both visible**: at `s = 0` the chart **overcounts**
and the excess evaporates; at `s = 1` the chart **undercounts** and the pole
appears only at the end.  Remark 9bis.2a tabulates them.  (iii) It gives
Theorem 9bis.4(iii) — the Mertens clause — a precise job at last: Mertens is
the *rate* at which `A_S(1) = Σ_{p≤x} log p/(p−1) + a(1) ∼ log x` diverges as
the chart grows, i.e. the rate at which the chart symbols manufacture the
`s = 1` pole they do not have.  Script check (3e) prints the comparison.

### 2.2 Disagreement, recorded: I did **not** rename `Ξ` out of the paper

`M3`(i) suggests renaming `T̂_∞^{mer}` to `Ξ(s) := A(s)+A(1−s)`, "the
reflected continuation of the formal Euler symbol", and demoting the vanishing
to a corollary.  I did both, but I kept the statement in the paper rather than

symbol is manifestly of the form `(\cdot)(s) + (\cdot)(1−s)` — will ask what
that reflection means.  Remark 9bis.2b answers it in four lines and says
explicitly that no clause rests on it.  If the auditor wants it gone entirely
rather than demoted, say so and I will delete it; the paper loses nothing.
