# Jensen cofinal gate audit

## Purpose

The Fourier-Bochner audit leaves a stronger possible route: prove that the
completed function belongs to the Laguerre--Pólya class by hyperbolicity of
Jensen polynomials.  This document states the exact level of Jensen
hyperbolicity needed to close A1 and eliminates weaker finite or merely
asymptotic versions.

## Jensen polynomials

Let
\[
  \Xi(t)=\sum_{m\ge0} a_m t^{2m}
\]
be the even Taylor expansion of the completed function in the line variable.
For \(d,N\ge0\), define the Jensen polynomial
\[
  J_{\Xi}^{d,N}(X)
  =
  \sum_{j=0}^{d}{d\choose j}a_{N+j}X^j.
\]

The relevant real-zero target is:

\[
  \Xi\in{\rm LP},
\]
the Laguerre--Pólya class of locally uniform limits of real-rooted
polynomials.  For the completed zeta function this is equivalent to all
zeros of \(\Xi\) being real in the line variable, hence to RH and to
Omega7 by Li.

## Sufficient cofinal theorem

A Jensen route closes A1 if it proves a theorem of this strength:

For every degree \(d\ge1\) and every shift \(N\ge0\), the Jensen polynomial
\[
  J_{\Xi}^{d,N}
\]
is hyperbolic, and the associated Jensen approximants converge locally
uniformly to \(\Xi\) in the topology defining the Laguerre--Pólya class.

Then \(\Xi\in{\rm LP}\), all zeros of \(\Xi\) are real, Li positivity follows,
and A1 follows through the phase-102 assembly.

This is a valid force-RH target.  It is not circular if the hyperbolicity of
all Jensen polynomials is proved directly from Euler--Gamma data, without
using zero locations.

## Finite hyperbolicity is insufficient

Hyperbolicity of
\[
  J_{\Xi}^{d,N}
\]
for finitely many pairs \((d,N)\) cannot close A1.  A finite list of
hyperbolic Jensen polynomials constrains only finitely many algebraic
conditions on the Taylor coefficients.  It cannot exclude a later
coefficient pattern generated by an off-line zero.

Thus the following implication is false as a proof rule:
\[
  \hbox{many finite Jensen checks}
  \Longrightarrow
  \Xi\in{\rm LP}.
\]

Finite Jensen checks are consistency tests, not a proof of Omega7.

## Fixed-degree asymptotic hyperbolicity is insufficient

Another weaker statement is:

For each fixed degree \(d\), the polynomials \(J_{\Xi}^{d,N}\) are
hyperbolic for all sufficiently large \(N\).

This does not imply that all Jensen polynomials are hyperbolic.  It leaves
open:

1. finitely many exceptional shifts for each fixed \(d\);
2. dependence of the threshold on \(d\);
3. diagonal regimes where \(d\) and \(N\) grow together;
4. the local-uniform convergence step from Jensen data to \(\Xi\).

Any one of these gaps can carry the full A1 obstruction.  Therefore
fixed-degree asymptotic hyperbolicity alone does not close Omega7.

## Required strengthening

The useful Jensen theorem must include all of the following:

1. degree-uniform control, or a separate argument for every finite
   exceptional region;
2. compatibility with the limiting entire function \(\Xi\);
3. preservation of multiplicities and conjugation;
4. an off-line discriminator: the theorem must fail for a typed control with
   a zero quartet off the line;
5. no use of the desired zero location in proving hyperbolicity.

Without these items, the Jensen route is another coordinate change around
the same missing positivity.

## Relation to A1

The Jensen route does not naturally prove the compact A1 inequality
directly.  Instead it proves the stronger zero-side statement
\[
  \Xi\in{\rm LP}.
\]

Then
\[
  \Xi\in{\rm LP}
  \Longrightarrow
  \lambda_n\ge0\quad(n\ge1)
  \Longrightarrow
  \hbox{A1 through A0 and the finite certificate}.
\]

This is acceptable because the phase rules allow proving a stronger
RH-equivalent theorem.  It must simply be proved, not assumed.

## Eliminated class

The following proof pattern is eliminated:

1. prove or compute Jensen hyperbolicity for a large finite range;
2. quote fixed-degree asymptotic hyperbolicity;
3. infer RH, Omega7, or A1.

The missing step is cofinal hyperbolicity plus the limiting theorem into the
Laguerre--Pólya class.

## Status

Closed as an audit of the Jensen route.  The viable theorem is cofinal
Jensen hyperbolicity with Laguerre--Pólya convergence from Euler--Gamma data.
Finite and fixed-degree asymptotic Jensen results do not close A1.
