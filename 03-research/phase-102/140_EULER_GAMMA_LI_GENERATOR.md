# Euler--Gamma Li generator

## Purpose

The zero side of Li is now closed in phase 102.  The remaining task is the
arithmetic sign theorem.  This document writes the exact Euler--Gamma
generating function for the Li coefficients and separates the archimedean and
prime/pole parts in the paired normalization used by the phase.

## Li generating function

Set
\[
  s=s(z)={1\over1-z}.
\]

For
\[
  F(z)=\log\xi(s(z)),
\]
the Li coefficients are generated by
\[
  \mathcal L(z):=zF'(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_n z^n.
\tag{1}
\]

Since
\[
  {ds\over dz}={1\over(1-z)^2},
\]
one has the exact identity
\[
  \boxed{
  \mathcal L(z)
  =
  {z\over(1-z)^2}
  {\xi'\over\xi}\!\left({1\over1-z}\right).
  }
\tag{2}
\]

This is the arithmetic object whose coefficient positivity is Omega7.

## Completed logarithmic derivative

Using
\[
  \xi(s)=
  {1\over2}s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\]
we get
\[
  {\xi'\over\xi}(s)
  =
  {1\over s}
  +{1\over s-1}
  -{1\over2}\log\pi
  +{1\over2}\psi\!\left({s\over2}\right)
  +{\zeta'\over\zeta}(s).
\tag{3}
\]

The terms
\[
  {1\over s-1}
  \quad\hbox{and}\quad
  {\zeta'\over\zeta}(s)
\]
must remain paired at \(s=1\).  Separating them produces two divergent
boundary objects.  Pairing gives the analytic combination
\[
  {1\over s-1}+{\zeta'\over\zeta}(s).
\tag{4}
\]

## Phase split in generator form

Define
\[
  \mathcal A(z)
  =
  {z\over(1-z)^2}
  \left[
    {1\over s(z)}
    -{1\over2}\log\pi
    +{1\over2}\psi\!\left({s(z)\over2}\right)
  \right],
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
  \mathcal P(z)
  =
  {z\over(1-z)^2}
  \left[
    {1\over s(z)-1}
    +{\zeta'\over\zeta}(s(z))
  \right].
\tag{6}
\]

Then
\[
  \boxed{
  \mathcal L(z)=\mathcal A(z)+\mathcal P(z).
  }
\tag{7}
\]

Writing
\[
  \mathcal A(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_n^{\rm arch}z^n,
  \qquad
  \mathcal P(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_n^{\rm prime}z^n,
\]
recovers the phase-102 split
\[
  \lambda_n=\lambda_n^{\rm arch}+\lambda_n^{\rm prime}.
\]

## Euler-product side of the paired prime generator

For \(\Re s>1\),
\[
  {\zeta'\over\zeta}(s)
  =
  -\sum_{m\ge2}{\Lambda(m)\over m^s}.
\]

Thus, away from the boundary and before taking the paired continuation,
\[
  \mathcal P(z)
  =
  {z\over(1-z)^2}
  \left[
    {1\over s(z)-1}
    -
    \sum_{m\ge2}{\Lambda(m)\over m^{s(z)}}
  \right].
\tag{8}
\]

Because
\[
  s(z)-1={z\over1-z},
\]
the pole contribution is
\[
  {z\over(1-z)^2}{1\over s(z)-1}
  =
  {1\over1-z}.
\tag{9}
\]

This is exactly the generating-function version of the phase rule: the pole
and the prime sum are one object.  The coefficient extraction at the boundary
is the same paired finite-part procedure used in the Laguerre formula.

## A1 in generator language

The direct arithmetic target for \(n\ge8\) is
\[
  \lambda_n^{\rm prime}\ge-\lambda_n^{\rm arch}.
\tag{10}
\]

Equivalently, coefficient positivity of
\[
  \mathcal L(z)=\mathcal A(z)+\mathcal P(z)
\]
from index \(8\) onward, together with the finite certificate for
\[
  1\le n\le7,
\]
closes Omega7.

After A0, the same coefficient inequality is reduced to the compact signed
core A1:
\[
  -n+\int_1^{e^{T_n}}(\psi(y)-y)f'_{n,0}(y)\,dy
  +{3\over4}\lambda_n^{\rm arch}\ge0
  \qquad(n\ge8).
\tag{A1}
\]

Thus (7)--(9) are not a new proof of A1; they are the exact generator form
of the arithmetic sign problem.

## Why Euler-product coefficient positivity is not immediate

In the half-plane \(\Re s>1\), the Euler product gives a positive weight
\(\Lambda(m)\), but the generator contains the negative logarithmic
derivative and the pole compensation:
\[
  {1\over s-1}
  -
  \sum_{m\ge2}{\Lambda(m)\over m^s}.
\]

After composing with \(s=1/(1-z)\), coefficient positivity is a global
statement about the pole, primes, Gamma factor and boundary continuation.
It is not implied by \(\Lambda(m)\ge0\) termwise.

This is the generator version of the already recorded sign-preservation
obstruction.
