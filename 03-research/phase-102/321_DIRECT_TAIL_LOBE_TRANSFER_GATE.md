# Direct/tail lobe transfer gate

## Purpose

`298`--`299` put the direct compact route into oriented Laguerre lobe
blocks on \([T_8,T_n]\).  `320` puts the tail route into oriented Laguerre
tail lobes on \([T_n,\infty)\).

This note records the transfer rule between those two lobe descriptions:
they are equivalent only after the full compact A1 identity is assembled.
There is no positive lobe-by-lobe transfer from one side to the other that
avoids the missing signed theorem.

## Direct lobe carrier

The direct high-block carrier from `298` is
\[
  \omega_n(u)=G_{n-1}(T_n)-G_{n-1}(u),
  \qquad
  G_{n-1}(u)=e^{-u}L_{n-1}^{(1)}(u),
\]
on
\[
  T_8\le u\le T_n.
\]

The direct lobe theorem asks for
\[
\boxed{
  H_n^+-H_n^-+B_n^{\rm base}\ge0.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, by `299`, it asks for an oriented Chebyshev-error inequality
over the compact lobe blocks of \(\omega_n\).

## Tail lobe carrier

The tail carrier from `320` is
\[
  K_n(u)=e^{-u}L_{n-1}^{(2)}(u)
\]
on
\[
  u\ge T_n.
\]

The tail-lobe theorem asks for
\[
\boxed{
  I_n(T_n)
  =
  \int_{T_n}^{\infty}E(e^u)K_n(u)\,du
  \ge
  \left(d_n-\frac14\right)A_n.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Equivalently, by `280`, it asks for the signed phase-lobe dominance
\[
  P^-_{n,T_n}-P^+_{n,T_n}
  \ge
  {1\over2}
  \left(
    \mathcal T_{n,T_n}+
    \left(d_n-\frac14\right)A_n
  \right).
\]

## No positive transfer

The direct carrier and the tail carrier differ in three structural ways:

1. their supports are disjoint except for the moving boundary \(T_n\);
2. their sign partitions are generated by different equations,
   \[
     G_{n-1}(u)=G_{n-1}(T_n)
     \quad\hbox{versus}\quad
     L_{n-1}^{(2)}(u)=0;
   \]
3. integration by parts connects them through boundary and pole terms,
   not through a positive kernel.

Indeed,
\[
  \omega_n'(u)=e^{-u}L_{n-1}^{(2)}(u)=K_n(u)
\]
on the compact interval.  This derivative relation changes sign at the
zeros of \(L_{n-1}^{(2)}\) and introduces boundary terms when integrated
against \(\psi(e^u)-e^u\).  Therefore it is not a positivity-preserving
map from compact lobe dominance to tail lobe dominance, or conversely.

## Exact transfer is just A1 bookkeeping

Both lobe routes enter the same compact identity
\[
\boxed{
  C_n(T_n)=\lambda_n-\frac14A_n-R_n(T_n).
}
\tag{3}
\]

The direct lobe theorem proves
\[
  C_n(T_n)\ge0
\]
by evaluating the compact prime-power certificate.  The tail lobe theorem
proves the same inequality through
\[
  R_n(T_n)\le \lambda_n-\frac14A_n.
\]

Thus the only exact transfer statement is:
\[
\boxed{
  \hbox{direct lobe dominance}
  \Longleftrightarrow
  C_n(T_n)\ge0
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \hbox{tail lobe dominance with the margin target.}
}
\tag{4}
\]

But using (4) to prove either lobe dominance from the other is circular
unless one has already proved \(C_n(T_n)\ge0\).

## Consequence for proof attempts

A proof may close compact A1 by either of these independent signed inputs:

1. the direct compact lobe inequality of `298`/`299`;
2. the tail one-sided lobe inequality of `320`;
3. the phase-lobe dominance of `280`;
4. the normalized margin-tail inequality \(s_n\ge d_n\).

What is not allowed is to prove only an unsigned or averaged statement on
one lobe system and transfer it to the other by the derivative relation
\(\omega_n'=K_n\).  That derivative relation is algebraic, not positive.
It preserves the exact identity only after all boundary, pole,
archimedean, and margin terms are included.

## Status

Closed as the transfer gate between the direct compact lobe route and the
tail lobe route.  A1 remains open until one of the oriented signed
inequalities above is proved non-circularly.
