# 107.03 -- Paper A, Part I: the free arithmetic divisor module

## 1. Purpose

This note executes Work Package I-A of 107.00.  Its role is modest but
foundational: it fixes the source algebra in which finite-support
arithmetic Lefschetz divisors are allowed to live before any local
intersection line or Green metric is attached.

The package must separate three different operations:

\[
 \text{composition of correspondences},
 \qquad
 \text{Euler disjoint union},
 \qquad
 \text{connected extraction}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The stop rule is that the first Eulerian idempotent may act only on the
Euler union side.  It may not be inserted into categorical composition.

## 3. The raw decorated correspondence module

Let

\[
 \mathcal P=\{(p,k): p \text{ prime},\ k\ge1\}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

For each \(a=(p,k)\in\mathcal P\), introduce two oriented raw
correspondence symbols

\[
 \Gamma_a,\qquad \Gamma_a^t,
 \tag{3.2}
\]

together with four place-independent symbols

\[
 F_{\mathrm v},\qquad F_{\mathrm h},\qquad \Delta,\qquad Z_\infty.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

For a finite support set \(S\subset\mathcal P\), define the free abelian
module

\[
 \mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)
 =
 \mathbf Z F_{\mathrm v}
 \oplus \mathbf Z F_{\mathrm h}
 \oplus \mathbf Z \Delta
 \oplus \mathbf Z Z_\infty
 \oplus \bigoplus_{a\in S}(\mathbf Z\Gamma_a\oplus\mathbf Z\Gamma_a^t).
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The finite-support raw correspondence module is the filtered union

\[
 \mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}
 =\varinjlim_{S\Subset\mathcal P}\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}(S).
 \tag{3.5}
\]

### Definition 3.1: raw transpose

The transpose involution
\(\tau_{\mathrm{raw}}:\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}
\to\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}\) is defined by

\[
 \tau_{\mathrm{raw}}(F_{\mathrm v})=F_{\mathrm h},
 \qquad
 \tau_{\mathrm{raw}}(F_{\mathrm h})=F_{\mathrm v},
 \qquad
 \tau_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Delta)=\Delta,
 \qquad
 \tau_{\mathrm{raw}}(Z_\infty)=Z_\infty,
 \tag{3.6}
\]

and

\[
 \tau_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Gamma_a)=\Gamma_a^t,
 \qquad
 \tau_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Gamma_a^t)=\Gamma_a.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

This is the minimal source-level record that orientation exists before
connected extraction.

### Definition 3.2: raw bidegree

Define the raw bidegree map

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}=(d_{\mathrm v},d_{\mathrm h}):
 \mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}\to\mathbf Z^2
 \tag{3.8}
\]

on generators by

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(F_{\mathrm v})=(0,1),
 \qquad
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(F_{\mathrm h})=(1,0),
 \qquad
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Delta)=(1,1),
 \tag{3.9}
\]

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Gamma_{p,k})=(1,p^k),
 \qquad
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Gamma_{p,k}^t)=(p^k,1),
 \qquad
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(Z_\infty)=(0,0).
 \tag{3.10}
\]

The archimedean symbol \(Z_\infty\) is kept degree-neutral at this stage:
its geometric contribution is metric, not algebraic.

### Definition 3.3: balanced weight

The balanced weight is the map

\[
 w:\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}\to\mathbf R_{>0}
 \tag{3.11}
\]

specified by

\[
 w(\Gamma_{p,k})=w(\Gamma_{p,k}^t)=p^{-k/2},
 \qquad
 w(F_{\mathrm v})=w(F_{\mathrm h})=w(\Delta)=w(Z_\infty)=1.
 \tag{3.12}
\]

It is read off from the raw bidegree:
\((d_{\mathrm v}d_{\mathrm h})^{-1/2}=p^{-k/2}\) on \(\Gamma_{p,k}\).

## 4. Euler union and the connected projector

Let \(X_{p,k}\) be a formal symbol for the connected \(k\)-fold return at
the prime \(p\).  For finite \(S\subset\mathcal P\), let

\[
 \mathcal H_S=\mathrm{Sym}\,\!\left(
 \bigoplus_{a\in S}\mathbf Q X_a
 \right)
 \tag{4.1}
\]

be the commutative Hopf algebra of Euler unions of return symbols, with
the generators \(X_a\) primitive:

\[
 \Delta_{\mathcal H}(X_a)=X_a\otimes1+1\otimes X_a.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Denote by \(I\) its identity endomorphism.  The first Eulerian
idempotent is

\[
 \mathfrak e_1=\log^\star(I)
 =\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{r-1}}{r}(I-\eta\varepsilon)^{\star r}.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

### Proposition 4.1: primitive extraction

On \(\mathcal H_S\), the map \(\mathfrak e_1\) satisfies:

\[
 \mathfrak e_1(X_a)=X_a,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak e_1(X_{a_1}\cdots X_{a_r})=0
 \quad(r\ge2).
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Proof.  This is the standard property of the first Eulerian idempotent
on a commutative Hopf algebra generated by primitive elements: its image
is the primitive subspace, and every product of two or more primitive
generators lies in the decomposable part annihilated by \(\mathfrak e_1\).
\(\square\)

Thus connected extraction is a Hopf-algebra operation on Euler unions.
It is not an operation on correspondence composition.

## 5. The connected divisor module

For finite \(S\subset\mathcal P\), define

\[
 \mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}(S)
 =
 \mathbf Z F_{\mathrm v}
 \oplus \mathbf Z F_{\mathrm h}
 \oplus \mathbf Z \Delta
 \oplus \mathbf Z Z_\infty
 \oplus \bigoplus_{a\in S}\mathbf Z Z_a,
 \tag{5.1}
\]

where \(Z_a\) is the connected divisor symbol attached to the primitive
return \(X_a\).  The finite-support source divisor module is

\[
 \mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}
 =\varinjlim_{S\Subset\mathcal P}\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}(S).
 \tag{5.2}
\]

### Definition 5.1: connected trace

Define the connected trace map

\[
 \mathrm{ctr}:
 \mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}\to\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}
 \tag{5.3}
\]

on generators by

\[
 \mathrm{ctr}(F_{\mathrm v})=F_{\mathrm v},
 \quad
 \mathrm{ctr}(F_{\mathrm h})=F_{\mathrm h},
 \quad
 \mathrm{ctr}(\Delta)=\Delta,
 \quad
 \mathrm{ctr}(Z_\infty)=Z_\infty,
 \tag{5.4}
\]

\[
 \mathrm{ctr}(\Gamma_{p,k})
 =\mathrm{ctr}(\Gamma_{p,k}^t)
 =Z_{p,k}.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

The point is not that transpose disappears from the raw category; it is
that the fixed-orbit class recorded after connected cyclic trace is
orientation-free.

### Definition 5.2: divisor degree and length

The source degree on \(\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) is the pair of
maps

\[
 \deg=(d,\ell):
 \mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}
 \to \mathbf Z[\mathcal P]\oplus\mathbf R.
 \tag{5.6}
\]

On generators:

\[
 d(F_{\mathrm v})=d(F_{\mathrm h})=d(\Delta)=d(Z_\infty)=0,
 \qquad
 d(Z_{p,k})=p^k,
 \tag{5.7}
\]

\[
 \ell(F_{\mathrm v})=\ell(F_{\mathrm h})=\ell(\Delta)=0,
 \qquad
 \ell(Z_\infty)=1,
 \qquad
 \ell(Z_{p,k})=k\log p.
 \tag{5.8}
\]

The algebraic divisor module therefore remembers, before any metric is
added, which prime tower and which logarithmic return length are present.

### Proposition 5.1: finite support

Every element of \(\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) has only finitely
many nonzero coefficients of the form \(Z_{p,k}\).

Proof.  By construction,
\(\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) is the filtered union of the finite
modules \(\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}(S)\).  Each element belongs to
one such finite stage.  \(\square\)

This is the module-theoretic shadow of the Phase 107 principle that a
compactly supported test sees only finitely many prime-power returns.

## 6. The source package summarized

The actual deliverable of Work Package I-A is the quintuple

\[
 \bigl(
 \mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}},
 \tau_{\mathrm{raw}},
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}},
 \mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}},
 \mathrm{ctr}
 \bigr),
 \tag{6.1}
\]

with the following meaning:

1. \(\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}\) stores oriented source
   correspondences and therefore admits transpose.
2. \(\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) stores connected fixed-orbit
   divisors with finite support.
3. \(\mathrm{ctr}\,\) is the bridge from raw correspondences to
   connected orbit divisors.
4. The Eulerian idempotent acts on the Euler Hopf algebra generating the
   \(Z_{p,k}\), not on categorical composition.

That separation is exactly what the program needs later in Paper B, where
composition and connected cyclic trace must coexist without being
identified.

## 7. Function-field specialization

Paper 0 supplies the positive control.  On a curve \(C/\mathbb F_q\),
replace \(\Gamma_{p,k}\) by the oriented graph \(\Gamma_{F^{kd}}\) of a
degree-\(d\) primitive closed point iterated \(k\) times.  Then:

1. raw transpose becomes graph transposition, hence Verschiebung;
2. raw bidegree becomes \((1,q^{kd})\) and \((q^{kd},1)\);
3. the balanced weight becomes \(q^{-kd/2}\);
4. the connected trace records one primitive closed point of degree \(d\)
   and its iterates;
5. the Eulerian projector turns
   \(\prod_d(1-u^d)^{-B_d}\) into
   \(\sum_d B_d u^d\) after logarithm.

Thus the source package specializes literally to the geometric chain
proved in 107.02.

## 8. Davenport--Heilbronn audit

The construction is required to fail before positivity if one replaces
\(\zeta\) by a function without an Euler orbit structure.  That happens
at the exact intended point.

### Proposition 8.1: failure point for Davenport--Heilbronn

If the arithmetic object has no Euler product by primitive prime towers,
then the Hopf algebra \(\mathcal H_S\) of connected prime-power returns
is undefined, and therefore \(\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) cannot
be formed.

Proof.  The symbols \(X_{p,k}\) are not formal decoration added after the
fact; they encode the primitive return decomposition indexed by prime
towers.  Without a genuine Euler orbit structure there is no canonical
indexing set \(\mathcal P\), no primitive generators \(X_{p,k}\), hence
no Eulerian idempotent and no connected-return basis \(Z_{p,k}\).
Therefore the failure occurs before any Hodge sign or explicit-formula
comparison is invoked.  \(\square\)

This is exactly the mandated falsifier: the package breaks at the orbit
input, not at a later positivity theorem.

## 9. Stop-test audit

Work Package I-A passes its three stop tests.

### Stop test 1

The relations do not identify connected and disconnected returns.

Reason.  The connected symbols \(Z_{p,k}\) are introduced only after
\(\mathfrak e_1\) is applied to the Euler Hopf algebra.  No relation in
\(\mathrm{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) equates \(Z_{p,k}\) with a product
of two or more primitive return symbols.

### Stop test 2

Transpose is defined before connected extraction.

Reason.  The transpose \(\tau_{\mathrm{raw}}\) is part of
\(\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathrm{raw}}\), while connected extraction is the
separate map \(\mathrm{ctr}\,\) built only afterward.

### Stop test 3

The construction fails for Davenport--Heilbronn exactly where the Euler
orbit structure is requested.

Reason.  Proposition 8.1 shows that the failure occurs already at the
definition of the primitive return Hopf algebra.
