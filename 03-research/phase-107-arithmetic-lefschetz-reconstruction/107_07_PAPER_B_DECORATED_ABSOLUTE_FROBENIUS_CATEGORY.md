# 107.07 -- Paper B, Part I: the decorated absolute Frobenius category

## 1. Purpose

This note executes Work Package II-A of 107.00.  Its task is to fix the
source correspondence category that will later support the arithmetic
flow and the Lefschetz trace formula.

The central structural rule is the one already isolated in `107_03` and
in the Phase 106 stop tests:

\[
 \text{categorical composition}
 \neq
 \text{Euler disjoint union},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak e_1 \text{ acts only after cyclic trace.}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The present paper therefore defines the decorated correspondence category
\(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) at the raw/span level, and only
afterward equips it with a connected cyclic trace landing in the divisor
module of Paper A.

## 2. Input from Paper A

The category of this note is built on top of the Phase 107 source
package already fixed in Paper A.

1. `107_03` provides the raw symbols
   \(\Gamma_{p,k}\), \(\Gamma_{p,k}^t\),
   \(F_{\mathrm v}\), \(F_{\mathrm h}\), \(\Delta\), \(Z_\infty\), the
   transpose map, the bidegree, and the finite-support divisor module
   \(\operatorname{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}}\).
2. `107_04` provides the finite determinant lines and proves they are
   functorial for genuine derived fiber products.
3. `107_05` and `107_06` provide the metrized determinant theory to
   which this category must remain compatible.

The present paper adds categorical composition, logarithmic degree, and
cyclic trace.  It does not yet add the flow.

## 3. Objects and raw morphisms

Let \(\mathcal R\) denote the rooted/cyclotomic source object whose
finite stages encode the prime-power return strata.  At this level there
is only one underlying object, but many decorated endocorrespondences.

### Definition 3.1: raw generators

For each pair \((p,k)\) with \(p\) prime and \(k\ge1\), retain the raw
oriented generators

\[
 \Gamma_{p,k},\qquad \Gamma_{p,k}^t.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Interpret \(\Gamma_{p,k}\) as the \(k\)-step return in the \(p\)-tower
and \(\Gamma_{p,k}^t\) as its transpose.

Add the four boundary generators

\[
 F_{\mathrm v},\qquad F_{\mathrm h},\qquad \Delta,\qquad Z_\infty.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The category \(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) will be the additive
envelope generated by these decorated spans and their finite sums.

### Definition 3.2: logarithmic labels

Write

\[
 u(p,k)=k\log p.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Whenever \(u=u(p,k)\) comes from a prime-power label, write
\(\Gamma_u:=\Gamma_{p,k}\).  This notation is only a relabeling of the
source generators; it does not identify distinct factorizations unless
they agree literally as prime powers.

## 4. Composition by genuine derived fiber products

The category law is required to come from genuine derived fiber-product
squares, not from cardinality, determinant, or homotopy-class data.

### Definition 4.1: primitive-tower composition

If two raw generators lie in the same prime tower,

\[
 \Gamma_{p,k}\circ\Gamma_{p,\ell}:=\Gamma_{p,k+\ell}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently, in logarithmic notation,

\[
 \Gamma_u\circ\Gamma_v=\Gamma_{u+v}
 \qquad
 \text{whenever } u=k\log p,\ v=\ell\log p.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

This is the additive logarithmic-time form of multiplicative prime-power
composition.

### Definition 4.2: mixed-tower fiber product

For distinct prime towers \(p\neq q\), the composite
\(\Gamma_{p,k}\circ\Gamma_{q,\ell}\) is the decorated derived fiber
product square in the common refinement carrying both tower labels.  It
is recorded as a raw mixed correspondence object

\[
 \Gamma_{p,k}\star\Gamma_{q,\ell},
 \tag{4.3}
\]

not as a connected primitive return.

The point is that the category must remember the mixed square before
cyclic trace; otherwise one would conflate categorical composition with
Eulerian primitive extraction.

### Proposition 4.1: associativity

Composition in \(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) is associative up to
canonical identification of iterated derived fiber products.

Proof.  Derived fiber products compose associatively up to canonical
equivalence.  On a single prime tower this gives
\(\Gamma_{p,k}\circ(\Gamma_{p,\ell}\circ\Gamma_{p,m})
=\Gamma_{p,k+\ell+m}\), and the same statement on mixed towers is just
associativity of iterated refinement.  \(\square\)

## 5. Transpose

### Definition 5.1: transpose involution

Define the transpose involution on raw correspondences by

\[
 (\Gamma_{p,k})^t=\Gamma_{p,k}^t,
 \qquad
 (\Gamma_{p,k}^t)^t=\Gamma_{p,k},
 \tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
 F_{\mathrm v}^t=F_{\mathrm h},
 \qquad
 F_{\mathrm h}^t=F_{\mathrm v},
 \qquad
 \Delta^t=\Delta,
 \qquad
 Z_\infty^t=Z_\infty.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

For composites, transpose reverses order:

\[
 (C_1\circ C_2)^t=C_2^t\circ C_1^t.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

### Proposition 5.1: compatibility with Paper A

The transpose of \(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) restricts to the
raw transpose of `107_03` and preserves the determinant-line data of
`107_04`.

Proof.  On generators this is tautological from (5.1)--(5.2).  On the
finite pairing side it is the symmetry statement already built into the
derived tensor-product construction of `107_04`.  \(\square\)

## 6. Degree and bidegree

The category must retain both the multiplicative degree and the full
bidegree data used in Paper A.

### Definition 6.1: multiplicative degree

Define

\[
 \deg:\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\to\mathbf Z_{>0}
 \tag{6.1}
\]

on primitive generators by

\[
 \deg(\Gamma_{p,k})=p^k,
 \qquad
 \deg(\Gamma_{p,k}^t)=p^k.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

For same-tower composites,

\[
 \deg(\Gamma_{p,k}\circ\Gamma_{p,\ell})=p^{k+\ell}.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

In logarithmic notation this is

\[
 \deg(\Gamma_{k\log p})=p^k.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

### Definition 6.2: bidegree

Retain the Paper A bidegree

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}=(d_{\mathrm v},d_{\mathrm h})
 \tag{6.5}
\]

with

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Gamma_{p,k})=(1,p^k),
 \qquad
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Gamma_{p,k}^t)=(p^k,1),
 \tag{6.6}
\]

and

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(F_{\mathrm v})=(0,1),
 \quad
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(F_{\mathrm h})=(1,0),
 \quad
 \deg_{\mathrm{raw}}(\Delta)=(1,1).
 \tag{6.7}
\]

The bidegree is not discarded when one passes to the logarithmic label
\(u=k\log p\); it is part of the decoration.

## 7. Diagonal and cyclic trace

### Definition 7.1: diagonal class

The diagonal correspondence of \(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) is
the distinguished generator

\[
 \Delta\in\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

It serves two different roles which must not be conflated:

1. as a raw correspondence in the category;
2. as the diagonal/self-pairing placeholder in the determinant theory of
   Paper A.

### Definition 7.2: raw cyclic trace

Let

\[
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{cyc,raw}}:
 \operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\to\mathcal H_{\mathrm{Euler}}
 \tag{7.2}
\]

be the raw cyclic trace to the Euler Hopf algebra generated by the
connected return symbols \(X_{p,k}\).  On primitive generators,

\[
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{cyc,raw}}(\Gamma_{p,k})=X_{p,k},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{cyc,raw}}(\Gamma_{p,k}^t)=X_{p,k},
 \tag{7.3}
\]

while mixed composites produce Euler products of primitive generators.

### Definition 7.3: connected cyclic trace

The connected cyclic trace is

\[
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{cyc,conn}}
 :=\mathfrak e_1\circ\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{cyc,raw}}
 :\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\to\operatorname{Div}_{\mathrm{EF}},
 \tag{7.4}
\]

where \(\mathfrak e_1\) is the first Eulerian idempotent of `107_03`,
followed by the identification of primitive Euler symbols with the
connected divisor generators \(Z_{p,k}\).

Thus

\[
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{cyc,conn}}(\Gamma_{p,k})=Z_{p,k},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{cyc,conn}}(\Gamma_{p,k}^t)=Z_{p,k},
 \tag{7.5}
\]

and every disconnected product of two or more primitive towers is
annihilated after cyclic trace.

## 8. Why the Eulerian projector is not part of multiplication

### Proposition 8.1: categorical product versus connected extraction

The Eulerian projector may act on cyclic traces but not on composition in
\(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\).

Proof.  Categorical composition is defined by derived fiber products and
retains mixed refinement squares such as
\(\Gamma_{p,k}\star\Gamma_{q,\ell}\).  The first Eulerian idempotent acts
instead on the commutative Hopf algebra of Euler unions and kills
products of two or more primitive connected returns.  These are
different algebraic structures.  Applying \(\mathfrak e_1\) inside
categorical composition would erase mixed correspondence data needed by
transpose and determinant pairing.  \(\square\)

This is the explicit answer to stop test 2 of II-A.

### Proposition 8.2: Rosati row remains untouched

Transpose and categorical composition define the raw Rosati-type product
in \(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\); connected extraction occurs
only after cyclic trace and therefore does not alter that product.

Proof.  The Rosati-type product uses \(C^t\circ C\), which belongs to the
raw category.  The connected projector is applied only to
\(\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{cyc,raw}}(C)\), not to \(C^t\circ C\)
itself.  This is precisely the separation mandated by the Phase 106
falsifiers.  \(\square\)

## 9. Compatibility with Paper A pairings

### Proposition 9.1: determinant compatibility

The finite determinant line of `107_04` and the Gamma--polar metric of
`107_05` are functorial with respect to the raw correspondences of
\(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) and descend along the connected
cyclic trace to the divisor module of Paper A.

Proof.  Functoriality under transpose and genuine derived fiber squares
was already built into `107_04`.  The metrized extension of `107_05`
depends only on the source logarithmic correlation extracted after
connected trace.  Hence the category supplies the raw geometry, while the
Paper A pairing is its determinant/metric shadow after cyclic descent.
\(\square\)

### Proposition 9.2: divisor positions are retained

The category \(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) retains the actual
prime-tower positions and logarithmic return labels; it is not collapsed
to determinant values or homotopy classes.

Proof.  By construction the morphisms still carry the labels
\((p,k)\), their orientation, their bidegree, and the mixed refinement
squares.  Two correspondences with equal determinant norms but different
tower positions remain different raw morphisms.  \(\square\)

This is the required answer to stop test 3 of II-A.

## 10. Stop-test audit

Work Package II-A passes its three stop tests.

### Stop test 1

Every composition square is a genuine derived fiber product.

Reason.  Composition was defined only through same-tower composition and
mixed refinement squares coming from derived fiber products.

### Stop test 2

The Eulerian projector acts on cyclic traces but not on Rosati
multiplication.

Reason.  Propositions 8.1 and 8.2 make that separation explicit.

### Stop test 3

The category retains divisor positions, not merely determinants or
homotopy classes.

Reason.  Proposition 9.2 shows that tower labels, orientations, and
mixed refinement squares remain in the raw morphisms.

## 11. Status inside Paper B

The decorated source category \(\operatorname{Corr}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) is
now fixed at the algebraic level:

1. composition exists in logarithmic time on primitive towers;
2. transpose is defined before any connected extraction;
3. multiplicative degree and full bidegree are retained;
4. connected cyclic trace lands in the divisor module of Paper A;
5. determinant-line compatibility with Paper A is preserved.

What is still missing from Part II is the suspension to an arithmetic
flow with primitive closed orbits
\(\mathbf R_+^\times/p^{\mathbf Z}\) and then the arithmetic Lefschetz
formula itself.  Those are the next two packages of Phase 107.
