# 107.144 -- Local Neron component no-go and rejection of A5

## 1. Result

This note closes the legacy local row of Work Package I-B in the precise
category in which that row was supposed to operate: local degree-zero
Neron/Arakelov pairings on regular minimal models, functorial under local
base change.

The result has two parts.

**Theorem 1 (local no-factorization).**  The local vertical-correction
functor for degree-zero horizontal divisors does not factor through

\[
 (p,v(c_4),v(c_6),v(\Delta),v(j),\mathrm{Kod},f_p,a_p,L_p).
 \tag{1.1}
\]

In particular, a source row containing only the prime/Euler/valuative
data in (1.1) cannot recover the full local pairing required on the
classical Faltings--Hriljac target.  It must either retain component-group
descent data or prove that its realized divisors lie in a smaller
component-trivial subspace.

**Theorem 2 (A5 rejection).**  The residue tuple

\[
 \rho_{32}(E)=\mathbf a_{\min}\pmod {32}
 \tag{1.2}
\]

does not descend under integral admissible changes of minimal
Weierstrass equation.  It is therefore neither an invariant local source
symbol nor a function of the zeta/L-data of the curve.  The A5 branch of
`107_134`--`107_143` is rejected in its current form.

These statements do not construct the Phase 107 surface or its divisor
map.  They close the legacy row for the full local target and remove A5
as a proposed repair.

## 2. Standard local correction

Let \(\mathcal E/\mathcal O_K\) be a regular minimal model and let
\(D\) be a horizontal divisor of degree zero.  There is a vertical
\(\mathbf Q\)-divisor \(\Phi(D)\), unique modulo the complete fiber,
such that

\[
 (D+\Phi(D))\cdot \Gamma=0
 \tag{2.1}
\]

for every irreducible vertical component \(\Gamma\).  This is the local
vertical correction in the Gross--Hriljac description of the Neron
pairing.  If the identity component is removed and \(M\) is the
remaining negative-definite component-intersection matrix, then (2.1)
is the exact linear system

\[
 M a=-b,
 \tag{2.2}
\]

where \(b_j=D\cdot\Gamma_j\).  Thus the correction depends on both the
intersection matrix and the component-incidence vector of \(D\).

For a fiber of type \(IV^*\), the seven geometric components have affine
\(E_6\) intersection matrix

\[
 M_{\widetilde E_6}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -2&0&1&0&0&0&0\\
 0&-2&0&1&0&0&0\\
 1&0&-2&0&1&0&0\\
 0&1&0&-2&1&0&0\\
 0&0&1&1&-2&1&0\\
 0&0&0&0&1&-2&1\\
 0&0&0&0&0&1&-2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Its kernel is generated by the fiber multiplicities

\[
 (1,1,2,2,3,2,1).
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Take \(\Gamma_0\) to be the identity component.  Deleting it gives
\(M=-C_{E_6}\), where

\[
 \det C_{E_6}=3.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

The other multiplicity-one components are \(\Gamma_1\) and
\(\Gamma_6\).  They represent the two nonzero classes in the geometric
component group \(\mathbf Z/3\mathbf Z\), and exact inversion gives

\[
 e_1^tC_{E_6}^{-1}e_1
 =e_6^tC_{E_6}^{-1}e_6
 =\frac43.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

Consequently a section on either nonidentity multiplicity-one component
has nonzero vertical correction of magnitude
\(\frac43\log N\mathfrak p\), while a section in the identity component
has zero component correction.  This calculation uses the component
matrix, not Sage's normalized `local_height`.

## 3. The forcing pair over Q_2

The verifier recomputes from the real Cremona curves:

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
 &v(c_4)&v(c_6)&v(\Delta)&v(j)&\mathrm{Kod}&f_2&a_2&L_2&c_2\\
\hline
20a1&4&6&8&4&IV^*&2&0&1&3\\
36a4&4&6&8&4&IV^*&2&0&1&1.
\end{array}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus (1.1) is equal for the two curves.

On `20a1`, the rational points

\[
 P_\pm=(0,\pm2)
 \tag{3.2}
\]

have order three and do not have smooth reduction at 2.  Since the
component quotient has order three, they represent the two nonidentity
classes.  For \(D_{20}=(P_+)-(O)\), equations (2.2)--(2.6) give the
nonzero component correction \(4\log(2)/3\).

On `36a4`, \(c_2=1\), so

\[
 E(\mathbf Q_2)/E^0(\mathbf Q_2)=0.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Every \(\mathbf Q_2\)-rational section meets the identity component.
The rational point \(T=(-6,0)\) has order two and smooth reduction, and
the component correction for \(D_{36}=(T)-(O)\) is zero.  Hence the
two curves have different local vertical-correction functors despite
having the same packet (1.1).

This is stronger than comparing the Tamagawa numbers as table entries:
the difference is detected by an actual degree-zero intersection
calculation.

## 4. Explicit unramified splitting extension

The scarcity of rational sections on `36a4` is part of the descent
phenomenon, not an absence of geometric components.  Fix before the
calculation

\[
 K=\mathbf Q_2(\omega),\qquad \omega^2+\omega+1=0.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

This is the unramified quadratic extension: \(e=1\), \(f=2\).  Sage
recomputes type \(IV^*\) for both curves and obtains \(c_2=3\) for both
after base change.  On `36a4/K`, the explicit sections

\[
 Q_\pm=(-9,\pm(12\omega+6))
 \tag{4.2}
\]

have order three and nonsmooth reduction.  They meet the two nonidentity
components and are exchanged by the nontrivial Galois automorphism
\(\omega\mapsto-1-\omega\).

Equivalently, Frobenius acts trivially on the geometric
\(\mathbf Z/3\mathbf Z\) for `20a1`, but by inversion for `36a4`.
The fixed subgroups therefore have orders three and one, respectively.
The local correction is sensitive to precisely the Galois descent data
that (1.1) omits.

## 5. No-factorization conclusion

Let \(\mathscr N(E/K)\) denote the degree-zero local correction functor:
it records \(\Phi(D)\) and the induced vertical contribution for
horizontal degree-zero divisors.  If a rule

\[
 \mathscr N(E/K)=F(S(E/K))
 \tag{5.1}
\]

factored through the packet \(S\) in (1.1), equal packets would give
equal correction functors.  Section 3 gives equal packets but one
functor has a rational component correction \(4\log(2)/3\) and the
other has only the zero component class over \(\mathbf Q_2\).  This
contradicts (5.1).

Therefore component-group descent data are necessary for the full
local Neron/Arakelov target.  Alternative (B) of the tranche is not
available for that target.

There is one exact scope boundary.  A future Phase 107 realization
could attempt to prove that every realized \(D_f\) is component-trivial
at every finite place.  No current construction proves this, and it
would be a genuine target-retraction theorem, not a reinterpretation of
the present row.  Until such a theorem exists, the legacy row remains
closed by the no-go above.

## 6. A5 does not descend

For a minimal Weierstrass equation, an admissible change with \(u=1\)

\[
 x'=x+r,\qquad y'=y+s x+t,
 \tag{6.1}
\]

preserves the discriminant and hence local minimality when
\(r,s,t\) are integral.  The verifier applies the single fixed change
\((r,s,t)=(1,0,0)\).

For `20a1@2` it gives

\[
 (0,1,0,4,4)\longmapsto(0,4,0,9,10)\pmod {32},
 \tag{6.2}
\]

and both equations are integral with \(v_2(\Delta)=8\).  For `36a4@2`
it likewise gives

\[
 (0,0,0,-135,-594)\longmapsto(0,3,0,-132,-728),
 \tag{6.3}
\]

again without changing local minimality.  Thus \(\rho_{32}\) is
coordinate-dependent.

The two equations in each line define isomorphic elliptic curves and
have the same zeta and local Euler data.  Since \(\rho_{32}\) changes,
it cannot be defined from zeta data alone.  A5 both fails descent and
reverses the required source order by reading a chosen model before a
model has been constructed.

## 7. Fixed controls and falsifiers

1. `20a1@2` and `36a4@2` as the forcing pair;
2. `14a5@7` and `21a1@7` as a split/nonsplit multiplicative control;
3. `14a1@5` as a supersingular control;
4. \(y^2=x^5+x+1\) over \(\mathbf F_5\) as a genus-two control.

The Davenport--Heilbronn and zero-free rules are unchanged.  This note
uses no zero, Li coefficient, Weil-form sign, or prescribed trace.  It
does not claim a construction for any later paper.
