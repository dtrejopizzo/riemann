# 107.20 -- Paper C, Part VIII: local construction of the packet intersection line

## 1. Purpose

This note executes the next proof-bearing step isolated in `107_19`.
Its goal is to construct the packet intersection line directly from a
local packet algebra compatible with the charts of `107_17`--`107_18`,
and to show that off the diagonal its canonical norm descends exactly to
the cyclotomic determinant line of `107_04`.

The output is intentionally local and finite:

\[
 \langle \mathcal P_{m,\chi_1},\mathcal P_{n,\chi_2}\rangle_{\rm pkt}
 \cong
 \langle Z_m,Z_n\rangle_{\rm fin}\otimes \mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2},
 \qquad
 \|\mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}\|=1.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus the rooted labels refine components, but they do not alter the
finite norm seen by Apostol's support law.

## 2. Inputs

Four earlier notes are used.

1. `107_04` constructs the off-diagonal determinant line
   \(\langle Z_m,Z_n\rangle_{\rm fin}\) and proves its canonical norm is
   \(|\mathrm{Res}(\Phi_m,\Phi_n)|\).
2. `107_17` gives the local chart equations for graph closures in the
   variables \((\xi,q,\theta)\).
3. `107_18` replaces \(\xi\) by finite visible rooted packets
   \((n,\chi)\).
4. `107_19` isolates the desired comparison morphism and the remaining
   diagonal caution.

## 3. Visible packet index algebra

Fix a support level \(T>0\), visible order set \(\mathcal N_T\), and
visible packet set

\[
 \mathcal I_T
 :=
 \{(n,\chi): n\in\mathcal N_T,\ \chi\in X_T^\vee,\ \mathrm{ord}(\chi)=n\}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

### Definition 3.1: packet idempotent algebra

Define the finite étale label algebra

\[
 \Lambda_T
 :=
 \prod_{(n,\chi)\in\mathcal I_T}\mathbf Z\,e_{n,\chi},
 \tag{3.2}
\]

with orthogonal idempotents
\(e_{n,\chi}e_{m,\psi}=\delta_{(n,\chi),(m,\psi)}e_{n,\chi}\) and
\(\sum_{(n,\chi)\in\mathcal I_T}e_{n,\chi}=1\).

For each visible packet \((n,\chi)\), write

\[
 \Lambda_{n,\chi}:=\mathbf Z\,e_{n,\chi}\subset\Lambda_T.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

This is a free rank-one \(\mathbf Z\)-module equipped with the trivial
Euclidean norm \(\|e_{n,\chi}\|=1\).

### Proposition 3.2: label factors are finite and unitary

Each \(\Lambda_{n,\chi}\) is finite type, projective of rank \(1\) over
\(\mathbf Z\), and contributes determinant norm \(1\).

Proof.  It is a direct summand of the finite product algebra
\(\Lambda_T\), generated by the idempotent \(e_{n,\chi}\).  With the
declared norm, its determinant is canonically trivial of norm \(1\).
\(\square\)

## 4. Local packet algebra over the cyclotomic model

Retain the cyclotomic base algebra of `107_04`:

\[
 A=\mathbf Z[x],
 \qquad
 B_n=A/(\Phi_n),
 \qquad
 Z_n=\mathrm{Spec}(B_n).
 \tag{4.1}
\]

### Definition 4.1: packet algebra of a visible chart

For a visible packet \((n,\chi)\), define its local packet algebra by

\[
 B_{n,\chi}^{\rm pkt}
 :=
 B_n\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Lambda_{n,\chi}.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The corresponding local packet chart is

\[
 \mathcal P_{n,\chi}^{\rm alg}
 :=
 \mathrm{Spec}(B_{n,\chi}^{\rm pkt}).
 \tag{4.3}
\]

This is the algebraic shadow of the local packet chart
\(\mathcal P_{n,\chi}\) of `107_19`: the order coordinate is recorded by
\(B_n\), while the rooted label is separated as a finite idempotent
factor.

### Proposition 4.2: order-forgetting factorization

There is a canonical projection

\[
 \pi_{\rm ord}^{\rm alg}:
 \mathcal P_{n,\chi}^{\rm alg}\longrightarrow Z_n
 \tag{4.4}
\]

induced by \(B_n\to B_n\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Lambda_{n,\chi}\),
\(b\mapsto b\otimes e_{n,\chi}\).

Proof.  This is the structural morphism attached to the tensor-factor
inclusion of the rank-one idempotent summand.  \(\square\)

## 5. Why the chart variables \(q,\theta\) do not change the finite line

In the local charts of `107_17`, packet graph equations have the form

\[
 (n_2,\chi_2)=\mu_r(n_1,\chi_1),\qquad q_2=q_1,\qquad \theta_2=\theta_1.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

The finite determinant support of `107_04` lives over
\(\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbf Z\) and is detected by torsion in the order
coordinate.  The equalities \(q_2=q_1\) and \(\theta_2=\theta_1\) are
shared graph constraints, but in this local finite model they do not
introduce new finite support away from the diagonal: they are frozen
matching directions attached to the same packet chart.

### Principle 5.1: packet finite support is carried by the order factor

Off the diagonal \(m\neq n\), the only finite torsion contributing to
the determinant line comes from the cyclotomic quotient pair
\((\Phi_m,\Phi_n)\); the rooted label and the common graph equalities in
\(q,\theta\) contribute only norm-one bookkeeping factors.

This is exactly the structural reduction needed to compare Part III with
Part I.

## 6. Derived packet intersection complex

### Definition 6.1: local packet derived intersection

For visible packets \((m,\chi_1)\) and \((n,\chi_2)\), define

\[
 C_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm pkt}
 :=
 B_{m,\chi_1}^{\rm pkt}\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}B_{n,\chi_2}^{\rm pkt}.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Because the label factors are pulled back from \(\mathbf Z\), there is a
canonical factorization

\[
 C_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm pkt}
 \simeq
 \left(B_m\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}B_n\right)
 \otimes_{\mathbf Z}
 \left(\Lambda_{m,\chi_1}\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Lambda_{n,\chi_2}\right).
 \tag{6.2}
\]

### Proposition 6.2: separation of order and rooted factors

For all visible packets, the quasi-isomorphism (6.2) is functorial in
\((m,\chi_1)\) and \((n,\chi_2)\).

Proof.  By Definition 4.1,
\(B_{m,\chi_1}^{\rm pkt}=B_m\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Lambda_{m,\chi_1}\) and
\(B_{n,\chi_2}^{\rm pkt}=B_n\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Lambda_{n,\chi_2}\).
Since the label factors are flat rank-one \(\mathbf Z\)-modules, they
can be pulled outside the derived tensor over \(A\), giving (6.2).
\(\square\)

This is the exact local algebraic form of the intuition from `107_19`:
packet labels refine the order intersection, but do not change its
cyclotomic torsion source.

## 7. The packet intersection line

### Definition 7.1: packet determinant line

For \(m\neq n\), define the off-diagonal packet intersection line by

\[
 \langle \mathcal P_{m,\chi_1},\mathcal P_{n,\chi_2}\rangle_{\rm pkt}
 :=
 \det_{\mathbf Z}\!\left(
 C_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm pkt}
 \right).
 \tag{7.1}
\]

Write \(\mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}\) for the rooted label line

\[
 \mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}
 :=
 \det_{\mathbf Z}\!\left(
 \Lambda_{m,\chi_1}\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Lambda_{n,\chi_2}
 \right).
 \tag{7.2}
\]

### Theorem 7.2: packet line splits as cyclotomic line times a rooted unit

For visible distinct orders \(m\neq n\),

\[
 \langle \mathcal P_{m,\chi_1},\mathcal P_{n,\chi_2}\rangle_{\rm pkt}
 \cong
 \langle Z_m,Z_n\rangle_{\rm fin}\otimes \mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}.
 \tag{7.3}
\]

Moreover, \(\mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}\) is canonically trivial of norm
\(1\).

Proof.  Take determinants in the functorial factorization (6.2).  The
cyclotomic term is exactly Definition 4.1 of `107_04`.  The rooted term
is the determinant of a rank-one free \(\mathbf Z\)-module generated by
\(e_{m,\chi_1}\otimes e_{n,\chi_2}\), hence canonically trivial of norm
\(1\).  \(\square\)

This supplies the missing concrete construction promised in `107_19`.

## 8. Canonical section and finite norm

The cyclotomic line of `107_04` carries its canonical Koszul section
\(s_{m,n}\).  Transport it through Theorem 7.2 and tensor with the unit
generator of \(\mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}\).

### Definition 8.1: packet canonical section

Define the packet canonical section

\[
 s_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm pkt}
 \in
 \langle \mathcal P_{m,\chi_1},\mathcal P_{n,\chi_2}\rangle_{\rm pkt}
 \tag{8.1}
\]

to be the image of
\(s_{m,n}\otimes (e_{m,\chi_1}\otimes e_{n,\chi_2})\) under the
isomorphism (7.3).

### Theorem 8.2: packet norm descends to Apostol's resultant

For visible distinct orders \(m\neq n\),

\[
 \left\|s_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm pkt}\right\|
 =
 \left\|s_{m,n}\right\|_{\rm fin}
 =
 \left|\mathrm{Res}(\Phi_m,\Phi_n)\right|.
 \tag{8.2}
\]

Proof.  Theorem 7.2 identifies the packet line with the cyclotomic
determinant line tensored by a norm-one rooted factor.  The norm of the
cyclotomic section is \(|\mathrm{Res}(\Phi_m,\Phi_n)|\) by
Proposition 4.1 of `107_04`.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 8.3: packetwise Apostol support law

For \(m>n>1\),

\[
 \frac1{\varphi(n)}
 \log
 \left\|s_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm pkt}\right\|
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \log p,&m/n=p^a,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{8.3}
\]

Proof.  Combine Theorem 8.2 with Proposition 5.1 of `107_04`.
\(\square\)

So Conjecture 6.1 of `107_19` is proved for the local packet algebra
model.

## 9. Comparison morphism

### Definition 9.1: packet-to-cyclotomic comparison

For \(m\neq n\), define

\[
 \Comp_{m,n}^{\chi_1,\chi_2}
 :
 \langle \mathcal P_{m,\chi_1},\mathcal P_{n,\chi_2}\rangle_{\rm pkt}
 \longrightarrow
 \langle Z_m,Z_n\rangle_{\rm fin}
 \tag{9.1}
\]

to be the projection obtained from the splitting (7.3) by evaluating the
trivial rooted factor \(\mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}\) on its unit
generator.

### Theorem 9.2: off-diagonal determinant compatibility

For visible distinct orders \(m\neq n\), the comparison morphism
\(\Comp_{m,n}^{\chi_1,\chi_2}\) is an isometric isomorphism carrying the
packet canonical section to the cyclotomic canonical section.

Proof.  Theorem 7.2 gives an isomorphism of lines, and the rooted factor
has norm \(1\).  Definition 8.1 shows the chosen sections correspond.
\(\square\)

Thus Conjecture 7.1 of `107_19` is also proved for the local packet
algebra model.

## 10. Diagonal caution remains unchanged

The previous off-diagonal argument does not extend to the diagonal
\(m=n\).

### Proposition 10.1: packet self-intersection still reduces to excess intersection

For any visible packet \((n,\chi)\),

\[
 C_{n,\chi;n,\chi}^{\rm pkt}
 \simeq
 \left(B_n\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}B_n\right)
 \otimes_{\mathbf Z}
 \left(\Lambda_{n,\chi}\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Lambda_{n,\chi}\right).
 \tag{10.1}
\]

Hence the packet diagonal still factors through the excess-intersection
complex of `107_04` and does not yield a finite scalar by itself.

Proof.  Apply Proposition 6.2 with \(m=n\).  The rooted tensor factor is
again rank one, so it cannot remove the excess-intersection phenomenon
already present in \(B_n\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}B_n\).  \(\square\)

Therefore the packet refinement preserves, rather than fixes, the
diagonal obstruction identified earlier.
