# 107.21 -- Paper C, Part IX: global descent of the packet determinant package on \(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\)

## 1. Purpose

`107_20` closes the local missing step of `107_19`: off the diagonal,
each packet intersection line is the cyclotomic determinant line of
`107_04` tensored with a norm-one rooted factor.

The present note globalizes that local result onto the first candidate
compactified model \(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\) of `107_15`--`107_16`.

The goal is to fix a concrete descent protocol

\[
 \{\langle \mathcal P_{m,\chi_1},\mathcal P_{n,\chi_2}\rangle_{\rm pkt}\}
 \Longrightarrow
 \mathcal L_{m,n,T}^{\rm glob}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

for a global line object on \(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\) whose local
trivializations recover the packet determinant package and whose metric
must later be completed by the boundary line \(\mathcal L_\infty\) of
`107_16`.

This is still a candidate construction protocol, not yet a full
realization theorem.  But it moves Part III past the purely local stage.

## 2. Inputs

The note uses five previously fixed components.

1. `107_15` gives the candidate model \(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\) and its
   strict transforms of the packet graphs and diagonal.
2. `107_16` gives the compactified square \(\overline{\mathfrak S}\),
   the common corner \(C_\infty\), and the boundary metric line
   \(\mathcal L_\infty\).
3. `107_17` gives the local chart atlas and graph equations.
4. `107_19` gives the packet charts and comparison target.
5. `107_20` gives the local packet determinant line and the off-diagonal
   isometric comparison with `107_04`.

## 3. Global packet strata on the candidate surface

Fix \(T>0\) and visible order set \(\mathcal N_T\).

### Definition 3.1: packet graph transforms

For a visible packet \((n,\chi)\), let

\[
 \Gamma_{n,\chi,T}^{(1)}
 \subset
 \mathcal X_T^{(1)}
 \tag{3.1}
\]

denote the strict transform of the corresponding packet graph closure
inside the regularized model candidate.

This notation refines the order-only transforms
\(\Gamma_{p,k,T}^{(1)}\) of `107_15` by keeping the visible rooted label.

### Definition 3.2: packet chart cover

Let

\[
 \mathfrak U_T^{\rm pkt}
 :=
 \{U_{n,\chi}\}_{(n,\chi)\in\mathcal I_T}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

be the open cover of the off-diagonal packet locus in
\(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\) obtained by pulling back the local packet charts
of `107_19` and the atlas of `107_17`.

On each \(U_{n,\chi}\), the packet data are expressed by the local
coordinates

\[
 (n,\chi,q,\theta).
 \tag{3.3}
\]

## 4. Local line objects on the cover

For visible distinct orders \(m\neq n\), `107_20` defines on each local
packet chart the line

\[
 \mathcal L_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm loc}
 :=
 \langle \mathcal P_{m,\chi_1},\mathcal P_{n,\chi_2}\rangle_{\rm pkt}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

By Theorem 7.2 of `107_20`, each such local line splits canonically as

\[
 \mathcal L_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm loc}
 \cong
 \langle Z_m,Z_n\rangle_{\rm fin}\otimes \mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2},
 \qquad
 \|\mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}\|=1.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

### Proposition 4.1: all local packet lines with the same order pair share one normed core

For fixed visible distinct orders \((m,n)\), every local line
\(\mathcal L_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm loc}\) contains the same normed
cyclotomic core \(\langle Z_m,Z_n\rangle_{\rm fin}\), and any change of
packet labels appears only through a norm-one factor.

Proof.  This is exactly Theorem 7.2 of `107_20`.  \(\square\)

This is the reason a global descent problem is even well posed.

## 5. Transition maps on overlaps

Suppose two packet charts overlap inside the off-diagonal packet locus
of \(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\):

\[
 U_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}
 \cap
 U_{m,\chi_1';n,\chi_2'}
 \neq \varnothing.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

### Definition 5.1: rooted transition unit

On such an overlap, define the transition map

\[
 g_{(\chi_1,\chi_2),(\chi_1',\chi_2')}
 :
 \mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal U_{\chi_1',\chi_2'}
 \tag{5.2}
\]

to be the unique isometry sending the unit generator
\(e_{m,\chi_1}\otimes e_{n,\chi_2}\) to
\(e_{m,\chi_1'}\otimes e_{n,\chi_2'}\).

Since every rooted factor is a trivial norm-one line, the overlap map on
the full local packet lines is

\[
 \mathrm{id}_{\langle Z_m,Z_n\rangle_{\rm fin}}
 \otimes
 g_{(\chi_1,\chi_2),(\chi_1',\chi_2')}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

### Proposition 5.2: the transition maps satisfy the cocycle condition

The maps (5.3) define a descent cocycle on the off-diagonal packet
cover.

Proof.  On triple overlaps, the cyclotomic core contributes the identity
and the rooted factor is generated by unit basis vectors.  The composed
isometry from one basis vector to another is independent of the route,
so the cocycle condition holds tautologically.  \(\square\)

Thus the local packet lines glue without changing the finite norm.

## 6. Global off-diagonal packet determinant line

### Definition 6.1: global packet determinant object

For visible distinct orders \(m\neq n\), define

\[
 \mathcal L_{m,n,T}^{\rm glob}
 \tag{6.1}
\]

to be the line object on the off-diagonal packet locus of
\(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\) obtained by gluing the local packet lines
\(\mathcal L_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}^{\rm loc}\) through the cocycle of
Proposition 5.2.

### Theorem 6.2: off-diagonal global descent

The global line \(\mathcal L_{m,n,T}^{\rm glob}\) is locally trivialized
by the packet determinant lines of `107_20` and carries a canonical
isometric identification

\[
 \mathcal L_{m,n,T}^{\rm glob}
 \big|_{U_{m,\chi_1;n,\chi_2}}
 \cong
 \langle Z_m,Z_n\rangle_{\rm fin}\otimes \mathcal U_{\chi_1,\chi_2}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

In particular, its finite off-diagonal norm is locally constant in the
rooted labels and equals
\(|\mathrm{Res}(\Phi_m,\Phi_n)|\).

Proof.  Existence follows from the cocycle of Proposition 5.2.  The
local norm statement is Theorem 8.2 of `107_20`.  \(\square\)

This is the first global line object in Part III whose local arithmetic
content is already controlled by Part I.

## 7. Deligne-pairing interpretation on the candidate model

The right interpretation of \(\mathcal L_{m,n,T}^{\rm glob}\) is as the
candidate Deligne-pairing object attached to the strict transforms of
the packet graph divisors.

### Definition 7.1: candidate global packet pairing

For distinct visible orders \(m\neq n\), define formally

\[
 \left\langle
 \Gamma_{m,\bullet,T}^{(1)},
 \Gamma_{n,\bullet,T}^{(1)}
 \right\rangle_{\mathcal X_T^{(1)}}
 :=
 \mathcal L_{m,n,T}^{\rm glob},
 \tag{7.1}
\]

where the bullet indicates that one has descended the rooted packet
cover while retaining the order pair.

This is not yet a theorem in classical arithmetic intersection theory.
It is the precise global object that later must be compared with an
actual Deligne pairing on a completed arithmetic surface.

### Principle 7.2: rooted packet descent before Picard realization

Before building the Picard/Jacobian map of `107_11`, one must first
descend the packetwise local determinant package to an order-indexed
global line object on \(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\).

`107_20` and the present note now fix exactly that object.

## 8. Boundary completion with \(\mathcal L_\infty\)

The off-diagonal global line is still only the finite piece.  The
Gamma--polar completion must enter through the corner line of `107_16`.

### Definition 8.1: boundary-completed packet line

Let

\[
 \widehat{\mathcal L}_{m,n,T}^{\rm cand}
 \tag{8.1}
\]

denote the candidate metrized completion of
\(\mathcal L_{m,n,T}^{\rm glob}\) obtained by gluing it, in a
neighborhood of the compactified diagonal/corner sector, with the
boundary metric line \(\mathcal L_\infty\).

The required gluing conditions are:

1. off the corner, the metric restricts to the packet determinant norm
   of `107_20`;
2. on the diagonal approach, the logarithmic singularity matches the
   Gamma--polar determinant distribution of `107_05`;
3. at the common corner \(C_\infty\), the vertical and horizontal
   boundary restrictions agree.

### Proposition 8.2: there is only one admissible boundary receiver

If the global packet determinant package is to remain compatible with
Paper A, then its archimedean completion must factor through
\(\mathcal L_\infty\) and not through separate ad hoc boundary
corrections.

Proof.  `107_05` and `107_16` already identify one common metrized
boundary receiver for diagonal, prime, and polar data.  Any separate
correction would break the single-metrized-determinant principle of
Paper A.  \(\square\)

## 9. Compatibility with the future Picard/Jacobian map

The global line object of Definition 6.1 is the exact input that
`107_11` needs in order to leave the level of pure source data.

### Requirement 9.1: transport to degree-zero metrized line bundles

The future realization map

\[
 \mathcal A_T:
 \mathrm{Div}_{\rm EF}(T)
 \longrightarrow
 \widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}^0(\mathcal X_T^{(1)})
 \tag{9.1}
\]

must send order-indexed packet combinations to metrized line bundles
whose pairings reproduce
\(\widehat{\mathcal L}_{m,n,T}^{\rm cand}\).

### Consequence 9.2: exact radical control remains the real bottleneck

Once the local packet determinant package has been descended globally,
the remaining hard obstruction in III-B is no longer the off-diagonal
finite comparison.  It is the faithful Picard/Jacobian realization with
exact kernel
\(\ker(f\mapsto\overline M_f)=\mathfrak R_W\).

## 10. What is now closed

This note closes the next structural gap after `107_20`.

1. the local packet determinant lines are now glued into a global
   order-indexed line object on the candidate surface;
2. the overlap maps are fixed and isometric because rooted labels only
   contribute norm-one units;
3. the correct global receiver for the future Deligne-pairing
   interpretation is now identified;
4. the unique place where the archimedean completion must enter is
   pinned down as the boundary line \(\mathcal L_\infty\).

## 11. What remains open

The present note still does not complete Part III.

1. It does not prove that \(\mathcal X_T^{(1)}\) exists with all needed
   algebraicity and regularity properties.
2. It does not yet construct the completed metrized line
   \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_{m,n,T}^{\rm cand}\) as an admissible adelic
   object.
3. It does not yet identify \(\mathcal L_{m,n,T}^{\rm glob}\) with a
   genuine Deligne pairing in a proved arithmetic-surface category.
4. It does not yet build the Abel--Jacobi/Picard realization map with
   exact radical kernel.

## 12. Next technical front
