# 107.237 -- Continuous correspondence divisors in the DC completion

## 1. Why the finite tropical square is insufficient

The arithmetic-site Frobenius correspondence \(\Psi_\lambda\) is the
congruence associated with the ray of slope \(\lambda>0\) in the Newton
polygon square.  On the real two-dimensional chart its tropical hypersurface
is

\[
 L_\lambda=\{(x,y):y=\lambda x\},
 \tag{1.1}
\]

represented by the convex potential

\[
 P_\lambda(x,y)=\max(y-\lambda x,0).
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Every element of the finite structure sheaf constructed in 107_236 is a
maximum of finitely many affine functions.  Its divisor is consequently
supported on a finite rational-polyhedral complex.  In particular, on every
compact angular interval it has only finitely many slope jumps.

For a nonzero continuous test function \(f\), the formal divisor required by
the RR strategy is

\[
 D_f=\int_0^\infty f(\lambda)\Psi_\lambda\,d^*\lambda,
 \qquad d^*\lambda=\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

If \(f\) is nonzero on an interval, (1.3) contains a continuum of distinct
rays.  It therefore cannot be a Cartier divisor of the finite-PL sheaf.
This is not a cardinality heuristic; the curvature computation below proves
the obstruction exactly.

## 2. The homogeneous DC potential

Let \(f\in C_c((0,\infty),\mathbb R)\), supported in \([A,B]\).  Define

\[
 U_f(x,y)=\int_A^B
 f(\lambda)\max(y-\lambda x,0)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda},
 \qquad x,y>0.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Writing \(r=y/x\), one has

\[
 U_f(x,y)=x\,u_f(r),
 \qquad
 u_f(r)=\int_A^{\min(r,B)}f(\lambda)(r-\lambda)
 \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

On \(A<r<B\), differentiation gives

\[
 u_f'(r)=\int_A^r f(\lambda)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda},
 \qquad
 \boxed{u_f''(r)=\frac{f(r)}r.}
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Write \(f=f_+-f_-\).  Each \(U_{f_\pm}\) is an integral of convex
functions with a positive measure and is therefore convex.  Hence
\(U_f=U_{f_+}-U_{f_-}\) is a well-defined homogeneous difference-of-convex
(DC) function.  Its distributional tropical divisor is

\[
 \operatorname{Div}(U_f)
 =\int_A^B f(\lambda)[L_\lambda]\,d^*\lambda.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Equation (2.4) is exactly (1.3) under the Newton-polygon realization of
\(\Psi_\lambda\). Here \(U_f\) is a local equation on the universal positive
chart, not yet a global rational function on the quotient topos.

### Theorem 2.1 (finite-PL no-go and DC realization)

If \(f\) is continuous and nonzero on an interval, then \(D_f\) is not the
divisor of any finite-PL rational section of \(\mathcal O_{\mathscr S^2}\).
It is, however, chartwise the divisor of the canonical DC potential \(U_f\),
uniquely defined modulo affine functions by (2.1).

### Proof

The angular second derivative of a finite-PL function is a finite atomic
measure.  By (2.3), the angular second derivative of \(U_f\) has the
nonzero continuous density \(f(r)/r\) on an interval.  These distributions
cannot agree.  Formula (2.1) constructs the DC realization, while (2.3)
recovers \(f\) from it and gives uniqueness modulo the kernel of the second
derivative, namely affine functions. \(\square\)

## 3. Degree, codegree and compact angular support

Set

\[
 M_0(f)=\int_A^B f(\lambda)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda},
 \qquad
 M_1(f)=\int_A^B f(\lambda)d\lambda.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

For \(r\geq B\), (2.2) becomes

\[
 u_f(r)=rM_0(f)-M_1(f).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the two Weil balancing conditions

\[
 \int f(\lambda)d^*\lambda=0,
 \qquad
 \int f(\lambda)d\lambda=0
 \tag{3.3}
\]

are exactly the conditions that \(U_f\) vanish both below \(A\) and above
\(B\).  They also give the two ruling intersections:

\[
 D_f\cdot F_1=M_0(f)=0,
 \qquad
 D_f\cdot F_2=M_1(f)=0.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Hence the source balance is not imposed after realization: it is equivalent
to compact angular support and primitive bidegree \((0,0)\) of the current.

## 4. Frobenius covariance

For positive integers \(m,n\), direct substitution in (2.1) gives

\[
 U_f(mx,ny)
 =n\,U_{f_{m,n}}(x,y),
 \qquad
 f_{m,n}(\mu)=f\left(\frac n m\mu\right).
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, put \(\mu=m\lambda/n\); then
\(d^*\lambda=d^*\mu\) and
\(\max(ny-\lambda mx,0)=n\max(y-\mu x,0)\).
Thus the completed divisor carrier intertwines the two-variable absolute
Frobenius action and transports the correspondence parameter by
\(\lambda\mapsto(m/n)\lambda\), exactly as the graph equation requires.

## 5. Constructed object and remaining theorem

Let \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{DC}}\) be the sheaf of local differences of finite
convex functions on universal charts of the product Scaling topos, modulo
affine functions, and let \(\operatorname{CorrCur}\) be the
Frobenius-equivariant current sheaf generated by the globally defined
correspondences \(\Psi_\lambda\). Equations (2.1)--(2.4) define a canonical
linear map

\[
 \boxed{
 C_c((0,\infty),\mathbb R)\longrightarrow
 \Gamma(\mathscr S^2,\operatorname{CorrCur}),
 \qquad f\longmapsto D_f.}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Its local equation is \(U_f\), and (4.1) is the reparametrization law on
overlaps. It is injective modulo equality almost everywhere, because (2.3)
recovers \(f\). On the balanced Weil test space its image has bidegree
\((0,0)\).

This does **not** prove that these local equations are transition functions
of a global invertible module. Treating \(U_f\) itself as one global rational
function would make \(D_f\) principal and would force every intersection
pairing on divisor classes to vanish. The nontrivial descent class must reside
in the Frobenius/adelic gluing, not in one chart potential.

The next gates are now exact: construct the nonprincipal adelic descent class

finite-PL Cartier divisors is closed by Theorem 2.1.
