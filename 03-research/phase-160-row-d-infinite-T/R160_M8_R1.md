## §1 Definitions

**1.1 Transforms.** For `F ∈ C_c^∞(R)` (additive variable `u = log x`), `Φ_F(s) := ∫F(t)e^{(s−½)t}dt` (P46
eq:W), entire; on `s = ½+iτ` it is `F̂(τ)`. Autocorrelation `c_F(u) := ∫F(t) conj F(t−u) dt` (P46 §1.1), so that
`Φ`-transform of `c_F` is `Φ_F(s)·conj Φ_F(1−s̄)` (substitute `t−u=r`). For `h ∈ C_c^∞(R²)` the same with
`ĥ(s₁,s₂) := ∫∫h(u)e^{(s₁−½)u₁+(s₂−½)u₂}du` and `c_h(u) := ∫h(t) conj h(t−u) dt`, `u,t ∈ R²`;
`ĉ_h(s₁,s₂) = ĥ(s₁,s₂) conj ĥ(1−s̄₁,1−s̄₂)`.

**1.2 The Weil distribution `𝔴`** (P46 def:KEP, eq:gdatum, extended to non-even tests by evenisation): for
`h ∈ C_c^∞(R)`,
```
⟨𝔴,h⟩ = C_0 h(0) + ½∫_R (h(0)−h(u)) K(|u|) du − ½ Σ_{n≥2} v_n (h(log n)+h(−log n)) + 2∫_R h(u) cosh(u/2) du,
```
`C_0 = ψ(¼)−log π = −5.3721834…`, `K` the Lévy density of lem:K, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`. Write `𝔴 = 𝔩 − 𝔠 + 𝔭`:
`𝔩 = C_0δ_0 + ½Pf K(|u|)` (archimedean; FT `= m(τ)` by eq:m), `𝔠 = ½Σ v_n(δ_{log n}+δ_{−log n})` (comb, a positive
measure), `𝔭 = 2cosh(u/2)du` (polar). For `F ∈ C_c^∞(R)` put `W(F) := ⟨𝔴, c_F⟩`. **Facts of record.** (a) For
`F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, `W(F) = W_T(F)` of eq:W (thm:A + `2Re(A conj B) = ∫c_F·2cosh(u/2)`, checked by the change
`t−s=u`); only contacts `log n < 2T` enter because `supp c_F ⊂ [−2T,2T]`. (b) Explicit formula: `⟨𝔴,h⟩ = Σ_ρ ĥ(ρ)`,
absolutely convergent (P45 Thm 9.4 / P46 thm:Z(ii); `ĥ` is entire of exponential type, rapidly decreasing on
vertical lines, `N(T) ≪ T log T`). Hence `W(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ) conj Φ_F(1−ρ̄)`, a Hermitian form, `≥ 0` termwise under
RH. (c) `W` is translation invariant: `W(F(·−x)) = W(F)` since `c_{F(·−x)} = c_F`.

**1.3 The Schur square.** `⟨𝔴⊗𝔴, H⟩ := ⟨𝔴^{(1)}, u₁ ↦ ⟨𝔴^{(2)}, H(u₁,·)⟩⟩` for `H ∈ C_c^∞(R²)`, and
`Q_2(h) := ⟨𝔴⊗𝔴, c_h⟩`. ("Schur square" because on the Fourier side of each variable it is the pointwise product
`Ŵ(ξ₁)Ŵ(ξ₂)`; restricted to a diagonal it would be the Hadamard product `Ŵ∗Ŵ`, the Minkowski sum of zeros.)

**1.4 Thickened class and rotated windows.** `h_{g,φ}(u₁,u₂) := g(u₁+u₂)φ(u₁−u₂)`, `g,φ ∈ C_c^∞(R)`;
`𝒯 := span{h_{g,φ}}`. Coordinates `σ = u₁+u₂`, `τ = u₁−u₂`, `du₁du₂ = ½dσdτ`. For `a,b>0`,
`R(a,b) := {|u₁+u₂|<a, |u₁−u₂|<b}` (a rotated rectangle; `R(a,b) ⊂ {|u₁|,|u₂|<(a+b)/2}`, and `R(a,b)` contains the
axis-parallel rectangle `(x−c₁,x+c₁)×(x−c₂,x+c₂)` whenever `2|x|+c₁+c₂ ≤ a` and `c₁+c₂ ≤ b`). `supp g ⊂ (−a,a)`,
`supp φ ⊂ (−b,b)` ⟹ `supp h_{g,φ} ⊂ R(a,b)`. The window form of record is `W_T` on `C_c^∞(I_T)`; `𝒯_T` denotes
tests supported in `I_T×I_T`.

**1.5 Zero-side notation.** `z_ρ := ρ−½`; for an off-line quadruple `{½±δ±iγ₀}`, `z₁ = δ+iγ₀`, `z₁* = −δ+iγ₀`
(the involution `ρ ↦ 1−ρ̄` is `z ↦ −z̄`). "On-line" means `Re ρ = ½`; `Z_on` the set of on-line zeros,
`N_0(r) := #{ρ ∈ Z_on : |γ| ≤ r}`.

## §2 Theorem A — the Schur square in the normalisation of record (Lemma 5.2 finished)

**Theorem A.** *(a) For `H ∈ C_c^∞(R²)` the function `u₁ ↦ ⟨𝔴, H(u₁,·)⟩` is in `C_c^∞(R)`, so `𝔴⊗𝔴` is well
defined, symmetric in the two variables, and*
```
⟨𝔴⊗𝔴, H⟩ = Σ_{ρ₁,ρ₂} Ĥ(ρ₁,ρ₂)      (absolutely convergent).
```
*(b) `𝒯` is closed under `⋆` (additive convolution on `R²`) and under `h ↦ h~ := conj h(−·)`: in `(σ,τ)`
coordinates `h_{g,φ} = g⊗φ`, so `c_{h_{g,φ}}(u) = ½ c_g(u₁+u₂) c_φ(u₁−u₂)` and*
```
ĥ_{g,φ}(s₁,s₂) = ½ Φ_g(½+(z₁+z₂)/2) Φ_φ(½+(z₁−z₂)/2),   z_i = s_i−½,
Q_2(h_{g,φ}) = ¼ Σ_{ρ₁,ρ₂} Φ_g(½+(z₁+z₂)/2) conj Φ_g(½−(z̄₁+z̄₂)/2) · Φ_φ(½+(z₁−z₂)/2) conj Φ_φ(½−(z̄₁−z̄₂)/2).
```
*(c) Arithmetic side: with `𝔴 = 𝔩 − 𝔠 + 𝔭`,*
```
𝔴⊗𝔴 = 𝔩⊗𝔩 − 𝔩⊗𝔠 − 𝔠⊗𝔩 + 𝔩⊗𝔭 + 𝔭⊗𝔩 + 𝔠⊗𝔠 − 𝔠⊗𝔭 − 𝔭⊗𝔠 + 𝔭⊗𝔭,
```
*every block a finite sum or an absolutely convergent integral of `H` against explicit data (`C_0, K, v_n, cosh`),
none involving a zero. The comb×comb block is*
```
⟨𝔠⊗𝔠, H⟩ = ¼ Σ_{n₁,n₂≥2} v_{n₁}v_{n₂} Σ_{ε₁,ε₂=±1} H(ε₁ log n₁, ε₂ log n₂),
```
*and for `H = c_{h_{g,φ}}`, grouping by `N = n₁n₂`,*
```
⟨𝔠⊗𝔠, c_{h_{g,φ}}⟩ = ¼ Σ_N Re[ c_g(log N) · 𝔏_φ(N) ] + ¼ Σ_{n₁,n₂} v_{n₁}v_{n₂} Re[ c_g(log(n₁/n₂)) c_φ(log n₁n₂) ],
𝔏_φ(N) := Σ_{d|N} v_d v_{N/d} c_φ(log(d²/N)) = (4/√N) Σ_{d|N} Λ(d)Λ(N/d) c_φ(log(d²/N)).
```
*Both sums are finite; `𝔏_φ` is supported on integers with at most two distinct prime factors and only on
"balanced" factorisations `|log(d²/N)| < 2b`. The block carries the plus sign.*

*Proof.* (a) Each of the four terms of `⟨𝔴, H(u₁,·)⟩` is smooth in `u₁` with support in the `u₁`-projection of
`supp H`: evaluations at `0, ±log n` (finitely many `n`, since `supp H` is compact) and an integral against
`cosh(u₂/2)` on a compact set are smooth; the Lévy term `½∫(H(u₁,0)−H(u₁,u₂))K(|u₂|)du₂` converges absolutely with
all `u₁`-derivatives because `H(u₁,0)−H(u₁,u₂) = O(|u₂|)` uniformly with derivatives and `K(u) = u⁻¹+O(1)`
(lem:K). Symmetry and the spectral side: apply 1.2(b) in `u₁` to `F(u₁) := ⟨𝔴, H(u₁,·)⟩`; `Φ_F(ρ₁) =
⟨𝔴, ∫H(u₁,·)e^{(ρ₁−½)u₁}du₁⟩` (each term of `𝔴` commutes with the `u₁`-integral: finite sums, and Fubini for
the two absolutely convergent integrals), and 1.2(b) in `u₂` gives `Σ_{ρ₂}Ĥ(ρ₁,ρ₂)`. Absolute convergence:
`|Ĥ(s₁,s₂)| ≤ C_N(1+|Im s₁|)^{−N}(1+|Im s₂|)^{−N}` uniformly for `Re s_i ∈ [0,1]` (integrate by parts `N` times in
each variable; compact support), and `N(T) ≪ T log T` in each factor. Both iterated sums equal the absolutely
convergent double sum, whence the symmetry. (b) In `(σ,τ)` coordinates convolution and reflection act
factorwise; the Jacobian `½` gives the constant. The transform: `(s₁−½)u₁+(s₂−½)u₂ = ((z₁+z₂)/2)σ + ((z₁−z₂)/2)τ`.
Then `Q_2 = Σ ĉ_h(ρ₁,ρ₂)` with `ĉ_h = ĥ · conj ĥ(1−s̄₁,1−s̄₂)`, and `1−ρ̄` has `z ↦ −z̄`. (c) Expand the tensor
square of `𝔩−𝔠+𝔭`; each block is the iterated pairing of two of the three terms of 1.2 and is manifestly
zero-free. Comb×comb: `𝔠⊗𝔠 = ¼Σ v_{n₁}v_{n₂} Σ_{ε} δ_{(ε₁log n₁, ε₂log n₂)}`. With `c_{h_{g,φ}}(u) =
½c_g(u₁+u₂)c_φ(u₁−u₂)` and `c` Hermitian (`c(−u) = conj c(u)`), the four sign choices pair into two real parts:
`(+,+)` and `(−,−)` give `2Re[c_g(log n₁n₂)c_φ(log(n₁/n₂))]`, `(+,−)` and `(−,+)` give
`2Re[c_g(log(n₁/n₂))c_φ(log n₁n₂)]`; the factor `½·¼·2 = ¼`. Group the first family by `N = n₁n₂`, `d = n₁`:
`n₁/n₂ = d²/N`. Support: `Λ(d)Λ(N/d) ≠ 0` forces `d = p₁^{k₁}`, `N/d = p₂^{k₂}`; `c_φ(log(d²/N)) ≠ 0` forces
`|log(d²/N)| < 2b`. Finiteness: `c_g(log N) ≠ 0` forces `N < e^{2a}`; in the second family `c_φ(log n₁n₂) ≠ 0`
forces `n₁n₂ < e^{2b}`. ∎

**2.1 What Theorem A settles.** Doc 106 Missing Lemma 5.2 asked for a functional `𝒲_2` whose quadratic form (1)
contains `(Q|_{K_off})^{⊗2}` as a spectral block and (2) has a zero-free arithmetic side. (2) is Theorem A(c);
(1) is Theorem A(b): on the sixteen evaluation functionals `h ↦ ĥ(ρ_i,ρ_j)` of a quadruple the zero-side Gram of
`Q_2` is the tensor square of the one-variable Gram (Doc 107 Thm 5.5, transported verbatim: the involution
`(z₁,z₂) ↦ (−z̄₁,−z̄₂)` is fixed-point free on off×off pairs and gives eight hyperbolic planes). Doc 107's caveat
Obs. 5.7(i) — that the block is not shown to be *separated* from the rest of the spectrum inside `Q_2` — is
answered negatively and rigorously by Theorem D below. So Lemma 5.2 is finished: (1) and (2) hold, and the
separation that Form B needed after them does not exist.

**2.2 Remark (the plus sign is the sign of a square).** `⟨𝔠⊗𝔠, c_h⟩` is not termwise positive: `c_g`, `c_φ` are
positive-definite functions, hence sign-changing. For product tests `h = F₁⊗F₂` it equals `(Σ v_n Re c_{F₁}(log n))
(Σ v_n Re c_{F₂}(log n))`, the product of two one-variable comb terms — positive exactly when both have the same
sign. Nothing about the sign of `W` is encoded in the sign of this block (consistent with I-11).

## §3 Theorem B — the Schur square is sign-faithful and binary

**Lemma 3.1 (`W_c` has infinite positive index and finite negative index, every `c>0`).** *For every `c>0` there
is a subspace of `C_c^∞(I_c)` of finite codimension on which `W_c(F) ≥ ½‖F‖²`; in particular `n_−(W_c) < ∞` and
`W_c` is positive definite on an infinite-dimensional subspace.*

*Proof.* `σ_c(τ) = m(τ) − Σ_{N_c} v_n cos(τ log n)` with `m(τ) = log(|τ|/2π)+O(τ⁻²)` (Stirling for `Re ψ`) and the
comb bounded by `A_c`; choose `τ₁` with `σ_c ≥ 1` for `|τ| ≥ τ₁` and put `M := sup_{|τ|<τ₁}|σ_c|`. Then, by
Plancherel, `𝔞_c(F) = (1/2π)∫σ_c|F̂|² ≥ (1/2π)∫_{|τ|≥τ₁}|F̂|² − (M/2π)∫_{|τ|<τ₁}|F̂|² = ‖F‖² − (1/2π)(1+M)∫_{|τ|<τ₁}|F̂|²`.
The operator `P₁ := (1/2π)(1+M)·P_{I_c}·1_{|τ|<τ₁}(D)·P_{I_c}` is Hilbert–Schmidt on `L²(I_c)` (bounded continuous
kernel `(1+M)(2π)⁻²∫_{|τ|<τ₁}e^{iτ(t−s)}dτ` on a bounded square), so `⟨P₁F,F⟩ ≤ ½‖F‖²` on the spectral subspace
`V₁` of `P₁` for eigenvalues `≤ ½`, which has finite codimension in `L²(I_c)`. `W_c = 𝔞_c + 2Re(A conj B)` differs
by a form of rank `≤ 2`; let `V := V₁ ∩ {A = B = 0}`, still of finite codimension. On `V ∩ (form domain)`,
`W_c ≥ ½‖F‖²`; hence `n_−(W_c) ≤ codim V < ∞`. Finally `V ∩ C_c^∞(I_c)` has finite codimension in `C_c^∞(I_c)`
(finitely many linear conditions), so it is infinite-dimensional, and `W_c ≥ ½‖F‖²` there. ∎

**Lemma 3.2 (tensor product of Hermitian forms).** *Let `Q_i` be Hermitian forms on vector spaces `V_i` and `Q =
Q₁⊗Q₂` on `V₁⊗V₂` (algebraic), `Q(Σ_k x_k⊗y_k) := Σ_{k,l} Q₁(x_k,x_l)Q₂(y_k,y_l)`. (i) If `Q₁,Q₂ ≥ 0` then
`Q ≥ 0`. (ii) If `Q₁(x_−) < 0` for some `x_−` and `Q₂` is positive definite on an infinite-dimensional `E ⊂ V₂`,
then `Q` is negative definite on the infinite-dimensional subspace `x_−⊗E`.*

*Proof.* (i) `Q(x) = ⟨(M∘N)𝟙,𝟙⟩` with `M = [Q₁(x_k,x_l)]`, `N = [Q₂(y_k,y_l)]` positive semidefinite Gram
matrices and `M∘N` their Hadamard product, `≥ 0` by Schur's product theorem. (ii) `Q(x_−⊗y) = Q₁(x_−)Q₂(y)`. ∎

**Theorem B.** *(i) Under RH, `Q_2 ≥ 0` on `C_c^∞(R²)`.
(ii) The following are equivalent: (α) `Q_2 ≥ 0` on the product class `{F₁⊗F₂}`; (β) `Q_2 ≥ 0` on

`Q_2(y+λz) = W(F)² + |λ|²W(G)² + 2Re(λ̄ W(F,G)²) ≥ 0` for all `λ ∈ C`, whence `|W(F)W(G)| ≥ |W(F,G)|²`. Suppose
`W(F) > 0 > W(G)`. Then `Q_2(x) ≥ 0` gives `|W(F,G)|² ≥ −W(F)W(G) = |W(F)W(G)| ≥ |W(F,G)|²`, so

Lemma 3.1 `W` takes positive values; hence `W ≥ 0` on `C_c^∞(R)`, hence `W_T ≥ 0` on `C_c^∞(I_T)` for every `T`,
hence on `L²(I_T)` (density, Lemma 3.1 proof). (ε)⟹(ζ) is Weil's criterion (P46 §1.3, P45 Thm 10.4 / Bombieri Thm
1; E1 of the brief). (iii) `Q_2|𝒯_T` is the closure of `W_T⊗W_T` on `C_c^∞(I_T)⊗C_c^∞(I_T)` (Theorem A(b): `c_{F⊗G}
= c_F⊗c_G`, sesquilinearity, density, continuity). If `W_T ≥ 0`, Lemma 3.2(i) and density give `Q_2 ≥ 0`. If

## §4 Proposition C — the negative constant squares; the one-variable form re-enters with coefficient `C_0`

Write `𝔯 := 𝔴 − C_0δ_0` (the "regular part": compensated Lévy kernel, comb, polar term).

**Proposition C.** *For every `h ∈ C_c^∞(R²)`,*
```
Q_2(h) = −C_0²‖h‖² + C_0 [ ∫_R W(h(t₁,·)) dt₁ + ∫_R W(h(·,t₂)) dt₂ ] + ⟨𝔯⊗𝔯, c_h⟩ .
```
*Consequently, under RH, `⟨𝔯⊗𝔯, c_h⟩ ≥ C_0²‖h‖² + |C_0|[∫W(h(t₁,·))dt₁ + ∫W(h(·,t₂))dt₂] ≥ C_0²‖h‖²`: the
positivity of the Schur square is carried entirely by the square of the regular part, and the two "linear"
terms are non-positive.*

*Proof.* `𝔴⊗𝔴 = (C_0δ+𝔯)⊗(C_0δ+𝔯)` and `δ⊗𝔯 = δ⊗𝔴 − C_0δ⊗δ` give `𝔴⊗𝔴 = −C_0²δ⊗δ + C_0(δ⊗𝔴+𝔴⊗δ) + 𝔯⊗𝔯`.
Pair with `c_h`: `c_h(0,0) = ‖h‖²`; `c_h(0,u₂) = ∫_{t₁} c_{h(t₁,·)}(u₂)dt₁`, so `⟨δ⊗𝔴, c_h⟩ = ∫W(h(t₁,·))dt₁`
(the pairing commutes with the `t₁`-integral as in Theorem A(a)); symmetrically for `𝔴⊗δ`. Under RH each slice
value `W(h(t₁,·)) ≥ 0` and `C_0 < 0`. ∎

**4.1 Reading against S-9 and S-6.** Bochner's obstruction `Ω(0) = C_0 < 0` (S-9) reappears in the square as the
strictly negative rank-one-type term `−C_0²‖h‖²`, and the one-variable Weil form re-enters *with the wrong sign*
`C_0 < 0`. On product tests the identity is the trivial `𝔯(F₁)𝔯(F₂) = (W(F₁)−C_0‖F₁‖²)(W(F₂)−C_0‖F₂‖²)`; on
thickened tests `⟨𝔯⊗𝔯,c_h⟩` is a genuinely two-variable quantity, but the identity shows that any proof of
`Q_2 ≥ 0` must control it below by `C_0²‖h‖²` *plus* `|C_0|` times the slice-averaged one-variable forms — i.e.
must already know the sign of `W` on every slice. This is the S-6 trilemma in one line: the square has exact
global phase and no positive primitive descent.

## §5 Theorem D — the relative block is not test-accessible (termwise), and why (Lemma P)

**Theorem D.** *Let `ρ₀` be any zero and `h ∈ C_c^∞(R²)` with `supp h ⊂ {|u₂| ≤ b}`. Put `k(u₂) :=
∫h(u₁,u₂)e^{(ρ₀−½)u₁}du₁ ∈ C_c^∞((−b,b))`, so that `ĥ(ρ₀,s) = Φ_k(s)`. Then either `k ≡ 0` (and `ĥ(ρ₀,ρ) = 0` for
every `ρ`, on-line or not), or `#{ρ ∈ Z_on : |γ| ≤ r, ĥ(ρ₀,ρ) = 0} ≤ (2br + C_h)/log 2` for all `r ≥ 1`, whereas
`N_0(r) ≥ c r log r` (Selberg, CITED). In particular `ĥ(ρ₀,ρ) ≠ 0` for all but `O(r)` of the `≫ r log r` on-line
zeros of height `≤ r`. The same holds in the first slot. Consequently: for an off-line quadruple with off×off
evaluation set `E_off` (16 functionals) and off×on evaluation set `E_mix = {ĥ(ρ_i,ρ), ĥ(ρ,ρ_i): ρ ∈ Z_on}`, there
is no `h ≠ 0` with `ev_{E_mix}(h) = 0` and `ev_{E_off}(h) ≠ 0`; a fortiori no finite-dimensional subspace `V` of
tests with `ev_{E_off}: V → C^{16}` onto and `ev_{E_mix}|_V = 0`.*

*Proof.* If `k ≢ 0`, `f := Φ_k` is a nonzero entire function with `|f(s)| ≤ ‖k‖_1 e^{b|Re s−½|}` (Paley–Wiener
bound; support in `(−b,b)`). Pick `s₀` with `f(s₀) ≠ 0`, `|s₀−½| ≤ 1`. Jensen's formula on the disc
`|s−s₀| ≤ 2r`: the number `n(r)` of zeros in `|s−s₀| ≤ r` satisfies `n(r) log 2 ≤ log max_{|s−s₀|=2r}|f| − log|f(s₀)|
≤ log‖k‖_1 + b(2r+1) − log|f(s₀)|`, so `n(r) ≤ (2br + C_h)/log 2`. The on-line zeros with `|γ| ≤ r−1` lie in that
disc and number `N_0(r−1) ≥ c(r−1)log(r−1)` (Selberg 1942: a positive proportion of zeros are on the line). For
`r` large the two counts are incompatible unless most on-line zeros are not zeros of `f`. For the last sentence:
if `ev_{E_mix}(h) = 0` then for each off-line `ρ_i`, `ĥ(ρ_i,·)` vanishes on `Z_on`, hence `k_i ≡ 0`, hence
`ĥ(ρ_i,ρ) = 0` for *all* `ρ`, including the off-line ones: `ev_{E_off}(h) = 0`. ∎

**5.1 What Theorem D closes.** Doc 107 Obs. 5.7(i) left open whether the off×off block is *separated* inside
`Q_2`; Doc 108 §2.4 and Paper 29 thm:autonomy asserted "not test-accessible" as an analysis ("no se conoce
ningún funcional…"). Theorem D is the rigorous termwise version: the sixteen off×off evaluations cannot be
isolated from the infinitely many off×on evaluations by any test, because killing the latter kills the former.
(A form-level version — no subspace on which the off×on *contribution to `Q_2`* vanishes by cancellation — is not
proved; see Open steps.) Since Form B needs a finite relative index to amplify (Doc 106 Lemma 5.3, Doc 107
Lemma 7.2) and the only test-accessible index is `n_−(Q_2|𝒯_T) ∈ {0,∞}` (Theorem B(iii)), there is nothing to
amplify: the Sterility Proposition of Paper 28 holds on the realised object.

**Lemma P (flat frequency average of an off×on plane is positive).** *For `g ∈ C_c^∞(R)` and real `δ`,*
```
∫_R Φ_g(½+δ/2+iy) · conj Φ_g(½−δ/2+iy) dy = 2π ‖g‖²_{L²} .
```
*Proof.* `Φ_g(½±δ/2+iy)` is the Fourier transform at `y` of `g(σ)e^{±δσ/2}`; Plancherel gives
`2π∫g e^{δσ/2} · conj(g e^{−δσ/2}) dσ = 2π‖g‖²`. ∎

**5.2 Remark (HEURISTIC, explains binarity and N-e).** On a thickened test with thin `φ` (`b` small, so
`Φ_φ(½±δ/2+ix) ≈ Φ_φ(½)` for `|x| ≪ 1/b`), the off×on plane `{(z₁,iγ),(z₁*,iγ)}` contributes to `Q_2(h_{g,φ})`
the term `½|Φ_φ(½)|² Re[Φ_g(½+δ/2+iy) conj Φ_g(½−δ/2+iy)]`, `y = (γ₀+γ)/2`. Replacing the sum over on-line `γ`
near height `γ₀` by the mean density `(1/2π)log(γ₀/2π) =: L/2π` (Riemann–von Mangoldt main term) gives
`Σ_γ ≈ (L/π)·2π‖g‖²·½|Φ_φ(½)|² = L‖g‖²|Φ_φ(½)|² > 0` by Lemma P. So at the mean-field level the off-line zero's
coupling to its on-line partners is *positive*; the negative directions of the hyperbolic planes are visible only
through the fluctuation `S(t)` of the zero counting, i.e. only to tests that resolve individual zeros at spacing
`2π/L`, which needs `a ≳ L = log γ₀`. This is Paper 29's "sub-resolution positivity" (L5) and the record's N-e
("super-critical topology"), and it is why the window index jumps from `0` to `∞` rather than growing: below the
resolution scale nothing is seen, above it everything is. The Riemann–von Mangoldt error `∫S(t)f'(t)dt` is
`O(log γ₀·a·e^{δa}‖g‖_1²)`, which exceeds the main term precisely when `a ≳ log γ₀`; hence the remark cannot be
upgraded to a theorem in the regime where it would matter, and is left HEURISTIC.

## §6 Proposition E — Schur products of certificates, and why they do not descend

Recall (P46 def:W, thm:Z): for a flat-top window `w ∈ 𝒲_T`, `f_w(τ) = ⟨𝔴, w(u)cos(τu)⟩` is the Fourier transform
of the distribution `w·𝔴`, and `w·𝔴` is positive definite iff `f_w ≥ 0` (Bochner; `f_w` is continuous of
logarithmic growth), in which case `W_T(F) = ⟨w𝔴, c_F⟩ ≥ (inf f_w)‖F‖²` for `F ∈ L²(I_T)` (prop:P with `β = (2,0)`).

**Proposition E.** *(i) If `w₁ ∈ 𝒲_{T₁}`, `w₂ ∈ 𝒲_{T₂}` are certificates (`f_{w_i} ≥ 0`) then `Ω := w₁⊗w₂` makes
`Ω·(𝔴⊗𝔴)` positive definite on `R²`, with Fourier transform `f_{w₁}⊗f_{w₂}`, and `Q_2(h) ≥ (inf f_{w₁})(inf f_{w₂})‖h‖²`
for `h ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₁}×I_{T₂})`. (ii) If `D` is a positive-definite distribution on `R²` and `Φ ∈ C_c^∞(R)` is positive
definite, the `σ`-distribution `D_Φ: G ↦ ⟨D, G(u₁+u₂)Φ(u₁−u₂)⟩` is positive definite on `R`. (iii) A positive
definite `Ω·(𝔴⊗𝔴)` with `Ω ≡ 1` on `R(a,b)` implies `Q_2 ≥ 0` on `C_c^∞(R(a,b))` and hence (Theorem B(iv)) only
`W_c ≥ 0` for `c < min(a,b)`.*

*Proof.* (i) `(w₁⊗w₂)(𝔴⊗𝔴) = (w₁𝔴)⊗(w₂𝔴)`; the Fourier transform of a tensor product is the tensor product,
`f_{w₁}⊗f_{w₂} ≥ 0`; for `h` supported in `I_{T₁}×I_{T₂}`, `c_h` is supported in `[−2T₁,2T₁]×[−2T₂,2T₂]` where
`Ω ≡ 1`, so `Q_2(h) = ⟨Ω𝔴⊗𝔴, c_h⟩ = (1/4π²)∫∫ f_{w₁}(ξ₁)f_{w₂}(ξ₂)|ĥ(½+iξ₁,½+iξ₂)|²dξ ≥ (inf f_{w₁})(inf f_{w₂})‖h‖²`
by Plancherel on `R²`. (ii) Let `ν := D̂ ≥ 0` (Bochner–Schwartz: a positive tempered measure on `R²`). With
`u₁ = (σ+τ)/2`, `u₂ = (σ−τ)/2`, `ξ₁u₁+ξ₂u₂ = ((ξ₁+ξ₂)/2)σ + ((ξ₁−ξ₂)/2)τ`, so the Fourier transform of
`G(u₁+u₂)Φ(u₁−u₂)` is `½Ĝ((ξ₁+ξ₂)/2)Φ̂((ξ₁−ξ₂)/2)`. Hence `⟨D_Φ,G⟩ = c∫Ĝ(η)dν_Φ(η)` (`c>0` a normalisation
constant), `ν_Φ` := the push-forward of the measure `Φ̂((ξ₁−ξ₂)/2)ν` under `(ξ₁,ξ₂) ↦ (ξ₁+ξ₂)/2`, a positive
measure since `Φ̂ ≥ 0`; so `D_Φ` is positive definite by Bochner. (iii) is Theorem B(iv). ∎

**6.1 Reading.** Certificates multiply (Schur), and thickened restrictions of positive-definite squares are
positive definite — but the only way back to one variable is through the thin side of the region (B(iv)) or
through Proposition C, where `𝔴` re-enters multiplied by `C_0 < 0`. There is no "square root" of a two-variable
certificate. This is the precise sense in which the Schur square is not a positivity *mechanism*: it converts
certificates into certificates and nothing into nothing.
