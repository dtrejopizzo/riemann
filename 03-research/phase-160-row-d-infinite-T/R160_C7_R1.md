## §1 Definitions

**1.1 Tests and the global forms.** `𝒟 := C_c^∞(ℝ,ℝ)`. For `F ∈ 𝒟`, `F̃(t) := F(−t)`, `c_{F,G}(u) := ∫F(s)G(s−u)ds`
(so `c_F := c_{F,F} = F∗F̃` is even, `c_F(0) = ‖F‖²`), `A(F) := ∫F(t)e^{t/2}dt = Φ_F(1)`, `B(F) := ∫F(t)e^{−t/2}dt =
Φ_F(0)`, `Φ_F(s) := ∫F(t)e^{(s−½)t}dt` (P46 main.tex:131–140). `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)` (P46 `lem:K`),
`C₀ = ψ(¼) − log π`, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`. The **linear Weil functional** on even `h ∈ 𝒟`:

  `𝔞^lin(h) := C₀h(0) + ∫₀^∞ (h(0) − h(u))K(u)du − Σ_{n≥2} v_n h(log n)`   (finite: `h` compactly supported).

For `F, G ∈ 𝒟` put `𝔞(F,G) := 𝔞^lin((F∗G̃)_ev)`, where `(·)_ev` is the even part. Explicitly

  `𝔞(F,G) = C₀⟨F,G⟩ + ¼∬(F(s)−F(t))(G(s)−G(t))K(|s−t|)dsdt − ½Σ_n v_n[c_{F,G}(log n) + c_{F,G}(−log n)]`,  (1.1)

the first two terms being the polarisation of `C₀‖F‖² + 𝔈(c_F)` of P46 `thm:A` (`2𝔈(c_F) = ½∬K(|s−t|)|F(s)−F(t)|²`,
main.tex:2530–2540). For `supp F, supp G ⊂ I_T` only `n` with `log n < 2T` contribute, and (1.1) is the
polarisation of `𝔞_T` (P46 main.tex:125–131, `thm:A`); **so `𝔞` is one form on `𝒟` restricting to every window
form `𝔞_T`, and it is not a truncation.** `W(F,G) := 𝔞(F,G) + A(F)B(G) + A(G)B(F)` is the polarisation of eq:W.

**1.2 The explicit formula (CITED in-repo).** For even `h ∈ 𝒟`: `Σ_ρ Φ_h(ρ) = 2∫h(u)cosh(u/2)du − 𝔞^lin(h)`
(P46 `thm:Z`(i) at `w = h`, `τ = 0`; P45 Thm 9.4), absolutely convergent. For odd `h`, `Σ_ρ Φ_h(ρ) = 0` (pair `ρ`
with `1−ρ`: `Φ_h(1−ρ) = −Φ_h(ρ)`). Hence for all `F, G ∈ 𝒟`: `W(F,G) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ)` and

  `A(F)B(G) + A(G)B(F) − Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ) = −𝔞(F,G)`.  (1.2)

The left side is `I_∞(Γ_F,Γ_G)` of P47 Thm 11.5 / (9.9) (brief §1.12, dictionary D17). **In this memo `I_∞` on
translation classes is *defined* by the right side of (1.2), which is prime-side and zero-free; (1.2) is the
identification with P47's zero-side `I_∞`.**

**1.3 The intersection algebra `𝒜`.** Let `𝒱 := ℝe⁺ ⊕ ℝe⁻ ⊕ 𝒟 ⊂ C^∞(ℝ)`, `e^±(u) := e^{±u/2}` (a direct sum:
the characters are not compactly supported). Convolution `Γ_F: ψ ↦ F∗ψ` preserves `𝒱`, with
`Γ_F e⁺ = B(F)e⁺`, `Γ_F e⁻ = A(F)e⁻`, `Γ_F Γ_G = Γ_{F∗G}`. Let `P₊, P₋` be the coordinate projectors of `𝒱` onto
`ℝe⁺`, `ℝe⁻`. Then `P₊Γ_F = Γ_FP₊ = B(F)P₊`, `P₋Γ_F = Γ_FP₋ = A(F)P₋`, `P₊P₋ = 0`, `P_±² = P_±`, so

  `𝒜 := ℝP₊ ⊕ ℝP₋ ⊕ Γ(𝒟)` (direct; `Γ(𝒟) =: (T)`) is a commutative algebra of operators on `𝒱`.

*Transpose:* the anti-involution `t` of `𝒜` with `Γ_Fᵗ := Γ_{F̃}`, `P₊ᵗ := P₋`, `P₋ᵗ := P₊`. (Well-defined: `𝒜` is
commutative and `(Γ_FP₊)ᵗ = B(F)P₋ = P₋Γ_{F̃}` since `A(F̃) = B(F)`.) *Supertrace:* the linear functional
`Str: 𝒜 → ℝ`, `Str(P_±) := 1`, `Str(Γ_h) := −𝔞^lin(h_ev)`. *Intersection form:* `B(x,y) := Str(yᵗx)`.
*Fibre classes:* `f₁ := P₊`, `f₂ := P₋`; `H := f₁ + f₂`. *Bidegree:* `deg(x) := (B(x,f₁), B(x,f₂)) ∈ ℝ²`.
For `T > 0`, `𝒟_T := {F ∈ 𝒟: supp F ⊂ I_T}`, `(T)_T := Γ(𝒟_T)`, `𝒦_T := ℝf₁ ⊕ ℝf₂ ⊕ (T)_T`.

**1.4 Kernel realisation of the fibre classes.** `P₊ = e⁺ ⊗ ℓ₊`, `P₋ = e⁻ ⊗ ℓ₋` with the boundary functionals
`ℓ₊(ψ) := lim_{v→+∞} e^{−v/2}ψ(v)`, `ℓ₋(ψ) := lim_{v→−∞} e^{v/2}ψ(v)` on `𝒱` (`ℓ₊(e⁺) = 1`, `ℓ₊(e⁻) = 0`,
`ℓ₊|_𝒟 = 0`; likewise `ℓ₋`). As generalized kernels on the compactified square `[−∞,∞]²`, `f₁` is supported on
the boundary line `v = +∞` and `f₂` on `v = −∞`: the fibre classes `"ℝ×{v₀}"` of Problem 25.13(β) exist as the
fibres over the two ends of the flow line. (On the toy `C_p×C_p` every fibre `C_p×{v₀}` is in the same class;
here the class is represented only at the ends, where the character normalisation `e^{∓v/2}` makes the pairing
finite. This is the exact reason P47 §25's rank-one `L²` kernels `φ⊗ψ` could not represent them: `1 ∉ L²`.)

**1.5 Reading of `Str`.** By 1.2, `Str(Γ_h) = Φ_h(0) + Φ_h(1) − Σ_ρ Φ_h(ρ) = Tr_{H⁰}(Γ_h) + Tr_{H²}(Γ_h) −
Tr_{H¹}(Γ_h)` with `H⁰ = ℝe⁺` (eigenvalue `Φ_h(0)`), `H² = ℝe⁻` (eigenvalue `Φ_h(1)`) and `H¹` the zeros: `Str` is
the Lefschetz supertrace, *declared* from the prime side, exactly as rows A–C declare their trace (brief §2 item

**1.6 Relation to Paper 43 Thm 9.13(a).** That theorem says the ruling plane is realisable only as a quotient

ruling plane is adjoined as the boundary classes of 1.4. No statement of Paper 43 is contradicted.

## §2 The polar completion answers Problem 25.13

**Theorem 1 (PROVED, [compl]).** (0) `B` is a well-defined symmetric bilinear form on `𝒜`, with Gram table

  `B(f₁,f₁) = B(f₂,f₂) = 0`, `B(f₁,f₂) = 1`, `B(f₁,Γ_F) = A(F)`, `B(f₂,Γ_F) = B(F)`, `B(Γ_F,Γ_G) = −𝔞(F,G)`.

(α) `B|_{(T)×(T)} = I_∞` (P47 Thm 11.5) by (1.2), so the zero data of `ξ` is carried.
(β) `f₁, f₂ ∈ 𝒜` are the boundary-supported generalized kernels of 1.4, and `B(f₁,f₂) = 1` is finite and nonzero.
(γ) The diagonal translations `T_a` (conjugation by the flow `Γ_{δ_a}` on `𝒱`) act trivially on `𝒜`; `B` is
`T_a`-invariant, hence a property of the square and not of a chart.
(δ) `deg: 𝒜 → ℝ²` is additive, `deg(f₁) = (0,1)`, `deg(f₂) = (1,0)`, `deg(Γ_F) = (A(F),B(F))`, and multiplicative on
`(T)`: `deg(Γ_{F∗G}) = deg(Γ_F)·deg(Γ_G)` componentwise. `H` is ample: `B(H,H) = 2 > 0`, and `B(H,Γ_F) = A(F)+B(F) > 0`
for `F ≥ 0`, `F ≢ 0`.

*Proof.* (0) Symmetry: `Str(zᵗ) = Str(z)` on `𝒜` (`Str(Γ_{h̃}) = −𝔞^lin((h̃)_ev) = −𝔞^lin(h_ev)`; `Str(P_±) = 1`), so
`B(y,x) = Str(xᵗy) = Str((xᵗy)ᵗ) = Str(yᵗx) = B(x,y)` using that `t` is an anti-involution and `𝒜` is commutative.
Table: `B(P₊,P₊) = Str(P₋P₊) = 0`; `B(P₊,P₋) = Str(P₊P₊) = 1`; `B(P₊,Γ_F) = Str(Γ_{F̃}P₊) = Str(B(F̃)P₊) = B(F̃) = A(F)`;
`B(P₋,Γ_F) = A(F̃) = B(F)`; `B(Γ_F,Γ_G) = Str(Γ_{G̃∗F}) = −𝔞^lin((F∗G̃)_ev) = −𝔞(F,G)` by definition 1.1.
(α) is (1.2). (β) is 1.4 and the table. (γ) `Γ_{δ_a}Γ_FΓ_{δ_{−a}} = Γ_F`; `Γ_{δ_a}P₊Γ_{δ_{−a}} = e^{a/2}e^{−a/2}P₊ = P₊`
(`e⁺` is an eigenvector of translation with eigenvalue `e^{a/2}`, and `ℓ₊∘Γ_{δ_{−a}} = e^{−a/2}ℓ₊`); same for `P₋`.
(δ) `Φ_{F∗G} = Φ_FΦ_G`. Ampleness: `B(H,H) = 2B(f₁,f₂) = 2`; `A(F)+B(F) = ∫F·2cosh(t/2) > 0`. ∎

**Remark 2.1 (what is and is not new).** The Gram table is eq:W read as an intersection table; its content is
the explicit formula. New relative to the record: (i) the *construction* — a single algebra with transpose and
supertrace in which the fibre classes are subobjects (boundary classes), answering (α)–(δ) of Problem 25.13,
which P47 :10961 and R148_005 §4 list as `OPEN` for (α) with (β)–(γ) ("on `C_Q×C_Q` the fibre classes have
infinite measure and that normalisation is not defined"); the normalisation *is* defined once the fibre is placed
at the end of the flow line with the character weight `e^{∓v/2}`; (ii) the `T_a`-invariance is exact, not up to

## §4 The Metzler structure: the arithmetic square counts points off the diagonal

**Theorem 3 (PROVED).**
(a) *(off-diagonal positivity)* Let `F, G ∈ 𝒟`, `F, G ≥ 0`, with `dist(supp F, supp G) > 0`. Then

  `B(Γ_F,Γ_G) = ½∬F(s)G(t)K(|s−t|)dsdt + ½Σ_{n≥2} v_n[c_{F,G}(log n) + c_{F,G}(−log n)] ≥ 0`,

with strict inequality if `F ≢ 0 ≢ G` (already the archimedean term is `> 0` since `K > 0`).
(b) *(infinite genus)* For real `φ ∈ 𝒟`, `φ ≢ 0`, and `F_ε(t) := ε^{−1/2}φ(t/ε)` (so `‖F_ε‖ = ‖φ‖`):
`B(Γ_{F_ε},Γ_{F_ε}) = −‖φ‖² log(1/ε) + O(1)` as `ε → 0`, the `O(1)` depending on `φ` only.
(c) *(Perron ground state of the full window form)* On `L²(I_T)` let `A_T` be the self-adjoint operator of the
closed form `𝔞_T` (P46 §1.2). Then `A_T` has compact resolvent, `e^{−tA_T}` (`t > 0`) is positivity improving, and
the bottom `λ₀(T) := inf spec(A_T|L²(I_T))` is a simple eigenvalue with an a.e. strictly positive eigenfunction
`F*_T`. In particular `A(F*_T) > 0`, `B(F*_T) > 0`, so `F*_T ∉ P_T`.

*Proof.* (a) In (1.1), `⟨F,G⟩ = 0` and `F(s)G(s) ≡ 0`, so `¼∬(F(s)−F(t))(G(s)−G(t))K = ¼∬[−F(s)G(t) − F(t)G(s)]K(|s−t|)
= −½∬F(s)G(t)K(|s−t|)`, the integral converging because `K` is bounded on `[d,∞)`, `d = dist > 0`. The comb terms
are `−½Σ v_n[…]` in `𝔞`, hence `+½Σ v_n[…]` in `B = −𝔞`, with `v_n ≥ 0` and `c_{F,G}(±log n) = ∫F(s)G(s∓log n)ds ≥ 0`.
(b) `c_{F_ε}(u) = c_φ(u/ε)`, so `𝔈(c_{F_ε}) = ∫₀^∞(c_φ(0) − c_φ(u/ε))K(u)du`. Let `R` be such that `c_φ` vanishes
outside `[−R,R]`. Split at `u = εR`: on `[εR,∞)` the integrand is `‖φ‖²K(u)`, and `∫_{εR}^∞K(u)du = log(1/(εR)) +
O(1)` because `K(u) = 1/u + O(1)` near `0` and `K` is integrable at `∞`; on `[0,εR]`, substituting `u = εw`,
`∫₀^R(c_φ(0) − c_φ(w))εK(εw)dw → ∫₀^R(c_φ(0)−c_φ(w))w^{−1}dw < ∞` (`c_φ` is `C^∞` and even, so `c_φ(0)−c_φ(w) = O(w²)`),
uniformly bounded in `ε`. The comb term `Σ_n v_n c_φ(log n/ε)` vanishes once `ε < (log 2)/R`. Hence
`𝔞(F_ε,F_ε) = C₀‖φ‖² + ‖φ‖²log(1/ε) + O(1)` and `B = −𝔞`.
(c) Write `A_T = Γ_T − C` with `Γ_T` the archimedean operator of P46 `prop:nodes` (form `C₀‖F‖² + 𝔈(c_F)`) and
`C := Σ_{n∈N_T} (v_n/2)(S_{log n} + S_{−log n})` compressed to `I_T`, a bounded operator with `‖C‖ ≤ A_T` which is
positivity preserving (shifts with nonnegative weights, extension by zero and restriction preserve positivity).
By `prop:nodes`(i) (PROVED, main.tex:2530–2551), `Γ_T` has compact resolvent and `e^{−tΓ_T}` is positivity
improving. A bounded perturbation keeps the form domain and the compact resolvent. By the Trotter product
formula, `e^{−tA_T} = s‑lim_k (e^{−tΓ_T/k}e^{tC/k})^k`. Since `e^{tC/k} = Σ_j (tC/k)^j/j! ≥ I` in the sense of
positivity-preserving operators (`e^{tC/k}φ ≥ φ` for `φ ≥ 0`), and products of positivity-preserving operators

inequality, so `e^{−tA_T}φ ≥ e^{−tΓ_T}φ > 0` a.e. for `φ ≥ 0`, `φ ≢ 0`: `e^{−tA_T}` is positivity improving. The
bottom of `A_T` is an eigenvalue (compact resolvent), and Reed–Simon IV Thm XIII.44 gives simplicity and an a.e.

## §5 The Lorentzian dichotomy without the ruling plane (EQUIV), and the Frobenius lattice

**Theorem 4 (PROVED equivalence; EQUIV, [compl] on `L²(I_T)`).** The following are equivalent.
 (i) RH.
 (ii) For every `T > 0`, `n₋(A_T|L²(I_T)) ≤ 1`.
 (iii) For every `T > 0`, the second eigenvalue `λ₁(T)` of `A_T` on `L²(I_T)` is `≥ 0`.
 (iv) For every `T > 0`, `𝒩_T := {F ∈ L²(I_T): 𝔞_T(F) < 0}` is empty or equals `𝒞_T ∪ (−𝒞_T)` with `𝒞_T` an open
     convex cone; when `λ₀(T) < 0`, `𝒞_T = {F ∈ 𝒩_T: ⟨F,F*_T⟩ > 0}` and `F*_T ∈ 𝒞_T`.
 (v) For every `T > 0`, `n₊(B|(T)_T) ≤ 1` (the intersection form is Lorentzian on the flow classes alone).

*Proof.* (ii)⟺(iii): `A_T` has compact resolvent (Thm 3(c)), so `n₋` is the number of negative eigenvalues.
(ii)⟺(v): `B(Γ_F,Γ_G) = −𝔞_T(F,G)` on `𝒟_T`, which is a form core of `𝔞_T` (`C_c^∞(I_T)` is dense in the form
domain `{F ∈ L²(I_T): ∫log(2+|τ|)|F̂|² < ∞}`; standard), and the negative index of a closed semibounded form equals
that of its restriction to a core.
(i)⟹(ii): `𝔞_T = W_T − 2AB` on real `F`, and `−2A(F)B(F) = ½(A−B)(F)² − ½(A+B)(F)²`. If `V ⊂ L²(I_T)` were a
2-dimensional subspace on which `𝔞_T < 0`, pick `0 ≠ F ∈ V` with `(A+B)(F) = 0`; then `𝔞_T(F) = W_T(F) +
½(A−B)(F)² ≥ 0` by RH (Weil), a contradiction.
(ii)⟹(i): Suppose RH fails. By P45 Thm 10.4 there are `F₊` even and `F₋` odd, real, compactly supported,
primitive, with `W(F_±) < 0`. Choose `T` with both supports in `I_T`. Primitivity gives `𝔞_T(F_±) = W_T(F_±) < 0`.
The symbol `σ_T` is even, so `A_T` commutes with the parity operator and `𝔞_T(F₊,F₋) = 0`. Hence `𝔞_T < 0` on
`span{F₊,F₋}`, i.e. `n₋(A_T) ≥ 2`, contradicting (ii).
(ii)⟺(iv): If `n₋ = 0`, `𝒩_T = ∅`. If `n₋ = 1` with eigenvector `F*` and eigenvalue `λ₀ < 0`: on `(F*)^⊥`,
`𝔞_T ≥ λ₁ ≥ 0`, so for `F = αF* + F'` (`F' ⊥ F*`), `𝔞_T(F) = λ₀α² + 𝔞_T(F')` with `𝔞_T(F') ≥ 0`; thus
`𝒞_T = {(α,F'): α > 0, √(𝔞_T(F')) < √|λ₀| α}` is the strict sublevel set of the convex function
`(α,F') ↦ √(𝔞_T(F')) − √|λ₀|α` (a seminorm minus a linear form), hence an open convex cone, and `𝒩_T = 𝒞_T ∪ (−𝒞_T)`.
Conversely, if `n₋ ≥ 2` there is a plane `V` with `V∖{0} ⊂ 𝒩_T`; if `𝒩_T = 𝒞 ∪ (−𝒞)` with `𝒞` an open convex cone
not containing `0`, then `𝒞 ∩ V` and `(−𝒞) ∩ V` are disjoint open convex cones in the plane covering `V∖{0}`
(disjoint because `x, −x ∈ 𝒞` would give `0 = ½x + ½(−x) ∈ 𝒞`); but two disjoint open convex cones in `ℝ²` cannot
cover `ℝ²∖{0}` (their union omits at least one ray). Contradiction. ∎

## §6 The Hodge–Riemann residue and what the Lorentzian package can and cannot do

**6.1 The open step, exactly.**

> **(HR-T) — OPEN.** For every `T > 0`, the quadratic form `−𝔞_T` on `L²(I_T)` has at most one positive
> eigenvalue. Equivalently (Theorem 4): `λ₁(A_T|L²(I_T)) ≥ 0` for all `T`; equivalently RH.

Everything else in the Hodge-index package on `𝒜` is proved: the object (Theorem 1), the ample class and the
Castelnuovo shape (Theorem 2), the Metzler/Perron structure identifying the unique candidate positive direction
(Theorem 3, Cor 4.2), and the non-existence of a finite-rank shadow (Theorem 5). (HR-T) is the Hodge–Riemann
relation in middle degree for the "surface" `𝒜`, restricted to the flow classes; it is RH.

**Proposition 6.2 (the tools of Lorentzian theory reduce to (HR-T) on a surface; PROVED as an analysis).**
(a) *Brändén–Huh degree-2 class.* A quadratic form `x ↦ xᵀMx` is Lorentzian iff `M` has nonnegative entries and
at most one positive eigenvalue (Brändén–Huh 2020, Def. of `L²_n`; CITED). Our `M` is `B` on `(T)_T`, whose
off-diagonal is nonnegative (Thm 3(a)) and whose diagonal is `−∞` (Thm 3(b)): it is not in the class, and the
class condition "at most one positive eigenvalue" is (HR-T) verbatim. Subtracting a nonnegative diagonal
preserves "at most one positive eigenvalue" (Weyl), so one could try to prove it for the zero-diagonal off-diagonal
part `M₀`; but `M₀` on a finite family is a positive matrix with zero diagonal, which has at most one positive
eigenvalue only in the complete-multipartite case (Smith 1970; CITED), and the Galerkin compressions of `M₀` to
point-like classes are the divisibility-plus-`K` matrices of Theorem 5 with the diagonal removed, which are not of
that type in general. So the diagonal cannot be dropped, and with it kept the statement is (HR-T).
(b) *Closure under limits.* "At most one positive eigenvalue" is closed under limits of matrices and is inherited
by every Galerkin compression; conversely (HR-T) holds iff every finite compression of `−𝔞_T` has at most one
positive eigenvalue. Theorem 5 shows the compressions to point-like classes are negative definite (trivially
Lorentzian) while, if RH fails, some compression to two spread-out classes `F₊, F₋` (Thm 4) has two positive
eigenvalues. There is no finite generating family (`thm:arena`, `cor:arena`: `N*(T) ≥ exp(4e^T(1+o(1)))`), so the
limit theorems give nothing beyond the definition.
(c) *Alexandrov–Fenchel.* Mixed-volume matrices `(V(K_i,K_j))` are Lorentzian; the toy Gram `J − I` *is* the
mixed-volume matrix of three segments in `ℝ²` (`V([0,a],[0,b]) = |det(a,b)|`), which is why the toy has the Hodge
signature for free. A mixed-volume model of `B` on `(T)` would require convex bodies `K_F` with
`V(K_F,K_G) = B(Γ_F,Γ_G)`, whose diagonal `V(K_F,K_F) = −∞` is impossible for volumes (`V(K,K) = vol(K) ≥ 0`).
The sign of the diagonal — the infinite genus — is exactly what excludes a convex-body model: a mixed-volume
matrix has nonnegative diagonal, i.e. `g ≤ 1`.
(d) *AHK / Babaee–Huh (O-14, Docs 125–126).* The Hodge–Riemann relations for a matroid Chow ring are proved by a
finite induction on flats and need a finite graded ring with a degree map; on a surface the middle-degree
relation is the Hodge index itself, and for `d = 2` the volume polynomial `vol(Σx_iD_i) = ½Σx_ix_j(D_i·D_j)` is the
same quadratic form. So on the square, "Lorentzian volume polynomial" and "Hodge index" coincide, and the AHK
machinery would have to be run on a *higher-dimensional* object (a product `X^n`, whose HR relations in degree
`1` with `ℓ^{2n−2}` are still the Hodge index of the divisor classes, or a ring of higher degree), not on the
square. Babaee–Huh's wrong-signature tropical surfaces are irrelevant here for a different reason than Doc 125
gave: the arithmetic square is not a limit of tropical surfaces at all, it is a limit of negative definite
lattices (Theorem 5) in which the positive direction is delocalised.

**6.3 What it would take to close (HR-T) — stated so that a future round can attack or refute it.**
(1) *A polarization of `H¹`.* Since `B(Γ_F^prim,Γ_G^prim) = −Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ)`, (HR-T) is the statement that
the distribution `Z(t) := Σ_ρ e^{(ρ−½)t}` is positive definite on `ℝ` (Bochner), and a proof "from the surface"
would need a real Hilbert structure on the span of the zero-characters making the primitive `(1,1)`-classes
anti-self-dual. That is OT-5′ / Lemma T territory: a postulated one is RH (O-2, S-7). Not attempted.
(2) *A higher-degree structure.* A homogeneous polynomial `p` of degree `k ≥ 3` on a cone of tests, with
nonnegative coefficients on the cone `{F ≥ 0}`, whose Hessian at every point of the cone is `−𝔞_T` up to a
positive factor, would put (HR-T) inside the Brändén–Huh class (Lorentzian polynomials of degree `k` are those
whose `(k−2)`-fold derivatives are Lorentzian quadratics with M-convex support). The natural candidates
`p_k(F) := Str(Γ_F^{k})` have Hessian `k(k−1)Str(Γ_{F^{∗(k−2)}}Γ_GΓ_H)`, a bilinear form in `(G,H)` which is *not*
of the form `Str(yᵗx)` (no transpose), i.e. `Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)^{k−2}Φ_G(ρ)Φ_H(ρ)` plus polar terms — a complex-bilinear,
not Hermitian, form whose signature is uncontrolled. The candidates with a transpose, `Str(Γ_F^{k−2}Γ_GΓ_H^t)`, are
`−𝔞` twisted by the multiplier `Φ_F^{k−2}` and are Lorentzian for all `F ≥ 0` only if (HR-T) already holds for the
twisted forms. So the degree-raising trick needs an *independent* source of nonnegative coefficients. OPEN; this
is the only place where the Huh toolkit could contribute more than linear algebra, and it is exactly the
tensor-power / amplification territory of B8 (brief 6.2.12), with the same missing sublinear input.
(3) *A convexity proof of Theorem 4(iv).* (HR-T) ⟺ `𝒞_T` convex for all `T`. A direct proof would be a
"hyperbolicity" argument: `−𝔞_T` hyperbolic with respect to `F*_T` (Gårding). Hyperbolic quadratics are those with
signature `(1,·)`, so again (HR-T); the theory of hyperbolic polynomials (Gårding, Bauschke–Güler–Lewis–Sendov)
would apply to a higher-degree `p` as in (2).
