### 1.2 Riemann's kernel `𝔎`
`𝔎∈C^∞(ℝ)` is defined by `𝔎̂=Ξ`, `Ξ(τ)=ξ(½+iτ)`, i.e. `Φ_𝔎(s)=ξ(s)` (entire). Classical facts (CITED, Titchmarsh §10.1;

### 1.3 The full-line Weil form and (EF)
Let `𝒯` be the space of real `F∈C^∞(ℝ)` with `F^{(k)}(t)=O(e^{−a|t|})` for every `k≥0` and every `a>0`. Then `𝔎r∈𝒯` for

```
            − Σ_{n≥2}(v_n/2)[c_{F,G}(log n)+c_{F,G}(−log n)] + A(F)B(G)+A(G)B(F),          (1.1)
```
`W_∞(F):=W_∞(F,F)`. Every term converges absolutely on `𝒯` (`|c_{F,G}(u)|` decays faster than any exponential;

**(EF)** (CITED). For `F,G∈𝒯`: `W_∞(F,G)=Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ)`, the sum over the nontrivial zeros of `ζ` converging
absolutely. For compactly supported `F=G` this is Paper 45 Thm 9.4 (PROVED there); for `𝒯` it is the classical form of

`½K(u)=O(u^{−1})`: finite, and bounded by `Ce^{a′|x|}sup_{|y−x|≤1}𝔎(y)/h(x)`. For `u≥1` the Γ-part is
`≤(c_𝔎/h(x))∫_1^∞(𝔎(x−u)+𝔎(x+u))(|f(x∓u)|+|f(x)|)K(u)du`, finite and of exponential type by the decay of `𝔎`; the prime
part is `(c_𝔎/h(x))Σ_nΛ(n)n^{−1/2}𝔎(x∓log n)(…)`: with `m=log n−x`, group `n∈[e^{x+m},e^{x+m+1})`, use Chebyshev
`Σ_{n∈[N,eN)}Λ(n)n^{−1/2}≤C√N` and `𝔎(y)e^{a|y|}≤C_a′exp(−e^{|y|})` to get `≤C e^{x/2}Σ_{m∈ℤ}e^{m/2}exp(−e^{|m|})e^{a|m|}/h(x)`,
bounded; the same for `x→−∞` by evenness of `𝔎`. Derivatives: differentiate under the integral (the same majorants apply
to `𝔎^{(k)}`, `h^{(k)}`, `f^{(k)}`). (ii) `E_𝔎(f,g)=∫∫𝔎(x)𝔎(x−u)(f(x)−f(x−u))(g(x)−g(x−u))dxν(du)`; expand the product, and
in the terms carrying `g(x−u)` substitute `x↦x+u`; absolute convergence (as in (i), with `g` compactly supported) justifies
Fubini and gives `E_𝔎(f,g)=−∫g(x)𝔎(x)∫[𝔎(x−u)(f(x−u)−f(x))+𝔎(x+u)(f(x+u)−f(x))]ν(du)dx=−∫g(x)(𝓛f)(x)(h(x)𝔎(x)/c_𝔎)dx`.
Since `f∈D(E)` and `C_c^∞` is a form core, `E_𝔎(f,g)=⟨−𝓛f,g⟩` for all `g∈D(E)`, so `f∈D(L_𝔎)`, `L_𝔎f=−𝓛f`. (iii) Same
substitution with `g=1` (absolute convergence holds since `μ` has double-exponential decay). (iv) `𝓛1=0` is (1.2);
`E_𝔎(r)=0` forces `r(x)=r(x−u)` for a.e. `x` and `ν`-a.e. `u`, i.e. all `u>0`, so `r` is constant. ∎

Following Bakry–Émery, for `f,g∈𝒜`:
```
Γ(f,g) := ½[𝓛(fg) − f𝓛g − g𝓛f] = ½∫(f(y)−f(x))(g(y)−g(x))J_x(dy),     Γ(f):=Γ(f,f) ≥ 0,
Γ₂(f)  := ½𝓛Γ(f) − Γ(f,𝓛f).                                                                        (1.3)
```
(The integral formula for `Γ` is the usual algebra for jump generators; `Γ(f,g)∈𝒜` by Lemma 1.1(i).) By Lemma 1.1(ii),(iii):
`∫Γ(f,g)dμ=E_𝔎(f,g)` and `∫Γ₂(f)dμ = −∫Γ(f,𝓛f)dμ = −E_𝔎(f,𝓛f) = ∫(𝓛f)²dμ = ‖L_𝔎f‖²`.                             (1.4)

For `ρ∈ℝ`, `N∈[1,∞]`, say `𝓛` satisfies **CD(ρ,N) on `𝒜`** if `Γ₂(f)(x) ≥ ρΓ(f)(x) + N^{−1}(𝓛f(x))²` for all `f∈𝒜`, `x∈ℝ`
(`N^{−1}=0` for `N=∞`).

## §2 Theorem 1: the Doob identity on both parities

**Theorem 1 (T1).** For real `r,s∈𝒜` (in particular `r,s∈C_c^∞(ℝ)`),
```
W_∞(𝔎r,𝔎s) = E_𝔎(r,s) − ½[⟨r,s⟩ − ⟨1,r⟩⟨1,s⟩ + ⟨τ,r⟩⟨τ,s⟩],
W_∞(𝔎r,𝔎r) = E_𝔎(r) − ½Var_μ(r) − ½⟨τ,r⟩²,                                                        (2.1)
```
with `⟨1,r⟩=∫r dμ = A(𝔎r)+B(𝔎r)`, `⟨τ,r⟩ = ∫τ r dμ = A(𝔎r)−B(𝔎r)`. [compl], full line.

*Proof.* Fix `N` and let `W_N(F,G)` be (1.1) with the prime sum restricted to `n≤N`; `W_N→W_∞` on `𝒯` as `N→∞`
(absolute convergence). Write `F=𝔎r`, `G=𝔎s`, `P=𝔎rs∈𝒯`. Compare `W_N(F,G)` with `W_N(𝔎,P)` term by term.

*Diagonal terms.* `C_0⟨𝔎r,𝔎s⟩ − C_0⟨𝔎,𝔎rs⟩ = 0`, and likewise the diagonal parts of the prime terms,
`−Σ_{n≤N}v_n[c_{F,G}(0)−c_{𝔎,P}(0)]=0`, since `c_{F,G}(0)=∫𝔎²rs=c_{𝔎,P}(0)`.

*Jump terms.* Using `½[c_{F,G}(u)+c_{F,G}(−u)] = c_{F,G}(0) − ½∫(F(t)−F(t−u))(G(t)−G(t−u))dt`, every prime term and the
Lévy term are of the shape `½∫(F(t)−F(t−u))(G(t)−G(t−u))dt` with weight `K(u)du` or `v_nδ_{log n}`, i.e. weight `2ν`.
With `𝔎_t:=𝔎(t)`, `𝔎_y:=𝔎(t−u)` etc., the pointwise algebraic identity
```
(𝔎_tr_t−𝔎_yr_y)(𝔎_ts_t−𝔎_ys_y) − (𝔎_t−𝔎_y)(𝔎_tr_ts_t−𝔎_yr_ys_y) = 𝔎_t𝔎_y(r_t−r_y)(s_t−s_y)                  (2.2)
```
(expand both products; the four terms `𝔎_t²r_ts_t`, `𝔎_y²r_ys_y` cancel and the cross terms regroup) gives
```
[jump terms of W_N(F,G)] − [jump terms of W_N(𝔎,P)] = ∫_{(0,∞)}∫𝔎(t)𝔎(t−u)(r(t)−r(t−u))(s(t)−s(t−u))dt ν_N(du) =: E_{𝔎,N}(r,s),
```
`ν_N` being `ν` with primes `≤N`. The constant `C_0−Σ_{n≤N}v_n` multiplies `c_{F,G}(0)−c_{𝔎,P}(0)=0`, so no divergence
arises as `N→∞`.

*Polar terms.* Put `a_r:=∫𝔎rh=c_𝔎⟨1,r⟩`, `b_r:=∫𝔎rσ=c_𝔎⟨τ,r⟩` (as `σ/h=τ` and `dμ=h𝔎dt/c_𝔎`). Then `A(𝔎r)=a_r+b_r`,
`B(𝔎r)=a_r−b_r`, `A(𝔎)=B(𝔎)=c_𝔎`, `A(P)=B(P)=∫𝔎rsh=c_𝔎⟨r,s⟩` (the last because `∫𝔎rs e^{±t/2}=∫𝔎rsh` — no parity is needed:
`A(P)+B(P)=2∫Ph` and the polar term of `W_N(𝔎,P)` is `A(𝔎)B(P)+A(P)B(𝔎)=c_𝔎(A(P)+B(P))=2c_𝔎∫Ph`). Hence
```
[A(F)B(G)+A(G)B(F)] − [A(𝔎)B(P)+A(P)B(𝔎)] = 2a_ra_s − 2b_rb_s − 2c_𝔎²⟨r,s⟩ = −½[⟨r,s⟩−⟨1,r⟩⟨1,s⟩+⟨τ,r⟩⟨τ,s⟩],
```
using `c_𝔎=½`. Altogether `W_N(𝔎r,𝔎s) − W_N(𝔎,𝔎rs) = E_{𝔎,N}(r,s) − ½[…]` exactly, for every `N`. Let `N→∞`:
`W_N(𝔎r,𝔎s)→W_∞(𝔎r,𝔎s)`; `W_N(𝔎,𝔎rs)→W_∞(𝔎,𝔎rs)=0` by (R); `E_{𝔎,N}(r,s)→E_𝔎(r,s)` by dominated convergence (for
`r=s` monotone convergence). This proves (2.1). ∎

**Remark 2.2 (parity).** For even `r`, `⟨τ,r⟩=0` and (2.1) is Paper 41's (2.4): `W_∞(𝔎r,𝔎r)=E_𝔎(r)−½Var_μ(r)`. For odd `r`,
`⟨1,r⟩=0`, `Var_μ(r)=‖r‖²`, and `W_∞(𝔎r,𝔎r)=E_𝔎(r)−½‖r‖²−½⟨τ,r⟩²`: the polar term is a full `−½‖r‖²` plus a rank-one
term in the direction `τ=tanh(t/2)`. Mixed even–odd terms vanish by (P), consistently with (2.1).

**Remark 2.3 (where the exact `Λ` enters).** The only input beyond algebra is `W_∞(𝔎,P)=0`, i.e. (R), i.e. the explicit
formula at the test `𝔎`. A modified comb `Λ′` gives a polarized form `W′_∞` that is not a zero sum (P46 main.tex:3375);
there is then no positive `𝔎′` with `W′_∞(𝔎′,·)≡0` and no identity (2.1). This is the memo's answer to I-1.

**Remark 2.4 (windows).** `W_T(F)=W_∞(F)` for `F∈C_c^∞(I_T)`, and `F=𝔎r` with `r=F/𝔎∈C_c^∞(I_T)`. So `W_T(F)=E_𝔎(F/𝔎)−½Var_μ(F/𝔎)−½⟨τ,F/𝔎⟩²`:
the window bottom `λ^W_T` is the bottom of the right side over `{r∈C_c^∞(I_T): ‖𝔎r‖_{L²(dt)}=1}`, a *different* normalization
from `‖r‖_μ=1`. No window statement is made below; the memo works with the `μ`-normalization, which is the one in which
`E_𝔎` is a Dirichlet form.

## §3 The structure of `L_𝔎`: exact jump rate, parity, both radicals

### 3.1 Lemma 3.1 (L1: the exact jump-rate identity)
For every `t∈ℝ`,
```
Σ_{n≥2}(Λ(n)/√n)[𝔎(t−log n)+𝔎(t+log n)] = 2c_𝔎h(t) + C_0𝔎(t) + ∫_0^∞[𝔎(t)−½(𝔎(t+u)+𝔎(t−u))]K(u)du .          (★)
```
Consequently the total prime jump rate `d_P(x):=J_x(prime atoms)=(c_𝔎/h(x))Σ_n(Λ(n)/√n)[𝔎(x−log n)+𝔎(x+log n)]` equals
```
d_P(x) = ½ + (2h(x))^{−1}[C_0𝔎(x) + ∫_0^∞(𝔎(x)−½(𝔎(x+u)+𝔎(x−u)))K(u)du],       d_P(x) → ½  (|x|→∞),        (3.1)
```
and more precisely `d_P(x)=½ − ½e^{−|x|}(1+o(1))`.

*Proof.* Take `G=g∈C_c^∞` in (R): `W_∞(𝔎,g)=0`. In (1.1) with `F=𝔎`: the Lévy term is
`½∫_0^∞∫(𝔎(t)−𝔎(t−u))(g(t)−g(t−u))dtK(u)du=∫g(t)·½∫_0^∞[2𝔎(t)−𝔎(t−u)−𝔎(t+u)]K(u)du dt` (substitute `t↦t+u` in the terms
carrying `g(t−u)`; Fubini is justified since `2𝔎(t)−𝔎(t−u)−𝔎(t+u)=O(u²)` at `0` uniformly on compacts and `𝔎` decays);
the prime term is `−Σ_n(v_n/2)∫g(t)[𝔎(t+log n)+𝔎(t−log n)]dt`; the polar term is `c_𝔎(A(g)+B(g))=2c_𝔎∫gh`. So
`0=∫g(t){C_0𝔎(t)+∫_0^∞[𝔎(t)−½(𝔎(t+u)+𝔎(t−u))]K(u)du−Σ_n(Λ(n)/√n)[𝔎(t−log n)+𝔎(t+log n)]+2c_𝔎h(t)}dt` for all `g∈C_c^∞`,
and the bracket is continuous in `t`, hence zero: (★). Multiply by `c_𝔎/h(x)` and use `2c_𝔎²=½`: (3.1). For the
asymptotics as `x→+∞`: `𝔎(x)/h(x)→0` doubly exponentially; `∫_0^∞𝔎(x+u)K(u)du/h(x)→0` likewise; and
`∫_0^∞𝔎(x−u)K(u)du=∫𝔎(y)K(x−y)dy` (over `y<x`) `=2e^{−x/2}∫𝔎(y)e^{y/2}dy(1+o(1))=2c_𝔎e^{−x/2}(1+o(1))=e^{−x/2}(1+o(1))`
by `K(u)=2e^{−u/2}(1+O(e^{−u}))` and dominated convergence. So the bracket in (3.1) is `−½e^{−x/2}(1+o(1))` and
`(2h(x))^{−1}=e^{−x/2}(1+o(1))`: `d_P(x)=½−½e^{−x}(1+o(1))`; the case `x→−∞` follows by evenness. Only `d_P→½` is
used below. ∎

**Remark 3.2.** (★) is the explicit formula for the even test `𝔎(·−t)+𝔎(·+t)`, whose Mellin side `2cosh((s−½)t)ξ(s)` vanishes
at every zero: the `𝔎`-smoothed Chebyshev sum has no oscillatory terms at all. Paper 41 `thm:ess` obtains `d_P→½`
("exact tail mass `2c_K²`") from the prime number theorem; (★) shows the limit is exact arithmetic, with the archimedean
correction `(2h)^{−1}[C_0𝔎+…]` carrying `C_0<0` (S-9) inside a term that vanishes at infinity.

### 3.2 The Doob process in words (no probability is used below)
`J_x(dy)=(c_𝔎/h(x))𝔎(y)ν̃(dy−x)`: from `x`, jumps land at `y=x∓log n` with rate `(c_𝔎/h(x))(Λ(n)/√n)𝔎(y)`, and at `y=x∓u` with
Γ-density `(c_𝔎/h(x))𝔎(y)·½K(u)`. The Γ-part has infinite total rate (`K(u)~u^{−1}`) but finite first moment on `(0,1)`.
The landing weight `𝔎(y)` confines the process to the bulk `|y|=O(1)`; from a tail point `x≫1` the only jumps of
non-negligible rate are those landing in the bulk, and their total rate is `d_P(x)+o(1)→½` by (3.1). The threshold ½ of
`σ_ess` (Paper 41 `thm:ess`) is this rate; the essential spectrum of a Markov jump generator "killed at rate `d_∞`" starts at
`d_∞`.

### 3.3 Lemma 3.3 (L2: both radicals)
For `m≥0` put `e_m:=𝔎^{(2m)}/𝔎−4^{−m}` (even) and `w_m:=𝔎^{(2m+1)}/𝔎` (odd); all lie in `𝒜`. Then
```
L_𝔎 e_m = ½ e_m   (m≥1),          L_𝔎 w_m = ½ w_m − 4^{−m−1} τ   (m≥0),          ⟨τ,w_m⟩ = −½·4^{−m}.           (3.2)
```
Hence `ker(L_𝔎−½)` contains the even family `{e_m}_{m≥1}` and the odd family `{w_m−4^{−m}w_0}_{m≥1}`, both linearly
independent (finite linear independence of the `𝔎^{(k)}/𝔎`, Paper 41 Entry C2), and `(L_𝔎−½)(−4w_0)=τ`, `⟨τ,−4w_0⟩=2`.

*Proof.* By Theorem 1 with `r=𝔎^{(k)}/𝔎`, `s∈C_c^∞`, and (R) (`W_∞(𝔎^{(k)},𝔎s)=0`):
`E_𝔎(𝔎^{(k)}/𝔎,s)=½[⟨𝔎^{(k)}/𝔎,s⟩−⟨1,𝔎^{(k)}/𝔎⟩⟨1,s⟩+⟨τ,𝔎^{(k)}/𝔎⟩⟨τ,s⟩]`. Now `⟨1,𝔎^{(k)}/𝔎⟩=c_𝔎^{−1}∫𝔎^{(k)}h=c_𝔎^{−1}(−1)^k∫𝔎h^{(k)}`,
which is `4^{−m}` for `k=2m` (`h^{(2m)}=4^{−m}h`) and `0` for `k` odd (parity); `⟨τ,𝔎^{(k)}/𝔎⟩=c_𝔎^{−1}∫𝔎^{(k)}σ=c_𝔎^{−1}(−1)^k∫𝔎σ^{(k)}`,
which is `0` for `k` even and `−c_𝔎^{−1}4^{−m}·½∫𝔎h=−½·4^{−m}` for `k=2m+1` (`σ^{(2m+1)}=4^{−m}·½h`). Boundary terms in the
integrations by parts vanish by the decay of `𝔎`. So `E_𝔎(𝔎^{(2m)}/𝔎,s)=½⟨𝔎^{(2m)}/𝔎−4^{−m},s⟩` and, as `E_𝔎(1,s)=0`,
`E_𝔎(e_m,s)=½⟨e_m,s⟩`; and `E_𝔎(w_m,s)=½⟨w_m,s⟩−¼4^{−m}⟨τ,s⟩`. By Lemma 1.1(ii) these weak identities are the operator
identities (3.2). ∎

**Remark 3.4.** (3.2) for `m=0` reads `(L_𝔎−½)(𝔎′/𝔎)=−¼τ`: the polar direction of the odd sector is reached from the
first odd radical vector by `L_𝔎−½`. Paper 41 P1 (radical saturation) therefore holds in the odd sector as well
(the family `w_m−4^{−m}w_0`), which the record did not state.

## §4 Theorems 2 and 3: the full-space Poincaré inequality and the Γ₂ form

### 4.1 Lemma 4.1 (odd absorption)
Suppose `E_𝔎(r) ≥ ½‖r‖²` for all odd `r∈C_c^∞` (equivalently `L_𝔎≥½` on `L²(μ)_−`). Then `W_∞(𝔎r,𝔎r) ≥ 0` for every odd
`r∈𝒜`, with equality at `r=𝔎′/𝔎`.

*Proof.* Let `A:=L_𝔎−½` on `L²(μ)_−`, `A≥0` by hypothesis and the core property. Put `w:=−4𝔎′/𝔎∈𝒜∩D(A)`; by (3.2),
`Aw=τ` and `⟨w,Aw⟩=⟨w,τ⟩=2`. For odd `r∈D(A)`, Cauchy–Schwarz for the nonnegative form `⟨·,A·⟩` gives
`⟨τ,r⟩²=⟨Aw,r⟩²≤⟨w,Aw⟩⟨r,Ar⟩=2⟨r,Ar⟩`, i.e. `E_𝔎(r)−½‖r‖²−½⟨τ,r⟩²≥0`, which is `W_∞(𝔎r,𝔎r)≥0` by (2.1). Equality holds
at `r=w`, i.e. at `𝔎′/𝔎`, consistently with `W_∞(𝔎′,𝔎′)=0` (R). ∎

### 4.2 Theorem 2 (T2)
The following are equivalent:
(a) RH; (b) `E_𝔎(r)≥½Var_μ(r)` for all even `r∈C_c^∞`; (c) `E_𝔎(r)≥½‖r‖²` for all odd `r∈C_c^∞`;
(d) `E_𝔎(r)≥½Var_μ(r)` for all `r∈C_c^∞(ℝ)`; (e) `σ(L_𝔎)⊂{0}∪[½,∞)` and `0` is a simple eigenvalue.

*Proof.* (a)⟹(d): RH gives `W_∞(F)≥0` for all `F∈C_c^∞` (Weil's criterion, the easy direction), so by (2.1)
`E_𝔎(r)≥½Var_μ(r)+½⟨τ,r⟩²≥½Var_μ(r)` for `r=F/𝔎∈C_c^∞`. (d)⟹(b),(c) trivially. (b)⟹(a): Paper 39
`cor:exact-spectral-floor` / Paper 41 `thm:doob` (¬RH gives an even `F∈C_c^∞` with `W_∞(F)<0`, Paper 45 Thm 10.4; then
`r=F/𝔎` violates (b)). (c)⟹(a): by Lemma 4.1, (c) gives `W_∞(F)≥0` for all odd `F=𝔎r∈C_c^∞`; if RH failed, Paper 45
Thm 10.4 (odd part; Paper 39 `thm:bilateral`) would give an odd `F∈C_c^∞` with `W_∞(F)<0`. (d)⟺(e): `E_𝔎(r)−½Var_μ(r)=
⟨r,(L_𝔎−½+½|1⟩⟨1|)r⟩`, and `1` spans `ker L_𝔎` (Lemma 1.1(iv)); on `1^⊥` the inequality on the core is `L_𝔎|_{1^⊥}≥½`. ∎

**Remark 4.2.** The two sectors are independently equivalent to RH, each through its own polar structure: even through the
variance (rank-one `|1⟩⟨1|`), odd through the absorbed rank-one `|τ⟩⟨τ|`. In both, *the statement is the absence of
spectrum of `L_𝔎` in `(0,½)`*. The number ½ is `d_∞=2ξ(1)²` (Lemma 3.1), the pole.

### 4.3 Theorem 3 (T3: the Γ₂ form)
The following are equivalent to RH:
(f) `‖L_𝔎r‖²≥½E_𝔎(r)` for all `r∈D(L_𝔎)`; (h) `‖(L_𝔎−¼)r‖≥¼‖r‖` for all `r∈D(L_𝔎)`. Each implies
(g) `∫Γ₂(r)dμ≥½∫Γ(r)dμ` for all `r∈𝒜`; and (g) implies (f) provided `𝒜` is an operator core for `L_𝔎` (graph-norm
dense in `D(L_𝔎)`), which is not proved here (O7).

*Proof.* Spectral theorem for `L_𝔎≥0`: with `dμ_r` the spectral measure of `r`, `‖L_𝔎r‖²−½E_𝔎(r)=∫λ(λ−½)dμ_r(λ)` and
`‖(L_𝔎−¼)r‖²−(1/16)‖r‖²=∫[(λ−¼)²−1/16]dμ_r=∫λ(λ−½)dμ_r`. Both integrands are `≥0` off `(0,½)` and `<0` on it; so (f)⟺(h)⟺
`σ(L_𝔎)∩(0,½)=∅` (if `λ_0∈σ(L_𝔎)∩(0,½)`, a vector spectrally supported in a small neighbourhood violates (f)). With Lemma
1.1(iv), this is (e) of Theorem 2. (f)⟹(g): `𝒜⊂D(L_𝔎)` (Lemma 1.1(ii)) and (1.4) gives `∫Γ₂(r)dμ=‖L_𝔎r‖²`,
`∫Γ(r)dμ=E_𝔎(r)`. (g)⟹(f) under the core hypothesis: `‖(L_𝔎−¼)r‖²−(1/16)‖r‖²` is continuous in the graph norm, so its
nonnegativity on a graph-dense subspace extends to `D(L_𝔎)`. ∎

**Remark 4.3 (the deficit functional).** `𝔇(r):=‖L_𝔎r‖²−½E_𝔎(r)=⟨r,L_𝔎(L_𝔎−½)r⟩`. On the ½-eigenspaces `𝔇=0`; `𝔇(1)=0`.
RH ⟺ `𝔇≥0`. (h) says: ¼, the tail curvature of §5, is the centre of the forbidden interval `(0,½)`, at distance ¼
from both ends.

## §5 Theorem 4: the tail-curvature cap

### 5.1 Lemma 5.1 (the tail lemma)
Let `g∈𝒜`, and suppose there are `b>0`, `x_0`, `C≥1` with `C^{−1}e^{bx}≤g(x)≤Ce^{bx}` and `|g′(x)|≤Ce^{bx}` for `x≥x_0`, and
`|g(y)|≤Ce^{b|y|}` for all `y`. Then `𝓛g(x) = −½g(x)(1+o(1))` as `x→+∞`.

*Proof.* `𝓛g(x)=∫(g(y)−g(x))J_x(dy)` with `J_x(dy)=(c_𝔎/h(x))𝔎(y)ν̃(dy−x)`. Fix `0<ε≤1` and `M,M′≥1`; split the landing point
`y` into five regions and use `h(x)^{−1}≤2e^{−x/2}`, `𝔎(y)e^{a|y|}≤C_a exp(−e^{|y|})` and Chebyshev's `Σ_{N≤n<eN}Λ(n)n^{−1/2}≤C√N`.
(i) `|y−x|<ε`: `|g(y)−g(x)|≤ε·sup_{|z−x|≤1}|g′(z)|≤Cεe^{b(x+1)}`, and `J_x(|y−x|<ε)`-integrals against `|u|` are
`≤(c_𝔎/h(x))sup_{|z−x|≤1}𝔎(z)·∫_0^ε uK(u)du·2`: the contribution is `≤C′εe^{bx}sup_{|z−x|≤1}𝔎(z)/h(x)=o(g(x))`.
(ii) `M≤y≤x−ε`: `|g(y)−g(x)|≤2Ce^{bx}`; `J_x([M,x−ε])≤(c_𝔎/h(x))𝔎(M)ν([ε,∞)∩[0,x])≤(c_𝔎/h(x))𝔎(M)(Ce^{x/2}+C_εx)≤C″𝔎(M)`
(the prime part by Chebyshev and partial summation, the Γ-part `½∫_ε^xK≤C_εx`). Contribution `≤C‴𝔎(M)g(x)`.
(iii) `−M′≤y≤M` (bulk): `g(y)−g(x)=−g(x)(1+O(C_{M,M′}/g(x)))`, so the contribution is `−g(x)J_x([−M′,M])(1+o(1))`. Here
`J_x([−M′,M])=d_P(x)−J^P_x(y>M)−J^P_x(y<−M′)+J^Γ_x([−M′,M])`, with `J^P_x(y>M)≤C″𝔎(M)` by (ii) (plus the negligible
rightward part), `J^P_x(y<−M′)≤ε_0(M′):=CΣ_{m≥M′}e^{m/2}𝔎(m)` (group `log n−x∈[m,m+1)`, Chebyshev, `𝔎(x−log n)≤𝔎(m)`; the
bound is uniform in `x`), and `J^Γ_x([−M′,M])≤(c_𝔎/h(x))·½sup_{u≥x−M}K(u)·∫𝔎≤Ce^{−x/2}e^{−(x−M)/2}→0`. By (3.1)
`d_P(x)→½`. So `J_x([−M′,M])=½+O(𝔎(M))+O(ε_0(M′))+o(1)`.
(iv) `y<−M′`: `|g(y)−g(x)|≤Ce^{b|y|}+g(x)`. The `g(x)` part contributes `≤g(x)J_x(y<−M′)≤g(x)(ε_0(M′)+o(1))`. The `e^{b|y|}`
part: the prime contribution is `≤(c_𝔎/h(x))Σ_{log n≥x+M′}Λ(n)n^{−1/2}Ce^{b(log n−x)}𝔎(x−log n)≤C′Σ_{m≥M′}e^{m/2}e^{b(m+1)}𝔎(m)=:ε_b(M′)`,
uniformly in `x`; the Γ contribution is `≤(c_𝔎/h(x))C∫e^{b|y|}𝔎(y)K(x−y)dy≤C′e^{−x/2}`. Both are `O(1)=o(g(x))`.
(v) `y≥x+ε`: `|g(y)−g(x)|≤Ce^{by}+g(x)`; `J_x(y≥x+ε)=(c_𝔎/h(x))∫_{u≥ε}𝔎(x+u)ν(du)` and its `e^{by}`-weighted version are
`≤Ce^{bx}exp(−e^{x})` (each prime term `Λ(n)n^{−1/2}e^{b(x+log n)}𝔎(x+log n)≤Cn^{b}e^{bx}exp(−ne^{x})`, summed; the Γ-part likewise),
so the contribution is `o(g(x))`.
Collecting: `𝓛g(x)=−g(x)[½+O(𝔎(M))+O(ε_0(M′))+o(1)]+o(g(x))`. Since `𝔎(M)→0`, `ε_0(M′)→0` as `M,M′→∞`, the claim follows. ∎

### 5.2 Lemma 5.2 (`Γ` at the tail)
Let `f∈𝒜` satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma 5.1 (with exponent `b`). Then `Γ(f)(x)=¼f(x)²(1+o(1))` as `x→+∞`, and `Γ(f)`
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma 5.1 with exponent `2b`. In particular this holds for `f=r_1:=e_1=𝔎″/𝔎−¼` (`b=4`) and for
`Γ(r_1)` (`b=8`).

*Proof.* `Γ(f)(x)=½∫(f(y)−f(x))²J_x(dy)`. Run the five regions of Lemma 5.1 with `(f(y)−f(x))²` in place of `|g(y)−g(x)|`,
using `(f(y)−f(x))²≤2f(y)²+2f(x)²` in (ii), (iv), (v) (the weights `e^{2b|y|}` are absorbed exactly as `e^{b|y|}` was) and
`(f(y)−f(x))²=f(x)²(1+o(1))` uniformly in the bulk (iii): `Γ(f)(x)=½f(x)²[½+o(1)]=¼f(x)²(1+o(1))`. Hence
`C^{−1}e^{2bx}≤Γ(f)(x)≤Ce^{2bx}` for large `x`, and `|Γ(f)(y)|≤Ce^{2b|y|}` everywhere (by evenness of the construction for
`y→−∞`, or directly by the same bounds). For the derivative, differentiate under the integral in
`Γ(f)(x)=½(c_𝔎/h(x))∫[𝔎(x−u)(f(x)−f(x−u))²+𝔎(x+u)(f(x)−f(x+u))²]ν(du)`: the factors produced are `h′/h` (bounded),
`𝔎′(x∓u)=(𝔎′/𝔎)(x∓u)𝔎(x∓u)` with `|𝔎′/𝔎|(y)≤Ce^{2|y|}` (an extra exponential weight at the landing point, absorbed as
before), and `2(f(x)−f(x∓u))(f′(x)−f′(x∓u))` with `|f′|≤Ce^{b|·|}`; all contributions are `≤Ce^{2bx}`. For `r_1`: by §1.2,
`r_1(x)=4π²e^{4x}(1+O(e^{−2x}))`, `r_1′(x)=(𝔎‴/𝔎−(𝔎″/𝔎)(𝔎′/𝔎))(x)=(−8π³e^{6x}+8π³e^{6x})+O(e^{4x})=O(e^{4x})` (the leading
terms cancel: `r_1′=(log(𝔎″/𝔎))′·𝔎″/𝔎` and `(log 𝔎″/𝔎)′→4`), so `|r_1′|≤Ce^{4x}`; and `|r_1(y)|≤Ce^{4|y|}` by evenness. ∎

### 5.3 Theorem 4 (T4)
(i) `Γ₂(r_1)=½𝓛Γ(r_1)+½Γ(r_1)` pointwise, and `Γ₂(r_1)(x)=¼Γ(r_1)(x)(1+o(1))`, `(𝓛r_1(x))²=Γ(r_1)(x)(1+o(1))` as `x→+∞`.
(ii) `𝓛` does not satisfy `CD(½,∞)` on `𝒜`.
(iii) If `𝓛` satisfies `CD(ρ,N)` on `𝒜` for some `ρ∈ℝ`, `N∈[1,∞]`, then `ρ+N^{−1}≤¼`. In particular no `CD(ρ,N)` with
`ρN/(N−1)≥½` holds, and `CD(ρ,∞)` fails for every `ρ>¼`.

*Proof.* (i) By Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 1.1(ii), `−𝓛r_1=L_𝔎r_1=½r_1` in `L²(μ)`; both sides are continuous and `μ` has a positive
density, so `𝓛r_1=−½r_1` everywhere. Then `Γ(r_1,𝓛r_1)=−½Γ(r_1)` and (1.3) gives `Γ₂(r_1)=½𝓛Γ(r_1)+½Γ(r_1)`.
By Lemma 5.2, `Γ(r_1)∈𝒜` satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma 5.1, so `𝓛Γ(r_1)(x)=−½Γ(r_1)(x)(1+o(1))`, whence
`Γ₂(r_1)(x)=(−¼+½)Γ(r_1)(x)(1+o(1))=¼Γ(r_1)(x)(1+o(1))`. And `(𝓛r_1)²=¼r_1²=Γ(r_1)(1+o(1))` by Lemma 5.2.
(ii) Suppose `Γ₂(f)≥½Γ(f)` on `𝒜`. At `f=r_1`, (i) gives `𝓛Γ(r_1)≥0` pointwise. By Lemma 1.1(iii), `∫𝓛Γ(r_1)dμ=0`, so
`𝓛Γ(r_1)=0` μ-a.e., hence everywhere (continuity). Thus `L_𝔎Γ(r_1)=0` and `Γ(r_1)` is constant by Lemma 1.1(iv) — but
`Γ(r_1)(x)→∞` by Lemma 5.2. Contradiction.
(iii) Evaluate `CD(ρ,N)` at `f=r_1`, divide by `Γ(r_1)(x)>0` and let `x→+∞` using (i): `¼≥ρ+N^{−1}`. The Lichnerowicz
bound of `CD(ρ,N)` is `λ_1≥ρN/(N−1)`; with `ρ≤¼−N^{−1}` it is at most `(N−4)/(4(N−1))<¼<½`. ∎

**Remark 5.4 (what T4 says and does not say).** (a) The failure is located at the *tail* and is caused by the *pole*:
`d_∞=2c_𝔎²=2ξ(1)²=½`. On functions concentrated where the process only jumps into the bulk, `Γ(f)≈½d_∞f²`,
`(𝓛f)²≈d_∞²f²`, `Γ₂(f)≈¼d_∞²f²`, so `Γ₂/Γ→d_∞/2`: **pointwise curvature sees half of the essential-spectrum threshold**.
This is a general feature of jump generators with a positive killing rate at infinity, not of ζ; what is ζ's is the value
`d_∞=½=` the gap RH demands (Remark 4.2). (b) T4 does not exclude `CD(ρ,N)` with `ρ+N^{−1}≤¼`; whether any `CD(ρ,∞)`, `ρ>0`,
holds is OPEN (§7 O3), as is the meaning of `σ(L_𝔎)∩(0,ρ)=∅` for `ρ<½` on the zero side (§7 O2). (c) T4 is a theorem about
the method (Bakry–Émery pointwise curvature), independent of RH: it holds whatever the zeros do (I-8). (d) T4 is the
Doob-coordinate instance of Paper 41 P1: the pointwise density `δ_2=Γ₂−½Γ` vanishes identically on the equality set only
if `𝓛Γ(r)=0` there, which is impossible; every nonnegative decomposition of `𝔇` must therefore be nonlocal in `x`.

## §6 The gauge form of the sign (Proposition P1)

For `f∈𝒜` put `δ_1(f):=(𝓛f)²−½Γ(f)`, `δ_2(f):=Γ₂(f)−½Γ(f)`, `𝔇(f):=‖L_𝔎f‖²−½E_𝔎(f)`.

**Proposition 6.1.** (a) `∫δ_1(f)dμ=∫δ_2(f)dμ=𝔇(f)`. (b) Pointwise,
`δ_1(f)−δ_2(f)=½[(𝓛f)²−f𝓛²f]+½𝓛(f𝓛f−Γ(f))`, where the first bracket has zero `μ`-integral and the second is a divergence.
(c) On the ½-eigenspace (`𝓛r=−½r`): `δ_2(r)=½𝓛Γ(r)` and `δ_1(r)=−¼𝓛(r²)`; both are pure divergences, `𝔇(r)=0`.
(d) (Gauge equivalence; uses `σ_ess(L_𝔎)⊂[½,∞)`, Paper 41 `thm:ess`, CITED.) For `f∈𝒜`: `𝔇(f)≥0` if and only if there is
`G∈D(L_𝔎)` with `δ_1(f)−L_𝔎G≥0` μ-a.e. (i.e. `δ_1(f)+𝓛G≥0`). The same holds with `δ_2` in place of `δ_1`.

*Proof.* (a) is (1.4) and Lemma 1.1(iii). (b) `Γ(f,𝓛f)=½[𝓛(f𝓛f)−f𝓛²f−(𝓛f)²]`, so `Γ₂(f)=½𝓛Γ(f)−½𝓛(f𝓛f)+½f𝓛²f+½(𝓛f)²` and
`δ_1−δ_2=(𝓛f)²−Γ₂(f)` is as displayed; `∫[(𝓛f)²−f𝓛²f]dμ=‖𝓛f‖²−⟨f,𝓛²f⟩=0` by symmetry. (c) `Γ₂(r)=½𝓛Γ(r)+½Γ(r)` (proof of T4(i)),
so `δ_2(r)=½𝓛Γ(r)`; and `𝓛(r²)=2Γ(r)+2r𝓛r=2Γ(r)−r²`, so `−¼𝓛(r²)=¼r²−½Γ(r)=(𝓛r)²−½Γ(r)=δ_1(r)`. (d) (⟸) integrate:
`𝔇(f)−⟨L_𝔎G,1⟩=𝔇(f)−⟨G,L_𝔎1⟩=𝔇(f)≥0`. (⟹) `σ_ess⊂[½,∞)` and Lemma 1.1(iv) make `0` an isolated simple eigenvalue, so `L_𝔎`
is invertible on `1^⊥`. The function `δ_1(f)−𝔇(f)·1∈𝒜⊂L²(μ)` has zero mean; put `G:=L_𝔎^{−1}(δ_1(f)−𝔇(f)·1)∈D(L_𝔎)`. Then
`δ_1(f)−L_𝔎G=𝔇(f)·1≥0`. Any `φ≥0` with `∫φdμ=1` may replace `1`. ∎

**Remark 6.2 (reading of P1 against the corridor).** RH ⟺ for every `f` a gauge exists (Theorem 3 + (d)). The two
"natural" gauges `G=0` (for `δ_1`: fails at the zeros of `r_1`, where `(𝓛r_1)²=0<½Γ(r_1)`) and the Bakry–Émery gauge
(`δ_2≥0`, i.e. `CD(½,∞)`: fails at the tail, T4) are both local in `x`. Paper 41's corridor (§13: signed, nonlocal, prime
locations before norms) is, in this coordinate, exactly the demand that `G_f` be a genuinely nonlocal quadratic functional
of `f` built from the displacements `log n`: `𝓛G_f` is signed (a divergence), nonlocal (`G_f(x)` may involve `f(x∓log n)`,
`f(x∓log n∓log n′)`, …), and uses the prime locations before any norm. On the equality set the gauge is known exactly
(`G=−¼r²` for `δ_1`, `G=½Γ(r)` for `δ_2`, by (c)); this is the only constraint the equality set imposes, and it is the
Doob-coordinate content of Paper 41 P1.

**Remark 6.3 (two-displacement structure).** Writing `𝓛f(x)=∫D_uf(x)j_x(du)`, `D_uf(x):=f(x−u)−f(x)`, `j_x(du):=(c_𝔎/h(x))𝔎(x−u)ν̃(du)`,
```
𝔇(f)=∫dμ(x)[(∫D_uf(x)j_x(du))² − ¼∫(D_uf(x))²j_x(du)],
```
a quadratic form in the displacement field `u↦D_uf(x)` at each `x`, whose off-diagonal part is supported on pairs
`(u,u′)∈(±log n,±log n′)` — the prime×prime structure that route M8 (brief 6.2.11) studies as `Λ⋆Λ`. Cauchy–Schwarz on the
prime part alone gives `(∫D_uf j^P_x)²≤d_P(x)∫(D_uf)²j^P_x` with `d_P→½`, which is why the prime-only density is bounded
by the prime energy density and why the tail is the critical region. No positivity is derived from this.

## §7 Open steps (exact statements)

- **O1 (the construction; RH-strength by Proposition 6.1(d)).** Construct, for every real `f∈𝒜` (or `f∈C_c^∞`), a function
  `G_f∈D(L_𝔎)`, depending quadratically on `f` and defined from the data `(𝔎, h, ν)` through the prime locations `log n`
  before any norm is taken, such that `(𝓛f)(x)²−½Γ(f)(x)−(L_𝔎G_f)(x)≥0` for μ-a.e. `x`. Equivalent to RH; what is open is
  the construction. On `ker(L_𝔎−½)` the gauge must reduce to `−¼f²` (Prop. 6.1(c)). Under an off-line zero no such `G_f`
  exists for the corresponding subthreshold eigenfunction (I-8 is respected).
- **O2 (the dictionary).** For a zero `ρ_0=½+α+iγ`, `α>0`, determine the eigenvalues of `L_𝔎` in `(0,½)` produced by the quadruple
  `{ρ_0,ρ̄_0,1−ρ_0,1−ρ̄_0}` (Paper 41 mentions the factor `(2α)^{−1/2}`, Entry C2, and `α<½`, Entry F4, without a formula).
  Consequently: what zero-free statement is `σ(L_𝔎)∩(0,ρ)=∅` for a given `ρ∈(0,½)`? (By U-7 it is presumably a strip
  `|Re ρ−½|≤g(ρ)` with `g(½)=0`; not proved here.)
- **O3 (curvature below the cap).** Does `𝓛` satisfy `CD(ρ,∞)` on `𝒜` for some `ρ∈(0,¼]`? T4 leaves this open; by the
  Bakry–Émery argument it would give `σ(L_𝔎)∩(0,ρ)=∅` and, via O2, a zero-free strip. The tail is not the obstruction
  for `ρ≤¼`; the bulk (`|x|=O(1)`, where `r_1` changes sign and `𝔎″/𝔎` is not monotone) has not been analysed.
- **O4 (windows).** Transport Theorem 2 to the window bottoms `λ^W_T`: by Remark 2.4 the `μ`-normalization and the
  `L²(I_T,dt)`-normalization differ by the factor `𝔎²/(h𝔎)=𝔎/h`, unbounded above and below on `I_T` as `T→∞`. A window
  statement in Doob coordinates is `inf{E_𝔎(r)−½Var_μ(r)−½⟨τ,r⟩²: supp r⊂I_T, ∫𝔎²r²dt=1}≥0`; its relation to
  `λ^W_T` and to `lem:monoT`/`lem:contT` is not worked out.
- **O5 ((EF) on `𝒯`).** Write out the extension of Paper 45 Thm 9.4 from `C_c^∞` to `𝒯` (dominated convergence on the
  prime and Lévy sides of (1.1) under truncation `F_n=Fχ_n`, and uniform absolute convergence of the zero sums), so that
  (R) and Theorem 1 are PROVED in the repository rather than CITED.
- **O6 (spectral synthesis).** Is `ker(L_𝔎−½)` exactly the closure of the two families of Lemma 3.3? (Paper 41 `thm:heat`
  addresses the even case "weighted spectral synthesis"; the odd family is new.)
- **O7 (operator core).** Is `𝒜` (or `C_c^∞(ℝ)`) an operator core for `L_𝔎`, i.e. is `L_𝔎|_𝒜` essentially self-adjoint?
  `C_c^∞` is a *form* core (CITED); the operator-core statement is what makes the pointwise Γ₂ criterion (g) of Theorem 3
  equivalent to (f), and is also what a Bakry–Émery-type argument on `𝒜` needs to conclude anything about `σ(L_𝔎)`.
  A sufficient condition is `P_t𝒜⊂𝒜` for the semigroup `P_t=e^{−tL_𝔎}` (an invariant dense subspace of `D(L_𝔎)` is a core).
