## §1 Definitions

**1.1 Meyer's spaces (CITED).** `𝓗_-=∩_{σ∈R}𝒮(R_+^×)x^{−σ}`, and `f̂(s)=∫_0^∞f(x)x^s d^×x`. By Thm Lap_range, `f↦f̂`
identifies `𝓗_-` with

 `𝒪 := {h entire : t↦h(σ+it) lies in 𝒮(R) for every σ∈R}`,

and the closed subspace `Z𝓗_∩` (Thm Zeta_estimate) with

 `I := {h∈𝒪 : h/ζ ∈ 𝒮 on each line Re s=σ≥½,  h(s)/ζ(1−s) ∈ 𝒮 on each line Re s=σ≤½}`.

Meyer's `𝓗⁰_-=𝓗_-/Z𝓗_∩` (l.625) is thus `H¹:=𝒪/I`. Its generator `Θ:=D_-` is multiplication by `s` (proof of Cor
Dminus_eigenvalues, l.723). `ρ_-(λ)` is multiplication by `λ^s` (R158_004 A10), and `𝓗⁰_+≅C(x⁰)⊕C(x¹)`.

**Lemma 1.1 (PROVED).** The Fréchet topology of `𝒪` is given by the seminorms `q_K(h):=sup_{abs(Re s)≤K}(1+|s|)^K|h(s)|`,
`K∈N`. The following are continuous on `𝒪`:
* multiplication by `s`, by `λ^s` (`λ>0`) and by `e^{(s−c)²}` (`c∈C`);
* the maps `h↦h(1−s)` and `h↦h^*(s):=\overline{h(\bar s)}`.

*Proof.* `s^k f̂=(−1)^k(D^kf)^` with `D=x d/dx`. So for `|Re s|≤K`, `(1+|s|)^K|f̂(s)|≤2^KΣ_{k≤K}∫|D^kf|(x^K+x^{−K})d^×x`,
which is a continuous seminorm on `𝓗_-`. Conversely, the topology of `𝓗_-` is given by the Schwartz seminorms of
`τ↦f̂(σ+iτ)`, `σ∈Z` (Fourier isomorphism, l.670). Cauchy's estimate on unit discs bounds these by `q_K` for
`K≥|σ|+1+N`. For the multipliers, `|λ^s|=λ^σ`, `|e^{(s−c)²}|≤e^{(K+|c|)²}` on `|Re s|≤K`, and reflection maps the strip
`|Re s|≤K` into `|Re s|≤K+1`. ∎

**1.2 The divisorial ideal, jets, Jordan blocks.** Put `J:={h∈𝒪: ord_ρh≥m_ρ for every ρ∈𝒵}`. It is closed, being an
intersection of kernels of `h↦h^{(j)}(ρ)`, and `I⊂J` (Meyer's parenthetical after Lap_range, together with
`𝒵=1−𝒵`). On `𝒪/J`, the jets `(h^{(j)}(ρ))_{j<m_ρ}` and the value `x(ρ):=h(ρ)` are well defined. Put
`E_ρ:=span{[ξ/(s−ρ)^k]:1≤k≤m_ρ}` and `H¹_alg:=⊕_ρE_ρ`.

By R158_002 Fact 2.4a (its proof works in `𝒪/J` and in `𝒪/I`), `E_ρ` is a single Jordan block of `Θ`, and the jet map
`E_ρ→C^{m_ρ}` at `ρ` is bijective (triangular). Put `N_ρ:=Θ−ρ`, `v_ρ:=[ξ/(s−ρ)^{m_ρ}]`; then
`N_ρ^jv_ρ=[ξ/(s−ρ)^{m_ρ−j}]`. For `h∈𝒪`, `P_ρ[h]∈E_ρ` denotes the unique element with the jet of `h` at `ρ`. Only the
generator `v_ρ` is nonzero at `ρ`, so `x(ρ)=κ_ρc_ρ(x)`, where `c_ρ(x)` is the `v_ρ`-coordinate of `P_ρx` and
`κ_ρ:=ξ^{(m_ρ)}(ρ)/m_ρ!≠0`.

**1.3 Compatible forms.** A sesquilinear form `𝔥` (linear in the first slot) on a `Θ`-stable subspace is *compatible*
if `𝔥(Θx,y)+𝔥(x,Θy)=𝔥(x,y)`. For a continuous `𝔥` on `H¹` this is equivalent to `𝔥(ρ_-(λ)x,ρ_-(λ)y)=λ𝔥(x,y)`: in one
direction differentiate at `λ=1`; in the other, `d/du[e^{−u}𝔥(ρ_-(e^u)x,ρ_-(e^u)y)]=0`. Equivalently, `λ^{−1/2}ρ_-(λ)` is
`𝔥`-isometric; this is R158_004's "Θ-compatible hermitian form" and R158_001's P-HS conformality. Define
`𝒫(H¹):={𝔥 continuous, hermitian, compatible, 𝔥≥0}`.

**1.4 Good heights, bands.** By Titchmarsh 9.7 there are `A₀,C₀` and, for each `n≥2`, a `T_n∈(n,n+1)` with
`|ζ(σ±iT_n)|^{−1}≤C₀T_n^{A₀}` for `−1≤σ≤2` (the minus sign follows from `ζ(\bar s)=\overline{ζ(s)}`). Put
`B_1:={abs(Im z)<T_2}` and `B_n:={T_n<abs(Im z)<T_{n+1}}` for `n≥2`, and `B_n^±:=B_n∩{±Im z>0}`. No zero lies on the
lines `Im z=±T_n`.

**Lemma 1.2 (PROVED from the CITED inputs).** For `L≥2` there are `C_L,A_L` such that:
* (a) `|ξ(s)|≤C_Le^{−π|t|/4}(1+|t|)^{A_L}` for `|σ−½|≤L`;
* (b) `|ξ(x±iT_n)|≥C_L^{−1}e^{−πT_n/4}T_n^{−A_L}` for `|x−½|≤L`;
* (c) `|ξ(x+iy)|≥C_L^{−1}e^{−π|y|/4}(1+|y|)^{−A_L}` for `3/2≤|x−½|≤L` and all `y`.

*Proof.* Stirling gives `|π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)|≍_L|t|^{(σ−1)/2}e^{−π|t|/4}` for `|t|≥1`, and convexity gives
`|ζ(s)|≪_L(1+|t|)^{L+1}`. For `σ≥2`, the Euler product gives `|ζ(s)|≥∏_p(1+p^{−σ})^{−1}=ζ(2σ)/ζ(σ)≥1/ζ(2)`; use
`ξ(s)=ξ(1−s)` for `σ≤−1`; and Titchmarsh 9.7 covers `−1≤σ≤2` at `t=±T_n`. ∎

## §2 Theorem A — the Poisson ideal is divisorial

**Lemma M (PROVED; an elementary Ehrenpreis–Malgrange division lemma).** Suppose `D(s,r)⊂D(z₀,R)`, `G` is holomorphic
near `\bar D(z₀,R)` with `G(z₀)≠0`, `H` is holomorphic near `\bar D(s,r)`, and `F:=HG`. Put `M_F:=max_{\bar D(s,r)}|F|`
and `M_G:=max_{\bar D(z₀,R)}|G|`. Then `|H(s)|≤(M_F/M_G)(M_G/|G(z₀)|)^{(R/r)²}`.

*Proof.*
* `log|H|` is subharmonic, so `log|H(s)|≤⨍_{D(s,r)}log|H| = ⨍_{D(s,r)}log|F| − ⨍_{D(s,r)}log|G|`.
* The first average is `≤log M_F`.
* Let `u:=log M_G−log|G|`. It is `≥0` and superharmonic on `D(z₀,R)`. Since `u≥0` and `D(s,r)⊂D(z₀,R)`,
  `∫_{D(s,r)}u ≤ ∫_{D(z₀,R)}u ≤ πR²u(z₀)`.
* So `−⨍_{D(s,r)}log|G| ≤ −log M_G+(R/r)²log(M_G/|G(z₀)|)`. ∎

**Theorem A (PROVED).** `I=J`. Hence Meyer's `H¹` is `𝒪/J`, which is R158_002's `H¹_red`.

*Proof.* Let `h∈J`. We show `h/ζ∈𝒮` on every line `Re s=σ≥½`.
* **Pointwise bound.** Let `w` satisfy `0≤Re w≤5/2` and `|Im w|≥9`. Apply Lemma M with:
  * `z₀:=3+i Im w`, `r:=3−Re w∈[½,3]`, `R:=2r`;
  * `G(z):=(z−1)ζ(z)` (entire), `H:=h/ζ`, `F=(z−1)h`.

  The disc `\bar D(w,r)` lies in `{Re z≥−3, |Im z|≥6}`. It contains no trivial zero and not the pole, and `h` vanishes
  to order `m_ρ` at every non-trivial zero, so `H` is holomorphic near `\bar D(w,r)`. The bounds are:
  * convexity on `−3≤Re z≤9` gives `M_G≪(1+|Im w|)^{9/2}`;
  * `|G(z₀)|≥(ζ(6)/ζ(3))|Im w|`;
  * `M_F≪q_N(h)(1+|Im w|)^{1−N}` for `N≥9`.

  Lemma M, with exponent `(R/r)²=4`, gives `|h/ζ(w)|≪q_N(h)(1+|Im w|)^{21/2−N}`.
* **Derivatives.** For `σ∈[½,2]` and `|t|≥10`, Cauchy's estimate on the circle `|w−(σ+it)|=½` (where `Re w∈[0,5/2]` and
  `|Im w|≥9`) bounds every `∂_t^k(h/ζ)`.
* **Small heights.** For `|t|≤10`, `h/ζ` is holomorphic near the compact rectangle `[0,5/2]×[−10,10]`: ζ has no zero
  there, and its pole at 1 is a zero of `1/ζ`.
* **Large σ.** For `σ≥2`, `1/ζ=Σμ(n)n^{−s}` has bounded derivatives.

Hence `h/ζ∈𝒮` on each line `σ≥½`. Next, `h̃(s):=h(1−s)` lies in `J`, because `𝒵=1−𝒵` with multiplicities. So
`h(s)/ζ(1−s)=(h̃/ζ)(1−s)∈𝒮` on each line `σ≤½`. Thus `h∈I`. ∎

## §3 Theorem B — the absolute eigen-expansion

For `h∈𝒪`, `s∈C` and `ρ∈𝒵`, define:
* `F_{h,s}(z):=h(z)e^{(z−s)²}/(ξ(z)(z−s))`;
* `R_ρ[h](s):=−ξ(s)Res_{z=ρ}F_{h,s}(z)`;
* `G_n^*[h]:=Σ_{ρ∈B_n^*}R_ρ[h]` for `*∈{∅,+,−}` (finite sums).

**Lemma B1 (PROVED).** `R_ρ[h]∈𝒪`, and it depends only on the jet of `h` at `ρ`. The difference `h−R_ρ[h]` vanishes to
order `m_ρ` at `ρ`, and `R_ρ[h]` vanishes to order `m_σ` at every `σ≠ρ`. Hence `[R_ρ[h]]=P_ρ[h]` in `𝒪/J`, and
`R_ρ[h]=0` for `h∈J`.

*Proof.* Let `m=m_ρ` and `q(z):=(z−ρ)^m/ξ(z)`, which is holomorphic and nonzero near `ρ`.
* **Structure of the residue.** Leibniz gives `Res_{z=ρ}F_{h,s}=Σ_{j<m}a_j(h)∂_z^j[e^{(z−s)²}(z−s)^{−1}]_{z=ρ}`, where
  the `a_j(h)` are linear in the jet `(h^{(i)}(ρ))_{i<m}`. Moreover
  `∂_z^j[e^{(z−s)²}(z−s)^{−1}]_{z=ρ}=e^{(ρ−s)²}Π_j(ρ−s)(ρ−s)^{−j−1}` with `Π_j` a polynomial.
* **Membership in 𝒪.** So `R_ρ[h]` is a finite combination of `Π_j(ρ−s)e^{(ρ−s)²}·ξ(s)(s−ρ)^{−j−1}` with `j+1≤m`. Each
  term is entire and lies in `𝒪` by Lemma 1.1 (`ξ∈𝒪` by Fact 2.4a). It vanishes to order `m_σ` at every `σ≠ρ`
  because of the factor `ξ`.
* **Jet at ρ.** Let `C` be a small circle around `ρ`, and `s` inside it. Then
  `(1/2πi)∮_CF_{h,s}=h(s)/ξ(s)+Res_ρF_{h,s}`, so `h(s)−R_ρ[h](s)=ξ(s)·(1/2πi)∮_CF_{h,s}(z)dz`. The integral is
  holomorphic in `s` inside `C`, so `h−R_ρ[h]` vanishes to order `m` at `ρ`.
* **Conclusion.** The last claims follow from the bijectivity of the jet map on `E_ρ`. ∎

**Lemma B0 (PROVED).** For `L≥1` there is `c_L` with the following property. For every `T∈R`, every segment
`Σ=[½−L,½+L]+iT` and every `s₀∈C`, `(1/2π)∫_0^{2π}∫_Σ|dz|/|z−s₀−e^{iθ}|dθ≤c_L`.

*Proof.* Let `d(θ):=|c−sin θ|` with `c=T−Im s₀`.
* The inner integral is `≤∫_{−L}^{L}du/(u²+d²)^{1/2}=2arsinh(L/d)≤2log(1+2L/d)`.
* `arcsin` is Hölder-½ on `[−1,1]`. So on each of the two monotone branches of `sin`, the preimage of `(c−δ,c+δ)` has
  length `≤C_*√δ`.
* The layer-cake formula gives
  `∫_0^{2π}log(1+2L/d)dθ = ∫_0^∞|{d<2L/(e^λ−1)}|dλ ≤ ∫_0^∞min(2π,2C_*(2L/(e^λ−1))^{1/2})dλ < ∞`. ∎

**Lemma B2 (band estimate; PROVED).** For `K,M∈N` there are `C,K′` such that `q_K(G_n^*[h])≤CT_n^{−M}q_{K′}(h)` for all
`n≥2`, `h∈𝒪` and `*∈{∅,±}`.

*Proof.* Put `L:=K+6`, and let `Q:=B_n^*∩{|Re z−½|<L}` (one or two rectangles).
* **Residue formula.** For `w∉∂Q` with `|Re w|≤K+1`, the residue theorem gives
  `G_n^*[h](w) = −ξ(w)(1/2πi)∮_{∂Q}F_{h,w}(z)dz + 1_Q(w)h(w)`. (3.1)
  The poles in `Q` are the zeros and, if `w∈Q`, the point `z=w` with residue `h(w)/ξ(w)`. The formula holds first for
  `w∉𝒵`, then by continuity.
* **Horizontal edges.** Take an edge `z=x+iT` with `T∈{±T_n,±T_{n+1}}`, and write `A=A_L`. Lemma 1.2(a, b) gives
  `|ξ(w)F_{h,w}(z)| ≤ C|h(z)|e^{(x−Re w)²}e^{π(|T|−|Im w|)/4−(T−Im w)²}T^A(1+|Im w|)^A/|z−w|`.
  Three facts bound this:
  * `π(|T|−|Im w|)/4−(T−Im w)² ≤ 1−(T−Im w)²/2`;
  * `1+|Im w| ≤ (1+|T|)(1+|T−Im w|)`;
  * `|h(z)| ≤ q_{K′}(h)(1+|T|)^{−K′}`.

  So the edge contributes at most `C_1q_{K′}(h)T_n^{2A+K−K′}∫_{edge}|dz|/|z−w|` to `(1+|w|)^K|G(w)|`.
* **Vertical edges.** On an edge `z=½±L+iy` with `|y|∈[T_n,T_{n+1}]`, we have `|z−w|≥4`, and Lemma 1.2(c) gives the same
  bound with the factor `e^{−(y−Im w)²/2}`. Its integral in `y` is `O(q_{K′}(h)T_n^{2A+K−K′})`.
* **Jump term.** `1_Q(w)≠0` forces `|Im w|≥T_n`. So `(1+|w|)^K|h(w)|≤q_{K′}(h)T_n^{K−K′}`.
* **Conclusion.** `G:=G_n^*[h]` is entire, so `|G(s₀)|≤(1/2π)∫_0^{2π}|G(s₀+e^{iθ})|dθ`. Lemma B0 bounds the average of the
  horizontal-edge integrals. Take `K′:=M+2A+K+L`. ∎

**Lemma B3 (reconstruction; PROVED).** For `h∈𝒪`, `S[h]:=Σ_{n≥1}G_n[h]` converges absolutely in `𝒪`, and `S` is
continuous. Moreover `V[h]:=h−S[h]∈J`. Explicitly, for `|Re s−½|<L`:

 `V[h](s)=ξ(s)(1/2πi)[∫_{½+L−i∞}^{½+L+i∞}−∫_{½−L−i∞}^{½−L+i∞}]F_{h,s}(z)dz`.

*Proof.*
* **Convergence.** Absolute convergence and continuity follow from Lemma B2.
* **The limit.** Summing (3.1) over `n≤N`, the interior horizontal edges cancel. Let `R_N:={|Re z−½|<L,|Im z|<T_{N+1}}`.
  Then `Σ_{n≤N}G_n[h](s)=−ξ(s)(1/2πi)∮_{∂R_N}F_{h,s}+1_{R_N}(s)h(s)`. Let `N→∞` with `s` fixed:
  * the horizontal edges tend to 0, by the bound of B2;
  * the vertical edges tend to the absolutely convergent line integrals (Gaussian factor).
* **Membership in J.** `V[h]=h−S[h]` lies in `𝒪`. On `|Re s−½|<L` it is `ξ(s)` times a function holomorphic there, and
  every zero satisfies `|Re ρ−½|<½`. Hence `ord_ρV[h]≥m_ρ` for all `ρ`, i.e. `V[h]∈J`. ∎

**Theorem B (PROVED).**
* (i) For every `x=[h]∈H¹`, `x=Σ_{n≥1}Σ_{ρ∈B_n}P_ρx`. The outer series converges absolutely for every quotient seminorm.
* (ii) `H¹_alg` is dense in Meyer's `H¹`.
* (iii) `x=0` iff `P_ρx=0` for every `ρ`.
* (iv) `S` is a continuous projection of `𝒪` with kernel `J`. So `𝒪=J⊕S(𝒪)` topologically, and `H¹≅S(𝒪)` as Fréchet
  spaces.

*Proof.* (i) By B1, `[G_n[h]]=Σ_{ρ∈B_n}P_ρ[h]`. By B3, `[h]=Σ_n[G_n[h]]` with `Σ_n\bar q_K([G_n[h]])≤Σ_nq_K(G_n[h])<∞`.
(ii) and (iii) follow from (i) and the definition of `J`. (iv) `J⊂ker S` by B1. If `S[h]=0` then `h=V[h]∈J`.
`S²=S` because `S[h]−h=−V[h]∈J`. The quotient map `S(𝒪)=ker(1−S)→𝒪/J` is a topological isomorphism. Theorem A gives
`𝒪/J=H¹`. ∎

*Remark B.1.* The grouping by bands is essential. Individual `P_ρ` are not claimed to be summable, since that would
need bounds for `1/ζ′(ρ)`. The only input on ζ beyond the definitions is the polynomial lower bound on Titchmarsh's
horizontal segments. This closes brief 6.1.13 and R158_002 doubt 2 (`H¹_red=H¹`, and density), unconditionally.

## §4 Theorem C — zero-free Riesz–Hodge projections

**Theorem C (PROVED).**
* (i) `P_±:=Σ_nG_n^±` converge absolutely, are continuous on `𝒪`, and vanish on `J`. On `H¹` they are complementary
  projections commuting with `Θ`, and `P_+H¹` is the closure of `⊕_{γ>0}E_ρ`.
* (ii) Zero-free formula. Let `U_L:={|Re z−½|<L, Im z>0}`, with `∂U_L=[½−L,½+L]∪(½±L+i[0,∞))` positively oriented.
  For `|Re s−½|<L`: `P_+[h](s)=−ξ(s)(1/2πi)∫_{∂U_L}F_{h,s}(z)dz+1_{U_L}(s)h(s)`. The contour meets no zero.
* (iii) For `z∉𝒵`: `(z−Θ)^{−1}[h]=[(h(s)−h(z)ξ(s)e^{(s−z)²}/ξ(z))/(z−s)]`. Moreover
  `P_+=lim(1/2πi)∫_{∂(U_L∩{Im z<T_N})}(z−Θ)^{−1}dz` is the Riesz projection.
* (iv) `⋆:=i(P_+−P_−)` is a continuous complex structure on `H¹`. It commutes with `Θ` and with `x↦x^*=[h^*]`.

*Proof.*
* (i) Lemma B2 with `*=±` gives convergence and continuity. The projections vanish on `J` by B1, and
  `P_++P_−=S≡id mod J`. In `H¹`, `P_±x` carries the jets of `x` at the zeros in `∪_nB_n^±` and zero jets elsewhere. By
  Theorem B(iii) this characterizes `P_±x`, which gives the projection identities and `P_±Θ=ΘP_±`.
* (ii) Sum (3.1) over the upper rectangles. The bottom edge is `[½−L,½+L]⊂R`, and the top edge is `o(1)` along good
  heights. The contour meets no zero because `ξ>0` on `R`:
  * for `x>1`, every factor of `ξ(x)` is positive;
  * for `0<x<1`, `x(x−1)<0` and `ζ(x)=(1−2^{1−x})^{−1}Σ(−1)^{n−1}n^{−x}<0`;
  * `ξ(0)=ξ(1)=½`;
  * for `x<0`, use `ξ(x)=ξ(1−x)`.
* (iii) Put `g_z:=(h(s)−h(z)ξ(s)e^{(s−z)²}/ξ(z))/(z−s)`. It is entire and lies in `𝒪`, and `(z−s)g_z≡h mod J` because
  `ξe^{(s−z)²}∈J`. If `(z−s)g∈J` then `g∈J`. So `z−Θ` is bijective with the stated inverse. Integrate the inverse over
  the truncated contour. The term `h(s)(1/2πi)∫dz/(z−s)` tends to `1_{U_L}(s)h(s)`, because the top edge has fixed
  length. Compare with (ii).
* (iv) `x↦x^*` maps `E_ρ` onto `E_{\bar ρ}`, so `P_+(x^*)=(P_−x)^*`, and antilinearity gives `⋆(x^*)=(⋆x)^*`. ∎

*Remark C.1 (correction of record, R158_004 row 5 / A15).* `⋆` is defined on the topological `H¹`, and its contour runs
through the real axis, where `ξ>0`. OT-3 passes for `⋆`; its content is the spectral splitting, as for any Riesz

## §5 Theorems D and E — compatible forms and the positive cone

**Lemma 5.1 (PROVED).** Let `N` be nilpotent on a finite-dimensional `E` with a cyclic vector `v`, and let `b≥0` be
hermitian with `b(Nx,y)+b(x,Ny)=0`. Then `NE⊂rad b` and `b(x,y)=b(v,v)c(x)\overline{c(y)}`, where `c` is the
`v`-coordinate.

*Proof.* `rad b` is `N`-stable, since `b(Nx,y)=−b(x,Ny)`. We show `N^jv∈rad b` for `j≥1` by descending induction; the
start is `N^mv=0`. If `N^{j+1}v∈rad b` with `j≥1`, then `b(N^jv,N^jv)=−b(N^{j−1}v,N^{j+1}v)=0`, so `N^jv∈rad b` because
`b≥0`. ∎

**Theorem D (PROVED).** Let `𝔥` be a separately continuous compatible sesquilinear form on `H¹`.
* (i) `𝔥(E_ρ,E_σ)=0` unless `σ=1−\bar ρ`.
* (ii) `𝔥(x,y)=Σ_nΣ_{ρ∈B_n}𝔥(P_ρx,P_{1−\bar ρ}y)`, absolutely convergent in `n`.
* (iii) If `𝔥` is hermitian and `≥0`, then `E_ρ⊂rad 𝔥` for `Re ρ≠½`, and `N_ρE_ρ⊂rad 𝔥` for `Re ρ=½`.

*Proof.* `H¹` is Fréchet, so Banach–Steinhaus makes `𝔥` jointly continuous: `|𝔥(x,y)|≤C\bar q_K(x)\bar q_K(y)`.
* (i) On forms `b` on `E_ρ×E_σ`, define `(Lb)(x,y):=b(N_ρx,y)+b(x,N_σy)`. `L` is nilpotent. Compatibility reads
  `L𝔥=(1−ρ−\bar σ)𝔥`, so `𝔥|=0` unless `ρ+\bar σ=1`.
* (ii) Expand `x=Σx_n` and `y=Σy_m` by Theorem B(i), and use joint continuity. Only `m=n` and `σ=1−\bar ρ` survive,
  because `Im(1−\bar ρ)=Im ρ`.
* (iii) For `Re ρ≠½`, (i) with `σ=ρ` gives `𝔥|_{E_ρ×E_ρ}=0`. For `Re ρ=½`, apply Lemma 5.1. Cauchy–Schwarz finishes both
  cases. ∎

**Theorem E (the positive compatible cone; PROVED).** Put `𝔢_ρ(x,y):=x(ρ)\overline{y(ρ)}`.
* (i) Every `𝔥∈𝒫(H¹)` has the form `𝔥=Σ_nΣ_{ρ∈B_n, Re ρ=½}w_ρ𝔢_ρ` with `w_ρ≥0`, convergent at every `(x,y)`.
* (ii) Conversely, weights with `Σw_ρ(1+|γ|)^{−2K}<∞` for some `K` define an element of `𝒫`.
* (iii) The extreme rays of `𝒫` are exactly `R_{≥0}𝔢_ρ`, `ρ` a distinct critical zero.
* (iv) `∩_{𝔥∈𝒫}rad 𝔥={x: x(ρ)=0 for all critical ρ}`. It contains every off-line `E_ρ` and every `N_ρE_ρ`.

The cone is defined without the zeros. Its rays **are** the critical zeros, and it cannot see an off-line zero.

*Proof.*
* (i) Theorem D(ii) decomposes `𝔥` into blocks. The off-line blocks vanish by D(iii). On a critical `E_ρ`, Lemma 5.1
  gives `𝔥=w′_ρc_ρ\bar c_ρ` with `w′_ρ≥0`. Since `c_ρ(x)=x(ρ)/κ_ρ`, take `w_ρ=w′_ρ/|κ_ρ|²`.
* (ii) `|x(ρ)|≤\bar q_K(x)(1+|γ|)^{−K}`, so the sum converges. Compatibility holds because `ρ+\bar ρ=1` on the line.
* (iii) An element with two nonzero weights decomposes. Conversely, `𝔥≤𝔢_ρ` forces `w_σ=0` for `σ≠ρ`.
* (iv) The `𝔢_ρ` lie in `𝒫`; then apply D(iii). ∎

**Corollaries.**
* **E.1.** The critical zeros are recovered, without zero data, as the extreme rays of `𝒫`. PROVED.
* **E.2.** RH ⟺ `Spec(Θ|H¹)` equals the set of eigenvalues of the rays of `𝒫` (`𝔢_ρ∘(Θ×1)=ρ𝔢_ρ`). RH∧SZ ⟺ `𝒫`
  separates the points of `H¹`, by (iv) and Theorem B(iii). EQUIV.
* **E.3 (the count).** Fix a good height `T`. Put `H¹_{(0,T]}:=P_{(0,T]}H¹`, where `P_{(0,T]}:=Σ_{B_n^+⊂(0,T)}G_n^+` is a
  zero-free rectangle contour. It is finite-dimensional, of dimension `N(T)` (argument principle). Let `Θ_nil` be the
  nilpotent Jordan–Chevalley part of `Θ` on it. Then RH ⟺ for every good `T`, the rank of `𝒫` on `H¹_{(0,T]}` equals
  `dim(H¹_{(0,T]}/Θ_nilH¹_{(0,T]})`. EQUIV. This is "the count, never the positivity" (N-a) in exact form.
* **E.4.** The rank of `𝒫` on `H¹_{(0,T]}` is the number of distinct critical zeros up to `T`. So it is infinite
  (Hardy) and exceeds `(2/5)N(T)` for large `T` (Conrey 1989, simple critical zeros). CITED, not re-verified. It is
  **not** a route: gate I-14 applies to any proportion argument.

## §6 The Weil form, the critical form, the off-line defect

**Lemma 6.1 (the Weil form on `H¹`; PROVED).** For `a,b∈𝒪` put
`𝔟(a,b):=(1/2πi)[∫_{2−i∞}^{2+i∞}−∫_{−1−i∞}^{−1+i∞}](ξ′/ξ)(s)a(s)b^*(1−s)ds`.
* (i) `𝔟` is continuous on `𝒪×𝒪` and vanishes on `J×𝒪` and on `𝒪×J`.
* (ii) On `H¹`, `𝔟` is hermitian and compatible, and `𝔟(x,y)=Σ_nΣ_{ρ∈B_n}m_ρx(ρ)\overline{y(1−\bar ρ)}`.
* (iii) Let `F∈C_c^∞(I_T)` be real and `g(x):=x^{−1/2}F(log x)`, so that `ĝ=Φ_F`. Then `𝔟([ĝ],[ĝ])=W_T(F)` [compl]. For
  `F∈P_T` it equals `𝔞_T(F)` [prim].
* (iv) `𝔟` is R158_002's `B_{ξ′}(·,\bar·)`, i.e. CCM's trace pairing (R158_004 A12, A16). CITED.

*Proof.*
* (i), continuity: `|ξ′/ξ(2+it)|≪log(2+|t|)`, and the same on `Re s=−1` by the functional equation.
* (i), vanishing on `J`. If `a∈J`, the integrand is holomorphic on `−1≤Re s≤2`. It tends to 0 on horizontal segments,
  uniformly in height:
  * for `Re s≥½`, `(ζ′/ζ)a=ζ′·(a/ζ)` with `a/ζ` Schwartz, uniformly in `σ` (proof of Theorem A), and `ζ′` polynomially
    bounded;
  * for `Re s≤½`, `ξ′/ξ(s)=−ξ′/ξ(1−s)` and `a(s)/ζ(1−s)` is Schwartz;
  * `Γ′/Γ` terms are logarithmic.

  So the two line integrals agree. For `b∈J`, substitute `s↦1−s`.
* (ii) Compatibility: `(sb)^*(1−s)=(1−s)b^*(1−s)`, so the multiplier becomes `s+1−s=1`. Theorem D(ii) reduces `𝔟` to
  blocks. For `e∈E_ρ` and `e′∈E_σ`, the integrand `(ξ′/ξ)e(s)e′^*(1−s)` has at most a pole at `ρ`, and only when
  `σ=1−\bar ρ`, with residue `m_ρe(ρ)\overline{e′(1−\bar ρ)}`. Its horizontal contributions vanish, because
  `(ξ′/ξ)e=ξ′×`(rational). The hermitian symmetry follows from the reindexing `ρ↦1−\bar ρ`.
* (iii) For real `F`, `\overline{Φ_F(\bar z)}=Φ_F(z)`, so `𝔟([ĝ],[ĝ])=Σ_ρm_ρΦ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`, which is `W_T(F)` by P47
  eq:W. The sum is absolutely convergent, so grouping is harmless. On `P_T`, `W_T=𝔞_T`. ∎

**Theorem G (the critical form; PROVED).** For `x,y∈𝒪` and `0<ε≤½` put
`𝔥_ε(x,y):=(1/π)∫_Rx(½+iτ)\overline{y(½+iτ)}·Re(ξ′/ξ)(½+ε+iτ)dτ`.
* (i) `|𝔥_ε(x,y)|≤Cq_4(x)q_4(y)`, uniformly in `ε`.
* (ii) `𝔥_crit(x,y):=lim_{ε↓0}𝔥_ε(x,y)` exists and equals `Σ_{Re ρ=½}m_ρx(ρ)\overline{y(ρ)}`.
* (iii) `𝔥_crit` vanishes on `J×𝒪`, descends to `H¹`, and lies in `𝒫(H¹)` with weight `m_ρ>0` at **every** critical
  zero.
* (iv) Its definition uses only `ξ` to the right of the critical line, and (ii)–(iii) hold whatever the position of the
  zeros.

*Proof.* For `s∉𝒵`, Hadamard gives `Re(ξ′/ξ)(s)=Σ_ρm_ρη_ρ/(η_ρ²+(τ−γ)²)` with `s=½+ε+iτ` and `η_ρ:=½+ε−β∈(−½,1]`.
* (i) Each term has `L¹(dτ)`-mass at most `π`, and `≤2/(1+dist(γ,[k,k+1]))²` on `[k,k+1]`. With
  `N(T+1)−N(T)=O(log T)`, `∫_k^{k+1}|Re ξ′/ξ|dτ≤Clog(|k|+2)`. Since `|x(½+iτ)|≤q_4(x)(1+|τ|)^{−4}`, (i) follows.
* (ii) Put `φ(τ):=x(½+iτ)\overline{y(½+iτ)}`. Each term satisfies
  `|(1/π)∫φ(τ)η/(η²+(τ−γ)²)dτ|≤C_φ(1+|γ|)^{−2}` with `C_φ≪q_4(x)q_4(y)`. This is summable over `ρ`, so dominated
  convergence applies termwise:
  * a critical term (`η=ε`) is a Poisson kernel and tends to `φ(γ)`;
  * an off-line pair `ρ=β+iγ`, `1−\bar ρ=1−β+iγ` (same `m`) gives `η=ε+½−β` and `η′=ε+β−½`. As `ε→0` its two terms tend
    to `±(1/π)∫φ(½−β)/((½−β)²+(τ−γ)²)dτ` and cancel.
* (iii) `J`-functions vanish at the critical zeros. Positivity is clear, and compatibility holds because `ρ+\bar ρ=1`.
* (iv) is the definition. ∎

**Theorem G′ (the decomposition; PROVED).** Put `𝔬:=𝔟−𝔥_crit` on `H¹`.
* (i) `𝔬` is continuous, compatible and hermitian, and `𝔬(x,y)=Σ_nΣ_{ρ∈B_n, Re ρ≠½}m_ρx(ρ)\overline{y(1−\bar ρ)}`.
* (ii) `𝔬` vanishes on every critical `E_ρ`. On each `E_ρ⊕E_{1−\bar ρ}` with `ρ` off-line, it has rank 2 and signature
  `(1,1)`.
* (iii) For real `F∈C_c^∞(I_T)`:
  `W_T(F) = lim_{ε↓0}(1/π)∫_R|F̂(τ)|²Re(ξ′/ξ)(½+ε+iτ)dτ + 𝔬([ĝ],[ĝ])`, and the first term is `≥0`.
  This is [compl]; for `F∈P_T` the left side is `𝔞_T(F)` [prim].
* (iv) RH ⟺ `𝔬=0` on `H¹` ⟺ `𝔬([ĝ],[ĝ])=0` for every real `F∈C_c^∞(R)` ⟺ `𝔟∈𝒫` ⟺ `𝔟=𝔥_crit`. EQUIV.

*Proof.*
* (i)–(ii) Compare the block formulas of Lemma 6.1(ii) and Theorem G(ii). On an off-line pair the block factors through
  `(u,u′)=(x(ρ),x(1−\bar ρ))∈C²`, and the map onto `C²` is surjective, because `v_ρ` vanishes at `1−\bar ρ`. There the
  block reads `m_ρ(u\bar u′+u′\bar u)`.
* (iii) `x(½+iτ)=Φ_F(½+iτ)=F̂(τ)`.
* (iv) If `ρ` is off-line, `𝔬(v_ρ+e^{iθ}v_{1−\bar ρ},·)≠0` for a suitable `θ`. The test classes `[ĝ]` are dense in `H¹`
  (`C_c^∞(R_+^×)` is dense in `𝓗_-`). For real tests `𝔬([ĝ₁],[ĝ₂])` is real, by pairing `ρ` with `\bar ρ`, so vanishing
  on real diagonals polarizes to `𝔬=0`. ∎

*Remark G.1 (PROVED; Lagarias' criterion).* `𝔥_ε≥0` on `𝒪` for all `ε∈(0,½)` ⟺ RH. Under RH every term
`η/(η²+u²)` has `η=ε>0`. If `β>½`, then `Re(ξ′/ξ)(½+ε+iτ)→−∞` as `(ε,τ)→(β−½,γ)` from the left, and a test concentrated
there makes `𝔥_ε<0`. **The approximants' positivity is RH (EQUIV; brief §3.5 "Herglotz"). The limit's positivity is
free, and blind.**

**Theorem H (compatible positivity cannot supply the sign; PROVED, [meth]).**
* (a) For every `𝔨∈𝒫` and every off-line `ρ`, `E_ρ⊕E_{1−\bar ρ}⊂rad 𝔨`, while `𝔬≠0` there.
* (b) Suppose `𝔨_1,…,𝔨_k∈𝒫`, `Ψ:[0,∞)^k→[0,∞)` with `Ψ(0)=0`, and `|𝔬(x,x)|≤Ψ(𝔨_1(x,x),…,𝔨_k(x,x))` on `H¹_alg`. Then RH
  holds. In particular, RH ⟺ there are `𝔨∈𝒫` and `C` with `|𝔬(x,x)|≤C𝔨(x,x)`.
* (c) So on this carrier, and on every continuous enlargement (F1), deriving `𝔬=0` from compatible positive structures
  is proving RH. A Hodge-index route must use a positive structure that is **not** `Θ`-compatible: one that couples
  `E_ρ` with `E_σ` for some `σ≠1−\bar ρ`.

*Proof.* (a) is Theorem D(iii) together with G′(ii). For (b), on an off-line block every `𝔨_i(x,x)=0`, so `𝔬(x,x)=0`
there, which contradicts (a). ∎

## §7 Theorem F — rigged, distributional and condensed enlargements

**F1 (continuous enlargements are blind; PROVED).** Let `𝔛` be a locally convex space with a representation `π` of
`R_+^×`, and `ι:H¹→𝔛` continuous with `ιρ_-(λ)=π(λ)ι`. Let `𝔨` be a continuous hermitian form on `𝔛` with `𝔨≥0` and
`𝔨(π(λ)u,π(λ)v)=λ𝔨(u,v)`. Then `ι^*𝔨∈𝒫(H¹)`. Hence `ι(E_ρ)⊂rad 𝔨` for every off-line `ρ`, and `ι(N_ρE_ρ)⊂rad 𝔨` for
every critical `ρ`. This covers Hilbert completions, D.192's strip-graph norms, and any Gelfand triple in which `H¹`
maps into the middle space.

*Proof.* `λ↦ρ_-(λ)x` is differentiable in `H¹` (Lemma 1.1), so conformality differentiates to compatibility. Then apply
Theorem E. ∎

**F2 (distributional carriers decouple; PROVED).** Consider the Gelfand triple `𝒪↪ℋ:=L²(R,dτ)↪𝒪′`, with the first map
`h↦h(½+i·)`, which is continuous with dense range (it contains `(C_c^∞(R))^`). Consider the unitary group
`U_uh:=e^{u(s−½)}h`. For every `λ∈C`, `ℓ_λ(h):=h(λ)` lies in `𝒪′` and `U_u^tℓ_λ=e^{u(λ−½)}ℓ_λ`. So every complex number
is a generalized eigenvalue of this unitary group. A rigged carrier escapes F30 (S2 survey; `PHASE_83_CLOSURE.md` §§2–3)
and S-7 exactly by decoupling its Hilbert positivity from the eigenvalues. Unitarity on `ℋ` says nothing about where
the Φ′-eigenvalues lie. ∎

**F3 (condensed quotients by dense ranges carry no scalar form; PROVED, mod CITED).** Let `V` be Fréchet and `W⊂V` a
dense subspace, e.g. D.191's critical Plancherel range `Ran M_Ξ⊂L²`. Then every sesquilinear map of condensed sets
`V/W×V/W→C` is zero.

*Proof.* `V` is compactly generated and `C` is T1, so condensed maps out of `V/W`, and out of
`(V×V)/(W×W)≅V/W×V/W`, are continuous maps on `V` or `V×V` that are constant on cosets (Clausen–Scholze Prop 1.7). A
continuous form vanishing on `W×V` vanishes on `\bar W×V=V×V`. ∎

**F4 (reading, [meth]).** A rigged or solid carrier can avoid the Hilbert-domain obstruction (F30; D.191 (4.5)) in three
ways:
* by continuity into a positive space, which makes it blind (F1);
* by distributional eigenvectors, which decouples it (F2);
* by a non-Hausdorff quotient, which leaves no scalar form (F3).

None couples positivity to an off-line zero. By Theorem H, only a non-compatible positive structure could.
