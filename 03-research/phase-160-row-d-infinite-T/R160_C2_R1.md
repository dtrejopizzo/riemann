## §2 The symplectic boundary space of `W_λ`

**Lemma 2.1 (block structure).** Put `Π_loc^±:=span{α_±,β_±}`, `Π_∞^±:=span{α̂_±,β̂_±}=𝔉Π_loc^±` in `𝓔_±`. Then
(i) `Ω(Π_loc^±,Π_∞^±)=0`; (ii) `Ω(γ,γ)=0` for every real `γ∈dom W_max`; (iii) `ω_±:=Ω(α_±,β_±)≠0` and
`Ω(α̂_±,β̂_±)=ω_±`, both purely imaginary; (iv) `Ω` has signature `(1,1)` on each of the four planes
`Π_loc^±, Π_∞^±`, and `𝓔_±=Π_loc^±⊕_ΩΠ_∞^±`.

*Proof.* (i) `L_{α_+}` and `L_{β_+}` depend only on the germ of `ξ` at `±λ` (`(L-even)`), and `α̂_+,β̂_+` are Fourier
transforms of compactly supported functions, hence real-analytic near `±λ`; for `ξ` smooth at `λ` the two lateral limits
in `L_{α_+}` both equal `−ξ(λ)` and `lim(λ−x)ξ′=0`, so `L_{α_+}(α̂_+)=L_{β_+}(α̂_+)=L_{α_+}(β̂_+)=L_{β_+}(β̂_+)=0`.
`L_{α̂_+}`, `L_{β̂_+}` are limits at `∞` of expressions in `ξ,ξ′`, and `α_+,β_+` vanish near `∞`, so they vanish on
`α_+,β_+`. Since `L_γ(ξ)=iΩ(ξ,γ)` and `Ω` is Hermitian, all eight cross pairings vanish. The odd case is identical
(`(L-odd)` has the same locality). (ii) For real `γ∈dom W_max`, `W_maxγ` is real, so `⟨W_maxγ|γ⟩∈ℝ` and
`Ω(γ,γ)=2Im⟨W_maxγ|γ⟩=0`. (iii) By (i) and (ii) the `Ω`-matrix on `𝓔_+` in CM's basis is block diagonal with blocks
`[[0,ω],[ω̄,0]]` on `Π_loc^+` and `[[0,ω′],[ω̄′,0]]` on `Π_∞^+`; nondegeneracy of `Ω` on `𝓔_+` (CM) forces `ω,ω′≠0`.
`𝔉` is unitary and commutes with `W_max`, so `Ω(𝔉ξ,𝔉η)=Ω(ξ,η)`; hence `ω′=Ω(𝔉α_+,𝔉β_+)=ω`. For real `α,β`,
`iΩ(α,β)=⟨Wα|β⟩−⟨α|Wβ⟩∈ℝ`, so `ω∈iℝ`. (iv) A Hermitian form `[[0,ω],[ω̄,0]]` with `ω≠0` has eigenvalues `±|ω|`. ∎

(For the record: from `(L-even)`, `p∂_xα_+=−2x` near `λ`, so `iΩ(α_+,β_+)=4λ` up to CM's normalising constants; the
value is not used.)

**Lemma 2.2 (sector decomposition).** For `ε∈{1,−1}` put `α_ε:=Π_εα_+=½(α_++ε𝔉α_+)`, `β_ε:=Π_εβ_+`; for
`ε∈{i,−i}` put `α_ε:=Π_εα_−=½(α_−−ε𝔉α_−)`, `β_ε:=Π_εβ_−` (one checks `𝔉(v−ε𝔉v)=𝔉v+εv=ε(v−ε𝔉v)` for odd `v`, using
`𝔉²v=−v` and `ε²=−1`). Then `{α_ε,β_ε}` is a basis of `𝓔_ε`, `dim 𝓔_ε=2`, `Ω(𝓔_ε,𝓔_{ε′})=0` for `ε≠ε′`,
`Ω(α_ε,α_ε)=Ω(β_ε,β_ε)=0`, and `ω_ε:=Ω(α_ε,β_ε)=½ω_±≠0` (sign `±` = parity of `ε`).

*Proof.* On `𝓔_+`, `𝔉²=1` swaps `Π_loc^+` and `Π_∞^+`, so `𝓔_+=E_1⊕E_{−1}` with `E_ε={v+ε𝔉v: v∈Π_loc^+}`,
`Π_ε(v)=½(v+ε𝔉v)` for `v∈Π_loc^+`. Using Lemma 2.1(i) and `𝔉`-invariance of `Ω`:

## §5 Theorem C — zero-blindness of the boundary condition

**Theorem C (PROVED; [meth]).** Let `W_1,W_2` be any two self-adjoint extensions of `W_min`, `N_j(a,b)` the number of
eigenvalues of `W_j` in `(a,b)` (with multiplicity). (i) For every bounded interval, `|N_1(a,b)−N_2(a,b)|≤4`. (ii) If both
are `𝔉`-invariant, then sector-wise `|N_1^{(ε)}(a,b)−N_2^{(ε)}(a,b)|≤1`. (iii) Consequently every asymptotic law of the
form `N(E)=Φ(E)+o(Φ(E))` (or with any remainder `≥1` in size) holds for one extension iff it holds for all; in
particular CM's semiclassical count `2σ(E,λ)`, `σ(E,λ)∼(E/2π)(log(E/2π)−1+log 4−2log λ)+λ²` (CM Prop `asympt1`, CITED,
a semiclassical approximation in the source) is, whatever its status for `W_sa`, boundary-condition independent.

*Proof.* (i) `σ(W_1)∪σ(W_2)` is discrete, so pick `c∈ℝ` below `a` and outside it. `W_min−c=(W_j−c)|dom W_min` satisfies
`‖(W_min−c)u‖≥dist(c,σ(W_1))‖u‖`, so `R:=ran(W_min−c)` is closed, and `R^⊥=ker((W_min−c)^*)=ker(W_max−c)` has
dimension `4` (all four solutions of `Wξ=cξ` lie in `L²` since all endpoints are LC — CM's proof of Lemma `Wdefic`
applies to every `c∈ℂ`). `B_j:=(W_j−c)^{−1}` are bounded self-adjoint and agree on `R` (`B_j(W_min−c)u=u`), so
`B_1−B_2` vanishes on `R` and, being self-adjoint, has range in `R^⊥`: `rank(B_1−B_2)≤4`. Write `B_1−B_2=P−N`,
`P,N≥0`, `rank P+rank N≤4`. Weyl's inequality: for every `s>0`, `N_{B_1}(s,∞)≤N_{B_2}(s,∞)+rank P` and
`N_{B_2}(s,∞)≤N_{B_1}(s,∞)+rank N` (finite counts, since `s>0` is away from the accumulation point `0`). The map
`μ↦(μ−c)^{−1}` sends `(a,b)` onto `(s,t):=((b−c)^{−1},(a−c)^{−1})⊂(0,∞)` bijectively, so
`N_j(a,b)=N_{B_j}(s,∞)−N_{B_j}(t,∞)` (endpoints not eigenvalues; otherwise pass to the limit), and
`|N_1(a,b)−N_2(a,b)|≤rank P+rank N≤4`. (ii) Same argument in `H_ε`, where `dim ker(W_max^{(ε)}−c)=1`.
(iii) Immediate from (i). ∎

**Corollary C′ (the phase-60 §4.B decisive question; PROVED).** *"Can the boundary condition at `q=±λ` be stated
without reference to the zeros? If yes, it is a genuine Hilbert–Pólya candidate; if not, that is where RH is being
inserted."* — Yes: every self-adjoint boundary condition is (Theorem A lists the Fourier-invariant ones as four angles,
Theorem B gives the resulting spectra as level sets of Herglotz functions of `W_sa`). And precisely because the
boundary space is 8-dimensional, Theorem C shows that no boundary condition can insert, remove, or detect the zeros:
changing it moves the counting function by at most `4` on every interval, while the zeros enter (if at all) only
through the leading UV term, which is a property of the symbol `(p,q)` and is shared by all extensions. Neither branch
of the phase-60 dichotomy is the case: the boundary condition is RH-neutral because it is spectrally inert. The
"exact locus of arithmetic input" that phase-60 §4.B item 1 expected to find in a one-parameter family of `B` does
not exist in the boundary condition: the 4-torus is arithmetic-free.

*Remark 5.1 (what C does not say).* C does not decide whether `W_sa` itself has negative spectrum equal to the zero
squares; CM claim only the leading UV behaviour, with numerical evidence, and an exact match would require an
`O(1)`-remainder counting law for `W_sa` beaten by Selberg's `Ω`-theorem for `S(t)`; that is OPEN and irrelevant to
row D (an exact match, if it held, would be Hilbert–Pólya = EQUIV, brief §5.1).
