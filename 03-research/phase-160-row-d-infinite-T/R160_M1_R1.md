## §0 Verdict

**No sign.** Nothing here proves `λ^A_T ≥ 0` or `λ^W_T ≥ 0` for any `T > 0.8`.

**Result for the route.** Open problem (6) of P46 is answered **negatively in its literal reading**, in both forms. The
problem asks whether the flat-top certificates can be chosen with `w_T` depending on `T` only through the primes outside
the window. In the literal reading the tail is `T`-consistent: one fixed measure, cut at `2T` (Def 1.4). The other
readings are EQUIV (Rem 2.6). Both candidates that `prob:B` names are excluded in both forms:
* the canonical column `ν_K` of `prop:M`, together with its whole `s = ½` family (Cor L2, Cor Σ1, Cor Σ2);
* the weights adapted to the outside primes, i.e. the unique flat-top certificate whose dual variable is the comb of
  outside primes (Prop ♮, Cor L2, Cor Σ1).

The mechanism of failure splits **exactly** into two pieces (Prop C):
* an **edge term** `−2e^{−T}E(e^{2T−})cos(2Tτ)`, where `E = ψ − x` is the Chebyshev remainder. Its size is Littlewood's
  `Ω_±`.
* an **inside term** `f♯_T`, which carries a **resolution dip** of depth `(8/3π)m_1T` at `γ_1 + 3π/(4T)`.

One atom at the window edge, of mass `e^{−T}E(e^{2T−})`, removes the first piece **identically in `τ`** (Prop E). This
atom is a prime datum: it is computed from the primes below `e^{2T}`, with no zeros. It gives a new canonical object, the
*Chebyshev-corrected canonical column* `ν♯_T`. The second piece cannot be removed by any correction of total variation
`o(T)`, in either form (Thm Σ). The route is therefore reduced to one precise step, the **lift** (§7 O-1). The lift is
**EQUIV** to RH, and every necessary property of it is recorded.

### 0.2 Why this is outside every excluded class

This memo derives no positivity. Its theorems are **obstructions for ζ**: statements about certificate rules for the
exact Λ, proved by two ζ-specific inputs. The first is Littlewood's `Ω_±` for `ψ(x) − x`. The second is Ingham's
explicit formula for `ψ₁`, which carries the exact zeros. So the class walls cannot refute them. A class wall refutes a
positivity derived from class membership (brief §4 preamble), and nothing here is a positivity. The gate table records,
line by line, which gate each failure *is*, and which walls of record the new theorems extend.

Relative to **S-5**, the new cases are these. **Jump edges** break Theorem R's hypothesis `φ(0) = 1`
(G2_B4_R2 Def 3.1). They also break Theorem RN's `D_T(ε) < ∞`, since for a jump `ŵ ∼ 1/x` (G2_B4_R5 Def 3.1).
**Cut families**, the canonical column `ν_K`, **edge atoms** and the corrected column `ν♯` are not flat-top rules of any
scale. For a **fast edge** (`a_T ≤ Ce^{−T}`), R5 §4.3 lists the degenerate scale `a_T = O(1/T)` as OPEN. Cor L1 settles
the sub-case `a_T = O(e^{−T})` in [compl].

Relative to **S-2** (`thm:O`), no identity certificate is claimed. `ν♯` is an explicit dual point, not a certificate:
Thm B♯ shows that its value is negative at all large `T`.

Relative to **I-1** (`cor:void`), the obstructions hold for ζ. The proof of Theorem L uses `E` only up to bounded
changes, so it applies equally to the deletion falsifier `Λ1_{n≥N}` (where `E` shifts by a constant). That is allowed for
an obstruction. No positivity is inferred from a class.

## §1 Definitions

**1.1** `X := e^{2T}`. `ψ(x) := Σ_{n≤x}Λ(n)` (right-continuous), `E(x) := ψ(x) − x`, `ψ(X^−) := Σ_{n<X}Λ(n)`,
`E(X^−) := ψ(X^−) − X`. Put `I(X) := ∫_1^X E(t)t^{−3/2}dt` and `J(t) := ∫_1^t E(y)dy`. `c_K(T) := ∫_{2T}^∞K`
(thm:S(iii)) and `ν_K := ½K(|u|)1_{|u|≥2T}du` (prop:M). `s := ½ + iτ`.

**1.2 (dual points; failure).** For a dual point `(ν,β)` at `T` (def:PD) put
`val(ν,β) := inf_τ[σ_T+ν̂+β_0ĝ_0+β_1ĝ_1]`. A family `T ↦ ν_T` ( `T ≥ T_0` ) **fails at `T` in [compl]** if
`val(ν_T,(2,0)) < 0`. It **fails at `T` in [prim]** if `val(ν_T,β) < 0` for every `β ∈ ℝ²`. It is a **certificate family**
if it fails at no `T ≥ T_0`. For a flat-top `w ∈ 𝒲_T`, `val(ν_w,(2,0)) = inf f_w` (def:W; `f_w := σ_T+ν̂_w+2ĝ_0`).

**1.3 (the canonical column).** `B_T := σ_T + ν̂_K + 2ĝ_0` is `prop:M`'s column at the polar multiplier `β = (2,0)`. When
`X` is not a prime power, `ν_K = ν_w` for the box `w = 1_{[−2T,2T]} ∈ 𝒲_T` (eq:nuw), so `B_T = f_box`.

**1.4 (cut families = the literal reading of open (6)).** Fix one finite even signed measure `ν^∞` on ℝ. Its **cut
family** is `ν_T := ν^∞|_{\{|u|≥2T\}}`. As `T` grows, the dual variable changes only by losing what enters the window.
In flat-top terms: `w_T = 1` on `[−2T,2T]`, and `w_T = ω` on `|u| > 2T` with `ω` fixed. So the tail at `u` does not depend
on `T` as long as `u` is outside the window.

**1.5 (tails, edge value, profile norm, edge-drop measure).** For `w ∈ 𝒲_T` write `ω := w|_{(2T,∞)}` (the tail) and
`G(t) := w(log t)t^{−½}` (`t ≥ 1`). The tail is **regular** if:
* `ω` is continuous on `(2T,∞)`;
* `G` is of bounded variation on `(X,∞)`, with `∫_{(X,∞)}t\,d|G|(t) < ∞`;
* `X` is not a prime power.

The **edge value** is `θ := ω(2T^+)`. The **edge-drop measure** is `μ_w := −e^T dG` on `[X,∞)`, including the atom
`1−θ` at `X`. Since `G(X^−) = X^{−½}` and `G(∞) = 0`, `μ_w([X,∞)) = 1`. If `ω ≥ 0` is nonincreasing, `μ_w` is a
probability measure. For `ω ∈ C¹(2T,∞)` with `ω′` locally absolutely continuous, the **profile norm** is
`N_T(ω) := |θ| + |ω′(2T^+)| + ∫_{2T}^∞(|ω|+|ω′|+|ω″|)du`.

**1.6 (the `s = ½` family).** For `θ ∈ [0,1]`, `w^{(θ)}_T := 1` on `[−2T,2T]` and `θe^{−(|u|−2T)}` outside, so
`w^{(θ)} = (1−θ)1_{[−2T,2T]} + θw^{R1}`. Here `w^{R1} = w^{(1)}` is the Riesz edge profile `φ(v) = e^{−v}`
(G2_B4_R2 Prop F, `ε = 1`). The a.c. part of `ν_{w^{(θ)}}` contains `2θe^Tν_{½,0}` (in `prop:T0` notation), because
`2e^{−(u−2T)}cosh(u/2) = e^{T}e^{−(u−2T)/2} + e^{−T}e^{−3(u−2T)/2}`. So `θ` interpolates between the canonical column
`ν_K ≈ 2e^{−T}ν_{½,0}` (`θ = 0`, prop:M) and the column `s = ½` at full polar strength (`θ = 1`). This is "the canonical
column `s = ½` as a family".

**1.7 (zero data; used only under RH).** Let `γ_1 > 0` be the least positive ordinate, `m_1 ≥ 1` its multiplicity,
`d_1 := dist(γ_1, Ord∖\{γ_1\})` and `d_1^+ ≥ d_1` the gap above `γ_1`. Put `τ_1 = τ_1(T) := γ_1 + 3π/(4T)`,
`τ_F := γ_1 + d_1^+/2`, `I_T := [τ_F − π/2T, τ_F + π/2T]`, `J_1 := [γ_1, γ_1 + d_1/4]`. For `b ∈ ℝ²` put
`ρ_b(s) := b_0/s + b_1/s²`, and define `q_* := min_{τ∈J_1}min_{|b|=1}|ρ_b(s)|` and
`L_* := max_{τ∈J_1}sup_{b≠0}|ρ_b(½+iτ)|/|ρ_b(½+iτ_F)|`. Both are finite and positive, because `1/s` and `1/s²` are
ℝ-linearly independent for `τ ≠ 0` (then `ρ_b(s) = 0` forces `s = −b_1/b_0 ∈ ℝ`), and by compactness.

## §2 The Chebyshev form of the canonical column (all PROVED, unconditional)

**Proposition C.** For every `T > 0` and `τ ∈ ℝ`,
```
B_T(τ) = f♯_T(τ) − 2e^{−T}E(X^−)cos(2Tτ),
f♯_T(τ) := Ar_T(τ) + 2∫_1^X E(t) g_τ(t) dt,   g_τ(t) := ∂_t[t^{−½}cos(τ log t)] = −Re[s·t^{−3/2−iτ}],
Ar_T(τ) := C_0 − 2 + c_K(T) + ∫_0^{2T}(1−cos τu)K(u)du + 2∫_0^{2T}e^{−u/2}cos(τu)du.
```
*Proof.* By eq:m, `m(τ) + ν̂_K(τ) = C_0 + ∫_0^∞(1−cos τu)K + ∫_{2T}^∞K cos τu = C_0 + c_K + ∫_0^{2T}(1−cos τu)K`. Put
`G_τ(t) := t^{−½}cos(τ log t)` on `[1,X]`. With `t = e^u`,
`2ĝ_0(τ) = 4∫_0^{2T}cosh(u/2)cos(τu)du = 2∫_1^X G_τ(t)(1+t^{−1})dt`, and
`Σ_{n∈N_T}v_n cos(τ log n) = 2∫_{[1,X)}G_τ dψ`. Hence `2ĝ_0 − Σ_{N_T}v_n cos = −2∫_{[1,X)}G_τ dE + 2∫_1^XG_τ dt/t`.
Integration by parts, with `G_τ ∈ C¹[1,X]` and `E` right-continuous of bounded variation, gives
`∫_{[1,X)}G_τ dE = G_τ(X)E(X^−) − G_τ(1)E(1^−) − ∫_1^X E G_τ′dt`, where `E(1^−) = −1` and `G_τ(1) = 1`. Also
`2∫_1^XG_τ dt/t = 2∫_0^{2T}e^{−u/2}cos τu\,du` and `G_τ(X) = e^{−T}cos(2Tτ)`. Collect terms. ∎

**Proposition E (the edge atom).** `ν♯_T := ν_K + e^{−T}E(X^−)(δ_{2T} + δ_{−2T})` is a finite even measure supported in
`\{|u| ≥ 2T\}`. So `(ν♯_T,β)` is dual feasible for every `β` (def:PD; atoms at `±2T` are allowed, cf. the proof of
prop:weak). Its complete certificate function is `σ_T + ν̂♯_T + 2ĝ_0 = f♯_T`.
*Proof.* The transform of the atom pair is `2e^{−T}E(X^−)cos(2Tτ)`. Use Prop C. ∎

*Reading.* `f♯_T` is the canonical column with its boundary term removed. The correcting mass `e^{−T}E(X^−)` is a finite
prime datum. `f♯_T` is **not** a flat-top `f_w`, since eq:nuw produces no edge atoms of arbitrary mass. It is a dual
point of (D), which is all that weak duality needs (prop:weak).

**Proposition C0 (the τ=0 law).** Let `w ∈ 𝒲_T` have a regular tail (Def 1.5). Then
```
f_w(0) = f♯_T(0) − 2e^{−T}∫_{[X,∞)}E\,dμ_w + ∫_{2T}^∞ω(u)(2e^{−u/2} − K(u))du,   |2e^{−u/2}−K(u)| ≤ 3e^{−5u/2} (u ≥ 1).
```
*Proof.* By eq:fw at `τ = 0`, `f_w(0) = C_0 + ∫_0^∞(1−w)K − 2ΣΛ(n)G(n) + 2∫_1^∞G(1+t^{−1})dt`. The sum and the
integrals converge absolutely, since `|G| ≤ Ct^{−1−δ}`. As in Prop C, this equals
`C_0 + ∫(1−w)K − 2 + 2∫_1^∞G dt/t + 2∫_{[1,∞)}E dG`. The integration by parts is legitimate for two reasons. First,
`G` is continuous off `X`, and `E` is continuous at `X`. Second, `EG → 0` and `∫t\,d|G| < ∞`.

On `[1,X]` we have `G = t^{−½}`. This contributes `−I(X) + 4 − 4e^{−T}`. At `X` the jump of `G` contributes
`−2e^{−T}(1−θ)E(X)`. Beyond `X`, the contribution is `2∫_{(X,∞)}E dG + 2∫_{2T}^∞ωe^{−u/2}du + ∫_{2T}^∞(1−ω)K`.
The jump and the part beyond `X` together give `−2e^{−T}∫_{[X,∞)}E dμ_w`. Finally,
`f♯_T(0) = C_0 + c_K + 2 − 4e^{−T} − I(X)` (Prop C at `τ = 0`) and `∫(1−ω)K = c_K − ∫ωK`. The bound on
`K − 2e^{−u/2} = 2Σ_{k≥1}e^{−(2k+½)u}` is elementary. ∎

For the box, `μ_w = δ_X`. In words: **the τ=0 value of any flat-top certificate is the canonical corrected value minus
twice the edge-drop average of the normalised Chebyshev remainder.**

**Proposition ♮ (the comb certificate).** Let `T ≥ ½log 2`, `d_*(u) := 2cosh(u/2) − ½K(u)` (`> 0` on `[0.3,∞)`, proof of
thm:Zexh) and `k_♮ := ½K/d_* = e^u/(e^{2u}−1−e^{−u})`. Define `w^♮ := 1` on `[−2T,2T]` and `−k_♮(|u|)` outside.
(i) `w^♮ ∈ 𝒲_T`, with `|w^♮(u)| ≤ 2e^{−|u|}`.
(ii) For `X` not a prime power, `ν_{w^♮} = ν^♮_T := Σ_{log n>2T}(v_n/2)k_♮(log n)(δ_{log n}+δ_{−log n})`. This is a
positive comb on the outside primes, of mass `≤ Σ_{n>X}4Λ(n)n^{−3/2} → 0`. For `u ≥ log 2` the denominator of `k_♮` is
`≥ (1 − ¼ − ⅛)e^{2u}`, so `k_♮ ≤ 1.6e^{−u}`.
(iii) `w^♮` is, up to null sets, the only `w ∈ 𝒲_T` for which `ν_w` has no absolutely continuous part.
*Proof.* The a.c. density of `ν_w` on `|u| > 2T` is `½(1−w)K + 2w cosh(u/2) = ½K + w\,d_*`. It vanishes iff
`w = −k_♮`, because `d_* > 0` there (and `2T ≥ log 2 > 0.3`). The closed form of `k_♮` is algebra from `K = 2e^{3u/2}/(e^{2u}−1)`.
The atoms are `−(v_n/2)w(log n) = (v_n/2)k_♮(log n)`, by eq:nuw. ∎

**Remark 2.6 (the readings of open (6)).** Suppose instead that `w_T|_{\{|u|>2T\}}` depends on `T` only through the set

## §3 The explicit formula in the form needed

**Lemma J (CITED: Ingham, Thm 28).** For `t ≥ 1`,
`J(t) = −Σ_ρ t^{ρ+1}/(ρ(ρ+1)) − t log 2π + j_0(t)` with `|j_0(t)| ≤ c_2`. The series converges absolutely and
uniformly on compacta, since `Σ|ρ(ρ+1)|^{−1} =: c_1 < ∞`. Under RH, `|J(t)| ≤ c_J t^{3/2}` for all `t ≥ 1`, with
`c_J := c_1 + log 2π + c_2`. (Here `J = ψ₁ − (t²−1)/2`, `ζ′/ζ(0) = log 2π`, and `j_0` collects `ζ′/ζ(−1) + ½` and the
trivial-zero series.)

**Lemma H (PROVED from Lemma J, under RH).** For `τ ∈ ℝ` put `H_τ(X) := ∫_1^XE(t)t^{−3/2−iτ}dt`. Then
```
H_τ(X) = −(3/2+iτ) Σ_ρ (X^{i(γ−τ)} − 1)/(ρ(ρ+1)\,i(γ−τ)) + h_τ(X),   |h_τ(X)| ≤ c_J + |3/2+iτ|(2log 2π + c_2),
```
where `(X^{iy}−1)/(iy) := 2T` at `y = 0`. Also `2∫_1^XE g_τ\,dt = −2Re[s H_τ(X)]`.
*Proof.* Since `J(1) = 0`, `H_τ = J(X)X^{−3/2−iτ} + (3/2+iτ)∫_1^XJ(t)t^{−5/2−iτ}dt`. Insert Lemma J and integrate
termwise (absolute uniform convergence). This uses `∫_1^Xt^{ρ−3/2−iτ}dt = (X^{i(γ−τ)}−1)/(i(γ−τ))`, since
`ρ − ½ = iγ` under RH. The boundary term is `≤ c_J` in modulus. The `t log 2π` and `j_0` parts of the integral are
`≤ |3/2+iτ|(2log 2π + (2/3)c_2)`. The last identity is `g_τ = −Re[s t^{−3/2−iτ}]` with `E` real. ∎

**Corollary H0 (bounded off resonance; under RH).** For every compact `Q ⊂ ℝ` and every `δ > 0` there is `C(Q,δ)` with
`|f♯_T(τ)| ≤ C(Q,δ)` for all `T ≥ ½log 2` and all `τ ∈ Q` with `dist(τ, Ord) ≥ δ`. In particular
`|f♯_T(0)| ≤ C♯_0` for all `T` (`dist(0,Ord) = γ_1 > 0`), and `|I(X)| ≤ C♯_0 + |C_0| + c_K + 6` (Prop C0 at `τ=0`).
For `T ≥ 2π/d_1` we have `dist(I_T, Ord) ≥ d_1/4`, so `sup_{I_T}|f♯_T| ≤ C_F`.
*Proof.* `|Ar_T(τ)| ≤ |C_0| + 2 + c_K(½log 2) + (m(τ)−C_0) + 4`. For the ρ-sum,
`|(X^{iy}−1)/(iy)| ≤ 2/|y| ≤ 2/δ`, so it is bounded by `2|3/2+iτ|Σ_ρ(|ρ||ρ+1|max(δ,|γ−τ|))^{−1} < ∞`, uniformly
in `X` and in `τ ∈ Q`. For the last claim, `|τ_F − γ| ≥ d_1^+/2 ≥ d_1/2` for every ordinate `γ`, and
`π/2T ≤ d_1/4`. ∎

**Corollary H1 (the resonance at `γ_1`; under RH).** For `T ≥ T_B := max(1, 3π/d_1)` and `0 < x ≤ d_1/4`,
```
f♯_T(γ_1+x) = 2m_1 sin(2Tx)/x + R_T(x),   |R_T(x)| ≤ C_1 + (12/γ_1)m_1Tx,
```
with `C_1` independent of `T` and `x`. In particular `f♯_T(τ_1) ≤ −(8/(3π))m_1T + C_B` with `C_B := C_1 + 9πm_1/γ_1`.
*Proof.* In Lemma H take `τ = γ_1 + x` and isolate the `m_1` zeros with `γ = γ_1`. For those, `γ − τ = −x`, and their
contribution to `−2Re[sH_τ]` is `2m_1Re[c(τ)(1−e^{−2iTx})/(ix)]` with `c(τ) := s(3/2+iτ)/(ρ_1(ρ_1+1))`. Since
`s = ρ_1 + ix` and `3/2 + iτ = ρ_1 + 1 + ix`, and `x ≤ d_1/4 ≤ γ_1/2`, we get `|c(τ) − 1| ≤ 3x/γ_1`. Moreover
`|(1−e^{−2iTx})/(ix)| ≤ 2T` and `Re[(1−e^{−2iTx})/(ix)] = sin(2Tx)/x`. Every other zero has `|γ − τ| ≥ 3d_1/4`, and is
bounded as in Cor H0. So are `Ar_T` and `h_τ`. At `x = 3π/(4T)` (`≤ d_1/4` since `T ≥ 3π/d_1`), `sin(3π/2) = −1` gives
`2m_1·(−1)·4T/(3π) = −(8/3π)m_1T`. ∎

## §4 The Littlewood obstruction ([compl])

**Theorem L.** Let `T ↦ ν_T` (`T ≥ T_0`) be any family of dual points (def:PD) with signed total mass
`ν_T(ℝ) ≤ M(T)`, where `M(T)/log log T → 0` (e.g. `M` bounded). Then the family fails in [compl] at arbitrarily large
`T`. Under RH, `val(ν_T,(2,0)) ≤ C − 2e^{−T}E(X^−) + M(T)`, with `C` independent of `T`, and this tends to `−∞` along a
sequence `T_k → ∞`. **PROVED.**
*Proof.* *¬RH.* By Weil's criterion (P46 §1) there is `T_1` with `λ^W_{T_1} < 0`. By lem:monoT, `λ^W_T < 0` for all
`T ≥ T_1`. By prop:weak and prop:P, every complete dual point at `T` has value `≤ λ^W_T < 0`.
*RH.* `val ≤ f(0) = σ_T(0) + 2ĝ_0(0) + ν_T(ℝ) = B_T(0) − c_K + ν_T(ℝ) = f♯_T(0) − c_K − 2e^{−T}E(X^−) + ν_T(ℝ)` (Prop C),
and `|f♯_T(0)| ≤ C♯_0` (Cor H0). By Littlewood (CITED) there are `x_k → ∞` with
`E(x_k) ≥ c√x_k log log log x_k`. Put `X_k :=` the midpoint of `(x_k, ⌊x_k⌋+1)`. Then `ψ(X_k^−) = ψ(x_k)`,
`E(X_k^−) ≥ E(x_k) − 1`, and `T_k := ½log X_k`. Now `2e^{−T_k}E(X_k^−) ≥ 2c(1−o(1))log log(2T_k) − o(1)`, which beats
`M(T_k) + C♯_0`. ∎

**Theorem L′ (jump edges).** Let `w_T ∈ 𝒲_T` (`T ≥ T_0`) have regular tails `ω_T ∈ C¹` with `ω_T′` locally absolutely
continuous, `(|ω_T|+|ω_T′|)(u) → 0` as `u → ∞`, `N_T(ω_T) ≤ N_0`, and edge values with `1 − θ_T ≥ θ_0 > 0`
(resp. `≤ −θ_0`). Then the family fails in [compl] at arbitrarily large `T`. Under RH,
`f_{w_T}(0) ≤ C(N_0) − 2θ_0e^{−T}E(X)` whenever `E(X) ≥ 0` (resp. `+2θ_0e^{−T}E(X)` whenever `E(X) ≤ 0`). **PROVED.**
*Proof.* ¬RH as in Thm L. Under RH, by Prop C0,
`f_w(0) = f♯_T(0) − 2(1−θ)e^{−T}E(X) + 2∫_{(X,∞)}EG′dt + O(e^{−5T}N_0)`. Integrating by parts with `J`,
`∫_X^∞EG′ = −J(X)G′(X^+) − ∫_X^∞JG″`, where `G′ = t^{−3/2}(ω′−½ω)(log t)` and
`G″ = t^{−5/2}(ω″−2ω′+¾ω)(log t)`. By Lemma J, `|J| ≤ c_Jt^{3/2}`. The boundary term at `∞` vanishes by the decay hypothesis. Hence
`|2∫_{(X,∞)}EG′| ≤ 2c_J(|ω′(2T^+)| + ½|θ| + ∫_{2T}^∞|ω″−2ω′+¾ω|du) ≤ 6c_JN_0`. Littlewood's `Ω_+` (resp. `Ω_−`) at
non-integers, as in Thm L, completes the proof. ∎

**Corollary L1 (fast edges).** Let `w_T ∈ 𝒲_T` have regular tails `0 ≤ ω_T ≤ 1`, nonincreasing, vanishing on
`[2T + a_T, ∞)`, with `a_T ≤ min(1, C_ae^{−T})`. Joins continuous at `2T` (`θ = 1`) are included. Then the family fails
in [compl] at arbitrarily large `T`. **PROVED.**
*Proof.* ¬RH as before. Under RH, `μ_{w_T}` is a probability measure on `[X, Xe^{a_T}]`. Since `ψ` is nondecreasing,
`E(t) ≥ E(X) − (t−X)` for `t ≥ X`, so `∫E\,dμ ≥ E(X) − 2Xa_T ≥ E(X) − 2C_ae^T`. By Prop C0,
`f_w(0) ≤ C♯_0 + 4C_a + 3 − 2e^{−T}E(X)`. Finish with Littlewood as in Thm L. ∎

**Corollary L2 (the literal reading of open (6) and the named candidates, [compl]).** Each of the following fails in
[compl] at arbitrarily large `T` (under RH, with depth `≥ c log log T − C` along a sequence):
(a) every cut family (Def 1.4). Here `ν_T(ℝ) = ν^∞(\{|u|≥2T\}) → 0`: Thm L.
(b) the canonical column `B_T` of `prop:M` at `β = (2,0)`. Here `ν_K(ℝ) = c_K → 0`: Thm L, or Cor L1 with `a_T = 0`.
(c) the comb certificate `ν^♮_T`, whose mass is `→ 0` (Prop ♮): Thm L.
(d) the `s = ½` family `w^{(θ)}` with `θ < 1` fixed. Here `N_T = 5θ` and `1 − θ_T = 1 − θ`: Thm L′.
**PROVED.**

## §5 The resolution threshold (both forms)

**Lemma G (one edge oscillation; PROVED, unconditional; the needed part of G2_B4_R5 Thm PT, reproved).** For
`β ∈ ℝ²` put `b := (β_0+2Tβ_1, −β_1)`. Then for all real `τ`,
`β_0ĝ_0(τ) + β_1ĝ_1(τ) = e^TRe[e^{2iTτ}ρ_b(s)] + ε(τ)`, with `|ε(τ)| ≤ (4T+6)e^{−T}|β|` and `|β| ≤ (2T+2)|b|`.
Let `F: ℝ → ℝ` be any function with `F + β_0ĝ_0 + β_1ĝ_1 ≥ 0` on `\{τ_1\} ∪ I_T`, where `τ_1 ∈ J_1`. Then there is
`T_G = T_G(q_*,L_*)` such that for `T ≥ T_G`,
```
−F(τ_1) ≤ 4L_*·max(0, sup_{I_T}F).
```
*Proof.* Differentiating `∫_0^{2T}cosh(σu)e^{iτu}du` in `σ` at `σ = ½` gives `ĝ_0 = 2Re[sinh(2Ts)/s]` and
`ĝ_1 = 2Re[2Tcosh(2Ts)/s − sinh(2Ts)/s²]`. Also `e^{2Ts} = e^Te^{2iTτ}`, `|e^{−2Ts}| = e^{−T}` and `|s| ≥ ½`. This gives
the expansion, with the error `≤ e^{−T}(2|β_0| + (4T+4)|β_1|)`.

Put `R := e^T|ρ_b(s_1)|` with `s_1 = ½+iτ_1`, and `A := −F(τ_1)`, `S := sup_{I_T}F`. By definition of `q_*`,
`|b| ≤ Re^{−T}/q_*`. So `|ε| ≤ δ_TR` with `δ_T := (4T+6)(2T+2)e^{−2T}/q_*`.

At `τ_1`: `0 ≤ −A + R + δ_TR`.

On `I_T`: `2Tτ` sweeps a full period, so some `τ* ∈ I_T` has `Re[e^{2iTτ*}ρ_b(s_F)] = −|ρ_b(s_F)| ≤ −R e^{−T}/L_*`.
Also `|∂_τρ_b| ≤ 17|b|` (because `|s| ≥ ½`), so `|ρ_b(s*)−ρ_b(s_F)| ≤ 27|b|/T`. Hence
`0 ≤ F(τ*) + (β-terms)(τ*) ≤ S − R(1/L_* − 27/(q_*T) − δ_T)`.

Take `T_G` such that `27/(q_*T) + δ_T ≤ 1/(2L_*)` and `δ_T ≤ 1` for `T ≥ T_G`. Then `R ≤ 2L_*S^+`, and
`A ≤ 2R ≤ 4L_*S^+`. ∎

**Theorem B♯ (the corrected canonical column fails).**

(b) *(Under RH.)* `f♯_T(τ_1(T)) ≤ −(8/(3π))m_1T + C_B` for `T ≥ T_B`. **PROVED.**
*Proof.* (b) is Cor H1.
(a) Under RH: [compl] follows from (b). For [prim], apply Lemma G to `F = f♯_T`, with `S ≤ C_F` (Cor H0). A certificate
would give `(8/3π)m_1T − C_B ≤ 4L_*C_F^+`, which is false for `T` large.
Under ¬RH: by primitive sufficiency (P46 §1; Paper 45 Thm 10.4) there is `T_1` with `λ^A_{T_1} < 0`, and by lem:monoT

(ii) We have `F + (β_0−2)ĝ_0 + β_1ĝ_1 ≥ 0`, `−F(τ_1) ≥ (8/3π)m_1T − C_B − M` and `sup_{I_T}F ≤ C_F + M`. Lemma G gives

**Corollary Σ1 (bounded mass fails in [prim]; the column of `prop:M` in primitive form).** Let `(ν_T,β(T))` be any
family with `‖ν_T‖_TV ≤ M_0`, where `β(T)` is arbitrary. Examples: `ν_T = ν_K`, which is `prop:M`'s canonical column

*Proof.* ¬RH: as in Thm B♯(a). Under RH, `‖ν_T − ν♯_T‖ ≤ M_0 + c_K + 2e^{−T}|E(X^−)|`. Put
`c_Σ := (8/3π)m_1/(1+4L_*)` and `C′ := (C_B + 4L_*C_F^+)/(1+4L_*) + M_0 + c_K`. By Thm Σ(ii) the family can survive at

(`x = e^{2T}`, `(ψ−x)²x^{−2}dx = 2(e^{−T}E)²dT`), gives `∫_Y^{2Y}(e^{−T}E(e^{2T}))²dT = O(Y)`. By Chebyshev's

**Corollary Σ2 (the `s = ½` family is dead in both forms).** For every `θ ∈ [0,1]` the family `w^{(θ)}_T` fails in

*Proof.* ¬RH: as in Thm B♯(a). Under RH: eq:fw is affine in `w`, since the coefficients of `w_1` and `w_2` in
`1 − ((1−θ)w_1+θw_2)cos τu` sum to one. So for `X` not a prime power,
`f_{w^{(θ)}} = (1−θ)B_T + θf^{R1}_T = (1−θ)[f♯_T − 2e^{−T}E(X^−)cos 2Tτ] + θf^{R1}_T`.
By G2_B4_R2 Lemma Z′ (record), `f^{R1}_T(τ) = Σ_γm_γŵ^{R1}(τ−γ)`, absolutely convergent, with
`ŵ^{R1}(x) = (2/(1+x²))[cos 2Tx + sin(2Tx)/x]` (G2_B4_R2 Lemma 3.2 at `ε = 1`; rechecked:
`2sin(2Tx)/x + 2Re[e^{2iTx}/(1−ix)]`). At `x = 3π/(4T)` this equals `−2/(x(1+x²)) = −(8/3π)T + O(1/T)`. For

`f^{R1}_T(τ_1) ≤ −(8/3π)m_1T + C` and `sup_{I_T}f^{R1}_T ≤ C`, with the same bounds for `f♯_T` (Cor H0, H1). Hence
`f_{w^{(θ)}}(τ_1) ≤ −(8/3π)m_1T + C + 2(1−θ)e^{−T}|E(X^−)|` and
`sup_{I_T}f_{w^{(θ)}} ≤ C + 2(1−θ)e^{−T}|E(X^−)|`.
In [compl], failure holds whenever `2e^{−T}|E| < (8/3π)m_1T − C`. In [prim], Lemma G gives failure whenever

all large `T` if `θ = 1`. ∎

*Reading (HEURISTIC).* Theorem R (G2_B4_R2) says that a `T`-uniform rule must carry the zero frequencies `e^{2iTγ}` in
its `T`-dependence. Prop E shows that the **edge part** of that encoding is legitimately prime-side: under RH,
`e^{−T}E(e^{2T}) = −Σ_ρ e^{2iTγ}/ρ + o(1)`, which is exactly an almost-periodic function with those frequencies. It is
computed from the primes below `e^{2T}`. Thm Σ shows that the rest cannot be carried by any small correction. The dip at
`γ_1 + 3π/4T` is the `γ_1`-resonance of `Σ_{n<X}Λ(n)n^{−½−iτ}`. Filling it with outside primes means continuing that
resonance past `X`, and by `thm:Z(ii)` that sharpens the kernel and deepens the dip.

## §7 Open steps

**O-1 (the lift; OPEN, EQUIV to RH).** For every `T ≥ T_0`, construct from the primes, without the zeros, a finite even
measure `λ_T` on `\{|u| ≥ 2T\}` with
```
f♯_T(τ) + λ̂_T(τ) ≥ 0 for all τ ∈ ℝ          [compl]
(or f♯_T + λ̂_T + (β_0−2)ĝ_0 + β_1ĝ_1 ≥ 0 for some β   [prim]),
```
where `f♯_T(τ) = Ar_T(τ) + 2∫_1^XE(t)∂_t[t^{−½}cos(τ log t)]dt` involves only the primes below `e^{2T}` (Prop C).
EQUIV: the dual feasible set is a vector space, and `ν = ν♯_T + λ_T` (Prop E; thm:D1 and its remark; cor:RH).
Necessary conditions, all proved here or on record:
* `λ̂_T(τ_1) ≥ (8/3π)m_1T − C_B` and `‖λ_T‖_TV ≳ T` (Thm Σ);
* `λ_T(ℝ) ≥ −f♯_T(0)`, and no new edge term may appear (Prop C0, Thm L);
* the resulting kernel must be non-resolving (G2_B4_R5 Cor N, u).

*What it would take:* a prime-side identity whose cosine transform dominates the negative part of the twisted inside
Chebyshev integral `2∫_1^XE\,∂_t[t^{−½}cos(τ log t)]dt` on `[0, τ_stop]`. It must produce a lift of height `≍ T` at
`γ + 3π/4T` for every ordinate `γ` below the resolution scale, without resonating at `γ` (§5 Reading). No identity of
this kind is known, and by `cor:zeros` any such identity is a statement about all zeros at once.

**O-2 (OPEN).** [prim] versions of Thm L′ and Cor L1 at arbitrarily large `T`, for the general jump-edge and fast-edge
classes. Cor Σ1 and Σ2 settle only bounded-mass families and the `s = ½` family. The obstacle: for a jump with a large
continuous part, `‖ν−ν♯‖_TV` is `≍ e^T`, so Thm Σ(ii) is empty. Lemma G then has to be combined with a twisted
Littlewood `Ω`-theorem at a fixed frequency `τ_1 ≠ 0`, uniformly with the phase window `I_T`.

**O-3 (OPEN).** Edge scales between the two regimes: monotone drops with `e^{−T} ≪ a_T` and `Ta_T = O(1)`. Cor L1 stops
at `a_T = O(e^{−T})`, and G2_B4_R5 Cor E1 starts at `Ta_T → ∞`. It would take an `Ω_+` theorem for the one-sided
smoothing `∫E\,dμ_T` at log-scale `a_T`. HEURISTIC: Littlewood's method, which aligns `N(Γ) ≍ Γ log Γ` ordinates with
`Γ ≍ log X/log log X`, loses nothing while `a_T = o(log T/T)`, and would then close O-3 in [compl].

**O-4 (record, OPEN).** `C^∞`-flat joins with `a_T/T` unbounded (G2_B4_R5 §4.3). Scale-free rules are `cor:RH` (EQUIV).

**Weakest steps.**
* Cor H1 rests on Ingham's `ψ₁` formula (CITED, not re-verified here) and on termwise integration, which is justified
  by absolute uniform convergence. The constant `C_1` is not tracked numerically, so `T*` in Thm B♯(a) is not effective.
* The density statements of Cor Σ1 and Σ2 rest on Cramér (CITED). The "arbitrarily large `T`" statements also need it
  in the RH branch, except Thm L, L′ and Cor L1, which need only Littlewood.
* Prop C0 needs regular tails (Def 1.5). Tails with jumps at prime powers are excluded only for convenience of the
  Stieltjes bookkeeping.
