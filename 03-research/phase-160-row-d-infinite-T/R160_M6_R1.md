# R160_M6_R1 — M6 (Maynard profile), round 1, front G2: self-scale evaluation in the Weil form

*Route (R160_002, row M6; brief 6.2.9): self-scale evaluation (Doc 153 Prop 153.6) and the missing prime side at
super-logarithmic adapted support (Rigidity 153.M′). Gates listed first: U-12, I-14. No file outside
this folder was edited. No sign is claimed and no label of record moves.*

**Headline.** Read in the complete Weil form, the self-scale route splits into two halves.

The *centered even* self-scale test of Doc 153 is the one frequency at which the Weil form stays **positive** under ¬RH
(Theorem C). Its "one-signed dominant excess" is the positive residue of a pole that is compatible with Landau's
theorem. The prime-side statement recorded as missing (Rigidity 153.M) is an **upper** bound on the Weil form. It is
**false for ζ under RH** (Theorem D), and its conditional repair 153.M′ is a restatement of Conjecture 144.D.

The *odd* self-scale test sees every off-line zero with a negative sign. Its dilation family detects at **every**
frequency γ₀ (Theorem B), and its prime side can be written exactly (Theorem E). This gives a one-parameter G2 target:
it uses the exact values of `Λ`, has zero margin, and fails when finitely many terms of the comb are deleted and also
when one term is amplified. The only step not proved is that target itself, which is EQUIV to RH (open step O-M6.1).

## §1 Definitions

**1.1 (P46, quoted; main.tex:115–145, 416–421, 756–766).** For `F ∈ L²(I_T)`: `F̂(τ) = ∫F(t)e^{iτt}dt`,
`c_F(u) = ∫F(t)\overline{F(t−u)}dt`, `Φ_F(s) = ∫F(t)e^{(s−½)t}dt`, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`, `N_T = {log n < 2T}`,
`A(F) = Φ_F(1)`, `B(F) = Φ_F(0)` for real `F`, and `W_T(F) = 𝔞_T(F) + 2Re(A(F)\overline{B(F)})` (eq:W). Lévy form
(`thm:A`): `𝔞_T(F) = C₀‖F‖² + 𝔈(c_F) − Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n Re c_F(log n)`, `𝔈(c) = ∫₀^∞(c(0) − Re c(u))K(u)du`,
`K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`, `C₀ = ψ(¼) − log π`. `lem:roughEF`: for real `F` with
`∫log(2+|τ|)|F̂|² < ∞` and `Σ_ρ|Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)| < ∞`, `W_T(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`.

**1.2 (complex tests).** For `F = F₁ + iF₂` (with `F_j` real) put `W_T(F) := W_T(F₁) + W_T(F₂)`. Since `σ_T` is even
and `F̂_j(−τ) = \overline{F̂_j(τ)}`, the cross terms of `(1/2π)∫σ_T|F̂|²` vanish, and
`Re(A\bar B) = A₁B₁ + A₂B₂`. Hence `W_T(F) = 𝔞_T(F) + 2Re(A(F)\overline{B(F)})` with the same formulas, and
`W_T(\bar F) = W_T(F)`. Under the two hypotheses of `lem:roughEF` for `F₁` and `F₂`, the zero side is

  `W_T(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)\overline{Φ_F(1−\barρ)}` (1.1)

because the imaginary cross sum `Σ_ρ[Φ_{F₂}(ρ)Φ_{F₁}(1−ρ) − Φ_{F₁}(ρ)Φ_{F₂}(1−ρ)]` vanishes under `ρ ↦ 1−ρ`.
`W_T(F)` does not depend on `T ≥` half the length of `supp F`, since `c_F(log n) = 0` for `log n ≥ 2T`. Write `W(F)`.
`W` is a real-linear functional of `c_F`:

  `W(F) = ℓ(c_F)`,  `ℓ(c) := C₀c(0) + 𝔈(c) − Σ_n v_n Re c(log n) + ∫_R c(u)·2cosh(u/2)du` (1.2)

## §3 Theorem A — the self-scale kernels and the parity dichotomy

**Theorem A (PROVED, [compl]).** For every `γ₀ ∈ R`, `L > 0`, both zero sums below converge absolutely, and

  `f⁺_{γ₀}(L) = Σ_ρ k⁺_L(ω_ρ)`,  `k⁺_L(ω) := 4sinh²(ωL/2)/ω²`;
  `f⁻_{γ₀}(L) = Σ_ρ k⁻_L(ω_ρ)`,  `k⁻_L(ω) := −16sinh⁴(ωL/4)/ω²`

(continuously extended at `ω = 0` by `L²` and `0`). Consequently:
(a) *(on-line zero, `ω = iv`)* `k⁺ = 4sin²(vL/2)/v² ≥ 0` and `k⁻ = 16sin⁴(vL/4)/v² ≥ 0`;
(b) *(off-line zero at the test frequency, `γ_ρ = γ₀`, `ω = δ ≠ 0` real)* `k⁺ = 4sinh²(δL/2)/δ² > L²` (the on-line
value) and `k⁻ = −16sinh⁴(δL/4)/δ² < 0`;
(c) *(the pole)* the polar term of eq:W is `k(½−iγ₀) + k(−½−iγ₀)` for the same kernel `k = k^±_L`;
(d) *(Doc 153)* for the quadruple `{½±δ±iγ₀}` with `κ = δL`, its contribution to `Σ_ρ h(ρ)` minus that of its
projection is `(4/L)[k⁺_L(δ) − L²] + (osc) = 8L[(cosh κ − 1)/κ² − ½] + (osc)`, where (osc) collects the two members at
`−γ₀` (with `ω = ±δ − 2iγ₀`). This is exactly the smooth term of Prop 153.6(b). **Its sign in `W` is `+`.**

*Proof.* `Φ_E(ρ) = ∫_{−L/2}^{L/2}e^{ω t}dt = 2sinh(ωL/2)/ω`. Also `1−\barρ − ½ = −\bar w_ρ`, so
`\overline{Φ_E(1−\barρ)} = \overline{2sinh((−\bar w−iγ₀)L/2)/(−\bar w−iγ₀)} = 2sinh(ωL/2)/ω`. Similarly
`Φ_O(ρ) = ∫_0^{L/2}(e^{ωt} − e^{−ωt})dt = 4sinh²(ωL/4)/ω` and `\overline{Φ_O(1−\barρ)} = −4sinh²(ωL/4)/ω`. Apply
(1.1). Summability: `|2sinh(ωL/2)/ω| ≤ min(Le^{|Re ω|L/2}, 2cosh(Re ω·L/2)/|ω|)` and `|Re ω_ρ| ≤ Θ`. Since
`Σ_ρ|ω_ρ|⁻² < ∞` (finitely many `ρ` with `|ω_ρ| < 1`), (b) of `lem:roughEF` holds for the real and imaginary parts,
and (a) holds because `|F̂(τ)| ≪ 1/|τ|`. (a), (b) are substitution. (c): `A = Φ(1)`, `B = Φ(0)` with `w = ±½`, and the
product rule above gives `A\bar B = k(½−iγ₀)`. (d): Prop 2.1(i), with `h = (1/L)(c_E + c_{\bar E})`. The members
`½±δ+iγ₀` have `ω = ±δ` and `k⁺_L(±δ) = 4sinh²(κ/2)L²/κ²`, and the projection replaces each by `L²`. So
`(2/L)·2[4sinh²(κ/2)L²/κ² − L²] = 8L[(cosh κ − 1)/κ² − ½]`. ∎

**Remark 3.1.** (c) is P47's "the pole is read like a zero" in self-scale coordinates. For odd tests the polar term is
`−2|A|² < 0`. This is P46's odd polar direction `ε_odd = −1` (`prop:polarcap`, `J = diag(2,−2)`): the odd self-scale
family lives in the sector where the polar capacity is negative.

## §4 Theorem B — Landau for the modulated and odd families

Put `H_{γ₀}(z) := (ξ'/ξ)(½+z+iγ₀) + (ξ'/ξ)(½+z−iγ₀)`, which is meromorphic on `C`.

**Theorem B (PROVED, [compl]).**
(i) For `Re z > Θ`, `𝓛f⁺_{γ₀}(z) = z⁻²H_{γ₀}(z)` and `𝓛f⁻_{γ₀}(z) = z⁻²[2H_{γ₀}(2z) − H_{γ₀}(z)]`, the integrals
converging absolutely.
(ii) *(odd detects at every frequency)* Fix any `γ₀ ∈ R`. If `f⁻_{γ₀}(L) ≥ 0` for all `L ≥ L₀`, then RH holds.
Conversely RH ⟹ `f⁻_{γ₀} ≥ 0` on `[0,∞)`. Hence **RH ⟺ ∀L > 0: W(O_{L,γ₀}) ≥ 0**, and under ¬RH `f⁻_{γ₀} < 0` at
arbitrarily large `L`.
(iii) *(even detects off the extremal ordinates)* If `f⁺_{γ₀} ≥ 0` on `[L₀,∞)`, then either RH holds or
`±γ₀ ∈ Ord_Θ`. For `γ₀ = 0` this is P47 Thm 17.14, since `0 ∉ Ord_Θ` (no real zero).
(iv) *(sub-doubling)* `f⁻_{γ₀}(L) = 4f⁺_{γ₀}(L/2) − f⁺_{γ₀}(L)`. Hence, for any fixed `γ₀`,
**RH ⟺ q_{γ₀}(2y) ≤ 2q_{γ₀}(y) for every y > 0**: the Rayleigh quotient of the modulated box at least doubles
nowhere.

*Proof.* (i) By Theorem A and Fubini: `Σ_ρ∫₀^∞|k^±_L(ω_ρ)|e^{−xL}dL ≤ C_x Σ_ρ|ω_ρ|⁻²` for `x > Θ`. Termwise, from
`4sinh²(y) = 2cosh 2y − 2` and `16sinh⁴(y) = 2cosh 4y − 8cosh 2y + 6`:
`∫₀^∞k⁺_L(ω)e^{−zL}dL = 2/(z(z²−ω²))` and
`∫₀^∞k⁻_L(ω)e^{−zL}dL = −(3/2)ω²/(z(z²−ω²)(z²−ω²/4)) = −(2/z)[1/(z²−ω²) − 1/(z²−ω²/4)]` (algebra checked
symbolically; `Re z > |Re ω|`). Next, `1/(z²−ω²) = (2z)⁻¹[(z−ω)⁻¹ + (z+ω)⁻¹]`, and the bracket is `O(|ρ|⁻²)`, so the
sum converges absolutely and equals its symmetric-truncation limit. Then `Σ_ρ(z−ω_ρ)⁻¹ = Σ_ρ((½+z+iγ₀) − ρ)⁻¹`.
Because the zero set is invariant under `ρ ↦ 1−ρ` (`w ↦ −w`), `Σ_ρ(z+ω_ρ)⁻¹ = Σ_ρ((½+z−iγ₀)−ρ)⁻¹`. Hadamard
(`ξ'/ξ(s) = Σ_ρ(s−ρ)⁻¹` in symmetric order) gives `Σ_ρ(z²−ω_ρ²)⁻¹ = H_{γ₀}(z)/(2z)`, and with `2z` in place of `z`,
`Σ_ρ(z²−ω_ρ²/4)⁻¹ = 4Σ_ρ((2z)²−ω_ρ²)⁻¹ = H_{γ₀}(2z)/z`. Substituting gives both formulas.

(ii) Put `f := f⁻_{γ₀}` and `M(z) := z⁻²[2H_{γ₀}(2z) − H_{γ₀}(z)]`.
*Pole structure.* `M` is meromorphic, with poles only in the discrete set `{0} ∪ {w_ρ ∓ iγ₀} ∪ {(w_ρ∓iγ₀)/2}`. For
`z₀ ≠ 0` let `N₁(z₀) := Σ{m_ρ : w_ρ − iγ₀ = z₀ or w_ρ + iγ₀ = z₀}` and `N₂(z₀) := N₁(2z₀)`. Since `ξ'/ξ` has residue
`m_ρ` at `ρ`, `2H_{γ₀}(2z)` has residue `N₂(z₀)` at `z₀` and `H_{γ₀}(z)` has residue `N₁(z₀)`. So

  `Res_{z₀}M = z₀⁻²(N₂(z₀) − N₁(z₀))`. (4.1)

*Suppose ¬RH, i.e. `Θ > 0`, and `f ≥ 0` on `[L₀,∞)`.* Put `f₁ := f·1_{[L₀,∞)} ≥ 0`. Then `𝓛f − 𝓛f₁` is entire, so
`𝓛f₁` continues to `M` minus an entire function, with the same singular points. If `Re z₀ > Θ/2`, then `N₂(z₀) = 0`,
because `Re(2z₀) > Θ` exceeds every `Re w_ρ`. Hence every zero with `Re w_ρ > Θ/2` produces a pole
`z₀ = w_ρ − iγ₀` of `M` with residue `−z₀⁻²N₁(z₀) ≠ 0`. These poles have real parts accumulating at `Θ` (or equal to
it). Since `𝓛f₁` is analytic on `Re z > σ_c`, we get `σ_c ≥ Θ`, and absolute convergence gives `σ_c ≤ Θ`, so
`σ_c = Θ`. By Landau's theorem, `z = Θ` is a singular point of `𝓛f₁`, hence a pole of `M` (the pole set is discrete).
Since `Θ > Θ/2`, (4.1) forces `N₁(Θ) > 0`: some zero satisfies `w_ρ ∓ iγ₀ = Θ`, i.e. `ρ = ½ + Θ ± iγ₀`, and
`Res_Θ M = −Θ⁻²N₁(Θ) < 0`. For real `x ↓ Θ` we then have `𝓛f₁(x) = M(x) − (entire)(x) → −∞`. But
`𝓛f₁(x) = ∫f₁e^{−xL} ≥ 0`. Contradiction. So `Θ = 0`. (If `Θ` is not attained, the contradiction arrives one step
earlier: `Θ` is not a pole.)
*Converse.* Under RH every `ω_ρ` is imaginary and Theorem A(a) gives `k⁻ ≥ 0` termwise.

(iii) Put `M⁺ := z⁻²H_{γ₀}(z)`. Its poles `z₀ ≠ 0` have residue `+z₀⁻²N₁(z₀)` and there is no cancellation. Under
¬RH and `f⁺ ≥ 0` eventually, the argument of (ii) gives `σ_c = Θ` and that `Θ` is a pole. That means
`ρ = ½+Θ±iγ₀` is a zero, i.e. `±γ₀ ∈ Ord_Θ`. The residue is now positive, so Landau raises no contradiction there.
(iv) By (1.2), (1.3) and real-linearity of `ℓ`, `W(O_{L,γ₀}) = ℓ(e^{−iγ₀u}(4Δ_{L/2} − Δ_L)) = 4W(E_{L/2,γ₀}) − W(E_{L,γ₀})`.
Divide by `L`: `f⁻ ≥ 0 ⟺ q(L) ≤ 2q(L/2)`. Apply (ii). (The Laplace form agrees:
`𝓛[f⁺(·/2)](z) = 2𝓛f⁺(2z)`.) ∎

## §5 Theorem C — the centered even family is blind, the centered odd family is not

**Hypothesis (H_iso)** at `γ₀ ∈ Ord_Θ`, with `ρ₀ = ½+Θ+iγ₀` of multiplicity `m₀`:
`S(γ₀) := Σ_{ρ∈Z_Θ, γ_ρ≠γ₀} 2/(Θ² + (γ_ρ−γ₀)²) < 2m₀/Θ²` (sum with multiplicity). If `Z_Θ` is a single
quadruple, this holds automatically (`S = 2m₀/(Θ²+4γ₀²)`).

**Theorem C (PROVED, as an implication; [compl]).** Assume ¬RH, `Θ` attained, and `γ₀ ∈ Ord_Θ`. Put
`A_∞(L) := Σ_{ρ∈Z_Θ} 2Re[e^{iv_ρL}/(Θ+iv_ρ)²]`, `v_ρ := γ_ρ − γ₀`. Then:
(i) `e^{−ΘL}f⁺_{γ₀}(L) − A_∞(L) → 0` and `e^{−ΘL}f⁻_{γ₀}(L) + A_∞(L) → 0` as `L → ∞`;
(ii) `A_∞` is Bohr almost periodic with mean `2m₀/Θ² > 0`. Hence `f⁺_{γ₀}(L) > (m₀/2Θ²)e^{ΘL}` on a set of `L` of
positive lower density, and `f⁻_{γ₀}(L) < −(m₀/2Θ²)e^{ΘL}` on the same set;
(iii) under (H_iso), with `κ := ½(2m₀/Θ² − S(γ₀)) > 0`: `f⁺_{γ₀}(L) ≥ κe^{ΘL}` and `f⁻_{γ₀}(L) ≤ −κe^{ΘL}` for all
large `L`. **At the self-scale frequency the even family is eventually positive and exponentially large under ¬RH.**

*Proof.* (i) Write `k⁺_L(ω) = (e^{ωL} + e^{−ωL} − 2)/ω²`. For `ρ ∈ Z_Θ`, the term `e^{ωL}/ω² = e^{ΘL}e^{iv_ρL}/(Θ+iv_ρ)²`.
Its mirror `ρ* = 1−\barρ` has the same ordinate, `ω = −Θ + iv_ρ`, and the term
`e^{−ωL}/ω² = e^{ΘL}e^{−iv_ρL}/(Θ−iv_ρ)²`, the complex conjugate. Together they give `e^{ΘL}·2Re[·]`. Every other
piece, multiplied by `e^{−ΘL}`, tends to `0` termwise (`|Re ω| < Θ`, or a decaying exponential), and is dominated by
`4/|ω_ρ|²`, which is summable. (A possible on-line zero at `ω = 0` contributes `e^{−ΘL}L² → 0`.) Dominated
convergence finishes the even case. For the odd case, `f⁻(L) = 4f⁺(L/2) − f⁺(L)` (B(iv)) and
`e^{−ΘL}f⁺(L/2) = O(e^{−ΘL/2}) → 0`.
(ii) `A_∞` is a uniformly convergent trigonometric series (`Σ_ρ(Θ²+v_ρ²)⁻¹ < ∞`). Only `v_ρ = 0` contributes to the
mean, which gives `2m₀/Θ²`. Also `sup|A_∞| ≤ 2m₀/Θ² + S =: M₁ < ∞`. For `P := {A_∞ > m₀/Θ²}`, averaging over
`[0,X]` gives `liminf|P∩[0,X]|/X ≥ (m₀/Θ²)/(M₁ − m₀/Θ²) > 0`. Combine with (i).
(iii) `A_∞(L) ≥ 2m₀/Θ² − S = 2κ` for every `L`. Combine with (i). ∎

**Corollary 5.1 (reading of Doc 153 Prop 153.6).** The "one-signed dominant excess" is Theorem A(d) with the sign `+`
in `W`. In Landau's picture it is the positive residue `+Θ⁻²N₁(Θ)` of a real pole of `z⁻²H_{γ₀}`, the one pole
Landau's theorem allows. So the centered even self-scale test is **exactly** the test that cannot witness
`λ^W_T < 0`, and the only contradiction it could supply comes from an *upper* bound on `W` (§6).

**Corollary 5.2 (the correct self-scale test is odd).** The centered odd test sees the same zero with the sign `−`,
exponentially (C(iii)), and by B(ii) it detects at every frequency. Adapting the frequency is unnecessary: `γ₀ = 0`
together with all `L` suffices.

**Remark 5.3 (141.B2).** Cor 141.B2 concerns countable families of support `L ≪ log γ`. The families `{O_{L,γ₀}}_L`
have unbounded support at a fixed frequency, and their detecting power sits in the super-log regime
`L ≳ log γ/δ`. This is how they get past 141.B2. Choosing the test *after* the configuration (Doc 153 Obs 153.6bis)
is not the mechanism.

## §8 The G2 construction attempt: the self-scale Landau programme, and where it stops

**The chain.**
1. (PROVED, Thm B(ii) at `γ₀=0`.) RH ⟺ `∀L: W(u_L) ≥ 0`, with `u_L` the odd box in `L²(I_{L/2})`.
2. (PROVED, Thm E.) `W(u_L) = B(L) − 2∫₀^L g ω_L`, with `B` elementary plus archimedean and `g` the normalized
   Chebyshev remainder.
3. (OPEN, O-M6.1.) Prove `2∫₀^L g ω_L ≤ B(L)` for every `L`, from the exact `Λ`.

Steps 1 and 2 use no zero information beyond the proofs of B and E, and step 3 is a statement about `Λ` alone. That
is the shape G2 asks for (F1–F3). Step 3 is EQUIV to RH, so the programme reformulates the problem in its sharpest
one-parameter form and does not solve it.

**Proposition F (PROVED; EQUIV; [compl]).** Put `G⁻(x) := 2x⁻²[2(ξ'/ξ)(½+2x) − (ξ'/ξ)(½+x)]`. For `x > ½`,

  `G⁻(x) = (2/x²)[R(x) + ψ(¼+x) − ½ψ(¼+x/2) − ½log π + Σ_nΛ(n)n^{−½}(n^{−x} − 2n^{−2x})]`,
  `R(x) = 2/(½+2x) + 2/(2x−½) − 1/(½+x) − 1/(x−½)`,

a convergent Euler/digamma expression. For one (every) `a > 0`: **RH ⟺ G⁻ is completely monotone on `(a,∞)`.**

*Proof.* `ξ'/ξ(s) = 1/s + 1/(s−1) − ½log π + ½ψ(s/2) + ζ'/ζ(s)` gives the display. Under RH, `f := f⁻_0 ≥ 0` is
bounded and `G⁻ = 𝓛f` on `(0,∞)` (Thm B(i)), so `G⁻` is completely monotone there. Conversely, by Bernstein's
theorem `G⁻(x) = ∫e^{−xt}e^{at}dμ(t)` for `x > a` with `μ ≥ 0`, and also `G⁻(x) = ∫f(t)e^{−xt}dt` for `x > Θ`. By
uniqueness of the Laplace–Stieltjes transform on a half-line, `f dt = e^{at}dμ ≥ 0`. So `f ≥ 0` (it is continuous),
and RH follows by B(ii). ∎

**Remark 8.1 (the self-scale as the Post–Widder scale).** By Post–Widder,
`f(L) = lim_k (−1)^k(k/L)^{k+1}G⁻^{(k)}(k/L)/k!`. So the sign at scale `L` is read from derivatives of order `k` at
