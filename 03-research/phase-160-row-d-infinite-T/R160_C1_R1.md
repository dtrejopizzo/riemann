## §0 Verdict

**Outcome.** I built the polarization the route asks for, in the widest class I could find: Riemann-surface laminations,
non-compact, fixed points and closed orbits allowed, no leafwise metric, a flow that is only leafwise holomorphic, and a
holonomy-invariant transverse measure scaled by `e^{−αt}`. Unitarity is derived, not postulated (Theorem P). I then
proved that this polarization **cannot carry ζ**. With Deninger's single pole it carries at most one conjugate pair of
eigenvalues (Theorem Z). The mechanism is new. Any L² eigenform `h` defines its own flat metric `|h|²` of finite trace
(Prop Φ). The ratio of any other eigenform to `h` is then forced to be leafwise constant, by a scaling argument that uses no
continuity, no integration by parts and no compactness (Theorem R). Other eigenvalues can therefore only come from
eigenfunctions of the flow on the leaf space, and each such eigenfunction also produces a pole. The route's premise fails
for a precise reason: the positive space is blind to every μ-null saturated set (Lemma N). The orbit topology can therefore
act only on the trace-formula side, never on the positive side. One residual survives, and I state it exactly: **Z1\***,
flows that preserve the Hodge decomposition of reduced L² cohomology but are not leafwise holomorphic up to leafwise
isotopy (§5).

## §1 Definitions

**1.1 Riemann-surface lamination.** `X` is a second-countable, locally compact Hausdorff space with an atlas of flow boxes
`U_i ≅ D × T_i`, where `D ⊂ C` is the unit disc and `T_i` is locally compact metrizable. Transition maps have the form
`(z,τ) ↦ (w_{ij}(z,τ), τ_{ij}(τ))`, with `w_{ij}` holomorphic in `z` and continuous in `(z,τ)`. Leaves are connected
Riemann surfaces. They need not be compact, complete, or of any conformal type.

**1.2 Forms.** A Borel leafwise `(1,0)`-form is, on `U_i`, `h = a_i(z,τ)dz` with `a_i` Borel, compatible on overlaps;
`(0,1)`-forms are conjugates. A leafwise density `β ≥ 0` is locally `b(z,τ)·(i/2)dz∧dz̄` with `b ≥ 0` Borel. `Ω^k_c` is the
space of compactly supported leafwise-`C^∞`, transversally continuous `k`-forms (inductive-limit `C⁰` topology).

**1.3 Transverse measure and integration.** `μ = (μ_i)` is a holonomy-invariant family of Radon measures on the `T_i`. For
Borel `β ≥ 0`, `C_μ(β) := Σ_i ∫_{T_i}∫_D χ_i b (i/2)dz∧dz̄ dμ_i(τ)`, for a Borel partition of unity `(χ_i)`. It does not
depend on the choices (CITED std., Candel–Conlon I §10.1). `𝔪 := [Leb_leaf ⊗ μ]` is the canonical measure class; "a.e."
refers to it. A saturated set is a union of leaves. For a holonomy-invariant (complex) Radon transverse measure `ν`, the
current `C_ν` on `Ω²_c` is continuous and vanishes on `dΩ¹_c` (Stokes on plaques; CITED std.). Such a current is called
*closed*.

**1.4 The class `𝓗_α` (α ∈ R∖{0}).** A tuple `(X,𝓕,J,φ,μ)` with 1.1–1.3 and a continuous flow `φ: R × X → X` such that
each `φ^t` maps every leaf onto a leaf, biholomorphically. Fixed points, closed orbits, non-transversality and
non-compactness are all allowed. It is assumed further that `φ^{t*}` preserves `Ω^k_c`, and that
> **(Sc_α)** `C_μ(φ^{t*}β) = e^{αt}C_μ(β)` for every Borel `β ≥ 0` and every `t`.

Since `C_μ∘φ^{t*} = C_{(φ^t)_*μ}` (change of variables plaque by plaque), and a transverse measure is determined by its
current, (Sc_α) is equivalent to `μ(φ^t τ) = e^{−αt}μ(τ)` for Borel transversals `τ`. It is also equivalent to (Sc_α) for
`β ∈ Ω²_c` alone. So class-level scaling of the trace is the same as pointwise scaling of `μ` (PROVED; the determination
of `μ` by `C_μ` is CITED std.).

**Remark 1.5 (containment).** For `d = 2`, DK_α (G1_B2_R4:86–99) ⊂ `𝓗_α`. A conformal, orientation-preserving map between
Riemann surfaces is holomorphic, and orientation is preserved because `t ↦ φ^t` is continuous from the identity
(G1_B2_R4 Thm H(iv)). (D2) is (Sc_α) with `α_d = α` (G1_B2_R4 Lemma 1.2(b)). Deninger's CM-elliptic system and Leichtnam's
laminations lie in `𝓗_{±1}`. In `𝓗_α` no leafwise metric is given, and the flow may distort any metric by a non-constant
factor.

**1.6 The polarized space.** `ℋ^{1,0}` is the set of Borel `(1,0)`-forms, holomorphic on every leaf outside an 𝔪-null
saturated set, with `‖h‖² := C_μ(i h∧h̄) < ∞`, modulo null forms. Its inner product is `⟨h,k⟩ := C_μ(i h∧k̄)`.
`ℋ^{0,1} := {h̄}`, `ℋ¹ := ℋ^{1,0} ⊕ ℋ^{0,1}`, and `Q(a,b) := C_μ(a∧b)`. In real leaf dimension 2, closed-and-coclosed
1-forms are exactly holomorphic ⊕ antiholomorphic (std.), so `ℋ¹` is the L² form of the Álvarez López–Kordyukov harmonic
space. `iQ(h,h̄) = ‖h‖²` is the Deninger–Singhof Hodge–Riemann form, and every `H¹` class is primitive when
`dim_C(leaf) = 1`. An *eigenform* is `0 ≠ h ∈ ℋ^{1,0}` with `φ^{t*}h = e^{ρt}h` for all `t`. For it,
`E_h := {x : h|_{L_x} ≢ 0}` is saturated and Borel. (In a flow box, `h ≢ 0` on the plaque over `τ` iff
`∫_D|a(·,τ)|² > 0`. Plaques of one leaf agree, by the identity theorem.)

**1.7 Leaf-space objects.** `L^∞(Sat)` is the algebra of bounded Borel functions constant on every leaf outside an 𝔪-null
saturated set, modulo null. It is the centre of Connes' foliation algebra (CITED std.). Define
`Γ_∞ := {δ ∈ R : ∃ f ∈ L^∞(Sat)∖0, f∘φ^t = e^{iδt}f ∀t}`.
* **(HE)** holonomy-ergodicity: every saturated Borel set is null or conull.
* **(1P)** every saturated Borel set `A` with `𝔪(A Δ φ^tA) = 0 ∀t` is null or conull.

(HE) ⟹ (1P). φ-ergodicity is used **nowhere**.

**1.8 Eigen-traces.** An eigen-trace of weight `λ ∈ C` is a nonzero closed current `C` on `Ω²_c` with
`C(φ^{t*}β) = e^{λt}C(β)` for all `t` and `β`. By (Sc_α), `C_μ` is one, of weight `α`. Closed currents factor through
`H̄²_c := Ω²_c/\overline{dΩ¹_c}`. Deninger's axiom "`H̄²` one-dimensional, `Θ = α`, trace `C_μ`" (DLS Thm 4.1 setting;
G1_B2_R5:103–113) implies that the only eigen-traces are the multiples of `C_μ`: this is "one pole".

**Lemma 1.9 (PROVED).** (a) The saturation of a `μ_i`-null Borel subset of `T_i` is 𝔪-null. (b) Under (HE), every Borel
function on a conull saturated set that is constant on every leaf outside an 𝔪-null saturated set is a.e. constant. Under
(1P) the same holds for such functions that are also a.e. φ-invariant.

*Proof.* (a) The saturation meets each `T_j` in a countable union of images under partial holonomy maps. There are
countably many, by second countability. Each image is null by holonomy invariance, so the saturation is 𝔪-null by
Fubini in the boxes. (b) Modify the function on the null saturated set so that it is leafwise constant everywhere. For
`c ∈ Q` the set `{F > c}` is saturated (and a.e. φ-invariant in the second case), hence null or conull. The standard
monotone argument gives `F = sup{c : {F>c} conull}` a.e. ∎

## §2 Theorem P: the polarization, without compactness

**Theorem P (PROVED) [other: ℋ¹].** Let `(X,𝓕,J,φ,μ) ∈ 𝓗_α`.
1. `ℋ^{1,0}` is a closed subspace of the separable Hilbert space `L²Ω^{1,0}(X;μ)`.
2. For each `t`, `φ^{t*}` maps `ℋ^{1,0}` bijectively onto itself, with `‖φ^{t*}h‖² = e^{αt}‖h‖²`. So
   `U_t := e^{−αt/2}φ^{t*}` is a strongly continuous unitary group on `ℋ^{1,0}`. The same holds on `ℋ^{0,1}`, and complex
   conjugation intertwines the two.
3. `Q(φ^{t*}a,φ^{t*}b) = e^{αt}Q(a,b)`. `Q` vanishes identically on `ℋ^{1,0}×ℋ^{1,0}` and on `ℋ^{0,1}×ℋ^{0,1}`, pointwise.
   `ℋ^{1,0} ⊥ ℋ^{0,1}`. For `h ∈ ℋ^{1,0}∖0`, `iQ(h,h̄) = ‖h‖² > 0` (Hodge–Riemann).
4. `φ^{t*} = e^{tΘ}` with `Θ = α/2 + iA` and `A` self-adjoint, so `σ(Θ|ℋ¹) ⊂ α/2 + iR`. Also
   `σ(Θ|ℋ^{0,1}) = \overline{σ(Θ|ℋ^{1,0})}`. Every eigenvalue satisfies `Re ρ = α/2` and `ρ̄ = α − ρ`.
5. Not used: compactness, completeness of leaves, any leafwise metric, absence of fixed points, transversality of `φ`,
   φ-ergodicity, (HE), `𝔪(X) < ∞`, integration by parts, continuity of forms, closed orbits and their germs, and any
   spectral or arithmetic datum.

*Proof.*
(1) In a box, `‖h|_U‖² = ∫_{T}∫_D|a|²·2dxdy dμ(τ)`. By Bergman's inequality, for `D′ ⋐ D` and `a(·,τ)` holomorphic,
`sup_{D′}|a(·,τ)|² ≤ C_{D′}∫_D|a(·,τ)|²dA`. Suppose `h_n → h` in `L²`. After passing to a subsequence,
`∫_D|a_n(·,τ)−a(·,τ)|² → 0` for `μ`-a.e. `τ` (Fubini). So `a_n(·,τ)` converges uniformly on `D′` and the limit is
holomorphic. Countably many boxes and Lemma 1.9(a) give holomorphy on every leaf outside a null saturated set.
Separability holds because `X` is second countable and the measures are Radon.

(2) `φ^t` is biholomorphic leaf to leaf, so `φ^{t*}h` is a Borel, leafwise-holomorphic `(1,0)`-form. Also
`i φ^{t*}h ∧ \overline{φ^{t*}h} = φ^{t*}(i h∧h̄)`, so (Sc_α) gives `‖φ^{t*}h‖² = e^{αt}‖h‖²`. The inverse is `φ^{−t*}`, and
`(φ^{s+t})^* = φ^{t*}φ^{s*}`. The map `t ↦ ⟨U_ta,b⟩ = e^{−αt/2}C_μ(iφ^{t*}a∧b̄)` is Borel, by joint measurability of `φ`
and Fubini. A weakly measurable unitary representation of `R` on a separable Hilbert space is strongly continuous
(von Neumann; CITED std.).

(3) Naturality `φ^{t*}(a∧b) = φ^{t*}a∧φ^{t*}b` and (Sc_α), extended to integrable complex densities by linearity. A
`(1,0)∧(1,0)` wedge vanishes pointwise. `⟨h,k̄⟩ = C_μ(h∧⋆k) = −iC_μ(h∧k) = 0`, using `⋆k = −ik` on `(1,0)`-forms. Finally
`i h∧h̄ = 2|a|²dx∧dy`.

(4) Stone's theorem gives `U_t = e^{itA}`, and then `φ^{t*} = e^{αt/2}U_t`. Conjugation is antiunitary and commutes with
the real flow `φ^{t*}`. ∎

**Remark 2.1.** DLS Thm 4.1 (Deninger) derives `Re ρ = α/2` from a pointwise conformal metric on a compact carrier.
Deninger–Singhof derive the polarization on compact Kähler–Riemann foliations through the Álvarez López–Kordyukov
decomposition. Theorem P is the version in the class the brief asks for (6.1.2). It is elementary; what it contributes is
the class. It passes OT-5′ *as a mechanism*: the form is built from `(J, μ)` before any spectrum, and its compatibility with
the flow is proved.

**Lemma N (null skeleton; PROVED) [other: ℋ¹].** Suppose a leaf `L` satisfies `φ^t(L) = L` for some `t ≠ 0`. This holds
for the leaf through a fixed point, and for `L_x` with `x` periodic of period `l`, since `φ^l(L_x) = L_{φ^lx} = L_x`. Then
`L` is 𝔪-null. For any 𝔪-null saturated φ-invariant Borel set `S` (for instance a countable union of such leaves),
`(X∖S,…) ∈ 𝓗_α` has the same `ℋ¹`, `U_t` and `Q`.

*Proof.* Suppose `μ` had an atom of mass `c > 0` at a point `x ∈ L` of some transversal. By holonomy invariance, every
point of `L` on every transversal has mass `c`. By (Sc_α), `μ({φ^tx}) = e^{−αt}c`, while `φ^tx ∈ L` has mass `c`. So
`e^{−αt} = 1`, contradicting `αt ≠ 0`. Hence `μ` has no atom on `L`. `L` meets each transversal in a countable set, so
`L` is null. The second claim holds because every object in 1.6 is defined modulo 𝔪-null saturated sets. ∎

## §3 The self-metric and ratio rigidity

**Proposition Φ (PROVED) [other: ℋ¹].** Let `h` be an eigenform with eigenvalue `ρ`.
* (a) `m_h(B) := C_μ(1_B·i h∧h̄)` is a finite Borel measure carried by `E_h`, with `m_h(X) = ‖h‖²`, and it is φ-invariant.
* (b) `g_h := |h|²` (locally `|dw|²`, where `h = dw`) is a flat leafwise metric on `E_h`, with cone points at the zeros of
  `h`, and `φ^{t*}g_h = e^{αt}g_h`. So `(E_h, g_h, φ, μ)` is a DK_α system of finite trace. In it `h` is parallel and
  `m_h = 2·vol_{g_h}⊗μ`.

*Proof.* (a) From `φ^{t*}h = e^{ρt}h` and `Re ρ = α/2` (P4): `φ^{t*}(1_B·ih∧h̄) = (1_B∘φ^t)e^{αt}ih∧h̄`. Then (Sc_α) gives
`e^{αt}m_h(φ^{−t}B) = e^{αt}m_h(B)`. (b) `φ^{t*}|h|² = |e^{ρt}h|² = e^{αt}|h|²`. ∎

So any holomorphic carrier becomes, on the support of each of its eigenforms, a finite-trace conformal carrier, whatever its
own trace is. This is why the finite-trace mechanism of G1_B3_R5 Thm F survives at infinite trace. Its hypotheses (R) and
(IBP) fail at the cone points and transversally, however, so I do not invoke it. Theorem R replaces its Bochner step by a
direct scaling argument.

**Theorem R (ratio rigidity; PROVED) [other: ℋ¹].** Let `h, h′` be eigenforms with eigenvalues `ρ, ρ′`. There is a Borel
function `F` on `E_h`, constant on every leaf outside an 𝔪-null saturated set, with
* `h′ = F·h` a.e. on `E_h`;
* `F∘φ^t = e^{(ρ′−ρ)t}F` a.e., for every `t`;
* `∫|F|²dm_h = ‖1_{E_h}h′‖² < ∞`.

*Proof.* On each leaf `L ⊂ E_h` where `h`, `h′` are holomorphic, `h|_L ≢ 0` has isolated zeros. So `F_L := h′|_L/h|_L`
is a meromorphic function (a ratio of sections of the line bundle `T^{*1,0}L`). This defines `F` 𝔪-a.e. on `E_h`, and
`h′ = Fh`.

*Equivariance.* Holomorphic maps act `C`-linearly on `(1,0)`-covectors, so pull-back preserves ratios. From
`φ^{t*}h′ = e^{ρ′t}h′` and `φ^{t*}h = e^{ρt}h` we get `F∘φ^t = e^{(ρ′−ρ)t}F`. This holds a.e. for each `t`, and on a.e. leaf
everywhere, by the identity theorem.

*The scaling function.* Put `G := dF/h`. It is the ratio of the meromorphic `(1,0)`-form `dF` to `h`, a meromorphic
function on each leaf of `E_h`, finite off a discrete set. So `|G| < ∞` `m_h`-a.e. Since `d` commutes with `φ^{t*}`,
`φ^{t*}dF = d(F∘φ^t) = e^{(ρ′−ρ)t}dF`. Hence `G∘φ^t = e^{(ρ′−2ρ)t}G` and `|G|∘φ^t = e^{−αt/2}|G|`, using
`Re ρ = Re ρ′ = α/2` (P4). (In Prop Φ's language, `|G| = |∇F|_{g_h}`: the gradient of the ratio, measured in the
self-metric.)

*Scaling kills it.* Set `N(s) := m_h{|G| > s}` for `s > 0`. Then `N(s) ≤ ‖h‖² < ∞`, and `N` is nonincreasing. For every
`t`, `φ^{−t}{|G|>s} = {|G| > e^{αt/2}s}`, and `m_h` is φ-invariant (Prop Φ(a)), so `N(s) = N(e^{αt/2}s)`. Since `α ≠ 0`,
every `s′ > 0` has the form `e^{αt/2}s`, so `N` is constant on `(0,∞)`. And `N(s) → m_h{|G| = ∞} = 0` as `s → ∞`. So
`N ≡ 0`, i.e. `G = 0` `m_h`-a.e.

*Conclusion.* `m_h` is equivalent to `𝔪` on `E_h`, because `|h|² > 0` off a discrete set on each leaf. So `dF = 0` a.e. on
every leaf of `E_h` outside a null saturated set (Fubini, Lemma 1.9(a)). `F` is meromorphic and `L` is connected, so `F` is
constant on each such leaf. Finally, `∫|F|²dm_h = C_μ(1_{E_h}i h′∧h̄′)`. ∎

The ratio gradient scales exactly as curvature does in G1_B3_R5 Lemma 2.1. The self-metric supplies the finite invariant
measure that the scaling argument needs, at any trace. No hypothesis (C), (IBP), (R), (D), (FT), (Sm)/(Orb), φ-ergodicity,
compactness or completeness enters.

## §4 The pole–zero coupling law and its consequences

**Theorem Z (pole–zero coupling; PROVED) [other: ℋ¹].** Let `(X,𝓕,J,φ,μ) ∈ 𝓗_α`.
* (a) *(poles from leaf-space eigenfunctions)* For `δ ∈ Γ_∞` with eigenfunction `f`, `C_{fμ}` is an eigen-trace of weight
  `α − iδ`. Eigen-traces `C_{fμ}` with distinct `δ` are linearly independent.
* (b) *(zeros from leaf-space eigenfunctions)* If `h` is an eigenform with eigenvalue `ρ`, then the eigenvalues of `Θ` on the
  eigenforms supported in `E_h` are exactly `ρ + iΓ_h`, with multiplicities preserved. Here `Γ_h` is the point spectrum of
  the Koopman flow `F ↦ F∘φ^t` on `L²(E_h, Sat, m_h)`.
* (c) *(one pole on the invariant part)* Assume (1P). Then `σ_p(Θ|ℋ^{1,0})` is empty or equals `ρ + iΓ_∞`, every eigenvalue
  is simple, and `Γ_∞` is a subgroup of `R`. Eigen-traces of weight `α + iΓ_∞` then exist by (a), since `Γ_∞ = −Γ_∞`.
  **So the L²-zeros and the poles on the line `Re = α` come in the same vertical translates.**
* (d) *(Deninger's single pole)* Suppose every eigen-trace of the form `C_{fμ}`, `f ∈ L^∞(Sat)`, is a multiple of `C_μ`.
  This holds, for instance, when `dim H̄²_c = 1`. Then (1P) holds, `Γ_∞ = {0}`, and `σ_p(Θ|ℋ^{1,0})` has at most one point,
  of multiplicity one. Hence `σ_p(Θ|ℋ¹) ⊆ {ρ, ρ̄}`, with each simple.

*Proof.*
(a) `f` is leafwise constant, so `fμ` is a holonomy-invariant complex Radon transverse measure, and `C_{fμ}` is closed and
continuous. Its weight:
`C_{fμ}(φ^{t*}β) = C_μ(φ^{t*}((f∘φ^{−t})β)) = e^{αt}C_μ((f∘φ^{−t})β) = e^{(α−iδ)t}C_{fμ}(β)`.
It is nonzero: fix a continuous positive leafwise density `β₀` (from any continuous leafwise metric and a partition of
unity). Then `g ↦ C_{fμ}(gβ₀)` is the nonzero measure `f·(β₀⊗μ)`. Independence: finitely many eigenvectors of the
transposed operator `(φ^{t*})^T`, for one `t` at which the values `e^{(α−iδ)t}` are distinct, are linearly independent.

(b) Every eigenform supported in `E_h` is `F h`, with `F` as in Theorem R, i.e. a Koopman eigenfunction in
`L²(E_h,Sat,m_h)`. Conversely, if `F` is such an eigenfunction (eigenvalue `iδ`), then `Fh` is leafwise holomorphic with
`‖Fh‖² = ∫|F|²dm_h < ∞`, and it is an eigenform with eigenvalue `ρ+iδ`. The correspondence `F ↦ Fh` is linear and
injective, so multiplicities agree.

(c) `E_h` is saturated and φ-invariant, hence conull under (1P). By Theorem R, any eigenform `h′` is `Fh`. `|F|` is
leafwise constant and a.e. φ-invariant, so `|F| = c` a.e. (Lemma 1.9(b)). If `h′ ≠ 0` then `c > 0`, so `F ∈ L^∞(Sat)` and
`γ′−γ ∈ Γ_∞`. Conversely `f ∈ L^∞(Sat)` gives the eigenform `fh`. Simplicity: if `F₁h`, `F₂h` have the same eigenvalue,
then `F₁/F₂` (with `|F₂| = c₂ > 0`) is leafwise constant and φ-invariant, hence constant. Group: under (1P) eigenfunctions
have constant modulus, so products and conjugates are again eigenfunctions (as in G1_B10_R4 Thm K(ii), l.123–137).

(d) If `A` is a saturated, a.e.-invariant set with `0 < 𝔪(A)` and `𝔪(X∖A) > 0`, then `C_{1_Aμ}` is an eigen-trace of weight
`α` (by (a) with `δ = 0`). It is not a multiple of `C_μ`, because the measures `1_A(β₀⊗μ)` and `β₀⊗μ` are not
proportional. So (1P) holds. If `0 ≠ δ ∈ Γ_∞`, then `C_{fμ}` has weight `α−iδ ≠ α`, so it is not a multiple of `C_μ`. So
`Γ_∞ = {0}`, and (c) gives one point. Conjugation gives `ℋ^{0,1}` (P2, P4). Finally, if `dim H̄²_c = 1`, then every closed
current is a multiple of the functional induced by `C_μ` (assuming `C_μ ≠ 0` on `H̄²_c`, which holds since `C_μ(β₀) > 0`
for a compactly supported `β₀`). ∎

## §5 What survives, and the construction attempt

**5.1 The escapes left open by Theorem Z.**
* **Z1\*.** The flow is not leafwise holomorphic, even up to leafwise isotopy, yet it preserves the Hodge decomposition of
  reduced L² cohomology. This is the class-level relaxation of Deninger and Leichtnam (G1_B3_R5:218).
* **Z2.** The zeros sit in leafwise classes of infinite polarization norm (resonant, distributional). Theorem P then gives
  nothing, and positivity would have to be transferred from another Hilbert space. That is the configuration of Connes'
  absorption picture (A17, EQUIV) and of the Selberg model (Ex 4.4), where the transfer goes through a self-adjoint
  Laplacian. For ζ on `SL₂(Z)\H` this is the dead route §5.13 (the `β = ¼` gap *is* RH).
* **Z3.** Leaves of complex dimension `g ≥ 2`. With `φ^{t*}[ω] = e^{αt}[ω]` and Hard Lefschetz `L^{g−1}: H̄¹ ≅ H̄^{2g−1}`
  (D–S Prop 4.3, CITED), the eigenvalues on `H̄^{2g−1}` are those on `H̄¹` plus `(g−1)α`. So the Lefschetz formula acquires
  terms `e^{(ρ+(g−1)α)t}` that ζ's formula does not have (PROVED as algebra; its relevance is HEURISTIC, since cancellations
  in infinite dimension are not excluded here).
* **Z4.** `α = 0` (isometric flows). Number theory needs `α = 1` (C5, R158_001:158).

**5.2 Theorem S (non-holomorphic flows: the functional equation is free; RH is Hodge invariance) (PROVED) [other: ℋ¹].**
Let `𝓗^{qc}_α` be defined as `𝓗_α`, except that each `φ^t` is only a leafwise `C¹` quasiconformal diffeomorphism with
locally bounded dilatation. The L² Hodge–Kodaira decomposition
`L²Ω¹ = ℋ¹ ⊕ \overline{dΩ⁰_c} ⊕ ⋆\overline{dΩ⁰_c}` holds. It is conformally invariant, since `⋆` on 1-forms is conformal.
Orthogonality comes from `⟨du,⋆dv⟩ = −C_μ(d(u dv̄)) = 0`, and harmonicity from Weyl's lemma leafwise (std.). The closed
forms are `Z¹ = ℋ¹ ⊕ \overline{dΩ⁰_c}`. Let `P` be the projection `Z¹ → ℋ¹` and `V_t := Pφ^{t*}|ℋ¹`.
* (a) `V_t` is a bounded group, and `Q(V_ta,V_tb) = e^{αt}Q(a,b)`. `Q` is bounded and non-degenerate on `ℋ¹`.
* (b) For every `t`, `σ(V_t) = e^{αt}·σ(V_t)^{−1} = \overline{σ(V_t)}`. This is the functional-equation symmetry
  `ρ ↔ α − ρ`, `ρ ↔ ρ̄`, at the level of spectra. The partner of a point eigenvalue may lie in the residual spectrum.
* (c) An eigenvector `a` with `Re ρ ≠ α/2` satisfies `Q(a,ā) = 0`. So it lies in neither `ℋ^{1,0}` nor `ℋ^{0,1}`, on which
  `iQ(·,·̄)` is definite of opposite signs.
* (d) `V_t(ℋ^{1,0}) ⊆ ℋ^{1,0}` ⟺ `C_μ(ν_t·(φ^{t*}h)^{1,0}⊗k) = 0` for all `h, k ∈ ℋ^{1,0}`. Here `ν_t` is the leafwise
  Beltrami differential of `φ^t` (with respect to `J`), paired with the integrable quadratic differential
  `(φ^{t*}h)^{1,0}⊗k`. When this holds, `e^{−αt/2}V_t` is unitary on `ℋ^{1,0}`, and conclusions P2–P4 hold for `V`.

*Proof.* (a) `φ^{t*}` preserves `Z¹` and `\overline{dΩ⁰_c}`, since `φ^{t*}du = d(u∘φ^t)`. So `V_{t+s} = V_tV_s` on `ℋ¹`.
If `b ∈ Z¹`, weak closedness says `C_μ(b∧dv) = 0` for `v ∈ Ω⁰_c`, and by continuity (`|Q(a,b)| ≤ ‖a‖‖b‖`) `Q` kills
`Z¹ × \overline{dΩ⁰_c}`. Hence `Q(V_ta,V_tb) = Q(φ^{t*}a,φ^{t*}b) = e^{αt}Q(a,b)`, by naturality and (Sc_α). For
non-degeneracy, take `a = h + k̄` and `b = h̄ − k` (`h, k ∈ ℋ^{1,0}`). Then `Q(a,b) = −i(‖h‖²+‖k‖²)` and `‖b‖ = ‖a‖`, so
`ι: b ↦ Q(·,b)` satisfies `‖ι(b)‖ ≥ ‖b‖`.
(b) From (a), `Q(V_ta,b) = e^{αt}Q(a,V_{−t}b)`, i.e. `V_t^T∘ι = e^{αt}ι∘V_{−t}`. `ι` is injective, bounded below and has
dense range (the same estimate applied to `ι^T`), so it is an isomorphism `ℋ¹ → (ℋ¹)*`. Hence
`σ(V_t) = σ(V_t^T) = e^{αt}σ(V_{−t}) = e^{αt}σ(V_t)^{−1}`. Conjugation commutes with `V_t`.
(c) `Q(V_ta,V_tā) = e^{2Re ρ t}Q(a,ā) = e^{αt}Q(a,ā)`.
(d) `V_th ∈ ℋ^{1,0}` ⟺ `⟨φ^{t*}h,k̄⟩ = 0` for all `k ∈ ℋ^{1,0}` (since `P` is self-adjoint) ⟺ `C_μ(φ^{t*}h∧k) = 0` ⟺
`C_μ((φ^{t*}h)^{0,1}∧k) = 0`. In a chart with `w = φ^t(z)` and `h = a(w)dw`,
`(φ^{t*}h)^{0,1} = a(w)w_{z̄}dz̄ = ν_t·a(w)w_zdz̄`, where `ν_t = w_{z̄}/w_z`. ∎

So in this class the functional equation is Poincaré duality plus trace scaling, and the Riemann hypothesis *for the
carrier* is the `V`-invariance of the Hodge decomposition. That is Theorem P's hypothesis, and Theorem Z then bounds the
result. The reading is the Kreĭn picture of record (dead routes §5.2), made geometric. I do not offer it as a lever.

**Proposition 5.3 (the isotopy-trivial part of Z1 obeys Theorem Z; PROVED under (H_iso)) [other: ℋ¹].** Let
`φ ∈ 𝓗_α`. Let `Ψ` be a leaf-preserving homeomorphism, leafwise `C¹` quasiconformal, such that `Ψ^*a − a` and

the identity). Put `φ′^t := Ψφ^tΨ^{−1}`. Then `φ′ ∈ 𝓗^{qc}_α`, and `V′_t = V_t` on `ℋ¹ = ℋ¹_J`. So Theorem Z(c)–(d) holds
for `V′`.
*Proof.* `Ψ` fixes every leaf, so `Ψ_*μ = μ` (holonomy invariance), and `φ′` satisfies (Sc_α). On `Z¹` modulo
`\overline{dΩ⁰_c}`, `(Ψ^{−1})^*φ^{t*}Ψ^* ≡ φ^{t*}`. ∎

**5.4 The construction problem that remains (the exact specification).** Combining P, R, Z, S and 5.3, a Deninger-type
carrier for ζ with Riemann-surface leaves, one pole, and its zeros on a polarized space must have a flow in **Z1\***. Such a
flow:
* (i) preserves the Hodge decomposition of reduced L² cohomology (Theorem S(d));
* (ii) is **not** conjugate to a leafwise-holomorphic flow by leafwise isotopies with (H_iso) (Prop 5.3);
* (iii) keeps a holonomy-invariant `μ` with (Sc_1), `(1P)` and `Γ_∞ = {0}` (one pole);
* (iv) has `σ_p(V|ℋ^{1,0})` infinite.

Condition (i)–(ii) says: the generator's Beltrami differential `ν̇` annihilates every product `h·k` of L² holomorphic
1-forms, yet is not `∂̄` of an admissible leafwise vector field. In Teichmüller language, the flow is infinitesimally
Torelli-trivial and Teichmüller-nontrivial along the leaves. So the lamination must **fail leafwise infinitesimal Torelli**:
the products `ℋ^{1,0}·ℋ^{1,0}` are not dense in the integrable holomorphic quadratic differentials. (This reading is
HEURISTIC. The implication "density ⟹ `ν̇` is `∂̄`-exact ⟹ (H_iso)-conjugacy" needs a lamination version of Teichmüller's
lemma, which I have not proved.) I tried to build such a flow on the three explicit carriers available: the CM solenoid, the
horospherical frame flow of Ex 4.4, and dense linear foliations of tori.
* In the first two, `ℋ^{1,0} = dz·L²(Sat)`. (HEURISTIC: leafwise-holomorphic L² functions on a dense-leaf solenoid or on
  `Γ\G` along `N` are leafwise constant, by the Fourier or representation-theoretic description; I have not written this
  out.) So (i) reduces to constraints `∫ν̇·F dz² dμ = 0` with `F ∈ L²(Sat)`. Every flow I could write down that satisfies
  them is either holomorphic up to isotopy (Prop 5.3), or leaves the spectrum of `V` equal to that of the holomorphic flow,
  so (iv) fails.
* In the third, L² harmonic forms have constant coefficients, and (i) forces complex-linearity.

I have **no candidate** for Z1\*. It is also the only place where B2's first candidate, "the leafwise Hodge projection and
its position relative to θ", can still act.
