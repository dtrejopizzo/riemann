## §1 Definitions

**1.1 The integral shadow (Paper 44 §6bis, CITED).** For `n ≥ 1`, `Sh_Z(Frob_n) = [L_S →^{n} L_S]` in degrees `−1,0`,
`H^0 = Z/n`, `deg_det Sh_Z(Frob_n) = log n`; exact triangles `Sh_Z(Frob_m) → Sh_Z(Frob_{mn}) → Sh_Z(Frob_n) →` (6bis.2);
Möbius cofiber `Prim_n ≅ [Z →^{Φ_n(1)} Z]`, `deg_det Prim_n = Λ(n)`; `contact_fin(Frob_n) = n^{−½}deg_det Prim_n =
Λ(n)/√n` (Thm 6bis.4). Unimodularity: `Frob_n ⋆ Frob_n^† = Δ^{(n)}` (Thm 3ter.6(ii)).

**1.2 The global Weil form.** For real `F` in the Schwartz class `𝒮(R)` put
```
W(F) := C_0‖F‖² + 𝔈(c_F) − Σ_{n≥2} v_n c_F(log n) + 2A(F)B(F),   𝔈(Φ) := ∫_0^∞ (Φ(0) − Φ(u))K(u)du,
```
and `W(F,G) := ½(W(F+G) − W(F) − W(G))`. Since `c_F` of `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` vanishes outside `[−2T,2T]`, `W = W_T` there
(P47 `eq:W`, `thm:A`), and `W = 𝔞_T` on `P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`. The comb sum converges absolutely on `𝒮(R)`
(`|c_F(u)| ≤ C_N(1+|u|)^{−N}`, `v_n ≤ 2 log n/√n`). By Weil's explicit formula (CITED: Weil 1952; Bombieri 2000 §1;
Iwaniec–Kowalski Thm 5.12, hypotheses met by Gaussian-type tests), for `F` such that `Φ_F` is entire with
super-exponential decay on vertical lines,
```
W(F) = Σ_ρ m_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ),   W(F,G) = Σ_ρ m_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ)   (absolutely convergent).        (1.1)
```

**1.3 Shifts and Tate moments.** `(τ_aF)(t) := F(t−a)`. Then `Φ_{τ_aF}(s) = e^{(s−½)a}Φ_F(s)`, `A(τ_aF) = e^{a/2}A(F)`,
`B(τ_aF) = e^{−a/2}B(F)`, `c_{τ_aF,τ_aG} = c_{F,G}`.

**1.4 The heat kernel of the flow.** `G_s(t) := (2πs²)^{−½}e^{−t²/(2s²)}`, `s>0` (`= e^{s²∂_t²/2}δ_0`; `∫G_s = 1`);
`γ_s(v) := c_{G_s}(v) = (4πs²)^{−½}e^{−v²/(4s²)}`; `Φ_{G_s}(σ) = e^{s²(σ−½)²/2}`; `a_s := A(G_s) = B(G_s) = e^{s²/8}`.

**1.5 The lattice and its completion.** `Z[Γ] = Z^{(Γ)}` (finitely supported integer functions on `Γ`), with the
shift action `S_n δ_r = δ_{nr}`. For `s > 0`, `G_r := τ_{log r}G_s` (`r ∈ Γ`), `F_c := Σ_r c_r G_r`,
`𝓛_s := {F_c : c ∈ Z[Γ]} ⊂ 𝒮(R)`, `V_s := 𝓛_s ⊗ R`. The **lattice form** is `Q_s(c) := W(F_c)`, the **kernel**
`w_s(u) := W(G_s, τ_uG_s)`, and the **Dirichlet polynomial** of `c` is `D_c(σ) := Σ_r c_r r^{σ−½}`, so that
`Φ_{F_c}(σ) = e^{s²(σ−½)²/2}D_c(σ)`, `A(F_c) = a_sD_c(1)`, `B(F_c) = a_sD_c(0)`.

**1.6 Positive-definiteness on a group.** A function `w: Γ → R` is positive-definite if `Σ_{r,r'} c_r c_{r'} w(r/r') ≥ 0`
for all `c ∈ Z[Γ]` (equivalently `R[Γ]`, equivalently `C[Γ]` when `w(r) = w(r^{−1})`). Such `w` satisfies
`|w(r)| ≤ w(1)`.

## §2 Theorem A — the lattice of the integral shadow

**Theorem A.** (i) Let `𝒦` be the Grothendieck group of finite abelian groups, generated by the `H^0Sh_Z(Frob_n) = Z/n`
with the relations of the exact triangles (6bis.2) (which are the short exact sequences `0→Z/m→Z/mn→Z/n→0`). Then
`𝒦 = ⊕_p Z·[Z/p]`, `[Z/n] = Σ_p v_p(n)[Z/p]`, and `n ↦ [Z/n]` extends uniquely to a group isomorphism `Γ → 𝒦`,
`n/m ↦ [Z/n]−[Z/m]`. Under it `deg_det` becomes `log: Γ → R`, an injective homomorphism.
(ii) For `n ≥ 2`, `Φ_n(1) = N_{Q(ζ_n)/Q}(1−ζ_n)`; hence `Prim_n = [Z →^{N(1−ζ_n)} Z]` and `Λ(n) = log|N_{Q(ζ_n)/Q}(1−ζ_n)|`.
(iii) `log Γ` is dense in `R`. Consequently, for every `Γ`-invariant symmetric form `q` on `Z[Γ]` whose kernel
`w(r) = q(δ_1, δ_r)` extends continuously to `1` along `log Γ ⊂ R`, `q(δ_r − δ_{r'}) = 2(w(1) − w(r/r')) → 0` along
sequences `r/r' → 1`, so `(Z[Γ], q)` is not a discrete subgroup of any inner-product space carrying `q`.

*Proof.* (i) `K_0` of finite abelian groups with all short exact sequences is free on the simple objects `Z/p`
(Jordan–Hölder), and `Z/n` has composition factors `Z/p` with multiplicity `v_p(n)`; the relations (6bis.2) are among
those sequences and already generate all relations between the `[Z/n]` (`[Z/mn] = [Z/m] + [Z/n]`). `deg_det Sh_Z(Frob_n)
= log n = Σ v_p(n) log p` (Paper 44 Thm 6bis.3(iii)). `Γ ≅ ⊕_p Z` by unique factorization, and `log` is injective on
`Γ` because `Σ a_p log p = 0` with `a_p ∈ Z` forces `∏ p^{a_p} = 1`. (ii) `Φ_n(x) = ∏_{(k,n)=1}(x − ζ_n^k)` and the
`ζ_n^k` are the conjugates of `ζ_n`, so `Φ_n(1) = ∏_k (1−ζ_n^k) = N(1−ζ_n)`; `|Φ_n(1)| = p` if `n = p^k` and `1`
otherwise (standard), i.e. `e^{Λ(n)}`, which is Paper 44 Thm 6bis.3(v) read as a norm. (iii) `log((n+1)/n) → 0`, so
`log Γ` is a subgroup of `R` that is not cyclic, hence dense. The rest is the displayed identity. ∎

*What is new.* (i) is the record's degree additivity (Thm 6bis.3(iii)) upgraded to a `K_0` identification, which is
what "a lattice from `Sh_Z`" means canonically: **the lattice is `Γ = Q_{>0}^×`, and `deg_det` is `log`**. (ii) is
elementary but not in Paper 44 (grep `cyclotomic`, `1−ζ`). (iii) is the precise form of O1 for this lattice.

## §3 Theorem B — the heat completion and the kernel `w_s`

**Theorem B.** Fix `s > 0`.
(i) `Q_s(c) = Σ_{r,r'∈Γ} c_r c_{r'} w_s(log(r/r'))` for `c ∈ Z[Γ]`; every value is finite.
(ii) (arithmetic side) For `u ∈ R`,
```
w_s(u) = C_0γ_s(u) + ∫_0^∞ [γ_s(u) − ½(γ_s(u+v) + γ_s(u−v))]K(v)dv
         − Σ_{n≥2} (v_n/2)[γ_s(u − log n) + γ_s(u + log n)] + 2a_s² cosh(u/2),                 (3.1)
```
an even real-analytic function; no zero of ζ enters (3.1).
(iii) (zero side, CITED explicit formula) `w_s(u) = Σ_ρ m_ρ e^{s²(ρ−½)²} cosh((ρ−½)u)`, absolutely and locally uniformly
convergent for `u ∈ C`; `w_s` is entire. Moreover
`Q_s(c) = Σ_ρ m_ρ e^{s²(ρ−½)²} D_c(ρ)D_c(1−ρ)`.
(iv) (isometry) `W(τ_aF, τ_aG) = W(F,G)` for all `a ∈ R`; in particular `Q_s(S_nc) = Q_s(c)` and `S_n` is an isometry of
`(Z[Γ], Q_s)` for every `n`. Under RH, `w_s(u) = Σ_γ m_γ e^{−s²γ²}cos(γu)` is the Fourier transform of a positive
finite measure and `Q_s ≥ 0`.

*Proof.* (i) `W` is bilinear on `𝒮(R)`, `W(G_r, G_{r'}) = W(τ_{log r}G_s, τ_{log r'}G_s) = W(G_s, τ_{log(r'/r)}G_s)` by
(iv), and `w_s` is even by (ii). (ii) Polarize the four terms of `W` with `F = G_s`, `G = τ_uG_s`: `c_{F,G}(v) = γ_s(u+v)`,
`‖·‖`-term `C_0γ_s(u)`, Lévy term as displayed (the quadratic `𝔈` polarizes to `∫_0^∞[Φ(0) − ½(Φ(v)+Φ(−v))]K`), comb
term `−Σ v_n·½[γ_s(u−log n)+γ_s(u+log n)]`, polar term `A(F)B(G)+B(F)A(G) = a_s²(e^{−u/2}+e^{u/2})`. Each term is
real-analytic in `u` (Gaussian sums with super-exponential decay in `log n`; `K` integrable against the Gaussian
differences, which vanish to second order at `v = 0`). (iii) Insert `Φ_{G_s}(ρ)Φ_{τ_uG_s}(1−ρ) =
e^{s²(ρ−½)²/2}e^{s²(½−ρ)²/2}e^{(½−ρ)u}` into (1.1) and symmetrize over `ρ ↔ 1−ρ`. Convergence: `|e^{s²(ρ−½)²}| ≤
e^{s²/4}e^{−s²γ²}`, `|cosh((ρ−½)u)| ≤ e^{(½+|γ|)|u|}`, `N(T) = O(T log T)`. The formula for `Q_s` follows from (1.1)
and `Φ_{F_c} = Φ_{G_s}D_c`. (iv) `c_{τ_aF,τ_aG} = c_{F,G}`, `‖τ_aF‖ = ‖F‖`, and `A(τ_aF)B(τ_aG) = e^{a/2}e^{−a/2}A(F)B(G)`.
Under RH, `ρ−½ = iγ` and `cosh(iγu) = cos γu`; Bochner gives positive-definiteness on `R`, hence on `Γ`. ∎

*Remarks.* (a) `G_s` is the heat kernel of the flow generator `∂_t` in the coordinate `t = log|x|`, **not** Tate's
archimedean Gaussian `e^{−πx²}`; the completion is canonical only in the sense that it is the flow's own semigroup
applied to the point classes, `𝓛_s = e^{s²∂_t²/2}·Z[Γ]δ`, and it commutes with the Frobenius shifts. (b) (iv) is
(D3) of Lemma T in half-density normalization (the bidegree product `1` of Paper 44 Thm 3ter.6(iii)); the same
invariance holds for the `Ξ_a`-form (its zero set is `ρ ↦ 1−ρ` symmetric), so **invariance is not the discriminating
datum; the edge residues of Thm F are.** (c) The space `𝓛_s ⊗ C` with the Hermitian form is a Kreĭn space in which
each off-line quadruple contributes one hyperbolic plane (S-7, 115_17 §8.5): nothing here contradicts the record.

## §4 Theorem C — the lattice criterion, and the Laplace lemma (Theorem Q)

**Lemma L (Laplace).** Let `(λ_k)` be distinct complex numbers with `|λ_k| → ∞`, `a_k ∈ C∖{0}` with
`Σ_k |a_k|(1+|λ_k|)^{−1} < ∞`, and suppose `Re λ_k ≤ Λ_0` for all `k`. Put `f(u) := Σ_k a_k e^{λ_k u}` (absolutely
convergent for `u ≥ 0`). If `f` is bounded on `[0,∞)`, then `Re λ_k ≤ 0` for every `k`.

*Proof.* `L(z) := ∫_0^∞ f(u)e^{−zu}du` is analytic on `{Re z > 0}` since `f` is bounded. For `Re z > Λ_0`, Fubini
(`Σ|a_k|/(Re z − Re λ_k) < ∞`) gives `L(z) = Σ_k a_k/(z − λ_k) =: M(z)`. The series `M` converges locally uniformly on
`C ∖ {λ_k}` (as `|z−λ_k| ≥ ¼(1+|λ_k|)` for `|z| ≤ ½|λ_k|`, `|λ_k| ≥ 1`, so the tail is dominated by `4Σ|a_k|/(1+|λ_k|)`), so `M` is meromorphic on `C`
with a simple pole of residue `a_k ≠ 0` at each `λ_k`. `L − M` is analytic on the connected open set
`{Re z > 0} ∖ {λ_k}` (a discrete set removed from a half-plane) and vanishes on the open subset
`{Re z > max(Λ_0,0)}`, hence identically; so `M = L` is analytic on `{Re z > 0}`, and
no `λ_k` lies there. ∎

**Theorem C (lattice criterion).** Fix `s > 0`. The following are equivalent: (a) RH; (b) `Q_s(c) ≥ 0` for all
`c ∈ Z[Γ]`; (c) `w_s` is positive-definite on the discrete group `Γ`; (d) `w_s` is bounded on `log Γ`; (e) `w_s` is
bounded on `[0,∞)`.

*Proof.* (a)⟹(b): Thm B(iv). (b)⟺(c): §1.6 with `w = w_s∘log` and Thm B(i). (c)⟹(d): `|w(r)| ≤ w(1)`.
(d)⟹(e): `w_s` is continuous (entire) and `log Γ` is dense (Thm A(iii)), so `sup_R |w_s| = sup_{log Γ}|w_s|`; `w_s`
is even. (e)⟹(a): by Thm B(iii), `w_s(u) = Σ_ρ m_ρ e^{s²(ρ−½)²}e^{(ρ−½)u}` on `u ≥ 0` (before symmetrization),
which is of the form of Lemma L with `λ_ρ = ρ − ½` (distinct, `Re λ_ρ ≤ ½`, `|λ_ρ| → ∞`) and
`a_ρ = m_ρ e^{s²(ρ−½)²} ≠ 0`, `Σ|a_ρ|(1+|λ_ρ|)^{−1} < ∞`. Lemma L gives `Re ρ ≤ ½` for every zero; the symmetry
`ρ ↦ 1−ρ` of the zero set then gives `Re ρ = ½`. ∎

**Theorem Q (closing Q-M of G2_B10_R3).** Let `T_0 > 0` and let `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T_0})` be real. Put
`β_F(u) := W(F, τ_uF) = Σ_ρ m_ρ c_ρ e^{(ρ−½)u}`, `c_ρ := Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)` (absolutely convergent; this is G2_B10_R3
Thm B(iii)'s shift function). If `β_F` is bounded on `[0,∞)` — in particular if `β_F` is positive-definite on `R`
or on `Γ` — then every zero `ρ` with `c_ρ ≠ 0` satisfies `Re ρ = ½`. Consequently `B_∞(T_0)` (G2_B10_R3 Thm B)
implies RH for every `T_0 > 0`, with no dominant-zero hypothesis: **Q-M is answered affirmatively.**

*Proof.* Absolute convergence: `Φ_F(β+iγ)` is the Fourier transform at `γ` of `F(t)e^{(β−½)t} ∈ C_c^∞`, hence
`O_N((1+|γ|)^{−N})` uniformly in `0 ≤ β ≤ 1`, against `N(T) = O(T log T)`. Apply Lemma L to the terms with `c_ρ ≠ 0`
(`λ_ρ = ρ−½`, `a_ρ = m_ρc_ρ`); it gives `Re ρ ≤ ½` for those, and `c_ρ = c_{1−ρ}` with the symmetry of the zero set
gives `Re ρ = ½`. For the consequence: if `ρ_0` is an off-line zero, then `{F real : Φ_F(ρ_0) = 0}` and `{F real : Φ_F(1−ρ_0) = 0}`
are proper real subspaces of `C_c^∞(I_{T_0})` (each is the common kernel of the real and imaginary parts of a
nonzero functional: the kernels `e^{±(ρ_0−½)t}` are nonzero continuous functions on `I_{T_0}`), and a real vector
space is not the union of two proper subspaces, so some real `F` has `c_{ρ_0} ≠ 0`; `B_∞(T_0)` makes `β_F` positive-definite on `Γ` (G2_B10_R3 (iii)), hence bounded, contradicting the
first part. ∎

*What Theorems C and Q give beyond the §3.5 list.* (1) The converse direction of a Toeplitz/positive-definite
reformulation of Weil positivity had been proved in the record only under a dominant off-line zero (G2_B10_R3
Thm B(iv)); Lemma L removes that hypothesis, because analyticity of a Laplace transform in a half-plane is
insensitive to which pole is dominant. (2) For the heat completion the criterion is a statement about **one entire
function on a countable discrete group**: `w_s|_Γ`. (3) Positive-definiteness on `Γ` is an *arithmetic* statement (a

## §5 Theorem E — the primitive sublattice and the square classes

**Theorem E.** Write each `r ∈ Γ` uniquely as `r = q_r² d_r` with `d_r ≥ 1 squarefree` and `q_r ∈ Q_{>0}`; `d_r` is the
image of `r` in `Γ/Γ² ≅ ⊕_p F_2`. Let `P^{prim} := {c ∈ Z[Γ] : A(F_c) = B(F_c) = 0} = {c : D_c(1) = D_c(0) = 0}`.
(i) `P^{prim} = ⊕_{d} P_d`, `P_d := {c supported on the coset `{q²d}` : Σ_r c_r q_r = 0 = Σ_r c_r/q_r}`.
(ii) `S_n P_d = P_{d'}` with `d' ≡ nd` in `Γ/Γ²`; so the Frobenius action permutes the square-class summands through
`Γ → Γ/Γ²`, and preserves each summand iff `n` is a square.
(iii) [prim] `RH ⟺ Q_s ≥ 0 on P^{prim} ⟺ Q_s ≥ 0 on the cyclic Z[Γ]-module Z[Γ]·c^♯`, where
`c^♯ := 2δ_1 − 5δ_4 + 2δ_{16} ∈ P_1` and `s > 0` is arbitrary.

*Proof.* (i) `D_c(1) = Σ c_r √r = Σ_d √d·(Σ_{d_r = d} c_r q_r)` and `D_c(0) = Σ c_r/√r = Σ_d √d·(Σ_{d_r = d} c_r/(q_r d))`;
the inner sums are rational, and the square roots of distinct squarefree integers are linearly independent over `Q`
(Besicovitch 1940, CITED), so both vanish iff every inner sum vanishes. (ii) `q_{nr}² d_{nr} = n q_r² d_r`.
(iii) `⟹`: RH gives `Q_s ≥ 0` on all of `Z[Γ]` (Thm B(iv)). Second `⟸` (the middle statement is sandwiched): the
shifts preserve `ker A ∩ ker B` (§1.3), so `Z[Γ]c^♯ ⊂ P^{prim}` and, if `Q_s ≥ 0` on `Z[Γ]c^♯`, the function
`β(u) := W(F_{c^♯}, τ_uF_{c^♯})` is positive-definite on `Γ`, hence bounded on `log Γ`, hence (entire, by the zero-side
formula `β(u) = Σ_ρ m_ρ e^{s²(ρ−½)²}D_{c^♯}(ρ)D_{c^♯}(1−ρ)e^{(ρ−½)u}`, absolutely convergent) bounded on `[0,∞)`.
Now `D_{c^♯}(σ) = 2 − 5x + 2x²` with `x = 4^{σ−½}`, which vanishes iff `x ∈ {2, ½}` iff `σ ∈ {0,1} + (πi/log 2)Z`;
no nontrivial zero has `Re ρ ∈ {0,1}`, so `D_{c^♯}(ρ)D_{c^♯}(1−ρ) ≠ 0` for **every** zero, and `c^♯ ∈ P_1` since
`2·1 − 5·2 + 2·4 = 0 = 2 − 5/2 + 2/4`. Lemma L (with `a_ρ = m_ρe^{s²(ρ−½)²}D_{c^♯}(ρ)D_{c^♯}(1−ρ) ≠ 0`) gives
`Re ρ ≤ ½` for all zeros, hence RH by symmetry. ∎

*What is new.* The Tate moments `A, B` on the `Sh_Z` lattice are the values `D_c(1), D_c(0)`, i.e. integer relations
among `√r`; Besicovitch converts the two archimedean constraints into a **grading of the primitive lattice by the
square classes of `Q^×`**, on which Frobenius acts through the Kummer quotient `Γ/Γ²`. (iii) is a one-generator
criterion for the primitive form with a dyadic generator; it is EQUIV, but its proof is self-contained (no density
lemma, no Paper 45 Thm 10.4), which is why it is recorded. It says nothing about `𝔊_T ≻ 0` per window (OT-11): the
