## §1 Definitions

**1.1 Transforms.** For `F∈L²(I_T;C)` put `F̂(z)=∫F(t)e^{izt}dt`, an entire function. Then `Φ_F(s)=F̂(−i(s−½))`,
`A(F)=F̂(−i/2)`, `B(F)=F̂(i/2)`, and `P_T={F̂(±i/2)=0}`. Write `F̂^#(z):=\overline{F̂(z̄)}`, which is the transform of
`\overline{F(−t)}`. Parity is `(ιF)(t)=F(−t)`. The form domain is `𝒟_T:={F∈L²(I_T;C): ∫log(2+|τ|)|F̂|²<∞}`.

**1.2 Window data.** A *window datum* is a pair `𝔡=(ν,Π)` where:
* `ν` is a real, even Radon measure on `R` with `∫|F̂|²d|ν|<∞` for `F∈𝒟_T`: typically `(σ/2π)dτ` with
  `|σ|≤C log(2+|τ|)`, or finitely many atoms;
* `Π⊂C∖R` is finite with `Π=Π̄=−Π`, carrying real weights `c_a=c_{ā}=c_{−a}`.

Its complete form is `W_𝔡(F,G):=∫F̂\overline{Ĝ}\,dν+Σ_{a∈Π}c_aF̂(a)\overline{Ĝ(ā)}` on `𝒟_T`. It is hermitian, real
(`W_𝔡(F̄)=W_𝔡(F)`) and ι-invariant.

For ζ take `ν=(σ_T/2π)dτ` (`eq:symbol`), `Π={±i/2}` and `c=1`. Then `W_𝔡=W_T` (`eq:W`), because
`F̂(−i/2)\overline{F̂(i/2)}+F̂(i/2)\overline{F̂(−i/2)}=2Re(A\bar B)`; on `P_T` it is `𝔞_T`.

Other data of the same shape:
* the comb models `Λ1_{n≥N}`, `Λ+cδ_{n₀}` and `a_θ`, and `Λχ₂`: change the comb in `σ` and keep `Π`;
* `Ξ_a`: its explicit-formula symbol, with `Π={±i(½±a)}`;
* DH and Epstein: their own Γ-symbols and polar sets;
* the zero-side form `Σ_{ρ∈Z}Φ_F(ρ)\overline{Φ_F(1−\barρ)}` of a finite symmetric multiset `Z`: here `ν` is a sum of
  atoms at the real `γ_ρ`, and `Π` collects the off-line pairs.

**1.3 Moves.** For `w∈C∖R` let `b_w(z)=(z−w̄)/(z−w)`, so `|b_w|=1` on `R`. If `F̂(w)=0`, the move `M_wF` is defined by
`(M_wF)^=b_wF̂`. A test is *real-rooted* if `F̂` has no non-real zero. A test in `P_T` is *primitively real-rooted* if
`F̂/(¼+z²)` has no non-real zero. For `F∈P_T` the *free* non-real zeros are those of `F̂` with one copy each of `±i/2`
removed.

**1.4 Admissible data and ground spaces.** `𝔡` is *admissible* (on `𝒟_T`, or on `P_T∩𝒟_T`) if its form is closed,
semibounded and has compact resolvent there. For ζ both `W_T` and `𝔞_T|P_T` are admissible (record, §0.4).

Put `λ(𝔡):=inf W_𝔡(F)/‖F‖²`, and let `E_C` be the set of minimisers together with `0`. By the standard variational
argument `E_C` is the bottom eigenspace: `F` is a minimiser iff `W_𝔡(F,·)=λ⟨F,·⟩`. The form is real, so
`E_C=E⊗C` with `E` real, and `E=E^ev⊕E^odd` by ι-invariance. For real `F₁,F₂` one has
`W(F₁+iF₂)=W(F₁)+W(F₂)`: the `ν`-cross term is odd in `τ` against an even `ν`, and the polar cross terms cancel
between `a` and `−a` (`Π=−Π`); for ζ this is R160_M6_R1 §1.2.

**1.5 First contact.** `T_c:=sup{T>0: λ^W_T>0}∈(0,∞]`. When `T_c<∞`, put `E_c:=E(W_{T_c})`. A *witness* is a nonzero
real parity-definite element of `E_c`. At the primitive first contact `T_*` of phase 121, `E_*:=ker(𝔞_{T_*}|P_{T_*})`.

## §2 Theorem B (Blaschke invariance). PROVED, [compl] [prim]

**Theorem B.** Let `𝔡` be a window datum at `T`, let `F∈𝒟_T`, and let `w∈C∖R` with `F̂(w)=0`. Put `G:=M_wF`. Then
`G∈𝒟_T` (in particular `supp G⊂[−T,T]`), `‖G‖=‖F‖` and `W_𝔡(G)=W_𝔡(F)`. If moreover `F∈P_T` and `w` is a free zero,
then `G∈P_T`, and `𝔞_T(G)=𝔞_T(F)`.

*Proof.*
1. *`G` is a Paley–Wiener function.* `Ĝ=b_wF̂` is entire, because the zero of `F̂` cancels the pole. For
   `|z−w|≥|Im w|` one has `|b_w(z)|≤3`; on the disc `|z−w|≤|Im w|` the maximum principle bounds `Ĝ` by its values on
   the circle. Hence `|Ĝ(z)|≤C e^{T|Im z|}`. On `R`, `|Ĝ|=|F̂|`, so `Ĝ|_R∈L²` with the same norm. By Paley–Wiener
   (CITED), `Ĝ` is the transform of some `G∈L²(I_T)` with `‖G‖=‖F‖`. The form-domain integral is also unchanged.
2. *The `ν`-part* depends only on `|F̂|²` on `R`, so it is unchanged.
3. *The polar terms.* Take `a∈Π∖{w,w̄}`. Since `\overline{b_w(ā)}=(a−w)/(a−w̄)`,
   `Ĝ(a)\overline{Ĝ(ā)} = F̂(a)\overline{F̂(ā)}·b_w(a)\overline{b_w(ā)} = F̂(a)\overline{F̂(ā)}`.
   If `a=w`, the old term vanishes (`F̂(w)=0`) and so does the new one (`Ĝ(w̄)=F̂(w̄)b_w(w̄)=0`). If `a=w̄`, both
   vanish, because `F̂(w)=0` and `Ĝ(w̄)=0`.
4. *The primitive case.* `Ĝ(±i/2)=b_w(±i/2)F̂(±i/2)=0` when `w∉{±i/2}`. If `w` is an extra copy of `±i/2`, the order
   of vanishing drops by one there and rises at the conjugate point, so `Ĝ(±i/2)=0` still holds. On `P_T`,
   `W_T=𝔞_T`. ∎

*Remark B.1 (class-level).* The proof uses only `|b_w|=1` on `R` and `Π=Π̄`. So B holds for every datum of §1.2: ζ,
the comb models, `Λχ₂`, `Ξ_a`, DH, Epstein, and the zero-side forms of any `Z`. It is de Branges' axiom (H2) (§6) and
carries no sign.

## §3 Theorem R (parity-crossing rigidity). PROVED, [compl] [prim]

**Lemma S (span).** Let `z_1,…,z_k∈C∖R`, repetitions allowed. For `S⊂[k]` put
`P_S:=Π_{j∈S}(z−z̄_j)Π_{j∉S}(z−z_j)`. Then `span{P_S}=Poly_{≤k}`.

*Proof.* Let `V_k` be the span; induct on `k`. Splitting `S` by whether `k∈S` gives
`V_k=(z−z_k)V_{k−1}+(z−z̄_k)V_{k−1}`. With `V_{k−1}=Poly_{≤k−1}` this contains `(z̄_k−z_k)Poly_{≤k−1}` and `z·Poly_{≤k−1}`; since `z_k≠z̄_k`, it is all of `Poly_{≤k}`. ∎

**Theorem R.** Let `𝔡` be admissible and ι-invariant on `𝒟_T` (resp. on `P_T∩𝒟_T`), with bottom eigenspace
`E_C=(E^ev⊕E^odd)⊗C`.
* **(a)** Let `F∈E_C∖0`, and let `z_1,…,z_k` be a sub-multiset of the non-real (resp. free non-real) zeros of `F̂`.
  Put `Q:=Π_j(z−z_j)`. Then `F̂P/Q∈Ê_C` for every `P∈Poly_{≤k}`. Hence `dim_C E_C≥k+1`, and `F̂` has only finitely many
  non-real zeros.
* **(b)** If `F∈E^ev` has `k` non-real (resp. free) zeros, then `k` is even, `dim E^ev≥k/2+1` and `dim E^odd≥k/2`. For
  `F∈E^odd` the same holds with the parities exchanged.
* **(c)** Every nonzero `E^par` contains a real-rooted (resp. primitively real-rooted) element.
* **(d)** If `E^odd=0`, every element of `E` is real-rooted; the same holds if `E^ev=0`.
* **(e)** (Connes–van Suijlekom's case.) If `dim E=1`, the ground state is real-rooted.

*Proof.*
* **(a)** For `S⊂[k]` let `G_S:=F̂·Π_{j∈S}b_{z_j}=F̂P_S/Q`. We show `G_S∈Ê_C` by induction on `|S|`. For `j∉S'`, the
  function `G_{S'}` vanishes at `z_j`: its order there is at least `ord_{z_j}F̂−#{i∈S': z_i=z_j}≥1`, since the
  multiset uses `z_j` at most `ord_{z_j}F̂` times. By Theorem B, `M_{z_j}` preserves `W_𝔡` and the norm, so it maps
  minimisers to minimisers, hence `E_C` into `E_C`. So `G_{S'∪{j}}∈Ê_C`. Lemma S gives
  `span{G_S}={F̂P/Q: P∈Poly_{≤k}}`. The map `P↦F̂P/Q` is injective, so `dim_C E_C≥k+1`. Since `dim E_C<∞`, `k` is
  bounded.
* **(b)** `F` is real and even, so `F̂^#=F̂` and `F̂(−z)=F̂(z)`. Its zeros are therefore closed under `z↦−z` and `z↦z̄`,
  with multiplicities. Take `{z_j}` to be all `k` non-real (free) zeros. Then `k` is even, because `z≠−z` for
  `z∉R`. The polynomial `Q` is even and has real coefficients. For real `P`, `(F̂P/Q)^#=F̂P/Q`, which is the transform
  of some `G` with `\overline{G(−t)}=G(t)`.
  * If `P` is even, `G` is even, hence real: `G∈E^ev`.
  * If `P` is odd, `G` is odd and `\overline G=−G`, so `G=iG_r` with `G_r∈E^odd` real.
  The real even polynomials of degree `≤k` form a space of dimension `k/2+1`; the real odd ones, of dimension `k/2`.
  Injectivity gives the two bounds. For odd `F` one has `F̂^#=−F̂`, and the same bookkeeping exchanges the parities.
* **(c)** With `F` and `Q` as in (b), take `P=Π_{i≤k/2}(z²−t_i²)` with `t_i` real. Then `F̂P/Q∈Ê^{par}` has only real
  zeros (apart from the forced `±i/2` in the primitive case).
* **(d)** If `E^odd=0` and `F∈E^ev` had `k≥2`, then (b) would give `dim E^odd≥1`, which is false. So `k=0`.
* **(e)** Part (a) gives `k+1≤1`. ∎

*Remark R.0.* Ground states with non-real zeros exist only when the bottom is **degenerate across parities**, with
multiplicity growing with the number of such zeros.

**Corollary R1 (CvS without simplicity; CCM's step E1).** For ζ let `μ₊(T)` and `μ₋(T)` be the bottoms of `W_T` on
even and on odd functions, so that `λ^W_T=min(μ₊,μ₋)` (P47 l.495–499; Bombieri 2000 Thm 5, CITED). If `μ₊(T)<μ₋(T)`,
every ground state of `W_T` is even and real-rooted, whatever its multiplicity; `μ₋<μ₊` gives the same with "odd".
CCM's zeta spectral triples take their ground state at `T=log λ` from `W_T`, whose trace form equals CCM's
(brief §7 D12). Their real-rootedness step (Doc 99 §3.3) therefore needs only **E1′: `μ₊≠μ₋`**, not E1 ("simple and
even"). The implication is PROVED; E1′ is OPEN (OS-2).

**Corollary R2.** `λ^W_T=min{W_T(F)/‖F‖²: F≠0 real-rooted}` and
`λ^A_T=min{𝔞_T(F)/‖F‖²: F∈P_T∖0 primitively real-rooted}`. Hence RH ⟺ `∀T: W_T≥0` on real-rooted tests. PROVED; the
equivalence is EQUIV. *Proof:* Theorem R(c) applied to the parity that attains the bottom. ∎

## §4 The first contact

**Lemma FC0. PROVED; the equivalence is EQUIV (= O4 of R157_004). [compl]**
* **(i)** Under RH, `W_T≥0` and `ker W_T=0` for every `T`, so `λ^W_T>0`.
* **(ii)** Under ¬RH, `T_c∈[0.005,∞)`, `λ^W_T>0` for `T<T_c`, `λ^W_{T_c}=0` is attained, `E_c` is nonzero and

## §5 Theorem G (the Gauss-node form of the first contact). PROVED, [compl]; [prim]

Write the sesquilinear complete form as `W(G,H)=∫Ĝ(p)Ĥ^#(p)dμ(p)` with `μ:=(σ_{T_c}/2π)dτ+δ_{−i/2}+δ_{i/2}`. At the
point masses, `Ĝ(−i/2)Ĥ^#(−i/2)=A(G)\overline{B(H)}` and `Ĝ(i/2)Ĥ^#(i/2)=B(G)\overline{A(H)}`.

**Theorem G.** Assume ¬RH. Let `F∈E_c` be real, and let `x,y` be distinct real zeros of `F̂`. Define the node tests by
`Ĥ_x:=F̂/(z−x)`. Then:
* **(a)** `H_x∈𝒟_{T_c}` and `W(F,H_x)=0`.
* **(b)** `W(H_x,H_y)=0` for `x≠y`.
* **(c)** `d_x:=W(H_x)≥0`, and `d_x=0` for at most `dim E_c−1` zeros `x`.
* **(d)** For a finite combination `G=Σc_xH_x`, `W(G)=Σ_xd_x|c_x|²`. At a simple zero `c_x=Ĝ(x)/F̂'(x)`, so
  `W(G)=Σ_xw_x|Ĝ(x)|²` with `w_x:=d_x/|F̂'(x)|²`.
* **(e) Hilbert law.** At every real zero `x`,
  `(1/2π)∫σ_{T_c}(τ)|F̂(τ)|²/(τ−x)\,dτ = 2A(F)B(F)\,x/(x²+¼)`.

At the primitive first contact `T_*`, (a)–(d) hold for `𝔞_{T_*}` on `P_{T_*}`, and (e) holds with right side `0`.

*Proof.*
* **(a)** `Ĥ_x` is entire of type `≤T_c`. It satisfies `|Ĥ_x|≤|F̂|` for `|τ−x|≥1` and is bounded near `x`, so
  `H_x∈𝒟`. For `P_{T_*}`: `Ĥ_x(±i/2)=0`. Since `F` lies in the kernel, `W(F,·)=0` on `𝒟`.
* **(b)** For real `x`, `Ĥ_x^#=F̂^#/(z−x)`. Hence
  `Ĥ_x(p)Ĥ_y^#(p)=[F̂F̂^#(p)/(p−x)−F̂F̂^#(p)/(p−y)]/(x−y)`,
  and integrating against `μ` gives `W(H_x,H_y)=[W(H_x,F)−W(F,H_y)]/(x−y)=0` by (a). The integrand is dominated by
  `|Ĥ_x||Ĥ_y|`, a product of two form-domain functions.
* **(c)** `W_{T_c}≥0` gives `d_x≥0`. If `d_x=0`, then `H_x` is a minimiser, so `H_x∈E_c`. The functions `F` and
  `{H_x: d_x=0}` are linearly independent, because `1` and the `1/(z−x)` are. This bounds their number by
  `dim E_c−1`.
* **(d)** Follows from (b). At a simple zero, `Ĥ_y(x)=0` for `y≠x` and `Ĥ_x(x)=F̂'(x)`.
* **(e)** Write (a) out. Since `B(H_x)=B(F)/(i/2−x)` and `A(H_x)=A(F)/(−i/2−x)`, the polar part of `W(F,H_x)` is
  `A\bar B/(−i/2−x)+B\bar A/(i/2−x)`. For real `A,B` this equals `−2ABx/(x²+¼)`. ∎

*Remark G.1 (the Kreĭn reading).* By (d), on the node span the degenerate window form *is* the positive discrete
measure `Σ_xw_xδ_x`, applied to `|Ĝ|²`. This is the determinate case of `def:KEP` at `λ^W=0`, and its support is the
witness's real zero set, of density `T_c/π`. By `lem:roughEF` the same form equals `Σ_ρΦ_G(ρ)\overline{Φ_G(1−\barρ)}`,
which contains the off-line zeros. So at a first contact the window would see ζ's zero measure through a real,
positive quadrature. The identity on the span is PROVED; whether the span is dense is OS-3. The weighted node density
`(T_c/π)·w_x` matches Riemann–von Mangoldt, `(1/2π)log(x/2π)`, at heights where `σ_{T_c}≈m`. This last point is
HEURISTIC (it is only the Plancherel–Pólya scale; no computation).

## §6 The de Branges reading (D), Schur vectors (H), boosts on `H¹` (Q)
