## §1 Definitions

**1.1 (the prime measure and the Gaussian correlation; Doc 152 Def 152.1, renormalised).** `μ := Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n) n^{−½} δ_{log n}`,
a positive measure on `(0,∞)` with `μ((0,u]) = Σ_{log n ≤ u} Λ(n)n^{−½} ≍ e^{u/2}` (Chebyshev, CITED); it is **not** tempered.
For `θ > 0`, `τ ∈ R`:
`S(θ,τ) := ∫ e^{−θu²} cos(τu) dμ(u) = Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n) n^{−½} e^{−θ (log n)²} cos(τ log n)`,
absolutely convergent. With `θ = 1/(4T²)` this is `S_T(τ)` of Doc 152 exactly. `ω(τ) := limsup_{T→∞} T^{−2} log(2+|S_T(τ)|)`
(Doc 152). **LP-152(τ₀)** (Doc 152 Def 152.8): `|S_T(τ₀)| = O_ε(e^{εT²})` for every `ε > 0`, i.e. `ω(τ₀) = 0`.

**1.2 (zeros).** `ρ = β_ρ + iγ_ρ` runs over the nontrivial zeros of `ζ` with multiplicity `m_ρ`; `δ_ρ := β_ρ − ½`;
`t_ρ := (ρ−½)/i = γ_ρ − iδ_ρ`. Facts used (all CITED, classical): (Z1) `|γ_ρ| > 14` for every `ρ`; (Z2) Riemann–von
Mangoldt: `#{ρ : |γ_ρ − y| ≤ 1} ≤ C log(2+|y|)`; (Z3) for `−1 ≤ σ ≤ 2` and `|y| ≥ 2`, `ζ′/ζ(σ+iy) = Σ_{|γ_ρ−y|≤1} 1/(s−ρ)
+ O(log|y|)`, and for every `Y ≥ 2` there is `Y′ ∈ [Y, Y+1]` with `|ζ′/ζ(σ+iY′)| ≤ C log² Y` uniformly in `−1 ≤ σ ≤ 2`
(Davenport §15–§17); (Z4) `−ζ′/ζ` has simple poles only: residue `+1` at `s = 1`, residue `−m_ρ` at `ρ`, residue `−1`
at the trivial zeros; (Z5) functional equation `ζ′/ζ(s) + ζ′/ζ(1−s) = log π − ½ψ(s/2) − ½ψ((1−s)/2)`.

**1.3 (heat kernel and heat extension).** `k_θ(x) := (4πθ)^{−½} e^{−x²/(4θ)}`; with `θ = 1/(4T²)`, `k_θ(x) = (T/√π) e^{−T²x²}`.
For a Borel measure `ν` on `R` with `∫ e^{−ax²} d|ν| < ∞` for some `a > 0` (**Widder class** `𝒲id`), the *heat extension*
is `(e^{θ∂²}ν)(τ) := ∫ k_θ(τ−x) dν(x)`, defined for `0 < θ < 1/(4a)`; it solves `∂_θ u = ∂_τ² u` there. For a complex
point `x₀ ∈ C` we write `e^{θ∂²}δ_{x₀}(τ) := k_θ(τ − x₀)`, an entire function of `τ` that solves the heat equation
(the kernel is entire in `x₀`, and the heat equation holds identically in `x₀ ∈ R`, hence in `x₀ ∈ C`). Note
`|k_θ(τ − x₀)| = (T/√π) e^{T²(Im x₀)² − T²(τ − Re x₀)²}`: the level sets in `(Re x₀ − τ, Im x₀)` are hyperbolae, the
modulus is `≥ T/√π` exactly on the closed **cone** `|τ − Re x₀| ≤ |Im x₀|`.

**1.4 (the archimedean density).** `m(y) := Re ψ(¼ + iy/2) − log π` (P46 main.tex:118; `= Ω_∞` of Doc 152); `|m(y)| ≤
C log(2+|y|)` (Stirling, CITED); `m(0) = C_0 = −5.372…`.

**1.5 (form).** Everything below is in the form [other: Gauss]: the Weil functional evaluated on the Gaussian tests
`h_{T,τ}(t) = e^{−T²(t−τ)²} + e^{−T²(t+τ)²}` (Doc 152 §2.2). It is not `W_T` on `L²(I_T)` and not `A_T|P_T`. The only

## §2 The heat-flow form and the one-sided contour shift

**Lemma 2.1 (the real part of `−ζ′/ζ` on the line is prime-free; PROVED).** For every `y ∈ R`,
`Re(−ζ′/ζ)(½+iy) = ½ m(y)`, where at an on-line zero the left side is the (continuous) real part of the regular part.

*Proof.* At `s = ½+iy`, `1−s = s̄`, so by (Z5) and `ζ′/ζ(s̄) = \overline{ζ′/ζ(s)}`: `2 Re ζ′/ζ(s) = log π − ½ψ(¼+iy/2)
− ½ψ(¼−iy/2) = log π − Re ψ(¼+iy/2) = −m(y)`. Near a zero `ρ = ½+iγ` of multiplicity `m_ρ`, `−ζ′/ζ(s) = −m_ρ/(s−ρ) +
g(s)` with `g` holomorphic; on the line `−m_ρ/(i(y−γ)) = i m_ρ/(y−γ)` is purely imaginary, so `Re(−ζ′/ζ)(½+iy) = Re g(½+iy)`
extends continuously and the identity, valid for `y ≠ γ`, holds at `y = γ` by continuity. ∎

(This is phase-108 108_91's fact "`ζ′/ζ(s) + ζ′/ζ(1−s)` is prime-free" restricted to the line, gate I-10; here it is used
in the direction the gate allows: the mirror is never summed over, the real part is taken pointwise.)

**Lemma 2.2a (Gaussian–Mellin representation; PROVED).** Let `w ∈ C` and `c > max(1, Re w)`. Then
`Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n) n^{−w} e^{−θ(log n)²} = ∫_R F_w(c+ix) dx`, `F_w(z) := (T/√π) e^{T²(z−w)²} (−ζ′/ζ)(z)`, `T² = 1/(4θ)`,
the integral absolutely convergent.

*Proof.* For real `u` and real `a`: `e^{−θu²} e^{−wu} = (1/(2√(πθ))) ∫_R e^{−(x−ia)²/(4θ)} e^{−(w+a+ix)u} dx`. Indeed at
`a = 0` this is the Fourier transform of the Gaussian, `(1/(2√(πθ)))∫ e^{−x²/(4θ)} e^{−ixu}dx = e^{−θu²}`; the integrand
is entire in `x` and decays like `e^{−(Re x)²/(4θ)}` in every horizontal strip, so the contour `Im x = 0` may be moved to
`Im x = a` (Cauchy), which gives the identity for every real `a`. Take `a := c − Re w > 1 − Re w`, so that

**Theorem 2.2 (the on/off decomposition; PROVED).** For every `θ > 0` and `τ ∈ R`, with `T = (4θ)^{−½}` and `w = ½ + iτ`:

Each of `S, P, R_on, R_off` solves `∂_θ u = ∂_τ² u` on `(0,∞)×R`.

*Proof.* `S(θ,τ) = Re Σ_n Λ(n) n^{−w} e^{−θ log²n}` with `w = ½+iτ`. Apply Lemma 2.2a with `c = 2`. Move the line

**Remark 2.6 (why parabolic tools cannot supply `(L)`).** `(L)` is a statement about the behaviour of the heat solution
`S − P` as `θ → 0⁺` at one point `τ₀`. The one theorem that controls a heat solution as `θ → 0` from positivity is Widder's:

to make the sum nonnegative — and that is Thm 2.7, which shows the added measure must contain the zeros. Harnack
inequalities compare a nonnegative solution at two points and are subject to the same remark. The heat structure is
therefore exact but not a mechanism: gate S-15 (heat flow arithmetic-blind, Paper 13 `thm:nogo`) holds here in the
sharpest form — the flow variable is `θ`, the zeros are fixed, and the Euler product enters only through the initial
datum.

## §3 One datum, forward flow, scale

**Theorem 3.1 (one datum; PROVED).** (a) For any `θ₀ > 0`, the function `S(θ₀,·)` determines `μ`, hence `Λ`, hence
`S(θ,·)` for all `θ > 0`. (b) For `θ ≥ θ₀`: `S(θ,·) = e^{(θ−θ₀)∂²} S(θ₀,·)` (forward heat flow from the datum at `θ₀`;
the datum is a bounded continuous function, the flow is classical). (c) For `θ < θ₀` the determination is the backward
heat problem: it is unique but not continuous in the datum; the target `(L)` is the boundary behaviour at `θ = 0`.

*Proof.* (a) `S(θ₀,τ) = ∫ cos(τu) e^{−θ₀u²} dμ(u)` is the Fourier transform of the finite even measure
`½ e^{−θ₀u²}(μ + μ̌)`; a finite measure is determined by its Fourier transform (CITED); `μ` lives on `(0,∞)`, so it is
recovered, and `Λ(n) = √n · μ({log n})`. (b) On the Fourier side the heat semigroup multiplies the even measure by
`e^{−(θ−θ₀)u²}`, which is exactly the passage from `e^{−θ₀u²}μ` to `e^{−θu²}μ`. (c) The backward problem for the heat
equation is ill-posed in every Banach norm (CITED, standard); uniqueness follows from (a). ∎

**Remark 3.1a (what "uniform in `T`" means for this route).** Larger `T` is smaller `θ`. The family `{S_T}` is one heat
solution; the quantifier `∀T` is the limit `θ → 0⁺`, and by (b) every statement at `T` is obtained from any statement at
`T₀ > T` by *forward* smoothing, never the reverse. This is gate U-2 (no upward induction in `T`) in heat form: the
information flows from small `θ` (large `T`) to large `θ` (small `T`), i.e. from the hard end to the easy end. Gate U-3
(uniformity in `T` is Weil positivity on `C_c^∞`): here the `θ → 0` limit of `S − P` is the Fourier transform of
`μ_e − (pole)`, which is Weil's functional on Gaussians of all widths; the memo adds nothing to U-3 beyond the heat
form (declared as required).

**Proposition 3.2 (each block flows; PROVED).** `P`, `R_on`, `R_off` are heat solutions with initial data (as `θ → 0⁺`)
`2π Re δ_{−i/2}` (a complex point source), `ν_on` (a real tempered measure; convergence in `𝒮′`), and
`−2π Re Σ_{β>½} m_ρ δ_{t_ρ}` (complex point sources) respectively. RH ⟺ the initial datum of `S − P` is a tempered measure.

*Proof.* The first statement is Thm 2.2 and 1.3; `e^{θ∂²}ν_on → ν_on` in `𝒮′` because `ν_on` is tempered. The last
statement is Cor 2.5 (i)⟺(iv) together with: if `S − P = e^{θ∂²}ν` with `ν` a tempered real measure then in particular
`|ν|([y,y+1])` is polynomially bounded, and Cor 2.4 shows a zero in a cone forces growth `e^{a_max T²}` at the apex,
which no heat extension of a tempered measure has (its growth at a fixed point is `O(T)` by the split in Thm 2.7). ∎

**Proposition 3.3 (contact scale and resolution; PROVED).** (a) The mass of `e^{−θu²}μ` is concentrated at `u = log n ∈
[T² − KT, T² + KT]`: `∫_{|u−T²|>KT} e^{−θu²} dμ(u) ≤ C e^{−K²/8} · ∫ e^{−θu²}dμ(u)` for `K ≥ 1`, `T ≥ 1`. (b) The zero-side

test detects a zero at depth `δ` with the rate `e^{δ²T²} = e^{2δ²T′}`, whereas the exponential test `F = e^{δt}1_{I_{T′}}`

*Proof.* (a) `∫ e^{−θu²}dμ = ∫_0^∞ e^{−θu²} dψ_½(u)`, `ψ_½(u) := Σ_{log n≤u}Λ(n)n^{−½}`; Chebyshev gives `c e^{u/2} ≤ ψ_½(u)
≤ C e^{u/2}` for `u ≥ 1`, and integration by parts turns the integral into `∫ ψ_½(u) 2θu e^{−θu²}du ≍ ∫ e^{u/2 − θu²} u θ
du`; the exponent `u/2 − u²/(4T²)` is maximal at `u = T²` with second derivative `−1/(2T²)`, so the integrand is a Gaussian

Here `S^a(θ,τ) := Σ a(n) n^{−½} e^{−θ log²n} cos(τ log n)` and `ω^a` is defined as in 1.1 for the comb `a`.

*Proof.* (i) `n^{−κ}|cos| ≤ 2^{−κ} < 1`. `Σ|b(n)|n^{−½} = Σ Λ(n) n^{−1+√r}|cos(τ₀ log n)| = ∞` since `Σ_{n≤x}Λ(n)n^{−1+√r}
