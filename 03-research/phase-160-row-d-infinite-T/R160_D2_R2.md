## §1 Definitions

**1.1 The Meyer class `𝔐` and the carrier of a divisor.** `𝔐` is the set of entire `ξ` with: `ξ=ξ^*` (real on `R`),
`ξ(1−s)=ξ(s)`, `ξ∈𝒪`, zeros `𝒵` in `{|Re s−½|<3/2}`, none real, `N(T+1)−N(T)=O(log T)`, and the three bounds of
C3 Lemma 1.2 (a),(b),(c) for every `L≥2` (polynomial upper bound in strips, polynomial lower bounds at good heights
`T_n∈(n,n+1)` and on the far lines). For `ξ∈𝔐` put `J_ξ:={h∈𝒪: ord_ρh≥m_ρ ∀ρ∈𝒵}` and `H¹(ξ):=𝒪/J_ξ`. Everything in
C3 §§1–7 that uses only Lemma 1.2, the zero count and Fact 2.4a holds verbatim for `H¹(ξ)`: Theorems B, C, D, E, G,
G′, H, F1–F3 and Lemma 6.1. (C3 Theorem A is the statement that for `ξ=ξ_ζ` the Poisson ideal is `J`.)
Members: `ξ_ζ` (PROVED: Lemma 1.2; no real zeros since `ξ>0` on `R`, C3 Thm C(ii)); `Ξ_a=ξ(s+a)ξ(s−a)`, `0<a<½`
(PROVED from `ξ∈𝔐`: (a),(c) by products, (b) at the good heights of `ξ` since `|Ξ_a(x±iT_n)|` is a product of two
lower bounds; zeros `ρ±a`, non-real); `ξ_K` for a number field `K` (CITED: (a),(c) standard; (b) is the `ζ_K`
analogue of Titchmarsh 9.7, not re-verified; real zeros may occur (Siegel), see the "non-real" clause in S); `Ξ_Q`
Epstein and DH (CITED-plausible for (b); OS-D2.2).

**1.2 Forms, defects, adjoint structures.** A *form* is a jointly continuous sesquilinear `𝔥` on `H¹` (Banach–
Steinhaus: separately continuous suffices). For `c∈C` the *weight-`c` defect* is
`𝔡_c(𝔥)(x,y):=𝔥(Θx,y)+𝔥(x,Θy)−c𝔥(x,y)`; `𝔥` is *`c`-compatible* if `𝔡_c(𝔥)=0`, equivalently
`𝔥(ρ_-(λ)x,ρ_-(λ)y)=λ^c𝔥(x,y)` (C3 §1.3 argument). `𝔥` has the *affine adjoint structure* `Θ^†=εΘ+c` (`ε=±1`) if
`𝔥(Θx,y)=𝔥(x,(εΘ+c)y)`; `ε=−1` is `c`-compatibility, `ε=+1,c=0` is `Θ`-symmetry. *Homogeneous* means `1`-compatible;
*non-homogeneous* means `𝔡_1(𝔥)≠0`. `𝒫⁺:=` the cone of positive forms; `𝒫=𝒫⁺∩{𝔡_1=0}` is C3's cone.

**1.3 Far-line forms.** `Γ:=(2−i∞,2+i∞)−(−1−i∞,−1+i∞)` (Lemma 6.1's contour; on it `ξ′/ξ` is given by the Euler
product and Stirling). A *multiplier* is a meromorphic `M` on `C`, holomorphic off `𝒵`, with `ord_ρ(M)≥−m_ρ`, and
`|M(s)|≤C(1+|s|)^A` on `Γ` and on the segments `[½−L,½+L]±iT_n`. Define
* Weil type: `𝔣^W_M(x,y):=(1/2πi)∫_ΓM(s)x(s)y^*(1−s)ds`; vertical type: `𝔣^V_M(x,y):=(1/2πi)∫_ΓM(s)x(s)y^*(s)ds`;
* `𝒮:=span{𝔣^W_M,𝔣^V_M}` (single-contour forms). `𝔟=𝔣^W_{ξ′/ξ}` (Lemma 6.1).
* Double-contour: for `G(s,t)` entire with `|G|≤C(1+|s|)^A(1+|t|)^A` on `Γ×Γ` and on the horizontal segments,
  `𝔮_G(x,y):=(1/2πi)²∫_Γ∫_Γ(ξ′/ξ)(s)(ξ′/ξ)(t)G(s,t)x(s)y^*(t)ds dt`; `𝒟:=span{𝔮_G}`.
A form is *arithmetic* (far-line computable) if it lies in `𝒮+𝒟`.

**1.4 Transfer.** A *transfer* of a positive form `𝔮` to the Weil form is an identity `𝔟(x,y)=𝔮(Dx,y)` on `H¹` with
`D:H¹→H¹` continuous, or `𝔟(x,y)=𝔮(Ex,Ey)` (a square). Evaluated on `[ĝ]` it is an identity for `W_T` [compl].

**1.5 The all-zeros Hermitian form.** `𝔥_all(x,y):=Σ_ρm_ρx(ρ)\overline{y(ρ)}` (all zeros; `𝔥_crit` is its critical
part). `𝔥_off:=𝔥_all−𝔥_crit≥0`.

## §2 Theorem K — all continuous forms; the descent lemma; the kernel forms

**Theorem K (PROVED).** Let `𝔥` be a form on `H¹(ξ)`, `ξ∈𝔐`.
* (i) `𝔥(x,y)=Σ_{n,m}Σ_{ρ∈B_n,σ∈B_m}𝔥(P_ρx,P_σy)`, the double series over bands absolutely convergent.
* (ii) `𝔥≥0` iff for every finite set `Z⊂𝒵` the block matrix `(𝔥|_{E_ρ×E_σ})_{ρ,σ∈Z}` is PSD.
* (iii) `𝔥` is *values-only* (depends on `x,y` only through `(x(ρ))`, `(y(σ))`) iff `Σ_ρN_ρE_ρ⊂rad 𝔥` on both sides;
  then `𝔥=Σ_{ρ,σ}k_{ρσ}x(ρ)\overline{y(σ)}` with `|k_{ρσ}|≤C(1+|γ_ρ|)^A(1+|γ_σ|)^A`.

*Proof.* (i) C3 Thm B(i): `x=Σ_nx_n`, `x_n:=Σ_{ρ∈B_n}P_ρx`, with `Σ_n\bar q_K(x_n)<∞` for every `K` (Lemma B2:
`q_K(G_n^*h)≤CT_n^{−M}q_{K′}(h)`). Joint continuity gives `|𝔥(x,y)|≤C\bar q_K(x)\bar q_K(y)`, so
`Σ_{n,m}|𝔥(x_n,y_m)|≤C(Σ_n\bar q_K(x_n))(Σ_m\bar q_K(y_m))<∞`, and continuity gives the equality. (ii) `H¹_alg` is
dense (Thm B(ii)); `𝔥(x,x)` is the limit of its values on the band partial sums, which are finite sums over zeros.
(iii) The jet map is bijective on `E_ρ` and `x(ρ)=κ_ρc_ρ(x)`; the growth bound is joint continuity with
`|x(ρ)|≤\bar q_K(x)(1+|γ|)^{−K}` read backwards on `v_ρ`. ∎

**Lemma Desc (descent; PROVED).** Let `μ≥0` be a Borel measure on a strip `S_L={|Re s−½|≤L}` with
`∫(1+|s|)^{−2K}dμ<∞`, and `𝔪(x,y):=∫x\bar y\,dμ` on `𝒪`. Then `𝔪` descends to `H¹` (vanishes on `J×𝒪`) iff
`supp μ⊂𝒵`, and then `𝔪=Σ_ρw_ρ𝔢_ρ`, `w_ρ=μ({ρ})≥0`.

*Proof.* `ξe^{(s−c)²}∈J∩𝒪` (Lemma 1.1; `ξ∈J`) and vanishes exactly on `𝒵`. If `𝔪` vanishes on `J×𝒪`, then
`∫|ξe^{(s−c)²}|²dμ=0`, so `μ(S_L∖𝒵)=0`. Conversely a measure on the discrete set `𝒵` is a sum of atoms, and every
`h∈J` vanishes at the atoms. ∎

*Reading.* No line-supported or area-supported Hilbert structure on `𝒪` (weighted Mellin `L²`, Bergman of a strip)
descends to `H¹`: only atoms at the zeros survive. This is the exact reason Connes' `L²` realisation fails OT-2
(O-12) and D.192's graph norms retain only the "model space of the inner factor". The positive forms on `H¹` that
descend from measures are the family `Σw_ρ𝔢_ρ`, which *uses the zeros* unless presented otherwise (Thm G for the
critical part; Thm P for the whole).

**Proposition 𝒟 (kernel forms; PROVED).** Let `G` be as in 1.3 and put `K(s,t):=G(s,\bar t)`. Assume `K` is a
positive-definite kernel (`K(t,s)=\overline{K(s,t)}`, `Σc_i\bar c_jK(s_i,s_j)≥0`) on a conjugation-symmetric domain

  form on `H¹`.
* (ii) `𝔮_G` is `c`-compatible for no `c`, and has no affine adjoint structure, as soon as `K(ρ,σ)≠0` for one pair with
  `σ∉{1−\bar ρ,\bar ρ}`; for the Hardy kernel `K_c(s,t)=(s+\bar t−c)^{−1}` of `{Re s>c/2}⊃𝒵` (any `c<−2`) and for the
  Bergman kernel of `{|Re s−½|<3/2}` this holds for every pair, and `𝔮_G` is *definite on values*:
  `rad 𝔮_G=\overline{Σ_ρN_ρE_ρ}` (`=0` if all zeros are simple).
* (iii) On `[ĝ]`: `𝔮_G([ĝ],[ĝ])=(1/2πi)²∫_Γ∫_Γ(ξ′/ξ)(s)(ξ′/ξ)(t)G(s,t)Φ_F(s)Φ_F(1−t)... ` — more precisely with
  `ĝ^*(t)=Φ_F(\bar t)^-=Φ_F(t)` for real `F` — is a double integral over the far lines, where `ξ′/ξ` is the Euler product
  plus `Γ`-terms: a double sum `Σ_{n,n′}Λ(n)Λ(n′)·𝒢_F(n,n′)` plus archimedean and polar terms. It is M8's
  two-variable Schur form on `H¹`, and it is `≥0` for every divisor.

*Proof.* (i) Shift the `s`-contour across the strip at good heights: on `[½−L,½+L]±iT_n`, `|ξ′/ξ|≤CT_n^{2A}`

the residues are `m_ρx(ρ)G(ρ,t)` (`ξ′/ξ` has a simple pole of residue `m_ρ` whatever `m_ρ`). Repeat in `t`:
`Σ_{σ′}m_{σ′}G(ρ,σ′)y^*(σ′)`; put `σ:=\bar σ′∈𝒵` (`m_{\bar σ}=m_σ`), `y^*(\bar σ)=\overline{y(σ)}`, `G(ρ,\bar σ)=K(ρ,σ)`.
Absolute convergence from `|x(ρ)|≤\bar q_K(x)(1+|γ|)^{−K}` and the zero count. Positivity: finite sections are
`Σc_ρ\bar c_σK(ρ,σ)` with `c_ρ=m_ρx(ρ)`, and (ii) of Thm K. Vanishing on `J×𝒪`: `x(ρ)=0`. (ii) Block computation: a
`c`-compatible form has `𝔥(E_ρ,E_σ)=0` unless `ρ+\bar σ=c` (C3 Thm D(i) with `1` replaced by `c`), a `Θ^†=Θ+c` form

kernel functions at distinct points are linearly independent, so the Gram matrix on any finite `Z⊂𝒵` is PD; hence
`𝔮_G(x,x)=0` iff `x(ρ)=0` for all `ρ`. (iii) is (i) read on `Γ×Γ` with `x=y=[ĝ]`. ∎

*Reading.* Non-homogeneous, zero-free, definite positive structures on `H¹` exist and are cheap; Paper 18 thm:A
already says they carry `κ=0` for every configuration. Theorem T below says what they can transfer: nothing.

## §3 Theorem S — single-contour forms are blind; Theorem AF

**Lemma 3.1 (PROVED).** For every multiplier `M`: `𝔣^W_M` and `𝔣^V_M` descend to `H¹`; `𝔣^W_M` is `1`-compatible;
`𝔣^V_M` is `Θ`-symmetric (`𝔣^V_M(Θx,y)=𝔣^V_M(x,Θy)`).

*Proof.* Descent: for `x∈J`, `Mx` is holomorphic in the strip and polynomially bounded on the horizontal segments, so
the two line integrals agree (as in Lemma 6.1(i)); for `y∈J`, `y^*(1−s)` vanishes at `ρ` to order `m_{1−\bar ρ}=m_ρ`
and `y^*(s)` to order `m_{\bar ρ}=m_ρ`, which cancels the pole of `M`. The identities: `(Θx)(s)y^*(1−s)+x(s)(Θy)^*(1−s)
=(s+(1−s))x(s)y^*(1−s)` and `(Θy)^*(s)=sy^*(s)`. ∎

**Theorem S (PROVED, [meth]).** Let `𝔣=𝔣^W+𝔣^V∈𝒮` be hermitian and `≥0`. Then for every non-real zero `ρ` with
`Re ρ≠½`, `E_ρ⊂rad 𝔣`; and `N_ρE_ρ⊂rad 𝔣` for every `ρ`. So every positive single-contour form is
`Σ_{Re ρ=½}` (a block form on the critical zeros, possibly coupling `ρ` with `\bar ρ`), blind to every non-real
off-line zero and to every Jordan tail.

*Proof.* By Lemma 3.1 and the block computation of C3 Thm D(i) (with `L` nilpotent), `𝔣^W(E_ρ,E_σ)=0` unless
`σ=1−\bar ρ`, and `𝔣^V(E_ρ,E_σ)=0` unless `σ=\bar ρ` (`(ρ−\bar σ)b=L′b` with `L′b(x,y):=b(x,N_σy)−b(N_ρx,y)`
nilpotent). If `ρ` is non-real and off-line, then `ρ∉{1−\bar ρ,\bar ρ}`, so `𝔣(E_ρ,E_ρ)=0`; positivity and
Cauchy–Schwarz give `E_ρ⊂rad 𝔣`. If `ρ` is critical, `𝔣|_{E_ρ×E_ρ}=𝔣^W|_{E_ρ×E_ρ}` is a positive compatible form on a
single Jordan block, so Lemma 5.1 of C3 gives `N_ρE_ρ⊂rad(𝔣|_{E_ρ})`, then Cauchy–Schwarz. If `ρ` is real and

* (ii) Every form `𝔮_G∈𝒟` with `K` positive-definite is positive for every `ξ∈𝔐`: its positivity carries no

* (iii) `𝔟∈𝒮`, and `𝔟≥0` ⟺ RH. Hence, within the arithmetic forms, the sign is carried exactly by the elements of

  positivity of a mixture is again free and the sign becomes the comparison `𝔟+𝔮_G≥𝔮_G`, i.e. Thm T(a).

*Proof.* (i), (ii) as stated; (iii): Lemma 6.1, `eq:W`, Weil's criterion; the mixture claim uses Thm P(ii). ∎

## §4 Theorem W (divisorial rigidity) and Theorem Q′ (affine adjoint structures over `𝔐`)

This is the mechanism of Thm Z.

**Theorem Q′ (affine adjoint structures; PROVED over `𝔐`).** Let `𝔥≥0` be a form on `H¹(ξ)`, `ξ∈𝔐`.

## §5 Theorem T — the transfer trichotomy

**Theorem T (PROVED; existence clauses EQUIV).** Let `𝔮≥0` be a form on `H¹(ξ)`, `ξ∈𝔐`, `ℋ_𝔮` the Hilbert completion
of `H¹/rad 𝔮`.
* (a) *Free existence.* If `rad 𝔮⊂rad 𝔟` and `|𝔟(x,y)|²≤C𝔮(x,x)𝔮(y,y)` (true e.g. for `𝔮=𝔮_G` with `K` the Hardy
  or Bergman kernel and all zeros simple, by Thm K(iii) and Prop 𝒟(ii)), there is a unique bounded selfadjoint `D` on
  `ℋ_𝔮` with `𝔟(x,y)=⟨Dx,y⟩_𝔮`; and `D≥0 ⟺ 𝔟≥0 ⟺ RH`. `D` exists for every such `𝔮`, for every divisor.
* (b) *Flow-natural transfer is EQUIV.* If `D:H¹→H¹` is continuous, `DΘ=ΘD`, and `𝔟(x,y)=𝔮(Dx,y)`, then RH holds
  (for `ζ`); over `𝔐`: then no zero is off the line. Conversely under RH `(𝔮,D)=(𝔥_crit,1)` works. So the existence
  of a flow-commuting transfer is Weil's criterion.
* (c) *Square transfer is EQUIV.* `𝔟(x,y)=𝔮(Ex,Ey)` for some `E:H¹→ℋ_𝔮` iff RH (OT-8's "Hermitian square").
* (d) *Affine-adjoint transfer.* If `𝔮` has an affine adjoint structure `Θ^†=εΘ+c` and `𝔟(x,y)=𝔮(Dx,y)` with any
  `D`, then all zeros lie on one line: `Re ρ=c/2` (`ε=−1`) or `Im ρ=Im c/2` (`ε=+1`). For `ξ∈𝔐` with zeros at
  infinitely many ordinates the second is impossible; the first forces `c=1` (symmetry `ρ↦1−\bar ρ`) and RH.
* (e) *The intertwining law.* For any `D` as in (a), `Dπ(λ)=λ\,π(λ^{−1})^{*_𝔮}D` on `H¹` (`π(λ)=ρ_-(λ)` extended
  to `ℋ_𝔮` when bounded). The commutator `[D,π(λ)]` equals `(λπ(λ^{−1})^{*_𝔮}−π(λ))D`, the `𝔮`-defect of the flow
  applied to `D`: a transfer commutes with the flow exactly when `𝔮` is homogeneous.

*Proof.* (a) Riesz representation on `ℋ_𝔮`; `⟨Dx,x⟩=𝔟(x,x)` on the dense image of `H¹`. (b) `D` commutes with the
resolvent `(z−Θ)^{−1}` (C3 Thm C(iii)) and with the Riesz projections, so `DE_ρ⊂E_ρ` and `N_ρ` preserves `DE_ρ`. Let
`ρ` be off-line, `u∈DE_ρ`, `y∈E_ρ`. Compatibility of `𝔟` and `DΘ=ΘD` give `𝔮(Θu,y)+𝔮(u,Θy)=𝔮(u,y)`; writing
`Θ=ρ+N_ρ`: `(Lb)(u,y)=(1−2β)b(u,y)` for `b:=𝔮|_{DE_ρ×E_ρ}`, `L` nilpotent, `1−2β≠0`; so `b=0`, in particular
`𝔮(u,u)=0`, so `u∈rad 𝔮`, so `𝔟(x,y)=𝔮(Dx,y)=0` for all `x∈E_ρ`, `y∈H¹`. But `𝔟(v_ρ,v_{1−\bar ρ})=m_ρκ_ρ\overline{κ_{1−\bar ρ}}≠0`
(Lemma 6.1(ii)). Contradiction. (c) `𝔮(Ex,Ex)≥0` and G′(ii): an off-line pair carries a negative direction of `𝔟`;
conversely `E=id`, `𝔮=𝔥_crit` under RH. (d) Thm Q′ gives `E_ρ⊂rad 𝔮` off the line; `y∈rad 𝔮` ⟹ `𝔟(x,y)=𝔮(Dx,y)=0`
⟹ `y∈rad 𝔟`; but `𝔟` is non-degenerate on values at every zero. (e) `𝔟(π(λ)x,y)=𝔟(x,λπ(λ^{−1})y)` (compatibility
integrated); substitute `𝔟=⟨D·,·⟩_𝔮`. ∎

*Reading.* A non-homogeneous positive structure never lacks a transfer operator (a); what it lacks is any structural
handle on the sign of that operator, because every handle of the three natural kinds — commuting with the flow (b),
being a square (c), an adjoint structure (d) — is RH. This is the non-homogeneous extension of Lemma T (O-2), Doc 164
(O-10) and 115_17 Prop 3 (S-7), and it is field-uniform.

*Remark T.1 (weight `c≠1`, PROVED).* If `𝔮` is `c`-compatible and `𝔟(x,y)=𝔮(Dx,y)` with `DΘ=ΘD` is only required on
the *support* of `𝔮`, then by (e) `Dv_ρ∈E_{ρ+c−1}`, so `D` is supported on `𝒵∩(𝒵+c−1)`. For `ζ` and real `c≠1`,
`𝒵∩(𝒵+c−1)=∅` means "no two zeros at the same ordinate with different real parts" — a consequence of RH, not known
unconditionally, and true by construction for no `Ξ_a` with `2a=|c−1|` (its zeros `ρ±a` share ordinates). This is a
phase-reading, `Ξ_a`-separating statement (§28.4(4)), but it governs only weight-`c≠1` transfers, which are void by
(d). Recorded as OS-D2.3; not a route.

## §6 Theorem L — the defect calculus

**Theorem L (PROVED).** Let `𝔮≥0` be a form on `H¹(ξ)`, `ξ∈𝔐`, `c∈R`.
* (i) *One-sided defects are half-plane blind.* If `𝔡_c(𝔮)≥0`, then `E_ρ⊂rad 𝔮` for every `ρ` with `Re ρ<c/2`; if
  `𝔡_c(𝔮)≤0`, for every `ρ` with `Re ρ>c/2`; if `𝔡_c(𝔮)=0`, both (Thm Q′(i)). Jordan chains are tolerated.
* (ii) *Rank-one positive defect forces the Hardy Gram* (all zeros simple). If `𝔡_c(𝔮)=ℓ⊗\bar ℓ` for a continuous
  functional `ℓ`, `ℓ_ρ:=ℓ(v_ρ)`, then `ℓ_ρ=0` for every `ρ` with `Re ρ≤c/2`, and
  `𝔮(v_ρ,v_σ)=ℓ_ρ\bar ℓ_σ/(ρ+\bar σ−c)` for all `ρ,σ`: `𝔮` is the Hardy-kernel Gram of `{Re s>c/2}` at the zeros where
  `ℓ` does not vanish (Paper 18 thm:B gives the positivity of that Gram; here the Gram is *forced*). Equivalently
  `𝔮(x,y)=∫_0^∞e^{−ct}ℓ(e^{tΘ}x)\overline{ℓ(e^{tΘ}y)}dt` hmm — with `ρ_-(e^{−t})`: `𝔮(x,y)=∫_0^∞e^{ct}ℓ(ρ_-(e^{−t})x)\overline{ℓ(ρ_-(e^{−t})y)}dt`
  (the observability Gramian), convergent exactly on the classes supported on `Re ρ>c/2`.
* (iii) *Accretive form of Lemma T.* RH ⟺ [every `𝔮∈𝒫⁺` with `𝔡_1(𝔮)≥0` has `𝔡_1(𝔮)=0`] ⟺ [`Θ−½` is
  `𝔮`-accretive for a positive `𝔮` only when it is `𝔮`-skew]. EQUIV.

*Proof.* (i) Put `𝔮_t(x,y):=e^{−ct}𝔮(ρ_-(e^t)x,ρ_-(e^t)y)`. Since `t↦ρ_-(e^t)x` is `C¹` in `H¹` (Lemma 1.1) and `𝔮`
is jointly continuous, `d/dt\,𝔮_t(x,x)=e^{−ct}𝔡_c(𝔮)(ρ_-(e^t)x,ρ_-(e^t)x)`. If `𝔡_c(𝔮)≥0`, `t↦𝔮_t(x,x)` is
nondecreasing. For `x∈E_ρ`, `ρ_-(e^t)x=e^{tρ}e^{tN_ρ}x`, so `𝔮_t(x,x)=e^{(2β−c)t}p(t)` with `p≥0` a polynomial. If
`2β<c`, `𝔮_t(x,x)→0` as `t→+∞`; nondecreasing with limit `0` forces `𝔮_t(x,x)≤0`, so `𝔮(x,x)=0` and `x∈rad 𝔮`. The
case `𝔡_c≤0` is the same with `t→−∞`. (ii) On `E_ρ×E_σ` (simple zeros) the defect equation reads
`(ρ+\bar σ−c)𝔮(v_ρ,v_σ)=ℓ_ρ\bar ℓ_σ`. For `2β<c`, (i) gives `v_ρ∈rad 𝔮`, so `|ℓ_ρ|²=𝔡_c(𝔮)(v_ρ,v_ρ)=0`; for `2β=c`,
`0=|ℓ_ρ|²`. The formula follows for `ρ+\bar σ≠c`, and for `ρ+\bar σ=c` both sides are `0` by what was just shown. The
integral: `ℓ(ρ_-(e^{−t})v_ρ)=e^{−tρ}ℓ_ρ`, and `∫_0^∞e^{(c−ρ−\bar σ)t}dt=(ρ+\bar σ−c)^{−1}` for `Re(ρ+\bar σ)>c`.
(iii) (⟸) If a zero `ρ₀` has `β₀>½`, `𝔮:=𝔢_{ρ₀}` has `𝔡_1(𝔮)=(2β₀−1)𝔮≥0`, `≠0`. (⟹) Under RH, on each `E_ρ×E_ρ`,
`𝔡_1(𝔮)|=Lb` (`2β−1=0`), and for `x∈E_ρ` the nondecreasing polynomial `p(t)=𝔮_t(x,x)≥0` on `R` is constant, so
`𝔡_1(𝔮)(x,x)=p′(0)=0`; on `E_ρ×E_σ` with `ρ≠σ` the diagonal blocks of the PSD form `𝔡_1(𝔮)` vanish, so `𝔡_1(𝔮)=0` on
every finite block matrix, hence everywhere by Thm K(ii). ∎

*Reading.* (i) is the exact "half-plane" mechanism behind Thm G's approximants and behind every dissipative /
Lax–Phillips-type route (§5.13): a one-sided defect sees one half-plane and is blind to the other, and the two
half-planes are exchanged by `ρ↦1−\bar ρ`, which is why the sign never appears. (iii) puts §5.1's unitarity wall in
accretive form; it is EQUIV and is recorded as such.

## §7 Theorem P — the all-zeros form: zero-free, dominating, interior

**Theorem P (PROVED; (iii) EQUIV).** For `ξ∈𝔐` and `L≥2`, put `S_L:={|Re s−½|≤L}` and, for `ε>0`,
`μ_ε:=(1/2π)Δ\log(|ξ|²+ε²)\,dA≥0` (a smooth positive measure: `Δ\log(|f|²+ε²)=4ε²|f′|²/(|f|²+ε²)²`).
* (i) *Poincaré–Lelong presentation.* For `x,y∈𝒪`: `𝔥_all(x,y)=\lim_{ε↓0}∫_{S_L}x\bar y\,dμ_ε`. So `𝔥_all` is defined
  from `ξ` without the zeros, exists whatever their position, is positive, values-only, and non-homogeneous
  (`𝔡_1(𝔥_all)=Σm_ρ(2β_ρ−1)𝔢_ρ`, which is `0` iff RH).
* (ii) *Domination.* `−𝔥_all≤𝔟≤𝔥_all` on `H¹`; on `[ĝ]`: `|W_T(F)|≤Σ_ρm_ρ|Φ_F(ρ)|²` [compl].
* (iii) RH ⟺ `𝔟=𝔥_all` ⟺ `𝔥_off=0` ⟺ `𝔥_all∈𝒫`. EQUIV.
* (iv) *Interior formula.* For `x∈𝒪`:
  `𝔥_all(x,x)=(2/π)∫_{S_L}\log|ξ(s)|\,|x′(s)|²dA(s)+(1/2π)∫_{∂S_L}(|x|²∂_ν\log|ξ|−\log|ξ|\,∂_ν|x|²)|ds|`.
  The boundary term is far-line (arithmetic: on `Re s=½±L`, `\log|ξ|` and `ξ′/ξ` are Euler product plus Stirling);
  the interior term is not.

*Proof.* (i) Let `R_n:=S_L∩{T_n<|Im s|<T_{n+1}}` (and `R_1:=S_L∩{|Im s|<T_2}`), `φ:=x\bar y`. Green on `R_n`:
`∫_{R_n}φ\,dμ_ε=(1/2π)[∫_{R_n}\log(|ξ|²+ε²)Δφ+∮_{∂R_n}(φ\,∂_ν\log(|ξ|²+ε²)−\log(|ξ|²+ε²)∂_νφ)]`. On `∂R_n` there
are no zeros and `|ξ|` is bounded below (Lemma 1.2(b),(c)), so `\log(|ξ|²+ε²)→2\log|ξ|` uniformly with its normal
derivative; in `R_n`, `2\log|ξ|≤\log(|ξ|²+ε²)≤\log(|ξ|²+1)` gives `L¹` convergence. The limit is
`(1/2π)⟨Δ(2\log|ξ|),φ⟩_{R_n}=Σ_{ρ∈R_n}m_ρφ(ρ)` (Poincaré–Lelong: `Δ\log|ξ|=2πΣm_ρδ_ρ`). Uniformly in `ε`:
`μ_ε(R_n)=(1/2π)∮_{∂R_n}∂_ν\log(|ξ|²+ε²)≤(1/π)∮_{∂R_n}|ξ′/ξ|\,|ds|≤CT_n^{A′}` (on good heights and far lines
`|ξ′/ξ|` is polynomial, Lemma 1.2 and Cauchy), while `\sup_{R_n}|φ|≤q_K(x)q_K(y)T_n^{−2K}`; dominated convergence
over `n`. Positivity: limit of positive forms. Values-only and `𝔡_1`: block formula. (ii) Cauchy–Schwarz on
`Σm_ρx(ρ)\overline{x(1−\bar ρ)}`, using that `ρ↦1−\bar ρ` is an `m`-preserving bijection of `𝒵`. (iii) `𝔟−𝔥_all=𝔬−𝔥_off`
vanishes on critical blocks and equals `m_ρ(u\bar u′+u′\bar u−|u|²−|u′|²)=−m_ρ|u−u′|²` on an off-line pair, which is
`≠0`; G′(iv). (iv) Green as in (i) with `ε→0`, `Δ|x|²=4|x′|²`, horizontal terms `→0` along good heights. ∎

*Reading.* Two zero-free positive structures exist on `H¹`: the compatible `𝔥_crit` (Thm G, far-line limit through
`Re ξ′/ξ` on `Re s>½`, blind) and the non-homogeneous `𝔥_all` (interior of the strip, not blind, not arithmetic).

## §8 Theorem Z — the collapse: on Meyer's carrier, G1 is G2

**Theorem Z (PROVED, [meth], in the sense of C3 Thm H and brief I-7/I-8).** Let `𝔓` be any positive structure on
`H¹(ζ)` (a positive form, a family of positive forms, an operator inequality) whose definition makes sense on
`H¹(ξ)` for every `ξ∈𝔐` and is a functional of the divisor there. Let `ℐ` be any identity or inequality relating `𝔓`
to `𝔟`, `𝔬`, `𝔥_crit`, `𝔥_all`, `Θ`, `ρ_-`, `P_±`, `⋆`, valid on `H¹(ξ)` for every `ξ∈𝔐`. Then `𝔓` and `ℐ` together
do not imply `𝔬_ζ=0`. Consequently a proof of `𝔬_ζ=0` must contain a premise `𝒬` that holds for `𝒵(ζ)` and fails for
`𝒵(Ξ_a)`; `𝒬` is a statement about the divisor; its proof cannot come from the carrier (Thm W) and must come from the
presentation `H¹≅𝓗_-/Z𝓗_∩` and the trace identity, i.e. from the prime side of the explicit formula; and it cannot be
"an arithmetic positive form on `H¹` is `≥0`" (Thm AF: blind or free) nor "a transfer operator is `≥0` for a structural
reason" (Thm T). What remains is an inequality between prime-side quantities that is not the positivity of a far-line
form: `∀T: W_T≥0` on `L²(I_T)` proved from `eq:W`'s left side — G2.

*Proof.* `Ξ_a∈𝔐` (§1.1) and `𝔬_{Ξ_a}≠0` (G′(ii): the zeros `ρ±a` are off-line for all but possibly finitely many
`ρ`... in fact for every `ρ` with `β=½`). By Thm W, `𝔓` and `ℐ` on `H¹(Ξ_a)` are the same statements as on any
`H¹(ξ)` with the same divisor, and they hold there by hypothesis; so `𝔓∧ℐ` is consistent with `𝔬≠0`. The remaining
clauses are Thm W(c), Thm AF, Thm T. ∎

approximants of Thm G); non-homogeneous; and equipped with an identity to `𝔟` whose proof uses the presentation
