## §1 Definitions

**1.1 Zeros and defects.** `Z` is the multiset of non-trivial zeros ρ of ζ; `δ(ρ) := Re ρ − ½ ∈ (−½, ½)`;
`δ_max := sup_ρ δ(ρ) = Θ − ½ ∈ [0, ½]`. RH ⟺ `δ_max = 0`. (All statements transfer verbatim to `ζ_K`, `Λ_K`.)

**1.2 Amplification datum (Doc 106 Def 1.1–1.2, quoted).** `(𝒞, ⊠, κ, 𝓕)`: a class `𝒞`, a binary operation
`⊠` with iterated powers `ψ^{⊠k}`, an invariant `κ: 𝒞 → N ∪ {∞}`, a subclass `𝓕`. (a) `κ(ψ^{⊠k}) ≥ kκ(ψ)`;
(b) `∃f`, `f(k)/k → 0`, `κ(ψ^{⊠k}) ≤ f(k)` whenever all `ψ^{⊠k} ∈ 𝓕`. Thm 1.4 (Doc 106, PROVED): (a)+(b) ⟹
`κ(ψ) = 0`. Circularity points (Doc 106 Rem 1.6): (C2) (a) uses the zeros; (C3) (b) is an RH-equivalent positivity.

**1.3 Defect datum.** Same, with `κ: 𝒞 → [0, ∞]` real-valued. This is Deligne's actual invariant: for a lisse
sheaf `F` on a curve `U/F_q` with local Frobenius eigenvalues `α_{x,i}`, `κ(F) := sup_{x,i} (2 log_{q_x}|α_{x,i}| − w)`
(weight defect; Weil I uses it with both signs via duality).

**1.4 The Künneth class over ζ.** Fix `k ≥ 1`. An *admissible transversal weight* is a measurable
`h: (R_+^×)^k → [0, ∞)`, bounded, homogeneous of degree 0 under the diagonal action
(`h(λx_1, …, λx_k) = h(x_1, …, x_k)` for λ > 0), and non-degenerate: `h ≥ c > 0` on a non-empty open set. Put
```
D_k^h(s) := Σ_{n_1,…,n_k ≥ 1} Λ(n_1)⋯Λ(n_k) h(n_1,…,n_k) (n_1⋯n_k)^{−s/k}     (s ∈ C),
```
a generalised Dirichlet series `Σ_μ c_μ μ^{−s}` with frequencies `μ ∈ {(n_1⋯n_k)^{1/k}}` (grouped) and coefficients
`c_μ ≥ 0`. For `k = 2` and `h(x_1,x_2) = φ(x_1/x_2)`, `φ ∈ C_c^∞(R_+^×)`, this is the Mellin form of the almost-prime
block of Doc 107 Thm 4.2 / Paper 28 `thm:almostprime` (`g(N) = N^{−s/2}`), whose spectral side (Doc 107 Cor 3.6,
Paper 28 `thm:signature`) is the Minkowski sum `ρ_1 + ρ_2` weighted by `φ̂((ρ_1−ρ_2)/2)`. The exponent `−s/k` is
chosen so that the pole-sum sits at `s = k` and the pure zero-sums at `s = ρ_1 + ⋯ + ρ_k` (un-normalised, as
`α^{2k}` in Weil I); the normalisation `−s` of Doc 107 (`σ = (s_1+s_2)/2`) is the same function at `s/k`.

**1.5 The abscissa.** For a generalised Dirichlet series with `c_μ ≥ 0`, `σ_c` = abscissa of convergence
(= abscissa of absolute convergence). Landau (Hardy–Riesz Thm 10): if `σ_c` is finite then `s = σ_c` is a singular
point of the sum function. Hardy–Riesz Thm 7: if `σ_c > 0` then `σ_c = limsup_{X→∞} log S(X)/log X`,
`S(X) := Σ_{μ ≤ X} c_μ`.

**1.6 Paper 46 forms (for the record only; never used to claim a sign).** `𝔞_T`, `W_T`, `P_T`, `λ^A_T`, `λ^W_T` as
in P46 main.tex:115–147; `RH ⟺ ∀T: W_T ≥ 0 ⟺ ∀T: 𝔞_T ≥ 0 on P_T`. The Hermitian square of the `k`-fold object is
`Q_k(h) := 𝒲_k(h ⋆ h̃)` (Doc 107 Def 5.1 for `k = 2`), with `Q_1 = W` on `C_c^∞`.

## §2 The skeleton on Deligne's scale

**Lemma 2.1 (defect form; PROVED, [meth]).** Let `(𝒞, ⊠, κ, 𝓕)` be a defect datum (κ real-valued ≥ 0) with (a)
and (b). Then `κ(ψ) = 0` for every ψ with all `ψ^{⊠k} ∈ 𝓕`.

*Proof.* `kκ(ψ) ≤ κ(ψ^{⊠k}) ≤ f(k)`, so `0 ≤ κ(ψ) ≤ f(k)/k → 0`. ∎ (Integrality, used in Doc 106 Thm 1.4 to pass
from `κ ≤ 0` to `κ = 0`, is not needed for a nonnegative real invariant.)

**Lemma 2.2 (Deligne's datum; CITED Weil I §3.2, the Landau step re-proved).** Let `U/F_q` be a smooth affine
curve, `F` a lisse `Q̄_ℓ`-sheaf on `U` with algebraic local eigenvalues, a perfect pairing `F ⊗ F → Q̄_ℓ(−w)` and
geometric monodromy with Zariski closure `Sp` (or `O`) — the hypotheses of Weil I 3.2. Then (a) for every `x`,
the local eigenvalues of `F^{⊗2k}` at `x` include `α_{x,i}^{2k}`, so the local defect is multiplied by `2k`; (b)
`L(U, F^{⊗2k}, T) = P_1(T)/P_2(T)` with `P_2(T) = det(1 − Frob·T | H²_c(U_{F̄}, F^{⊗2k}))`, `H²_c = (coinvariants)(−1)`,
and by invariant theory of `Sp`/`O` every invariant of `F^{⊗2k}` is a contraction of pairings, of weight exactly
`kw`; hence all zeros of `P_2` have `|T| = q^{−kw−1}`.

*Landau step.* `log L(U, F^{⊗2k}, T) = Σ_x Σ_{n≥1} tr(Frob_x^n | F^{⊗2k}) T^{n·deg x}/n`, and `tr(Frob_x^n | F^{⊗2k}) =
tr(Frob_x^n | F)^{2k} ≥ 0` (traces rational under Weil I 3.2's hypothesis that every `det(1 − F_x t, F)` has rational
coefficients; an even power of a real number is ≥ 0). So `L` is a power series in `T`
with nonnegative coefficients, meromorphic with poles only at `|T| = q^{−kw−1}`; by Landau (Pringsheim) its radius
of convergence is `≥ q^{−kw−1}`. Each local factor `det(1 − Frob_x T^{deg x} | F^{⊗2k})^{−1}` is a power series with
nonnegative coefficients dominated termwise by those of `L` (all other factors have constant term 1 and
nonnegative coefficients), so it converges in the same disc, hence `|α_{x,i}|^{2k} ≤ q_x^{kw+1}`, i.e.
`2k·(log_{q_x}|α_{x,i}| − w/2) ≤ 1`. In the datum: `f(k) ≡ 1`. ∎

**Remark 2.3 (the two structural features).** (i) The amplified object `F^{⊗2k}` has **no trivial constituent of
its own** (`H⁰_c(U, F^{⊗2k}) = 0` for `U` affine, and the invariants sit at weight `kw`, the *target*); the pole is
supplied by the **base** (`H²_c(U) = Q̄_ℓ(−1)`, weight `2 = 2 dim U`) and its excess over the target is
`k`-independent. (ii) The amplified data are **local** (eigenvalues at closed points); the a-priori bound is
**global** (location of the pole). Both features are absent over `Spec Z` for ζ: (i) fails by Theorem A/C, (ii)
by Theorem B.

## §3 Theorem A: the pole-sum pins the abscissa of every Künneth amplification

**Theorem A (PROVED, [other: `D_k^h`]).** For every `k ≥ 1` and every admissible transversal weight `h`, the
series `D_k^h(s)` converges absolutely for `Re s > k`, diverges for `Re s < k`, and `s = k` is a singular point of
its sum function. In particular the maximal holomorphic continuation of `D_k^h` from its half-plane of convergence
is contained in `{Re s > k}` ∪ (whatever lies beyond a singularity at `s = k`): the a-priori region supplied by
nonnegativity is exactly `Re s > k`.

*Proof.* Coefficients are nonnegative. Write `S(X) := Σ_{(n_1⋯n_k)^{1/k} ≤ X} Λ(n_1)⋯Λ(n_k) h(n_1,…,n_k)`.

*Upper bound.* `S(X) ≤ ‖h‖_∞ Σ_{N ≤ X^k} Λ^{⋆k}(N)`. Claim: `Σ_{N≤Y} Λ^{⋆k}(N) ≪_k Y (log Y)^{k−1}` for `Y ≥ 2`. For
`k = 1` this is Chebyshev's `ψ(Y) ≪ Y`. Inductively,
`Σ_{N≤Y} Λ^{⋆k}(N) = Σ_{M ≤ Y} Λ^{⋆(k−1)}(M) ψ(Y/M) ≪ Y Σ_{M≤Y} Λ^{⋆(k−1)}(M)/M ≪ Y (log Y)^{k−1}`,
the last step by partial summation from the inductive bound. Hence `S(X) ≪ X^k (log X)^{k−1}`, so `σ_c ≤ k`.

*Lower bound.* Let `Ω` be an open set with `h ≥ c > 0` on Ω. By homogeneity Ω contains an open cone, hence a set
`{λ·x : λ > 0, x ∈ B}` with `B = ∏_{i=1}^k [a_i, b_i]`, `0 < a_i < b_i`. Put `λ := X/(∏ b_i)^{1/k}`; every lattice
point `n ∈ λB` has `(n_1⋯n_k)^{1/k} ≤ λ (∏ b_i)^{1/k} = X`. Therefore
`S(X) ≥ c Σ_{n ∈ λB} ∏_i Λ(n_i) = c ∏_{i=1}^k (ψ(b_iλ) − ψ(a_iλ^−)) ~ c ∏_i (b_i − a_i) λ ≫ X^k`
by PNT. Hence `σ_c ≥ k`. With Hardy–Riesz Thm 7, `σ_c = k`; with Thm 10, `s = k` is singular. ∎

**Corollary A1 (PROVED, [meth]).** Let `𝔖 ⊂ C` be the singular set of the maximal continuation of `D_k^h`. Then
`sup Re 𝔖 = k`. Consequently, if `kρ ∈ 𝔖` for some zero ρ (as the Minkowski-sum spectral side gives for `k = 2`,
Doc 107 Cor 3.6), the only inequality that nonnegativity yields is `k(½ + δ(ρ)) ≤ k`, i.e. `δ(ρ) ≤ ½`. In the
defect datum `(𝔇, Künneth, κ = amplified defect, 𝓕 = all)`: (a) holds with `κ(D_k) = kδ_max` **and (b) holds with
`f(k) = k/2` and with no smaller function**; `f(k)/k = ½ ↛ 0`. Lemma 2.1 gives nothing.

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem A: the singularity at `s = k` is on the boundary of the a-priori region, so no
singularity is excluded from `Re s ≤ k`; `f(k) = k − k/2`. Minimality: `s = k` *is* singular, so no a-priori region
larger than `Re s > k` exists. ∎

**Proposition A2 (mixed-level domination; PROVED for `k = 2` modulo Doc 107's continuation; HEURISTIC for `k ≥ 3`).**
Let `k = 2`, `h(x_1,x_2) = φ(x_1/x_2)`, `φ ∈ C_c^∞(R_+^×)`, `φ ≥ 0`, `φ̃(z) := ∫_0^∞ φ(y) y^{z−1} dy` (entire, rapidly
decreasing on vertical lines). For `Re s > 2`,
```
D_2^h(s) = (1/2πi) ∫_{(0)} φ̃(z) (−ζ'/ζ)(s/2 + z) (−ζ'/ζ)(s/2 − z) dz .
```
Shifting `s` to the left, the poles of the integrand at `z = 1 − s/2`, `z = s/2 − 1` (pole of ζ) and at `z = ρ − s/2`,
`z = s/2 − ρ` (zeros) cross the contour; the residues are `φ̃(1 − s/2)(−ζ'/ζ)(s − 1)` (pole slot), and
`−m_ρ φ̃(ρ − s/2)(−ζ'/ζ)(s − ρ)` (zero slot), up to orientation signs. Hence the singular levels of `D_2^h` are
`s = 2` (pole-sum), `s = 1 + ρ` (mixed: one pole, one zero), `s = ρ + ρ'` (pure, weight `m_ρ m_{ρ'} φ̃((ρ−ρ')/2)`, the
Doc 107 Cor 3.6 spectral side), and the trivial-zero levels. Real parts: `2`, `3/2 + δ(ρ)`, `1 + δ(ρ) + δ(ρ')`.
For general `k` (Doc 107 §6.5, sketch): level `j` (j zeros, `k−j` poles) has `Re = k − j/2 + Σ_{i≤j} δ_i`. The
amplified level `j = k` is `k/2 + Σδ_i ≤ k/2 + kδ_max`; the level `j = 1` is `k − ½ + δ ≥ k − ½`; their difference is
`≥ (k−1)(½ − δ_max) ≥ 0`, with equality only if `k = 1` or `δ_max = ½`. So for `k ≥ 2` the amplified singularity is
never the rightmost non-polar singularity: any "rightmost singularity" argument (Landau, Perron, Tauberian) sees
the un-amplified defect at most. ∎ (The `k = 2` residue bookkeeping is the standard pinch analysis; I take the
singular-level *set* from Doc 107 Thm 3.4/Cor 3.6 and only add the pole-slot residues; the general-`k` level formula
is Doc 107 §6.5's sketch, labelled HEURISTIC there and here.)

**Remark 3.1 (why this is not R151 T6).** T6(i) (`σ_c(k) ≥ 2kβ_max` for nonnegative *additive* families) is a
statement about Hölder-type moments where the a-priori exponent is an assumption (`H(k,c)`, P47 Thm 26.4, Rem
26.4a). Theorem A concerns the *multiplicative* Künneth object whose a-priori exponent is a **theorem**: it is
`k`, forced by the pole, and it is the pole-sum, not a family factor. The two results are complementary halves of
the same fact: over `Z` every nonnegative amplification has its a-priori abscissa at the image of the pole.

**Remark 3.2 (the geometric control).** For a curve `C/F_q`, `Z(C^k, T)` also has nonnegative coefficients and its
first singularity is the pole `|T| = q^{−k}` (weight `2k`, `H^{2k}(C^k)`), while `H^1(C)^{⊗k}` sits at weight `k`:
excess `k`. Landau on `Z(C^k)` is exactly as useless as Theorem A says it is over `Z`. Weil I never applies Rankin to
a zeta function of a variety; it applies it to `L(U, F^{⊗2k})` for a local system without trivial constituent
(Rem 2.3). The Künneth-power trick on varieties (`X^k`) is used in Weil I only *after* an excess-1 bound has been
obtained from such a local system; it never starts from Landau on `Z(X^k)`. The over-`Z` transcription in Doc 106
Candidate 2 / Doc 107 started from the Künneth product of the base, which is the useless half.

## §4 Theorem B: ζ's zeros are never local data

**Theorem B (PROVED, (ii) mod CITED six exponentials; [meth]).**

(i) Every lisse `Z_ℓ`- or `Q̄_ℓ`-sheaf on `Spec Z` (an inverse system of locally constant constructible sheaves,
i.e. a continuous representation of `π₁^ét(Spec Z)`; local constancy needs no inversion of ℓ, only the cohomology
formalism does, and Weil I's formalism would live on `Spec Z[1/ℓ]`, covered by (ii)) is constant. Hence for `F`
lisse on `Spec Z`, `F^{⊗2k}` is constant, all
local eigenvalues are `1` (or those of a constant twist), and Lemma 2.2's conclusion is empty.

(ii) Let `U = Spec Z[1/N]` and `F` lisse on `U` with algebraic local Frobenius eigenvalues at every closed point
(the standing hypothesis under which Weil I §3 applies). Let ρ be a non-trivial zero of ζ and `a_p ∈ Q̄^×`. Then
`a_p p^{ρ}` is a local eigenvalue of `F` at `p` for at most two primes `p`.

(iii) For the constant sheaf on `Spec Z`, the local datum at `n` is `Λ(n)` and the "Ramanujan bound"
`|Λ(n)| ≤ log n` is trivial: there is no local defect to amplify.

*Proof.* (i) Lisse sheaves on `Spec Z` correspond to continuous representations of `π₁^ét(Spec Z, Q̄)`, which
classifies finite extensions of `Q` unramified at every finite prime. By Minkowski, `|d_K| > 1` for `K ≠ Q`, and a
prime dividing `d_K` ramifies; so `π₁^ét(Spec Z) = 1` and every lisse sheaf is constant. (ii) If `a_p p^{ρ}` is an
eigenvalue at `p` then `a_p p^{ρ} ∈ Q̄`, so `p^{ρ} ∈ Q̄`. Suppose this at three primes `p_1, p_2, p_3`. If `ρ ∉ Q`,
then `{1, ρ}` and `{log p_1, log p_2, log p_3}` are `Q`-linearly independent, and the six exponentials theorem says
one of `e^{x_i y_j} ∈ {p_j, p_j^{ρ}}` is transcendental — contradiction. Hence `ρ ∈ Q`; but a non-trivial zero is
non-real (ζ has no real zeros in `(0,1)`, classical), so `ρ ∉ Q`. (This is G1_B4_R5 Lemma B + Lemma C, Cor R1.)
(iii) `Λ(n) = log p` for `n = p^m`, else 0. ∎

**Corollary B1 (PROVED mod CITED).** The local incarnation of Deligne's datum — `𝒞` = lisse sheaves on opens of
`Spec Z`, `⊠ = ⊗`, κ = local weight defect, (b) from an a-priori pole — exists (it is the Rankin–Deligne scheme for
Ramanujan–Petersson, modulo continuation of `L(F^{⊗2k})`), but its invariant κ never involves a zero of ζ: at most
two primes could carry `p^{ρ}` and Lemma 2.2's Euler-product domination needs the estimate at every `x`. The zeros
of ζ enter any object over `Z` only as **global singularities**, and for those Theorem A applies.

**Remark 4.1 (scope).** Theorem R (O-3) was recorded for carriers (G1). Its use here is different: it shows that
the one incarnation of Rankin whose excess is `k`-independent is unavailable *for ζ's zeros*, not merely for a
carrier. The hypothesis "algebraic local eigenvalues" is what makes Lemma 2.2's traces real; without it there is no
Landau step, so no weaker hypothesis rescues the incarnation.

## §5 Theorem C: the sign–pole trilemma

**Theorem C (PROVED, [other: `Q_2`]; EQUIV clause).** On the Künneth class of §1.4:

(1) (*nonnegative ⟹ pole-sum*) Every `D_k^h` has nonnegative coefficients and `σ_c = k` (Theorem A).

(2) (*pole-free ⟹ signed*) Replace one factor `Λ(n_i)` by the mean-zero comb `Λ(n_i) − 1` (or by `Λ(n_i) − μ`, any
`μ > 0`). The resulting series has coefficients of both signs (a tuple with `n_i` not a prime power contributes
`−μ·∏_{j≠i}Λ(n_j) h < 0` on the cone where `h > 0`, while prime-power tuples contribute `> 0`). Landau's theorem does
not apply, and the abscissa of convergence carries no singularity a priori. More generally, any modification of
the coefficients that removes the `X^k` main term of `S(X)` must introduce negative coefficients, because a
nonnegative sequence with `S(X) = o(X^k)` on the cone would contradict the lower bound in Theorem A's proof.

(3) (*the residual positivity is the Hermitian square*) On the pole-removed (primitive) sector the only positivity
available is `Q_k(h) = 𝒲_k(h ⋆ h̃) ≥ 0`. For `k = 2`: `Q_2 ≥ 0` on the thickened class ⟺ RH.

*Proof of (3).* (⇐) Doc 107 Obs 5.3 (under RH the spectral side `Σ_{ρ_1,ρ_2} ĥ(ρ_1,ρ_2) conj ĥ(1−ρ̄_1, 1−ρ̄_2)`
is a sum of squares). (⇒) Suppose ¬RH, so some ρ₀ has `δ(ρ₀) ≠ 0`. By Hardy there are infinitely many zeros
`½ + iγ_j` on the line. Doc 107 Prop 6.1: for each `j` the pairs `(ρ₀, ½+iγ_j)` and `(1−ρ̄₀, ½+iγ_j)` form a
fixed-point-free orbit of the involution `(ρ,ρ') ↦ (1−ρ̄, 1−ρ̄')` (free because `Re ρ₀ ≠ ½`), and the corresponding
2×2 Gram block is a hyperbolic plane; the evaluations at distinct pairs are independent on the thickened class
(Doc 107 Lemma 5.4). Hence `Q_2` has infinitely many negative directions, in particular is not ≥ 0. (Doc 107 proves
this in the toy with one quadruple; the argument uses only one off-line zero and infinitely many on-line partners,
so it holds under any ¬RH.) ∎

**Corollary C1 (the trilemma; PROVED, [meth]).** On the Künneth class, a (b)-bound must come from a positivity of
the amplified object. There are exactly two positivities on record: coefficientwise (Landau) and Hermitian-square
(Weil form of `𝒲_k`). The first forces the pole-sum abscissa and `f(k) = k/2` (Cor A1). The second, on the
pole-free sector, is `Q_k ≥ 0`, which is RH at `k = 2` (Thm C(3)) and, at the window level, RH for each `k`
separately (Doc 108 Prop 7.3; Paper 29 `thm:B`). No third positivity of the object is on record; if one is
proposed, Gate S-6 and I2a (Doc 99) apply to it.

## §6 Proposition T: towers dilute

**Proposition T (PROVED, [meth]).** Let `Q = K_0 ⊂ K_1 ⊂ ⋯` be a tower of number fields with
`g_n := log|d_{K_n}| → ∞`. Fix ρ₀ ∈ C and let `m_n := ord_{s=ρ₀} ζ_{K_n}`. Define the tower datum by
`ψ^{⊠n} := ζ_{K_n}` and either κ_mult := `m_n` or κ_dens := `m_n/g_n`.

(i) κ_dens: the contribution of ρ₀ to the normalised explicit formula `(1/g_n) Σ_{ρ ∈ Z(K_n)} ĥ(ρ)` is
`m_n ĥ(ρ₀)/g_n`. Since `N_{K_n}(T) ≪ T g_n` uniformly (Riemann–von Mangoldt for `ζ_K`, CITED classical), `m_n ≤ C g_n`
with `C` absolute. Take ρ₀ a zero of ζ, so `m_1 ≥ 1`. Then (a) in the form `κ(ψ^{⊠n}) ≥ nκ(ψ)` reads
`m_n/g_n ≥ n m_1/g_1 ≥ n/g_1`, while `m_n/g_n ≤ C`; this fails for every `n > C g_1`. So (a) fails on the density
scale, for every tower.

(ii) κ_mult: (a) `m_n ≥ n m_1` requires ρ₀ to be a zero of the Artin `L`-functions of `K_n/Q` with total weighted
multiplicity `≥ n − 1` (`ζ_{K_n} = ζ ∏_{χ≠1} L(χ)^{χ(1)}` for Galois `K_n`). This is a statement about zeros of other
`L`-functions: circular in the sense (C2) of Doc 106 Rem 1.6. And (b) `m_n ≤ f(n)` with `f` sublinear is a
multiplicity bound; no such bound is known for any tower, and its only conceivable source is GRH plus a simplicity
hypothesis.

(iii) Under GRH for all `K` there is no off-line ρ₀ to amplify; under ¬RH the tower's own zero measure obeys the
same explicit formula with the same prime-side (splitting statistics) and cannot distinguish "ρ₀ off-line with
`m_n = 1`" from "ρ₀ absent" in the limit `g_n → ∞`. Hence no field-uniform tower argument supplies ⊠ or `f`. ∎

**Remark 6.1.** For Galois steps Aramata–Brauer gives `ζ_{K_n} | ζ_{K_{n+1}}` (entire quotient), so `m_n` is
non-decreasing: (a) holds only as monotonicity — Doc 106 Candidate 5 (direct sum). Doc 136 Thm 2.5 (density
`κ_N/n_N → 0` in a band scheme) is the same dilution seen in the index. Consistent with U-9 (Paper 15 `thm:hr`): the
tower's "gap" is `m_n/g_n → 0` in both worlds.
