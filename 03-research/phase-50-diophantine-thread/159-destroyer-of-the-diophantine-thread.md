## 1. What "the completeness of {p^{iτ}}" means — two readings that must be separated

The CTD is deliberately ambiguous between two distinct objects. Separating them is the first
adversarial act, because the confusion between them is where a false hope hides.

### 1.1. Reading A: Kronecker-completeness (over the value torus)

**[DEFINITION 159.1] (system of primes and its torus).** Fix τ ∈ ℝ. The system
$\{p^{i\tau}\}_p = \{e^{i\tau\log p}\}_p$ is a family of points on the unit circle
indexed by the primes. The ℚ-linear independence of $\{\log p\}$ (unique factorization,
Doc 112 Lemma 2.2 and 3.3) implies, by Kronecker–Weyl, that the orbit
$\tau \mapsto (p^{i\tau})_{p}$ is dense and equidistributed in the infinite-dimensional torus
$\mathbb{T}^\infty = \prod_p S^1$. This is the engine of:
- the Bohr almost-periodicity of $\zeta$ for $\sigma > 1$ (where the Euler series
  converges absolutely: $\zeta(s) = \prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$ is a continuous function of the
  point $(p^{-it})_p$ on the torus);
- the Voronin universality / Bagchi limit theorem on the strip $\tfrac12<\sigma<1$
  (Doc 112 §2.4): the limiting measure $Q$ is the law of the random Euler product
  $\zeta(s,\omega) = \prod_p(1-\omega_p p^{-s})^{-1}$ with $\omega = (\omega_p)$ uniform on
  $\mathbb{T}^\infty$.

**Status of Reading A: unconditional, with no individuation content.** It is completeness
in the sense of "the orbit fills the value torus." It says nothing about the position of
the zeros. It is the pure VALUE SIDE (P43 §4). I detail it and kill it in §3.

### 1.2. Reading B: crossing-completeness (the one that would give individuation)

**[DEFINITION 159.2] (crossing-completeness).** The CTD needs the completeness/independence
of $\{p^{i\tau}\}$ to imply an INDIVIDUATION statement: that the Weil defect
$F_{\mathrm{off}}(h) = \sum_{\rho\text{ off}}\hat h(\cdots)$ be forced to zero (or that the
position of A zero be decidable) from the Diophantine structure of the prime system,
without going through the explicit formula / the zero density.

**The central point of the whole document.** The CTD is the conjecture that Reading A ⟹
Reading B with an RH-free bridge. The three routes are three ways of proving that that bridge
either does not exist (Route 2: the two readings live in distinct σ-algebras, inertia is not
measurable with respect to the value torus), or exists but reintroduces ζ (Route 1: the bridge
is the Euler product), or exists only qualitatively and dies upon quantification
(Route 3: as with Beurling–Malliavin).

## 2. Route 1 — Nyman–Beurling circularity: the exact Mellin dictionary

### 2.1. The Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte criterion, recalled with precision

I collect from Doc 82 §1 (verified there against the classical literature):

- **[Nyman 1950] / [Beurling 1955].** RH ⟺ $\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}$ is in the closure in
  $L^2(0,\infty)$ of the span of the dilations of the fractional part
  $r_a(t) = \{a/t\}$, $a\in(0,1)$ (with the restriction $\sum c_k a_k = 0$ for
  squarability). [Nym50; Beu55].
- **[Báez-Duarte 2003].** Computable discrete version: RH ⟺ $\lim_{N\to\infty} d_N^2 = 0$,
  where $d_N^2 = \inf_{c_k}\int_0^\infty |\mathbf 1_{(0,1)} - \sum_{k\le N} c_k\{k/t\}|^2dt$
  using only INTEGER dilations $k = 1,\dots,N$. [BD03].

**Key observation (the nature of the system).** The Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte system
is indexed by the INTEGERS $\{1,2,\dots,N\}$ (the dilations $\{k/t\}$). NOT by the
primes. This is the point the CTD needs to erase, and the one I am going to uphold.

### 2.2. The Mellin dictionary, written exactly

**[CALCULATION 159.3] (Mellin transform of the dilations).** The Mellin transform
$\mathcal M: L^2(0,\infty, dt/t) \to L^2(\tfrac12+i\mathbb R, \tfrac{ds}{2\pi})$,
$(\mathcal M f)(s) = \int_0^\infty f(t) t^{-s}\frac{dt}{t}$ (on the critical line $s=\tfrac12+i\xi$),
is an isometry (Plancherel for Mellin). From Doc 82 §7.4, the integer dilation
$r_k(t) = \{k/t\}$ has Mellin transform (for $\mathrm{Re}\,s\in(0,1)$)
$$\mathcal M[r_k](s) \;=\; -\frac{\zeta(s)}{s}\,k^{-s}, \qquad \mathcal M[\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}](s) = \frac{1}{s}.$$
(The factor $\zeta(s)/s$ comes from $\int_0^\infty\{1/t\}t^{-s}\frac{dt}{t} = -\zeta(s)/s$ for
$0<\mathrm{Re}\,s<1$; dilation by $k$ multiplies by $k^{-s}$.)

**[PROPOSITION 159.4] (the Nyman–Beurling completeness in spectral coordinates).**
Via $\mathcal M$, the Báez-Duarte criterion is:
$$\mathrm{RH} \iff \frac1s \in \overline{\mathrm{span}}^{L^2(\frac12+i\mathbb R)}
\Bigl\{ \frac{\zeta(s)}{s}\,k^{-s} : k = 1,2,3,\dots \Bigr\}.$$
Equivalently, factoring out $\zeta(s)/s$ (non-vanishing a.e. on the line), RH is equivalent to
$\zeta(s)^{-1}$ being in the closure of the span of the functions $\{k^{-s}\}_{k\ge1}$ weighted by
$\zeta(s)/s$, i.e. to the **system of integer powers $\{k^{-s} = e^{-s\log k}\}_{k\ge 1}$
with frequencies $\{\log k\}_{k\ge 1}$.**

*Proof.* Direct application of the Mellin isometry to the Báez-Duarte statement
(Doc 82, Theorem 1.3 and §7.4); the factorization uses $\zeta(s)\ne 0$ a.e. on
$\mathrm{Re}\,s = \tfrac12$ (unconditionally true: the zeros are countable). $\square$

### 2.3. The distinction ℕ vs. primes — the heart of Route 1

Now the decisive adversarial act. The CTD wants "the completeness of $\{p^{i\tau}\}$"
to be the Nyman–Beurling completeness. Prop. 159.4 shows that the Nyman–Beurling
system has frequencies $\{\log k\}_{k\ge1}$ — **ALL the integers**, not just the
primes. The relation between the two frequency systems:
$$\{\log k\}_{k\ge1} \;=\; \Bigl\{\, \textstyle\sum_p a_p \log p \;:\; a_p\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\ \text{almost all }0 \,\Bigr\}
\;=\; \mathbb Z_{\ge0}\text{-semigroup generated by }\{\log p\}_p.$$

**[PROPOSITION 159.5] (the two systems are NOT the same completeness).** The Nyman–Beurling
frequency system $\{\log k\}_{k\ge1}$ is the additive semigroup multiplicatively
generated by $\{\log p\}_p$. The completeness of $\{e^{-s\log k}\}_{k\ge1}$
(which is RH-equivalent, Prop. 159.4) does NOT reduce to the linear completeness of
$\{e^{-s\log p}\}_p$ (which is Reading A, unconditional). Concretely:
- $\{e^{-s\log p}\}_p = \{p^{-s}\}_p$ are the **generators**; their closed linear span is
  an unconditional object (it is the first "level" of the Dirichlet series).
- $\{e^{-s\log k}\}_k = \{n^{-s}\}_n$ includes all the **products** $p_1^{a_1}\cdots$;
  reconstructing these from the primes is exactly the Euler product
  $\sum_n n^{-s} = \prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$, an identity over $\mathrm{Re}\,s=\tfrac12$
  that involves $\zeta$.

*Proof.* That $\{\log k\}$ is the additive semigroup generated by $\{\log p\}$ is
unique factorization. That the step from the linear span of the generators to the linear span of the
products requires $\zeta$: the generating function that packages $\{n^{-s}\}_n$ from
$\{p^{-s}\}_p$ is $\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$, whose a.e. evaluation on the critical line is
$\zeta(\tfrac12+i\xi)$; a linear span of the $p^{-s}$ does NOT contain the composite $n^{-s}$
unless the (non-linear) multiplicative operation encoded by Euler is allowed. The
Nyman–Beurling completeness is that of the $n^{-s}$, not of the $p^{-s}$. $\square$

### 2.4. The Mellin isomorphism "handled with care" — what it transports and what it does not

The goal asked to verify the Mellin isomorphism carefully. The care consists of
this: the Mellin transform DOES carry dilations $t\mapsto t/a$ to multiplications
$a^{-s}$, and therefore carries $\{a^{i\tau}\}$ (characters of the multiplicative group
$(0,\infty)$) to exponentials $\{e^{-i\tau\log a}\}$. In that sense, "dilations ↔
characters $\{a^{i\tau}\}$" is correct and is a group isomorphism. **But the
system relevant to RH (Nyman–Beurling) uses dilations by INTEGERS $a=k$, not by primes.** The
Mellin isomorphism is faithful; what the CTD slips in is replacing "integers" with "primes"
AFTER the isomorphism, and that replacement is NOT a Mellin step: it is the Euler product.

**[BRIDGE 159-A] (where Route 1 lands).** The Nyman–Beurling circularity does NOT consummate
directly, because:
- The RH-equivalent completeness is that of $\{k^{-s}\}_{k\ge1}$ (integers).
- The unconditional completeness (Reading A, Kronecker torus) is that of $\{p^{-s}\}_p$ /
  $\{p^{i\tau}\}_p$ (primes).
- The bridge between the two is the Euler product over $\mathrm{Re}\,s=\tfrac12$, which IS
  $\zeta$ — so any attempt to DEDUCE the integer-completeness (RH) from the
  prime-completeness (unconditional) goes through evaluating Euler on the critical line, where
  the full problem lives.

**Route 1 verdict: DOES NOT KILL by direct circularity (the systems are distinct: ℕ≠primes),
but leaves the thread marked.** The thread is not "trivially RH" by being Nyman–Beurling
in disguise — because it is not: the prime system is strictly smaller than the
integer one. What Route 1 establishes is the cost of going from one to the other: a Euler product
over the critical line. This is NOT a death; it is identifying the toll. The death,
if there is one, has to come from another route showing that toll to be unpayable RH-free.
**[GAP-159.A]:** does there exist a way to transport the prime-completeness to an
individuation conclusion WITHOUT reconstituting the integers (without Euler on the line)? If the
answer is no, Route 1 becomes death; if yes, the thread evades Route 1. I do not close it
here.

## 3. Route 2 — Bohr–Voronin confinement to the value side

This is the route with the most lethal force. The thesis: the ℚ-linear independence of
$\{\log p\}$ produces structure ONLY on the σ-algebra of the VALUES of $\zeta$ (the
Kronecker torus / Bagchi's support), and individuation (position of zeros, inertia) is NOT
measurable with respect to that σ-algebra.

### 3.1. The value σ-algebra and its Diophantine generation

**[DEFINITION 159.6] (value σ-algebra).** Let $D\subset\{\tfrac12<\sigma<1\}$ be a
compact disc. Bagchi's limiting measure $Q$ lives on $H(D)$ (holomorphic functions with the
compact-open topology). Let $\Sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$ be the Borel σ-algebra of $H(D)$
induced by the law of $\zeta(s,\omega)=\prod_p(1-\omega_p p^{-s})^{-1}$ with $\omega$
uniform on $\mathbb T^\infty$. Every event about the VALUES of $\zeta$ in the strip (that
$\zeta$ is close to a given function, that it takes a certain value, etc.) is
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$-measurable.

**[PROPOSITION 159.7] (the Diophantine independence generates EXACTLY $\Sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$).**
The only arithmetic input of Bagchi's limit theorem (Doc 112 §2.4, §3.2) is the
ℚ-linear independence of $\{\log p\}$, which gives the equidistribution of the orbit
$\tau\mapsto(p^{-i\tau})_p$ in $\mathbb T^\infty$ (Kronecker–Weyl). The support of $Q$ is
$$S = \{f\in H(D) : f(s)\ne 0\ \forall s\in D\}\cup\{0\}$$
([Bag81]; [Lau96, Ch. 5–6]; Doc 112 Prop. 2.6). All of the structure of $Q$ —its support,
its moments, Voronin's universality— is a function of the Diophantine independence plus the
random Euler product; NO datum about the position of the zeros of $\zeta$ enters into
the construction of $Q$.

*Proof.* This is the content of Bagchi's theorem as used in Doc 112: the
measure $Q$ is defined by the law of the random product, whose only input is the
independence of the $\omega_p$ (reflection of the ℚ-linear independence of $\{\log p\}$ via
Kronecker–Weyl). The support $S$ is obtained by Hurwitz's theorem applied to the
limits of non-vanishing Euler products. None of this uses where the zeros are. $\square$

### 3.2. Inertia is NOT $\Sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$-measurable

**[PROPOSITION 159.8] (structural blindness of the value measure to individuation).**
The event "ζ has an off-critical zero in the disc $D$" is not decidable from
$Q$, and more strongly: the set of $\tau$ that witness individuation (the $\tau$
for which $\zeta(\cdot+i\tau)|_D$ is close to having an off-critical zero) has
$Q$-measure zero (zero density). Voronin's universality holds IDENTICALLY under RH and
under ¬RH.

*Proof.* (a) Unconditional universality: Voronin's universality theorem
([Vor75]) and Bagchi's limit theorem are UNCONDITIONAL — they do not assume RH and their
statement does not change according to whether RH is true or false (this is the central
point of Bagchi, cited in Doc 112 §0 and the "useless detector" note in §2.4(3)). The
measure $Q$ is the same in both worlds. (b) Zero density of the witnesses: by
Prop. 2.6 of Doc 112 (Bagchi + Hurwitz + Portmanteau), under H(m) (¬RH with finite $m$) the set
$A_\varepsilon = \{\tau : \sup_D|\zeta(\cdot+i\tau)-\zeta|\le\varepsilon\}$ has
UPPER density zero, because the functions close to $\zeta|_D$ (which would have an off
zero) lie outside the support $S$ (which is non-vanishing $\cup\{0\}$). That is: individuation is a
$Q$-null event. (c) Conclusion: a $Q$-null object is not decidable by any functional of $Q$;
individuation is invisible to the value σ-algebra. $\square$

### 3.3. The confinement argument, distilled

**[THEOREM 159.9] (confinement to the value side — Route 2 kills the ambient reading).**
Let $\mathsf P$ be any individuation property (forcing $F_{\mathrm{off}}=0$, deciding
the position of a zero) and let $\mathsf D$ be the use of the ℚ-linear independence of
$\{\log p\}$ as an AMBIENT FACT (that is, through any functional of the limiting
measure $Q$ / of the Kronecker orbit / of Voronin's universality). Then
$\mathsf D$ does not decide $\mathsf P$: every conclusion obtainable from $\mathsf D$ is
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$-measurable (Prop. 159.7), and $\mathsf P$ is not (Prop. 159.8). The
Diophantine independence, read as structure of the value torus, EMPTIES the oscillation of
values (it gives universality, B² almost-periodicity, the support) but is structurally
blind to inertia.

*Proof.* Composition of Prop. 159.7 (everything $\mathsf D$ produces factors through
$Q$, is $\Sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$-measurable) and Prop. 159.8 ($\mathsf P$ is $Q$-null / not
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$-measurable). A measurable functional of $Q$ cannot separate two worlds
(RH/¬RH) that induce the same $Q$. $\square$

**Route 2 verdict: KILLS the reading "independence as ambient fact."** This is Reading
A of §1.1, and is the natural reading of the CTD ("the Diophantine independence produces the
torus, the torus produces universality, universality collapses the defect"). Theorem
159.9 says: NO — universality is exactly what does NOT distinguish RH from ¬RH (Bagchi). The
thread, read as structure of the Kronecker torus, is confined to the value side and dies.

**[GAP-159.B] (the crack Route 2 does NOT cover).** Theorem 159.9 covers the use of
independence as an AMBIENT fact (via the measure $Q$, which is an AVERAGE functional in
$\tau$). It does NOT cover the use of independence as a POINTWISE-QUANTITATIVE condition: an
irrationality measure of $\{\log p\}$, or a discrepancy bound of the Kronecker flow
controlling the orbit in a FIXED window (the disc of the zero), not an average over $\tau$.
This is precisely what escapes $\Sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$ because it is NOT a functional of
$Q$ (which averages over $\tau$). The remaining residue of life in the thread is exactly Route 3.

## 4. Route 3 — Reintroduction of zeros (as Beurling–Malliavin in D157)

Route 2 confined the ambient reading. What remains is the pointwise-quantitative reading
(GAP-159.B). Route 3 proves the dichotomy: qualitative = unconditional but useless; quantitative =
reintroduces the zeros.

### 4.1. QUALITATIVE completeness: unconditional and useless

**[PROPOSITION 159.10] (the qualitative completeness of $\{p^{i\tau}\}$ is unconditional).**
The ℚ-linear independence of $\{\log p\}$ (unique factorization) implies, by Kronecker–Weyl,
that the flow $\tau\mapsto(p^{i\tau})_p$ is minimal and equidistributed in $\mathbb T^\infty$.
In particular the linear span of $\{p^{i\tau}\}$ is complete in the Kronecker sense
(the orbit is dense). This is unconditional.

*Proof.* Doc 112 Lemmas 2.2 and 3.3: independence is unique factorization, and
Kronecker–Weyl gives minimality and equidistribution. $\square$

**[PROPOSITION 159.11] (but the qualitative completeness is useless for individuation).**
The density of the orbit in $\mathbb T^\infty$ is an AVERAGE statement (the orbit visits
every neighborhood with positive density in $\tau$). By Obs. 2.4 / Prop. 2.6 of Doc 112, the
individuation (the goodness in the fixed disc of the zero) lives in a zero-density coordinate,
invisible to the average. Qualitative completeness gives exactly the average side of the
master quantifier; it does not cross.

*Proof.* This is the translation of Obs. 2.4 of Doc 112 (the B² seminorm annihilates
compacts) and of Prop. 159.8 (the individuation witnesses are $Q$-null) into the language of
orbits: the orbit density is positive in $\tau$, but the individuation condition is
realized on a zero-density subset of $\tau$. Orbit density ⇏ presence in the
exceptional subset. $\square$

**This is exactly the structure of Doc 157 (T5/LP-152) and of Doc 112 (LP-112).** The
qualitative completeness is the "surviving candidate" that survives as a STATEMENT but whose
individuation verification fails. The qualitative thread is RH-free but useless.

### 4.2. QUANTITATIVE completeness: reintroduces the zeros — the exact parallel with D157

For the thread to CROSS (not merely survive), it needs a QUANTITATIVE version: an
effective bound on the equidistribution rate / on the completeness radius that is fine down
to the scale of the disc of the zero. Here I reproduce the death of Doc 157.

**[PROPOSITION 159.12] (the classical quantification reintroduces the zeros, as with Beurling–Malliavin).**
Every quantification of the completeness of $\{p^{i\tau}\}$ of the type "Beurling–Malliavin
radius / effective density of the frequency set $\{\log p\}$ sufficient to control
$\zeta$ on the disc $D$" reintroduces the zero count, by the same mechanism of Doc 157
Prop. 157.7.

*Proof.* The Beurling–Malliavin theorem (Doc 157 §3.3; Docs 82–85) crosses
spectral-density → pointwise-control. The spectral density relevant to controlling
$\zeta$ on the disc of the zero is NOT the raw density of $\{\log p\}$ (that one is unconditional:
$\#\{p\le e^u\}\sim e^u/u$ by PNT), but the RESONANCE density — where the transform
of the prime measure grows, which by the explicit formula (Doc 157 identity 152.E) IS the
density of off zeros. Any quantitative version of Beurling–Malliavin over the
frequencies $\{\log p\}$ that is fine down to the scale of the zero is, by the same argument
of Doc 157, deciding the density of zeros in that window. Circle in the input of
Beurling–Malliavin: input (resonance density) = output (position of zeros). $\square$

**This is death by reintroduction of zeros, identical to D157.** If the quantification
the thread needs is of the "effective density of the spectral set" type, it is dead:
it is Beurling–Malliavin again, and it reintroduces the divisor of $\zeta$.

### 4.3. The slit: is there a Diophantine quantification that is NOT resonance density?

Here I am scrupulously frank, because this is where the death might not be consummated.

The ℚ-linear independence of $\{\log p\}$ is a QUALITATIVE statement (yes/no about the
existence of relations $\sum a_p\log p = 0$ with $a_p\in\mathbb Z$). Its NATURAL
quantitative form is not a resonance density, but a **simultaneous irrationality measure /
discrepancy of the Kronecker flow**: bounds of the type
$$\Bigl\| \sum_p a_p \log p \Bigr\| \;\ge\; \Phi\bigl((|a_p|)_p\bigr) \qquad (\text{distance to the nearest integer}),$$
which control how well the orbit $\tau\mapsto(p^{i\tau})_p$ approximates a point of the torus
as a function of time $\tau$ — i.e. the effective discrepancy in a window of $\tau$.

**[PROPOSITION 159.13] (the genuine Diophantine quantification is NOT, a priori, a
resonance density).** The irrationality measure / discrepancy of the Kronecker flow of
$\{\log p\}$ is a property of the NUMBERS $\{\log p\}$ as points of $\mathbb R$
(Diophantine theory: simultaneous approximation, linear forms in logarithms), NOT a
