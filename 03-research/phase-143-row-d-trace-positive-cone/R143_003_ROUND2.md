## 2. The two pieces of new mathematics this round produced

### 2.1 Lemma D.34.9′ — `(G⁺)` is unconditional (H2, and it closes O-25)

The auditor's H2 asked only for a quantifier. The coordinator asked the sharper question — *can
`(G⁺)` be replaced by an unconditional condition on `g`, and in particular, does the canonical class
work?* — and the answer turns the finding into an improvement.

**First, why `K` cannot be the test class (Remark D.34.1a).** `K` is real, even, `K > 0`, in `D^W` —
and **not admissible**, failing the Baire condition *maximally*: `Φ_K(1−ρ) = ξ(1−ρ) = 0` at **every**
zero. This is not a technicality. With `g = K` the traces are purely polar,
`t_n(E) = ½(n^{½}Φ_E(0) + n^{−½}Φ_E(1))`, so **every primitive class would satisfy `t_n ≡ 0 ≥ 0`** and
(T1′) — the round's main theorem — would be **false**. The Baire condition is precisely what makes the
traces see the zero side, and it is used in exactly one line (the last line of Theorem D.26.5's proof,
inherited by Theorem D.34.4(ii)).

**Second, the repair.** Take `g_λ := K + λh` with `h` admissible (Lemma D.26.1) and `λ > 0` small:

    Φ_{g_λ}(ρ) = λ\,Φ_h(ρ) \quad(\text{since } Φ_K(ρ) = 0),\qquad
    Φ_{g_λ}(0) = Φ_{g_λ}(1) = ½ + λΦ_h(0) > 0 .

So `g_λ` is admissible (the Baire condition and the rapid decay are inherited from `h`), while

    W^{\mathrm{abs}}(g_λ) = λ²\,W^{\mathrm{abs}}(h) ≤ \tfrac12 ≤ 2\,Φ_{g_λ}(0)Φ_{g_λ}(1)
    \qquad\text{for } λ ≤ \big(2W^{\mathrm{abs}}(h)\big)^{−1/2} ,

which is `(G⁺)` in the absolute (unconditional) form of Definition D.34.8b; equivalently
`I_∞(g_λ,g_λ) = ½ + λ\deg h + λ²I_∞(h,h) > 0`, using `I_∞(K,K) = ½` and `I_∞(K,h) = ½\deg h`
(Theorem 11.8(i)). **One line, unconditional, no Baire perturbation, no RH.** The mechanism is worth
naming: *the canonical class is invisible to the zero side and dominant on the polar side, so buying
the Baire condition from `h` costs `O(λ)` on the polar side and `O(λ²)` on the zero side — and `(G⁺)`
is exactly the comparison of those two sides.*

**Third, what `(G⁺)` is.** By (11.6) and Theorem 11.5, `2Φ_g(0)Φ_g(1) − W(g) = I_∞(g,g)` is the
arithmetic bracket `PRIME − ARCH` of the even part of `g⋆g^∨`: `(G⁺)` is a **zero-free, arithmetic**
condition on the test class, not a spectral one. (Verifying it for a *prescribed* `g` by a Chebyshev
bound is the route the corpus records as a no-go — `phase-114/114_d_185`, `R138_001` D.17.3 — which
is why the `K + λh` route, which never computes a prime sum, is the right one.)

*Consequences:* O-25 **closed**; Theorem D.34.10 uses RH exactly **twice** (`W(E) ≥ 0`,
`|n^{½−ρ}| = 1`); Corollary D.34.12's `∃g` is **constructive**.

### 2.2 Proposition D.34.16 — the clause is a finite condition (the auditor's item-5(4), proved and extended)

*(Full statement, proof, the `d = 400` example and the `Ξ_a` run are in `R143_001` §D.34.4; the exact
statement is reproduced in §3 below at the coordinator's request.)*

The content, in one line: **`(b′)_{B4}` is not an infinite family of conditions per class — it is a
finite one.** If `b = Φ_E(0) > 0`, the polar part of `t_n(E)` grows like `bΦ_g(1)n^{½}` while every
zero term is `O(n^{½−\mathrm{Re}ρ})` with `\mathrm{Re}\,ρ > 0` strictly by (E2), so the zero part is
`o(n^{½})` by dominated convergence — **unconditionally**, even if `\inf_ρ\mathrm{Re}\,ρ = 0`, in
which case the resulting `N(E)` is finite but not effective. Under RH everything is effective and
uniform on bounded-degree slices (`N ≤ 64d²`).

Three things this buys, none of them a sign:
* it **localises** O-24 to a finite head `n < N(E)` per class, which is where an unconditional attack
  must live;
* it makes the phase-142 witness a `15`-inequality check (`N ≤ 15.96`), consistent with J3's uniform
  margin;
* run against the falsifier it says something sharper than anything in round 1: **`Ξ_a` also has
  eventually positive traces**, so the failure of `(b′)_{B4_a}` is confined to the finite head — *the
  A3-line is in the head, not in the tail*. The head is exactly where `ξ`'s **two** polar exponents
  give the AM–GM floor `2\sqrt{ab\,Φ_g(0)Φ_g(1)}` of Theorem D.34.10 and `Ξ_a`'s **four** give none.

## 3. The exact statement of Proposition D.34.16 (as requested)

> **Proposition D.34.16.** Let `E ∈ D^W` be real, `a := Φ_E(1)`, `b := Φ_E(0)`,
> `c_ρ := Φ_E(ρ)Φ_g(1−ρ)`, `S(E) := Σ_ρ|c_ρ| < ∞`, and
> `β_{\min}(E) := \inf\{\mathrm{Re}\,ρ : Φ_E(ρ) ≠ 0\} ∈ [0,1]`. **In (D.34.1) it is `b = Φ_E(0)`
> that pairs with `n^{½}` and `a = Φ_E(1)` with `n^{−½}`**, and `|n^{½−ρ}| = n^{½−\mathrm{Re}ρ}`.
> Then:
> **(i)** *(unconditional, non-effective)* if `b > 0` then
> `t_n(E) ≥ n^{½}\big(bΦ_g(1) − Σ_ρ|c_ρ|n^{−\mathrm{Re}ρ}\big) − |a|Φ_g(0)n^{−½}` and
> `Σ_ρ|c_ρ|n^{−\mathrm{Re}ρ} → 0` (dominated convergence, using `\mathrm{Re}\,ρ > 0` by (E2) and
> `S(E) < ∞`); hence there is a finite `N(E)` with `t_n(E) > 0` for all `n ≥ N(E)`.
> **(ii)** *(effective when `β := β_{\min}(E) > 0`)*
> `N(E) = \max\{(2S(E)/(bΦ_g(1)))^{1/β},\; 2|a|Φ_g(0)/(bΦ_g(1))\}`.
> **(iii)** *(the reduction)* hence `E ∈ B4` is the **finite** statement `t_n(E) ≥ 0` for
> `1 ≤ n < N(E)`; and under RH, on `\{R(E) > 0, \deg E > 0\}` one has `b > 0`, `β = ½`,
> `S(E) ≤ \sqrt{W(E)W(g)}` and `N(E) ≤ 4W(E)W(g)/(b²Φ_g(1)²)`.
> **(iv)** *(uniformity)* under RH, with `(G⁺)` and `g` even, on
> `\{R(E) > 0,\ \deg E > 0,\ \deg E ≤ d\}` one has `a,b > ¼`, `W(E) < 2ab ≤ d²/2`, hence
> **`N(E) ≤ 64d²` uniformly**.
> **Labels:** (i),(ii) `PROVED` unconditional; (iii),(iv) `PROVED` under RH.
> **Corollary D.34.16a (`PROVED`).** `(b′)_{B4}` for `h⁰_{RR}^{+}` is equivalent to: *for every real
> `E` with `R(E) > 0`, `\deg E > 0`:* `b > 0` *and* `t_n(E) ≥ 0` *for the finitely many `n < N(E)`*.
> Unconditionally the range is **not** known to be uniform, for exactly two reasons: `β_{\min}(E)`
> may be `0`, and `b > 0` may fail (off RH it does not follow from `R(E) > 0`, since `W(E)` may be
> negative).
> **Example D.34.16b (`CERTIFIED`).** The D.31.10 witness (`a = b = 200`, `W(E) = 79780`, `d = 400`)
> has `N(E) ≤ 4·79780·2p_0²/(200²p_0²) = 15.956`: **only `n ≤ 15` is at issue**, and J3 verified all
> `n` with the uniform margin `0.55\sqrt{Φ_g(0)Φ_g(1)}`. The slice bound (iv) gives `64d² = 1.02×10^7`
> at `d = 400`, three orders worse — the slice bound is dominated by the corner `a` or `b ↓ ¼`.
> **Remark D.34.16c (`PROVED`).** For `Ξ_a` the leading polar exponent is `n^{½+a}` and the zero
> exponents are `n^{½−\mathrm{Re}ρ}` with `\mathrm{Re}\,ρ ∈ (−a,1+a)` **strictly**, so (i) transports:
> traces are eventually positive there too. The violation of `(b′)_{B4_a}` therefore sits at a fixed
> finite index: with `E_r = rF_+ + K_a` (`F_+` of Prop. 12.4B, `q_a > 0`, so `R_a(E_r) → ∞` and
> `\deg_a E_r > 0` independently of the sign of `r`), `F_+ ∉ 𝓝_∞^{(a)}` gives some `m` with
> `t^{(a)}_m(F_+) ≠ 0`, and replacing `r` by `−r` if necessary makes
> `t^{(a)}_m(E_r) = t^{(a)}_m(K_a) + r\,t^{(a)}_m(F_+) < 0` for `|r|` large, with `m < N_a(E_r)`.
