### D.33.2 The elementary lower bound, and monotonicity

**Lemma D.33.2 (`PROVED`, three lines, unconditional).** For every `γ > 0`,

    L(γ) := \frac1{\cosh\big(π/(4γ)\big)} \;≤\; m(γ) \;≤\; s(γ,π/2) \;<\; 1 ,

and `L` is strictly increasing in `γ` with `L(γ) → 1`.

*Proof.* Fix `θ`. The set `\{u : |\cos(γu+θ)| = 1\}` is `\{(kπ−θ)/γ\}_{k∈Z}`, an arithmetic

infimum over `θ` gives `L(γ) ≤ m(γ)`. The upper bound is the special value `θ = π/2`, and
`s(γ,θ) < 1` for `γ ≠ 0`, `θ ∉ πZ` is Proposition D.31.5. Monotonicity of `L` is monotonicity of

### D.34.2 The decidable first question (T1), decided

The key is that the trace index runs over the **multiplicative semigroup `N`**, so the generating
function of the traces is a Dirichlet series and the samples are not merely "a discrete set".

**Lemma D.34.3 (the trace Dirichlet series; `PROVED`, unconditional).** Let `E ∈ D^W` be real and put
`c_ρ := Φ_E(ρ)Φ_g(1−ρ)`. Then `|t_n(E)| ≤ A(E)\,n^{½}` for all `n ≥ 1`, the Dirichlet series

    D_E(w) := Σ_{n≥1} t_n(E)\,n^{−w}

converges absolutely for `\mathrm{Re}\,w > 3/2`, and there

    D_E(w) = Φ_g(1)\,b\,ζ(w−½) + Φ_g(0)\,a\,ζ(w+½) − Σ_ρ c_ρ\,ζ(w+ρ−½) .   (D.34.2)

The right-hand side continues `D_E` **meromorphically to all of `C`**, holomorphic except for at most
simple poles at

    w = 3/2 \;(\text{residue } Φ_g(1)b),\quad w = ½ \;(\text{residue } Φ_g(0)a),\quad
    w = 3/2−ρ \;(\text{residue } −m(ρ)\,c_ρ) ,

`m(ρ) ≥ 1` the multiplicity. Every pole `3/2−ρ` has `\mathrm{Re}(3/2−ρ) ∈ (½,3/2)` and
`\mathrm{Im}(3/2−ρ) = −γ_ρ ≠ 0`.

*Proof.* `|n^{½−ρ}| = n^{½−β_ρ} ≤ n^{½}` by (E2) (`0 < β_ρ < 1`), and `Σ_ρ|c_ρ| < ∞` (proof of
Theorem D.26.5: `|Φ_E(ρ)| ≤ C_2(E)|γ_ρ|^{−2}` by (11.2), `|Φ_g(1−ρ)| ≤ C_M(1+|γ_ρ|)^{−M}` by `M`
integrations by parts, `Σ(1+|γ_ρ|)^{−2} < ∞` by (E3d)); with `|n^{±½}| = n^{½}` this gives the bound
on `|t_n|` and absolute convergence of `Σ t_n n^{−w}` for `\mathrm{Re}\,w > 3/2`. Term-by-term
summation of (D.34.1) against `n^{−w}` is legitimate there by the same absolute convergence, and
`Σ_n n^{½−ρ−w} = ζ(w+ρ−½)`; this is (D.34.2). The continuation is the argument of Lemma D.26.4
verbatim: on a compact `𝒦 ⊂ C`, only finitely many `ρ` have `3/2−ρ` within distance `1` of `𝒦` (since
`|γ_ρ| → ∞`), and for the rest `|ζ(w+ρ−½)| ≪ (1+|γ_ρ|)^{A_0}` by the classical polynomial bound
(`CLASSICAL`), so the tail converges uniformly on `𝒦` by the rapid decay of `(c_ρ)`. The residues are
`\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}ζ = 1` times the coefficients, with multiplicity as in Lemma D.26.4's repair note.
`γ_ρ ≠ 0` because `ξ` has **no real zero** (Paper 46 Lemma 13.5b: `ξ > 0` on `R`; classical). ∎
`PROVED`

**Import (X20) (`CLASSICAL`).** *Landau's theorem for Dirichlet series:* if `D(w) = Σa_nn^{−w}` has
`a_n ≥ 0` for all `n` and finite abscissa of convergence `σ_c`, then `w = σ_c` is a **singular point**
of every analytic continuation of `D`. (Landau 1905; Titchmarsh, *The Theory of Functions*, §9.2;
Hardy–Riesz, *The General Theory of Dirichlet's Series*, Thm 10.) *Corpus status:* the Laplace-integral
form of the same theorem is already in the corpus and already used on row D — `R138_001` Theorem
D.17.2 (Widder II.5b), `R139_001` D.19.5; see §Corpus check. The **mechanism is not new**; the object
it is applied to (the traces `t_n`, a Dirichlet series in the arithmetic index `n`) is.
