# R163_162 — Two scale orientations and the exact Euler product of the integer comb

**Status:** exact algebraic factorization in distributions, with finite prime cutoffs. It is not a first-contact identity nor a sign; Row D remains open.

Let `G∈C_c^∞(ℝ)`, `τ_aG(u)=G(u+a)`, and for each prime `p` set

```
T_p=p^(−1/2)τ_(−log p),
U_p=p^(1/2)τ_(−log p).                                (1)
```

Then `||T_p||=p^(−1/2)<1`, `||U_p^(−1)||=p^(−1/2)<1` and, crucially,

```
U_p^(−1)=T_p^*.                                      (2)
```

For a finite set `P` of primes, define the causal product

```
E_P^+G:=∏_(p∈P)(I−T_p)^(−1)G
       =Σ_(n∈⟨P⟩) n^(−1/2)τ_(−log n)G,                (3)
```

where `⟨P⟩` is the semigroup of integers generated by `P`. The equality converges in `L²` for finite `P` (a product of geometric series of operators with norm less than one). In particular, each `n∈⟨P⟩` appears **exactly once**: this uses unique factorization and is exactly `b_ζ(n)=1`. If the Euler factor of `2` is duplicated `K` times, the corresponding factor becomes `(I−T_2)^(−K−1)`, and `2^j`'s coefficient becomes `binom(K+j,j)=b_K(2^j)`, as in `R163_147`.

The unstable dual product is

```
E_P^∨G:=∏_(p∈P) [Σ_(r≥0)U_p^r]G
       =Σ_(n∈⟨P⟩) n^(1/2)τ_(−log n)G.                (4)
```

Each sum in (4) is locally finite as a distribution: for `u` in a compact set, only the `n` with `log n` in a compact interval determined by `supp G` contribute. Moreover,

```
∏_(p∈P)(I−U_p) E_P^∨G=G.                            (5)
```

Product (4) is not a bounded operator `L²→L²`; precisely for that reason each dual factor's stable solution uses negative powers `U_p^(−r)=(T_p^*)^r` and lives in the **opposite spatial sense**. Replacing (4) with that stable solution loses `R163_161`'s homogeneous components. Formulas (2)–(5) exhibit an exact causal/anticausal pair between the integer comb and the Euler–Poisson crossing.

As `P` grows to include all primes, for each fixed `u` the causal sum in (3) stabilizes locally to

```
E_ℕ^+G(u)=Σ_(n≥1)n^(−1/2)G(u−log n),                (6)
```

which is the ordinary comb with coefficients **exactly one**. The sum is locally finite, although `L²` convergence of the infinite product is not asserted. The same local stabilization holds for (4), with coefficient `√n`. This observation does not replace `(PC-ren)`'s polar subtractions, nor does it prove the two orientations share the same energy limit.

**Logical limit.** The factorization holds for every `G`; it uses neither the radical, the Gamma factor, nor the functional equation. So it does not imply `R161_030`'s saturation nor the vanishing of the scattering defect. The next step would have to build a physical energy identity that simultaneously preserves (2), the infinite product's polar term, and the exterior terms, and whose first-contact side has the required sign. Declaring (3)=(4) after changing weights would be false.

## Audit of the finite energy: the unitary phase is not the gate

On the critical line `s=1/2+iτ`, (3)'s multiplier is the finite Euler product

```
Z_P(s)=∏_(p∈P)(1−p^(−s))^(−1),
(E_P^+)^* ↔ Z_P(1−s).                                (7)
```

Consequently `E_P^+[(E_P^+)^*]^(−1)` is unitary, with symbol `Z_P(s)/Z_P(1−s)`. After inserting the unitary Gamma/Fourier multiplier, this is the same finite colligation structure as `R161_052 §1–2`, though here the cutoff is by primes and there by a renormalized sum. The compression-defect identity of a unitary gives a positive exterior square, but its **phase tangent** differentiates the mean orientation, not the critical difference, as `R161_056` proves. So the adjoint relation (2), alone, does not add the needed sign to the gate. Moreover, (6)'s local distributional convergence does not license passing `P→all primes` in `L²` norms or in multiplier quotients over `Re s=1/2`: the Euler product does not converge absolutely there. Factorization (3) preserves the exact multiplicities; using it in a proof requires additionally keeping the compression block and the polar term, not just the unitary quotient.

## Physical collar of a prime: exact computation

Let `P_T=1_[−T,T]`, `Q_T=I−P_T`, and `F=P_TF`. Since `T_p` shifts the support to the right, with `ℓ_p=log p`,

```
||Q_T T_pF||²
 =p^(−1)∫_(max(−T,T−ℓ_p))^T |F(t)|²dt.            (8)
```

The adjoint shifts to the left and gives

```
||Q_T T_p^*F||²
 =p^(−1)∫_(−T)^(min(T,−T+ℓ_p)) |F(t)|²dt.        (9)
```

In particular, for `p>e^(2T)` both collars are `p^(−1)||F||²`: the prime is invisible to `P_T T_pP_T`, but **not** to the exterior leak. Formulas (8)–(9) are exact positive squares. However, product (3)'s leak also contains every cross term between integers, with ratio frequencies `m/n` per `R161_034`; summing only (8) over primes does not compute that leak. Any passage to infinity must also compensate the growth of the universal tail via `(PC-ren)`'s polar term before inferring a first-contact sign.
