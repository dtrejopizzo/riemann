**Task.** Find a mechanism that uses the *global* integrality of the frequencies of $\zeta$ (the fact that $-\zeta'/\zeta$ is supported on $\log\mathbb N$ at *every* height; Hamburger-type rigidity) to prove $(\star_{\rm cell})$ (R163_401 §4.8) at the hypothetical first-contact witness, in a way that is **not** reducible to "$\zeta/E$ is an ordinary Dirichlet series" (R163_113/114) nor to "$\zeta=\zeta$" (R162_2801). Three angles were prescribed: (A) deformation $f_t\to\zeta$, (B) theta/modular periodicity applied to the witness, (C) a window Hamburger theorem against the exterior field.

**Answer in one sentence.** All three are NO-GO, for one reason stated exactly in §5: at conductor 1 the class "integer frequencies + $\zeta$'s functional equation" is the single point $\{c\zeta\}$, so global integrality can only *identify* $\zeta$; in every enlargement of the class in which integrality still has content (conductor $q>1$) there are members with integer frequencies, $\zeta$'s exact $\Gamma$-shape, $2$-periodic modular theta functions **and off-line zeros** (Theorem B.3: even-character Davenport–Heilbronn functions). Integrality therefore never carries the sign; and every window invariant is continuous in the deformation (Theorem A.3), so no integer jump is forced at $t=0$ except by the kernel of $W^\zeta_T$ itself, which is $\neg$RH.

**Lemma 1.1 (even Davenport–Heilbronn function). PROVED.** Let $\chi$ be a character mod $7$ of order $3$ ($\chi(3)=e^{2\pi i/3}$, $3$ a generator). Then $\chi(-1)=\chi(6)=\chi(3)^3=1$: $\chi$ is even and primitive. Let $\varepsilon_\chi=\tau(\chi)/\sqrt7$ be its root number, $|\varepsilon_\chi|=1$, so that $\Lambda(s,\chi):=(7/\pi)^{s/2}\Gamma(s/2)L(s,\chi)$ satisfies $\Lambda(s,\chi)=\varepsilon_\chi\Lambda(1-s,\bar\chi)$ and $\varepsilon_\chi\varepsilon_{\bar\chi}=1$. Choose $\alpha=e^{i\varphi}$ with $e^{2i\varphi}=\bar\varepsilon_\chi$ and put
$$f_\chi(s):=\alpha L(s,\chi)+\bar\alpha L(s,\bar\chi)=\sum_{n\ge1}a(n)n^{-s},\qquad a(n)=2\,\mathrm{Re}\big(\alpha\chi(n)\big)\in\mathbb R .$$
Then: (i) $\mathrm{Fr}(f_\chi)\subset\mathbb N$ and $a$ is $7$-periodic with $a(1)=2\cos\varphi$; (ii) $\Lambda_\chi(s):=(7/\pi)^{s/2}\Gamma(s/2)f_\chi(s)$ is entire of order $1$ and $\Lambda_\chi(s)=\Lambda_\chi(1-s)$; (iii) $\theta_\chi(z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}a(|n|)e^{i\pi n^2z/7}$ (with $a(0):=0$) satisfies $\theta_\chi(z+14)=\theta_\chi(z)$, and in the variable $z'=z/7$ it is $2$-periodic; $\theta_\chi(-1/z)=(z/i)^{1/2}\theta_\chi(z)$ up to the standard conductor normalisation; i.e. $\theta_\chi$ is a weight-$\frac12$ form for a congruence subgroup (finite covolume).
*Proof.* (i) is the definition; $a(n)=\alpha\chi(n)+\bar\alpha\bar\chi(n)$ is real. (ii) $\Lambda_\chi(s)=\alpha\varepsilon_\chi\Lambda(1-s,\bar\chi)+\bar\alpha\varepsilon_{\bar\chi}\Lambda(1-s,\chi)$. Now $\alpha\varepsilon_\chi=e^{i\varphi}\varepsilon_\chi=\bar\alpha\cdot e^{2i\varphi}\varepsilon_\chi=\bar\alpha$, and $\bar\alpha\varepsilon_{\bar\chi}=\bar\alpha\bar\varepsilon_\chi=\alpha$. So $\Lambda_\chi(s)=\bar\alpha\Lambda(1-s,\bar\chi)+\alpha\Lambda(1-s,\chi)=\Lambda_\chi(1-s)$. Entireness and order $1$: each $\Lambda(\cdot,\chi)$ is entire of order $1$ ($\chi$ non-principal). (iii) $n^2\equiv n'^2$ gives nothing to check: $e^{i\pi n^2(z+14)/7}=e^{i\pi n^2z/7}e^{2\pi in^2}=e^{i\pi n^2z/7}$. The inversion is the theta transformation for even primitive characters (Mellin transform of (ii)). ∎

## 2. Angle (A): deformation $f_t\to\zeta$. NO-GO (PROVED)

Write $\lambda_T(t):=\lambda^{\rm W}_T(f_t)$ (bottom of $W^{f_t}_T$ on $L^2(I_T)$), $N(T;t):=n_-(W^{f_t}_T)$ (Sonin count of R162_2602 Def 5, on the full space; the primitive version $n_-(\mathfrak a_T^{f_t}|_{\mathcal P_T})$ behaves identically), $T_c(t):=\inf\{T:\lambda_T(t)\le0\}$.

**Proposition A.1 (window locality in $t$). PROVED.** If $N_0\ge e^{2T}$ then $W^{f_t}_T=W^\zeta_T$ for every $t\in\mathbb R$. Hence $\lambda_T(t)$, $N(T;t)$, the kernel, the witness, its nodes and $(\star_{\rm cell})$'s truth value at $T$ are independent of $t$; and if $T_c(\zeta)<\frac12\log N_0$ then $T_c(t)=T_c(\zeta)$ for all $t$ with the same witness.
*Proof.* R163_901 Thm F(3): $b_t(\lambda)=\Lambda(\lambda)$ for $\lambda<N_0$ and $b_t(\lambda)=0$ for non-integer $\lambda<N_0$; the window symbol uses only $\lambda<e^{2T}\le N_0$ (R163_401 §4.6). The rest is R163_901 Prop 1.1. ∎

**Proposition A.2 (analytic dependence for $T\ge\frac12\log N_0$). PROVED.** Fix $T$. Then $W^{f_t}_T=W^\zeta_T+\sum_{k=1}^{K}t^kB_{T,k}$ with $K\le1+2T/\log N_0$ and each $B_{T,k}$ a finite comb form (bounded on $L^2(I_T)$). Consequently the eigenvalues of $W^{f_t}_T$ (compact resolvent) are continuous in $t$, $\lambda_T(\cdot)$ is continuous, and $N(T;\cdot)$ is lower semicontinuous and locally constant on $\{t:0\notin\mathrm{spec}\,W^{f_t}_T\}$.
*Proof.* $-f_t'/f_t=-(\zeta'+tg')\zeta^{-1}\sum_{k\ge0}(-tg/\zeta)^k$ in a right half-plane. $g/\zeta$ has frequencies $\ge N_0$, so $(g/\zeta)^k$ has frequencies $\ge N_0^k$, and the coefficient $b_t(\lambda)$ of a fixed $\lambda<e^{2T}$ receives contributions only from $k\le\log\lambda/\log N_0$: $b_t(\lambda)$ is a polynomial in $t$ of degree $\le1+2T/\log N_0$. The window form is a finite linear combination of the $b_t(\lambda)$ against shift forms $c_F(\pm\log\lambda)$, each bounded by $2\lVert F\rVert^2$. Continuity of eigenvalues under bounded form perturbations is standard (Kato); lower semicontinuity of $n_-$ and local constancy away from $0\in\mathrm{spec}$ follow. ∎

**Theorem A.3 (no forced jump). PROVED.**
- (a) Under RH, for every $T$ and every $N_0,M$: $N(T;t)=0$ and $\ker W^{f_t}_T=0$ for all $|t|<t_0(T)$. *Proof.* Under RH, $W^\zeta_T(F)=\sum_\gamma|\widehat F(\gamma)|^2$, and $\ker W^\zeta_T=0$ (R163_401 Prop 4.0 ⇐, general complex case); the bottom is attained (compact resolvent), so $\lambda_T(0)>0$; by A.2, $\lambda_T(t)>0$ for small $t$. ∎
- (b) If for some $T$ the function $t\mapsto N(T;t)$ is not constant on a neighbourhood of $0$, then $0\in\mathrm{spec}\,W^\zeta_T$; in particular $\neg$RH. *Proof.* Contrapositive of A.2's local constancy plus (a). ∎
- (c) $\liminf_{t\to0}T_c(t)\ge T_c(0)$; and if $\lambda_T(0)<0$ for every $T>T_c(0)$, then $T_c(t)\to T_c(0)$. Under RH, $T_c(t)\to\infty$. *Proof.* For $T<T_c(0)$, $\lambda_T(0)>0$, so $\lambda_T(t)>0$ for small $t$ and $T_c(t)\ge T$. If $\lambda_{T'}(0)<0$ then $\lambda_{T'}(t)<0$ for small $t$ and $T_c(t)\le T'$. ∎

*Reading.* The only integer-valued window invariant that can jump at $t=0$ is a count whose jump is produced by a zero eigenvalue of $\zeta$'s own form; that zero eigenvalue is the witness. Integrality forces nothing: in (a) all of $\zeta$'s integrality is in place and nothing jumps. "A jump at $t=0$" is therefore a restatement of "$\zeta$ has a kernel at some $T$" (E1), and the jump, when present, is in the wrong direction for a contradiction (it is *allowed*, not forbidden).

**Proposition A.4 (global integer invariants are decoupled from $T_c$). PROVED.** The following invariants of the whole function jump at $t=0$: (i) the rank of the $\mathbb Z$-module generated by $\log\mathrm{Fr}(f_t)$ modulo $\mathbb Q\log\mathbb N$ ($0$ for $\zeta$, $1$ for $t\ne0$: $\log(m/\sqrt M)=\log m-\frac12\log M$ with $\sqrt M\notin\mathbb Q$); (ii) the minimal period of $\theta_t$ ($2\to2M$, Lemma B.1); (iii) the dimension of the space of self-dual measures with the given hole, i.e. of the Hamburger-type class containing $f_t$ ($1\to\ge\lfloor M/4\rfloor-R-1$, R163_901 Lemma 2.1). None of them bounds $T_c$: for every $N_0$ and $M$, the deformed values hold for all $t\ne0$ while (RH) $T_c(t)\ge\frac12\log N_0$ for all $t$ and $T_c(t)\to\infty$ as $t\to0$, or ($\neg$RH, $T_c(0)<\frac12\log N_0$) $T_c(t)=T_c(0)$ for all $t$. So no inequality "deformed invariant $\Rightarrow T_c\le C$" holds uniformly in $t$, and "undeformed invariant $\Rightarrow T_c\ge X$" is RH at scale $X$.
*Proof.* (i)–(iii) as stated; the $T_c$ statements are Prop A.1 and Thm A.3(c). ∎

**Verdict (A): NO-GO (PROVED).** Every window invariant is continuous at $t=0$ unless $\zeta$ itself has a zero eigenvalue; every global invariant that jumps is decoupled from $T_c$. The deformation sees integrality only as the label "$t=0$".

## 3. Angle (B): Hamburger's periodicity applied to the witness. NO-GO (PROVED)

**Lemma B.1 (theta of $f_t$). PROVED.** For $\mathrm{Im}\,z>0$ let $\theta_t(z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{i\pi n^2z}+t\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}c(m)e^{i\pi m^2z/M}$. Then (a) $\theta_t(-1/z)=(z/i)^{1/2}\theta_t(z)$ for all $t$; (b) $\theta_t(z+2M)=\theta_t(z)$; (c) if $M$ is squarefree, $\theta_t(z+2)=\theta_t(z)$ iff $t=0$.
*Proof.* (a) Each summand is the theta of a self-dual measure ($\sum_n\delta_n$ by Poisson; $\nu=\sum c(n)\delta_{n/\sqrt M}$ by R163_901 Lemma 2.1), and for $\widehat\mu=\mu$ one has $\int e^{-\pi\lambda^2x}d\mu=x^{-1/2}\int e^{-\pi\xi^2/x}d\mu$. (b) $e^{i\pi m^2(z+2M)/M}=e^{i\pi m^2z/M}e^{2\pi im^2}$. (c) $\theta_t(z+2)-\theta_t(z)=t\sum_mc(m)(e^{2\pi im^2/M}-1)e^{i\pi m^2z/M}$; the exponents $m^2/M$, $m\ge0$, are distinct, so this vanishes iff $c(m)(e^{2\pi im^2/M}-1)=0$ for all $m$, i.e. $c(m)=0$ unless $M\mid m^2$, i.e. (squarefree $M$) unless $M\mid m$. Then $c$, being $M$-periodic, is $c(0)\mathbf 1_{M\mathbb Z}$, and $c(0)=0$ by the hole; so $c\equiv0$, excluded. ∎

So $2$-periodicity of the theta function is precisely the property of $\zeta$ that $f_t$ lacks, and it is the property Hamburger's proof uses:

**Proposition B.2 (the mechanism, and why it is sign-free for $\zeta$). PROVED.** (a) *Siegel's form of Hamburger.* Given $f=\sum a_nn^{-s}$, $g=\sum b_nn^{-s}$ with $\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(\frac s2)f(s)=\pi^{-(1-s)/2}\Gamma(\frac{1-s}2)g(1-s)$ and finite order, the Mellin inversion of the FE yields, for $\mathrm{Re}\,y>0$, an identity of the shape
$$\sum_{n\ge1}b_ne^{-2\pi ny}=\text{(elementary)}+C\sum_{n\ge1}a_n\frac{y}{n^2+y^2},$$
whose left side is invariant under $y\mapsto y+i$ **because $n\in\mathbb Z$**, while the right side is meromorphic with poles at $y=\pm in$ and residues $\propto a_n$. Periodicity forces equal residues at $i,2i,3i,\dots$, so $a_n=a_1$: $f=a_1\zeta$. The entire content of "integer frequencies" is this translation invariance of the Laplace series; frequencies $m/\sqrt M$ destroy it (Lemma B.1(c) is its theta version).
(b) *The same identity for $\zeta$'s prime side.* Let $L(y):=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}e^{-2\pi ny}$, $\mathrm{Re}\,y>0$. Mellin inversion of $\Gamma(s)(2\pi y)^{-s}(-\zeta'/\zeta)(s+\frac12)$ gives
$$L(y)=\Gamma(\tfrac12)(2\pi y)^{-1/2}-\sum_\rho\Gamma(\rho-\tfrac12)(2\pi y)^{\frac12-\rho}+\sum_{j\ge1}\Gamma(-2j-\tfrac12)(2\pi y)^{2j+\frac12}+\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(-2\pi y)^k}{k!}\Big(-\frac{\zeta'}\zeta\Big)(\tfrac12-k),$$
with the zero sum over all nontrivial $\rho$ (symmetric summation; the pole of $\Gamma$ at $s=\rho-\frac12$ and of $-\zeta'/\zeta$ at $s+\frac12=\rho$; the last two sums are the trivial zeros and the poles of $\Gamma$). Since $L(y+i)=L(y)$, the zero sum $Z(y):=\sum_\rho\Gamma(\rho-\frac12)(2\pi y)^{\frac12-\rho}$ satisfies $Z(y+i)-Z(y)=$ (the corresponding difference of the elementary terms). This is an **unconditionally true identity** of $\zeta$, valid with $\zeta$'s actual zero set, whatever it is: it is the integrality of $\mathrm{supp}(-\zeta'/\zeta)$ read in the Laplace variable. It therefore contains no information about $\mathrm{Re}\,\rho$. The off-line zeros enter it through $|(2\pi y)^{\frac12-\rho}|=(2\pi y)^{-\delta_\rho}$ for real $y$, and the identity is consistent for every $\delta_\rho\in(-\frac12,\frac12)$. ∎

**Theorem B.3 (periodicity is blind to zeros). PROVED (given Lemma 1.2).** Let $\mathcal H$ be the set of hypotheses: real coefficients; $\mathrm{Fr}\subset\mathbb N$; functional equation $\Lambda_f(s)=\Lambda_f(1-s)$ with $\Lambda_f=(q/\pi)^{s/2}\Gamma(s/2)f$ for some $q\ge1$; finite order with at most a pole at $s=1$; $2$-periodicity and modularity of the theta function; positive integer zero multiplicities; atomic prime side; the zero-side identity (1.1) for the window forms. Then:
- (i) $f_\chi\in\mathcal H$ (Lemma 1.1) and $f_\chi$ has off-line zeros (Lemma 1.2). Hence **no** argument whose non-window inputs lie in $\mathcal H$ can prove "no first contact" (equivalently $(\star_{\rm cell})$ at the witness, or $W_T\succeq0\ \forall T$) — it would prove $W^{f_\chi}_T\succeq0\ \forall T$, which is false by the Weil criterion for $f_\chi$.
- (ii) The only hypothesis in the list that excludes $f_\chi$ is $q=1$. But $\mathcal H\cap\{q=1\}=\{c\zeta\}$ by Hamburger (R163_113 formulation, Bombieri–Ghosh §1.1): two Dirichlet series over $\mathbb N$ convergent in some right half-plane, finite order, $\zeta$'s FE. So an argument using $q=1$ together with $\mathrm{Fr}\subset\mathbb N$ has $\zeta$ as its only model; its use of integrality is the identification "$f=\zeta$", after which it is a proof of $W^\zeta_{T_c}\succeq0$ by other means.
- (iii) In particular, Hecke's dichotomy — the group $\langle z\mapsto z+\lambda,\ z\mapsto-1/z\rangle$ has finite covolume iff $\lambda\le2$, and finite-dimensional spaces of forms exactly then — is what makes Hamburger a one-dimensional statement at $\lambda=2$; for $f_t$ the relevant period is $2M>2$ (Lemma B.1), consistent with the infinite-dimensional family of R163_901 Lemma 2.1. But finite covolume is also enjoyed by $\theta_\chi$ (congruence subgroup) and by Epstein theta series (weight $1$, $\Gamma_0(|D|)$), whose Mellin transforms have off-line zeros. So "finite covolume / finite dimension" is sign-blind; only "dimension one" (= identification) is specific to $\zeta$. ∎

**Proposition B.4 (no periodic object through the window). PROVED.** Let $F$ be the witness, $\Psi=G^2$ (Lemma 3.1(b)), $\check\Psi=c_F$ supported in $[-2T,2T]$. The zero side with the sliding test $\tau\mapsto G(\tau)^2e^{i\tau u}$ is, by (0.1) of R163_801,
$$Z_G(u):=\sum_\rho G(t_\rho)^2e^{it_\rho u}=\text{polar}(u)+\text{arch}(u)-\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\big[c_F(u-\log n)+c_F(u+\log n)\big],$$
and $u=0$ is the balance (3.1). Any theta/Laplace object built from $F$ through the window is a transform of $u\mapsto\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}c_F(u-\log n)$, i.e. of the comb **smeared by $c_F$**. Such an object is never periodic in the Laplace variable: periodicity of $y\mapsto\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\int c_F(u-\log n)e^{-2\pi ye^u}du$ under $y\mapsto y+i$ would require $e^{-2\pi ie^u}=1$, i.e. $e^u\in\mathbb Z$, for $u$ in an interval (the support of $c_F(\cdot-\log n)$), which is impossible. The periodicity of Prop B.2(b) exists only for the un-smeared comb, i.e. for the full function and not for any window object. Moreover $Z_G(u)$ for $u\ne0$ is the explicit formula on a test that is not of the form $\widehat\Phi\widehat\Phi^\#$, so it carries no positivity; its growth in $u$ is the R162_2601 Thm T temperedness statement (C$_B$, E1). ∎

**Verdict (B): NO-GO (PROVED).** The only object whose $1$-periodicity is "available only for $\zeta$" is the un-windowed Laplace series of the prime side (Prop B.2); its periodicity identity is unconditionally true and sign-free; through the window no periodic object exists (Prop B.4); and periodicity plus modularity plus integer frequencies plus $\zeta$'s $\Gamma$-shape is satisfied by $f_\chi$ with off-line zeros (Thm B.3), so the light-cone balance §4.1 cannot be contradicted by it. The light-cone balance $N_-=P_+$ is itself an identity valid at every witness of every member of $\mathcal H$ (R163_401 Lemma 3.1 uses only (1.1), parity and reality).

## 4. Angle (C): a window Hamburger theorem against the exterior field. NO-GO (PROVED)

**Theorem C.1 (window Hamburger theorem; vacuous). PROVED.** Fix $T>0$ and the window data $D_T$. Let $\mathcal C_{\mathbb N}$ be the class of $f=\sum_{n\ge1}a_nn^{-s}$, $a_1=1$, convergent in some right half-plane, with $(s-1)f$ entire of finite order and $\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(\frac s2)f(s)$ invariant under $s\mapsto1-s$. Then the set of $f\in\mathcal C_{\mathbb N}$ whose prime side agrees with $D_T$ is $\{\zeta\}$ — and so is $\mathcal C_{\mathbb N}$ itself. Hence the "unique extension" of $D_T$ in $\mathcal C_{\mathbb N}$ exists, is $\zeta$, and does not depend on $D_T$: the window data are redundant, and the statement carries no information about the zeros (R162_2801 Lemma 3/NG: Hamburger returns $\zeta=\zeta$ for any configuration of zeros).
*Proof.* Hamburger in the Bombieri–Ghosh formulation (R163_113, CITED there): $\mathcal C_{\mathbb N}=\{\zeta\}$. ∎

**Theorem C.2 (the honest class is not rigid). PROVED.** In the class $\mathcal C_{\rm hon}$ of real, finite-order completed functions with $\zeta$'s exact FE, integer zero multiplicities and atomic prime side, the extensions of $D_T$ form, for each non-square $M$ with $\sqrt M>4N_0+4$, $N_0\ge e^{2T}$, a real family of dimension $\ge\lfloor M/4\rfloor-R-1$ (the $c$'s of R163_901 Lemma 2.1, one $t$-line each), all with $\zeta$'s witness if $\zeta$ has one below $\frac12\log N_0$, and each with its own contact otherwise (R163_901 Thm F(4), for $|t|$ large). The frequencies of these extensions leave $\mathbb N$ only above $N_0$; Variant F′ (ARGUED in R163_901) pushes even that above the contact. So "FE-structure on $[-T,T]$ + integer-frequency prime side **up to the window**" has infinitely many extensions, and uniqueness is restored only by integer frequencies **at all heights** — which is Theorem C.1, i.e. identification. ∎

**Theorem C.3 (the exterior field is compatible with the unique extension). PROVED (restatement).** Let $F$ be the witness, $G$ as in §3 of R163_401, $x_m=\int_IFe^{a_mu}$ the Gamma-lattice data (R162_1201), and $(\mathcal KF)(t)=\sum_{m\ge1}x_me^{-a_mt}$ the exterior field for $|t|>T$ (R162_1201 Thm 1.2). The two identities in which the exterior field meets the global integer-frequency tail are
- R162_1201 Thm 3.1: $\sum_{m\ge1}\Lambda(n)n^{-2m-1}x_m=w_n(\mathcal KF)(\log n)$ for $\log n>T$ — a resummation of the archimedean kernel $\kappa(\log n-u)=\sum_kn^{-a_k}e^{a_ku}$; it holds for any weight vector and any frequency set (R162_1201 Thm 5.1(ii));
- R163_801 Thm 3: $\sum_\rho G(t_\rho)^2/(t_\rho-x)=C_T(x)-iG(x)^2\mathcal T_T(x)$, $\mathrm{Im}\,x>\frac12$, $\mathcal T_T(x)=\sum_{\log n\ge2T}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2+ix}$ — Weil's explicit formula on the test $G^2/(z-x)$, rearranged; it holds for $f_t$ with $\mathcal T_T$ replaced by the tail of $-f_t'/f_t$ and $\rho$ by the zeros of $f_t$ (R163_801 §5 does this for $M_m$; the derivation uses only (0.1)).

Both are identities of $\zeta$'s own explicit formula and hold with $\zeta$'s actual zero set. "Incompatibility of the exterior field with the unique extension $\zeta$" is therefore impossible: it would be an inconsistency of $\zeta$'s explicit formula with itself. The only non-tautological statement in this direction is: *the meromorphic continuation of $C_T(x)-iG(x)^2\mathcal T_T(x)$ to $\mathrm{Im}\,x\le\frac12$ has poles exactly at the $t_\rho$ with residues $G(t_\rho)^2$ (R163_801 Cor 3.1), so it has no poles with $\mathrm{Im}\,x\in(-\frac12,0)$ iff RH.* That is E1. Finally, the exterior values $(\mathcal KF)(\log n)$, $n\ge e^{2T}$, that the global tail samples are **free** (R162_1201 Thm 1.2: $HF=0$ constrains the interior only), so no window Hamburger theorem can read a contradiction off them. ∎

**Verdict (C): NO-GO (PROVED).** The window Hamburger theorem exists only in the vacuous form C.1 (the data are redundant); in the honest class it is false (C.2); and the exterior field cannot be incompatible with $\zeta$ (C.3).

## 5. The exact obstruction. PROVED (given Lemma 1.2)

**Theorem O.** Let $\mathcal X$ be the class of $f$ with real coefficients, discrete frequency set $\mathrm{Fr}(f)\subset[1,\infty)$ (so $-f'/f$ is an atomic Dirichlet-type series convergent in a right half-plane), finite order with at most a pole at $s=1$, and functional equation $\Lambda_f(s)=\Lambda_f(1-s)$, $\Lambda_f=(q/\pi)^{s/2}\Gamma(s/2)f$. For $f\in\mathcal X$ the window forms $W^f_T$, their first contact and witness are defined as for $\zeta$ and are functions of $\big(q,\ \mathrm{Fr}(f)\cap[1,e^{2T}),\ \text{coefficients there},\ \text{polar}\big)$ (window locality, R163_901 Prop 1.1). Then:
1. **Integrality and conductor are global.** $\mathrm{Fr}(f)\subset\mathbb N$ is not a function of any finite window (Thm C.2); $q$ is read by every window but is shared by $f_t$.
2. **Both are needed, and together they identify.** $\{f\in\mathcal X:\ \mathrm{Fr}\subset\mathbb N\}$ contains $f_\chi$ (off-line zeros, witness); $\{f\in\mathcal X:\ q=1\}$ contains $f_t$ (off-line zeros, $\zeta$'s witness when $\zeta$ has one below $\frac12\log N_0$); $\{f\in\mathcal X:\ \mathrm{Fr}\subset\mathbb N,\ q=1\}=\{c\zeta\}$ (Hamburger). The same holds with "Euler product" in place of $\mathrm{Fr}\subset\mathbb N$: $f_t$ has no Euler product either, and $\{q=1,\ \text{Euler},\ \mathrm{Fr}\subset\mathbb N\}=\{\zeta\}$; while $\{\mathrm{Fr}\subset\mathbb N,\ \text{Euler},\ q>1\}$ is the set of degree-one Dirichlet $L$-functions (Kaczorowski–Perelli, CITED), where a mechanism would be a proof of GRH in degree one, uniform in $\chi$ (wall W9/T1) — there integrality is shared by every member and does no work.
3. **Consequence.** Every statement of the form "global integrality of frequencies $+$ [data shared with $f_t$ or with $f_\chi$] $\Rightarrow$ $(\star_{\rm cell})$ at the witness" is either **false** (if it does not use $q=1$: model $f_\chi$) or **has $\{c\zeta\}$ as its only model** (if it does). In the second case the use of integrality is the identification $f=\zeta$; after it, the argument must prove $W^\zeta_{T_c}\succeq0$ from $\zeta$'s own data, and at the window those data are the finite vector $(\Lambda(n))_{n<e^{2T_c}}$ (R163_401 §4.6; R163_901 §3). Integrality then has no further content: the atoms $\log n$, $n<e^{2T_c}$, are shared with $f_t$.
4. **Where the non-identification content of global integrality actually lives.** The one formulation in the corpus where "frequencies in $\mathbb N$ at all heights" has content *about zeros* that is not identification is the Beurling frontier BF$_\theta^{\rm disc}$ (R163_101/102): integer counting $N(x)=\lfloor x\rfloor$ with integer prime multiplicities versus zero-free regions. Its known theorems (Hilberdink 2010 / Révész Obs. 7, record R163_191) run in the RH-compatible direction only (discrete systems with $\theta<\frac12$ *force* zeros with $\beta>\theta$), and the only separating property of $\zeta$ there is again the integrality of multiplicities, exactly as in R163_901 §3. This memo adds nothing to that frontier; it shows that the three prescribed window-side routes cannot reach it.

*Proof.* 1: Thm C.2 and Prop A.1. 2: Lemmas 1.1–1.2 ($f_\chi$), R163_901 Thm F ($f_t$), Hamburger (R163_113), and $f_t$ ($t\ne0$) has no Euler product: its coefficients would have to be multiplicative on its frequency set $\mathbb N\cup\{m/\sqrt M\}$, but $a_{\lambda_1}=tc(m_1)\ne0$ while $\lambda_1^2=m_1^2/M$ is rational and non-integer ($M$ squarefree, $M\nmid m_1^2$ since $c(m_1)\ne0$ forces $M\nmid m_1$ by the hole, cf.
