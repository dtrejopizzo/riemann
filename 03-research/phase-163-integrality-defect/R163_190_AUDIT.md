## R163_001

**§1 convergence.** Put A=w²+γ². The numerator is (w²+δ²+γ²)²−4δ²w², so
E_j−1 = [2δ²A+δ⁴−4δ²w²]/A². On a compact K that avoids ±iγ_j, with |w|≤R, and for γ_j ≥ 2R,
we have |A| ≥ γ_j²/2. Since δ<1/2, |E_j−1| ≤ C_R γ_j^{-2}, uniformly in δ. The bound Σγ_j^{-2}<∞ follows from
N(T)=O(T log T). The product therefore converges normally away from the poles. The remark that N(σ,T)=o(N(T)) is not
needed is also right. The zeros of the numerator are w=±δ±iγ and the poles are double at w=±iγ. The symmetries hold.
**CORRECT.**

**Central value.** At w=0, E_j=(δ²+γ²)²/γ⁴, so log E_j(1/2)=2log(1+δ_j²/γ_j²)≥0. The sum is ≤2Σδ_j²/γ_j²<∞, and it is 0
exactly when there are no quartets. **CORRECT.**

**§2 Frullani.** Use log((s−a)/(s−b))=∫₀^∞e^{-su}(e^{bu}−e^{au})du/u for Re s>max(Re a,Re b). Take a over the
denominator roots (1/2±iγ, each double) and b over the four numerator roots. Then Σ_b e^{bu}=4e^{u/2}cosh(δu)cos(γu)
and Σ_a e^{au}=4e^{u/2}cos(γu), which gives the stated q_j. The integrand is O(u) at 0, and it converges at ∞ for
Re s>1/2+δ. The branch is fixed by E→1. The sign convention also checks: dΠ_{Z₀}=dΠ_ζ+q_E. **CORRECT.**

**Distributional bound.** q_j=f_j(u)cos(γ_j u), where f_j=4e^{u/2}(cosh δ_j u−1)/u. On supp φ, f_j is smooth with all
derivatives bounded uniformly in δ_j∈(0,1/2). Two integrations by parts give |⟨q_j,φ⟩|≤γ_j^{-2}‖(f_jφ)''‖_{L¹}≤C_φγ_j^{-2}.
The text correctly warns that Σ|q_j| does not converge pointwise for large u (there is no decay in j), so the global
Laplace identity for the infinite sum is not automatic. The exp_* and Möbius identities are exact for finite
truncations. **CORRECT.**

**§3 D_test.** Fixes needed:
1. Each summand is continuous and the series converges uniformly, so D_test is **continuous** in the distributional
   topology on (0,∞), not merely lower semicontinuous. Fatou is not needed. The stated conclusion is weaker than what
   holds, so it is not false.
2. The implication "q_E=0 ⇒ E≡1" is correctly marked as open. The fix proposed in the text (add min(1,log E(1/2)))
   is valid and faithful by the central-value formula. With it, D_test is openly a zero-side object, as the text says.
3. The phrase "dense family" should say "dense in C_c^∞(0,∞) with its LF topology". That space is separable, so such a
   family exists.

**Verdict: CORRECT WITH FIXES (minor).** The final status paragraph (it does not close Gate 1, O2601.1 or Row D) is accurate.

## R163_002

**§1 support lemma.** If κ=δ₀+κ' with κ'≥0, then ν₀*κ=ν₀+ν₀*κ'≥ν₀. By symmetry ν₀*κ≥κ. So supp ν₀ and supp κ lie in
supp ν_N=log ℕ. The remark that nontrivial positive factorizations exist (split the set of primes into two sets) is
correct and properly limits the lemma. **CORRECT.**

**Expansion.** From E_j=1+2δ²/A+δ⁴/A²−4δ²w²/A² with A=w²+γ², we get E_j=1−2δ_j²/w²+O(w⁻⁴). For a finite product this
gives E(σ)=1−2Σδ_j²/w²+O(w⁻⁴)<1 for large real σ. The Laplace transform of a positive measure with an atom of mass 1
at 0 is ≥1 on its real half-line of convergence. So E is not such a factor. **CORRECT.** For the infinite case the
O(w⁻⁴) term is not uniform, but the text claims only the finite case.

**§2 counterexample.** Take a=−1 and b=c−1. The Frullani density is (e^{-(1−c)u}−e^{-u})/u>0 for 0<c<1, and
log Z₀=log ζ+log(1/K). So dΠ_{Z₀}=dΠ_ζ+(positive density), and dN_{Z₀}=exp_*(dΠ_{Z₀})≥0. K is the transform of
δ₀−c e^{-u}du, which is not positive, and K(σ)<1. This is consistent with §1 and matches the sign behavior of E. The
limits on scope (no functional equation, not an exchange of zeros) are stated. **CORRECT.**

**§3 capacity.** If q_E is a signed measure and dΠ_ζ+q_E≥0, then on the carrier of q_E^- (which is singular to
q_E^+) we get q_E^-≤dΠ_ζ. That is exactly the displayed condition. In the finite case q_E is absolutely continuous and
changes sign (because of the factor cos γu), so q_E^- cannot be dominated by atoms. This reproduces R162_2901 Thm A.
The closing caveat (capacity would exclude only positivity of the quotient, not E≠1) is correct. **CORRECT.**

§4 is a statement of targets and makes no mathematical claim.

## R163_003

**§1 "q_E is exactly the off-line part".** This is wrong as written. In u=log x, dΠ_ζ=dψ/u exactly. By the explicit
formula, each zero ρ contributes −e^{ρu}du/u. So the quartets contribute −Σ_b e^{bu}/u. But
q_E=(Σ_b e^{bu}−Σ_a e^{au})/u. That is, q_E = (minus the off-line term) + (the term of the inserted double zeros at
1/2±iγ_j). The claim "the quartet term is −q_E" is therefore false: −q_E is the quartet term minus its on-line
replacement. **Fix:** replace it with "q_E is the difference between each quartet's explicit-formula contribution and
that of its projection onto the critical line".

The consequence still holds. q_E=0 ⟺ there are no quartets ⟺ RH. This uses R163_001 §3 faithfulness, or the
central-value term to avoid the open step. So "property 2 ⟺ RH" and "the exchange adds no content" are correct.
**CORRECT WITH FIXES.**

**§2 equivalence.** For infinitely many zeros, "the growing component e^{(½+β)u}" has no meaning by itself: an infinite
sum of such terms has no canonical decomposition. The correct statement is the classical one (von Koch/Landau): RH ⟺
ψ(x)=x+O(x^{½+ε}) for every ε>0. Equivalently, Π(x)=li(x)+O(x^{½+ε}). The ⟸ direction follows because the Mellin
transform ∫x^{-s}d(ψ−x) is analytic in Re s>½+ε. State the quantifier ∀ε and drop "component".
**CORRECT WITH FIXES.**

**§2–3 reading of the references.** These points were checked against the files:
- DMV, as restated in Broucke 2409.10051 ll. 2687–2696 and BDV 2004.11501 §1: for θ∈(½,1) there is a system with
  N=Ax+O(x^θ), zeros on σ=1−a/log(|t|+2), and PNT error no better than dlVP. **Correct.**
- Broucke 2409.10051 Thm 1.2 gives N(α,T)≪T^{3−2α−θ}(log T)⁹ uniformly for α≥(1+θ)/2. The range is optimal for θ=0
  (by ζ) and for θ∈[½,1) (Thm 6.3). The question about whether a_j≡1 or the Euler product can be exploited is at
  ll. 222–224. **Correct.** Note that it is optimal for θ≥½, including ½ with a log factor.
- BDV 2004.11501 is a system with RH and large oscillation of N. The Bateman–Diamond exponents are open except at the
  extremes (ll. 246–250). **Correct.**
- **Wrong:** "Nothing in these references constructs a system with N(x)=ρx+O(x^θ), θ<½, and zeros with Re s>½."
  Broucke 2409.10051 §6.1 (ll. 2252–2300) defines ζ_θ(s)=ζ((s−θ)/(1−θ)) as a **Beurling system in the extended sense**
  (Π and N non-decreasing, dN=exp_*(dΠ)). This works for every θ∈(0,1). Its integers are n^{1/(1−θ)} with weights
  n^{θ/(1−θ)}, so N_θ(x)=Σ_{n≤x^{1−θ}}n^{θ/(1−θ)}=(1−θ)x+O(x^θ). Its zeros are θ+(1−θ)ρ, with real part (1+θ)/2>½.
  So (BF_θ) has answer **YES for every θ∈(0,½)** in the extended class. The restriction θ≥½ in Thm 6.3 comes only
  from the discretization step (the approximation error ≍x^{1/2}). It does not come from the zeros.

**Consequences for §3.**
1. Branch 1 ("a class theorem: integer error <√x ⇒ RH, uniform in the Beurling class") is **false in the extended
   class**. This is the class the programme uses for quotients such as ζ/E and Z₀ in R163_002 §2. It can survive only
   for **discrete** systems (integer multiplicities). Then it is again an integrality statement, which is not the
   "class with margin" advertised. The claimed escape from W1/W3 has to be re-examined on that basis.
2. For discrete systems the question is genuinely not settled by these files. The zbMATH record (Zhang, Math. Ann.
   337, 2007) gives discrete examples at N=kx+O(x^{½}exp(c(log x)^{2/3})), with zeros near σ=1−1/log t. These sit just
   above the ½ threshold. Any discrete (BF_θ), θ<½, must beat √x-scale discretization noise.
3. The dichotomy is not exhaustive. There could be a threshold θ₀∈(0,½). Also "θ=0 with bounded error but without the
   lattice" is left undefined.
4. The falsifier list should add the rescaled ζ_θ (extended class, every θ>0). It is the most direct falsifier of
   branch 1.

**Verdict on 003 §2–3: WRONG** on the key literature claim and on branch 1 as a class statement. The target is
salvageable only if restricted to discrete Beurling systems, labeled (BF_θ^disc).

## Overall

R163_001 and R163_002 are mathematically sound. Only cosmetic fixes are needed, and their stated limitations are
accurate. R163_003 correctly finds that the exchange is a reorganization of the explicit formula. But it misidentifies
q_E (it is a difference, not the off-line part), and its Beurling frontier misreads Broucke §6.1, which already
answers (BF_θ) positively for all θ>0 in the extended sense. No label change. Row D remains open.
