## 1. Section 1: the collapse

**Prop 1.1.** $W^Z_T(F)=\sum_{\rho\in Z}(\widehat F\widehat F^\#)(t_\rho)$ for $F\in\mathcal D_T$, and for $T\le T_0$ these
are $PW_{T_0}$-products. $T_c$, $E_c$, the witness and $X$ are defined from $\{W^Z_T\}_{T\le T_c}$ (R163_501 FC0(ii), Thm R).
Correct. It is a tautology once "zero-side identity with a symbol" is assumed; the proposition's role is to make the

**Prop 1.2.** The proof says T5.1 "uses only (1.1) and the positivity of on-line weights". Read T5.1's proof (R163_603
§5): "By Lemma 3.4(b), $\mathrm{Re}\,G(t_q)^2\ge0$ for every $q$. Then (3.1) gives $0\ge P_+>0$ (balance, §4.1)". Balance

$Z$ and $\sum_q\mathrm{Re}\,G(t_q)^2=0$. For $Z=$ zeros of ξ balance holds (record). For a general "configuration with
positive integer multiplicities and a zero-side identity" it is an extra hypothesis. **CORRECT WITH FIXES**: add

R163_501 FC(i)), or simply "$\gg R\log R$ distinct on-line points".

A second, cosmetic fix: "violates the cone condition" should be "violates the (A-loc) box" — the box has two clauses,
$|E_q|<\pi/4$ and the distance bound, and the contrapositive negates their conjunction.

## 2. Lemma 2.1

Unitary DFT $(\mathcal F_Mc)(k)=M^{-1/2}\sum_rc(r)e^{-2\pi irk/M}$. Eigenvalue multiplicities (McClellan–Parks): eigenvalue $1$ has
multiplicity $\lfloor M/4\rfloor+1$ for every $M\ge1$; so "$\ge\lfloor M/4\rfloor$" is safe. Eigenvectors of $\pm1$ are even ($\mathcal F_M^2=$
parity). $\mathcal F_M$ on even vectors is the real symmetric cosine matrix, so a real basis exists. Constraints $c(0)=\dots=c(R)=0$:
$R+1$ linear conditions on even $c$. From $\sqrt M>4N_0+4$: $M/4>N_0\sqrt M+\sqrt M\ge R+\sqrt M$, so $\lfloor M/4\rfloor\ge R+\sqrt M-1>R+1$
(as $\sqrt M>2$). Nonzero solution exists. $2R\le2N_0\sqrt M<M/2$, so residues $|r|\le R$ are distinct and $n/\sqrt M\in(-N_0,N_0)$ ⟹ $|n|\le R$
⟹ $c(n)=0$. Poisson: $n=r+Mj$, $\sum_je^{-2\pi ij\sqrt M\xi}=M^{-1/2}\sum_k\delta_{k/\sqrt M}$, giving $\widehat\nu=\sum_k(\mathcal F_Mc)(k)\delta_{k/\sqrt M}=\nu$.
$\sum_rc(r)=\sqrt M(\mathcal F_Mc)(0)=\sqrt Mc(0)=0$. Every step checks. **CORRECT.**

Two remarks the memo should carry. (a) $\sum_rc(r)=0$ forces **sign changes** in $c$: the new prime-side atoms of $f_t$
have both signs (see §3.3 below for why this does not rescue a "positivity of atoms" separator). (b) **Novelty**: self-dual
tempered measures are exactly the objects behind Riemann's functional equation; that the solution space is infinite-dimensional
once non-integral support is allowed is classical (Bochner, "Some properties of modular relations", 1951/58; Kahane–Mandelbrojt,
"Sur l'équation fonctionnelle de Riemann et la formule sommatoire de Poisson", 1958; Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan; Hecke's
theory for signature $(\lambda,\frac12,1)$ with $\lambda>2$). Lemma 2.1 is a clean finite-lattice instance, not a new phenomenon.
The record should cite this rather than present the lemma as the mechanism.

## 3. Theorem F

### 3.1 Item 1 — CORRECT
$\theta_t(x)=\int e^{-\pi xy^2}d\mu_t(y)$, $\mu_t=\sum_n\delta_n+t\nu$ tempered and self-dual (both summands are, in the stated
convention). Self-duality tested on $e^{-\pi xy^2}$ gives $\theta_t(x)=x^{-1/2}\theta_t(1/x)$. $\mu_t(\{0\})=1$ because $c(0)=0$, so
$\theta_t-1\to0$ exponentially as $x\to\infty$ (the $\nu$-part decays like $e^{-\pi(R+1)^2x/M}$), and $\theta_t(x)=x^{-1/2}(1+O(e^{-c/x}))$ as
$x\to0$. Riemann's split of $\frac12\int_0^\infty(\theta_t-1)x^{s/2}dx/x$ gives $\Lambda_t(s)=\frac1{s-1}-\frac1s+\int_1^\infty(\theta_t-1)(x^{s/2}+x^{(1-s)/2})\frac{dx}{2x}$,
with $\frac12\int(\theta_t-1)x^{s/2}dx/x=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(\frac s2)(\zeta(s)+tg(s))$ because $\nu$ is even and $g$ sums over $m>R$ only.
Hence FE, same two poles with ζ's residues, $\Lambda_t+\frac1s+\frac1{1-s}$ entire of order $\le1$ (and $=1$). Real coefficients. ✓

*Hamburger check (as requested).* Hamburger's theorem (in the Bombieri–Ghosh form cited by R163_113) assumes an **ordinary**
Dirichlet series $\sum a_nn^{-s}$. $g$ has frequencies $m/\sqrt M$ with $M$ non-square, hence irrational for $m\ne0$; the theorem does
not apply, and this is exactly the classical escape (Bochner). $f_t=c'\zeta$ is impossible: $(c'-1)\zeta=tg$ would equate two
absolutely convergent general Dirichlet series with disjoint frequency sets ($\mathbb N$ versus irrationals), so by uniqueness all
coefficients vanish, contradicting $c\ne0$, $t\ne0$. Non-integrality is used correctly, and the memo's "Why this is not ζ"
paragraph is right. In Variant F′ ($\sqrt M=K\in\mathbb N$) the frequencies are rationals $m/K$ and the FE of conductor 1 for
$K^s\sum c(m)m^{-s}$ is Hecke's signature $(K,\frac12,1)$, whose solution space is infinite-dimensional for $K\ge3$; consistent.

### 3.2 Item 2 — TRIVIAL
Every nonzero meromorphic function has positive integer zero multiplicities; the symmetries follow from real coefficients and
the FE. The statement has no mathematical content and "PROVED" is cosmetic. It is **not vacuous in its role**: the memo's thesis
is that integrality is "idle", and the way to show that is to exhibit a *genuine zero set* with all the other properties.
The correct phrasing is "the zero-side measure of $f_t$ is $\sum\mathrm{mult}\cdot\delta_{t_\rho}$ with weights in $\mathbb Z_{>0}$, trivially".

### 3.3 Item 3 — CORRECT WITH FIXES
*Is the prime side well defined?* Yes. $f_t=1+\sum_{\lambda\ge2}a(\lambda)\lambda^{-s}$ is a general Dirichlet series (ζ contributes $n\ge2$,
$g$ contributes $\lambda=m/\sqrt M>N_0\ge2$), absolutely convergent for $\sigma>1$. For $\sigma$ large $\sum_{\lambda\ge2}|a(\lambda)|\lambda^{-\sigma}<1$, so
$\log f_t$ and $-f_t'/f_t$ are absolutely convergent general Dirichlet series over the multiplicative semigroup generated by $\mathbb N$ and
$\{m/\sqrt M\}_{m>R}$, which is discrete (all generators $\ge2$). So the "prime side" of the Weil formula is the coefficient
measure $\sum_\lambda b_t(\lambda)\lambda^{-1/2}\delta_{\pm\log\lambda}$, and the Weil/Barner derivation uses only meromorphy, FE, order 1 and this
absolutely convergent Dirichlet series in some half-plane (contour at $\sigma_1$ and $1-\sigma_1$; test $\widehat F$ entire of exponential
type, $F\in C_c^\infty$). CITED is acceptable. "Atomic" is meaningful: the measure is a discrete sum of point masses.

*But* the atoms are **not** on $\log\mathbb N$, let alone on $\log$ of prime powers; they are on $\log$ of a semigroup with irrational
generators. R163_114 identifies "support in $\log\mathbb N_{\ge2}$" as the property with full RH strength. So the §2 phrase "every structural
property used in R163_401/603/801 (… an **atomic** prime side …)" is accurate for what 401/603/801 *used* (atomicity, $V_T$ budget),
but not for what ζ *has*. The memo is honest about this in Var F′ and in the Hamburger paragraph; the §3 list should say
"atomic (on a discrete semigroup), not on $\log\mathbb N$".

*Agreement window.* $g/\zeta=g\cdot\sum\mu(d)d^{-s}$ has all frequencies $\ge\lambda_1=m_1/\sqrt M$, so $\log(1+tg/\zeta)$ does too. Hence
$b_t(\lambda)=\Lambda(\lambda)$ (zero off $\mathbb N$) for **all $\lambda<\lambda_1$**, not just $\lambda<N_0$; the forms $W^{f_t}_T=W^\zeta_T$ coincide exactly for
$T\le\frac12\log\lambda_1$. Fix the statement (it only helps).

*Sign of the atoms.* By Lemma 2.1, $c$ has both signs, so $b_t$ has negative atoms somewhere. Does "all atoms $\ge0$" separate ζ from
$f_t$ *below the contact*? No: below $e^{2T_c}\le\lambda_1e^{2\varepsilon}<2\lambda_1$ the only non-ζ atom is $\lambda_1$ (products are $\ge2\lambda_1$),
and item 4 works for either sign of $t$; choose $t\,c(m_1)>0$. So positivity of atoms is not a separator at contact level. The memo
does not say this; it should, because it is the natural objection.

### 3.4 Item 4 — CORRECT WITH FIXES
Checked: $c_\phi=(1+\eta^2)k_0+\eta(\tau_ak_0+\tau_{-a}k_0)$; $W$ is linear in $c_\phi$; $A=\langle\mathcal W,k_0\rangle$ has no prime term since
$\mathrm{supp}\,k_0\subset(-2\varepsilon,2\varepsilon)$, $2\varepsilon<\log2$; $B_t=\langle\mathcal W,\tau_ak_0\rangle$ with the single prime term at $\lambda_1$ by the choice
of $\varepsilon$, $k_0(-2a)=0$; $b_t(\lambda_1)=t\,c(m_1)\log\lambda_1$ (coefficient of $\log f_t$ at the irrational frequency $\lambda_1$ is $a(\lambda_1)=tc(m_1)$,
no product of smaller frequencies hits it); $\min_\eta Q=A-B_t^2/A<0$ for $|B_t|>A$; zero side $\sum_\rho|\widehat\phi(t_\rho)|^2\ge0$ if all $t_\rho$

So $\lambda^W_T(f_t)<0$ and, since $W^{f_t}_T=W^\zeta_T>0$ on $(0,\frac12\log5]$ (Paper 46 certificate) and $N_0\ge5$ may be assumed, $T_c(f_t)$ is

### 3.5 Corollary F — witness existence (ARGUED, acceptable) and (A-loc) (GAP)

*Does R163_501 transfer to $f_t$?* The ingredients and their status for $f_t$:
- Form structure: symbol $\sigma^{f_t}_T=m-\sum_{\lambda<e^{2T}}\frac{2b_t(\lambda)}{\sqrt\lambda}\cos(\tau\log\lambda)$, a finite comb; closedness, semiboundedness,
  compact resolvent as in 501 §2. ✓ transfers verbatim.
- Zero-side identity (1.1) on $\mathcal D_T$: 401 Lemma 3.0 assumes $|\delta_\rho|<\frac12$ and the local zero count $\ll\log(2+|\tau|)$. For $f_t$ the
  zeros lie in $|\delta|\le\sigma_1(t)-\frac12$ (zeros with $\sigma>1$ are not excluded) and the local count bound holds for any order-1 function
  with FE. The proof adapts with a larger disc. ARGUED, plausible.
- $T_c<\infty$: item 4. ✓ Positivity on an initial range: equals ζ's. ✓ Monotonicity/continuity in $T$ (`lem:monoT`, `lem:contT`):
  structural, finite comb. ✓
- Real-rooted witness (Thms B, R): use $|\widehat F|$ on $\mathbb R$, the polar pair at $\pm i/2$ with $A\bar B$ (ζ's pole, present), parity
  ($\sigma$ even). ✓ transfers. "Real on the line": $\Lambda_t(\frac12+it)\in\mathbb R$ by FE + real coefficients. ✓
So witness existence for $f_t$: ARGUED is the right label; I see no obstruction.

*$(\star_{\rm cell})$ fails at the witness.* Rem 4.8a's identity $\sum_j(P_j-N_j)=W(F)=0$ is a partition of (1.1); cells without off-line
ordinates have $N_j=0$, $P_j\ge0$; one cell with an off-line ordinate (item 4) and $(\star_{\rm cell})$ there would give $W(F)>0$.
No balance needed. **CORRECT given witness existence.**

*(A-loc) fails at the witness.* Via Prop 1.2, hence via T5.1, hence via **balance**. Suppose (A-loc) holds at $f_t$'s witness. Then
$\mathrm{Re}\,G(t_q)^2\ge0$ for all $q$ (Lemma 3.4(b), valid for any $\delta>0$), and $0=\sum_{\rm on}\mathrm{mult}\,G(\gamma)^2+\sum_{\rm off}$ forces every
term to vanish: $G$ vanishes at every on-line zero of $f_t$. To contradict this one needs $f_t$ to have more distinct on-line zeros in
$(-R,R)$ than the witness has zeros there, i.e. more than $\frac{2T_c}\pi R+o(R)$ (FC(i): the witness has zero density exactly $T_c/\pi$ per unit
length). For ζ this is Levinson/Conrey ($\gg R\log R$ distinct). For $f_t$ — a generalised Dirichlet series with no Euler product — **no
such result exists in the literature I know**: for ordinary non-Euler series with FE (Epstein zeta with $h>1$, Davenport–Heilbronn) the
unconditional results are $\gg R$ on-line zeros (Voronin; Karatsuba's $R(\log R)^{1/2-\epsilon}$), with no control of the constant against
$2T_c/\pi$, and a positive proportion is known only under hypotheses (Bombieri–Hejhal). For $f_t$ not even a Hardy-type "infinitely many"
is on record. Hence Cor F's (A-loc) clause rests on an **unproved property of $f_t$**, distinct from witness existence. The label
"PROVED given witness existence" is **wrong**; correct label: ARGUED, with two named gaps (witness existence: transfer; balance: open).

The degenerate alternative ($G$ vanishing at all on-line zeros of $f_t$ and $\sum_q\mathrm{Re}\,G(t_q)^2=0$) is implausible but not excluded,
and it is exactly the case in which (A-loc) would hold at $f_t$'s witness. The meta-statement cannot ignore it.

*Statement about $T_c(f_t)$.* With the §3.3 correction: if ζ has no contact below $\frac12\log\lambda_1$ then $T_c(f_t)\in[\frac12\log\lambda_1,\frac12\log\lambda_1+\varepsilon]$,
pinned at the first new atom. (If ζ does have a contact below, $f_t$'s witness is ζ's and Cor F says nothing new.) Correct as a reading of
the memo's weaker interval.

### 3.6 Variant F′ — ARGUED (label right)
Dimension count fine ($\approx N_1K$ constraints against $K^2/4$). The coefficient formula $b_t(n)=\Lambda(n)+t\,c(nK)\log n$ for $N_0\le n<N_0^2$ is wrong
in general: the coefficient of $tg/\zeta$ at integer $n$ is $t\sum_{d\mid n,\ n/d\ge N_0}\mu(d)\,c((n/d)K)$ (Möbius convolution over divisors $d$ with
$nK/d$ a multiple of $K$; the other $d\mid nK$ give $c$ at non-multiples of $K$, which vanish by construction below $N_1K$). At the minimal $n_0$
with $c(n_0K)\ne0$ the formula is right, and that is all item 4 uses. Fix the formula; the ARGUED label stands. Note F′'s atoms below the
contact are on $\log\mathbb N$ but **not on prime powers** in general ($n_0$ composite is allowed), so even F′ does not match R163_114's
structure fully; the memo's own sentence "supported on logs of integers" is accurate, "prime side" in the Euler sense is not.

## 4. Section 3: does the meta-statement stand?

Three different statements are bundled under one PROVED.

1. **"No argument whose inputs are the listed structures proves Row D ($W_T\succeq0$ for all $T$)."** This follows from Thm F.4 alone:
   $f_t$ has FE with $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}$ and ζ's pole, real coefficients, a genuine integer zero set, an atomic prime side equal to ζ's below
   $\lambda_1$, and $W^{f_t}_T\not\succeq0$ at $T=\frac12\log\lambda_1+\varepsilon$. No witness theory, no balance. **CORRECT, unconditional.** This is the strong,
   clean form and the memo should review with it.
2. **"No such argument proves $(\star_{\rm cell})$ at the witness."** Needs witness existence for $f_t$ (ARGUED, transfer plausible).
   **CORRECT given witness existence.**
3. **"No such argument proves (A-loc) at the witness."** Needs in addition balance for $f_t$ (OPEN, §3.5). And there is a logical
   point the memo misses: balance ($\gg R\log R$ distinct on-line ordinates, via Levinson/Conrey) **is itself one of the inputs** of
   401 §4.1 / 603 T5.1, which §3 lists among the structures $\mathcal A$ may use. A falsifier excludes an argument only if it satisfies every
   input the argument uses; $f_t$ is not known to satisfy this one. So as written §3 is **not proved** for (A-loc). Label: ARGUED, conditional
   on witness existence and on-line abundance for $f_t$.

**"Hamburger is the only structural separator and it is global."** Overclaim. Separators of ζ from the $f_t$ family that are not Hamburger:
(a) the Euler product / prime-power support of $-f'/f$ (R163_114 "support in $\log\mathbb N$" is the weak form; F′ only pushes it above the contact
and is ARGUED); (b) on-line abundance (Levinson/Conrey), used by balance and not available for $f_t$; (c) the Riemann–Siegel / approximate
functional equation structure of $Z(t)$ used in R163_603 §2 (requires the Euler product only weakly, but the Dirichlet polynomial length
$\sqrt{t/2\pi}$ refers to integer frequencies). Each of these is "global" in the same sense as Hamburger (none is a window statement), so the
memo's *conclusion* — that no window-level structural input separates — survives; the *uniqueness* claim does not. Replace by "the only
separator identified among the listed inputs".

**"Integrality is idle."** Correct for the literal 801 §6 question (Thm 1.3) and correct in the sense that $f_t$ trivially has integer
multiplicities. But the phase-163 "integrality defect" programme (R163_102 Thm R, R163_803) concerns integrality **together with**
$\log\mathbb N$-support of the prime side (R163_114), which $f_t$ lacks and F′ has only below the contact and only generically. So "the
phase-163 integrality-defect programme cannot supply (A-loc)" is established only against the *atomic* abstraction of that programme,
not against its actual hypothesis (support in $\log\mathbb N$, which R163_114 shows has RH strength). The memo's final sentence should say so.
