## (B) Blueprint claims refuted, repaired or modified

Eight refutations/repairs are tabulated in Paper 46 §14.4; I restate them here
numbered, with the reason, and add the two errors I fixed in this round.

1. **D.6(i), the decay exponent.**  `REFUTED as an instruction`.  On the
   blueprint's class (`g,g'` of decay `e^{−(½+ε)|t|}`) `Φ_g` is holomorphic
   only in `−ε < Re s < 1+ε`, so Paper 45's contour proof — which runs on
   `L_c`, `c > 1` — is unavailable, and with one derivative the zero sum is
   **not** known to converge absolutely (`Σ|γ|^{−1} = ∞`).  Repaired by
   Definition 11.1 (`C²`, exponent `1+ε`).  (§11.1; the classical Weil–Barner
   statement is recorded as `IMPORTED` and deliberately **not used**.)
2. **D.6(iii), the shape of the perp.**  `REFUTED`.  `P^⊥ = ξ·(entire) ∩
   Mellin(D^W)` holds **iff every zero of `ξ` is simple**, which is `OPEN`;
   Theorem 11.10 gives only order-one vanishing.  Recording the equality would
   have been an unproved statement about the zeros inside the one paper
   forbidden to make one.  Unconditional form: (11.13).
3. **D.6(iii), the independence argument.**  `REPAIRED`.  As the blueprint
   wrote it (`Σ c_ρρ(1−ρ)e^{(ρ−½)t}`) the series need not converge.  The
   correct coefficients are `c_ρ = Φ_G(1−ρ)` without the factor `ρ(1−ρ)`; the
   proof is a Laplace transform and a residue (Lemma 11.9), not almost
   periodicity.  This also independently re-derives the blueprint's `R135_003` §0.3.
4. **D.7(i), `𝓝_∞ = ΠK⋆D^W`.**  `REFUTED` in the direction asked for:
   `τ_a(ΠK) ∈ 𝓝_∞ ∖ ΠK⋆D^W` (Prop. 11.11a).  Structurally `ξ` decays like
   `e^{−π|τ|/4}` on the critical line while `Φ_N` is only `O(τ^{−2})`, so
   `ΠK⋆D^W` is a *very small* subideal.  Nothing later uses it.
5. **D.7(ii), "a new proof of Paper 45 Theorem 12.2".**  `REFUTED as a
   description`.  Theorem 12.2 is a fixed-chart Plancherel statement about
   `I_A`; Theorem 11.12 is a colimit statement about `I_∞` by zero counting.
   It supplies what Paper 45 **Remark 12.4** explicitly declined to assert, and
   is recorded as a new theorem with a `[phase 135]` remark there.
6. **D.7(iv)/§1, `A = e_+ + e_-` and `F + mA`.**  `REPAIRED`.  `A` is not an
   element of `D^W` or of any function class here (Paper 43 Thm 9.14c: the
   rulings live in the distributional completion and are its radical).  `2K` is
   an intrinsic degree-computing ample class, and the ample family is
   `F + 2mK`.  This is exactly the gap flagged at `R135_003` §0.5, and
   the paper's repair is the stronger of the two available (an honest class,
   not a functional).
7. **§1, "the Hodge-index shape exists in the colimit completion".**
   `REFUTED as stated, REPAIRED`.  Every element of the colimit is compactly
   supported (Finding 11.0) and the colimit's own perp is still `0`
   (Theorem 11.12).  The shape appears in `D^W`, on which (8A.21) does not even
   converge.
8. **D.9(iii), "`deg K = 1 = 2(2g−2)`".**  `REFUTED as a reading`; nothing
   derives the factor `2`.  `deg K = 2g−2` gives `g_D = 3/2`, itself a choice
   of formula.  Also **improved** relative to the blueprint: no `χ ≥ 0` is
   needed in the transfer theorem (Thm 12.3, Step 3), and `H⁰(0) = {0}`
   *exactly*, not modulo `ker Π` (Lemma 11.2(v)).
   *Confirmed* by round 1: `T_∞(K) = 1`, `I_∞(K,K) = ½`, `M(K) = (½,½)`,
   `∫K = ξ(½)`, and both requirements of (b′).

**Errors found and fixed in this round (the paper was otherwise not touched).**

9. **Theorem 11.7(ii), the error term at `t → −∞` was too small and
   contradicted evenness.**  The proof gives
   `4x^{9/4}·O(x^{−9/2}e^{−π/x}) + 6x^{5/4}·O(x^{−5/2}e^{−π/x})`, whose
   dominant term is `O(x^{−9/4}e^{−π/x})`, not `O(x^{−5/4}e^{−π/x})`; the
   printed conclusion `K(t) = O(e^{5|t|/2}e^{−πe^{2|t|}})` as `t → −∞` was
   therefore **smaller than the true two-sided asymptotic** `4π²e^{9|t|/2}
   e^{−πe^{2|t|}}` stated two lines earlier, i.e. inconsistent with the
   evenness of `K`.  Fixed: `x^{−5/4} → x^{−9/4}` (twice) and
   `e^{5|t|/2} → e^{9|t|/2}`.  Nothing depends on the exponent — every use is
   "`K ∈ D^W` for every decay exponent" — so no statement changes.
10. **Theorem 11.14(ii), the constant in (11.17).**  The case `u < T` bounds
   the prime sum by `1.03883‖F‖_∞e^{u+T} ≤ 1.03883‖F‖_∞e^{2T}`, which is
   **not** `≤ 1.03883‖F‖_∞e^{2T}e^{−u/2}` on `2T/3 < u < T` (there
   `e^{2T}e^{−u/2} < e^{2T}` can be as small as `e^{3T/2}`).  The smallest
   constant that works with the argument as written is `e^{5T/2}`
   (`e^{u+T} ≤ e^{2T} = e^{5T/2}e^{−T/2} ≤ e^{5T/2}e^{−u/2}`).  Fixed: all four
   occurrences of the constant `e^{2T}` in (11.17) and its proof replaced by
   `e^{5T/2}` (the occurrence `n ≤ e^{u+T} ≤ e^{2T}`, which is correct, is
   left).  `C_F` is only ever used as "some finite constant depending on `F`"
   (Thm 12.3, Step 1: *fix an integer `m > C_F`*), so no statement changes.

11. **Theorem 11.8's numerical-confirmation note was unreproducible.**  It
   claimed that `I_∞(F,τ_{log n}K)` agrees with the ruling prediction of (iii)
   "to `10^{−11}` for `n = 2,3,4` and both test classes".  The shipped script's
   check `P2` **fails** on all six of those pairings, by `8·10^{−10}` at
   `n = 2` and by `0.6` at `n = 4` (§C2).  The theorem is not in doubt —
   (iii) is a one-line corollary of (i), which *does* reproduce to `10^{−18}` —
   but the numerical claim is false as printed.  Replaced by the reproduced
   figure for (i) plus a bracketed `[phase 135, R135_001]` note recording the
   failure, its diagnosis (quadrature: an oscillatory `n^{−iτ}` inside an
   archimedean integral truncated at `τ = 80` at 18 digits) and the statement
   that no numerical confirmation of (iii) is claimed until the script is
   repaired.  §16 (Reproduction) carries the same warning.
12. **§13's printed extrema of `ΠK` were unreproducible.**  The paper printed
   `max = 5.0287·10^{−1}`, `min = −4.4906` on `t = -4.0(0.04)4.0`; the script
   produces `16.9538` and `−8.6699` on that grid (§C3).  Withdrawn and replaced
   by the reproduced values, with the `#H⁰_Z(mK)` table restricted to the
   parameters actually run (`m ≤ 4`, `n ≤ 3`, `|a_n| ≤ 3`; the defaults exceed
   1500 s).  The reading — count `1` against a right-hand side growing like

### E1. `I_∞` is defined for all `F,G ∈ D^W`, but the explicit formula is proved only for **even** `h`

This is the weakest joint in §11 and it is load-bearing.  Theorem 11.3 carries
the hypothesis "`h ∈ D^W` **real and even**" — the proof uses
`Φ_h(1−s) = Φ_h(s)`, and it uses evenness again to double the prime term.
Definition 11.4 then sets `I_∞(F,G) := ⟨F⋆G^∨, W_fin+ω_∞⟩` and asserts that
`h = F⋆G^∨` is "real and even when `F,G` are real, by `h^∨ = h`".
**`(F⋆G^∨)^∨ = F^∨⋆G`, which is `F⋆G^∨` only when `F` and `G` are both even**
(or in other special positions); it is false for general real `F,G ∈ D^W`.
Theorem 11.5 is nevertheless stated "for all real `F,G ∈ D^W`", and the
generality is used: Theorem 11.10 and Theorem 11.11 quantify over arbitrary
primitive `F ∈ C_c^∞` (no evenness), Theorem 11.14 over arbitrary real
`F ∈ C_c^∞` and arbitrary effective `E`, and Theorem 12.3 over `E = nF`,
`E = K−nF` with `F` an arbitrary primitive test.
*What I think is true:* the Weil explicit formula does hold for non-even `h`
with the prime term `Σ Λ(n)n^{−1/2}[h(log n)+h(−log n)]` exactly as written —
the two halves come from `−ζ'/ζ(s)` and from its reflection, and the
`Φ_h(1−s) = Φ_h(s)` step can be replaced by applying the contour argument to
`−ξ'/ξ` directly.  But **that is not the proof written**, and the paper's own
rule (§B, `R135_000` rule 5) is that a prose repair is not evidence.  *Repair
required:* either restate Theorem 11.3 for general real `h ∈ D^W` with the
extra contour step written out, or restrict `I_∞` to the even sector and check
that every later quantifier is over even tests (`K`, `τ_aK`, `ΠK`, `K⋆K` are
even; a general primitive `F ∈ C_c^∞` is not, and symmetrizing `F` changes
`I_∞(F,F)`).  I did **not** attempt this repair: it is a change to a theorem,
not a typo.

### E2. Proposition 12.6 is the load-bearing step of the headline result, and it is proved by assertion

Corollary 12.4a — "the Euler product must enter through `h⁰`" — is the main
structural output of Part II, and it rests entirely on Theorem 12.4, which
rests entirely on Proposition 12.6 ("§§11–12 hold for `Z` after (S1)–(S4)").
Proposition 12.6's proof is one sentence: "*each substitution is a line-by-line
re-reading of the proofs of §§11–12*".  I would press three specific points.

 (a) **(S1) and the rank-4 ruling plane.**  For `Ξ_a` the polar set has four
     elements and `Π` becomes `Π_P` of order 4, so "primitive" is four
     conditions.  Theorem 11.10(a)⇒(c) uses Lemma 3.1 — *every primitive
     `F ∈ C_c^∞` is `ΠH` with `H ∈ C_c^∞` of the same support* — which is a
     theorem about the **second-order** operator `−∂² + ¼` on an interval.  The
     analogue for `Π_P = ∏(∂ − (p−½))` (order 4, with `P = {−a,a,1−a,1+a}`) is
     **asserted, not proved**; the surjectivity onto compactly supported
     functions satisfying four moment conditions is plausible but is exactly
     the kind of statement Lemma 3.1 needed a proof for.
 (b) **The `Ξ_a`-analogue of Paper 45 Theorem 10.4** (off-line zero ⇒ a
     primitive class with `I_∞ > 0`) is invoked inside the proof of Theorem
     12.4 in a **parenthesis**, with its ingredients named ("the interpolation
     construction of `106_11` (20)–(21)") but not checked against the four-pole
     geometry.  For `Ξ_a` the zeros sit on two lines `Re s = ½ ± a` and the
     quartet structure `{ρ, 1−ρ, ρ̄, 1−ρ̄}` is the same, but the interpolation
     must now produce a test that is primitive against **four** poles.  Until
     that is written, Theorem 12.4 and hence Corollary 12.4a are conditional on
     an unwritten transfer.
 (c) **(S2) and (S3) quantitatively.**  The claim that the weight exponent
     `½+θ` of the Chebyshev bound "always agrees" with the growth of the polar
     weight is asserted with a one-line reason.  For `Z = Ξ_a` it needs
     `Σ_{n≤x}Λ(n)(n^a+n^{−a}) = O(x^{1+a})`, which is fine, but the whole of
     Theorem 11.14's two-case split must be redone with `e^{θ|u|}` in place of

    `P4` run green, Theorem 11.8(iii) and Proposition 11.11a have no numerical
    support at all**, and `P3` — the only check that ever touches the zeros,
    and the only test of the comparison identity itself — has **never been
    run to completion** in this phase (40 min was not enough).
 2. The scripts are far too slow for their content: 40 minutes to compute a
    dozen one-dimensional integrals is a sign that `mp.quad` is being nested
    three deep (`conv` inside `prime_term` inside `geometric`) with no caching.
    A rewrite that precomputes `h` on a grid would make `P3` reachable and the
    whole suite a minute's work.  I did not do it: it is a rewrite of a
    deliverable, not a fix.
 3. `canonical_pairings.py` has no precision, grid or subset flags, so a slow
    machine cannot get a partial green; `h0_candidate.py` does (`--M`,
    `--nmax`, `--amax`), and that is the right pattern.
 4. `polya_class.py` is clean: it recomputes `K` from the literal series at 120
    digits and compares against `mpmath`'s `ξ`, which is a genuine independent
    check of (11.7)+(11.8) and of `Φ_K = ξ`.
