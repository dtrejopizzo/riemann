### H1 — (0.1) defines `I_∞^{(Z)}` with a spurious weight `Z(p)`; the round's own constants contradict it
**Location:** §0.1, display (0.1); propagates to D.19.3(ii), (D.20.4).
**Severity: MAJOR.**
**Exact claim.** "`I_∞^{(Z)}(F,G) = Σ_{p∈P} Z(p)·Φ_F(p)Φ_G(1−p) − Σ_ρΦ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ)` — Paper 46
(11.6) together with Proposition 12.6 (S2) … For `Z = ξ` this is Theorem 11.5 verbatim."
**Why wrong.** Paper 46 Theorem 11.5 (`main.md:2122`) is
`I_∞(F,G) = Φ_F(0)Φ_G(1)+Φ_F(1)Φ_G(0) − Σ_ρ…` — polar weight **one**. The factor `Z(p)` enters only
through `Φ_{K_Z}(1−p) = Z(1−p) = Z(p)` when one pairs *against `K_Z`*, which is precisely what (S2)
records: `deg_Z F := Σ_pZ(p)Φ_F(p) = I_∞^{(Z)}(F,K_Z)` (`main.md:3095`). With (0.1) as written and
`Z(p)=½` one gets `½(Φ_F(0)Φ_G(1)+Φ_F(1)Φ_G(0))`, which is **not** (11.6); and for `Ξ_Q` one gets
`I(F,K_Q) = (1/16)\deg F` and `I(K_Q,K_Q) = 1/32`, contradicting this round's own D.19.2(vi)
(`¼\deg F`, `⅛`), D.19.3(v) (`I(X,X)=2z²AB`, `I(X,K)=z²(A+B)`, `I(K,K)=2z²`) and the reduction
"Hodge ⟺ `Ŵ ≤ 0`", all three of which are computed in the correct weight-one convention.
**Minimal repair.** Delete the factor `Z(p)` from (0.1) and from `B_pol^{(Z)}`; delete the sentence
about "the normalization recorded in (S2)". Everything downstream is then consistent, and the
spurious `r_L/r_K` factor in (D.20.4) disappears with it (see H5).

### H2 — P46-5 is a misreading of Proposition 12.6, not an erratum
**Location:** Remark D.19.3b; §7 item **P46-5 (MAJOR)**; README row "D.19.3b".
**Severity: MAJOR** (it is asserted as a `PROVED` erratum on the paper-facing list).
**Exact claim.** "Proposition 12.6 uses one symbol `θ` for two logically independent quantities …
Its hypothesis (12.4) is the assertion `θ_Z ≤ θ_P`. **This is not a consequence of the other
hypotheses**" — offered as a correction of record, with `Ξ_Q` as "the counterexample".
**Why wrong.** Read literally (`main.md:3067–3086`), Proposition 12.6 (i) defines
`θ := max_{p∈P}|p−½| ≥ ½` — unambiguously `θ_P` — and (ii) *assumes* (12.4) as a displayed,
separately numbered hypothesis, with a `[REPAIRED]` note stating in as many words that "(12.4) is a
hypothesis on `Z`, verified for `ξ` and for `Ξ_a` from (E2); **it is imported data, not a claim**".
A hypothesis that the text explicitly flags as imported cannot be "conflated" with a definition, and
producing a function that violates a hypothesis is not a counterexample to a theorem — it is a
function outside the theorem's class. `Ξ_Q` in fact violates **four** of Proposition 12.6's
hypotheses (`Z > 0` on `R`; `c_n ≥ 0`; `Σ_{n≤x}c_n = O(x^{½+θ})`; (12.4)), so it is not even a
borderline case.
**What survives.** Two true and useful statements, neither of which is an erratum: (α) Theorem 11.14
and hence Theorem 12.3 are the steps that consume (12.4) — this is what (S3) and step (c) already
say; (β) `Ξ_Q` therefore lies outside Proposition 12.6's class, and the round's own explicit formula
for `Ξ_Q` (D.19.3(ii)) is a *new* proposition, not an instance of 12.6.
**Minimal repair.** Withdraw P46-5 as an erratum; relabel it **editorial (MINOR)**: "Proposition 12.6
would read better with two symbols `θ_P` and `θ_Z`, the hypothesis (12.4) being `θ_Z ≤ θ_P`", and
state separately, as new content, the `Ξ_Q` explicit formula with its own hypotheses. Correct the
README row and §7 accordingly. **The claim as written must not reach Paper 46's Part III.**

### H3 — Theorem D.19.3(ii): absolute convergence of the Dirichlet series at the contour is unjustified
**Location:** Theorem D.19.3, proof of (ii) ("any `c ∈ (σ_1, ½+α)`, which is a nonempty interval").
**Severity: MAJOR** (repairable).
**Exact claim.** The contour step needs "an abscissa `c` with `Φ_h` holomorphic on `L_c` and
`Σ_nc_Q(n)n^{−c}` absolutely convergent"; this round supplies `c ∈ (σ_1, ½+α)`.
**Why unproved.** `σ_1 = \sup\{Re ρ\}` is the abscissa beyond which `−Z_Q'/Z_Q` is *holomorphic*. It
is **not** in general the abscissa of absolute convergence `σ_a` of its Dirichlet series: for a
Dirichlet series without an Euler product there is no theorem placing `σ_a(−Z'/Z)` at `σ_1`, and the
coefficients `c_Q(n)` come from expanding `1/Z_Q`, whose absolute abscissa can exceed
`\max(σ_1, 1)`. Paper 46's Proposition 12.6 obtains this convergence from a hypothesis it *assumes*
(`Σ_{n≤x}c_n = O(x^{½+θ})`); this round has deleted that hypothesis (correctly, it is false here)
and has replaced it with nothing.
**Minimal repair.** Let `σ_a < ∞` be the abscissa of absolute convergence of `Σ_nc_Q(n)n^{−s}`
(finite, since the series converges absolutely far to the right). Take
`α > \max(θ_Z,\ σ_a − ½) + ε` and `c ∈ (σ_a, ½+α)`. `D^W(α)` still contains `K_Q` (double-exponential
decay) and `C_c^∞(R)`, so nothing downstream changes. One line.

### H4 — (D.19.5) is false for every non-principal `Q`
**Location:** Theorem D.19.3(ii), display (D.19.5); used in D.19.6.
**Severity: MAJOR** (repairable).
**Exact claim.** "`Σ_{n≥2}c_Q(n)n^{−s} := −Z_Q'/Z_Q(s)`."
**Why wrong.** `Z_Q(s) = Σ_{n≥n_0}r_Q(n)n^{−s}` with `n_0 = \min_{v≠0}Q(v) = a` for a reduced form.
Hence `−Z_Q'/Z_Q(σ) → \log n_0` as `σ → +∞`, so `−Z_Q'/Z_Q` is a Dirichlet series supported on
`n ≥ 2` **only when `n_0 = 1`**, i.e. only for the principal class. For `h(−D) > 1` there are classes
with `a ≥ 2`, and D.19.3/D.19.6 are stated for arbitrary `Q` with `h(−D)>1`.
**Minimal repair.** Write `−Z_Q'/Z_Q(s) = \log n_0 + Σ_{n≥2}c_Q(n)n^{−s}` (expand
`Z_Q = r_Q(n_0)n_0^{−s}(1+\cdots)`), and carry the constant `\log n_0` through the explicit formula
as an extra `\log n_0·Φ_h(?)`-type term, or restrict D.19.3–D.19.6 to the principal class — which is
all that D.19.4a and D.19.5 actually need (`Q_k = x²+ky²` is principal).

### H5 — Theorem D.20.2 is conditional on Artin's holomorphy conjecture, and its per-`χ` forms are ill-defined for complex `χ`
**Location:** D.20.2, (D.20.2)–(D.20.4), Theorem D.20.2, Corollary D.20.2a(b),(d).
**Severity: MAJOR.**
**Exact claims.** "`Λ(s,χ)` is entire and pole-free for `χ ≠ 1`"; "the zero multiset of `ξ_L` is the
disjoint union … of `χ(1)` copies of the zero multiset of each `Λ(·,χ)`"; hence (D.20.3), labelled
`PROVED`.
**Why wrong / unproved.** (a) **Aramata–Brauer gives only that the *quotient* `ζ_L/ζ_K` is entire.**
That individual non-abelian Artin `L`-functions are entire is **Artin's conjecture**, open. If some
`Λ(·,χ)` has poles, the zero multiset of `ξ_L` is *not* the union of the factors' zero multisets
(poles cancel zeros), and the splitting of the zero sum into per-`χ` terms fails. The conditionality
is admitted only in passing, inside Corollary D.20.2a(d) ("given Artin holomorphy for the
non-Aramata–Brauer part"), while the theorem itself is labelled `PROVED`.
(b) For a **complex** `χ`, `Λ(s,χ)` satisfies `Λ(s,χ) = W(χ)Λ(1−s,\barχ)`, not `Z(s)=Z(1−s)`; its
zero multiset is not `ρ ↦ 1−ρ` invariant, so `Ŵ^{(Λ(·,χ))}(F,G) = −Σ_ρΦ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ)` is neither
symmetric nor real, and A1 fails for it. Consequently **Corollary D.20.2a(b)'s proof is invalid as
written**: it applies "the Landau criterion … to the relevant entire order-`1` function with
functional equation", and the criterion of D.17.2/D.19.5a requires the self-dual equation
`Z(s) = Z(1−s)`.
(c) Minor consequence of H1: the constants `r_L/r_K` and `u(s)` in (D.20.2)/(D.20.4) are spurious.
By the conductor–discriminant formula `Λ_L = Λ_K∏_{χ≠1}Λ(s,χ)^{χ(1)}` exactly, so
`ξ_L = ξ_K∏Λ(s,χ)^{χ(1)}`, and in the weight-one convention the polar blocks of `ξ_L` and `ξ_K`
are literally equal — no ratio appears.
**Minimal repair.** Relabel Theorem D.20.2 `PROVED-CONDITIONAL` on Artin holomorphy (unconditional
for `L/K` abelian, where the `Λ(·,χ)` are Hecke `L`-functions and entire); pair `χ` with `\barχ` and
state the additivity over the *real* packets `Λ(·,χ)Λ(·,\barχ)`, which do satisfy `Z(s)=Z(1−s)`;
restrict D.20.2a(b) to such packets; delete `r_L/r_K` and `u`.

### H8 — D.20.1(iii): the existence of `K_K` is a sketch labelled `PROVED`
**Location:** Theorem D.20.1(iii) and its proof.
**Severity: MAJOR** (load-bearing: the whole `ξ_K` row of the axiom table, the field-uniform frame,
