## D.19 — The Epstein falsifier in row D's frame

### D.19.1 The data (CLASSICAL; exactly what is cited, and from where)

Let `Q(x,y) = ax² + bxy + cy²` be integral, positive definite, `disc Q = b²−4ac = −D < 0`, and let
`A_Q := \begin{pmatrix} a & b/2 \\ b/2 & c\end{pmatrix}`, so `\det A_Q = D/4`.

**(C1) The Epstein zeta function.** `Z_Q(s) := ½Σ'_{(m,n)∈Z²}Q(m,n)^{−s} = Σ_{n≥1}r_Q(n)n^{−s}`,
`Re s > 1`, where `r_Q(n) := ½\#\{v ∈ Z² : Q(v) = n\} ≥ 0`. Epstein, *Zur Theorie allgemeiner
Zetafunktionen*, Math. Ann. **56** (1903), 615–644. `CLASSICAL`.

**(C2) The completion, the functional equation, the poles.** `Λ_Q(s) := (√D/2π)^sΓ(s)Z_Q(s)` continues
meromorphically to `C`, is holomorphic except for simple poles at `s = 1` and `s = 0`, and
`Λ_Q(s) = Λ_Q(1−s)`; `Res_{s=1}Λ_Q = ½`, `Res_{s=0}Λ_Q = −½`; equivalently
`Res_{s=1}Z_Q = π/√D` (**independent of the class**) and `Z_Q(0) = −½`. Epstein, *op. cit.*;
Siegel, *Advanced Analytic Number Theory*, Ch. 1. `CLASSICAL`. A self-contained proof is given inside
the proof of Theorem D.19.2 below (it is the Mellin computation we need anyway), so (C2) is used there
as a **check**, not as an import.

**(C3) The class-group decomposition (Hecke/Dirichlet).** Let `K = Q(√−D_0)` be the imaginary
quadratic field of the fundamental discriminant underlying `−D`, `Cl(K)` its class group of order
`h = h(−D)`, `w` the number of roots of unity. For `Q` in the class `𝔄 ∈ Cl(K)` (primitive forms,
`D` fundamental),

    Z_Q(s) = ζ_K(s,𝔄) = \frac1h Σ_{χ ∈ \widehat{Cl(K)}} \overline{χ}(𝔄)\,L(s,χ) ,          (D.19.1)

with `L(s,χ)` the Hecke L-function of the class-group character `χ` (an Euler product); and
`ζ_K(s) = Σ_{𝔄}ζ_K(s,𝔄) = L(s,χ_0) = ζ(s)L(s,χ_{−D})`. Dirichlet; Hecke. `CLASSICAL`.

**(C4) Off-line zeros.** *(Davenport–Heilbronn, 1936.)* If `h(−D) > 1` then `Z_Q(s)` has **infinitely
many zeros in the half-plane `Re s > 1`**. H. Davenport and H. Heilbronn, *On the zeros of certain
Dirichlet series* I, II, J. London Math. Soc. **11** (1936), 181–185 and 307–312. `CLASSICAL`.
*(Voronin, 1976: infinitely many zeros in `½ < Re s < 1`; Bombieri–Hejhal for the distribution. These
are **not used**: see the Warning in D.19.5.)*

**(C5) Heilbronn.** `h(−D) → ∞` as `D → ∞`; in particular `h(−4k) > 1` for all large `k`.
H. Heilbronn, Quart. J. Math. **5** (1934), 150–160. `CLASSICAL`. (Used only to know that the family
of Theorem D.19.4a is nonempty inside `h > 1`.)

**What is *not* cited and *not* needed.** Nothing about the zeros of `Z_Q` on or near `Re s = ½`;
nothing about `L(s,χ)` beyond (D.19.1); no GRH; no Siegel zero statement. The single zero fact used is
(C4), and it is used once, in Theorem D.19.5.

### D.19.3 The Weil class, the form, and the splitting for `Ξ_Q`

**Standing restriction from here to the end of D.19 [ADDED, round 2, finding H4 of `R139_002`
(MAJOR)].** From D.19.3 to D.19.6 inclusive, `Q` is the **principal** form of its discriminant, so
that `n_0 := \min_{v≠0}Q(v) = 1` and `r_Q(1) = 1`; hence
`Z_Q(s) = 1 + Σ_{n≥2}r_Q(n)n^{−s}` and `−Z_Q'/Z_Q` is an ordinary Dirichlet series supported on the
integers `n ≥ 2`. *Why the restriction is needed:* round 1 wrote
`−Z_Q'/Z_Q(s) = Σ_{n≥2}c_Q(n)n^{−s}` for arbitrary `Q`, which is **false** whenever `n_0 ≥ 2` —
then `−Z_Q'/Z_Q(σ) → \log n_0 ≠ 0` as `σ → +∞`, and expanding
`Z_Q = r_Q(n_0)n_0^{−s}(1+W(s))` produces `\log n_0` plus a *generalized* Dirichlet series
`Σ_λ c_Q(λ)λ^{−s}` over the multiplicative semigroup generated by the ratios `n/n_0`, which need not
be integers. *Why it costs nothing:* every object this report needs is principal —
`Q_k = x²+ky²` of D.19.4a is principal, and Davenport–Heilbronn (C4) applies to the principal form
of any discriminant with `h(−D) > 1`, since `Z_{Q_0} = \frac1hΣ_χL(s,χ)` is then a nontrivial
combination of `h ≥ 2` distinct Euler products. The non-principal case is recorded as `OPEN`
(item O-10): it requires the frame of §§11–12 over a generalized Dirichlet series, which Paper 47
does not carry.

Fix such a principal `Q` with `h(−D) > 1`. Let `Z(Ξ_Q)` be the zero multiset of `Ξ_Q` and put

    σ_1 := \sup\{Re ρ : ρ ∈ Z(Ξ_Q)\},\qquad θ_Z := \sup_{ρ}|Re ρ − ½| = σ_1 − ½ .

**Lemma D.19.3a (`PROVED`).** `1 < σ_1 < ∞`, and `Z(Ξ_Q) ⊂ \{|Re s − ½| ≤ θ_Z\}`, symmetric under
`ρ ↦ 1−ρ` and `ρ ↦ \barρ`; `Σ_ρ(1+|Im ρ|)^{−2} < ∞`; and `N_{Ξ_Q}(T) = O(T\log T)`.

*Proof.* `σ_1 > 1` is (C4). Finiteness: `Z_Q(s)n_0^{s} → r_Q(n_0) > 0` as `Re s → +∞`, where `n_0` is
the least `n` with `r_Q(n) > 0`; so `Z_Q` has no zero in `Re s ≥ σ_0` for some `σ_0 < ∞`, and `Γ` and
`½s(s−1)` have none. By `Ξ_Q(s) = Ξ_Q(1−s)`, `Z(Ξ_Q) ⊂ \{1−σ_0 ≤ Re s ≤ σ_0\}`, so
`θ_Z ≤ σ_0 − ½`; the symmetry under conjugation is reality on `R`. The counting bound and the
`ℓ²`-summability are Jensen's formula for an entire function of order `1` (Titchmarsh, *Theory of

### D.19.4 Real zeros: the blueprint's subtlety, settled in both directions

**Theorem D.19.4a (`Λ_Q` has real zeros in `(0,1)` for a family with `h(−D) > 1`; `PROVED`).**
For an integer `k ≥ 1` let `Q_k(x,y) := x² + ky²`, of discriminant `−4k`. Then for every
`σ_0 ∈ (0,1)` there is `k_0(σ_0)` such that for all `k ≥ k_0(σ_0)`:

    Λ_{Q_k}(σ_0) > 0 ,

and consequently `Λ_{Q_k}` — hence `Z_{Q_k}` and `Ξ_{Q_k}` — has a **real zero in `(σ_0,1)`**. By (C5),
`h(−4k) > 1` for all large `k`, so the family of such `Q_k` with class number `> 1` is infinite.

*Proof.* Here `D = 4k`, so `c_k := 2π/√D = π/√k`. Apply (D.19.3) at the real point `s = σ_0`:

    2Λ_{Q_k}(σ_0) = \frac1{σ_0−1} − \frac1{σ_0} + J_k(σ_0) ,\qquad
    J_k(σ_0) := ∫_1^∞A_k(u)\big(u^{σ_0−1}+u^{−σ_0}\big)\,du ,

with `A_k(u) = Σ'_{v≠0}e^{−c_kuQ_k(v)} > 0`. The two vectors `v = (\pm1,0)` have `Q_k(v) = 1`, so

    A_k(u) ≥ 2e^{−πu/√k}\qquad (u ≥ 1) ,
    \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
    J_k(σ_0) ≥ 2∫_1^∞ e^{−πu/√k}\,u^{σ_0−1}\,du .

As `k → ∞` the integrand increases pointwise to `u^{σ_0−1}`, and `∫_1^∞u^{σ_0−1}du = ∞` because
`σ_0 − 1 > −1`; by monotone convergence `J_k(σ_0) → ∞`. The remaining term
`\frac1{σ_0−1}−\frac1{σ_0}` is a fixed real number depending only on `σ_0`. Hence
`Λ_{Q_k}(σ_0) > 0` for all `k ≥ k_0(σ_0)`.

On the other hand `Res_{s=1}Λ_{Q_k} = ½ > 0` (Theorem D.19.2), the pole at `s = 1` is simple, and
`Λ_{Q_k}` is real-analytic on `(0,1)`; approaching `s = 1` from the left, `Λ_{Q_k}(σ) ∼ ½/(σ−1) → −∞`.
By the intermediate value theorem `Λ_{Q_k}` vanishes at some `σ' ∈ (σ_0,1)`. Since `Γ(σ') > 0` and
`(√D/2π)^{σ'} > 0`, also `Z_{Q_k}(σ') = 0`; and `Ξ_{Q_k}(σ') = ½σ'(σ'−1)Λ_{Q_k}(σ') = 0` with
`σ' ∈ (0,1)`. ∎ `PROVED`

**Lemma D.19.4b (real zeros are confined; `PROVED`).** Every real zero of `Ξ_Q` lies in the open
