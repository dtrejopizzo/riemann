## 1. What they prove

**Local Sonin space at a finite prime (`soninppp`).**  For \(p\) finite and
\(\alpha=e_p\), the \(\mathbb Z_p^\times\)-invariant part of
\(\mathfrak{Son}_1(\mathbb Q_p,e_p)\) is **one-dimensional**, generated by

\[
 \sigma_p:=\epsilon_0-\tfrac1p\,\epsilon_1,
 \qquad
 \epsilon_n=\mathbf 1_{\{x\in\mathbb Q_p\,:\,|x|=p^n\}},
\]

and \(\mathcal F_{e_p}\sigma_p=\sigma_p\) (Fourier-invariant).

**Semilocal Sonin space (`soninsemiloc`, `sonintensor`).**  With
\(\sigma_S:=\bigotimes_{p\in S\setminus\{\infty\}}\sigma_p\) and
\(\theta_S(f):=[\sigma_S\otimes f]\), one has
\(\theta_S(f)\in\mathfrak{Son}_\lambda(X_S,\alpha)\) and

\[
 \mathcal F_\mu\bigl(w_S(\theta_S(f))\bigr)(s)
 =\mathcal F_\mu\bigl(w_\infty(f)\bigr)(s)\times
 \prod_{p\in S\setminus\{\infty\}}\bigl(1-p^{-\frac12-is}\bigr).
\]

**Stability (`isosonin`, their Theorem 4.6).**  \(\theta_S\) is a Hilbertian
isomorphism \(\mathfrak{Son}_\lambda(\mathbb R,e_\infty)\to
\mathfrak{Son}_\lambda(X_S,\alpha)\).

**The de Branges realization (`propbb`, and \eqref{ess}).**  With
\(E_S(s):=\prod_{v\in S}L_v(\tfrac12+is)\), the space \(\mathcal B_\lambda\) of
entire functions is **the same for every \(S\)**; only the inner product
changes, being induced by the embedding into
\(L^2\bigl(\mathbb R,\,ds/|E_S(s)|^2\bigr)\).

## 2. The consequence, stated as one formula

Since \(|E_S|^2=|E_\infty|^2\prod_{p\in S\setminus\infty}|L_p(\tfrac12+is)|^2\)
and \(L_p(\tfrac12+is)^{-1}=1-p^{-\frac12-is}\):

> **Proposition 1.**  Passing from the archimedean Sonin space to the semilocal
> one multiplies the spectral measure by the finite Euler factor:
> \[
>  \boxed{\;\frac{ds}{|E_S(s)|^2}
>   =\frac{ds}{|E_\infty(s)|^2}\;
>   \prod_{p\in S\setminus\{\infty\}}\bigl|1-p^{-\frac12-is}\bigr|^{2}. \;}
> \]

Nothing else changes: same space of entire functions, same \(\lambda\), same
archimedean data.  And by `soninppp` the weight is **forced** — the local Sonin
space is one-dimensional, so \(\sigma_S\) is unique up to scale and there is no
choice to make.

## 3. The gap in (H), measured

(H) asks for
\(\operatorname{Tr}(\vartheta(f)\mathbf S_S)=\sum_{v\in S}W_v^{CC}(f)+E_S(f)\).
Compare the two sides after Proposition 1.

**What the Sonin side supplies.**  Expanding the weight,

\[
 \prod_{p\in S}\bigl|1-p^{-\frac12-is}\bigr|^{2}
 =\sum_{m,n}\frac{\mu(m)\mu(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\Bigl(\frac mn\Bigr)^{-is},
\]

the sum over \(m,n\) squarefree with prime factors in \(S\).  The coefficients
are **Möbius**, and the structure is **multiplicative**.

**What the Weil side needs.**  By `eq:Ktest`,
\(W_{\rm fin}(h)=\sum_{p,k\ge1}\log p\,(h(p^k)+p^{-k}h(p^{-k}))\), i.e.
coefficients \(\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n\), **von Mangoldt**, and the structure is
**additive**.

> **Proposition 2 (the gap).**  The two are a logarithmic derivative apart:
> the Sonin weight is \(|L_S(\tfrac12+is)|^{-2}\), whereas the Weil finite term
> is built from \(-\tfrac{d}{ds}\log L_S\).  Converting a multiplicative
> Euler-product weight into the additive von Mangoldt sum is exactly the content
> of a trace formula, and **that computation is what (H) requires and what CCM
> 2310.18423 does not perform.**

This is not a guess about their paper.  Searching the source for any evaluation
of \(\operatorname{Tr}(\vartheta(f)\mathbf S_S)\) returns nothing; the only
statements are programmatic (lines 197–198):

> *"We **expect** that the use of such operator-theoretic tools in the semilocal
> case **opens a way** to handle Weil's positivity as in [CCweil].  In fact, the
> operator theoretic aspect of the present paper provides a more precise
> **strategy** for addressing the semilocal Weil positivity by **comparing** the
> trace functional associated to the operator — which is automatically positive
> for a selfadjoint operator — with the Weil functional."*

So (H) is one explicit analytic step away from what is proved, and the step is
named: compute the trace of the scaling action against the measure of
Proposition 1.

## 4. Independent confirmation of the point-1 refutation

`115_11` Proposition 1 computed the Fourier coupling on the shell basis and
found a **single** negative band at \(u+v=-1\), refuting the identification of
row (a)'s radix\(-2\) code with the shell grading.

CCM's `soninppp` confirms this from their side: the generator of the local
Sonin space is \(\sigma_p=\epsilon_0-\tfrac1p\epsilon_1\) — a **two-term
combination with exactly one sign change**, and it is the *whole* space, which
is one-dimensional.  A radix\(-2\) structure would require an alternating
\((-1)^j\) across all digit places, i.e. an infinite-dimensional alternating
object.  Two independent computations, same verdict.

This also sharpens why: there is no room for a digit expansion at all, because
the invariant local Sonin space has dimension one per prime.
