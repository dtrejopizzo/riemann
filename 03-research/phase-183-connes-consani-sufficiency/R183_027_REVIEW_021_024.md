* **MAJOR.** R183.021 Thm 3.4 depends on (CF-S), and (CF-S) is most likely **false**.
  As written, (CF-S)'s "weak form" does not suffice for Thm 3.4. The §3.3 argument,
  "θ_S only changes the metric, hence it cannot carry a prime atom," is invalid (§2.3 below).
* **MAJOR (new; CONDITIONAL on RH).** There is a stronger falsifier than
  R183.024 §3.4's Szegő one and than the editor's suspicion. Below the first zero γ₁≈14.13 there are no
  zeros and Son's density is > 0. Hence, for every fixed η<1,

  PROVED label of 021–024 falls because of this, but three readings do (§5).

  (CC (99), Lemma 5.4). Moreover ε̂(τ) ≈ −2ε'(1⁺)/τ² < 0 at high frequency. −∫c_hε is not
  negative semidefinite.
* **Statement discriminating between 021 and 024 (§6).** Is the distribution
  F^{−1}[2π(ρ_S − ρ_∞)], restricted to (−2T,2T), equal to
  −Σ_p log p Σ_k p^{−k/2}(δ_{k log p}+δ_{−k log p})

## 1. R183.021 — Semilocal Sonin as a positive part

### Errors

1. **MAJOR — §3.3, "Why it cannot help, even without (CF-S)".** The reasoning "D only changes
   the metric; the space remains archimedean; nothing forces the prime oscillation" is false. It is
   checked against the source itself. Let V_∞ = U_∞(Son(R)) = H ∩ W, with H the support condition
   (a Hardy in the Mellin variable) and W the Fourier condition (a reflected Hardy twisted
   by χ_∞). By CCM (ms1), V_S = D·V_∞, with D(s)=∏(1−p^{−½−is}).
   * D and D^{−1} are causal and bounded, so they preserve H: D·H = H.
   * conj D and its inverse preserve W, so D·W = (D/conj D)·W.
   * Hence V_S = H ∩ (D/conj D)·W.

   The semilocal Sonin space is **the same construction with the unimodular twist χ_∞ replaced by
   χ_∞·(D/conj D)**, consistent with F_S. The phase derivative of that twist contains
   exactly Σσ_p(s log p). R183.015 (PROVED) shows that the relative trace of a twisted Hardy
   space is exactly the phase derivative. Hence the prime oscillation **does** enter the diagonal
   density of the projection onto one condition. What remains to be decided is only whether the intersection
   correction (the overlap term PP̂_SP, the semilocal analogue of ε) cancels it. That is the
   open question, and the Christoffel heuristic does not answer it.
2. **MAJOR — (CF-S) and its heuristic.** The Christoffel heuristic assumes that the reproducing
   kernel is localized at scale 1/log s against the period 2π/log p of the weight |D|². But the
   de Branges kernel decays like sinc (the text itself admits this), and the weight acts through a
   Hilbert-type term, ∫(w(s')−w(s))/(s−s')² ds' = −π|∂|w. That term gives corrections
   **absolute O(1) and oscillating at frequency log p**: the same order and the same frequency as
   the prime term. An exact model confirms this: in PW_T with weight |1−εe^{−isa}|², the diagonal
   density changes by O(a) via boundary layers. (CF-S) is unmotivated and most likely
   false. Thm 3.4 is CONDITIONAL on a probably-false hypothesis, and the heading "NO-GO
   for CCM's semilocal Sonin" must be withdrawn from the verdict.
3. **MAJOR — "The weak form liminf(ρ_S−ρ_∞) ≥ 0 on average suffices".** False. Lemma 3.2(b) measures
   M(ψ)+½a_{log2}(ψ): the Bohr coefficient at cos(s log 2). An r with zero average over windows
   can have a nonzero coefficient at that frequency: r = c·cos(s log 2) satisfies the weak form and
   changes the limit. The coefficient of cos(s log 2) in ρ_S−ρ_∞ must vanish. It is
   exactly what point 3 of the file's own "Review points" fears.
4. **MINOR — Lemma 3.2(a), "R' bounded ⟺ φ bounded".** The "only if" is false. The symbol is only
   determined modulo distributions whose inverse transform vanishes on (−2T,2T) (R183.022
   Prop. 1.1(c)). For example, φ = s²cos(3Ts) has zero compression to I_T.
5. **MINOR — Lemma 3.2(b) with infinite spectrum of ψ.** The vanishing of cross correlations
   "by choice of t_j" requires avoiding a countable set of differences and using dominated
   convergence in Σ|a_λ|. It is fixable, but not written out.
6. **MINOR — §4.4 labeled PROVED "modulo the A_T ↔ E_S dictionary".** A PROVED modulo an
   unverified identification (rule: check against Paper 46's def:W) is
   CONDITIONAL. Paper 46's Thm C is also of primitive form only.

### Checks that pass

* **Thm 3.1 (structure).** Correct, and valid **without** scale invariance of P. The reason is that
  ϑ(g)^*ϑ(g) is a function of the generator (multiplication by |ĝ|² on U_S), and
  Tr(ϑ(g)Pϑ(g)^*) = Tr(P^{1/2}U^*|ĝ|²UP^{1/2}). The measure μ_P is absolutely continuous, because
  each vector has spectral measure |f|²ds. The theorem is trivial but correct.
* m_S = m − Σσ_p, with σ_p = −2Re(L_p'/L_p)(½+is): verified.
* Rem. 3.0: verified (m(0) = −log π + ψ(¼) ≈ −5.372).
* Prop. 3.3 (sandwich): correct. For S={∞,2}, |D|² = 3/2 − √2 cos(s log 2) ∈ [0.0858, 2.914].
* **Constant −log2/√2: verified.** The coefficient of cos(s log 2) in −σ_2 is −2 log2·2^{−½};
  the limit is a_0 + ½a_a = −log2/√2. Matches R183.018 Thm 5.1.
* Thm 4.3: the proof is correct. The form E_S is closed because m_S is bounded below;
  q_C is monotone and T_{C₀} is compact.

### Labels to downgrade

## 2. R183.022 — partial Sonin multiplier

### Errors

1. **FATAL (label) — Thm 3.3, "without assuming RH".** See §0. For each fixed T it is repairable by taking
   R = R(T) ≳ e^{T/(2K−1)}, which forces |J| ≫ R(T)log b / T. The statement "interval of length
   ≥ 1 and T ≥ T_1(η,φ)" is not proved without RH. With RH, the proof (Schur Gram,
   zero counting) goes through.
   * The Gram is T·(|φ|²)^∧(C_φπ(j−k)), and the Schur bound with rapid decay is correct.
   * Uniformity over distant zeros is only correct with |Im γ| = 0.
   * **New label: CONDITIONAL(RH)**, or PROVED for the version with |J| ≥ e^{εT}.
2. **MAJOR — Rem. 3.2 and Cor. 3.4: the given-up part (1/2π)∫Hm|F|² is not η·Son.** Son's
   density is 2πρ_∞ = m + ε̂ (CC (83), R183.018 Prop. 3.1), not m. The given-up part H·m is not ≥ 0 where

   object different from (L-Son), and the phrase "it is η·Son" is false. It is repairable by replacing m with
   2πρ_∞. Then the restriction a > s_m+1 loses its justification and the

3. **MINOR — Thm 2.1, Landau–Widom term.** With τ = ε/(M+ε), the factor is log((1−τ)/τ) =
   log(M/ε), not log((M+ε)/ε).

## 4. R183.024 — Connes's later route

### Errors

1. **MAJOR — N_T's strong falsifier is not the Szegő one.** N_T's natural falsifier
   is the low-band one (§5) and does not require ε ∈ L¹. Under RH, if inf_{|s|≤Ω} ρ_{S(T)}(s) ≥ e^{−o(T)}
   for some Ω < γ₁, then n₋(E_S − Son_S | prim ∩ ker ℓ) ≥ (2ΩT/π)(1−o(1)) − 3. Hence
   N_T is false for large T. Since also ∀T C_T ⟹ RH (Prop. 2.5 / §3.2.2), **"C_T for all T"
   is false unconditionally**, unless ρ_{S(T)} becomes exponentially small throughout the
   band [−γ₁, γ₁].
   * For fixed S that condition holds: 021's sandwich Prop. 3.3 gives ρ_S ≥ |D|²ρ_∞/C_D > 0.
   * For S = S(T), the sandwich only gives e^{−O(e^T/T)}, which is not enough.

   §4's L2 ("decides viability") must be replaced by this statement: "lower bound on
   ρ_{S(T)} at low frequency".
2. **MINOR — Lemma 2.3, conjugation.** C^*C's kernel is N(u,v) = c_h(u−v). With y = x−a, the
   trace of QVP·C^*C equals a·c_h(−a) = a·conj c_h(a), not a·c_h(a). Summing with the adjoint gives
   2a Re c_h(a) and the result does not change. **The direction is correct**: (Vf)(x) = f(x−a) breaks
   L²(−∞,0) and the kernel lives on x ∈ (0,a). The first-order expansion P_{gK} = P + ε(QAP+PA^*Q)
   is correct.
3. **MINOR — Prop. 2.4 mislabeled (too conservative).** In the single-condition model,
   (1−εV)H₋ = u·H₋ with u unimodular, after factoring out the invertible part preserving H₋. The
   relative trace of a twisted Hardy is already **PROVED** in R183.015, with all k, with no
   need for Widom. Prop. 2.4 can be raised to PROVED-in-the-model citing R183.015. The
   claim about cross terms between primes is unnecessary: the phase is additive.
4. **MINOR — Prop. 2.2.** "φ_S' grows like log" and finiteness of the trace are not proved. The
   identification 2φ_∞' − m = ε̂ (the file's own point 1) is consistent with 2πρ_∞ = m + ε̂ if
   the same scale of ĥ is used, but it is not verified in the text.

### Central question: is the half-line model faithful?

**Partially, and precisely.**
* In the log-Mellin variable, Son(R)^⊥ = closure(H^⊥ + W^⊥), where:
  * H^⊥ = functions supported on |x| < 1, i.e. **exactly a half-line** t < 0;
  * W^⊥ = its Fourier transform, i.e. **exactly a reflected, twisted Hardy space**.
* Each summand of the complement is, hence, a half-line space in a suitable frame, and 024's
  model faithfully represents the **Fourier condition alone**. In it, the
  semilocal twist produces exactly the prime term (by R183.015).
* What the model discards is the **overlap** between H^⊥ and W^⊥ (the operator PP̂P, with
  eigenvalues λ(n)²). For S={∞}, that overlap is exactly what produces ε.
* In the semilocal case, PP̂_SP is still a compact Hankel type: F followed by dilations by
  p^k, restricted to |x| < 1. It is plausible that its contribution is a continuous kernel, but that
  **is** I_S.
* The model is faithful for the dominant term (one condition) and unfaithful for the intersection.
  §2.4 is correctly labeled "given I_S".

**On the sign risk (the file's own point 2).** The semilocal twist is
χ_∞·(D/conj D), and its orientation is uniform in v by Tate's local functional equation
(γ_v = L_v(½−is)/L_v(½+is) for every v ∈ S). Hence the prime term enters the density of
the Fourier condition with the **same** relative sign as the Gamma phase, which in CC (83) enters
with a + sign in Son. The conclusion is E_S's sign, not the opposite. ARGUED, not
PROVED.

### Checks that pass

* §2.1: V_S^⊥ = (D^*)^{−1}V_∞^⊥ = η_S(V_∞^⊥). Verified against groundstatedual (iii) (l. 78 of
  the block: η_S multiplies by ∏L_p(½−is)) and against isosonin's proof.
* Prop. 2.5, §3.2(2), and §3.3 (deletion): correct.
* §3.4: λ_max(P K P) ≤ sup K̂ holds for any extension of the kernel, so sup K̂ ≥ 1.0516 is
  correct. The application of Szegő to fixed S is correct, CONDITIONAL on ε ∈ L¹.
* The chain C_T ⟹ D_T|ker ℓ ⟹ (∀T) RH is correct.

### Labels to downgrade

* No PROVED falls.
* §4 "first lemma": the recommended order must change (see error 1).
* Prop. 2.4: can be **raised**.

### What survives

* §2.1 and §2.4 (given I_S).

## 5. The editor's suggestion (archimedean comparison)

* **The reading of (84) is false.** ε(ρ) = Σλ(n)(1−λ(n)²)^{−½}⟨ξ_n|ϑ(ρ^{−1})ζ_n⟩ with ξ_n ≠ ζ_n,
  so it is not a sum of diagonal coefficients.
  * By CC (99)/Lemma 5.4, ε(ρ) = Σλ²/(1−λ²)·ρ^{½}∫_{ρ^{−1}}^1 ξ_n(x)ξ_n(ρx)dx, so ε(1) = 0.
  * If ε(e^{|x|}) were of positive type, Bochner would give |ε| ≤ ε(1) = 0, contradicting
    ε'(1⁺) ≈ 22.9965.
  * Moreover ε(e^{|x|}) ≈ ε'(1⁺)|x| near 0, hence ε̂(τ) ≈ −2ε'(1⁺)/τ² < 0 at high frequency.
    The symbol of −E is **positive** at infinity, consistent with CC (102)–(104):
    2ε'(1⁺)(1−K̂) → 2ε'(1⁺) > 0.
  * A pointwise argument about the symbol's sign is also invalid, because E_S's symbol
    with S = S(T) is not fixed and the pointwise sign is not invariant (022 Prop. 1.1(c)).
* **The correct version (ARGUED; CONDITIONAL on RH).** Let Ω < γ₁ ≈ 14.13 and
  c_Ω := inf_{|s|≤Ω} 2πρ_∞(s) > 0. That c_Ω > 0 is because ρ_∞ = φ_∞'/π, with φ_∞ the de Branges
  phase of B_1 (CCM propbb).
  * Let V be the space of the ≈ 2ΩT/π first bandwidth-Ω prolates of I_T. There the leakage outside
    [−Ω,Ω] is e^{−cT}.
  * By the explicit formula (R183.022 Prop. 3.1, normalization verified) and under RH,
    E_S(h) = Σ_γ|ĥ(γ)|² ≤ e^{−c'T}‖h‖², using Plancherel–Pólya with log t zero density.
  * Meanwhile, (1−η)Son(h) ≥ (1−η)(c_Ω/2)‖h‖².
  * Hence, n₋(E_S − (1−η)Son | prim ∩ ker ℓ) ≥ 2ΩT/π − O(log T) − 3 → ∞ for every η < 1.
  * **Control:** at T = ½log 2, with Ω = γ₁, one obtains 2c/π ≈ 3.1 modes, and minus 3 restrictions
    leaves ≈ 0. This is consistent with CC's n₋ ≤ 1.
* **Effect on 021–024.** It invalidates no PROVED. It does force:
  * reclassifying 023 §4.4;
  * qualifying 023 §5.3 (η_1(T) → 1);
  * replacing 022 Thm 3.3's high band with the low band, with no need to kill zeros;
  * replacing 024's L2 (§4, error 1).

  The mechanism is **not** "m < 0 and ε̂ ≥ 0", but "there are no zeros below γ₁ and the
  Sonin density is positive there".

## 6. Cross-consistency 021 vs 024

* **Both start from the same source fact**: V_S = D·V_∞ (CCM ms1/isosonin).
* **They disagree on a single statement:**

  > **(Δ)** For S ∋ 2 and T > ½log 2, the distribution F^{−1}[2π(ρ_S − ρ_∞)], restricted to
  > (−2T,2T), has at ±log 2 an atom of mass −log 2·2^{−½} (with all ±k log p
  > analogues). Equivalently, the Bohr coefficient of cos(s log 2) in 2π(ρ_S − ρ_∞) is
  > −√2 log 2, instead of 0.

  * I_S (024) ⟹ (Δ).
  * (CF-S) (021) ⟹ ¬(Δ).
  * 021's Lemma 3.2(b) turns (Δ) into "limit 0" and ¬(Δ) into "limit −log2/√2" on
    the pair family. It is an exact test between the two.
* **Which case is stronger.** 024's, for three reasons.
  1. The reduction V_S = H ∩ (D/conj D)·W (§1, error 1) shows that the semilocal Sonin space is
     the archimedean construction with the twist χ_S.
  2. R183.015 (PROVED) gives the exact relative trace of a twisted Hardy space as its phase
     derivative, with the full prime term and uniform orientation in v.
  3. Hence (Δ) holds for the part of one condition. It would only fail if the overlap correction
     PP̂_SP contained opposite atoms at ±k log p.

  021's heuristic ignores the hard-edge structure, and its own direct computation gives an error
  O(1). Neither is proved: the open content is exactly the regularity of the
  semilocal overlap term.
* **Final note.** Even if (Δ) were proved, §5's falsifier leaves C_T ∀T with no options,
  unless ρ_{S(T)} degenerates at low frequency. I_S survives as an eigenvalue trace theorem,
  not as a route to D.
