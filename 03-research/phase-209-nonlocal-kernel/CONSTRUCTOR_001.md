## 1. Result and limit

I did not obtain a kernel with its own arithmetic positivity. I did obtain an exact control for a subclass of O1:

> **PROVED.** A compact integral correction of the FE involution keeps infinitely many negative directions on any closed subspace of finite codimension. On a bounded interval of heights, this remains visible on the linear ideal of Dirichlet polynomials generated by a fixed nonzero arithmetic comb, even after imposing finitely many continuous linear constraints.

The class is `J + C_b`, with `C_b` compact self-adjoint; not that of all O1 kernels. The result **does not require** ambient positivity of the sought object. It says that this subclass can only serve if positivity is asserted on an arithmetic domain considerably smaller than the linear ideals considered here. Nothing is proved against a nonlinear intersection of complete Euler product and FE, nor against a compression to PW_T.

The `non-compact correction` exit does not produce a sign either: the example `C=I−J`, giving `J+C=I`, is a generic norm and is discarded before attempting a link with the node.

## 4. The test subclass: FE plus regular kernel

To separate the height from the window of the node, I use A>0 for the cut in height and reserve T for the support in x. Let

`H_A=L²([-A,A],dt)`, `χ_t=χ(½+it)`, `(Jf)(t)=χ_t f(−t)`.

Only `|χ_t|=1` and `χ_tχ_{−t}=1` are used. Then J is self-adjoint unitary. The eigenspaces V₊ and V₋ have infinite dimension: one can freely choose f on (0,A) and extend via `f(−t)=±χ_{−t}f(t)`.

Let b be a real comb. To each b one is allowed to assign **any** Hermitian kernel

`c_b(t,t′)∈L²([-A,A]²)`, `(C_bf)(t)=∫c_b(t,t′)f(t′)dt′`.

C_b is bounded compact self-adjoint. The test operator is

`K_b=J+C_b`.

The kernel of K_b is distributional: `χ_t δ(t+t′)+c_b(t,t′)`. The regular integral part can be genuinely two-variable; the class does not require `w(t+t′)`. **We do not claim it is the final regular kernel requested by the task**: it is a first class of repair of the FE examined before looking for positivity.

### Lemma 4.1 — The correction is not decorative locality (PROVED)

The algebra of multipliers and J on H_A contains no nonzero compacts; hence, if C_b≠0, K_b lies outside that algebra on H_A.

**Proof.** Identify H_A with `L²((0,A);C²)` pairing t and −t and absorbing the unitary phases. Each element of the algebra is multiplication by an essentially bounded measurable 2×2 matrix. If such an operator B is nonzero, there exist ε>0, a constant vector v among a finite family generating C² and a set E of positive measure where `|B(t)v|≥ε`. Split E into infinitely many disjoint sets of positive measure and normalise `1_Ej v`. Their images are orthogonal and of norm ≥ε, contradicting compactness. The same argument covers the norm closure, since it is still matrix multiplication. ∎

**Effective limit.** This claim refers to H_A, which is not PW_T. After another restriction or change of representation the analysis must be repeated. It has not been proved that the compression of C_b to the domain of the node lies outside the effective algebra; that step is not claimed.

### Theorem 4.2 — A compact repair gives no sign on a large domain (PROVED)

Let C be compact self-adjoint on H_A. For every closed subspace L⊂H_A of finite codimension, the restriction of `J+C` to L has infinitely many negative directions. More precisely, for each ε∈(0,1) there is an infinite-dimensional subspace E⊂L∩V₋ where

`⟨f,(J+C)f⟩≤−(1−ε)‖f‖²`.

**Proof.** By compactness there is a finite-rank operator R with `‖C−R‖<ε`. The space `E=L∩V₋∩ker R` still has infinite dimension. For f∈E: `Jf=−f`, `Rf=0`, and hence the form is `−‖f‖²+⟨f,(C−R)f⟩≤−(1−ε)‖f‖²`. ∎

This is an exact obstruction, not a bound on primes. It does not claim that the actual correction must be compact nor that the arithmetic domain must have finite codimension.

## 5. An explicit arithmetic domain where the test also fails

For fixed X>2, let

`P_b(t)=Σ_{2≤n≤X} b(n)n^{−½−it}`,

and let 𝒟 be the space of all finite Dirichlet polynomials with free complex coefficients. I define the **test ideal**

`𝕀_{b,X}={P_b D_a : D_a∈𝒟}`.

The coefficients of each element are the Dirichlet convolution of truncated b with a, with weight ½. This introduces arithmetic as a fixed generator of the domain; it is not the variety of complete Euler products. It is not identified with it.

### Lemma 5.1 — Density (PROVED)

𝒟 is dense in H_A. If P_b is not identically zero, 𝕀_{b,X} is also dense.

**Proof.** If h is orthogonal to all functions `n^{−it}`, its entire transform `H(z)=∫_{−A}^A h(t)e^{itz}dt` satisfies `|H(z)|≤C e^{A|Im z|}` and has zeros at `log n`, n≥1. If H were not identically zero, Jensen's formula, centred at a point where it does not vanish, would give O(r) zeros in discs of radius r/2: each zero in that disc contributes at least log2 in the disc of radius r and `log|H|≤O(r)`. But there are at least `exp(r/3)` zeros `log n` in that disc for large r. Contradiction. Hence H=0 and h=0 by Fourier uniqueness.

P_b is analytic nonzero and vanishes only on a set of measure zero. If h is orthogonal to `P_b𝒟`, then `overline(P_b)h` is orthogonal to 𝒟 (P_b bounded on the interval); by the above it is zero, and h=0 almost everywhere. ∎

### Corollary 5.2 — Failure of the repair on that ideal (PROVED)

If P_b≠0, the form of J+C_b takes a negative value on 𝕀_{b,X}. The same happens after imposing any finite number of continuous linear constraints on that ideal.

**Proof.** The form is continuous and the ideal is dense. For constraints `L:H_A→C^r`, the intersection `𝕀_{b,X}∩ker L` is dense in ker L: choose in the ideal preimages of a basis of `L(H_A)` and correct a dense approximation via that finite family. Theorem 4.2 provides a negative vector in ker L, which is approximated while keeping the constraints. ∎

**What it does not prove.** This density uses free a. It does not say that complete Eulerian coefficients are dense, nor that their intersection with the FE is large. Nor does it turn the domain of tests of D into an ideal of polynomials. It only discards an explicit and natural domain that could be mistaken for a sufficient arithmetic restriction.

## 6. Interior deletion and specificity

For a prime p, I define exactly

`b^(p)(n)=0 if n=p^k (k≥1); b^(p)(n)=b(n) otherwise`.

If `p<e^{2T}`, the complete identity `Σ_{d|n}Λ(d)=log n` fails for b=Λ^(p) already at n=p: its left side is 0 and the right side log p. It is an exact separator; no sign is attributed to it.

The previous class allows c_b to depend arbitrarily on that identity. Even so, Theorems 4.2 and 5.2 hold both for Λ and for Λ^(p) whenever the generating polynomial is nonzero. **We do not obtain a positivity for Λ that breaks upon deletion:** the test fails earlier, already for Λ. The extreme case P_b=0 is a null ideal, not a success of specificity.

Consequently no attempt is made to link this form with Q_T. The complement of N(T), the coupling, the odd part and the polar sector have not been controlled. There is no claim about certified windows.

## 8. Residue left by this check

For the ansatz J+compact C, a positivity domain that is a closed linear subspace would have to have infinite codimension; moreover, it cannot contain one of the dense ideals examined. For a nonlinear arithmetic variety no notion of codimension is imposed here. Another possibility is to change the operator to a non-compact correction and prove a specific positivity or link. These are search possibilities, not proved engines.

This lemma is not an absolute barrier for O1 and does not recommend abandoning O1. It avoids one specific test: keeping the full signature of J and trying to cure it with a compact correction while arithmetic only restricts to a dense ideal or finitely many linear conditions.
