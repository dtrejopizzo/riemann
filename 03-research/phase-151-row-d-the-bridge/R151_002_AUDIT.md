## §1. Lemma 2.2, line by line (lines 162–231)

**(a), lines 176–191.** Correct. (2.4) is the standard smoothed explicit formula for `w ∈ C_c^∞`,
`supp w ⊆ [1,2]`; absolute convergence of `Σ_ρ m_ρ\|W(ρ)\|` is justified correctly (rapid decay of `W` in
vertical strips against `N(T) ≪ T\log T`). (2.5) and the conclusion `σ_2(w) ≤ β_max` follow. **Two remarks
R151_001 does not make:** (i) the deduction is via a *pointwise* bound, so (a) is not an `L²` statement
at all — it is `L^∞` in disguise and is *exactly* the trivial half of Theorem 27.10; (ii) the claim in
Remark 2.2b that (a) "is strictly stronger than the `L^∞ ⟹ L²` direction because it is unconditional" is
incoherent: (a) *is* the `L^∞` bound, followed by `L^∞ ⟹ L²`. **REPAIR: delete that sentence.**

**Is Plancherel applicable?** The question is moot because Plancherel is never invoked. It *would* be
applicable in the form `∫_0^∞ \|S_w(e^u)e^{−σu}\|²du = (2π)^{−1}∫_R \|G(σ+it)\|²dt` for `σ > β_max`
(Mellin–Plancherel on the multiplicative measure `dN/N` after the substitution `N = e^u`), given
`S_w(e^u)e^{−σu} ∈ L²(du)` — which (a) supplies. But R151_001's `σ_2` is defined with `dN` and the
weight `N^{−2σ−1}`, i.e. `∫ \|S_w\|²N^{−2σ}\,dN/N`, so the correct `L²` measure is `dN/N = du`, and the
transform whose vertical `L²` norm appears is `G(σ+it)` — the *same* `G` as in (b). A genuine
Mellin–Plancherel proof of (b) would read the pole of `G` at `ρ` off the divergence of
`∫_R \|G(σ+it)\|²dt` as `σ ↓ Re ρ`, which is **not** what is written. **What is written is strictly
weaker and strictly simpler:** absolute convergence (Cauchy–Schwarz, lines 193–197 — exponents verified,
`(−σ'−1/2)·2 = −2σ'−1` and `(σ'−σ+1/2−1/2)·2 = 2σ'−2σ−1`, finite iff `σ' < σ`) gives holomorphy of `G` on
`Re s > σ_2(w)`; a pole of residue `−m_ρW(ρ) ≠ 0` at `ρ` then forces `Re ρ ≤ σ_2(w)`. **Sound.** The
sign is right (`ζ` has a zero of order `m_ρ`, so `−ζ'/ζ ∼ −m_ρ/(s−ρ)`).

**Line 204–206, `E(s)` entire: FALSE.** `E(s) = ∫_0^1(ψ_w(N) − Nŵ(0))N^{−s−1}dN`. Since `ψ_w ≡ 0` on
`(0,1/2)`, the first piece `∫_{1/2}^1ψ_wN^{−s−1}dN` is entire; but the subtraction contributes
`−ŵ(0)∫_0^1N^{−s}dN = −ŵ(0)/(1−s)`, meromorphic with a **pole at `s=1`**. R151_001's own parenthetical
writes that term and still calls the total entire. (Additionally, if one splits at `1/2` the constant is
`(1/2)^{1−s}/(1−s)`, not `1/(1−s)`; R151_001's split at `1` is the consistent one.) **The same error
sits in Corollary 26.5 (main.md:10914, "with `E_θ` entire") and should be corrected there too.** No
consequence for (b) or (c), since `ρ ≠ 1`. **Minimum repair: replace "entire" by "holomorphic on
`C ∖ \{1\}`, with at most a simple pole at `s = 1`".**

**(c), the `θ`-family, lines 212–216.** **Sound.** `w_θ(u) = w(u)u^θ` is admissible (`C_c^∞`, `≥ 0`,
same support); `W_θ(s) = W(s+θ)`; if `β_max > σ_0` pick `ρ` with `Re ρ > σ_0`, apply (b) to every `θ`,
get `W(ρ+θ) = 0` for all real `θ`, so `W` vanishes on a horizontal line, so `W ≡ 0`, contradicting
`W(1) > 0`. No gap. **But this is verbatim Corollary 26.5's proof** (main.md:10905–10922), including the
horizontal-line/limit-point argument and the `W(1) > 0` contradiction. The device is not merely
"Corollary 26.5's device", as R151_001 says in Remark 2.2a; the *whole of (b)+(c)* is Corollary 26.5's
proof with the input bound weakened from `≪ N^{1/2+ε}` to `L²`-finiteness.

**Consequence for the novelty claim (§0 item 1).** "Lemma 2.2 is not in the corpus (grep #4)" is an
artefact of the grep: the auditor's grep `-n "Corollary 26.5"` finds the collision immediately. Lemma 2.2
should be labelled **`RESTATEMENT` of Corollary 26.5 in `L²` form**, `PROVED`. Its only genuine
increment is that it needs no pointwise bound on `S_w` — which matters only if some family hypothesis
delivers `L²` control without `L^∞` control, and **no such hypothesis is exhibited anywhere in the
round**. Until one is, the increment is empty.

**Is the `θ`-family device sound? Yes — and it is a real weakening of T6(ii).** Theorem 26.4 needs
`H(k,c)` for one weight; T6(ii) needs it for *every* `w_θ`, `θ ∈ R`. So on the hypothesis side T6(ii) is
**strictly stronger a hypothesis for the same conclusion**. R151_001 flags the proviso (Remark 2.2a,
O-151.1) but never draws this comparison, and §0's claim that the `L²` route "makes the argument survive
at `k ≥ 2`" is unsupported: Theorem 26.4 already survives at every `k`, by taking `2k`-th roots.

## §2. Lemma 2.4 (lines 245–274): the direction of Hölder and the pigeonhole

Checked in full; **no error found.**

* *Normalisations.* `σ_2` uses `N^{−2σ−1}`, `σ_{2k}` uses `N^{−σ−1}`. This asymmetry is deliberate and
  correct (it is what makes the conclusion read `σ_{2k} ≥ 2kσ_2`), but it is a trap for the reader and
  should be spelled out in one sentence.
* *Step 2, Hölder direction.* `f := g^{2k}N^{−2kσ−1} ≥ 0`, `f^{1/k} = g²N^{−2σ−1/k}`, and
  `f^{1/k}·N^{1/k−1} = g²N^{−2σ−1}` ✓. Hölder with `(k, k/(k−1))` bounds `A(X)` **above** by
  `(∫f)^{1/k}(\log X)^{(k−1)/k)}`, since `(1/k−1)·k/(k−1) = −1` exactly ✓. An upper bound on `A` is
  what yields the **lower** bound (2.8) on `∫f`. **The direction is right** (the natural error — using
  Hölder to bound `∫f` above — is not made).
* *Step 1, pigeonhole.* `σ'' ∈ (σ,σ_2)` ⟹ `Σ_j a_j = ∞` ✓ (definition of the infimum). Hence for every
  `j_0` some `j > j_0` has `a_j ≥ j^{−2}` ✓. `A(2^{j+1}) ≥ 2^{2j(σ''−σ)}a_j` ✓ (`σ'' > σ`, `N ≥ 2^j`).
  With `X_i = 2^{j_i+1}`: `2^{2j_i(σ''−σ)} = X_i^{2(σ''−σ)}2^{−2(σ''−σ)}`, so
  `A(X_i) ≥ c\,X_i^{2(σ''−σ)}j_i^{−2} ≥ X_i^{σ''−σ}` once `X_i^{σ''−σ} ≥ c^{−1}(\log_2X_i−1)²`, true for
  large `i` ✓. The `j^{−2}` choice is the standard divergent-series pigeonhole and is correct.
* *Step 3.* `X_i^{k(σ''−σ)}/(\log X_i)^{k−1} → ∞` ✓; power beats log, as Remark 2.4a says.
* *Edge cases.* `k = 1`: vacuous, correctly noted. `σ_2 = −∞`: statement vacuous. `σ_2 = +∞`: the proof
  runs for every real `σ`, fine. `g` merely measurable: fine, no continuity used.

**Verdict: Lemma 2.4 STANDS, `PROVED`, and is the one piece of genuinely new (if elementary) mathematics
in the round.** It is worth keeping on its own merits. *Attack that remains:* it is not needed for
anything the corpus wants, because Theorem 26.4 reaches the same destination at every `k` by taking
`2k`-th roots pointwise; Lemma 2.4 is needed only inside the abscissa reformulation, which §4 shows buys
nothing.

## §3. T6(iv): theorem or slogan?

**Exactly what is proved.**

* **(A)** For every `𝔉` of Definition 2.1: `σ_c(k) ≥ 2kσ_2(w)`, hence `≥ 2kβ_max` after quantifying over
  `θ`. Proof: `M_{2k} ≥ w_0\|S_w\|^{2k}` + monotonicity + Lemma 2.4 + Lemma 2.2. **`PROVED`.** This is a
  theorem, and it is the whole of the `Z` side.
* **(B)** The `F_q` side: `IMPORTED`, and **misstated**. (i) The sheaf `F^{⊗k}⊗\bar F^{⊗k}` has weight
  `2kw`, so `H^2_c` has weight `2kw+2` and its inverse roots have modulus `q^{kw+1}` — R151_001 writes
  "weight `kw+2`… modulus `q^{kw/2+1}`", and then concludes `\|α_x\|^{2k} ≤ q^{kw/2+1}`, which on letting
  `k → ∞` gives `\|α_x\| ≤ q^{w/4}`, **not** `q^{w/2}`. The blueprint's "`kw+1`" was the correct order of
  magnitude. (ii) `H^0_c(U) = 0` for `U` open affine, so the `\det(1−FT\mid H^0_c)` factor in the
  denominator is spurious. (iii) The nonnegativity of the coefficients of `\log L` requires the trace
  hypothesis (real traces / the `F ⊗ \bar F` real structure), which is stated nowhere. **REPAIR or
  delete (B); as written it does not prove Weil I's bound.**
* **(C)** "Rationality is the one ingredient of Deligne's argument with no nonnegative-additive
  counterpart over `Z`." **This is a slogan, not a theorem.** There is no definition of "ingredient", no
  enumeration of the ingredients of Weil I, and no proof that the other ingredients (the family sum's
  positivity; Pringsheim–Landau; the `k → ∞` limit; the local-factor convergence) *do* have counterparts
  while rationality alone does not. The `k → ∞` step in particular has no counterpart either: over `Z`
  the exponent `a_k` is not known for a single `k ≥ 2`. What (C) is entitled to say is the conjunction
  (A)+(B) **as an asymmetry of two inequalities**, which is a comparison, not a uniqueness theorem.

**Minimum honest restatement of (iv):** *In any family of Definition 2.1, the distinguished fibre enters
`𝓜_{2k}` as a nonnegative summand and therefore `σ_c(k) ≥ 2kβ_max`; hence no upper estimate of `σ_c(k)`
can be an input independent of the zero bound. Over `F_q`, by rationality, the corresponding object is a
ratio and the distinguished data enter through the numerator `H^1_c`, so the analogous inequality does
not hold.* Label: **`PROVED` (`Z` half), `IMPORTED` (`F_q` half), `INTERPRETATION` (the conjunction)** —
the same downgrade §27 applied to the T5 sterility analogy, which R151_001 correctly took in
Remark 2.9a and then failed to take here.

*Credit where due.* Remark 2.9a's self-downgrade to `ANALOGY`, and the numbering correction (Doc 106
Prop. **3.6**, not 3.9), are exemplary.

## §4. Corollary 3.2: is it new?

**No. The novelty claim must be withdrawn.**

Theorem 26.4 (main.md:10874–10889) proves, from `H(k,c)`, the display
`\|S̃(0;N)\| ≤ N^{1/2+c/k+o(1)}` **at fixed `k`**, and only then lets `k → ∞`. Stopping at fixed `k` and
feeding that display into Corollary 27.10a (main.md:11501, `ψ(x)−x ≪_ε x^{θ+ε} ⟺ β_max ≤ θ`) gives

    β_max ≤ 1/2 + c/k ,

which **is** Corollary 3.2, verbatim, for one weight, with no off-diagonal hypothesis (26.9a), no
Granville, and no abscissa. The derivation is two lines from material already in §§26–27. Therefore:

* the comparison in Remark 3.2a is against the **wrong** corpus item. Corollary 26.19 is not the relevant
  competitor — it is a *different, stronger* statement (RH at fixed `c`) bought with a stronger
  hypothesis. The relevant competitor is **Theorem 26.4 + Corollary 27.10a**, which R151_001's own
  §0 identifies as equal to T6(ii) forward and then forgets when evaluating Corollary 3.2;
* R151_001's §0 item 3, "Corollary 3.2 … the one place where T6 buys something the corpus did not
  already have", is **false**;
* worse, T6's route to Corollary 3.2 is **strictly weaker**: it needs `H(k,c)` for every `w_θ`, `θ ∈ R`
  (Lemma 2.2(c)), whereas the Hölder route needs one weight. A route with a stronger hypothesis and the
  same conclusion is not an improvement;
* Corollary 26.5 already gives RH from `H(k,c)` for all `k` and all `θ` — so even the `θ`-quantified form
  is in the corpus.

**Verdict: `RESTATEMENT`.** It may be *recorded* (the strip at fixed `k` is a useful explicit reading of
Theorem 26.4 that §26 does not print), but as a **corollary of Theorem 26.4**, not of T6, and with the
sentence "no off-diagonal hypothesis, no Granville" attached to Theorem 26.4, which is where it belongs.

**Consequence for the round.** With item 1 (Lemma 2.2 = Cor 26.5 in `L²`) and item 3 (Cor 3.2 =
Thm 26.4 + Cor 27.10a), **three of the four claimed increments of §0 fall.** What survives: (i) the
blueprint correction (the Landau-oscillation route yields only `(2k+1)β_max − 1` — genuinely useful, it
closes a route before it is attempted); (ii) Lemma 2.4; (iii) the reformulation itself, as exposition.

## §5. §4's classification of positivity mechanisms

**"Exhaustiveness `CERTIFIED` only" is honest — and correctly so.** Row 663 of §6 and O-151.2 refuse the
theorem status explicitly and say what a real formulation would need (an axiomatisation comparable to
Definition 27.2). This is the right call and survives audit unchanged.

**But the rows are overstated in two ways.**

1. **"Closed" is carrier-relative, not shape-relative.**
   * Row (β): Theorem 19.25 refutes the existence of an `h^0` satisfying clauses **(a), (b′), (c)** for
     `ξ`; Theorems 24.10/25.6/25.7 extend it to named enlargements (Galois group ring, mixed cone);
     Theorem 21.15 shows the defect is not an artefact of `Spec Z`. Together these close the *Hodge-index
     route as formulated on the declared carrier and its named enlargements*. They do **not** close "any
     positivity of indefinite signature controlled by an index theorem" — that would require the
     axiomatisation §4 admits it does not have. The phrase "closed five times over" is rhetoric: five
     theorems about one carrier family are one closure, not five.
   * Row (γ): Theorem 22.17 (`Spec Z` terminal ⟹ no ring of Frobenius correspondences) is a strong and
     correct no-go for correspondence-degree counting. But "a counting positivity needs a ring in which
     to count" is an unargued premise: `h^0`-type integrality (row (γ)'s own third entry) needs a
     cohomology, not a ring of correspondences, and Lemma 24.1 / Theorem 24.10 are statements about
     *specific* carriers. **REPAIR: restate both rows as "closed on the declared carrier and its named
     enlargements; not closed as a shape".** (This also aligns §4 with the standing record that the
     row-D Hodge-index transfer is conditional on `h^0`.)
2. **The partition is not a partition (the sharpest structural objection).** Spectral positivity
   `⟨Hf,f⟩ = Σ_j λ_j\|⟨f,e_j⟩\|²` with `λ_j ≥ 0` **is** a sum of nonnegative terms; and conversely the
   Weil explicit-formula functional is a *signed* sum. So "additive with nonnegative weights" does not
   separate row (α) from the closed row. What actually separates them is the property T6(iv) is really
   about: **is the object carrying the zeros a nonnegative summand, or does it enter through a
   numerator/cancellation?** Definition 2.1 encodes this by fiat (the distinguished fibre `f_0` with
   `w_{f_0} ≥ w_0 > 0` *and* `S_{f_0} = S_w`), and T6 is then the observation that a hypothesis of that
   exact shape self-refutes. **REPAIR: re-axis the classification on "summand vs numerator", note that
   O-151.3 (signed weights) is then not a crack but the entire other half of the taxonomy, and state
   that (α) survives only because its distinguished object is not a summand of the positive form — which
   needs an argument, since for a Hilbert–Pólya operator the zeros *are* the `λ_j`.**

Row (α)'s paragraph itself (MW-5, MW-4, phases 62/63/66/71, NG-E5 `RETRACTED`) is accurate against the
corpus record, reopens nothing, and proposes nothing. It stands.
