# P123.044 — The induced normal space is an actual open source chart

Status: PROVED for the open-chart identification,
its generic overlap, and the coefficient-level cover sequence. This
does not assert descent of the regularized trace across that sequence.

## 1. Open subsets of the original additive carrier

Fix a nonempty finite set S_f and use the positive real component

    X^+ = R_(>0) times product_(ell in S_f) Q_ell,
    Gamma = product_(ell in S_f) ell^Z.

Gamma acts by diagonal multiplication. For p in S_f define the invariant
open subset

    U_p = {x in X^+ : x_ell != 0 for every ell != p}.

The p-coordinate is unrestricted. Let O be the subset where every
finite coordinate is nonzero, and U=union_p U_p. Thus U consists exactly
of points with at most one zero finite coordinate. For distinct p,q,

    U_p intersection U_q = O.                       (1.1)

In particular these charts do not cover any multiple-zero stratum.

## 2. Exact identification with the associated normal bundle

Let G=H_S, K=j_p(Q_p^*) as in P123.043. For z in Q_p, let b(z) have
real coordinate 1, p-coordinate z and other coordinates 1. The map

    (h,z) -> [h b(z)] in U_p/Gamma

descends to

    G times_K Q_p  ->  U_p/Gamma,                  (2.1)

where (hk,z) is identified with (h,kz), using the identification of K
with Q_p^*. To verify this relation, an unnormalized representative of
j_p(x) is the idele with entry x at p and 1 elsewhere; it sends b(z)
to b(xz). Normalization changes it only by a Gamma multiplier.

The map is onto: the real coordinate and the nonzero coordinates away
from p can be matched by an external idele, leaving a free p-coordinate.
If h b(z) and h' b(z') differ by a Gamma multiplier, equality of every
coordinate away from p says that h^-1 h' belongs to K; the remaining
coordinate gives precisely the relation in (2.1). This proves injectivity.

For the topology, first divide by the subgroup generated by primes
ell!=p to normalize their valuations to zero. Valuations are locally
constant on Q_ell^*, so this is a continuous slice on each open valuation
piece. The residual group is p^Z. In logarithmic real coordinates its
action has translation j log p, together with multiplication by p^j on
the remaining finite coordinates. Real intervals of length less than
log p give local slices for this action. The same slices trivialize
both sides of (2.1), and the displayed map and inverse are continuous
there. Consequently (2.1) is a G-equivariant homeomorphism. Each U_p
has a free proper Gamma action: the first valuation normalization bounds
all exponents except that of p on compact sets, and the real coordinate
bounds the last exponent.

The zero section is the boundary orbit G/K. The complement of the zero
section is

    G times_K Q_p^* ~= G,    [h,x] -> h j_p(x).      (2.2)

Under (2.1) this is O/Gamma. Thus the generic overlap is the same free
external orbit in every chart, rather than a separate generic orbit
for each prime.

P123.043's induced L² representation is exactly the representation on
fiber half-densities of this actual chart, with quotient Haar measure on
G/K and additive Haar in its normal coordinate. The normal half-density
transition under x is D_x, so its equivariance is the one used there.
This identification is stronger than an infinitesimal normal analogy.
It does not identify its regularized trace with a trace on the completed
common correspondence category.

## 3. Explicit coefficient-level overlap sequence upstairs

Choose p_0 in S_f, and write r=|S_f|. Extension by zero from open subsets
gives an exact sequence of Gamma- and external-equivariant vector spaces
(and C_0(U)-modules)

    0 -> C_0(O)^(r-1) --d--> direct_sum_p C_0(U_p)
          --sigma--> C_0(U) -> 0.                 (3.1)

Here sigma sums extensions, and d sends (g_p)_(p!=p0) to components
g_p at p!=p0 and -sum g_p at p0. This is a linear/module sequence;
sigma is not claimed to be a homomorphism from the direct-product
algebra, whose cross-products would be zero.

Injectivity of d is immediate. If sigma(f_p)=0, at a point of U_p\O
only the p-component can be nonzero, so that component must vanish.
Hence f_p is the zero extension of an element of C_0(O). Their sum is
zero, proving exactness in the middle. Surjectivity follows by a finite
partition of unity subordinate to the cover of the locally compact
metrizable space U; multiplying a function in C_0(U) by these bounded
partition functions gives the required C_0(U_p) pieces. The maps commute
with Gamma and the external action because the open subsets are invariant.
No equivariant choice of the partition is asserted.

For r=1 the sequence reduces to the identity. For r>=2 it explicitly
records the r-1 redundant copies of the generic overlap. A relative
shadow must respect this relation before local boundary traces can be
summed as a trace on U. Exactness after the rapid completion of P123.019,
and trace compatibility with that completion, are additional obligations;
they do not follow merely by writing (3.1).

## 4. Why gluing ordinary Hausdorff orbit spaces is insufficient

Even though each U_p/Gamma is Hausdorff, U/Gamma need not be Hausdorff.
Here is an explicit example for S_f={p,q}, p!=q. Choose positive integers
k,l tending to infinity with p^k/q^l bounded above and below (take
l=floor(k log p/log q)). Passing to a subsequence makes this ratio
converge to c>0 and q^l converge in the compact group Z_p^* to v.
Set

    x_k=(1; p^k, 1),    gamma_k=p^-k q^l.

Then x_k converges to x=(1;0,1), while

    gamma_k x_k=(p^-k q^l; q^l, p^-k q^l)
          -> y=(1/c; v,0).

The last coordinate converges to zero in Q_q; p^-k is a q-adic unit.
The two limit points lie in U and have different zero-coordinate sets,
so they are in distinct Gamma orbits. Yet [x_k]=[gamma_k x_k] converges
to both orbit classes. Those two classes cannot have disjoint open
neighborhoods. Thus a global C_0 algebra of a presumed locally compact
Hausdorff orbit space is not a justified replacement for the source.

This is why (3.1) was constructed upstairs, where the original space is
Hausdorff, with the group action retained. It provides concrete overlap
data for equivariant/groupoid descent rather than discarding that data.

## 5. Next load-bearing step

The finite-place normal model now comes from an actual invariant open
chart of the additive carrier. The construction still needs a shadow
compatible with the overlap sequence and a localization triangle for
the closed complement X^+\U. That complement includes all multiple-zero
strata. The real-zero boundary lies outside X^+ and also remains to be
treated in the full source. Neither complement can be removed by the
single-zero calculation.

The integral primitive contact and mixed RR must be transported through
these same source operations. No complete row or global sign conclusion
is asserted here.
