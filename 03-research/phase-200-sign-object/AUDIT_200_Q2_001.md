## 1. Family and FE

**Rederivation.** Q_τ(m,n) = |mτ+n|² has matrix [[|τ|²,x],[x,1]] with determinant y². By Epstein (CITED), Λ(s) = (√det/π)^s Γ(s) Z_τ(s) = (y/π)^sΓ(s)Z_τ(s) satisfies Λ(s) = Λ(1−s). This is the same expression as the memo's (2y/2π)^s ✓.

**Duplication.** One has Γ_ℝ(s)Γ_ℝ(s+1) = 2^{1−s}π^{−s}Γ(s). From this:
- (y/π)^sΓ(s) = ½(2y)^sΓ_ℝ(s)Γ_ℝ(s+1) ✓;
- the analytic conductor is (2y)² = 4y² ✓.

**Classical form.** With E = ½y^sZ_τ, the equation ξ(2s)E(τ,s) = ξ(2−2s)E(τ,1−s) is equivalent ✓.

**Constant (NUMERICAL, mpmath).**
- ψ(¼)+ψ(¾)−2logπ = −7.6027742. The memo writes "−7.60276…": this is a rounding error in the fifth decimal digit and must be corrected to −7.60277.
- log 20 − 7.6027742 = −4.6070277 = C₀^Q of Lemma E ✓. This confirms that the Lemma E template is the same form at τ = i√5.

**Control points.**

- Z_ρ = 6ζL(χ₋₃) with 4y² = 3 ✓.
- Observation: for the non-maximal orders in the h = 1 list (D = −12, −16, −27, −28), Z_{τ_D} is **not** literally w·ζ(s)L(s,χ_D). It is ζ·L(χ_{D₀}) multiplied by a finite Euler factor at p | f. The memo (§4.1) states it without this precision. It must be corrected; it does not change the conclusion.

## 2. L1: continuity in τ

**What is correct.**
- For c ∈ C_c^∞, the comb sum on the window is in fact **finite**. For τ in a compact set, the values Q_τ < e^{2T} are finite, and so are the products of values > 1 below e^{2T}.
- The contour representation with G entire and of rapid decay is correct on a zero-free half-plane σ ≥ σ₁.

**The gap.** The memo says r_τ(1) jumps only at i and ρ. That is false. On the whole arc |τ| = 1 of 𝓕 the vectors (0,1) and (1,0) both have norm 1, so r_τ(1) ≥ 4.

Near the arc, with |τ|² = a = 1+ε, one has Z/2 = 1 + a^{−s} + O(λ₂^{−σ}). The bound |Z/r(1)| ≥ ½ on σ ≥ σ₁ requires 1 − a^{−σ₁} ≳ Cλ₂^{−σ₁}, i.e. σ₁ → ∞ as ε → 0. The uniformity "σ₁ = σ₁(K)" does **not** hold on compacta touching the arc.

**The real phenomenon.** For a = 1+ε, the term log(1+a^{−s}) produces frequencies k·log a → 0 with coefficients (−1)^{k+1}log a. There are ≈ 2T/ε such frequencies within the window. Their contribution Σ_k (−1)^{k+1} log a · c(k log a)a^{−k/2} is an alternating Riemann sum tending to 0, the value at a = 1. This suggests continuity does hold, but the memo's proof does not cover it: the sum is no longer "uniformly finite", and dominated convergence via contour fails.

**Labels for L1.**
- **PROVED** on compacta of {τ ∈ 𝓕 : |τ| > 1} (interior) and for F ∈ C_c^∞.
- **ARGUED** on the arc (and at i, ρ) and in the extension to P_T ⊂ L². This extension requires bounding the form in L², which was not done.

The closure of S_T then holds only in that interior. That is enough for everything used afterward, except the neighborhood of τ = i and τ = ρ, which happen to be two of the τ_D. That case is the relevant one.

## 3. L2: cusp lemma

**Verification.** Write Z = 2ζ(2s) + R(s), where R is the part with m ≠ 0, with values ≥ m²y² ≥ y². Then

−Z′/Z = −(ζ(2s))′/ζ(2s) − (d/ds) log(1 + R/(2ζ(2s))).

The series R/(2ζ(2s)) has frequencies of the form (value ≥ y²)·(n²) ≥ y². Its powers in log(1+X) likewise have frequencies ≥ y². Hence, in the window log μ < 2T < 2log y, only the coefficients of −(d/ds)log ζ(2s) = Σ 2Λ(n)n^{−2s} remain ✓.

**The memo's coefficient.**
- Each coefficient is 2Λ_τ(n²)/n = 4Λ(n)/n ✓.
- The Γ factor matches the duplication of §1 ✓.
- The explicit formula applies because Z_τ has an FE and poles only at 0 and 1 (Weil, CITED; the contour-shift proof does not use an Euler product).

**Label for the identity: PROVED.**

**Positivity.** One must use the following three facts:
- A(F) ≥ 0, because |c(u)| ≤ c(0) ✓;
- |c| ≤ ‖F‖² ✓;
- Mertens Σ_{n≤e^T}Λ(n)/n = T + O(1), with explicit Rosser–Schoenfeld constants ✓.

For the polar term, the sign must be fixed in P47's convention. In the Weil form the poles enter with favorable sign, and on P_T they vanish by primitivity. It must be written out, not left as "O(1)".

**Label for the containment S_T ⊇ {y ≥ e^{2T+c₁}}: PROVED**, except for pinning down c₁. This is routine and explicit with the Rosser–Schoenfeld constants; Mori is not needed.

**Comment.** The cusp's positivity does **not** use any sign property of the ζ(2s) form. It follows from log(4y²) dominating 4T. The question of whether this "is REF-D or an already certified case" turns out empty: it is neither, it is trivial by conductor.

**What reduces on the window.** With fixed conductor, the restricted form is the window form of ζ(2s). A change of variable u = 2v carries it to that of ζ on window T (frequencies log n on window [0,T]) with a modified archimedean constant. The memo's NO-GO, "no sign is transported from ζ", is correct: as y → ∞ the constant diverges and the problem becomes trivial.

**Confirmation of §5 as a RETRY.** The index (README l.203–208, Front A) already records "Maass–Selberg = window form of ζ(2s) on the squares". The memo correctly declares this and does not retry it. The additional reason (1), the nonlinearity in E, is correct: 𝔞 goes through −Z′/Z.

## 4. Proposition 4.1: citations (verified on the web)

**Bateman–Grosswald (Acta Arith. 9, 1964). The memo's value is incorrect.**
- BG's criterion has main term y* = 4πe^{−γ} = 7.0555…
- E(z,s), with z = x+iy reduced, always has a real zero in (½,1) if y ≥ 7.0556, and never has one if y ≤ 7.0554. Between the two values there is a transition strip depending on x.
- The memo cites "7.0236". That number does not appear in the sources consulted. It must be replaced by 7.0556.
- Conclusion with the corrected citation: S_∞ ∩ {y ≥ 7.0556} = ∅ (CONDITIONAL on 4.2). The margin against √163/2 = 6.3836 remains positive.
- Sources: arXiv 2110.09368; arXiv math/0412039 (Lagarias–Suzuki).

**Lagarias–Suzuki (JNT 118, 2006).**
- What they prove: "RH" for the constant term a_y(s) = ζ*(2s)y^s + ζ*(2s−1)y^{1−s} for y ≥ 1. All zeros lie on the line if y ≤ y* = 4πe^{−γ}; for y > y* there are exactly two real zeros off it.
- y* is also the unique y with F′(y,½) = 0.
- The memo cites it correctly in substance (CITED ✓).

**Davenport–Heilbronn (JLMS 11, 1936). Error.**

The memo attributes to DH II and to Cassels results about **Epstein** x² + ty² with t transcendental or algebraic. No support was found and it should be withdrawn.

What is reasonable to expect: for τ with (1, 2x, |τ|²) ℚ-linearly independent and transcendental, a DH/Kronecker-type argument gives zeros for σ > 1. But that is ARGUED, not CITED.

**Consequences.**

  S_∞ ∩ 𝓕 ∩ {τ CM} ⊆ {τ_D : h = 1}.

  This is CONDITIONAL on 4.2. Nothing is said about non-CM τ with y < 7.0556.

**Heegner–Baker–Stark.** There are 9 fundamental discriminants with h = 1, and 13 counting orders ✓ (CITED). Heilbronn: h(D) → ∞ ✓.

**Step 4.2: off-line zero ⇒ negativity.**
- For the full form on compact-support tests, this is the standard direction of Weil's criterion for a function with an FE (CITED: Weil 1952; holds without an Euler product).

## 5. C-τ

**The density is correct.** The τ = (−b+√D)/2a are dense in ℍ, and only 13 D have h = 1, so the CM τ with h > 1 are dense ✓.

**Prop 6.1 is weaker than it appears.** The hypothesis "B continuous in τ and inf_{U,T} B ≥ 0" already implies 𝔞_T^{(τ₁)} ≥ 0 for every T at a τ₁ with h > 1. That contradicts 4.2 at that single point. The continuity of B is not used. Nor is it necessary that the neighborhood be of a τ_D. The precise statement is:

> **C-τ (reformulated; CONDITIONAL on 4.2 + DH-I).** S_∞ ∩ 𝓕 has empty interior. Moreover, S_∞ ∩ {τ CM} ⊆ {τ_D : h = 1}. Hence no method giving positivity for every T simultaneously on an open set of τ can be correct. A method proving positivity at τ_D must use some property that is not stable in any neighborhood of τ_D: integrality or an Euler product, not the FE, the Γ factor, or the conductor.

**What was not proved.** "T*(τ_D) = ∞ and T* → ∞ near τ_D" (Cor. 6.2).
- It assumes positivity at τ_D, which is OPEN.
- It also requires a quantitative link between the height of the zero and the detecting T, which does not exist in the corpus.
- **Label for Cor. 6.2: ARGUED / CONDITIONAL.**

**The uniformity of T(τ) in question.** Prop 4.1 gives negativity at some T(τ), not at a uniform T₀, and T(τ) can diverge near τ_D. Hence one cannot claim "S_T is never a neighborhood" for a fixed T. In fact L2 shows the opposite: for fixed T, S_T contains a neighborhood of the cusp. The correct version is the empty-interior statement about S_∞, not one about a given S_T.
