## §R1 — R-1: the collapse, proved without decay and without the Hilbert–Schmidt clause

### R1.1 What is withdrawn

> **WITHDRAWN (A-1).** Round 1's Lemma D.56.3 read: *"for every `φ, ψ : R → R` continuous with
> `φ(u) → 0` and `ψ(u) → 0` as `|u| → ∞` — in particular for every `φ, ψ ∈ D^W`, **and more generally for
> every pair for which the `(R)×(R)` block of `B` is finite** — `(φ ⊛ ψ)(w) ≥ 0`."* The clause in bold is
> **false**: `φ ∈ L²(R)` does not imply `φ(u) → 0` (the auditor's witness: `φ = n` on intervals of width
> `n^{−3}` about `u = n`, which is in `L²` and unbounded). Every sentence of round 1 asserting that the
> collapse is *structural for the whole domain of `B`* because the `(R)×(R)` block is Hilbert–Schmidt is
> withdrawn with it: **D.56.4's closing remark**, the third sentence of **§D.58**, and the first sentence
> of the **Recommendation**. The statement of Lemma D.56.3 restricted to `D^W` (where (11.1) gives the
> decay) is correct and Theorem D.56.4 was never damaged.

### R1.2 The replacement

**Lemma D.56.3′ (`PROVED`, unconditional; the auditor's, adopted verbatim in content).**
Let `φ, ψ : R → R` be measurable and `w ∈ R`; write `p_w(u) := φ(u)ψ(u+w)`.

 (i) *(nonnegative factors: no hypothesis at all)* If `φ ≥ 0` and `ψ ≥ 0` then `(φ⊛ψ)(w) ≥ 0`.
 (ii) *(signed factors: the escape costs the weaker block)* `(φ⊛ψ)(w) < 0` holds **iff** there is
      `ε > 0` with `p_w(u) ≤ −ε` for **every** `u ∈ R`. In that case `|p_w(u)| ≥ ε` for every `u`, so
      `p_w ∉ L¹(R)` and the cross-correlation of Lemma D.55.4 is `χ_{φ,ψ}(w) = −∞`.
 (iii) *(sufficient conditions)* If `p_w ∈ L¹(R)` then `(φ⊛ψ)(w) ≥ 0`. In particular this holds when
      `φ, ψ ∈ L²(R)` (Cauchy–Schwarz gives `p_w ∈ L¹`), and when `φ, ψ` are continuous with
      `p_w(u) → 0` along some sequence `|u| → ∞`.

*Proof.* (i) A supremum of nonnegative numbers is nonnegative. (ii) `\sup_u p_w(u) = −ε < 0` is by
definition `p_w(u) ≤ −ε` for every `u`; then `∫|p_w| ≥ ∫ε\,du = +∞` and `∫p_w = −∞`. (iii) If
`p_w ≤ −ε < 0` everywhere then `∫|p_w| = +∞`, so `p_w ∈ L¹` forbids it and (ii) gives `(φ⊛ψ)(w) ≥ 0`;
`φ, ψ ∈ L²` gives `‖p_w‖_1 ≤ ‖φ‖_2‖ψ‖_2 < ∞`; and along a sequence with `p_w → 0`,
`\sup_u p_w ≥ \lim p_w(u_n) = 0`. ∎ `PROVED`

> **Why this is strictly stronger, and why it answers the loophole.** Round 1 made the collapse a fact
> about the `(R)×(R)` block (decay of the factors). D.56.3′ makes it a fact about the **`(T)×(R)` block**
> — the *weaker* of the two, the one that can stay finite while `(R)×(R)` diverges. The auditor's own
> arithmetic on that loophole is recorded and is correct: with `ψ(w) ≍ e^{|w|/2}` mimicking `w_{2K}` and
> `φ` decaying faster than `e^{−|u|/2}`, `χ_{φ,ψ}(w) = ∫φ(u)ψ(u+w)du` is finite and `O(e^{|w|/2})`,
> integrable against `Φ = O(e^{−(1+ε)|w|})` by (11.1) — so `(T)×(R)` is finite while `(R)×(R)` is `+∞`.
> **The loophole between the two blocks is real, and it is empty as a route**, because for nonnegative
> data the collapse is (i), a supremum of nonnegative numbers, on which no growth condition bears.

### R1.3 The continuity plug, and the exact price of any escape

**Lemma D.56.3″ (`PROVED`).** Let `Φ ∈ D^W` (so `Φ ∈ C²(R)`, Def. 11.1) and let `φ, ψ` be such that
`Φ ≥ φ⊛ψ` pointwise. Put `W⁻ := \{w ∈ R : (φ⊛ψ)(w) < 0\}`. If `Φ(w_0) < 0` for some `w_0`, then by
continuity `Φ < 0` on a nonempty open interval `I ∋ w_0`, and `I ⊆ W⁻`. Hence:

**an enlargement of the translation shadow beyond `𝓔 ∪ \{0\}` requires `(φ⊛ψ) < 0` on a nonempty open
set.** A null set, or any set with empty interior, of "bad" `w` cannot enlarge the shadow. ∎ `PROVED`

**Corollary D.56.3‴ (R-1a; `PROVED`).** `𝓣(M) ⊋ 𝓔 ∪ \{0\}` is possible **only** for pairs `(φ,ψ)` for
which `χ_{φ,ψ} ≡ −∞` on a nonempty open set `W⁻`, hence for which `B(k_Φ, φ⊗ψ) = −∞` for every `Φ ∈ D^W`
that is `> 0` somewhere on `W⁻` — i.e. for every `Φ` in a nonempty open cone of translation tests.
**Therefore Theorem D.56.4's conclusion holds on the entire domain of definition of `B`**, and not merely
on `D^W × D^W`. There is **no intermediate carrier** between "shadow `= 𝓔`" and "`B` undefined against an
open set of `(T)`-tests".

*Proof.* Lemma D.56.3″ gives the open set `W⁻`; Lemma D.56.3′(ii) gives `χ_{φ,ψ} = −∞` on it; Lemma
D.55.4 then gives `B(k_Φ, φ⊗ψ) = ∫Φχ_{φ,ψ} = −∞` whenever `Φ > 0` on a subset of `W⁻` of positive
measure. ∎ `PROVED`

## §R2 — R-2: finite rank

`(R)` is defined in D.55.1 as the **finite real span** of rank-one kernels. Round 1 stated D.56.2–D.56.4
for a single `φ⊗ψ`. The gap is real; here are the finite-rank statements.

**Definition D.56.1′.** For `φ_1,…,φ_n, ψ_1,…,ψ_n : R → R` and `w ∈ R` put
`q_w(u) := Σ_{i=1}^n φ_i(u)ψ_i(u+w)` and `(⊛)(w) := \sup_{u∈R} q_w(u)` — the tropical correlation of the
finite-rank kernel `P := Σ_iφ_i⊗ψ_i`, written `(⊛P)(w)`. For `n = 1` this is `φ⊛ψ`.

**Theorem D.56.2′ (characterisation, finite rank; `PROVED`).** For `Φ ∈ D^W` and `P = Σ_{i≤n}φ_i⊗ψ_i`,

    k_Φ − P ≥ 0 \text{ on } X \iff Φ(w) ≥ (⊛P)(w) \text{ for every } w ∈ R .

*Proof.* Identical to D.56.2: `Φ(v−u) ≥ Σ_iφ_i(u)ψ_i(v)` for all `(u,v)`, substitute `v = u+w`, take the
supremum in `u` at fixed `w`. The order of quantifiers is all that is used, and `n` plays no role. ∎
`PROVED`

**Lemma D.56.3⁗ (nonnegativity of the finite-rank tropical correlation; `PROVED`).** Fix `w`.

 (i) If `q_w ∈ L¹(R)` then `(⊛P)(w) ≥ 0`. **In particular this holds whenever the `(T)×(R)` block of
     `B` is finite at `w` on every generator**: if `φ_i(·)ψ_i(·+w) ∈ L¹` for each `i`, then `q_w ∈ L¹`
     by the triangle inequality.
 (ii) If every `φ_i, ψ_i ∈ D^W` then `q_w(u) → 0` as `u → +∞` by (11.1), hence `(⊛P)(w) ≥ 0`.
 (iii) Conversely, if `(⊛P)(w) < 0` then `q_w ≤ −ε < 0` everywhere, so `q_w ∉ L¹`, so
      `Σ_{i}χ_{φ_i,ψ_i}(w)` is **not** a sum of finite numbers: at least one generator has
      `χ_{φ_i,ψ_i}(w) = ±∞`, i.e. the `(T)×(R)` block of `B` is undefined on `(R)` at `w`.

*Proof.* (i),(iii) are Lemma D.56.3′(ii),(iii) applied to `q_w` in place of `p_w`, plus linearity of
`∫`: if every `∫φ_iψ_i(·+w)` were finite then `∫q_w` would be finite, contradicting `∫q_w = −∞`.
(ii) is (11.1) with `j = 0` for each of the `2n` factors. ∎ `PROVED`

> **Note on (i) versus (i) of D.56.3′.** At finite rank the *sign* hypothesis is no longer available:
> even with all `φ_i, ψ_i ≥ 0` the sum `q_w` is nonnegative — so (i) of D.56.3′ **does** extend, for
> nonnegative generators, with no hypothesis — but a general element of `(R)` has real coefficients of
> both signs, and then only integrability (or `D^W`-decay) is available. Both are recorded above, and
> D.56.3″/D.56.3‴ apply verbatim with `φ⊛ψ` replaced by `⊛P`.

**Theorem D.56.4″ (THE KILL, finite rank; `PROVED`).** Let `k = k_Φ − P` with `P ∈ (R)` and `k ≥ 0`, and
suppose the `(T)×(R)` block of `B` is finite on `P` at almost every `w` (the minimum for `B` to be defined
on `k`). Then `Φ ≥ 0` pointwise, and

    𝓣(M) := \{Φ ∈ D^W : ∃\,P ∈ (R),\ k_Φ − P ∈ (M)\} \;=\; 𝓔 ∪ \{0\} .

*Proof.* `⊆`: by D.56.2′, `Φ ≥ ⊛P`; by D.56.3⁗(i) `(⊛P)(w) ≥ 0` at every `w` where the block is finite,
so `Φ ≥ 0` off a null set, and `Φ ∈ C²` gives `Φ ≥ 0` everywhere (Lemma D.56.3″). `⊇`: take `P = 0`. ∎
`PROVED`

> **On the `⊇` direction (audit §3, first bullet).** It takes `P = 0`, i.e. **it does not use the square
> at all**; `𝓣(M) ⊇ 𝓔` is content-free and is stated only to make the equality exact. All the content is
> in `⊆`. Recorded as the auditor asks.

## §R3 — R-3: the `+` quadrant is closed, and O-148.3 is withdrawn as an open item

> **WITHDRAWN (R-3).** Round 1's O-148.3 read: *"the `k_Φ + φ⊗ψ ≥ 0` quadrant … the one direction
> Theorem D.56.4 does not close … was not pursued because … the enlarged `Φ` would then fail Theorem
> 11.13(i) (`\deg Φ > 0` for effective `Φ`), so the normalization in Definition D.56.5 breaks and there
> is no support function to compare. **Recorded as the only unexamined quadrant of the square, and
> flagged as probably ill-posed.** `OPEN`."* **Both halves are withdrawn.** The reason given is a
> non-argument — Definition D.56.5 already carries `\deg Φ > 0` as a side condition, and a cone some of
> whose elements have `\deg ≤ 0` is one you restrict, not one that is ill-posed. And the quadrant is not
> a separate direction at all: `k_Φ + φ⊗ψ = k_Φ − φ⊗(−ψ)`, so it is the `−` quadrant with signed data,
> already governed by D.56.3′(ii). Leaving it flagged as "the one direction the collapse does not close"
> in a closing document is an invitation to reopen the square on a false premise, and is the single
> sentence of round 1 the audit calls most dangerous. It is deleted.

**Theorem D.56.4′ (both signs; `PROVED`; the auditor's, with the finite-rank extension of §R2).**
Let `P ∈ (R)` and `Φ ∈ D^W`, and suppose the `(T)×(R)` block of `B` is finite on `P` at almost every `w`.
Then

    k_Φ − P ≥ 0 \ \ \text{or} \ \ k_Φ + P ≥ 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad Φ ≥ 0 .

The translation shadow of the **full quadrant, both signs, at every finite rank**, is `𝓔 ∪ \{0\}`.

*Proof.* `k_Φ + P = k_Φ − (−P)` and `−P ∈ (R)`, with `χ_{−φ_i,ψ_i} = −χ_{φ_i,ψ_i}` finite exactly where
`χ_{φ_i,ψ_i}` is. So both cases are Theorem D.56.4″. Explicitly, in the rank-one case:
`k_Φ + φ⊗ψ ≥ 0 ⟺ Φ(w) ≥ −\inf_u φ(u)ψ(u+w)`, and `\inf_u φ(u)ψ(u+w) > 0` at a given `w` means
`φ(u)ψ(u+w) ≥ ε > 0` for **every** `u`, whence `χ_{φ,ψ}(w) = +∞`; while if the infimum is `≤ 0` the
constraint reads `Φ(w) ≥ −\inf ≥ 0`. ∎ `PROVED`

**O-148.3: `CLOSED` (audit D.56.4′).** No residue.

## §R4 — R-6 / A-3: D.55.5's title withdrawn; the gluing recorded as a choice

> **WITHDRAWN (A-3).** The title of round 1's Theorem D.55.5, *"the three blocks are the three

regularisation in the only sense available (they never meet), and in no stronger sense.** `PROVED`

## §R5 — R-4: the "density anomaly" renamed

> **RENAMED (R-4).** Round 1's Theorem D.55.6 was titled *"the density anomaly"*, and the round-1 text
> asserted both that `B` is well defined and that it has an anomaly — a form of words that reads as a
> self-contradiction and will be quoted as one. **There is no inconsistency and `B` is a single
> well-defined bilinear form.** The two displays of (148.1) pair *different arguments* (`Ψ ∈ D^W` in the
> first, `χ_{φ,ψ} ∈ D^W` in the second) through *different representations*; they could conflict only if
> some `k` admitted both readings, and Theorem D.55.2 says none does.

**Theorem D.55.6′ (non-existence of a common radial representation; `PROVED`, content unchanged).**
There is **no** linear map `R : 𝒦 → D^W` with `B(k, k_Φ) = ∫ R(k)(w)\,w_Φ(w)\,dw` for every `k ∈ 𝒦` and
every `Φ ∈ D^W`. Equivalently: `B(·,k_Φ)` is represented on `(T)` by the Weil density `w_Φ` and on `(R)`
by `Φ` itself, and these differ — at `Φ = 2K`, `w_{2K}(w) = 2\cosh(w/2)` is unbounded and increasing in
`|w|` while `2K` is positive and decays faster than every exponential (Thm 11.7(ii)).

*Proof.* Unchanged from round 1's D.55.6: the first display is Thm 11.14(i), the second is Lemma D.55.4,
and `w_{2K} = 2\cosh(w/2)` follows from (11.16) with `Φ_{2K}(0) = Φ_{2K}(1) = 1` and
`Φ_{2K}(1−ρ) = 2ξ(1−ρ) = 0`. A common `R` would force `w_Φ = Φ` for the representation to agree on both
summands, which fails at `Φ = 2K`. ∎ `PROVED`
