# Phase 180 — Kadison equality and oriented Euler--Poisson interference

## 0. Verdict

The multiplicative-domain idea gives a genuine equality-rigidity theorem, but
it does not produce the missing first-contact equality.  For the natural
random-unitary Poisson channel, Kadison equality says exactly that the tested
operator is fixed by every relative translation.  In the multiplicative
translation representation this is automatic and carries no Euler--Poisson
information.  In the affine additive representation it is incompatible with

Kadison defect is zero.  Status: `PROVED NO-GO` for this mechanism.

## 1. Exact Kadison defect

Let `alpha_j` be star-automorphisms of a unital C-star algebra and let
`pi_j>0`, `sum_j pi_j=1`.  Define

\[
 \Phi(X)=\sum_j\pi_j\alpha_j(X).                         \tag{1.1}
\]

### Theorem 1 (pairwise form of the Kadison defect). `PROVED`

For every `X`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi(X^*X)-\Phi(X)^*\Phi(X)
 =\frac12\sum_{j,k}\pi_j\pi_k
   (\alpha_j(X)-\alpha_k(X))^*
   (\alpha_j(X)-\alpha_k(X)).}                           \tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently the defect is positive.  It vanishes if and only if

\[
 \alpha_j(X)=\alpha_k(X)\quad\text{for every }j,k        \tag{1.3}
\]

in any faithful representation.

**Proof.**  Expanding the right side of (1.2), the two diagonal sums give
`Phi(X^*X)` because the probabilities sum to one.  The two mixed sums give
`-Phi(X)^*Phi(X)`.  A sum of positive operators with strictly positive
coefficients is zero precisely when every squared difference is zero. `QED`

For a random-unitary channel

\[
 \alpha_j(X)=U^jXU^{-j},                                 \tag{1.4}
\]

(1.3) is equivalent to `X` commuting with every `U^{j-k}` occurring in the
support of the difference distribution.  If that support generates the
integer group, the multiplicative domain is the commutant of `U`.

## 2. Application to the two natural carriers

### 2.1 Logarithmic translations

In D.195 the channel is generated by `U_p=S_{log p}`.  All logarithmic
translations commute.  If `X` is another translation, in particular a prime
translation or any function of the translation generator, then

\[
 U_p^jXU_p^{-j}=X                                        \tag{2.1}
\]

and the Kadison defect is identically zero.  Equality therefore holds before
using `Lambda*1=log`, the Gamma place, the Tate moments or first-contact
minimality.  It cannot force the physical boundary class to vanish.

### 2.2 Additive--multiplicative affine carrier

On the integer carrier of P122.024 the isometries satisfy

\[
 V_pS=S^pV_p.                                            \tag{2.2}

Thus `V_p` is not in the commutant of the additive successor.  Whenever a
unital dilation turns the successor into a unitary `U` while retaining the
covariance corresponding to (2.2), Theorem 1 says that Kadison equality for
`V_p` requires

\[
 U^jV_pU^{-j}=U^kV_pU^{-k}\quad(j,k\text{ in the support}). \tag{2.3}
\]

For two consecutive indices this gives `UV_p=V_pU`, contrary to the affine
law unless the representation collapses the distinction between `U` and
`U^p` on the range of `V_p`.  In the faithful integer representation that
collapse is impossible: on basis vectors the two sides land at distinct
integers.

Hence the two canonical choices give the exact dichotomy

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{channel}&\text{Kadison equality}&\text{content}\\ \hline
 \text{logarithmic translation}&\text{automatic}&\text{no additive coupling}\\
 \text{additive successor}&\text{fails for }V_p&\text{detects affine noncommutation}.
 \end{array}                                             \tag{2.4}
\]

## 3. Why first contact does not bridge the dichotomy

For a vector state `omega_F`, (1.2) gives

\[
 \omega_F(K_\Phi(X))
 =\frac12\sum_{j,k}\pi_j\pi_k
   \|(\alpha_j(X)-\alpha_k(X))F\|^2\ge0.                \tag{3.1}
\]

The first-contact data are

\[
 H_T\ge0,\qquad H_TF=0,                                  \tag{3.2}

with the two Tate moment constraints.  Neither (3.2) nor
`Lambda*1=log` identifies `H_T` with the Kadison defect (3.1).  D.195 computes
the exact relation in the logarithmic channel: the physical arithmetic term
is a scalar load minus the positive Markov defect, and the missing assertion
is the spectral-gap inequality

\[
 \mathcal E_P\ge A_PI                                    \tag{3.3}

\]

on the primitive space.  Equation (3.3) is row D, not a consequence of
complete positivity.

In the additive carrier, setting (3.1) to zero would indeed impose strong
affine rigidity, but no established first-contact identity makes that defect
zero.  Adding it as a nonnegative term and declaring equality repeats the
common-radical assumption of Phase 179.

## 4. Relation to Phase 178

Kadison's defect is a full covariance square.  It avoids the indefinite
connected cumulant of Phase 178, but its equality set forgets the oriented
coefficient: (1.2) depends on squared differences and is invariant under
reversing the affine commutator.  The oriented relation

\[
 [H,V_p]=(\log p)V_p                                     \tag{4.1}
\]

can label the size of a difference, but Kadison positivity cannot choose its
linear sign.  Thus this route lands on the positive/unoriented side of the
dichotomy proved in R178.
