## 0. Conventions (self-contained)

$\zeta$ = Riemann zeta. An **off quadruple** is a zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$, $\gamma>0$, together with its three mirrors $1-\beta+i\gamma$, $\beta-i\gamma$, $1-\beta-i\gamma$ (functional equation + conjugation). **Off-height** $b:=\beta-\tfrac12>0$; ordinate $\gamma$. $m$ := number of quadruples; RH ⟺ $m=0$. If $m=\infty$, we enumerate the quadruples $(b_j,\gamma_j)_{j\ge1}$ with $\gamma_j$ non-decreasing (finite per height, so $\gamma_j\to\infty$).

Heat flow (Polymath 15 chart $H_t$, normalization of Doc 177 §0): $\partial_tH_t=-\partial_z^2H_t$, $H_0\leftrightarrow\xi$; zeros of ζ at height $\gamma$ ↔ zeros of $H_0$ near $z\approx\gamma$ (linear rescaling); off-height $b$ ↔ imaginary part $\beta_H=2b$ modulo the scale invariance of Doc 177 §0 (the laws $\beta^2=c\,t$ are chart invariants: we do not convert constants). $\Lambda$ = De Bruijn–Newman constant ($H_t$ has all real zeros ⟺ $t\ge\Lambda$; RH ⟺ $\Lambda\le0$; Rodgers–Tao: $\Lambda\ge0$). **Landing time** $t_j>0$ of quadruple $j$: the forward time at which its zeros become real (exists and is $\le\Lambda$ if $m\ge1$; local finiteness of landings by [KKL]). **Lifespan** $\ell_j\le t_j$ (Doc 174). Local density $\rho(\gamma)\asymp\frac1{4\pi}\log\gamma$ (RvM); **diffusive scale** at height $\gamma$: $t_{\rm dif}(\gamma):=(\text{mean spacing})^2\asymp(\log\gamma)^{-2}$ — the time heat needs for a zero to "feel" its neighbors.

**LP-134** (canonical form, Def. 141.1): $\displaystyle\liminf_{\rho\ \rm off}\ b_\rho\log\gamma_\rho>0$ (convention $+\infty$ if the off zeros are finite). Vacuously true under RH; its entire content is the failure mode of ¬RH with $m=\infty$.

## 1. The minimal form: LP-134∞, and Cor 177.8 rewritten

### 1.1. What Cor 177.8 actually uses

The proof of [Cor 177.8] is: if $m=\infty$, [Thm 177.7(2)] (conditional on 177.B) gives $b_j\log\gamma_j\le(\log\gamma_j)^{-\eta}\to0$ **along the entire sequence**; LP-134 gives $b_j\log\gamma_j\ge c>0$ for all large $j$: contradiction. But the contradiction does not need "for all large $j$": it suffices that **a single** $j$ violate the conclusion of 177.7(2) beyond any threshold — i.e. it suffices that the conclusion "$b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$" be false. This identifies the exact minimal form:

**[NEW-DEFINITION 179.1] (the ladder of weakenings).** For the sequence of quadruples of a configuration with $m=\infty$ (statements vacuously true if $m<\infty$):

- **LP-134** (Def. 141.1): $\exists c>0:\ b_j\log\gamma_j\ge c$ for all but finitely many $j$.
- **LP-134-d1** (density 1): $\exists c>0:\ \#\{j\le J:\ b_j\log\gamma_j\ge c\}/J\to1$.
- **LP-134∞** (the minimal form): $\exists c>0:\ b_j\log\gamma_j\ge c$ for **infinitely many** $j$. Equivalently, its negation is: $b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$ along the entire sequence.

**[PROP 179.2] (strict hierarchy and sufficiency of the minimal form).**
1. $\mathrm{RH}\Rightarrow m<\infty\Rightarrow\mathrm{LP\text{-}134}\Rightarrow\mathrm{LP\text{-}134\text{-}d1}\Rightarrow\mathrm{LP\text{-}134^\infty}$, and no arrow reverses within the class of admissible configurations (entire, order 1, functional equation, real on the line, ζ-type counting).
2. **Cor 177.8 holds with the minimal form:** Lemma 177.B $\wedge$ LP-134∞ $\Longrightarrow m<\infty$.

*Proof.* (1) The first two arrows are Prop. 141.P1. LP-134 ⟹ d1 ⟹ ∞: immediate from the definitions (cofinite ⟹ density 1 ⟹ infinitely many). Non-reversal, via configurations in the class (all variants of the Hadamard counterexample 141.R2, whose construction admits any sequence $(b_j,\gamma_j)$ with controlled $\sum b_j$ and $\gamma_j=2^j$, keeping order 1, functional equation, reality and counting):
(a) ∞ $\not\Rightarrow$ d1: interleave — for $j$ in a sequence of density zero (e.g. $j=2^k$) take $b_j=\tfrac14$ ("fat"), for the rest $b_j=e^{-\gamma_j}$. Then $b_j\log\gamma_j\ge\tfrac14\log2$ for infinitely many $j$ (LP-134∞ ✓) but the proportion of $j\le J$ with $b_j\log\gamma_j\ge c$ tends to $0$ for every $c>0$ (d1 ✗).
(b) d1 $\not\Rightarrow$ LP-134: the same with roles reversed (thin at $j=2^k$, fat elsewhere): density-1 fat ✓, $\liminf=0$ ✗.
(c) LP-134 $\not\Rightarrow m<\infty$: $b_j\equiv\tfrac14$ (Prop. 141.P1(b)).
(2) The same proof as Cor 177.8: if $m=\infty$, 177.7(2) gives $b_j\log\gamma_j\le(\log\gamma_j)^{-\eta}$ for **all** large $j$, hence $b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$; LP-134∞ is exactly the negation of that convergence. $\square$

**Reading.** Direction (c) of the Doc 177 verdict asked for "LP-134 in density 1"; the answer is that one can go lower: **it suffices to deny convergence to zero**. LP-134∞ is the weakest arithmetic statement that closes the second tower with 177.B, and it is strictly weaker than LP-134 and than d1 (Prop 179.2(1)). It is also, by construction, the weakest individual statement in the whole program to date: weaker than LP-134, which was already (Obs. 141-1.2) weaker than $m<\infty$. The new architecture of the second tower is:
$$m<\infty\;\Longleftarrow\;\text{[Lemma 177.B]}\ \wedge\ \mathrm{LP\text{-}134^\infty}.$$

**Immediate honesty warning.** Weaker does not mean more accessible: no-go 141.B2 (no countable family of polynomial-resolution data separates LP-134 from its negation) also covers LP-134∞ — the diagonal configuration $Z_\varepsilon$ of Cor. 141.B2 violates LP-134$^{(\psi)}$ for every sub-exponential scale **along the entire sequence** ($\delta_j\psi(\gamma_j)\le1/j\to0$), i.e. it violates LP-134∞, and is $\varepsilon$-indistinguishable from the RH world on all such data. The descent from LP-134 to LP-134∞ does not change sides of the wall; it changes (as seen in §2) **the dynamic geometry** of the statement.

### 1.2. What the negation says, plainly

$\neg$LP-134∞ (given $m=\infty$): $b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$ — **all** quadruples, except finitely many, live at depth $o(1/\log\gamma_j)$ from the axis: sub-resolution with respect to the mean spacing, with no asymptotic exception. It is the "RH barely false, uniformly barely" failure mode: exactly the stratum that the Rodgers–Tao heuristic ("$\Lambda\ge0$: RH, if true, is barely true") flags as the natural failure mode, and exactly the stratum where all σ-sensitive technology dies (§2.1). This must be said without gloss: **plausibility lies on the side of the negation**, and the only structural evidence in favor of LP-134 remains Euler's continuation rigidity (Conj. 141.E).

## 2. Route 1: density and detection of zeros; the pivot to Lehmer and the diffusive reformulation

### 2.1. Frank confirmation: the density industry is exactly blind

We do not re-derive; we consolidate what is certified. (i) [Lemma 141.M1]: density theorems ($N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{1-\frac14(\sigma-1/2)}\log T$, Selberg; and the whole Ingham–Huxley–Heath-Brown school, which improves exponents far from the line) become **trivial** exactly at $\sigma-\tfrac12\asymp1/\log T$: for $\delta(T)=\psi(T)/\log T$ with $\psi$ bounded, the bound is $\gg N(T)$. (ii) [Prop 141.M2]: the only thing proved at that scale is **clustering** (100% of the zeros live in the tube $|\beta-\tfrac12|\le\psi(\gamma)/\log\gamma$, $\psi\to\infty$ arbitrary) — the opposite sense of a repulsion. (iii) [Prop 176.9]: no exponential-type density $N(\tfrac12+s,T)\ll T^{1-cs}(\log T)^\kappa$ — not even the full family of moment conjectures through that channel — sees anything below $s\asymp1/\log T$: the fatal mass lives there. (iv) [Lemma 141.B0]: moments of $S(t)$, short counts, ordinate gaps: **exact** blindness (functionals of the ordinates). (v) Pair correlation: Montgomery's theorem [M73] is conditional on RH; the unconditional versions (Goldston–Montgomery) carry weights $x^{\beta-1/2}\to1$ sub-resolution — blind (141 §2.3). (vi) [Thm 170.5] ($E(T)\ll T/\log T$, unconditional): $\neg$LP-134∞ gives $\sum_jb_j^2\le\sum_j(\varepsilon_j/\log\gamma_j)^2<\infty$ — the negation is **compatible with infinite slack** with respect to the program's only second-order theorem. **Partial verdict: nothing in the counting industry touches LP-134∞, and weakening LP-134 to LP-134∞ opens no averaging door (on the contrary: the negation concentrates even less energy).** Confirmed; we pivot.

### 2.2. The pivot: what distinguishes an off zero is not counting but symmetry — twin pairs and the Lehmer dual

An off zero at distance $b$ from the line does not come alone: the functional equation imposes its **twin** $1-\beta+i\gamma$ at the **same ordinate**: two zeros of $\xi$ at horizontal distance $2b$, symmetric about the line. For $b\ll1/\log\gamma$ this is an **almost-double** of $\xi$ at sub-spacing scale: the complex version of a **Lehmer pair**. Recall the real version: Csordas–Smith–Varga [CSV94] call a Lehmer pair two consecutive real zeros of $\Xi$ (equivalently, of $H_0$ with local RH) anomalously close, $g\ll$ mean spacing, and prove such pairs force **lower bounds on $\Lambda$**: a real almost-double at $t=0$ is the footprint of a nearby collision/takeoff in flow time.

The exact duality, in the program's language (fold, Prop 167-2.6; quadruple model, Thm 174.4): the complex twin pair $(\tfrac12\pm b+i\gamma)$ and the real Lehmer pair are **the two faces of the same fold of the heat flow** — the complex pair is the side $t<t_c$, the real pair with gap $g(t)=\Theta(\sqrt{t-t_c})$ is the side $t>t_c$, and $t_c$ is the landing. The CSV machinery for a complex pair is then immediate, and it must be stated frankly what it gives and what it does not:

**[PROP 179.3] (complex CSV: quantitative but saturated).** If ζ has an off quadruple $(b,\gamma)$ whose cluster at the current scale has $n$ zeros (Def. 177 §2.2) and satisfies the separation of Thm 177.6 on $(0,t_c)$, then
$$\Lambda\;\ge\;t_c\;\ge\;\frac{b^2}{2n^2}\,(1-C\varepsilon)\;>\;0.$$

*Proof.* $H_t$ has non-real zeros for all $t<t_c$ (the quadruple is off until its landing; the heights do not vanish earlier by definition of $t_c$), hence $\Lambda\ge t_c$ by definition of $\Lambda$. The lower bound on $t_c$ is [Thm 177.6] read forward from the landing (Doc 177 §4.1: $b^2\le 2n^2t_c(1+C\varepsilon)$). $\square$

**Frank reading of 179.3.** It is the complex version of the CSV mechanism ("almost-double ⟹ $\Lambda$ bounded below"), and it is quantitative. But as a lever against $\neg$LP-134∞ it is **saturated**: under $\neg$LP-134∞, $b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$ gives $t_j\le b_j^2/2\cdot(1+o(1))=o((\log\gamma_j)^{-2})$ (isolated quadruple, Thm 174.4), and Prop 179.3 only delivers $\Lambda\ge\sup_jt_j$ — a fixed positive number (that of the first quadruple), information already contained in "$\neg$RH ⟹ $\Lambda>0$". And conversely, the known ceiling $\Lambda\le0.2$ [P15] gives via 179.3 only $b\le n\sqrt{0.4}$ — vacuous ($b<\tfrac12$ always). **The CSV bridge exists, is exact, and does not bite**: the direction "almost-doubles ⟹ $\Lambda$ large" works upward and LP-134 needs a prohibition downward. Clean death of the lever; but the computation leaves the route's central dividend:

### 2.3. The dividend: LP-134∞ IS a statement about the scale of landings

**[THEOREM 179.4] (diffusive dictionary; the exact dynamic reformulation of the minimal form).** Let $m=\infty$ and suppose each quadruple $(b_j,\gamma_j)$ has, on $(0,t_j)$, an admissible cluster at the current scale with multiplicity $n_j$ and separation as in Thm 177.6, and denote $t_{\rm dif}(\gamma):=(\log\gamma)^{-2}$. Then:
1. (Unconditional within the class, via 177.6.) $\ \displaystyle t_j\;\ge\;\frac{(b_j\log\gamma_j)^2}{2\,n_j^2\,(\log\gamma_j)^2}\,(1-C\varepsilon)\;=\;\frac{(b_j\log\gamma_j)^2}{2n_j^2}\;t_{\rm dif}(\gamma_j)\,(1-C\varepsilon).$
   Consequently: **LP-134∞ with bounded multiplicities ($n_j\le n_0$ on the witness subsequence) ⟹ infinitely many landings at time $\ge c'\,t_{\rm dif}(\gamma_j)$** — landings at the diffusive scale or later.
2. (Converse for isolated quadruples, via 174.4.) If moreover quadruple $j$ is isolated at its scale ($n_j=2$, sea at distance $\gg\sqrt{t_j}$, i.e. $t_j\ll(\log^2\gamma_j)^{-1}$ from Prop 177.3), then $t_j=\tfrac12b_j^2(1+o(1))$ and
$$\mathrm{LP\text{-}134^\infty}\iff\limsup_j\ \frac{t_j}{t_{\rm dif}(\gamma_j)}>0:$$
**the minimal form of LP-134 is exactly the assertion that not all landings are sub-diffusive.**

*Proof.* (1) Thm 177.6 forward: $b_j^2\le2n_j^2t_j(1+C\varepsilon)$; solve for $t_j$ and write $b_j^2=(b_j\log\gamma_j)^2/(\log\gamma_j)^2$. If $b_j\log\gamma_j\ge c$ on a subsequence with $n_j\le n_0$: $t_j\ge\frac{c^2}{2n_0^2}t_{\rm dif}(\gamma_j)(1-C\varepsilon)$ on that subsequence. (2) The isolated quadruple model ([Thm 174.4] with the remainder control of Prop 177.3, applicable in the indicated range) gives $t_j=\tfrac12 b_j^2(1+o(1))$; then $t_j/t_{\rm dif}(\gamma_j)=\tfrac12(b_j\log\gamma_j)^2(1+o(1))$, and "$\limsup>0$" ⟺ "$b_j\log\gamma_j\ge c$ infinitely often" ⟺ LP-134∞. The isolation clause is self-consistent in the ⟸ direction: if $t_j\le\delta\,t_{\rm dif}$, the cluster radius $\sqrt{8t_j}$ is $\ll$ mean spacing and $n_j=2$ generically; in the ⟹ direction the hypothesis is assumed. $\square$

**Reading — what was gained.** LP-134∞ ceases to be a static statement about where the zeros are and becomes a statement about **when they land**: the negation says the entire pathology lives in landings at times $o(t_{\rm dif})$ — **before heat has communicated a zero with its neighbor**. This matches exactly (forced consistency, not new information) the corner of [Thm 177.7]: there $t_j\le(\log\gamma_j)^{-2/(1-2\theta)}\ll t_{\rm dif}(\gamma_j)$ — Theorem 177.7 already pushed the pathology into the sub-diffusive regime, and LP-134∞ is the statement that the sub-diffusive regime cannot contain all of it. The two halves of the tower thus end up on the **same axis** (the ratio $t_j/t_{\rm dif}$): 177.B governs the $\ge$ diffusive side (interior, ζ-free), LP-134∞ the sub-diffusive side (corner, arithmetic). The axis is new; the boundary between the halves is the diffusive scale itself.

**And the quadruple near-collision.** Geometrically, $\neg$LP-134∞ says: infinitely many (all, except finitely many) quadruples with all **four** zeros inside a ball of radius $2b_j=o(1/\log\gamma_j)=o(\text{mean spacing})$ — quadruple near-collisions in the gas. Does any unconditional repulsion forbid this? Frank inventory: Montgomery's repulsion is conditional on RH (and even under RH it speaks of pairs of ordinates, not off quadruples); the unconditional versions are blind (§2.1(v)); Thm 170.5, infinite slack (§2.1(vi)); real–real repulsion under the flow ($\dot g=4/g+O(1)>0$, Doc 174 §3.2) forbids real collisions forward, but landings are folds, not forbidden collisions. **No known unconditional obstruction to sub-resolution quadruple near-collisions exists.** Route 1 dies as a proof route; it delivers dictionary 179.4 and the unification of the axis.

## 3. Route 2: local Jensen/Landau and the discriminant — the wall of multiple zeros

### 3.1. What the disk of radius $1/\log\gamma$ does say (and why it is not enough)

Classical local counting: $N(\gamma+1)-N(\gamma-1)\ll\log\gamma$ ([T86, Thm 9.2]) and, more finely, $\sum_\rho\bigl(1+(\gamma-\gamma_\rho)^2\bigr)^{-1}\ll\log\gamma$ (Hadamard formula + $\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(2+i\gamma)=O(1)$; [T86, §9.6]). Consequence: the disk of radius $r$ around $\tfrac12+i\gamma$ contains $\ll\log\gamma$ zeros for every $r\le1$ — and **that is all**: the method (Jensen from $2+i\gamma$ with $|\zeta|\ll\gamma^A$ in the strip, $|\zeta(2+i\gamma)|\asymp1$) pays a budget of $\log(\gamma^A)\asymp\log\gamma$ in total and cannot give $O(1)$ in a disk of radius $1/\log\gamma$: the unconditional multiplicity bound for a zero is $m(\rho)\ll\log\gamma$, and nothing better than $\log\log$-factor improvements in the denominator is known. In particular, **Jensen forbids neither two nor $\log\gamma$ zeros clustered at arbitrarily small distance $2b$**. Confirmed: local counting is structurally unable to separate "two zeros at distance $2b$" from "a double zero".

### 3.2. The discriminant: the correct quantity, and why it is unreachable

The quantity that does distinguish is the local discriminant: for $\xi$ around $s_0=\tfrac12+i\gamma$, the twin pair $\{\tfrac12\pm b+i\gamma\}$ versus the double zero at $\tfrac12+i\gamma$ differ in the local polynomial $w^2-b^2$ versus $w^2$ ($w=s-s_0$): discriminant $4b^2$ versus $0$. Two routes to it, and their deaths:

(a) **Logarithmic derivative.** $\frac{\xi'}{\xi}$ near $s_0$ equals $\frac{2w}{w^2-b^2}+O(\log\gamma)$ versus $\frac2w+O(\log\gamma)$: the difference is $\frac{2b^2}{w(w^2-b^2)}$, of size $\asymp b^2/|w|^3$ — it exceeds the sea noise ($O(\log\gamma)$) only for $|w|\lesssim b^{2/3}(\log\gamma)^{-1/3}$, and integrating it against a test requires resolution $\lesssim b$ in $t$: support $e^{1/b}$, super-polynomial in $\gamma$ when $b=o(1/\log\gamma)$. This is exactly [Computation 141.M5] (second-order signal, zero mean, width $b$) in its local incarnation: **GAP-141.G1 again**, with no discount from having passed from LP-134 to LP-134∞ (the witness window is chosen by the adversary; we need error $o(1)$ on infinitely many self-selected windows — §5.2 gives the precise statement).

(b) **"ζ has no multiple zeros".** If it were known that ζ has no double zeros **with effectivity** (a lower bound on the discriminant, i.e. on the separation of nearby zeros, as a function of $\gamma$), a twin pair at distance $2b$ would have $2b\ge$ that bound: LP-134 at the corresponding scale. Actual state, without gloss: (i) it is **not known** that ζ has no multiple zeros — neither on the line nor off it; simplicity of zeros is an independent open conjecture (a consequence of GUE/Montgomery, conditional); (ii) the unconditional direction goes the opposite way in counting: a positive proportion of zeros is simple (Levinson, Conrey; $\zeta\zeta'$ moment methods) — a **proportion**, not the totality, and with no effectivity of separation whatsoever; (iii) even the qualitative conjecture "all zeros are simple" **would not give LP-134**: simplicity is not effective separation (the zeros of the Hadamard counterexample 141.R2 are all simple and violate every sub-exponential scale). What would be needed is a **Liouville-type bound for the discriminant of ξ**: "two distinct zeros of ξ at ordinate $\gamma$ are at distance $\ge\phi(\gamma)$" with explicit $\phi$ — a transcendence/effective-irrationality statement about the zeros, for which not even a conjectural analogue exists in the literature. **[Literature GAP, named]:** no effective lower bound, conditional or not, is known for the separation of two distinct zeros of ζ (the studied direction — small ordinate gaps, Lehmer pairs, discrete moments of $\zeta'$ — produces *upper* bounds and evidence that small gaps *occur*).

**Verdict of route 2: a frank wall.** The correct quantity is identified (local discriminant = effective separation of almost-doubles) and both routes to it lead back, one to GAP-141.G1 and the other to an effectivity statement absent from the literature even as a conjecture. We record it as our own GAP in §6 (GAP-179.B) because it is the exact "static" form of LP-134, while 179.4 is its "dynamic" form: both faces of the same object.

## 4. Route 3: is LP-134 avoidable? — the already-proved laws against the corner

Question of the mandate: can LP-134(∞) be replaced in Cor 177.8 by something **already proved** in the program? We do the accounting with each certified law against the enemy configuration: $m=\infty$ in the corner of Thm 177.7 — $t_j\le(\log\gamma_j)^{-2/(1-2\theta)}$, $\gamma_j\ge\exp(c\,t_j^{-(1/2-\theta)})$, $b_j^2\le2n_j^2t_j$, $b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$.

1. **Lifespan budget ([Thm 174.8]: $\sum\ell_j\le I(0^+)/4$, given $I(0^+)<\infty$).** In the corner, $\ell_j\le t_j\asymp b_j^2/2$ (isolated quadruple; in general $\ell_j\le t_j$ and $b_j^2\le2n_j^2t_j$). Then $\sum\ell_j\lesssim\sum b_j^2\le\sum(\varepsilon_j/\log\gamma_j)^2$, which with superexponential $\gamma_j$ converges **violently**: the budget is satisfied with infinite slack. Consistent, no contradiction. ✗
2. **Exponential coercivity (Doc 174: $\dot I\le-2I$).** Gives $I(t)\le I(0^+)e^{-2t}$: a decay bound for the total, blind to the distribution of landings in $(0,\varepsilon)$ — the corner lives at $t\to0^+$, where coercivity has had no time to act. ✗
3. **Balance law ($\dot I=-2\kappa-D$, Doc 167) and its integrals.** $\kappa(t)\to\infty$ as $t\to0^+$ is perfectly compatible with $\int_0^\Lambda\kappa<\infty$ (= summability of lifespans, already covered in 1). ✗
4. **Anti-recurrence and witnesses ([Thm 175.6], [Prop 175.8]).** The rates are $T^{1-b_0/8}$ and the budgets $I/b_0^2$ — they **degenerate exactly as $b_0\to0$**: for the corner ($b_j\to0$) the exponent $1-b_j/8\to1$ and the budget $I/b_j^2\to\infty$. The energy tower is asymptotically mute about the corner: its currency is fixed $b_0$. ✗ (Note the symmetry: the energy tower dies in the corner via $b\to0$; the density one via $s\asymp1/\log T$ [Prop 176.9]; the dynamic one via $t\to0^+$. The corner is the simultaneous escape point of the program's three currencies.)
5. **The formal closure of avoidability.** It is not just that the listed laws fail to suffice: **no law of the class can suffice.** Doc 174 §3.3 exhibits the repaired Hadamard configuration (Doc 144/168: real zeros at the exact RvM points, quadruples at $\gamma_j=2^j$, $b_j=e^{-\gamma_j}$) that (i) is in the Pólya class and enters the flow, (ii) has $m=\infty$ with $b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$ (violates LP-134∞), (iii) satisfies all certified dynamical laws (balance, budget, coercivity — verified in 174 §3.3) and (iv) is consistent with Lemma 177.B (double-consistency check of Doc 177 §4.3: its landings occur well below the threshold $\Gamma(t_j)$). Conclusion:

**[PROP 179.5] (LP-134∞ is unavoidable in the class; substitution is impossible without arithmetic).** There is no theorem valid in the Pólya class with RvM density (in particular: no combination of the program's certified laws — balance, coercivity, lifespan budget, takeoff bounds 177.6, Lemma 177.B) that can replace LP-134∞ in Cor 177.8: the Hadamard configuration of Doc 174 §3.3 satisfies all candidate premises and has $m=\infty$. Any proof of $m<\infty$ via the second tower must use an input that distinguishes ζ within the class (Euler product / explicit formula with primes). $\square$ *(Proof: the cited configuration is the universal counterexample; checks (i)–(iv) are certified in Docs 174 §3.3 and 177 §4.3, which we cite without re-deriving.)*

**Verdict of route 3: the question is settled negatively, with proof.** LP-134∞ is not avoidable: it is exactly the tower's minimal arithmetic quota. The value of the route is having turned "we cannot see how to avoid it" into "it is impossible to avoid it within the class".

## 5. Route 4: averaged forms and the corner's counting account

### 5.1. Does any unconditional statistic of ζ imply LP-134∞?

$\neg$LP-134∞ forces: $\sum_jb_j^2<\infty$ (in fact $\sum_jb_j^2(\log\gamma_j)^2\cdot$anything sub-quadratic converges if $\varepsilon_j\to0$ fast — but even without a rate, $b_j\le\varepsilon_j/\log\gamma_j$ with $\gamma_j\to\infty$ gives $\sum b_j^2\le\sum\varepsilon_j^2/(\log\gamma_j)^2$, convergent if $\gamma_j\gg e^{j^{1/2+}}$, and in the corner the $\gamma_j$ are superexponential). Contrast with every exact statistic of the program and of the literature:

- **Classical Littlewood** ($\int_0^T\log|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|dt=O(T\log\log T)$... and the program's version, [Thm 173.C]: $I=$ second transversal moment of the Riesz measure of $\log|\zeta|$, exact; [Thm 176.4]: the Cesàro limit of $\iint\log|\zeta|$ always exists and equals $\pi I-\pi/8$): all of these are functionals of $I=\sum b_j^2$ and of the **first** moments $\sum b_j$. Under $\neg$LP-134∞ both converge — no moment identity distinguishes this from $m<\infty$. The difference between "$\sum b_j^2$ finite with $m=\infty$" and "$m<\infty$" is invisible to the whole Green–Littlewood family: it is the lesson of Phase 55 (A ⟂ $m<\infty$), reconfirmed in the corner. ✗
- **Fine Riemann–von Mangoldt / $S(t)$:** exact blindness [Lemma 141.B0]. ✗
- **Moments of ζ on the line:** feed either Littlewood (first moment of $b_j$, [Doc 176 §4.3]) or density ([Prop 176.9]): both blind. ✗

### 5.2. The counting account: is the corner too sparse to be ζ's?

Let us quantify the rarity. In the corner, $\gamma_j\ge\exp(c\,t_j^{-(1/2-\theta)})$ with $t_j\to0$: the ordinates of the quadruples grow at least as a sequence that, when ordered, satisfies $\log\gamma_{j}\ge c\,t_j^{-(1/2-\theta)}\to\infty$ as fast as $t_j\downarrow0$ — with no monotonicity imposed in $j$, but with the following counting restriction: the number of quadruples landing in $[\varepsilon,1]$ is finite ([KKL]), and those landing at $\le\varepsilon$ live at height $\ge\exp(c\varepsilon^{-(1/2-\theta)})$. Hence
$$\#\{j:\gamma_j\le T\}\;\le\;\kappa\bigl(c'(\log T)^{-2/(1-2\theta)}\bigr)\;<\;\infty\quad\text{for every }T,$$
with $\kappa(\varepsilon)$ = number of quadruples whose lifespan reaches $\varepsilon$ — finite but **without effective bound**: the corner is compatible with any growth function of $\#\{j:\gamma_j\le T\}$, from towers ($\gamma_j$ = iterated exponentials) to barely-superexponential. The question of the mandate: is there any unconditional theorem forbidding such sparse sequences of off zeros? Frank answer: **no, and there cannot be one in the class** — the Hadamard example of Doc 144 is exactly an ultra-sparse sequence of off zeros in the class, and Prop 179.5 has already sealed that the class does not decide. The only hope is that the **arithmetic** of ζ (explicit formula against primes) forbids the corner. We formulate the minimal arithmetic statement, which is the exact residue of the four routes:

**[GAP-179.A — the minimal residual arithmetic statement, named precisely].** *(io form of GAP-141.G1.)* Let $(b_j,\gamma_j)$ be any sequence of quadruples of ζ with $b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$. Prove that there exists an infinite subsequence $j_k$ and tests $F_{j_k}$ of support $L_{j_k}\asymp\log\gamma_{j_k}$ such that the explicit formula evaluated at the window of $\gamma_{j_k}$ has error term (archimedean + prime sum $\sum_{p^m\le e^{L}}$ + on-line sea) $o\bigl((b_{j_k}L_{j_k})^2\bigr)$ — and that the accumulated signal contradicts something. Differences from GAP-141.G1 (which required evaluation **in every window, uniformly**): here it suffices in **infinitely many windows of an enemy-chosen sequence** — existential quantifier over the subsequence, but the enemy chooses the sequence and, worse, the signal $(b_jL_j)^2=(b_j\log\gamma_j)^2\to0$ **tends to zero by hypothesis**: to refute $\neg$LP-134∞ it is not enough to have error $o(1)$ — one needs error $o((b_j\log\gamma_j)^2)$ with $b_j\log\gamma_j\to0$, strictly harder than the old wall. **Total honesty: the minimal form LP-134∞ is weaker as a hypothesis (§1) but its direct analytic verification is harder than that of LP-134** — the minimal enemy's signal is evanescent. The asymmetry is route 4's structural moral: weakening the needed conclusion does not weaken the estimate that would prove it; it makes it more costly. The analytic path is doubly dead; only the rigidity path (141.E) remains.

What does change favorably with LP-134∞ is the **target of the class route** (Conj. 141.E: analytic continuation of an Euler product ⟹ resolution repulsion). To close the tower it is no longer necessary to prove all of 141.E ($\liminf b\log\gamma>0$): it suffices to have

## 7. Verdict

**LP-134: NOT proved, NOT refuted (unfalsifiable for ζ without refuting RH, Prop 141.R1 — unchanged). What has been decided in this session is its EXACT REDUCTION and its DYNAMIC REFORMULATION, plus a proof of substitution impossibility.**

1. **[PROP 179.2] (the minimal form).** Cor 177.8 needs only **LP-134∞**: "if $m=\infty$, there exist $c>0$ and infinitely many quadruples with $b_j\log\gamma_j\ge c$" — equivalently, $\neg(b_j\log\gamma_j\to0)$. Strict hierarchy within the class: RH ⟹ $m<\infty$ ⟹ LP-134 ⟹ LP-134-d1 ⟹ LP-134∞, all without converse. The second tower stands as: $m<\infty\Longleftarrow$ [Lemma 177.B] ∧ LP-134∞. It is the program's weakest arithmetic statement to date.
2. **[THEOREM 179.4] (diffusive dictionary — the conceptual finding).** Under the cluster hypotheses of Thm 177.6 (and exactly for isolated quadruples, via 174.4): **LP-134∞ ⟺ not all heat-flow landings are sub-diffusive** ($\limsup t_j/t_{\rm dif}(\gamma_j)>0$, $t_{\rm dif}=(\log\gamma)^{-2}$). The two halves of the tower live on the same axis $t_j/t_{\rm dif}$: 177.B governs the diffusive side (ζ-free), LP-134∞ denies that the sub-diffusive side contains all the pathology (arithmetic). CSV duality established and saturated ([Prop 179.3]: an off quadruple gives $\Lambda\ge b^2/2n^2$ — quantitative, but the lever works upward and does not bite downward).
3. **[PROP 179.5] (unavoidability, with proof).** LP-134∞ **cannot be replaced** by any law valid in the Pólya class with RvM — in particular by any combination of balance, coercivity, lifespan budget, 177.6 or 177.B: the Hadamard configuration (Doc 174 §3.3) satisfies all of them and violates the conclusion. The program's three currencies (energy at fixed $b_0$, density $s\gtrsim1/\log T$, dynamics $t>0$) simultaneously degenerate in the corner: the corner is their common escape point.
4. **The four routes, one line each:** (1) density/detection: blind, with exact confirmation; pivot to Lehmer yields 179.3/179.4. (2) Jensen/discriminant: the correct quantity is effective separation of almost-doubles; it does not exist even conjecturally in the literature ([GAP-179.B]). (3) substitution: impossible, with proof (179.5). (4) averaging: every unconditional statistic is a functional of $\{\gamma_j\}$ or of $\sum b_j,\sum b_j^2$ — blind; and the minimal enemy's analytic signal is evanescent: verifying LP-134∞ via explicit formula is **harder** than GAP-141.G1, not easier.
