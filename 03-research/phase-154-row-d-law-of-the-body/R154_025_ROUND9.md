## 2. (A1) The smooth column class and **Lemma T** — `PROVED`

### 2.1 The columns

For `s > 0` and an integer `k ≥ 0` define the even measure on `R`

> `dν_{s,k}(u) = ½ (|u| − 2T)^k e^{−s(|u|−2T)} 1_{|u| ≥ 2T} du`.

It is a finite (positive) measure supported in `{|u| ≥ 2T}`, of total variation `‖ν_{s,k}‖ = k!/s^{k+1}`, hence
admissible in Theorem D1's dual `(D)`.

> **Proposition T0 (exact transform).** `ν̂_{s,k}(τ) = Re[ e^{2iTτ}·k!/(s − iτ)^{k+1} ]`, and for **every** `τ`
> `|ν̂_{s,k}(τ)| ≤ k!/(s² + τ²)^{(k+1)/2}`.

*Proof.* `ν̂_{s,k}(τ) = ∫_{2T}^∞ (u−2T)^k e^{−s(u−2T)}cos(τu)du = Re[e^{2iTτ}∫_0^∞ t^k e^{−(s−iτ)t}dt]`
`= Re[e^{2iTτ}k!/(s−iτ)^{k+1}]`, the Laplace integral converging absolutely for `s > 0`. The bound is
`|e^{2iTτ}| = 1` and `|s − iτ| = √(s²+τ²)`. ∎
(`k = 0`: `ν̂_s(τ) = (s cos 2Tτ − τ sin 2Tτ)/(s²+τ²)`.) Verified against oscillatory quadrature at
`(s,k) ∈ {0.05,0.5,3,50}×{0} ∪ {(0.5,1),(2,2)}` and `τ ∈ {0,1.3,17,59.5,300}` to `< 10⁻⁷` (`r9check.py`).

**The pairing is legitimate, and the endpoint `u = 2T` needs no care.** For `F ∈ 𝒫_T`, `‖F‖ = 1`, put
`dμ = |F̂|²dτ/2π`; then `μ̌ = c_F = F⋆F̃`, which is **continuous and identically zero on `|u| ≥ 2T`**, endpoint
included (`c_F(2T) = ∫F(t)\overline{F(t−2T)}dt = 0`, the supports meeting in a null set — the same remark Paper 42
makes after `eq:localizedprimitiveoperator`). Since `ν` is finite and `c_F` is bounded, Fubini gives
`∫ν̂ dμ = ∫ c_F dν = 0`. So `ν̂` may be added to `σ_T` for free, whether `ν` is atomic, absolutely continuous, or has
an atom at `±2T`. Weak duality `inf_τ[σ_T + ν̂ + β·ĝ] ≤ λ_min(A_T(v))` is unaffected.

### 2.2 Lemma T

Let `ν = Σ_{k=1}^{K} c_k ν_{s_k,p_k} + Σ_{j=1}^{J} a_j(δ_{u_j} + δ_{−u_j})` with `c_k, a_j ∈ R`, `u_j ≥ 2T`, and put
`f = σ_T + ν̂ + β₀ĝ₀ + β₁ĝ₁`.

> **Lemma T (tail by construction).** Define, for `τ ≥ 2`,
> `L(τ) := log(τ/2) − log π − 1/(12.5τ²) − Σv − Σ_k |c_k| p_k!/(s_k²+τ²)^{(p_k+1)/2} − 2Σ_j|a_j| − |β₀|k₀(τ) − |β₁|k₁(τ)`.
> Then (i) `f(τ) ≥ L(τ)` for all `τ ≥ 2`; (ii) `L` is nondecreasing on `[2,∞)`; hence
> **(iii) `L(τ₁) ≥ c` for one `τ₁ ≥ 2` implies `f(τ) ≥ c` for all `τ ≥ τ₁`.**

*Proof.* (i) Four bounds, all valid pointwise: `m(τ) ≥ log(τ/2) − log π − 1/(12.5τ²)` for `τ ≥ 2` (the corrected
auxiliary inequality of `R154_016 E1`; re-verified here, minimal margin `3.7·10⁻¹⁴` over a 2·10⁵-point geometric grid
on `[2,10⁶]`, attained at `τ = 2`); `−Σ_n v_n cos(τ log n) ≥ −Σv`; `Σ_k c_kν̂_{s_k,p_k}(τ) ≥ −Σ_k|c_k|p_k!/(s_k²+τ²)^{(p_k+1)/2}`
by Proposition T0; `Σ_j 2a_j\cos(u_jτ) ≥ −2Σ_j|a_j|`; and `|β_iĝ_i| ≤ |β_i|k_i`.
(ii) `log(τ/2)` is increasing; `−1/(12.5τ²)` is increasing; each `−|c_k|p_k!/(s_k²+τ²)^{(p_k+1)/2}` is increasing
(the denominator increases); `−2Σ|a_j|` and `−Σv` are constant; `k₀, k₁` are decreasing, so `−|β_i|k_i` is increasing.
(iii) Immediate from (i)+(ii). ∎

**What Lemma T replaces.** Lemma D2.1 / D2.3 / F1 (`R154_007/010/015`) needed the atoms on a lattice `u_j = j·2T/M`
so that `ν̂` was `P`-periodic with `P = 2πM/2T`, and then verified `N+1` shifted copies of one period plus a worst-case
row. That machinery exists **only** because an atomic `ν̂` does not decay, so the flat bound `−2Σ|a_j|` is the only
pointwise bound available and it forces `τ₁ ≈ 2πe^{Σv + 2Σ|a_j|}` (the "flat corollary", `τ₁ ≈ 1.1·10³` at
`ρ ≤ 2.9`). A smooth column **does** decay, and the flat bound and the decaying bound coincide. Consequences:
the period `P` disappears, and with it the slack that scales with `P` (`1.8·10⁻²` at `M = 24`, `6.0·10⁻¹` clustered,
`R154_022 §3.3`); the arithmetic frequencies `log 2, log 3` are never required to be commensurable with anything,
because they are bounded by `Σv` and not sampled at all; and the whole tail is **one linear row** in the LP.

> **Corollary T1 (tail price of a column).** Write `m_k = ‖c_kν_{s_k,0}‖ = |c_k|/s_k` for the total variation of a
> `k = 0` column. Its contribution to the tail row at `τ₁` is `m_k·s_k/√(s_k²+τ₁²) ≤ m_k·min(1, s_k/τ₁)`.
> An atom of mass `m` pays the full `m`; a column of width `1/s ≫ 1/τ₁` pays `m·s/τ₁`. **Broad smooth columns are
> asymptotically free in the tail; atoms are not.** (Immediate from T0.)

> **Corollary T2 (the tail row is vacuous for smooth certificates).** Fix any finite smooth `ν = Σ_k c_kν_{s_k,p_k}`
> (no atoms) and `β ∈ R²`. For `τ ≥ 1` each column term obeys `|c_k|p_k!/(s_k²+τ²)^{(p_k+1)/2} ≤ |c_k|p_k!/τ` and
> `|β₀|k₀ + |β₁|k₁ ≤ A′/τ` with `A′ := |β₀|(2cosh T + sinh T) + |β₁|·8T sinh T`; hence with `A := Σ_k|c_k|p_k!`
> `L(τ) ≥ log(τ/2π) − Σv − (A + A′ + 1)/τ`. So `L(τ₁) ≥ c` holds for every
> **`τ₁ ≥ max( A + A′ + 1, 2π e^{c + Σv + 1} )`**, and `L(τ₁) → +∞`: the tail row is satisfied with strict slack,
> and eventually with arbitrarily large slack. (At `T = 0.58`, `c = 0`, `A + A′ ≤ 10`: `τ₁* ≤ 161.9`.)
> **Hence the outside-window tail imposes no constraint at all on smooth certificates; the entire content of the
> certificate problem is the inequality `f ≥ c` on the compact interval `[0,τ₁]`.** `PROVED`.

Atoms are covered too, just less cheaply: with atoms present, `L(τ₁) ≥ c` holds as soon as
`log(τ₁/2π) ≥ c + Σv + 2Σ_j|a_j| + o(1)`, i.e. `τ₁ ≈ 2πe^{Σv + 2Σ|a_j|}` — finite, always, with **no** period and
**no** graded rows. At `T = 0.58` and `2Σ|a_j| = 1` this is `τ₁ = 161.9`.

## 3. (A2) Exhaustion by the smooth class — **Theorem E**, `PROVED`

Theorem D1 (`R154_007`) exhausts the dual by *atomic* `ν`. The LP of §4 uses smooth `ν`, so the class must be shown
to lose nothing. Let `𝓔 := span_R{ν_{s,k} : s > 0, k ∈ Z_{≥0}}` (finite real combinations) and
`D_T^𝓔 := sup{ inf_τ[σ_T + ν̂ + β·ĝ] : ν ∈ 𝓔, β ∈ R² }`.

> **Theorem E.** `D_T^𝓔 = D_T = λ_min(A_T(v))` for every `T > 0` and every finite weight family.

*Proof.* `≤` is weak duality (§2.1: every `ν ∈ 𝓔` is a finite signed measure supported in `{|u| ≥ 2T}`).
For `≥`, fix `ε > 0`. Three steps.

**(a) An atomic near-certificate.** By Theorem D1 there are `ν₀ = Σ_{j≤J} a_j(δ_{u_j}+δ_{−u_j})`, `u_j ≥ 2T`, and
`β` with `c₀ := inf_τ[σ_T + ν̂₀ + β·ĝ] > λ_min − ε/2`.

**(b) Replace each atom by a narrow bump, uniformly on a compact and flatly outside it.** By Lemma T applied to
`ν₀` there is `τ₁ < ∞` with `σ_T + ν̂₀ + β·ĝ ≥ c₀ − ε/8` on `[τ₁,∞)`; moreover Lemma T's bound uses only
`Σ_j|a_j|`, so it holds verbatim for **any** signed measure of the same total variation supported in `{|u| ≥ 2T}`.
For `η > 0` let `ν₁` replace each `a_jδ_{u_j}` by `a_j·(uniform density of mass 1 on (u_j, u_j+η))` (still inside
`{u ≥ 2T}`, same total variation). Since `|cos(uτ) − cos(u_jτ)| ≤ |τ|η`, `sup_{|τ|≤τ₁}|ν̂₁ − ν̂₀| ≤ 2Σ_j|a_j|τ₁η`,
which is `< ε/8` for `η` small. So `inf_τ[σ_T + ν̂₁ + β·ĝ] ≥ c₀ − ε/4` (on `[0,τ₁]` by the uniform estimate, on
`[τ₁,∞)` by Lemma T).

**(c) `L¹`-approximate the bumps by the column class.** Fix `s = 1`. The set `{t^k e^{−t}}_{k≥0}` is total in
`L¹(0,∞)`: if `g ∈ L^∞(0,∞)` satisfies `∫_0^∞ g(t)t^k e^{−t}dt = 0` for all `k`, then `G(z) := ∫_0^∞ g(t)e^{−zt}dt`
is holomorphic on `Re z > 0` with `G^{(k)}(1) = 0` for all `k`, hence `G ≡ 0`, hence `g = 0` a.e. by uniqueness of
the Laplace transform; by Hahn–Banach the span is dense in `L¹`. Translating by `2T` and evenising, the span of
`{ν_{1,k}}_k` is dense in `{½h(|u|−2T)1_{|u|≥2T}du : h ∈ L¹(0,∞)}` for the total-variation norm. Pick `ν₂ ∈ 𝓔`
with `‖ν₂ − ν₁‖ < ε/8`. Then `‖ν̂₂ − ν̂₁‖_∞ ≤ ‖ν₂−ν₁‖ < ε/8` **on all of `R`**, so
`inf_τ[σ_T + ν̂₂ + β·ĝ] ≥ c₀ − ε/2 > λ_min − ε`. ∎

*Remark.* Step (c) is where a *norm* (not merely weak-\*) approximation is available, and it is what makes the smooth
class legitimate: uniform control of `ν̂` on `R` is exactly control of `‖ν‖`. Atoms are the one thing not reachable in
that norm — and by Theorem O they are not needed, since nothing is ever attained.

**Dimension count (the honest one).** Theorem E says nothing about *how many* columns are needed, and the columns are
badly conditioned: for `s ≪ τ`, `ν̂_{s,0}(τ) = −sin(2Tτ)/τ + O(s/τ²)`, so all broad `k = 0` columns become collinear
on `[τ₁/10, τ₁]` and differ only near `τ ≲ s`. Raising `k` shifts the phase by `(k+1)π/2` and the decay by `τ^{-(k+1)}`,
which adds genuinely new directions; varying `s` adds directions only at the scale `τ ≈ s`. So the useful dimension of
`K` columns with `s` log-spaced on `[s_min,s_max]` and orders `0..p` is roughly `(p+1)·log(s_max/s_min)/log 2`, not `K`.
This is the same "atom count ≠ useful dimension" phenomenon diagnosed in `R154_022 §3.5`, now in a class where it can
be *computed* rather than guessed. `PROVED` (the asymptotics) / `DIAGNOSTIC` (the count).

## 5. (B1) **Lemma V** — the mass–value exchange, and why it is sharp

Write `B := Σv − m(0) = 7.62100976…` at `T = 0.58` (`= −σ_T(0)`, the depth of the symbol), and let (REL₀) be the
relaxed primal `inf{∫σ_Tdμ : μ ∈ M_+(R)` even, `μ(R) = 1`, `μ̌ ≡ 0` on `|u| ≥ 2T}`.

> **Lemma V.** (i) *(exchange inequality, exact form)* For every `μ` feasible for (REL₀) or for any of its finite
> relaxations, with `ε := μ({|τ| ≥ R})`,
> `∫σ_Tdμ ≥ −B + ε·(m(R) − m(0))`, and `m(R) − m(0) = ∫_0^∞(1 − cos Ru)K(u)du = log(R/2) − log π − m(0) + O(R^{−2})`,
> `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`.
> (ii) *(the rate is attained)* There are `μ₀` feasible for (REL₀) and `C₁ < ∞` such that for every `R ≥ 1` and
> `ε ∈ [0,1]` the measure `μ_{ε,R} := (1−ε)μ₀ + ε·½(μ₀(·−R) + μ₀(·+R))` is feasible for (REL₀), has
> `μ_{ε,R}({|τ| ≥ R − π/T}) ≥ ε(1 − o(1))`, and `∫σ_Tdμ_{ε,R} ≤ V₀ + ε(log R + C₁)`, `V₀ := ∫σ_Tdμ₀`.
> (iii) *(obstruction)* Consequently no inequality of the form `∫σ_Tdμ ≥ −B + ε·ω(R)` with `ω(R)/log R → ∞` can hold
> on the feasible set; the exchange rate is **exactly** `Θ(log R)`, and turning a depth `B` into a nonnegative value
> requires escaped mass `ε ≳ B/log R`, i.e. `R ≳ e^{B/ε}`. **The barrier of `R154_020 §2.3` is sharp.**

*Proof.* (i) `σ_T ≥ m(0) − Σv = −B` everywhere (`m` increasing, `−Σv_n cos ≥ −Σv`), and `σ_T(τ) ≥ m(R) − Σv` for
`|τ| ≥ R`. Splitting the integral, `∫σ_Tdμ ≥ −(1−ε)B + ε(m(R) − Σv) = −B + ε(B + m(R) − Σv) = −B + ε(m(R) − m(0))`.
The Lévy form of `m(R) − m(0)` is the Lévy–Khintchine representation of Theorem A's archimedean multiplier
(`R154_004/005`; the generator `ν = K/2` is completely monotone), verified numerically here to `2·10⁻⁹` at
`R ∈ {0.7, 3, 17, 59.544}`.
(ii) Take `μ̌₀(u) = (1−|u|/2T)_+`, i.e. `dμ₀ = (T/π)(sin(Tτ)/(Tτ))²dτ`: a probability measure with `μ̌₀ ≡ 0` on
`|u| ≥ 2T` (the Fejér witness of `R154_020 §2.2`), so it is feasible, and `V₀ < ∞`. Its symmetric translate has
`μ̌(u) = cos(Ru)μ̌₀(u)`, which still vanishes for `|u| ≥ 2T`; hence `μ_{ε,R}` is feasible for every `ε ∈ [0,1]`.
Its value is `(1−ε)V₀ + ε∫σ_T(τ±R)dμ₀(τ)`; the archimedean part is `≤ m(R + O(1/T)) + O(1) = log R + O(1)` because
`μ₀` has `1 − O(1/(TR))` of its mass in `|τ| ≤ R/2` and `m` is increasing with `m(τ) ≤ log(τ/2) − log π`; the prime
part is `−Σ_n v_n cos(R log n)μ̌₀(log n) ∈ [−Σv, Σv]`. Collecting gives the stated bound with an explicit `C₁`.
(iii) is (i) + (ii). ∎

> **Corollary V1 (the atomic minimisers gain nothing).** In the finite relaxation (REL) with constraints
> `μ̌(u_i) = 0` (`i ≤ N`) and `∫ĝ_jdμ = 0`, Corollary K0 makes the minimiser atomic with `≤ N+3` atoms and its value
> is `≤` the value of any feasible `μ` with the same constraints — in particular `≤` that of `μ_{ε,R}` after the two
> `ĝ`-moments are restored (a perturbation of size `O(ε/R)`, since `ĝ_j(τ) = O(1/τ)`). So the logarithmic rate of
> Lemma V(ii) transfers verbatim to the atomic minimisers, and **no rate theorem linear in the class resolution
> exists**: the deficit `λ_min − c(class)` cannot be lower-bounded through escaped mass. `PROVED`.

**Reading.** (P154.8) was posed as "find the missing rate lemma". Lemma V says the lemma is missing because it is
false: the symbol `σ_T` grows only like `log τ`, so mass pushed to radius `R` is worth only `log R`, and any
mass-based route to a rate is quantitatively inert. This is the **second** obstruction theorem of phase 154 (after
Theorem O) and it closes the item rather than leaving it open. It also explains round 8's failure in one line: the
clustered class did not fail because of clustering, it failed because *no* rearrangement of a fixed budget can buy
value faster than logarithmically.

## 6. (C1) Riemann's tools: is there an exact identity?

**Poisson summation.** This is precisely what Lemmas D2.1/D2.3/F1 use — a lattice `ν` makes `ν̂` periodic — and it is
what Lemma T *discards*. Poisson gives an identity only for lattice measures, whose transforms do not decay; that
non-decay is the whole source of (P154.7). Nothing further is available here.

**Theta / the functional equation.** They produce `m` itself: the archimedean factor of the explicit formula. Their
content, in the coordinates of this phase, is the Lévy–Khintchine identity re-verified in §5,
`m(τ) = m(0) + ∫_0^∞(1 − cos τu)K(u)du`, `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`.

**Mellin inversion — and the one genuine identity of this round.** The Lévy identity splits the archimedean integral
at the window edge. Taking the **canonical column** `dν_K(u) := ½K(|u|)1_{|u| ≥ 2T}du` (a *positive* finite measure,
`‖ν_K‖ = c_K = ∫_{2T}^∞K = 2.28619…` at `T = 0.58`) one gets, exactly and for all `τ`,

> **Identity M.** `σ_T(τ) + ν̂_K(τ) = [m(0) + c_K] + ∫_0^{2T}(1 − cos τu)K(u)du − Σ_n v_n cos(τ log n)`.

Every frequency on the right lies in `[0, 2T]` (`log n < 2T` for the contacts): **the canonical outside-window
measure converts the unbounded, slowly growing symbol into a bounded, band-limited one.** The identity also
selects the natural column: `K(u) = 2e^{−u/2}(1 + O(e^{−u}))` (ratio `1.000006` at `u = 6`), and since `ν_{½,0}` has
density `½e^{−(u−2T)/2}` while `½K(u) → e^{−u/2}`, the correct statement carries a constant **[amended
per R154_026 R4]**: `ν_K = 2e^{−T}·ν_{½,0}·(1 + O(e^{−u}))`, i.e. `ν_K = 1.1198·ν_{½,0}` at `T = 0.58` (total
variations `2.2396` vs `c_K = 2.28619`). The "magic function" framing of the first draft is withdrawn.
The privileged scale of the smooth class is `s = ½`, not a free parameter — the archimedean Lévy density picks it.

**Against Theorem O.** Identity M is *not* a certificate: its right-hand side at `τ = 0` is `m(0) + c_K − Σv = −5.334`,
and `∫_0^{2T}(1−cos τu)K du ≤ 2∫_0^{2T}K ≈ 2.6`, so the right-hand side is bounded and the identity alone cannot be
nonnegative. Nor could it be: `ν_K` is a *finite* measure, and Theorem O forbids any finite `ν` from attaining the
infimum. The correct conclusion is structural: **an exact identity making `σ_T + ν̂` manifestly nonnegative must use a
non-finite object** — the only candidate in sight is the full `½K1_{|u|>0}du`, which has infinite mass at `u = 0`
(`K(u) ∼ 1/u`) and whose "transform" is the divergent `∫K`, i.e. exactly the constant `m` is missing. **[amended per R154_026 R4: this "missing constant" remark restates `K(u) ∼ 1/u` at the origin and
Theorem O, i.e. it is a tautology; it is kept, labelled as one. The non-tautological content of this section is
(i) the identity, (ii) the band-limitation of its right-hand side, (iii) the selection of `s = ½`.]** `PROVED` (the identity) / `DIAGNOSTIC` (the reading).
