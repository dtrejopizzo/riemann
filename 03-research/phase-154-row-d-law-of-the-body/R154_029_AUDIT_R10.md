## 1. The two moments, rederived from scratch

Let `F` be real, `supp F ⊂ [−T,T]`, `Φ = c_F = F ⋆ F̃`, i.e. `Φ(u) = ∫F(t)F(t−u)dt`, so
`∫h(u)Φ(u)du = ∬h(t−t')F(t)F(t')dtdt'`.

**`Q₀`.** `cosh((t−t')/2) = ½e^{t/2}e^{−t'/2} + ½e^{−t/2}e^{t'/2}`, hence
`∫Φ(u)cosh(u/2)du = ½AB + ½BA = A·B`. ✓ (earlier `Q₀ = AB`, **no factor 2**).

**`Q₁`.** `sinh((t−t')/2) = ½e^{t/2}e^{−t'/2} − ½e^{−t/2}e^{t'/2}`, so
`(t−t')sinh((t−t')/2) = ½(t−t')e^{t/2}e^{−t'/2} − ½(t−t')e^{−t/2}e^{t'/2}`. Integrating,
first term `= ½(A′B − AB′)`; second term `= −½(B′A − BA′) = ½(A′B − AB′)`. Total

> `Q₁ = A′B − AB′`.  ✓ correct; **not** `A′B + AB′`, and **no factor 2**.

**Where the proof breaks.** The round picks `(A,B,A′,B′) = (1,0,±1,0)` and claims `Q₀ = 0`, `Q₁ = ±1`. With
`B = B′ = 0` one gets `Q₁ = A′·0 − A·0 = 0`. The step is void as written.

> **Repair W-a (mandatory).** Use `(A,B,A′,B′) = (1,0,0,∓1)`: then `Q₀ = AB = 0` and `Q₁ = −AB′ = ±1`, forcing
> `t₁ = 0`. The second witness `(1,±1,0,0)` is correct as written (`Q₀ = ±1`, `Q₁ = 0`), forcing `t₀ = 0`.

With W-a, Lemma W stands: surjectivity of `F ↦ (A,B,A′,B′)` on `C_c^∞(−T,T)` follows from the linear independence
of `e^{±t/2}, te^{±t/2}` (a nonzero combination is real-analytic, so cannot annihilate `C_c^∞(−T,T)`); `𝓜` is convex
because `𝔪` is **linear in `μ`** (the quadratic dependence is on `F`, which only supplies witnesses inside `𝓜`);
and the supporting-line argument then closes. The `‖F‖₂`-normalisation is harmless — it scales `Q_j` by a positive
factor and the argument uses signs only.

> **Repair V-a.** Delete "by convolving with a `C_c^∞` mollifier in `u` … `μ̌₀` of bounded variation". Mollifying
> `μ̌₀` in `u` **enlarges its support beyond `[−2T,2T]`** and destroys feasibility. It is also unnecessary:
> `g_jμ̌₀ ∈ L¹` and plain Riemann–Lebesgue gives `|d(R)| → 0`, which is all that `λ(B+V^*) = o(ε)` needs. Drop the
> `O(1/R)` rate claim with it (it is nowhere used).

> **Repair V-b.** State that the corrector is feasible for the *finite* relaxation of Cor. V1 as well, because it is
> band-limited and all lattice nodes satisfy `u_j ≥ 2T`.

> **Repair V-c.** Restate the conclusion as: *there is no `ω` with `ω(R)/log R → ∞` such that
> `∫σ_Tdμ ≥ −B + μ({|τ|≥R})·ω(R)` holds for **all** constrained-feasible `μ`.* Class-dependent rate lemmas are not
> excluded. The sentence "no rate theorem linear in the class resolution exists" over-reaches unless the class is
> quantified away.

## 2. Proposition X does not refute the hybrid

What Prop X proves, exactly: *in the `k = N` row, a column `c₀ν_{½,0}` is debited `|c₀|·cap_{½,0}(x+2P)`, which for
`x ∈ [30,160)` lies in `[2.381, 3.448]·10⁻³` per unit.* That is correct arithmetic and a genuine, useful fact.

What it does **not** prove:

1. **It is a fixed-lattice statement.** The row reads `ν̂_lat(x) + m − Σv − |β|k − |c₀|cap ≥ c`. Prop X observes that
   at the round-5 optimum the row has slack `3.7·10⁻¹⁰`, and concludes the charge "must be repaid by extra lattice
   work". But `a` is a free variable of the hybrid LP; the conclusion presupposes that the lattice cannot raise
   `ν̂_lat` at the binding `x` without losing the same amount elsewhere. That is precisely the unproved claim.
2. **It only prices `c₀ = 1.1198`.** The bound is linear in `|c₀|`: at `|c₀| = 0.3` the tail charge is `1.0·10⁻³`,
   well inside the deficit. Prop X therefore cannot exclude a small-`c₀` improvement, and §4.4's "the canonical Lévy
   column does not buy the tail" is a statement about one value, not about the class.
3. **It ignores the column's contribution where the deficit lives**, which is an order of magnitude larger than the
   quantity being traded. Direct evaluation: `ν̂_{½,0}(15.2176) = +6.190·10⁻²` — the control's deficit sits at
   `τ = 15.2176` and the column is worth **`+0.062` per unit** there, ~19× the tail charge. Conversely, holding the
   round-5 lattice fixed, switching on `c₀ = 0.05` drives `min_{[0,30]} f` to `−3.58·10⁻²`, a loss of `0.65` per
   unit. The column's effect on `[0, τ₁]` is `O(10⁻¹)` in both directions — two orders of magnitude above the
   `3.18·10⁻³` being contested. **Both** the "it helps" and the "it costs" perturbation arguments are therefore
   inconclusive at this scale: the hybrid's value is decided by a cancellation between the lattice and the column
   that only an optimiser can resolve.

> **Required relabel.** §0 line (H3), §4.2 Prop X corollary, §4.4 "REFUTED", and the §6 map row
> "does the canonical column buy the tail? → **No**" must all become **`UNRESOLVED at τ₁ = 30, N = 2`**. Prop X
> itself keeps `PROVED` for the cost bounds only, with its inference relabelled `HEURISTIC (scaling)` and the
> §4.5 `N ≈ 6 / N ≈ 27` extrapolation relabelled likewise (it is the same heuristic).

## 3. The LP anomaly: the earlier diagnosis is half wrong

Two symptoms were reported. They have different causes.

**(a) "LP value rises with cuts, which a cutting-plane method cannot do."** It can, in *this* code.
`r10lp.py` **prunes rows every iteration**:
`keep = (slack < slacktol*S) | (|marginals| > 0)`, then unions with the base grid and rows younger than
`keepage = 5`. The row set is therefore **not monotone**, and `−4.0071 → −3.7714 → −3.7252` (`·10⁻³`) is fully
consistent with exact solves on a shrinking-then-growing row family. This symptom is **not** evidence about HiGHS,
and the earlier stated reasoning ("a cutting-plane method cannot do this with exact solves") is simply false for
the implemented method. Correct the sentence.

**(b) "The objective lies below a strictly feasible point."** This one is real and is the serious symptom: for a
maximisation, `opt ≥ −3.18034·10⁻³` if the control is feasible for the same row set and bounds — and it is
(`c = 0 ∈ [−50,50]`, `d = 0`, `t_i = |β_i|`, reported max row violation `−2.0·10⁻⁵` unscaled, i.e. `−0.2` after the
`S = 10⁴` scaling, comfortably inside any tolerance). A `26 %` optimality gap is far too large for tolerance
effects alone; "scaling/conditioning" is a *plausible* but **unverified** attribution. A dense `20 508 × 423`
problem with `S = 10⁴` row scaling and near-collinear column variables (`R154_026 R6` already flagged
`l1_cols = 1.9·10⁵`) is a credible setting for a dual-simplex early exit, but nothing in the run tests it.
The cheap tests that were not run and cost seconds: pass the control as a warm start; re-solve iteration 0 with
`method='highs-ipm'`; re-solve with `S = 1`; print `res.status`, the crossover flag and the primal/dual residuals.

> **Conclusion on (H3): UNRESOLVED.** There is no evidence that the hybrid class is worse than the lattice class.
> There is evidence that the round-10 LP did not solve its own programme. The one reproducible result of §4 is the
> control: `truemin = −3.180338728·10⁻³`, `Σ|a_j| = 25.8360`, `F1 slack = 3.73·10⁻¹⁰` — which does establish,
> correctly and for the first time, that **the worst-case tail row is active at the round-5 optimum**. That fact
> survives everything above and is worth keeping.

## 5. Honest reading: mathematics or computation?

After rounds 5–10 the two-prime step at `T = 0.58` has become, on the present attack, a **computation problem
wrapped in one unproved mathematical claim**. The mathematics of the tail is finished: Lemma F1 and Lemma T are
proved, the Hybrid Lemma composes them with no loss, Theorem E says the column class is exhaustive, Theorem O and
Lemma V′ say the two obvious escape routes (unbounded `ℓ¹`, escaped mass) are quantitatively inert, and Cor. K0
says the optimiser is atomic. What remains is a finite-dimensional convex programme whose value at the
configurations tried is `−3.18·10⁻³`, and rounds 8, 9 and 10 have all foundered not on a theorem but on a solver
(round 8's sampling artefact, round 9's `l1_cols = 1.9·10⁵` conditioning, round 10's `26 %` optimality gap). The
one unproved mathematical claim is the one the programme actually needs and has never been stated: **a lower bound
on the deficit of a class, i.e. a dual certificate that `c(class) < 0` for all classes of a given description** —
every "no-go" so far (O, V′) excludes a *mechanism*, none bounds the *value*. That is the remaining lever, and it
is the exact converse of what Lemma V′ does: V′ shows the primal side cannot be pushed; nobody has tried to
construct the *dual* object (a feasible `μ` with `∫σ_Tdμ < 0` and the two moments, i.e. an explicit
`F ∈ 𝒫_T` with negative energy) — Lemma W has just, for the first time in the phase, handed over a constructive
supply of such `F`'s with prescribed moments and finite value. Two untried mathematical levers remain: (1) use
Lemma W's four-parameter family as an **ansatz for the primal minimiser** at `T = 0.58` and compute `∫σ_Tdμ_F`
directly — no LP, no solver, and it would settle the sign of `λ_min(A_{0.58})` in either direction; (2) exploit that
`ker M_−` is a signature-`(1,1)` kernel (Lemma W's remark) to parametrise `𝒫_T` explicitly. Until one of those is
tried, "the two-prime step is a computation problem" is an assumption, not a finding — and the LP evidence for it is
currently void.
