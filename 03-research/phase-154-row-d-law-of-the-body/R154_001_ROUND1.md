**One-line result.** The archimedean multiplier is a **negative-definite (Lévy–Khintchine) function** with explicit Lévy
density `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`, and therefore, for every unit `F` supported in `I_T` with autocorrelation `Φ`,

  **`⟨A_T(v)F,F⟩ = C_0 + 𝔈(Φ) − Σ_n v_n Φ(log n)`, `C_0 = ψ(¼) − log π = −5.3721834192…`,
  `𝔈(Φ) := ∫_0^∞ (1−Φ(u))K(u)du`,**

so that `λ_min(A_T(v)) = C_0 − Λ_T(v)` **exactly**, where `Λ_T(v) := sup{Σ_n v_nΦ(log n) − 𝔈(Φ) : Φ ∈ 𝒦_{2T}}` is the
new object: a **window-constrained Lévy transform**. All of the `T`-dependence of the archimedean side is the elementary
tail `R(T) = ∫_{2T}^∞K = log coth(T/2) + π − 2 arctan e^T` (`= log3 + π − 2arctan2` at `T = log 2`;
`= log(2+√3) + π − 2π/3` at `T = ½log3`). The body's law is `x_0 = C_0 + 𝔈(Φ)`: **the first coordinate of `W_T` is the
Lévy energy of the autocorrelation, shifted by `ψ(¼) − log π`.**

## 2. (A1) The orbit fibration: type I below `½log3`, type II₁ above

Let `Λ := Σ_n (log n)Z ⊂ R` be the group generated by the active contacts and `I_T = (−T,T)`.

**Proposition 2.1 (one contact; `PROVED`, = R153_019 Lemma 3.1's mechanism).** If only `n = 2` is active (`T < ½log3`),
`Λ = (log2)Z` is discrete, `[−T, −T+log2)` is a measurable transversal, and
`L²(I_T) = ∫^⊕ ℂ^{k(s)}ds` with `k(s) = ⌈(2T−s')/log2⌉`. **At `T = ½log3` (the largest such `T`) there are exactly two
graph types**: the edge `K_2`, for `s` in a set of length `2T − log2 = log(3/2)` (frequency `log(3/2)/log2 = 58.50%`), and
the isolated vertex `K_1`, frequency `log(4/3)/log2 = 41.50%`. The shift part `v_2·½(S+S^*)` has spectrum
`{−v_2/2, 0, +v_2/2}` with weights `(0.5850/2, 0.4150, 0.5850/2)`; its top is `v_2/2 = cos(π/3)·v_2` — Lemma 3.1 with
`m = 3`. The spectral measure of the shift operator is therefore purely atomic, three atoms, computed exactly.

**Theorem 2.2 (two contacts: the fibration does not exist; `PROVED`, new).** Let `T ≥ ½log3`, so both `log2` and `log3`
are active. Then `Λ = (log2)Z + (log3)Z` is dense in `R` (`log3/log2 ∉ Q`), the orbit equivalence relation `R_Λ` on
`(I_T, dt)` is **ergodic** (Weyl equidistribution), hence **non-smooth**: there is no measurable transversal and no
decomposition `L²(I_T) = ∫^⊕ℓ²(O_s)dμ(s)`. *Proof.* A measurable transversal would give a measurable `Λ`-invariant
partition into null sets carrying positive total measure; ergodicity of a dense translation group on a finite interval
(equivalently of the `Z²`-action `t ↦ t + k log2 + l log3` on `R`, by Weyl) forbids it. ∎
**Correct home.** The truncated shifts `P_T S_{log n} P_T` generate a von Neumann algebra `M_T` inside the groupoid
algebra `W^*(R_Λ|_{I_T})`, which is the **hyperfinite II₁ factor** (`Z²` amenable, relation ergodic and finite-measure),
with trace `τ(x) = (2T)^{-1}⟨x·1,1⟩`. The shift part `B_T(v) = Σ_n v_n·½(S_n+S_n^*)` is a self-adjoint element of the
compression, and "the spectral measure of the graph" is its **trace (IDS) measure**, which has no atoms and no finite
graph types. This is the finite-window form of D.88's obstruction (`114_d_88:29–38`).

**What replaces graph types.** For `x ∈ I_T`, the pointed graph of radius `r` around `x` — vertices
`x + k log2 + l log3 ∈ I_T`, edges for a step `±log2`, `±log3` — is determined by the finitely many inequalities
`|x + k log2 + l log3| < T`, `|k|+|l| ≤ r`; so the *radius-`r` type* is a piecewise-constant function of `x` with at most
`2r²+2r+1` pieces, whose frequencies are the lengths of the pieces (a cut-and-project / Sturmian family). Each `Γ_x` is
the induced subgraph of the square lattice `Z²` on the irrational strip `{(k,l) : |x + k log2 + l log3| < T}` — an
infinite quasi-one-dimensional graph. Connectivity of `Γ_x` for a.e. `x`: `OPEN` (not needed below).

**Spectra at `T = log 2` (`DIAGNOSTIC-NUMERIC` for the joint value, `PROVED` for the axes).** Single-shift restrictions:
`⌈2T/log2⌉ = 2` and `⌈2T/log3⌉ = 2`, so both axis tops are `cos(π/3) = ½` (Lemma 3.1) and
`max v_2Φ(log2) = 0.49013`, `max v_3Φ(log3) = 0.63428` at `v = w_ζ`. Jointly, `max(v_2Φ(log2)+v_3Φ(log3)) = 1.054143`
(`R153_019 §8`, the `(x_2,x_3)` pairwise relaxation), against `1.13442` for the box and against
`‖B_∞(v)‖ = v_2+v_3 = 2.24883` for the **untruncated** operator on `L²(R)` — the last value is `PROVED` by Kronecker
(the symbol `v_2cos(τlog2)+v_3cos(τlog3)` has supremum `v_2+v_3`, approached but never attained). **The entire sign
problem lives in the `2.24883 → 1.05414` truncation deficit.**

## 3. (A2) The archimedean kernel: closed form, and it is a Lévy density

From `ψ(z) = −γ + ∫_0^∞(e^{−t} − e^{−zt})/(1−e^{−t})dt` with `t = 2u` and `z = ¼+iτ/2`:

  `m(τ) := Reψ(¼+iτ/2) − logπ = C_0 + ∫_0^∞ (1 − cos τu)·K(u)du`, `C_0 = ψ(¼) − logπ`,
  **`K(u) = 2e^{3u/2}/(e^{2u}−1) = ½·1/sinh(u/2) + ½·1/cosh(u/2)`** (partial fractions in `e^{u/2}`).

`PROVED`; the closed form is the corpus kernel `¼[1/sinh+1/cosh]` of `R129_007:33` after folding `u ↔ −u`. **Verified
numerically** (`levy.py`, mpmath `dps=30`): agreement `< 10⁻¹⁶` for `τ ≤ 7`, `1.7·10⁻⁶` at `τ = 20` and `0.057` at
`τ = 100` — the drift is the known loss of the oscillatory quadrature, not of the identity (`mp.quad` with fixed
breakpoints; at `τ = 20` the integrand oscillates `100×` per panel). `DIAGNOSTIC-NUMERIC` for the check.

So `m` is **conditionally negative definite** with Lévy measure `K(u)du`: `m(τ) = C_0 + ∫(1−cos τu)K(u)du`,
`K ≥ 0`, `∫min(1,u²)K(u)du < ∞` at `0` (`K ~ 1/u`) — not quite a Lévy measure at the origin (`∫_0^1 u K = O(1)`, fine)
and `K(u) ~ 2e^{−u/2}` at `∞`. This is the exact analytic reason `m(τ) = log|τ| + O(1)`: `∫_0^1(1−cos τu)du/u = log τ + O(1)`.

**Tail (the only `T`-dependence): `R(T) := ∫_{2T}^∞K(u)du = log coth(T/2) + π − 2 arctan(e^T)`.** `PROVED`
(`∫du/(2sinh(u/2)) = log tanh(u/4)`, `∫du/(2cosh(u/2)) = 2arctan e^{u/2}`), verified to `10⁻³⁰` at four values of `T`.
Closed values: `R(½log3) = log(2+√3) + π − 2π/3 = 2.364155448`, `R(log2) = log3 + π − 2arctan2 = 2.025907507`;
generally `R(½log N) = log((√N+1)/(√N−1)) + π − 2arctan√N`.

## 4. The object

### 4.1 Definition

`𝒦_{2T} := {Φ : Φ = c_F/‖F‖², F ∈ P_T}` — the normalised autocorrelations: `Φ` positive definite, `Φ(0) = 1`,
`supp Φ ⊂ [−2T,2T]`, and `∫Φ(t)cosh(t/2)dt = 0` (the two Paper-42 moments; by evenness they are one real condition).
By Krein–Fejér this is exactly `{Φ : Φ̂ ≥ 0, type 2T, Φ̂(±i/2) = 0}` (`R153_016 §2.3`).

  **`𝔈(Φ) := ∫_0^∞ (1 − Φ(u))K(u)du`  (the *Lévy energy*; `T` enters only through `supp Φ`),**
  **`Λ_T(v) := sup{ Σ_n v_n Φ(log n) − 𝔈(Φ) : Φ ∈ 𝒦_{2T} }`  (the object).**

### 4.2 Characterisation theorems

**Theorem A (exactness; `PROVED`).** For every `F ∈ P_T`, `‖F‖ = 1`, with `Φ = c_F`:
`⟨A_T(v)F,F⟩ = C_0 + 𝔈(Φ) − Σ_n v_nΦ(log n)`. Hence `λ_min(A_T(v)) = C_0 − Λ_T(v)`, and
**`A_T(v) ≥ 0 ⟺ Λ_T(v) ≤ C_0`.** *Proof.* §3 and Plancherel: `⟨(G_Γ−m_0)F,F⟩ = (2π)^{-1}∫m(τ)|F̂|² = C_0 + 𝔈(Φ)`,
since `(2π)^{-1}∫(1−cos τu)|F̂(τ)|²dτ = 1 − Φ(u)`; the shift terms are `Φ(log n)`. ∎
*Verified* (`levy.py`, Galerkin `K = 160`, exact shift blocks, `40`-node graded quadrature in `u`): at `T = ½log3` the
minimiser gives `⟨A F,F⟩ = 9.791464·10⁻⁴` vs `C_0 − Λ = 9.791586·10⁻⁴` (`Δ = 1.2·10⁻⁸`), at `T = log2`
`1.068·10⁻⁷` vs `1.068·10⁻⁷` (`Δ = 2.9·10⁻¹²`); on random vectors the two sides agree to `3·10⁻⁶`, the `u`-quadrature
error. **Values of the object: `Λ_{½log3}(w_ζ) = −5.3712043`, `Λ_{log2}(w_ζ) = −5.3721833`**, both just below
`C_0 = −5.3721834`. (Equivalently `𝔈 = 5.460` at the `log2` minimiser, against `min 𝔈 = 5.2190 = −λ_min(M_0) − C_0`.)

**Theorem A′ (the law of the body; `PROVED`).** In the coordinates of R153_019, `x_0(F) = C_0 + 𝔈(c_F)`. Therefore
`W_T` is the epigraph of `Ψ_T(y) = C_0 + ℰ_T(y)`, where
  **`ℰ_T(y) := inf{𝔈(Φ) : Φ ∈ 𝒦_{2T}, Φ(log n) = y_n}`** (the *window-energy profile*),
and `Λ_T = ℰ_T^*` is its **Legendre transform**: `Λ_T(v) = sup_y(v·y − ℰ_T(y))`. The sign question is the statement that
the convex function `ℰ_T` lies above the explicit affine function `y ↦ v·y − C_0`. This is characterisation (ii)
(variational/duality form), `PROVED`, and it answers R153_019 §9.3: the law of the body **is** explicit — it is the
minimal Lévy energy at prescribed contact values; what is not explicit is `ℰ_T` itself.

**Theorem M (monotone law in `T`; `PROVED`).** With the contact set held fixed, `T ↦ Λ_T(v)` is **nondecreasing**
(because `𝒦_{2T} ⊂ 𝒦_{2T'}` for `T < T'`, and `𝔈` does not depend on `T`), hence `T ↦ λ_min(A_T(v))` is
**nonincreasing**. This proves, with no numerics, the monotone part of R153_012's decay law. *Quantitatively*
(`tscan.py`, `K = 200`, `h = 0.002`, contacts `{2,3}`): `λ = 4.212·10⁻⁵` at `T = 0.6082`, `7.296·10⁻⁶` at `0.6343`,
`1.0·10⁻⁷` at `log2`, with `dλ/dT = −2.7·10⁻³` and `−5.1·10⁻⁴`. Note `|dλ/dT| ≪ |R′(T)| = 1/sinh T + 1/cosh T ≈ 2.3`:
the archimedean tail and the object's growth cancel to four digits. `DIAGNOSTIC-NUMERIC`.

**Theorem C (semigroup law; `PROVED`).** `𝒦_{2T}` is closed under pointwise products (products of positive-definite
functions are positive definite; supports intersect; the moment condition is *not* preserved, so the statement is for the
unconstrained class `𝒦⁰_{2T}`), the contact map `Φ ↦ (Φ(log n))_n` is multiplicative, and
`𝔈(Φ₁Φ₂) ≤ 𝔈(Φ₁) + 𝔈(Φ₂)` because `(1−Φ₁Φ₂) = (1−Φ₁)+(1−Φ₂)−(1−Φ₁)(1−Φ₂) ≤ (1−Φ₁)+(1−Φ₂)` (`|Φ| ≤ Φ(0) = 1`).
Hence `Λ` obeys, on `𝒦⁰`, the *subadditive-energy/multiplicative-contact* law; in particular for `Φ = Φ_0^k` the trial
values `(Φ_0(log n)^k, ≤ k𝔈(Φ_0))` are admissible, which is the natural one-parameter family for hand bounds.

**Theorem B (the dual certificates; `PROVED`).** For every even signed measure `ν` with `supp ν ⊂ {|u| ≥ 2T}` and every
`β ∈ R`,
  **`λ_min(A_T(v)) ≥ inf_{τ∈R} [ m(τ) − Σ_n v_n cos(τ log n) + ν̂(τ) + β·h_T(τ) ]`,
  `h_T(τ) = 2(½ sinh T cos 2Tτ + τ cosh T sin 2Tτ)/(τ²+¼)`,**
because `∫Φ dν = 0` and `∫Φ(t)cosh(t/2)1_{|t|<2T}dt = 0` for every `Φ ∈ 𝒦_{2T}`. By LP duality the supremum over
`(ν,β)` equals `λ_min`. The canonical choice `dν = −K·1_{|u|>2T}du` gives the *window-corrected symbol*
`m_T(τ) = m(τ) − ∫_{2T}^∞(1−cos τu)K(u)du` and the clean form `λ_min ≥ R(T) + inf_τ[m_T(τ) − Σ v_ncos(τ log n)]`.
**This is exactly the minorant class that `R153_016 §2.3` proved to be the only possible one** ("any working minorant
must use the compact support"): it uses `Φ ≡ 0` outside the window, which no Bochner argument sees.

## 5. (B2) The breakpoint theorem, corrected

**Theorem D (`PROVED`).** Fix the contact set `{2,3}`.
(a) For `T < ½log3` only `n = 2` is active; the fibre graph is a chain of `k(s) ∈ {m−1,m}` vertices with
`m = ⌈2T/log2⌉`, and the pair of graph types changes **exactly** at `2T ∈ (log2)Z`, i.e. `e^{2T} = 2^k`. The
corresponding spectra are `{cos(πj/(k+1))}` (Chebyshev), obeying `U_{k+1} = 2xU_k − U_{k−1}`.
(b) For `T ≥ ½log3` the radius-`r` local type of §2 changes exactly at those `T` with `2T = |k log2 + l log3|`,
`0 < |k|+|l| ≤ r`. Hence the set of all structural breakpoints is `{½log(2^k3^l) : k,l ∈ Z, 2^k3^l > 1}`, the
**3-smooth positive rationals**, which is **dense** in `(0,∞)`. The 3-smooth *integers* `N` are exactly the breakpoints
with `k,l ≥ 0`; among these, `N = n` is also the threshold at which the contact `n` enters. ∎

So the earlier conjecture is **true in the one-contact regime and false in general**: the breakpoint set is dense as soon
as two incommensurable contacts are present. What survives quantitatively is a *radius filtration*: a breakpoint at
`½log(2^k3^l)` first affects the local structure at radius `|k|+|l|`, so its effect on `λ` is suppressed with `|k|+|l|`.
In `(½log3, log2)` the two smallest-radius interior breakpoints are `½log(27/8) = 0.608198` (radius 6) and
`½log(32/9) = 0.634324` (radius 7). **Prediction and test** (`tscan.py`): `λ(T)` should show no detectable kink there.
Measured second differences (central, `h = 0.002` and `2h = 0.004`, `K = 200`): at `T = 0.6082`, `0.1549 / 0.1547`;
at `0.6343`, `0.0336 / 0.0344`; at the control point `0.5900`, `0.4187 / 0.3827`. Agreement of the two step sizes to
`0.1%` and `2%` at the two breakpoints (and `9%` at the control point, where the second derivative is largest) —
**no kink is detected**, as the filtration predicts. `DIAGNOSTIC-NUMERIC`. The kinks observed in R153_012 at
`½log2, ½log3` are the radius-1 breakpoints, which are also contact thresholds; both effects coincide there and this
round does not separate them.
