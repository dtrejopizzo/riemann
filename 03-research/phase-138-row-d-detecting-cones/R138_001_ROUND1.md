## 0. Notation, and the two data of a Weil class

Throughout, `D^W` is Paper 46 Definition 11.1, `Φ_F(s) = ∫F(t)e^{(s−½)t}dt`, and

    I_∞(F,G) = Φ_F(0)Φ_G(1) + Φ_F(1)Φ_G(0) − Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ)        (Thm 11.5)

with the zero sum absolutely convergent. Following Paper 46 §14.1 we write the **ruling datum**

    M(F) := (deg⁺F, deg⁻F) := (Φ_F(1), Φ_F(0)) ∈ R² ,

    deg F := deg⁺F + deg⁻F = ∫F·2cosh(t/2)dt ,
    μ(F)  := deg⁺F − deg⁻F = ∫F·2sinh(t/2)dt ,

and the **zero datum** `Z(F) := (Φ_F(ρ))_{ρ ∈ Z(ξ)} ∈ ℓ¹(Z(ξ))` (in `ℓ¹` by (11.2) and (E3d)). The
**polar block** is

    B_pol(F,G) := Φ_F(0)Φ_G(1) + Φ_F(1)Φ_G(0) = β_T(M(F),M(G)) ,  β_T = [[0,1],[1,0]] ,

so that

    I_∞(F,G) = B_pol(F,G) − Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ) .                          (0.1)

`P := P^W = {F : deg⁺F = deg⁻F = 0} = ker M`. `K` is the canonical class (Thm 11.7), `Φ_K = ξ`,
`K > 0`, `M(K) = (½,½)`, `I_∞(F,K) = ½deg F`, `I_∞(K,K) = ½`. `E := {F ∈ D^W : F ≥ 0}` is the effective
cone; `A := {H : I_∞(E,H) > 0 for every effective E ≢ 0}` is the ample cone (Thm 11.14(iii)).

**Definition 0.1 (detecting cone; blueprint).** A convex cone `C ⊆ D^W` is *detecting* if for every zero
`ρ_0` of `ξ` with `Re ρ_0 ≠ ½` there is `F ∈ C` with `I_∞(F,F) > ½(deg F)²`.

**Remark 0.1a (the failure condition is even).** `I_∞(−F,−F) = I_∞(F,F)` and `(deg(−F))² = (deg F)²`, so
failure of the Hodge-index inequality at `F` and at `−F` are the same statement. Consequently a cone
`C` and the symmetric cone `C ∪ (−C)` (and the convex cone they generate) detect together. `PROVED`
(elementary); used in D.16.1(c).

## D.15 — Intrinsic characterization of the primitive sector

### D.15.1 The primitive sector is the radical of the polar block, and `D^W` splits

**Lemma D.15.1a (`M` is surjective; `PROVED`).** For `a ≠ 0`, `M(K) = (½,½)` and
`M(τ_aK) = ½(e^{a/2}, e^{−a/2})` (Thm 11.8(iii)) are linearly independent, since
`det [[½,½],[½e^{a/2},½e^{−a/2}]] = ¼(e^{−a/2} − e^{a/2}) ≠ 0`. Hence `M : D^W → R²` is onto and
`P = ker M` has codimension `2`. ∎

**Definition D.15.1b (the odd canonical class).** Fix `a > 0` and put

    K_odd := (2 sinh(a/2))^{−1} (τ_aK − τ_{−a}K) .

Then `K_odd ∈ D^W` (Lemma 11.2(i), Thm 11.7(ii)), `K_odd` is an **odd** function (`K` is even),
`Φ_{K_odd}(s) = sinh((s−½)a)/sinh(a/2) · ξ(s)`, so `Φ_{K_odd}` vanishes at every zero of `ξ`, and

    M(K_odd) = (½, −½) ,   i.e.  deg⁺K_odd = −deg⁻K_odd = ½ ,  deg K_odd = 0 , μ(K_odd) = 1 .

`PROVED` (direct substitution; `Φ_{τ_{\pm a}K}(s) = e^{\pm(s−½)a}ξ(s)` by Lemma 11.2(iv)).

### D.15.2 The Lorentzian criterion — corrected, and the cone on which it can be run

**Theorem D.15.2 (Lorentzian criterion; `PROVED`, pure linear algebra).**
Let `V` be a real vector space, `Q` a symmetric bilinear form on `V`, and `K ∈ V` with `Q(K,K) > 0`.
Write `Q(F) := Q(F,F)`. The following are equivalent.

 (a) `Q(F₀) ≤ 0` for every `F₀ ∈ K^⊥ := {F : Q(F,K) = 0}`;
 (b) `Q(F,K)² ≥ Q(F)Q(K)` for every `F ∈ V`;
 (c) there is a subset `C ⊆ V` with `K ∈ C` which is **absorbing at `K`** (for every `F ∈ V` there is
     `ε > 0` with `K + εF ∈ C`), such that `Q(F,K)² ≥ Q(F)Q(K)` for every `F ∈ C`.

Moreover, under (a):

 (d) `Q(F,G)² ≥ Q(F)Q(G)` holds for every pair with `Q(F) ≥ 0` **or** `Q(G) ≥ 0`;
 (e) (d) is sharp: if `dim V ≥ 3`, (a) does **not** imply `Q(F,G)² ≥ Q(F)Q(G)` for all `F,G` in a convex
     cone with `K` in its interior.

*Proof.* (a)⇔(b): for `F ∈ V` put `λ := Q(F,K)/Q(K)` and `F₀ := F − λK ∈ K^⊥`. Then
`Q(F) = λ²Q(K) + Q(F₀)`, so `Q(F,K)² − Q(F)Q(K) = λ²Q(K)² − λ²Q(K)² − Q(F₀)Q(K) = −Q(K)Q(F₀)`. Since
`Q(K) > 0`, (b) at `F` is `Q(F₀) ≤ 0`; and every `F₀ ∈ K^⊥` arises (take `F = F₀`). (b)⇒(c) trivially
(`C = V`). (c)⇒(b): fix `F ∈ V` and `ε > 0` with `K + εF ∈ C`. Apply (c) to `K + εF`:
`(Q(K) + εQ(F,K))² ≥ (Q(K) + 2εQ(F,K) + ε²Q(F))Q(K)`. The terms of order `ε⁰` and `ε¹` cancel on both
sides, leaving `ε²Q(F,K)² ≥ ε²Q(F)Q(K)`, i.e. (b) at `F`.
(d): let `W := span(F,G)`. If `F,G` are dependent both sides are equal. Otherwise, `Q|_W` cannot be
positive definite: `W ∩ K^⊥` has dimension `≥ 1` and `Q ≤ 0` there by (a). If `Q(F) > 0` then `Q|_W`
has a positive vector, hence exactly one positive eigenvalue, hence `det Gram(F,G) ≤ 0`, i.e.
`Q(F)Q(G) − Q(F,G)² ≤ 0`. If `Q(F) = 0` then `det Gram = −Q(F,G)² ≤ 0` directly. Symmetrically in `G`.
(e): `V = R³`, `Q = diag(1,−1,−1)`, `K = e₀`; (a) holds. `C = V` is a convex cone with `K` interior;
`F = e₁`, `G = e₂` give `Q(F,G)² = 0 < 1 = Q(F)Q(G)`. ∎ `PROVED`

## D.17 — The Pólya cone

### D.17.1 Pólya's criterion, the cone, and a refutation of its membership in `D^W`

**Criterion D.17.1 (`CLASSICAL`; Pólya 1949; Lukacs, *Characteristic Functions*, Thm 4.3.1).**
Let `h : R → R` be even, `h(0) = 1`, convex and non-increasing on `(0,∞)`, with `h(t) → 0` as `t → ∞`.
Then `h` is positive definite, i.e. `ĥ ≥ 0`, i.e. `h = F⋆F^∨` for a (generally non-compactly-supported)
`F` with `\hat F = \sqrt{ĥ}` (Bochner plus a Fourier square root). **No zero of `ξ` enters the
criterion.**

**Definition D.17.1a.** `C_Pó := {h : R → R even, h ≥ 0, convex and non-increasing on (0,∞), h(t) → 0}`.
This **is** a convex cone (sums and positive multiples of even convex non-increasing functions are
such), and every nonzero member is positive definite after normalization by `h(0)`.

**Theorem D.17.1b (`REFUTED`: the Pólya cone is not in the Weil class; `PROVED`).**
`C_Pó ∩ C¹(R) = {0}`; in particular `C_Pó ∩ D^W = {0}`.

*Proof.* Let `h ∈ C_Pó ∩ C¹(R)`. Convexity on `(0,∞)` makes `h'` non-decreasing there; monotonicity
makes `h' ≤ 0`. Evenness and `h ∈ C¹` force `h'(0) = 0`, hence `h'(0+) = 0`. A non-decreasing function
that is `≤ 0` and equals `0` at the left endpoint is identically `0`: `h' ≡ 0` on `(0,∞)`, so `h` is
constant there, so `h ≡ 0` by `h(t) → 0`. ∎ `PROVED`

**Consequence and repair.** The corner at `t = 0` is not an accident of the triangle: it is forced, and
it is exactly what makes Pólya's criterion bite. Therefore (i) the corpus's explicit formula (Theorem
11.3) is **not** available on `C_Pó` — and the classical Weil–Barner class is explicitly refused by
Paper 46 §11.1 (the contour of Paper 45 Thm 9.4 leaves the domain of `Φ_g`, and the zero sum is not
known to converge absolutely there); (ii) the honest object inside `D^W` is the **smoothed Pólya cone**

    C_Pó^{sm} := the convex cone generated by { h ⋆ ψ ⋆ ψ^∨ : h ∈ C_Pó ∩ C_c(R), ψ ∈ C_c^∞(R) even } ,

which lies in `C_c^∞(R) ⊂ D^W`, consists of positive-definite functions (`\widehat{h⋆ψ⋆ψ^∨} = ĥ|ψ̂|² ≥ 0`),
and is defined with no zero information. `PROVED`. All statements below are proved for the triangle
family directly (through the absolutely convergent zero sum, which needs no membership in `D^W`) and
then transported to classes of `C_c^∞(R) ⊂ D^W` by Lemma D.17.2c. **[Round-2 note, finding H1:
`C_Pó^{sm}` is a cone of *kernels*; it is used below only as the natural smoothed home of the `h`'s,
never as a "detecting cone" in the sense of Definition 0.1, which is a notion about cones of
classes.]**

### D.17.2 The triangle family detects; `E` and `C_c^∞` are detecting cones

**[TITLE REPAIRED, round 2, finding H1: round 1's heading read "The Pólya cone is detecting".]**

**Notation.** For `T > 0` let `h_T(t) := (1 − |t|/T)_+ ∈ C_Pó` (the Fejér/triangle kernel), so that
`h_T = T^{−1}·1_{[−T/2,T/2]} ⋆ 1_{[−T/2,T/2]} = F_T ⋆ F_T^∨` with `F_T := T^{−1/2}1_{[−T/2,T/2]}`, and

    Φ_{h_T}(s) = Φ_{F_T}(s)Φ_{F_T}(1−s) = 4 sinh²((s−½)T/2) / (T(s−½)²) .    (D.17.1)

Write `w_ρ := ρ − ½` and `W(h_T) := Σ_ρ Φ_{h_T}(ρ)`, absolutely convergent since
`|Φ_{h_T}(ρ)| ≤ 4e^{|Re w_ρ|T}/(T|w_ρ|²)` and `Σ_ρ|w_ρ|^{−2} < ∞` ((E3d)). `F_T` is even, so
`μ(F_T) = 0`, and by D.15.3(iii),

    Hodge index at `F_T`  ⟺  W(h_T) ≥ 0 .                                   (D.17.2)

**Lemma D.17.2a (the Laplace transform of the family; `PROVED`).** Put `θ₀ := sup_ρ|Re w_ρ| ≤ ½`
((E2)). For `Re z > θ₀` the integral `G(z) := ∫_0^∞ T·W(h_T)·e^{−zT}dT` converges absolutely and

    G(z) = (2/z²)·(ξ'/ξ)(½ + z) .

*Proof.* Absolute convergence: `T|W(h_T)| ≤ 4e^{θ₀T}Σ_ρ|w_ρ|^{−2}`, so the integral converges
absolutely for `Re z > θ₀` and Fubini licenses termwise integration. For one zero, with `w := w_ρ`,
`4sinh²(wT/2) = 2cosh(wT) − 2` and, for `Re z > |Re w|`,

    ∫_0^∞ (2cosh(wT) − 2) e^{−zT} dT = 2z/(z²−w²) − 2/z = 2w²/(z(z²−w²)) ,

so the `ρ`-term of `G` is `w^{−2}·2w²/(z(z²−w²)) = 2/(z(z²−w²))`, and

    G(z) = (2/z)·Σ_ρ 1/(z² − w_ρ²) .

It remains to identify `Σ_ρ (z²−w_ρ²)^{−1} = z^{−1}(ξ'/ξ)(½+z)`. From Hadamard's factorization
((E2),(E3a)) `ξ'/ξ(s) = B + Σ_ρ(1/(s−ρ) + 1/ρ)` in the symmetric order; subtracting the expansions at
`s = ½+z` and `s = ½−z` cancels `B` and every `1/ρ`, and the resulting series is **absolutely**
convergent because each bracket is `O(|ρ|^{−2})`:

    (ξ'/ξ)(½+z) − (ξ'/ξ)(½−z) = Σ_ρ [ 1/(z−w_ρ) + 1/(z+w_ρ) ] = 2z Σ_ρ 1/(z²−w_ρ²) .

Since `ξ(s) = ξ(1−s)` gives `(ξ'/ξ)(½−z) = −(ξ'/ξ)(½+z)`, the left side is `2(ξ'/ξ)(½+z)`. ∎ `PROVED`

If `W(h_T) ≥ 0` for every `T > 0`, then every zero of `ξ` lies on `Re s = ½`. Equivalently,
