## §0. Verdict in three lines

1. **L1 is a negative result, and it corrects the blueprint.** The Wiener–Hopf symbol of Theorem W grows like
 `log τ`, so the endpoint profile of `F⋆` is **not** `(T−|t|)^α` with `α > 0`: for a log-symbol Dirichlet problem
 the optimal boundary rate is `|log(T−|t|)|^{−1/2}` (Chen–Weth; the sharp version is in our own corpus,
 `phase-116/.../loglap_boundary_src`). The predicted spectral decay is therefore `|F̂⋆(τ)|² ≍ τ^{−2}(\log τ)^{−1}`,
 i.e. `μ⋆(|τ|>R) ≍ R^{−1}(\log R)^{−1}` — one single power, no `α` to compute. What is **PROVED unconditionally**
 is only the Chebyshev tail `μ⋆(|τ|≥R) ≤ (λ^prim + B)/(m(R) − C₀)` (rate `1/\log R`); everything sharper is
 `CONDITIONAL` on a regularity statement that the corpus does *not* contain (Bombieri's Lemma 2 is the Morrey
 embedding for `W₀^{1,2}`, not a statement about our `L²` minimiser — §1, §2.4).
2. **The bounded-set reduction is PROVED** in the form `λ^prim − c ≤ ∫_{[0,R]}(f−c)dμ⋆ + ε(R)` with
 `ε(R) ≤ ∫_{|τ|>R} m dμ⋆ + (A_T + ‖ν‖_TV + |β₀|k₀(R) + |β₁|k₁(R) − c)·μ⋆(|τ|>R)`, but `ε(R) → 0` **without a
 rate** unless the conditional decay is granted; this is exactly the gap Theorem V′ predicts.
3. **L2 produces the one genuinely new theorem of the round (Theorem TK), and it is sharp enough to bite.** For the
 lattice class `d = 2T/M` the *non-negative* trigonometric polynomials of degree `< M` in `θ = dτ` are orthogonal
 to every admissible `ν̂` over any full period; integrating `f ≥ c` against them gives a **certificate-independent
 ceiling** `c ≤ λ_min(C_M(τ₀,β))`, `C_M` an explicit `M×M` Hermitian **Toeplitz** matrix of windowed Fourier
 coefficients of `σ_T + β·ĝ` (Fejér–Riesz). At `T = ½log3`, `M = 24`, `β` the certified pair, the ceiling is
 **`4.1525·10⁻⁴`** — so the archived 437-atom certificate (`8.8577·10⁻⁵`) already captures **15 %** of everything
 its own class can ever give [amended per R155_010: corrects a surviving "21 %" figure; 15 % is
 `8.8577·10⁻⁵/5.9224·10⁻⁴`, the value of record per R155_003 §1 and R155_007 R-9], and the class `M = 24`
 can never reach the Galerkin upper bound `9.89·10⁻⁴`. At
 `M = 8` the ceiling is **negative** (`−1.33·10⁻²` at the certified `β`; `−5.40·10⁻³` after the concave

## §2. L1 — what the minimiser's spectral density does

**Notation, amended.** [amended per R155_002] Paper 46 fixes `λ^prim_T := λ^P`, the value of the primal relaxation (P)
(`main.tex:352,408`), and records `λ^P ≤ λ^A` with "the equality `λ^P = λ^A` is not needed" (`main.tex:376`).
Throughout §2–§3 below, `λ^A` denotes `inf 𝔞_T` on the unit sphere of `P_T` and `λ^P` the value of (P);
**every occurrence of `λ` and of `λ^prim` in §2–§3 means `λ^A`**. Theorem 3.3 is therefore a bound on
`λ^A − c`; the Problem-A deficit `λ^P − c` is the same statement with `μ⋆` replaced by a minimiser of (P)
(existence: the tightness argument in `thm:D1(iii)`), and the two differ by `λ^A − λ^P ≥ 0`, which Paper 46
declines to bound.

Throughout `T > 0` is fixed, `F⋆ ∈ P_T` is a unit minimiser of `𝔞_T` (it exists: Paper 46 Thm O's proof, or
Bombieri Thm 3), `λ := λ^A_T = 𝔞_T(F⋆)`, and
`μ⋆ := |F̂⋆|² dτ/2π`, a **probability** measure on `ℝ` (Plancherel, `‖F⋆‖ = 1`). Write
`B := A_T − C₀ > 0` as in Paper 46 Thm V′.

### 2.1 The unconditional tail (`PROVED`)

> **Proposition L1.1.** For every `R > 0`,
> `μ⋆({|τ| ≥ R}) ≤ (λ^A + B)/(m(R) − C₀)`, and `m(R) − C₀ = log(R/2π) − C₀ − O(1/R) → ∞`. [amended per R155_002]
> Explicitly, using Paper 46 Lemma 4.1(ii), `μ⋆({|τ|≥R}) ≤ (λ+B)/(\log(R/2π) − C₀ − R^{-1} − ½R^{-2})` for `R>1`.

*Proof.* `λ = ∫σ_T dμ⋆ = ∫m dμ⋆ − ∫Σ_n v_n\cos(τ\log n)dμ⋆ ≥ ∫m dμ⋆ − A_T`, so `∫(m − C₀)dμ⋆ ≤ λ + A_T − C₀ = λ+B`.
The integrand is non-negative (`m` is increasing on `[0,∞)`, even, `m(0) = C₀`, Lemma 4.1(i) of Paper 46), and on
`{|τ|≥R}` it is `≥ m(R) − C₀`. Chebyshev. ∎

This is **the only decay statement in this round that needs no hypothesis**, and it is worthless numerically: at
`T = ½log3` (`B = 6.3524`, `λ ≤ 9.89·10⁻⁴`) the tail reaches `10⁻²` only at `R ≈ 2π e^{630}`.

> **Corollary L1.2 (`PROVED`, sign corrected [amended per R155_002]).** Since `C₀ < 0`,
> `∫_{|τ|>R} m\,dμ⋆ ≤ λ^A + B + C₀·μ⋆(|τ|>R) ≤ λ^A + B`; the `+|C₀|` of the round-1 statement was a slip in
> the conservative direction (MINOR-1). Moreover
> `∫_{|τ|>R} m\,dμ⋆ → 0` as `R → ∞` (dominated convergence, `∫ m dμ⋆ < ∞`), **with no rate**.

### 2.2 What the Euler–Lagrange equation does and does not give (`OPEN`, with the obstruction identified)

Theorem W says `(𝒜 − λ)F⋆ = α\cosh(t/2) + β\sinh(t/2)` on `I_T`. Equivalently, on the Fourier side,

 `(σ_T(τ) − λ)·F̂⋆(τ) = Ĥ(τ)`, where `H − (α\cosh(t/2)+β\sinh(t/2))·1_{I_T}` is supported in `{|t| ≥ T}`. (2.1)

Two remarks, both elementary and both negative.

*(a) (2.1) cannot be bootstrapped by pairing.* Pairing (2.1) with `F⋆` gives
`∫(σ_T−λ)|F̂⋆|²/2π = ⟨α\cosh+β\sinh, F⋆⟩ = 0` by the two primitivity constraints `A(F⋆)=B(F⋆)=0` — which is the
statement `𝔞_T(F⋆) = λ` again. No information.

*(b) `H ∈ L²` is **equivalent** to the decay one wants, not a route to it.* By (2.1),
`H ∈ L² ⟺ ∫|σ_T−λ|²|F̂⋆|² < ∞ ⟺ ∫ m²dμ⋆ < ∞` (the prime comb and `λ` are bounded). So "multiply the equation by
itself" is circular: the unknown is the behaviour of `H` on `{|t| ≥ T}`, i.e. the boundary data, which is exactly
the Wiener–Hopf datum. Theorem W already records that `σ_T − λ` changes sign and has **no Wiener–Hopf index**, so
no factorisation argument is available. `OPEN`.

### 2.3 What the symbol's growth actually predicts (`CONDITIONAL`, and it is not a power law)

The blueprint expected `F⋆ ~ (T−|t|)^{α}` with a computable `α > 0`, hence `|F̂⋆| ≍ τ^{−1−α}`. **This is the wrong
ansatz for a logarithmic symbol**, and the corpus already knows why (`114_d_174 §3`).

`𝒜` is, by Paper 46 Thm A, the operator `C₀ + ∫(F(t) − F(t−u))K(|u|)du − (\text{primes})` with `K(u) = u^{−1}+O(1)`
at `0`: a **zero-order** (logarithmic) non-local operator, the same class as the logarithmic Laplacian `L_Δ`
(symbol `2\log|ξ|`). For the Dirichlet problem `L_Δ u = f ∈ L^∞(Ω)`, `u = 0` outside `Ω`, the boundary behaviour is

 `|u(x)| ≤ C·ℓ^{1/2}(\mathrm{dist}(x,∂Ω))`, `ℓ(r) = 1/|\log \min\{r,0.1\}|`, (2.2)

and **`τ = ½` is optimal**, with a matching two-sided estimate for the torsion function
(`phase-116/working-notes/work/references/loglap_boundary_src/main.tex`, Thms 1.1 and 1.2; the weaker `τ<½` version
is Chen–Weth arXiv:1710.03416 Thm 1.11, recorded at `114_d_174:118`). There is no power `ρ^α`; the vanishing is
`|\log ρ|^{−1/2}`, slower than every power.

> **Heuristic dictionary (`DIAGNOSTIC`).** If `F⋆(t) ≍ |\log(T−|t|)|^{−1/2}` near `±T` with a bounded smooth
> interior, then `F⋆` is a bounded function with a "log-attenuated jump" at `±T`, and integration by parts on the
> two endpoint intervals gives `|F̂⋆(τ)| ≍ τ^{−1}(\log τ)^{−1/2}`, hence
> `|F̂⋆(τ)|² ≍ τ^{−2}(\log τ)^{−1}` and
> **`μ⋆(|τ| > R) ≍ R^{−1}(\log R)^{−1}`, `∫_{|τ|>R} m\,dμ⋆ ≍ R^{−1}`.**
> Every power-law refinement `R^{−1−2α}` suggested by the blueprint is therefore **excluded** at the level of the
> heuristic: the exponent is `1`, exactly, and the only gain over a jump discontinuity is one logarithm.

Three caveats, stated so that they need not be found independently. (i) (2.2) is for `L_Δ` on a bounded domain in
`ℝ^N` with bounded forcing; our operator is `𝒜 − λ` with the prime comb subtracted and the forcing
`α\cosh + β\sinh` (bounded on `I_T`, so that part is fine), but `𝒜` is not `L_Δ`: the difference has symbol
`m(τ) − 2\log|τ|`, which is bounded on `|τ| ≥ 1` but tends to `−∞` like `−2\log|τ|` as `τ → 0`; it is a bounded
*perturbation only after the low frequencies are handled*. (ii) Our `F⋆` is a critical point in a
**codimension-2 subspace**, not a solution of a Dirichlet problem with `L^∞` data; the two Lagrange multipliers are
harmless (their symbols are analytic) but the equivalence "critical point ⇒ weak solution in `ℍ(Ω)`" has not been
checked. (iii) The cited theorems are upper barriers plus one matching example (the torsion function of a ball);
the lower bound is not proved for a general solution. **(iv)** [amended per R155_002] The cited boundary theory holds
*under a positive maximum principle*, and the corpus flags precisely this: `114_d_174:112–118` ("under a
positive maximum principle") and `114_d_174:134–137` ("its maximum-principle hypothesis is itself a positivity
assertion and cannot replace row D"). For `𝒜 − λ` on `P_T` the prime comb is subtracted and `λ` has unknown
sign at general `T`, so assuming a maximum principle for it assumes a positivity statement of the very family
under decision. **Label: the dictionary and its consequence are `CONDITIONAL`/`DIAGNOSTIC`, conditional on a
positivity hypothesis of the same family as the conclusion, and therefore NOT usable in any closing argument;
the only `PROVED` decay in this round is L1.1.**

### 2.4 One constraint that *is* proved, on any endpoint exponent

> **Proposition L1.3 (`PROVED`).** If `F⋆(t) = c(T−|t|)^{α}(1+o(1))` near `±T` for some `α ∈ ℝ`, `c ≠ 0`, then
> `α > −½`.

*Proof* [amended per R155_002] **(direct, replacing the Fourier detour; MINOR-2).** `F⋆ ∈ L²(I_T)` and
`∫_{T−ε}^{T}(T−t)^{2α}dt < ∞` if and only if `2α > −1`, i.e. `α > −½`. (The round-1 argument went through
`|F̂⋆(τ)|² ≍ τ^{−2−2α}`, which additionally assumes that the two endpoint contributions, carried by an
oscillatory factor `\cos(Tτ)`, do not cancel; that assumption is not needed.) ∎

So the *only* a-priori exclusion available from the form domain is the inverse-square-root singularity familiar
from finite-Hilbert-transform (order-`−1`) problems. Combined with §2.3: the log symbol lives at the boundary case
`α = 0`, with a logarithmic correction, which is precisely why nothing sharper is free.

## §3. The bounded-set reduction (`PROVED`)

### 3.1 The slack identity

> **Lemma 3.1 (Paper 46 Prop. 3.2, restated).** Let `(ν,β)` be dual feasible with
> `f := σ_T + ν̂ + β₀ĝ₀ + β₁ĝ₁ ≥ c` on `ℝ`. Then `∫ f dμ⋆ = λ^prim` and hence
> **`λ^prim − c = ∫ (f − c)\,dμ⋆`, an integral of a non-negative function against a probability measure.**

*Proof.* `∫ν̂ dμ⋆ = ∫ μ̌⋆ dν = ∫ c_{F⋆} dν = 0` because `c_{F⋆}` vanishes on `{|u| ≥ 2T}` (continuity gives
`c_{F⋆}(±2T) = 0`, so atoms at `±2T` are harmless), and `∫ĝ_j dμ⋆ = 0` by primitivity. So
`∫f dμ⋆ = ∫σ_T dμ⋆ = 𝔞_T(F⋆) = λ^prim`. Subtract `c = ∫c\,dμ⋆`. ∎

### 3.2 The tail of the slack

> **Lemma 3.2 (`PROVED`).** For every `τ` and every dual feasible `(ν,β)`,
> `f(τ) − c ≤ m(τ) + E_R`, where for `|τ| ≥ R > 0`
> `E_R := A_T + ‖ν‖_{TV} + |β₀|k₀(R) + |β₁|k₁(R) − c`
> with `k₀,k₁` the decreasing majorants of Paper 46 Lemma 3.3.

*Proof.* `f = m − Σ_n v_n\cos(τ\log n) + ν̂ + β·ĝ ≤ m + A_T + ‖ν‖_{TV} + |β₀|k₀(τ) + |β₁|k₁(τ)`, and `k_i` decrease.
∎ (This is the exact upper companion of Paper 46 Prop. 6.3(i), which is the lower bound.)

### 3.3 The reduction

> **Theorem 3.3 (bounded-set reduction; `PROVED`).** For every dual feasible `(ν,β)` of value `c` and every `R>0`,
> ```
> 0 ≤ λ^prim − c ≤ ∫_{[−R,R]} (f − c)\,dμ⋆ + ε(R),
> ε(R) := ∫_{|τ|>R}(f−c)dμ⋆ ≤ ∫_{|τ|>R} m\,dμ⋆ + E_R·μ⋆(|τ|>R),
> ```
> and `ε(R) → 0` as `R → ∞` for each fixed certificate. Consequently, for each fixed certificate the deficit is
> decided on a bounded set: `λ^prim − c = ∫_{[−R,R]}(f−c)dμ⋆ + o_R(1)`.

*Proof.* Split Lemma 3.1's integral, apply Lemma 3.2 on the tail, and use Cor. L1.2 and Prop. L1.1 for the two
limits. Both terms are non-negative because `f ≥ c`. ∎

> **Corollary 3.4 (`CONDITIONAL`, on §2.3).** If `μ⋆(|τ|>R) ≤ κ R^{−1}(\log R)^{−1}` then
> `ε(R) ≤ κ'R^{−1} + κ E_R R^{−1}(\log R)^{−1}`, so `R = R(ε)` may be chosen **linearly** in
> `(A_T + ‖ν‖_{TV} + |β|)/ε`. Under the unconditional Prop. L1.1 only
> `ε(R) ≤ ∫_{|τ|>R}m\,dμ⋆ + E_R(λ+B)/(m(R)−C₀)` is available, whose second term needs
> `R ≳ 2π\exp(E_R(λ+B)/ε)` — **doubly exponential in `‖ν‖_{TV}`**, i.e. of exactly the shape Prop. 6.4 of Paper 46
> (the envelope threshold `2πe^{A_T+‖ν‖_{TV}}`) already forces.

### 3.4 Honest reading

Theorem 3.3 is a *qualitative* compactification, not a rate: the second term of `ε(R)` is uniform over certificates
only through `‖ν‖_{TV}` and `|β|`, and the first is uniform over **no** class at all, because
`∫_{|τ|>R}m\,dμ⋆` depends on `μ⋆` alone and no rate for it is proved (§2). This is consistent with, and is in fact
the primal face of, Theorem V′: a rate in `R` alone, uniform over the feasible set, does not exist. The conditional
`R^{−1}` of Cor. 3.4 is not excluded by V′ because it is a statement about the **single** measure `μ⋆`, not about
the feasible set.

## §4. L2 — the test-kernel ceiling on the lattice class

### 4.1 Set-up

Fix `T`, `M ∈ ℕ`, `d = 2T/M`, `P = 2π/d = πM/T`. The **lattice class** `L(T,d,U)` consists of the dual feasible
pairs `(ν,β)` with `ν = Σ_{j=M}^{J} a_j(δ_{u_j}+δ_{−u_j})`, `u_j = jd ≤ 2T+U`, and `β ∈ ℝ²`. Then

 `ν̂(τ) = Σ_{j≥M} 2a_j\cos(jdτ)` is `P`-periodic with **Fourier modes supported in `{|j| ≥ M}`** — the mean
 (`j = 0`) included in the gap. (4.1)

Let `c(T,d,U;β)` be the supremum of the values `c` attainable in this class with the given `β`, and
`c(T,d,U) = \sup_β c(T,d,U;β)`.

**Definition.** A **test kernel of order `M`** is a function `φ(τ) = |q(e^{idτ})|²` with
`q(z) = Σ_{j=0}^{M−1} q_j z^{j}`, `q ≠ 0`. By Fejér–Riesz, these are *exactly* the non-negative `P`-periodic
trigonometric polynomials whose modes lie in `{|j| ≤ M−1}`.

### 4.2 The orthogonality

> **Lemma 4.1 (`PROVED`).** For every `ν` as in (4.1), every test kernel `φ` of order `M` and every `τ₀ ∈ ℝ`,
> `∫_{τ₀}^{τ₀+P} ν̂(τ)φ(τ)\,dτ = 0`.

*Proof.* Both factors are `P`-periodic; over a full period the integral of `e^{i(j−k)dτ}` is `Pδ_{jk}`. The modes
of `ν̂` are `|j| ≥ M`, those of `φ` are `|j| ≤ M−1`; the sets are disjoint. (Absolute convergence is trivial: both
sums are finite.) ∎

The placement `τ₀` is free: orthogonality holds over *any* full period, not only `[0,P)`.

### 4.3 The theorem

> **Theorem TK (test-kernel ceiling; `PROVED`).** Let `(ν,β)` be dual feasible in the lattice class `L(T,2T/M,U)`
> with value `c`. Then for every `τ₀ ∈ ℝ` and every test kernel `φ` of order `M`,
> ```
> ∫_{τ₀}^{τ₀+P} (σ_T + β₀ĝ₀ + β₁ĝ₁)(τ) φ(τ) dτ
> c ≤ Q(τ₀,φ,β) := ─────────────────────────────────────────.
> ∫_{τ₀}^{τ₀+P} φ(τ) dτ
> ```
> Moreover the infimum over test kernels is an eigenvalue: with
> `S := σ_T + β₀ĝ₀ + β₁ĝ₁` and
> **`(C_M)_{k j} := P^{-1}∫_{τ₀}^{τ₀+P} S(τ)\,e^{i(j−k)dτ}\,dτ` (`0 ≤ j,k ≤ M−1`)**,
> a Hermitian **Toeplitz** matrix (its entries depend on `j−k` only, and it is Hermitian because `S` is real), one has
> ```
> inf_{φ} Q(τ₀,φ,β) = λ_min(C_M(τ₀,β)), hence c(T,2T/M,U;β) ≤ inf_{τ₀∈ℝ} λ_min(C_M(τ₀,β)).
> ```
> The bound is **independent of `ν`, of the range `U` and of the number of atoms**: it is a property of the class.

*Proof.* `f − c ≥ 0` on `ℝ` and `φ ≥ 0`, so `∫_{τ₀}^{τ₀+P}(f−c)φ ≥ 0`. Expand `f = S + ν̂` and apply Lemma 4.1:
`∫Sφ − c∫φ ≥ 0`, which is the displayed inequality (`∫φ > 0` since `φ ≥ 0` is a non-zero trigonometric
polynomial). For the eigenvalue form, write `φ = |q|²` (Fejér–Riesz) and expand:
`∫_{τ₀}^{τ₀+P} φ = P Σ_j |q_j|²` and
`∫_{τ₀}^{τ₀+P} S φ = Σ_{j,k} q_j\overline{q_k}∫ S e^{i(j−k)dτ}dτ = P\,q^*C_M q`.
Hence `Q = q^*C_M q / q^*q`, and the Rayleigh quotient of a Hermitian matrix has infimum `λ_min`. Every `q ≠ 0`
gives an admissible `φ`, and every admissible `φ` arises from some `q` (Fejér–Riesz), so the infimum is attained
at the bottom eigenvector. ∎

> **Corollary TK⁻ (class impossibility; `PROVED`).** If `λ_min(C_M(τ₀,β)) < 0` for some `τ₀`, then **no** dual
> certificate on the lattice `2T/M` with that `β` has non-negative value, whatever the number and range of its
> atoms.

### 4.4 Why this is a genuine class bound, and what it is in disguise

Write `ρ := φ·1_{[τ₀,τ₀+P]}/∫φ`, a probability density. Its cosine transform satisfies `ρ̌(jd) = 0` for
`|j| ≥ M`, i.e. `ρ` satisfies **exactly the lattice relaxation constraints** `μ̌(u_j) = 0` of the finite relaxation
`P(U)` in the proof of Theorem D1 (Paper 46 §3.2(i)), the two moment constraints being carried by the
`β·ĝ` terms as Lagrange multipliers. So Theorem TK is the **primal side of the same finite linear programme**, and
by strong duality for the relaxation (`P(U) = D(U)`, Paper 46 Thm D1(ii)) the *sharp* bound of this type is
`c(T,d,U;β)` itself. Two consequences, both worth stating:

* **(completeness — relabelled [amended per R155_002], MAJOR-4)** `PROVED`: the supremum over **all** relaxed-feasible `μ`
 equals `c(·;β)` (`thm:D1(ii)`). Whether the **test-kernel subfamily** `|q|²1_{window}` is lossy is **`OPEN`** —
 a general relaxed-feasible `μ` need be neither `P`-periodic, nor supported in one window, nor a non-negative
 trigonometric polynomial. (Round 2 settles the limit `M → ∞`: `R155_004` §6, Theorem R.);
* **(no free lunch, `PROVED`)** computing the sharp bound is the same LP, so Theorem TK's value is precisely that
 it gives **cheap, certificate-independent, one-eigenvalue upper bounds** (an `M×M` Hermitian eigenvalue and
 `2M−1` Fourier coefficients of an explicit function), not that it replaces the LP.

The resolution limit is worth recording because it explains the numbers of §4.5:
a test kernel of order `M` has period `P = πM/T` but its narrowest possible peak has width `≈ P/M = π/T`,
**independent of `M`**. ([amended per R155_002] MINOR-3: this resolution statement is `DIAGNOSTIC`, asserted not proved,
and it duplicates Paper 46 §8.5.) The uncertainty scale of the class is the window, not the atom count — which is the dual
statement of "the resolution near the window edge, not the range of the atoms, is the binding parameter"
(Paper 46 §5.1, observed there empirically).

### 4.6 What the dual direction does **not** give (`OPEN`; this is Problem A, unsolved)

* **(a) One-sidedness is not symmetric.** Theorem TK works because `f − c ≥ 0` is *tested*. The lower bound of
 Problem A, `c ≥ λ^prim − D`, requires **constructing** an admissible `ν̂` that majorises `c − S` everywhere —
 a spectral-gap one-sided approximation, for which no dual test can substitute. Formally: testing gives the easy
 half of LP duality (any feasible primal object bounds the dual value), and Problem A is the hard half
 (exhibiting a feasible dual object with a controlled value).
* **(b) Dualising Theorem TK returns the LP.** By §4.4 the sharp ceiling *is* `c(T,d,U;β)`, so "dualise the test
 kernel" is literally "solve the lattice LP". There is no shortcut hidden here. `PROVED` (it is Thm D1(ii)).
* **(c)′ The `β`-uniform version is a well-posed concave maximisation** ([amended per R155_002], MAJOR-5, replacing the
 round-1 item (c) "may be `+∞`"). `Q(τ₀,φ,β)` is affine in `β`, so `Ceil_M(β) := \inf_{τ₀,φ}Q(τ₀,φ,β)` is a
 pointwise infimum of affine functions, hence **concave and u.s.c.** on `ℝ²`; `sup_β Ceil_M(β)` is a
 two-dimensional concave maximisation with no local maxima, not a possible `+∞`. Only coercivity along each ray
 needs checking, and round 2 supplies an a priori `|β|` bound outright (`R155_004` §4). Measured
 (`DIAGNOSTIC`): `sup_β Ceil_8 = −5.40·10⁻³`, `sup_β Ceil_{10} = −1.70·10⁻³`, `sup_β Ceil_{12} = +7.17·10⁻⁵`,
 `sup_β Ceil_{24} = +5.9224·10⁻⁴`, the last `43 %` above the per-`β` value. Round-1 text struck:
 ~~which may be `+∞` (no a priori bound on `|β|` is known; Paper 46 Open (2) asks whether the optimal
 `β → (2,0)`).~~ Every number of §4.5 is therefore a statement about certificates with the
 certified `β`, not about the class tout court. Making it `β`-free needs an a priori bound on `|β₀|,|β₁|` for
 near-optimal certificates — a clean, small, `OPEN` problem, and the natural target of round 2.
* **(d) No rate in `M`.** The table is nine numbers; Theorem TK contains no proof that the ceiling converges, nor at
 what speed. A rate here would be a *ceiling* rate (`λ^prim − \text{ceiling}(M) ≤ ?`), the mirror image of
 Problem A and quite possibly easier, since `C_M` is an explicit Toeplitz matrix and Szegő-type asymptotics apply
 — except that the symbol of `C_M` changes with `M` (the period grows with `M`), so the classical first Szegő
 limit theorem does **not** apply and the naive conclusion `λ_min → \inf S = σ_T(0)+β·ĝ(0) = −1.732` is false.
 Understanding why the resolution stays at `π/T` (§4.4) is the substance. `OPEN`.

## §6. Recommendation for round 2

1. **Certify Theorem TK's numbers** (cheap, high value). The ceiling at `M = 8` being negative is a
 *class-impossibility theorem* and deserves interval arithmetic: `2M−1 = 15` Fourier coefficients of an explicit
 real-analytic function over one period, plus a certified `λ_min` (Gershgorin on `C_M − θI`, or a certified
 Cholesky of `C_M − θI` at `θ = −10⁻²`). This would give the corpus its **first proved negative class statement**,
 and it costs one afternoon of exactly the machinery Paper 46 §5 already has.
2. **Close §4.6(c): an a priori bound on `β` for near-optimal certificates.** Without it the ceilings are
 `β`-conditional. Candidate: pair `f ≥ c` against two explicit relaxed-feasible measures with prescribed moments
 (Lemma W gives interior), which bounds `|β·(\text{moment vector})|` and hence `|β|` since the moment map is
 non-degenerate (the determinant `\sinh T\cosh T + T > 0` of Theorem D1's proof is exactly what is needed).
 This looks provable in a page and would make §4.5 a theorem about the class.
3. **Do not pursue L1 further as stated.** The decay question is a boundary-regularity question for a zero-order
 non-local operator; the sharp answer in the model case is already in the literature and in our corpus, and the
 transport to `𝒜 − λ` on `P_T` (caveats (i)–(iii) of §2.3) is a genuine analysis project, not a round. If
 pursued, the right statement to target is the *single* implication
 "`F⋆ ∈ L^∞` with `|\log`-modulus of continuity at `±T` ⇒ `∫_{|τ|>R} m dμ⋆ ≪ R^{−1}\log R`", and the right tool
 is the barrier/comparison method of `loglap_boundary_src`, not Wiener–Hopf factorisation (which Theorem W
 already says is unavailable).
4. **The reframed target for Problem A.** §4.6(d) suggests the tractable half is the **ceiling rate**
 `λ^prim − \text{ceiling}_M → 0`, a Toeplitz problem with an `M`-dependent symbol. Proving
 `\text{ceiling}_M ↑ λ^prim` with a rate would not solve Problem A but would give the first proved
 `M`-dependence in the programme and would tell the off-machine sweep E1 where to stop.
