## R162_1501

**§1 derivation (R_LF = 0 ⟺ F = 0 on the radical). CORRECT.**
- Paper 47 (l. 4470–4495) defines K_F(a) = ∫_{−T}^T e^{−at}F, a_m = 2m + ½ and b_m = √a_m e^{−a_m(T+δ)} ≠ 0.
- With x = e^{−2t}, e^{−a_m t} = e^{−t/2}x^m (m ≥ 1). The density of x·ℝ[x] in C[e^{−2T}, e^{2T}] (Weierstrass
  applied to p(x) ≈ f(x)/x) gives faithfulness. This matches the paper's text.
- "The radical is finite-dimensional, so some finite L makes R_L injective": the kernels of R_L restricted to N
  form a decreasing chain of subspaces with intersection 0 in a finite-dimensional space. ✓
- eq:equivgate is quoted correctly: Paper 47 states it for a fixed L injective on the radical, and the second
  equivalence uses d_{m,N} > 0 (prop:GEgap). ✓
- The translation "x_m = ζ(s_m)b_mK_F(a_m)" is also used; ζ(s_m) > 0 at s_m = 2m + 1 ≥ 3. ✓

**Lemma D. CORRECT.**
- The classical fact holds. For F with S(F) < ∞ sign changes on a compact interval, the bilateral Laplace
  transform ∫e^{−at}F(t)dt has at most S(F) real zeros counted with multiplicity. It is entire, and not
  identically 0 when F ≠ 0.
- The proof sketch is the standard one. Multiply by e^{at₁} and differentiate in a. This produces
  ∫(t₁ − t)e^{a(t₁−t)}F, whose integrand has one sign change fewer; then apply Rolle. ✓
- References: the Pólya–Szegő Part V Laplace-transform Descartes problems and Karlin, *Total Positivity* Ch. 5,
  are the right places. The exact problem number (80) was not checked against the book.
- (i) Even F gives an even K_F. The zeros ±a_1..±a_L and ±½ are 2L + 2 distinct points, since a_m ≥ 5/2 > ½. ✓
- (ii) Odd F gives K_F(0) = ∫F = 0, hence 2L + 3 zeros. ✓
- (iii) An infinite zero set gives S = ∞ (and also F = 0 by Müntz). ✓
- (iv) If S = 2, the zeros ±½ use the whole budget, so they are simple and there are no other real zeros. Hence
  K_F has constant sign on (½, ∞). ✓
- **Fix (wording).** In (iv), "all x_m share one sign" holds for x_m = ζ(s_m)b_mK_F(a_m) because ζ(s_m) > 0 and
  b_m > 0. Say so; the note leaves it implicit.

**Cor D′. CORRECT, with a precision.**
- What holds is lower semicontinuity of S under L² convergence: S(lim) ≤ liminf S(F_n). This follows because
  sets of positive measure with alternating strict sign persist under convergence in measure. So {S ≤ S₀} is
  closed. ✓
- The consequence "limit has S = 2" additionally needs the limit to be nonzero and to lie in P_{T_*}. That holds
  because P_T ⊂ P_{T_*} isometrically (lem:monoT) and the Tate moments are continuous.
- It also needs the bottom vectors to converge in norm. That requires simplicity plus a gap, which is not
  automatic. State it as a hypothesis.

**Thm K2(a). CORRECT WITH FIXES.**
- The inference "k(u) = h(u²) with h CM, hence φ complete Bernstein" is **not valid in general**. It gives a
  subordinate Brownian motion, i.e. φ Bernstein. The complete-Bernstein property requires the subordinator's Lévy
  measure to have a CM density.
- In this case it does. e^{−α|u|} = ∫(4πs)^{−½}e^{−u²/4s}·2αe^{−α²s}ds, so the subordinator density is
  Σ_α 2αe^{−α²s}, over α = 2j + ½, which is CM. Hence φ **is** CBF. Insert this computation.
- The remaining steps check out:
  - 1/(c + φ) is Stieltjes;
  - the coefficient of 1/τ² is 0 because c > 0, and the constant is 0 because φ → ∞;
  - G_c is a nontrivial mixture of e^{−√r|x|}, hence strictly log-convex;
  - the minor at (0, h; d, d + h) is G(d)² − G(d−h)G(d+h) < 0. ✓

**K2(b).** Declared PARTIAL, pending the Bingham–Goldie–Teugels asymptotic G ≍ 1/(r log²(1/r)). That asymptotic is
plausible for the symbol ~ log τ, and the minor −⅓G(d)² at h = d/2 is arithmetically right. It stays PARTIAL.
