**Thm Λ̃. CORRECT WITH FIXES.**
- (i) is correct: |n^{ρ_j−1}| = n^{β_j−½} → 0, and a finite modification is a compactly supported atomic distribution, hence in S′.
- (ii) is correct: Σ n^{ρ−1−s} = ζ(s+1−ρ), which has a pole at s = ρ with residue −1 (after the minus sign). The residue at s = 1 is +1. This is the polar pattern of −ζ′/ζ at the chosen zeros only.
- (iii), Euler–Maclaurin: the bound ∫|f′| + |f(1)| = O(e^{−a/2}) is correct. f is supported on x ≍ e^a, f′ = O(x^{−3/2}), and the support has length ≍ e^a.
- **(iii), factor-2 error.** The proof itself computes the pairing e^{u/2} − Σ_j 2e^{β_j u}cos(γ_j u), which equals e^{u/2} − ½E for u > 0, since E = Σ 4e^{β|u|}cos γu. So the proof gives μ̃ − 2cosh(u/2) + ½E ∈ S′, not + E.
  - Fix: either replace 2Re by 4Re in the definition of Λ̃, or restate the result with ½E. Positivity for n ≥ n_0 is unaffected.
  - The consequence ("non-tempered part exactly that of D") needs the corrected constant.
- The partners 1−ρ̄_j are omitted. Their contributions are e^{−β|u|}, which is tempered, so the omission is harmless. Say so.
- The falsifier stands after the fix.

**Thm H. CORRECT WITH FIXES.**
- The deduction is right. On the half-plane where −F′/F = Σ a(n)n^{−s} converges absolutely, F = c·exp(Σ a(n)n^{−s}/log n). The exponential of an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series supported on n ≥ 2 is again one, supported on ℕ.
- The cited form must be pinned down. Hamburger (1921) assumes absolute convergence for Re s > 1 and that P(s)F(s) is entire of finite order for a polynomial P. The extension to an arbitrary half-plane of absolute convergence with finitely many poles is due to Bochner (1951, *Ann. of Math.* 53, "Some properties of modular relations") and Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan (1961, *Ann. of Math.* 74). The file must cite the theorem number and check its hypotheses. In particular, "finite order" must be the order of P·F, and the dual side is the same Dirichlet series.
- Consequence (ii) ("conductor q ⇒ linear combinations of L(s,χ)") is not Hamburger. It is the degree-1 classification, which Kaczorowski–Perelli prove inside the Selberg class. Either cite that result with its axioms, or mark the statement heuristic.
- Consequence (i) is correct. ζ·(rational) is not a Dirichlet series unless the rational factor is constant.
