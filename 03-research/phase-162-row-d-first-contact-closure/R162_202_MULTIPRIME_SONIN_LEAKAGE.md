# R162_202 — The exact multiprime Sonin leakage identity


Status: **PROVED IDENTITY / GATE 1 STILL OPEN.** This note globalizes the one-prime collar formula
of R162_201 without discarding any crossed term. It identifies the exact nonnegative quantity that
the oriented finite `(PC-ren)` identity would have to control in order to prove the Euler-stripping
bound.

## 1. Translation convention and the physical edge

Do **not** translate the physical edge to zero: such a translation would not preserve the Sonin
eigen-equation. Work instead with

    H_T=L2([T,infinity)),

embedded by zero extension in `L2(R)`. On `L2(R)` write

    (U_a f)(t)=f(t-a).

For `a>=0`, its restriction to `H_T` is the unilateral delay `S_a`. Its oppositely directed
compression is

    P_T U_(-a)|_(H_T)=S_a^*.

Let `C_log` be the logarithmic cosine transform in parity `epsilon`. The dilation covariance of the
cosine transform is

    C_log U_a=U_(-a) C_log.                                  (1.1)

A vector `g` in the corresponding Sonin eigenspace satisfies, as a full-line identity,

    g in H_T,             C_log g=-epsilon g in H_T.          (1.2)

The second inclusion means that both `g` and its transform have no left-hand tail.

## 2. The complete finite Euler history

For a finite set of primes `P`, put

    r_p=p^(-1/2),       a_p=log p,
    A_P(S)=product_(p in P)(I-r_p S_(a_p)).                    (2.1)

All delays commute. Expanding over squarefree integers generated by `P` gives

    A_P(S)g=sum_(n squarefree, p|n => p in P)
                 mu(n)n^(-1/2) S_(log n)g.                    (2.2)

Define the oppositely oriented *full-line* history

    A_P^-(U)g=sum_n mu(n)n^(-1/2) U_(-log n)g.                 (2.3)

It is essential that (2.3) is formed before compression. Its left tail contains all crossed
interferences between distinct divisors.

## 3. Multiprime leakage theorem

**Theorem 3.1 (exact Sonin leakage; PROVED).** For every finite prime set `P` and every `g`
satisfying (1.2),

    ||A_P(S)g||^2-||A_P(S)^*g||^2
       =||(I-P_T) A_P^-(U)g||^2 >=0.                          (3.1)

Equivalently,

    <g,[A_P(S)^*,A_P(S)]g>
       =||(I-P_T) A_P^-(U)g||^2.                             (3.2)

*Proof.* Apply (1.1) term by term to (2.2), and then use (1.2):

    C_log A_P(S)g
       =A_P^-(U) C_log g
       =-epsilon A_P^-(U)g.                                  (3.3)

Since `C_log` is unitary,

    ||A_P(S)g||^2=||A_P^-(U)g||^2.                            (3.4)

Split the last vector orthogonally at the physical edge. Because `g` is supported on the right,

    P_T A_P^-(U)g
       =sum_n mu(n)n^(-1/2) S_(log n)^*g
       =A_P(S)^*g.                                           (3.5)

Equations (3.4) and (3.5) give (3.1). Formula (3.2) is the same equality written as a quadratic
form. No crossed term has been removed. ∎

**Check: one prime.** If `P={p}`, the left tail in (3.1) is supported on `[T-log p,T)` and equals
`-r_p U_(-log p)g` there. Hence (3.1) reduces exactly to

    ||(I-r_pS_a)g||^2-||(I-r_pS_a^*)g||^2
       =r_p^2||1_[T,T+a)g||^2,

the collar identity R162_201 (3.2).

## 4. What the right side really contains

Let `N_P=product_(p in P)p`. The leakage is supported in `[T-log N_P,T)`, but it is not a sum of
independent prime collars. Explicitly,

    (I-P_T) A_P^-(U)g
      =(I-P_T) sum_(n|N_P) mu(n)n^(-1/2)U_(-log n)g,           (4.1)

so its square contains every pair `(m,n)` of squarefree divisors of `N_P`. Thus (3.1) keeps exactly
the global divisor coherence lost by arguments restricted to the first chamber `2,3,4`.

This also locates the role of the two orientations. Forward Euler stripping is the analytic
multiplier

    product_(p in P)(1-p^(-1/2-z)),

whereas the cosine transform turns the same history into the full backward translation (2.3).
Compression of that backward history produces its adjoint part plus the nonnegative exterior
square in (3.1).

## 5. Consequence for the open gate

For the corrected product port `g` of R162_201, the Euler-stripping bound is

    sup_P ||A_P(S)g|| < infinity.                              (5.1)

Theorem 3.1 rewrites it exactly as

    sup_P ( ||A_P(S)^*g||^2
            +||(I-P_T)A_P^-(U)g||^2 ) < infinity.             (5.2)

Therefore it is enough to obtain, from the finite oriented `(PC-ren)` Green identity, a common
upper bound for the adjoint interior history and the complete exterior leakage in (5.2). This is a
strictly sharper target than bounding prime collars one at a time: all mixed Möbius terms are
already assembled with their correct signs.

What (3.1) does **not** prove is (5.1). Positivity gives a lower comparison of the forward norm with
the adjoint norm, not an upper bound. Claiming closure from (3.1) alone would reverse the useful
inequality. The remaining step is to identify the `(PC-ren)` polar/Tate quadratic form with, or
bound it below by, the full right side of (5.2) while using the first-contact equality.

## 6. Falsifier audit

- **F1 (first contact).** Theorem 3.1 holds for every Sonin vector, so it cannot itself use the
  spectral first-contact condition. That condition must enter only in the upper bound requested in
  Section 5.
- **F2 (orientation).** The identity is not obtained by squaring an unoriented `(PC)` equation. It
  uses the intertwining `C_log U_a=U_(-a)C_log` before compression; the lost left tail is retained.
- **F3 (arithmetic).** The finite identity can be written for arbitrary chosen factors, but the
  exhaustion relevant to R162_201 is the actual prime Euler exhaustion of zeta. A
  Davenport--Heilbronn model has no such global port factorization. Finite modifications acquire
  the infinite divisor defect precisely when one tries to identify (5.2) with `(PC-ren)`.
- **F4 (linear limit).** Every statement above is a finite quadratic identity. No linear graph
  limit is used.
