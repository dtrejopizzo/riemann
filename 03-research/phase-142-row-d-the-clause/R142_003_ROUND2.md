### D.32.2 The geometric diagnosis, stated as a restatement

**Restatement D.32.1 (`RESTATEMENT` — classical geometry; `PROVED` — the arithmetic half).**
On a smooth projective surface `S` over an algebraically closed field, with `H` ample:

 (i) *(Riemann–Roch + Serre duality)* `h⁰(D) + h⁰(K_S−D) ≥ χ(𝒪_S) + ½D·(D−K_S)` — Hartshorne,
     *Algebraic Geometry*, GTM **52**, Ch. V, Theorem 1.6;
 (ii) *(the positive cone is filled by the effective cone)* if `D² > 0` and `D·H > 0` then `nD` is
      effective for `n ≫ 0` — Hartshorne, Ch. V, Lemma/Theorem 1.8; equivalently `D` is **big**,
      Lazarsfeld, *Positivity in Algebraic Geometry I*, Ergebnisse **48**, Springer 2004, §2.2;
 (iii) *(Hodge index)* Hartshorne, Ch. V, Theorem 1.9 — the theorem whose proof Paper 46 Remark 12.3a
      transports, with `H = 2K`.
 **[Numbering: V.1.9 is pinned by Paper 46 Remark 12.3a, which is an import of record. V.1.6 and V.1.8
 are cited from the same chapter and their numbers are to be re-verified against the book in Part III;
 the *statements* are standard and are what is used.]**

 (iv) *(the arithmetic half, proved here)* In `D^W` the analogue of (i) is **free** — D.30.5 gives
      (a) and (c) unconditionally, for every `χ`, with the **geometric** normalisation: the bound
      `I_∞(E,E) ≤ 2ab ≤ (\deg E)²/2 = (\deg E)²/I_∞(2K,2K)` is the surface inequality
      `D² ≤ (D·H)²/H²` at `H = 2K`. But the analogue of (ii) is **false**: by D.31.10–11, `R(E) > 0`
      and `\deg E > 0` do **not** imply that `E`, or any multiple of `E`, is `𝓝_∞`-equivalent to a
      nonzero effective class. The exact deficit is measured by
      `s(γ,θ) = \sup_u|\cos(γu+θ)|/\cosh(u/2) < 1`, whose geometric analogue is `1`.

**The diagnosis.** In geometry the effective cone **fills** the positive cone, so the support function
of the effective cone in any direction equals that of the positive cone. In `D^W`, effectivity means
*pointwise non-negativity of a density on the single real modulus line*, so `T(𝓔)` is a **moment cone**
— the image of the positive measures `E'(u)du` under `(\deg,(Φ_{E'}(ρ))_ρ)` — and a moment cone is
strictly thinner than the positive cone of the form: the degree weight `2\cosh(u/2)` grows while the
zero-datum weight `\cos(γu+θ)` oscillates, and the ratio of their suprema is exactly `s(γ,θ) < 1`.
**Lemma D.31.4 is the analogue of the nef/Castelnuovo pairing bound, with an extra strict factor that
geometry does not have.** The named cause: `D^W` **retains the intersection theory of `C × C` and
discards its linear systems** — it supplies far too few effective classes (radial, pointwise
non-negative densities on the modulus line) relative to how many classes `I_∞` declares positive; in
geometry the missing classes come from the rest of `NS`. Theorem D.31.12 says one cannot keep the first
without the second. `RESTATEMENT`.

*Consistency remark (heuristic, not a check).* One cannot "run the refutation for a curve over `F_q`
and watch it contradict Weil": the function-field zeta is not an admissible `Z` for this frame (the
modulus line is the lattice `\log q·Z`, not `R`, and the zero counting is not of the `(E3d)` type). The
**absence of a function-field model of `D^W`** is itself part of the diagnosis, and is recorded as
open item **O-23**.

### D.32.3 The two repair horns for phase 143, with the exact test each must pass

Both horns aim at the same target: a Theorem-12.3-type transfer whose hypotheses are **satisfiable** and
whose conclusion is still RH. Theorem 12.3's proof applies (b′) at `E = K − nF` (`\deg = 1`) and
`E = nF` (`\deg = 0`), and applies (c) **only** at `E = nF` (`\deg = 0`); D.30.10's Proof 2 applies the
clause at `rF + K` (`\deg = 1`). **Verified line by line, round 2** (D.31.14(iv)): the load-bearing
degrees are `0` and `1`, both far below `d_*`.

#### HORN A — weaken (c) by a defect supported above `d_*`

**Definition D.32.2.** For `Δ : R → R_{≥0}` with `Δ(0) = Δ(1) = 0`, let

    (c′)   h⁰(E) + h⁰(K−E) ≥ R(E) − Δ(\deg E)  for every E ∈ D^W ,

with `Δ` symmetric under `d ↦ 1−d` (so that (c′) respects the Serre symmetry `R(K−E) = R(E)`).

**Proposition D.32.3 (`PROVED`).** (i) **Theorem 12.3's proof is intact under (c′)**, for any `Δ` with
`Δ(0) = 0`: (c) is used only at `E = nF` with `\deg nF = 0`, so Step 3 still gives
`h⁰(nF) ≥ ½n²q + χ > 0`, and Steps 1,2,4 do not use (c) at all.
(ii) The **strongest** `Δ` that the D.31.10 family does not refute is bounded below by
`Δ(d) ≥ \tfrac{d²}{4}(1−s_*²) − \tfrac d4` for `d > d_*` (and `Δ ≡ 0` below `d_*` is then admissible),
because the witnesses `E_{t,d} ∉ 𝔐` have `R(E_{t,d})` ranging up to that value.
(iii) With `Δ(d) := \max\big(0,\ \tfrac{D²}{4}(1−s_*²) − \tfrac D4\big)`, `D := \max(d,1−d)`, the
**degree-gated** candidate `h⁰(E) = \max(R(E),0)·1_{\{0<\deg E ≤ d_*\}}` **fails (c′)** — at `E = dK`,
`d > d_*`, both gates are off while `R(E) = \tfrac{d²}4 − \tfrac d4 > Δ(d)` unless `s_* = 0`. *(This is
H1's counterexample doing useful work: the canonical ray is effective, so a degree gate switches `h⁰`
off exactly where `R` is largest and legitimately positive.)*
(iv) The **(★)-gated** candidate `h⁰_★(E) := \max(R(E),0)·1_{\{\deg E>0\}}·1_{\{E ∈ S_0^{(★)}\}}`
satisfies (c′) with that `Δ` **under RH**: if `\deg E > 1` and `E ∉ S_0^{(★)}`, some `F` with
`\deg F = 0` has `|I_∞(E,F)| > C_F\deg E = s_F\sqrt{W_F/2}\,\deg E`, and under RH Cauchy–Schwarz gives
