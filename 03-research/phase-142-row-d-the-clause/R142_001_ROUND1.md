## D.31.2 The sharp modulus bound at an on-line zero: Lemma D.24.6's constant is not sharp

This is the whole of the decision, and it is elementary.

**Definition D.31.3.** For `γ ∈ R` and `θ ∈ R` put

    s(γ,θ) := sup_{u∈R} \frac{|cos(γu+θ)|}{cosh(u/2)} ∈ (0,1] .

**Lemma D.31.4 (`PROVED`, unconditional, two lines).** Let `E' ∈ D^W`, `E' ≥ 0`. Then for every real
`γ, θ`,

    \Big|∫E'(u)\cos(γu+θ)\,du\Big| ≤ \tfrac12\,s(γ,θ)\,\deg E' .

In particular, if `ρ = ½+iγ` is a zero of `ξ` on the critical line, then for every `θ`

    |Re\big(e^{iθ}Φ_{E'}(ρ)\big)| ≤ \tfrac12 s(γ,θ)\deg E' ,
    and hence  |Φ_{E'}(ρ)| ≤ \tfrac12\Big(\max_θ s(γ,θ)\Big)\deg E' = \tfrac12\deg E' .   (D.31.1)

*Proof.* `|cos(γu+θ)| ≤ s(γ,θ)cosh(u/2)` pointwise by Definition D.31.3; integrate against `E' ≥ 0`
and use `deg E' = ∫E'·2cosh(u/2)du` (Theorem 11.13(i)). For the second display,
`Φ_{E'}(½+iγ) = ∫E'(u)e^{iγu}du`, so `Re(e^{iθ}Φ_{E'}(ρ)) = ∫E'(u)cos(γu+θ)du`. ∎ `PROVED`

**Proposition D.31.5 (`PROVED`; the sharpness gap, certified).** `s(γ,θ) < 1` for every `γ ≠ 0` and
every `θ ∉ πZ`, and at `θ = π/2`, `γ = γ_1`,

    s_1 := s(γ_1, π/2) = \sup_u \frac{|\sin(γ_1u)|}{\cosh(u/2)}
         = 0.99846016271880910923…,\qquad u^* = 0.110991563370513694… ,

with `1 − s_1² = 3.0773034635…×10^{−3}`.

*Proof.* `|cos(γu+θ)| ≤ 1 = cosh(0)` with equality in the numerator only on the discrete set
`u ∈ (πZ−θ)/γ`, which contains `0` iff `θ ∈ πZ`; off `u = 0` the denominator is `> 1`; and
`|cos|/cosh ≤ 1/cosh(u/2) → 0`, so the supremum is attained. The numerical value: the critical equation
is `γ_1\cos(γ_1u)\cosh(u/2) = ½\sin(γ_1u)\sinh(u/2)`; outside `|u| ≤ 0.4` the quotient is
`≤ 1/cosh(0.2) = 0.9803… < s_1`; inside, a `40`-digit scan plus one Newton refinement gives `u^*` and
`s_1` above. Certified by `scripts/certify_sF.py` (mpmath, `mp.dps = 40`). ∎ `PROVED`

> **What just happened.** Lemma D.24.6 gives, at `β = ½`, `|Φ_{E'}(ρ)| ≤ √(ab) ≤ ½deg E'`, and
> Corollary D.30.8(ii) calibrated `χ = 0` so that `R(E) > 0` forces exactly `|Φ_E(ρ)| < √(ab)`. The
> calibration is against the constant `√(ab)`. But `√(ab)` is **not sharp**: at an on-line zero the
> sharp constant in any fixed *direction* `θ` is `½s(γ,θ)deg E'` with `s(γ,θ) < 1`, and when `a = b`
> (so `√(ab) = ½deg E'`) the gap is a genuine factor `s_1 < 1`. **The gate `R > 0` at `χ = 0` is
> therefore strictly *wider* than the moment cone, not "exactly calibrated to it".** The rest of §D.31
> is the consequence.

**Remark D.31.5a (relation to Theorem 11.14 and to (★)).** Lemma D.31.4 is precisely (★) at the test
class `F_0` of §D.31.3, whose density is `w_{F_0}(u) = −2G\sin(γ_1u)` up to `10^{−91}`; then
`C_{F_0} = G·s_1` by Definition D.31.1. Using instead the upper bound (11.17),
`C_{F_0} ≤ |Φ_{F_0}(0)|+|Φ_{F_0}(1)|+Σ_ρ|Φ_{F_0}(1−ρ)| = 2G + O(10^{−91})`, one gets the condition

constant: (11.17) loses the factor `2` that the decision lives inside, and `s_1` is the further
`0.15%` that decides it. `PROVED`

    ∫E'(u)e^{u/2}du = a ,\quad ∫E'(u)e^{−u/2}du = b ,\quad ∫E'(u)e^{(ρ−½)u}du = z_ρ\ \ (∀ρ) .

This is Proposition D.24.9's problem. Two things are now known that were not:

 (i) **the necessary conditions include (★)**, i.e. `|Σ_ρ z_ρ\overline{Φ_F(ρ)} − (\text{polar})| ≤
     C_F(a+b)` for every `F ∈ D^W`, and by Theorem D.31.2 these are **exactly** the closed-convex-hull
     conditions: (M) with the requirement relaxed to `T(E') ∈ \overline{\mathrm{conv}}\,T(𝓔)` is
     **equivalent** to (★) plus `a+b > 0`. The remaining content of (M) over (★) is exactly the
     **non-convexity gap** between `𝔐` and its closed convex hull, plus the decay requirement;
 (ii) under RH, (M) is a **positive-definite interpolation problem**: with `ρ = ½+iγ`, the constraints
     read `\hat μ(−γ) = z_ρ` for the *finite positive* measure `dμ(u) = E'(u)du` (finite because
     `∫E' ≤ ∫E'2\cosh = a+b`), together with the two weighted masses. So (M) asks: **extend the
     partial function `γ_ρ ↦ z_ρ` from the set `Γ := \{γ_ρ\}` to a continuous positive-definite
     function on `R` with prescribed total mass and prescribed weighted moments at `±i/2`.**
