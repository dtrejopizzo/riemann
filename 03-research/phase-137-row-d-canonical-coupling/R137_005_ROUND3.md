## 1. N1 — the audit of G4 / R3 point 4 that the two previous rounds declined to do

### 1.1 (i) What `‖ι_Pφ_α‖²_{H_P^∘}` is, and what point 4's sum is (`PROVED`)

From `D0-FOUNDATIONS.md` §3, verbatim:

> `A_P` is the self-adjoint canonical operator of the positive von Mangoldt Hamiltonian
> `H_P = Σ_{p^k≤P²}(\log p)p^{−k/2}|v_{p,k}⟩⟨v_{p,k}|\,δ_{k\log p} ≥ 0` (G1). Its reproducing kernel is
> `K_{A_P}(z,w) = ∫_0^{ℓ_P}Y_P(t,w)^*H_P(t)Y_P(t,z)dt ⪰ 0` (G2).

> `H` is the constant/degree mode carrying the divergent trace, `⟨H,K_PH⟩ ∼ ½(\log P)²` (G4). It is
> **positive**: `[H,H]_{A_P} > 0` … the primitive projection is `Q_P = I − |\hat h_P⟩⟨\hat h_P|`.

> An **admissible source** … with all `φ_α ⊥ H` (so sources live on the primitive complement).

Three consequences, each elementary and each needed below.

**(a) The Gram energy of a source is exactly point 4's sum.** `H_P` is a sum of **rank-one positive
atoms** indexed by prime powers `p^k ≤ P²` with `k ≥ 1`, sitting at the modulus points `k\log p`, with
weights `w_{p,k} = (\log p)p^{−k/2} > 0`. Hence for any source profile `φ`,

    ‖ι_Pφ‖²_{H_P} \;=\; ∫_0^{ℓ_P}⟨φ(t),H_P(t)φ(t)⟩dt
      \;=\; Σ_{p^k≤P²}(\log p)\,p^{−k/2}\,\big|⟨v_{p,k},\hatφ(k\log p)⟩\big|²
      \;≤\; C\,Σ_{p}Σ_{k≥1}(\log p)\,p^{−k/2}\big|\hatφ(k\log p)\big|² .        (1.1)

With R3 point 4's core `S_a^∘ = \{|\hatφ(t)| ≤ C_Ne^{−at}(1+t)^{−N}\}`, `a > \tfrac14`, one has
`|\hatφ(k\log p)|² ≤ Cp^{−2ak}`, so the right-hand side of (1.1) is at most
`CΣ_pΣ_{k≥1}(\log p)p^{−k(1/2+2a)} < ∞` **exactly when `\tfrac12+2a > 1`**, i.e. `a > \tfrac14`, and the
bound is **independent of `P`** because the `P`-sum is a partial sum of a convergent series with
nonnegative terms. **So the auditor's identification is correct: point 4's sum *is* `‖ι_Pφ_α‖²_{H_P}`
up to a constant, and it is uniform in `P`. `PROVED`.**

*(Note in passing, because it is the only place the divergence could have hidden: the `k = 0` term is
absent from `H_P` by construction — the index set is `p^k ≤ P²` with `k ≥ 1` — and it is also exactly the
term whose presence would make the series diverge, since `Σ_p\log p = ∞`. The convergence of point 4's
sum is therefore not an accident of the decay `a > ¼` alone; it also uses that the diagonal atom is not
there. This matters for the `Ξ_a` run in §1.3 and nowhere else.)*

**(b) The shorting cannot spoil the bound (`PROVED`).** `H_P^∘` is the pole-relative space: either the
orthogonal complement of the pole line `H` with `Q_P = I − |\hat h_P⟩⟨\hat h_P|`, or the Feshbach/Schur
complement of the `H`–`H` block. In **both** readings the norm can only decrease:

 * orthogonal projection: `‖Q_Pu‖ ≤ ‖u‖`;
 * Schur complement of a positive form: if `K = \begin{pmatrix}K_{HH}&K_{H∘}\\K_{∘H}&K_{∘∘}\end{pmatrix}
   ⪰ 0` then `S := K_{∘∘} − K_{∘H}K_{HH}^{−1}K_{H∘} ⪯ K_{∘∘}`, so `‖u‖_S ≤ ‖u‖_{K_{∘∘}}`.

Hence `‖ι_Pφ‖²_{H_P^∘} ≤ ‖ι_Pφ‖²_{H_P} ≤ C_φ` uniformly in `P`. **`PROVED`.** This answers the
coordinator's (ii) in the negative: *the passage to the shorted space does not re-introduce the
`½(\log P)²` divergence into the source norms.* `D8.5b-i` §4's finding — that resolvents are not additive
and the Feshbach cross terms carry the divergence — is about the **assembly of the resolvent**, not about
the norm of a source vector, and it does not block point 4. Round 2's parenthetical was therefore wrong
on the mathematics it asserted, and I withdraw it.

**(c) Consequently the Montel premise *is* available — for the sourced channel matrix.** For
`φ_α ⊥ H` and `A_P` self-adjoint (G2),

    |G_P^∘(z)_{αβ}| = \big|⟨Q_P(A_P−z)^{−1}Q_Pι_Pφ_β,\;ι_Pφ_α⟩\big|
      ≤ \frac{‖ι_Pφ_α‖\,‖ι_Pφ_β‖}{|\mathrm{Im}\,z|} ≤ \frac{C_F}{|\mathrm{Im}\,z|},\qquad
      C_F := \max_α‖ι_Pφ_α‖²_{H_P} ,                                            (1.2)

uniformly in `P` by (a)+(b). **L1 holds, as R3 point 4 claims.** `PROVED`.

### 1.2 The fork: the bound and the identification are on different objects (`PROVED`)

(1.2) is a statement about `G_P^∘`, the **sourced compressed resolvent**. The chain needs, in addition,
that `G_P^∘ → G_Ξ^{G5}` on `U_-`. That is pre-R3 §3, "Check 4". And `CORRECTIONS-CONNES-R3.md` **point 1**
declares it illegitimate, verbatim:

> "**Arbitrary-pairing realization is illegitimate (old Theorem 5.3 / 'Check 4').** A resolvent is not
> additive; its Neumann expansion produces cross words `V_{v1}…V_{vr}` (convolutions of all orders) which
> `−ζ'/ζ = ΣΛ(n)n^{−s}` (a log-derivative of an Euler product) does **not** contain. **The cancellation

> **Vitali acts on the one-variable `M_P`**; `sq₋` is read off `K_Ξ` at the end."

**Proposition 1.1 (the fork; `PROVED`, internal to phase 65).** There are exactly two objects in the
phase-65 corpus to which the Vitali/Montel step can be applied, and **neither carries both premises**:

| object | uniform Montel bound | identification of the limit |
|---|---|---|
| `G_P^∘`, the sourced channel matrix (pre-R3 §2, §5) | **available**: (1.2), from G4 + R3 point 4 | **withdrawn** by R3 point 1 ("the cancellation was asserted") |
| `M_P = −∂_z\log D_P^∘ − E_P'/E_P`, the renormalized trace (R3 points 1, 3) | **not available and not listed**: `−∂_z\log D_P^∘ = Σ_jm_j/(z_j−z)` is `\mathrm{Tr}(A_P−z)^{−1}`, whose Nevanlinna measure is `Σ_jm_jδ_{z_j}` with total mass the **eigenvalue count** of `A_P`, divergent; and no source-frame bound bounds it, because `M_P` is not `⟨(A_P−z)^{−1}u,u⟩` for any vector `u` | **available**: the trace-log identity, R3 point 1 |

### 1.3 (iii) The `Ξ_a` falsifier, run on point 4 specifically (`PROVED`)

Substitute `Λ(n) \rightsquigarrow Λ(n)(n^a+n^{−a})`, `0 < a < ½`. The atoms of the Hamiltonian become
`w^{(a)}_{p,k} = (\log p)p^{−k/2}(p^{ka}+p^{−ka}) ≤ 2(\log p)p^{−k(1/2−a)}`, still **positive**, so
`H_P^{(a)} ⪰ 0` and G1, G2 (the canonical Gram identity `K = ∫Y^*HY ⪰ 0`, which is formal in `H ⪰ 0`)
transfer verbatim. For the source core `S_{a'}^∘`,

    Σ_pΣ_{k≥1}(\log p)\,p^{−k(1/2−a)}\,p^{−2a'k} < ∞ \iff \tfrac12 − a + 2a' > 1
      \iff a' > \tfrac14 + \tfrac a2 .

**So point 4 transfers to `Ξ_a` verbatim, with the source-core threshold moved from `a' > ¼` to
`a' > ¼ + a/2`; the core is still non-empty and the bound is still uniform in `P`.** `PROVED`.

**Consequence (`PROVED`).** Point 4 **cannot be the load-bearing step**: it holds for an object with
off-line zeros. This is consistent with §1.2 — the load-bearing step is not point 4 but the *fork*, and
the fork is a statement about which object carries which premise, not about the weights.

### 1.4 Where the chain must fail for `Ξ_a`, and why the falsifier cannot discharge the flag

Since G1, G2 and point 4 transfer, the falsifier must bite at G3, G4 or G5.

**G4 is the one that fails (`PROVED`).** G4 reads (`PROBLEM-STATEMENT.md`): "**The divergence is rank-one
and definite.** `\mathrm{Tr}K_P ∼ ½(\log P)²`, carried *entirely* by the constant/degree mode `H` (the
pole of `ζ` at `s = 1`) … The pole has definite (`+`) sign." For `Ξ_a` the polar set is
`\{−a,\,a,\,1−a,\,1+a\}` (Paper 46 §12.5 (S1), Lemma 12.4A) — **four** points, so the divergent block has
rank `≥ 2` and the rank-one projection `Q_P = I − |\hat h_P⟩⟨\hat h_P|` of `D0-FOUNDATIONS.md`
Definition (pole line) does **not** remove it. And `RH-PROOF.md` §2 uses rank-one-ness explicitly — "The
divergent `½(\log P)²` is the excluded `H–H` element" (this is the sentence round 2 restored under H7).
So `Ξ_a` fails a hypothesis the chain actually uses. `PROVED`.

**But that is not a defence of the chain, and it must not be recorded as one (`PROVED`).** "The falsifier
fails a hypothesis" means only that *the falsifier does not apply*; it is strictly weaker than "the
chain distinguishes `ξ` from `Ξ_a`". Worse:

**Observation 1.2 (the falsifier class is empty for this chain; `PROVED` in the class stated,
`OPEN` in general).** Consider the class in which the phase-65 chain's hypotheses make sense:
nonnegative von Mangoldt weights (G1), a functional equation, and a **simple** pole (G4). Inside the
family `F = ∏_jζ(s+a_j)^{m_j}` with `m_j > 0` and real `a_j` — the family the corpus's falsifiers are
drawn from — one has `−F'/F(s) = Σ_nΛ(n)\big(Σ_jm_jn^{−a_j}\big)n^{−s}` with nonnegative coefficients,
and `F` has one pole per distinct `a_j`. A **simple** pole forces a single `a_j`, and the functional
equation `F(s) = F(1−s)` then forces `a_j = 0`, i.e. `F = ζ^m`, whose zeros are those of `ζ`.
**So this family contains no object with nonnegative weights, a simple pole and off-line zeros:
the `Ξ_a` test is *vacuous* against the phase-65 chain, not passed by it.** Whether some object outside
this family qualifies (a degree-1 `L`-function with a simple pole, nonnegative `Λ`-weights and a known
off-line zero) is `OPEN`, and I know of none. `PROVED` / `OPEN` as stated.

**Consequence (`FLAGGED`, not fixed).** The corpus's faithfulness test cannot
adjudicate this chain. The verdict therefore rests entirely on §1.2 — the fork — which is internal
mathematics and does not need a falsifier. That is the right place for it to rest, and it is why
outcome (B) is **not** reached: the chain is not complete modulo G1–G5, because the object that has the
uniform bound is not the object that has the identification.
