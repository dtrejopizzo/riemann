# 106.12 — The moving co-Poisson radical and the exact prolate leakage gate

## Purpose

The preceding residual audit compared the CCM vector with Riemann's fixed
kernel \(k=\mathcal E(h)\). That comparison leaves an in-band Meixner error
of order \(\lambda^{-1/2}\).  There is a sharper coordinate which removes
that error completely.

For every value of \(\lambda\), use the **moving** co-Poisson vector

\[
 F_\lambda=\mathcal E(f_\lambda),
 \qquad
 \mathcal E(f)(u)=u^{1/2}\sum_{n\geq1}f(nu),
 \tag{1}
\]

and then restrict it to

\[
 I_\lambda=[\lambda^{-1},\lambda],
 \qquad P_I=\mathbf1_{I_\lambda}.
 \tag{2}
\]

The complete co-Poisson vector is in the polarized radical of the global
Weil form.  Consequently the semilocal Weil value of \(P_IF_\lambda\) is
exactly the Weil value of the exterior leakage.  This is a joint identity:
the Gamma factor, every ordinary prime power, the two polar evaluations and
the boundary terms have already cancelled before an estimate is made.

The main conclusions are:

1. the polynomial Meixner error is absent from the exact form identity;
2. the exterior error is generated by the prolate angle defect;
3. an exact codimension-two prolate vector has leakage on the scale
   \(d_4\asymp\lambda^9e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}\);
4. the first two constrained angle levels have ratio
   \(d_4/d_8\asymp\lambda^{-8}\), which is more than sufficient for the
   Paley--Wiener weight in Gate B;
5. the missing statement is now one precise ordinary-prime inequality:
   comparison of the induced Weil leakage form with the prolate angle form,
   together with exclusion of a lower negative branch.

The exact identities are proved below.  The comparison in item 5 is not
proved by the audited sources and is isolated as the new gate.

## 1. Source normalization

Let

\[
 \widehat G(z)=\int_{\mathbb R_+^*}G(u)u^{-iz}\,d^*u.
 \tag{3}
\]

The zero-side definition used by Connes--Consani--Moscovici is

\[
 QW(G,H)=\sum_{1/2+is\in Z}
       \overline{\widehat G(\bar s)}\,\widehat H(s).
 \tag{4}
\]

See `Spectraltriples.tex`, lines 169--175.  The same source states that the
range of \(\mathcal E\) on the codimension-two Schwartz space is contained
in the radical, and records the Poisson identity; see lines 181--188 and
699--708.

The finite semilocal form is, without separating its terms,

\[
 QW_\lambda(G,H)
 =W_{0,2}(G^**H)-W_{\mathbb R}(G^**H)
  -\sum_{1<n\leq\lambda^2}
    \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
    \bigl((G^**H)(n)+(G^**H)(n^{-1})\bigr).
 \tag{5}
\]

In operator form its diagonal is the Gamma multiplier, the polar rank-two
term and the finite ordinary-prime translation block; see
`Spectraltriples.tex`, lines 254--298, or the 2025 CCM source, lines
530--540.

Connes' semilocal trace formula keeps the local terms coupled:

\[
 -\sum_{v\in S}W_v(H)
 =\log(TW)H(1)
  +\mathrm{Tr}\,\!\left(
       \vartheta(H)(1-P_T^S-\widehat P_W^S)\right).
 \tag{6}
\]

This is equation (weilqb), lines 1228--1261 of
`arXiv-2602.04022v1.tex`.  Formula (6), rather than a separate Gamma and
prime estimate, is the appropriate operator coordinate for the identity
below.

## 2. A BV extension of the co-Poisson radical

The prolate eigenfunctions used by CCM are smooth on the closed interval
\([ -\lambda,\lambda]\) and are extended by zero.  They are therefore
compactly supported BV functions, but are not literally Schwartz at the
two endpoints.  The following elementary extension removes that
technical ambiguity.

### Theorem 1 — BV co-Poisson continuation

Let \(f\) be an even, compactly supported BV function on \(\mathbb R\),
supported in \([ -\lambda,\lambda]\), and assume

\[
 \int_0^\infty f(t)\,dt=0.
 \tag{7}
\]

Put \(F=\mathcal E(f)\). Then:

1. \(F(u)=0\) for \(u>\lambda\) and

   \[
   |F(u)|\leq u^{1/2}\mathrm{Var}(f),
   \qquad u>0;
   \tag{8}
   \]

2. \(F\in L^2(\mathbb R_+^*,d^*u)\);
3. with

   \[
   \mathcal M F(s)=\int_0^\infty F(u)u^{s-1}\,du,
   \tag{9}
   \]

   one has throughout \(\Re s>-1/2\)

   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal M F(s)
     =\zeta(s+\tfrac12)\,
       \mathcal M f(s+\tfrac12);}
   \tag{10}
   \]

4. in particular,

   \[
   \mathcal M F(\rho-\tfrac12)=0
   \qquad\text{and}\qquad
   \mathcal M F(\tfrac12-\rho)=0
   \tag{11}
   \]

   for every nontrivial zero \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\).

#### Proof

Partition the positive half-line into the half-open intervals
\(I_n=((n-1)u,nu]\). Since the integral of \(f\) is zero,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|u\sum_{n\geq1}f(nu)\right|
 &=\left|\sum_{n\geq1}
     \left(uf(nu)-\int_{I_n}f(t)\,dt\right)\right| \\
 &\leq \sum_{n\geq1}\int_{I_n}|f(nu)-f(t)|\,dt \\
 &\leq u\sum_{n\geq1}\mathrm{Var}_{I_n}(f)
 \leq u\mathrm{Var}(f).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{12}
\]

Multiplication by \(u^{-1/2}\) gives (8). Compact support gives the upper
cutoff, while (8) gives square integrability at zero.

For \(\Re s>1/2\), absolute summation and the substitution \(t=nu\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal M F(s)
 &=\sum_{n\geq1}\int_0^\infty
      u^{s-1/2}f(nu)\,du  \\
 &=\sum_{n\geq1}n^{-s-1/2}
      \int_0^\infty t^{s-1/2}f(t)\,dt,
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

which is (10) in that half-plane. By (8), the left side is holomorphic
for \(\Re s>-1/2\). The Mellin transform of \(f\) is holomorphic in the
same range after the shift. The possible pole of \(\zeta(s+1/2)\) at
\(s=1/2\) is removed by (7). The identity theorem proves (10) throughout
the asserted half-plane.  The first equality in (11) follows from
\(\zeta(\rho)=0\), and the second from \(\zeta(1-\rho)=0\).

Equivalently, in the Fourier convention (3),

\[
 \widehat F(t)
 =\zeta(\tfrac12-it)\,\mathcal M f(\tfrac12-it).
 \tag{11a}
\]

If \(1/2+it=\rho\), the zeta factor in (11a) is
\(\zeta(1-\rho)=0\). This is the convention used directly in (4).
\(\square\)

### Remark 1 — Why the \(u^{1/2}\) tail is admissible

Condition \(f(0)=0\) is not needed for Theorem 1. If only the zero-mass
condition (7) is imposed, Poisson summation gives

\[
 \mathcal E(f)(u)
 =\mathcal E(\widehat f)(u^{-1})
  -\frac12u^{1/2}f(0).
 \tag{14}
\]

For a zero-extended BV function this formula is read with the standard
midpoint convention at jump points. Endpoint values form a null set for
the \(L^2\) and Mellin identities used below, so this convention changes
none of the stated forms.

The last term is not to be discarded. On \((0,\lambda^{-1})\) its
\(L^2(d^*u)\) norm squared is

\[
 \frac{|f(0)|^2}{4\lambda}.
 \tag{15}
\]

Its Mellin values decay like \(1/|\rho|\), so its zero-side Weil square is
finite because

\[
 \sum_\rho |\rho|^{-2}<\infty.
 \tag{16}
\]

Thus this term remains inside the coupled identity.  It does not invalidate
the form, but its size must be recorded.

## 3. Exact semilocal leakage identity

Let

\[
 V_\lambda=P_I F,
 \qquad
 R_\lambda=(1-P_I)F.
 \tag{17}
\]

On \(I_\lambda\), \(V_\lambda\) is piecewise smooth with only finitely many
break points, hence belongs to the semilocal Weil-form core described in
the CCM source. The zero-side series of two compactly supported piecewise
smooth vectors is absolutely convergent: each transform is
\(O(|s|^{-1})\), while the zero density is \(O(\log |s|)\).

### Theorem 2 — Moving radical identity

For every semilocal core vector \(G\) supported in \(I_\lambda\),

\[
 \boxed{
 QW_\lambda(V_\lambda,G)
   =-QW(R_\lambda,G).}
 \tag{18}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{
 QW_\lambda(V_\lambda,V_\lambda)
   =QW(R_\lambda,R_\lambda).}
 \tag{19}
\]

Here the right sides are defined by their absolutely convergent zero-side
values; at every zero the transform of \(R_\lambda\) is the negative of
the transform of \(V_\lambda\).

#### Proof

Equation (11) says that the transform of \(F=V_\lambda+R_\lambda\)
vanishes at every member of the complete nontrivial divisor.  Substitution
in (4) gives (18).  Taking \(G=V_\lambda\), and then using the same
vanishing in the first variable, gives

\[
 QW(V_\lambda,V_\lambda)
 =-QW(R_\lambda,V_\lambda)
 =QW(R_\lambda,R_\lambda).
 \tag{20}
\]

\(\square\)

### Corollary 3 — Exact joint prime--Gamma cancellation

Let \(H=V_\lambda^* * G\), choose the finite set \(S\) containing the
archimedean place and every prime whose powers meet the support of \(H\),
and take any admissible pair \(T,W\) in (6). Since both factors are supported in
\(I_\lambda\), the multiplicative-convolution support lies in
\([\lambda^{-2},\lambda^2]\), and the global form on this core equals its
finite semilocal expression. Thus (18) is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
 W_{0,2}(H)
 +\log(TW)H(1)
 +\mathrm{Tr}\,\!\left(
   \vartheta(H)(1-P_T^S-\widehat P_W^S)\right)
 =-QW(R_\lambda,G).}
\tag{21}
\]

No triangle inequality has separated the prime and Gamma terms in (21).
It is therefore the strongest exact cancellation identity supplied by the
co-Poisson and semilocal-trace frameworks.

## 4. Exterior leakage in additive prolate coordinates

Let \(P_\lambda\) now denote multiplication by
\(\mathbf1_{[-\lambda,\lambda]}\) on the additive line and put

\[
 \ell_f=(1-P_\lambda)\widehat f.
 \tag{22}
\]

Since \(f=P_\lambda f\), the upper component of \(R_\lambda\) is zero.
For \(0<u<\lambda^{-1}\), (14) and the support of
\(P_\lambda\widehat f\) give the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
 R_\lambda(u)
 =\mathcal E(\ell_f)(u^{-1})
  -\frac12u^{1/2}f(0),
 \qquad 0<u<\lambda^{-1}.}
\tag{23}
\]

If \(f(0)=0\) as well as \(\int f=0\), (23) contains only the Fourier
leakage.  This is the clean codimension-two coordinate.

## 5. The exact codimension-two prolate vector

Let \(\psi_{j,\lambda}\), \(j=0,4,8,\ldots\), be normalized even prolate
functions, extended by zero outside \([-\lambda,\lambda]\), in the Fourier
(+1) sector.  Write

\[
 P_\lambda\widehat\psi_{j,\lambda}
   =\chi_j(\lambda)\psi_{j,\lambda},
 \qquad 0<\chi_8<\chi_4<\chi_0<1.
 \tag{24}
\]

CCM record the compressed-Fourier identity and

\[
 \int\psi_{j,\lambda}
 =\chi_j(\lambda)\psi_{j,\lambda}(0)
 \tag{25}
\]

at lines 1324--1335 of the 2025 source.

Put

\[
 a_j=\psi_{j,\lambda}(0),
 \qquad b_j=\int\psi_{j,\lambda}=\chi_j a_j.
 \tag{26}
\]

The vector of cofactors

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_0&=a_4b_8-a_8b_4,\\
 c_4&=a_8b_0-a_0b_8,\\
 c_8&=a_0b_4-a_4b_0
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

is orthogonal to both rows \((a_0,a_4,a_8)\) and
\((b_0,b_4,b_8)\). Hence, after normalization,

\[
 f_\lambda^{(0)}
 =c_0\psi_{0,\lambda}
  +c_4\psi_{4,\lambda}
  +c_8\psi_{8,\lambda}
 \tag{28}
\]

satisfies exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 f_\lambda^{(0)}(0)=0,
 \qquad \int f_\lambda^{(0)}=0.}
 \tag{29}
\]

Thus Theorems 1--2 and the clean form of (23) apply without a polar tail.

### Lemma 4 — Smooth \(\mathcal S_0\) regularization

For every \(\eta>0\) there exists an even

\[
 \widetilde f_{\lambda,\eta}\in
 C_c^\infty((-\lambda,\lambda))
 \tag{30}
\]

such that

\[
 \widetilde f_{\lambda,\eta}(0)=0,
 \qquad
 \int\widetilde f_{\lambda,\eta}=0,
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde f_{\lambda,\eta}-f_\lambda^{(0)}\|_2<\eta.
 \tag{31}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \|(1-P_\lambda)\widehat{\widetilde f}_{\lambda,\eta}\|_2
 \leq
 \|(1-P_\lambda)\widehat f_\lambda^{(0)}\|_2+\eta.
 \tag{32}
\]

#### Proof

Multiply \(f_\lambda^{(0)}\) by an even smooth cutoff which equals one on
a neighborhood of zero and outside two arbitrarily small endpoint
collars. The resulting \(L^2\) and \(L^1\) errors tend to zero with the
collar width.  Its value at zero remains zero.  Correct the small integral
error by subtracting a multiple of a fixed even bump supported in the
interior, vanishing at zero and having integral one.  This gives (30)--(31).
Plancherel and contractivity of \(1-P_\lambda\) give (32). \(\square\)

This proves literal membership in the codimension-two Schwartz space.  It
is an existence regularization; by itself it supplies no Weil-form norm,
because the Weil form is not continuous in the \(L^2\) norm.

## 6. Exact angle-defect size

For any orthonormal prolate combination

\[
 f=\sum_j c_j\psi_{j,\lambda},
 \tag{33}
\]

orthogonality both inside and outside the interval gives

\[
\boxed{
 \|(1-P_\lambda)\widehat f\|_2^2
 =\sum_j|c_j|^2(1-\chi_j^2).}
\tag{34}
\]

Indeed the full Fourier transforms are orthogonal, while their in-band
parts are \(\chi_j\psi_{j,\lambda}\) and are also orthogonal.

For the original two-mode CCM combination, normalized by

\[
 c_0\chi_0a_0+c_4\chi_4a_4=0,
 \tag{35}
\]

one also has

\[
 |f_\lambda(0)|
 =|c_0a_0|\left|1-\frac{\chi_0}{\chi_4}\right|
 \leq C(1-\chi_4).
 \tag{36}
\]

Thus the \(u^{1/2}\) correction in (23) is of second order relative to the
\(L^2\) Fourier leakage.

The fixed-order Slepian--Fuchs asymptotic gives, for the physical prolate
mode \(j\),

\[
 d_j(\lambda):=1-\chi_j(\lambda)^2
 \asymp C_j\lambda^{2j+1}e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}.
 \tag{37}
\]

The 2025 CCM source records explicitly for \(j=4\)

\[
 1-\chi_4(\lambda)
 \sim \frac{2^{14}}3\sqrt2\,\pi^5
       \lambda^9e^{-4\pi\lambda^2};
 \tag{38}
\]

see lines 1324--1326.  Monotonicity controls the lower fixed mode.

For completeness, put \(q_j=1-\chi_j\), so that
\(d_j=q_j(2-q_j)\asymp q_j\). In the variables \(x_j=a_jc_j\), the two
constraints are \(\sum x_j=\sum q_jx_j=0\). On the three modes
\(0,4,8\), a spanning constrained vector is

\[
 x=(q_4-q_8,\ q_8-q_0,\ q_0-q_4).
 \tag{39a}
\]

Fixed-order convergence to the corresponding Hermite values makes the
numbers \(|a_j|\) uniformly bounded above and away from zero. Moreover,

\[
 q_0\ll q_4\ll q_8,
 \qquad
 q_0/q_4=O(\lambda^{-8}),
 \qquad
 q_4/q_8=O(\lambda^{-8}).
 \tag{39b}
\]

Consequently \(\|x\|^2\asymp q_8^2\), while

\[
 \sum_{j\in\{0,4,8\}}q_j|x_j|^2
 \asymp q_4q_8^2.
 \tag{39c}
\]

Equivalence of the weighted \(x\)-norm with the coefficient norm, and
\(d_j\asymp q_j\), therefore prove

\[
\boxed{
 \|(1-P_\lambda)\widehat f_\lambda^{(0)}\|_2^2
 \asymp d_4
 \asymp \lambda^9e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}.}
\tag{40}
\]

The coefficient of the eighth mode is
\(O(q_4/q_8)=O(\lambda^{-8})\), so the extra mode enforces the two exact
moment conditions without changing the leading leakage scale.

## 7. The constrained prolate ladder

Use the four modes \(0,4,8,12\) and impose the two linear conditions in
(29). The resulting space has dimension two. On that space let

\[
 \mathfrak A_\lambda(f,g)
 =\left\langle
  (1-P_\lambda)\widehat f,
  (1-P_\lambda)\widehat g
 \right\rangle.
 \tag{41}
\]

### Theorem 5 — First two constrained angle levels

Let \(\alpha_{0,\lambda}\leq\alpha_{1,\lambda}\) be the two generalized
eigenvalues of \(\mathfrak A_\lambda\) relative to the \(L^2\) norm on the
codimension-two four-mode space.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \alpha_{0,\lambda}\asymp d_4,
 \qquad
 \alpha_{1,\lambda}\asymp d_8,}
 \tag{42}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\alpha_{0,\lambda}}{\alpha_{1,\lambda}}
 \asymp\lambda^{-8}.}
 \tag{43}
\]

#### Proof

Index the modes by \(r=0,1,2,3\), corresponding to physical indices
\(0,4,8,12\), and put

\[
 q_r=1-\chi_{4r},\qquad d_r=1-\chi_{4r}^2.
 \tag{44}
\]

Then \(d_r\asymp q_r\), and fixed-order asymptotics give

\[
 q_0/q_1,\ q_1/q_2,\ q_2/q_3=O(\lambda^{-8}).
 \tag{45}
\]

For \(x_r=a_{4r}c_r\), the constrained plane is

\[
 K_\lambda=\{x:\ \sum x_r=0,\ \sum q_rx_r=0\}.
 \tag{46}
\]

The values \(|a_{4r}|\) stay bounded above and away from zero, so the
coefficient norm and angle energy are uniformly comparable to
\(N(x)=\sum|x_r|^2\) and \(E(x)=\sum q_r|x_r|^2\).

For every \(x\in K_\lambda\), \(|x_0|^2\leq3\sum_{r=1}^3|x_r|^2\), whence
\(E(x)\geq(q_1/4)N(x)\). Conversely,

\[
 x^{(0)}=(q_1-q_2,q_2-q_0,q_0-q_1,0)\in K_\lambda
 \tag{47}
\]

has \(N(x^{(0)})\asymp q_2^2\) and
\(E(x^{(0)})\ll q_1q_2^2\). Thus \(\alpha_{0,\lambda}\asymp q_1\).

For every \(x\in K_\lambda\), the second constraint yields
\(|x_3|^2\leq3(q_2/q_3)^2\sum_{r=0}^2|x_r|^2\), hence
\(E(x)/N(x)\ll q_2\). Finally,

\[
 x^{(1)}=(0,q_2-q_3,q_3-q_1,q_1-q_2)\in K_\lambda
 \tag{47a}
\]

has \(N(x^{(1)})\asymp q_3^2\) and
\(E(x^{(1)})\gg q_2q_3^2\). Therefore
\(\alpha_{1,\lambda}\asymp q_2\). Since \(q_1\asymp d_4\) and
\(q_2\asymp d_8\), the min--max principle proves (42)--(43).
\(\square\)

The relevance to the curvature gate is immediate:

\[
 \lambda^{2B}\frac{d_4}{d_8}
 =O(\lambda^{2B-8})\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad B<\frac12.
 \tag{48}
\]

Thus the prolate ladder has much more polynomial separation than Gate B
requires.  What is missing is the transfer of this ladder to the actual
Weil operator.

## 8. The strongest proved Weil--angle statement

For an admissible \(f\), define the lower-tail transform

\[
 (\mathcal T_\lambda\ell_f)(s)
 :=\int_\lambda^\infty
       \mathcal E(\ell_f)(v)v^{-s}\,d^*v.
 \tag{49}
\]

In Mellin normalization, define the complete leakage coordinate at a zero
by

\[
 a_{\lambda,f}(\rho)
 :=\mathcal M R_\lambda(\tfrac12-\rho).
 \tag{50}
\]

Formula (23) gives the exact expression

\[
 \boxed{
 a_{\lambda,f}(\rho)
 =(\mathcal T_\lambda\ell_f)(\tfrac12-\rho)
 -\frac{f(0)}2\frac{\lambda^{\rho-1}}{1-\rho}.}
 \tag{50a}
\]

Indeed, the zero parameter \(s_\rho\) in (4) is determined by
\(1/2+is_\rho=\rho\), and hence
\(\widehat R_\lambda(s_\rho)=
\mathcal M R_\lambda(1/2-\rho)=a_{\lambda,f}(\rho)\).

For the exact codimension-two vector the second term is zero.  Theorems
1--2 give, without RH,

\[
\boxed{
 QW_\lambda(P_I\mathcal E f,P_I\mathcal E g)
 =\sum_\rho
   \overline{a_{\lambda,f}(1-\bar\rho)}
   a_{\lambda,g}(\rho),}
\tag{51}
\]

with the equivalent zero-parameter convention obtained directly from
(4).  On RH, (51) becomes the positive sampling square

\[
 QW_\lambda(P_I\mathcal E f,P_I\mathcal E f)
 =\sum_\gamma|a_{\lambda,f}(\tfrac12+i\gamma)|^2.
 \tag{52}
\]

Equations (21), (23), (34) and (51) are the strongest exact joint
prime--Gamma/prolate statements obtained from the audited sources.  They
do **not** imply a bound of the sampling norm in (52) by the \(L^2\) angle
norm in (34).

The reason is functional analytic and quantitative.  The co-Poisson map
is Mellin multiplication by \(\zeta(1/2-it)\), and the Weil form contains
the unbounded logarithmic Gamma multiplier. Consequently no \(L^2\)-only
continuity estimate

\[
 \|\mathcal T_\lambda\ell\|_{\text{Weil sampling}}
 \leq C\|\ell\|_2
 \tag{53}
\]

is furnished by Plancherel, by the prolate angle identity, or by the trace
formula. In operator language, if \(A_L\) is the semilocal Weil operator,

\[
\boxed{
 \|A_LP_I\mathcal E f\|_2
 =\sup_{\substack{G\in\mathrm{Dom}(A_L)\\\|G\|_2=1}}
 \left|QW(R_\lambda,G)\right|.}
\tag{54}
\]

The operator norm in (54) is legitimate, not merely formal. The compactly
supported piecewise-smooth vector \(P_I\mathcal E f\) has Fourier transform
\(O(|t|^{-1})\). Since the archimedean multiplier is
\(O(\log(2+|t|))\), its square times \(|t|^{-2}\) is integrable; the polar
and finite prime blocks are bounded. Hence
\(P_I\mathcal E f\in\mathrm{Dom}(A_L)\), and (18) identifies the
right side with the norm of its represented form functional.

The right side is a form-dual norm, not the \(L^2\) norm of the prolate
leakage.

## 9. The exact new arithmetic gate

The identity has reduced the required theorem to the following comparison.

### Gate CWA — Coupled Weil--angle comparison

There exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), independent of \(\lambda\), such
that on the fixed low-mode codimension-two prolate spaces,

\[
\boxed{
 c\,\mathfrak A_\lambda(f,f)
 \leq
 QW_\lambda(P_I\mathcal E f,P_I\mathcal E f)
 \leq
 C\,\mathfrak A_\lambda(f,f),}
\tag{55}
\]

together with two operator-level statements. If \(U_\lambda\) denotes the
two-dimensional moving co-Poisson space, write the closed Weil form in
Feshbach blocks \(\begin{psmallmatrix}A&B\\B^*&D\end{psmallmatrix}\)
relative to \(U_\lambda\oplus U_\lambda^\perp\). Here \(D\) is the
restricted closed form and \(BD^{-1}B^*\) denotes the associated
form-short; equivalently, the second inequality below asserts boundedness
of \(D^{-1/2}B^*A^{-1/2}\). The required statements are

\[
 D\geq c d_8 I,
 \qquad
 BD^{-1}B^*\leq\theta A
 \quad\text{for some fixed }\theta<1.
 \tag{55a}
\]

The second inequality is the required cross/form-dual control. A lower
bound on the orthogonal complement alone does not prevent the off-diagonal
block from lowering the first Schur-complement branch.

For clarity, an equivalent one-vector sufficient package can be stated
without block notation. Let \(q_L\) be the normalized first moving vector
and set

\[
 R_L=\langle A_Lq_L,q_L\rangle,\qquad
 b_L=(1-|q_L\rangle\langle q_L|)A_Lq_L,
\]

\[
 \beta_L=\inf_{\substack{v\perp q_L\\\|v\|=1}}
             \langle A_Lv,v\rangle.
 \tag{55b}
\]

It is sufficient to prove

\[
 R_L=O(d_4),\qquad \beta_L\ge c d_8,qquad
 \lambda^B\frac{\|b_L\|}{\beta_L-R_L}\longrightarrow0
 \quad(B<\tfrac12).
 \tag{55c}
\]

Indeed, min--max gives \(\lambda_2(A_L)\ge\beta_L\), while the scalar
Schur complement gives

\[
 0\le R_L-\epsilon_{0,L}
 \le\frac{\|b_L\|^2}{\beta_L-R_L}.
 \tag{55d}
\]

Thus (55c) is the quantitative branch-selection assertion in its narrowest
operator form. Gate CWA and (55a) are a stronger two-mode formulation of
the same required arithmetic control; low-space comparison without the
cross residual in (55c) is insufficient.

If Gate CWA, (55a), and the branch identification used in Gate B all hold
-- including normalization of the first moving constrained vector and
locally uniform convergence of its transform to \(\Xi\) --,
then the Schur complement \(A-BD^{-1}B^*\) is comparable to the prolate
angle form. Consequently (42)--(48) give

\[
 R_L-\epsilon_{0,L}
 =O(d_4),
 \qquad
 g_L^+\gg d_8,
 \qquad
 \lambda^{2B}\frac{R_L-\epsilon_{0,L}}{g_L^+}\to0,
 \tag{56}
\]

for every \(B<1/2\). This closes the weighted curvature gate conditionally
on precisely those three assertions; (55) alone does not.

The upper inequality in (55) is already a nontrivial signed
prime--Gamma estimate.  The lower inequality and branch separation are
the decisive arithmetic content.

### Mandatory falsifier

The angle form \(\mathfrak A_\lambda\) depends only on the additive Fourier
pair and is unchanged if the zeta divisor is replaced by a divisor
containing an off-line quartet.  The corresponding Weil form acquires an
exponentially negative mode. Therefore prolate concentration, Poisson
summation and abstract projection geometry alone cannot prove the
complement and Schur assertions (55a), identify the fundamental branch,
or promote (55) to the global gate: the same geometric argument would be
available for the off-line divisor. This falsifier does not by itself
disprove the low-space comparison (55); it shows that the branch-selection
part must contain arithmetic information absent from the angle form.

An admissible proof must use the specific ordinary-prime weights in the
coupled trace expression (21).  This is exactly where the arithmetic, and
not merely the signal geometry, enters.

## 10. Verdict

The moving-radical coordinate produces a real improvement:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{quantity}&\text{proved scale or status}\\ \hline
\text{Meixner in-band error}&\text{cancelled exactly}\\
\text{two-mode polar tail}&O(d_4)\text{ in amplitude}\\
\text{exact three-mode polar tail}&0\\
\text{Fourier leakage square}&d_4\asymp\lambda^9e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}\\
\text{next constrained angle level}&d_8\asymp\lambda^{17}e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}\\
\text{weighted angle ratio}&O(\lambda^{2B-8})\to0\\
\text{Weil--angle comparison}&\text{open}\\
\text{operator residual}&\text{not implied by }L^2\text{ leakage}.
\end{array}
\tag{57}
\]

Thus the remaining theorem is no longer an unspecified cancellation at an
arbitrary double-exponential scale.  It is the concrete two-sided coupled
inequality (55), at the first two fixed prolate levels, plus exclusion of a
lower even branch.  No statement in this document claims that Gate CWA, A1
or RH has been proved.
