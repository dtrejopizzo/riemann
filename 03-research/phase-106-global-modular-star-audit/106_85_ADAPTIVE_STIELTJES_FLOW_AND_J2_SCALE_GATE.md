# 106.85 — Adaptive Stieltjes flow and the \(j_2\)-scale gate

## Purpose and verdict

The augmented prime-block gain of 106.81 and the matched-filter identity
of 106.83 reduce a negative staircase pivot to three adaptive scalars

\[
 \delta=-\sigma_0>0,
 \qquad R=\|r\|^2,
 \qquad C=\|A^{-1/2}U^*r\|^2.                    \tag{1}
\]

This note calculates the complete block-strength flow generated by those
quantities.  The calculation gives an exact positive Stieltjes measure
\(\nu\) for which

\[
 \sigma_1-\sigma_0
 =\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{d\nu(s)}{1+s},
 \qquad
 R=\int d\nu(s),
 \qquad
 C=\int s\,d\nu(s).                              \tag{2}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_1-\sigma_0\ge \frac{R^2}{R+C}.}           \tag{3}
\]

The bound is optimal among all conclusions that use only \((R,C)\).
Thus the scalar inequality

\[
 \boxed{R^2-\delta(R+C)>0}                        \tag{4}
\]

is the sharp universal two-moment certificate for crossing.

Expanding (4) with the literal von Mangoldt atoms gives a positive-weight
double sum, but not the Dirichlet convolution
\(j_2=\delta_{\log}\Lambda+\Lambda*\Lambda\).  The adaptive kernel depends
separately on both factors, on the preceding prime--Gamma inverse \(A^{-1}\),
and on the selected residual \(q^*\).  Coefficient positivity of \(j_2\)
therefore supplies no sign for (4).  An explicit one-atom counterexample
shows that no such coefficientwise implication exists.

The surviving arithmetic theorem is precise: for every negative adaptive
pivot one must find a finite ordinary-prime block whose Stieltjes mass
satisfies the exact inequality in (40) below, or whose first two moments
satisfy (41), before theta attenuation exhausts the available mass.

## 1. Block-strength Schur flow

Let

\[
 H_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}A&c\\c^*&h\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad A\succ0,
 \qquad
 \sigma_0=h-c^*A^{-1}c=-\delta<0.                \tag{5}
\]

For one finite literal block, write its feature matrix as

\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathcal B}=[\,U\ \ v\,],            \tag{6}
\]

where \(U\) acts on the preceding \(M-1\) modes and \(v\) is the feature
of the new mode.  Put

\[
 a=A^{-1}c,
 \qquad r=v-Ua.                                   \tag{7}
\]

Scale the whole block by \(t\in[0,1]\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_t&=A+tU^*U,& c_t&=c+tU^*v,\\
 h_t&=h+t\|v\|^2,& a_t&=A_t^{-1}c_t,\\
 r_t&=v-Ua_t,&
 \sigma_t&=h_t-c_t^*A_t^{-1}c_t.
\end{aligned}                                    \tag{8}
\]

### Theorem 1 — Exact adaptive differential system

For every \(t\in[0,1]\),

\[
 \boxed{
 a_t'=A_t^{-1}U^*r_t,
 \qquad
 r_t'=-UA_t^{-1}U^*r_t,}                          \tag{9}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_t'=R_t:=\|r_t\|^2,
 \qquad
 \sigma_t''=-2C_t,
 \qquad
 C_t:=\|A_t^{-1/2}U^*r_t\|^2.}                   \tag{10}
\]

#### Proof

The normal equation is \(A_ta_t=c_t\).  Differentiating it gives

\[
 A_ta_t'=U^*v-U^*Ua_t=U^*r_t,
\]

which proves the first identity in (9).  The second follows by
differentiating \(r_t=v-Ua_t\).

Differentiate the Schur complement in (8).  The terms containing
\(a_t'\) cancel by the normal equation, leaving

\[
 \sigma_t'
 =\|v\|^2-2\operatorname {Re}\langle Ua_t,v\rangle
   +\|Ua_t\|^2
 =\|r_t\|^2.
\]

Finally, (9) gives

\[
 R_t'
 =2\operatorname {Re}\langle r_t,r_t'\rangle
 =-2\langle U^*r_t,A_t^{-1}U^*r_t\rangle
 =-2C_t.
\]

Since \(R_t'=\sigma_t''\), this proves (10). \(\square\)

The gain is positive and concave as a function of block strength.  This
concavity is an adaptive regression effect; it is not coefficient
positivity of an arithmetic convolution.

## 2. Exact Stieltjes representation

Set

\[
 B=UA^{-1}U^*\succeq0                              \tag{11}
\]

on the observation space, and let \(E_B\) be its spectral resolution.
Define the finite positive measure

\[
 \nu(\Omega)=\langle r,E_B(\Omega)r\rangle,
 \qquad \Omega\subset[0,\infty).                  \tag{12}
\]

### Theorem 2 — Adaptive Stieltjes theorem

The exact solutions of (9)--(10) are

\[
 r_t=(I+tB)^{-1}r,                                 \tag{13}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_t-\sigma_0
 =\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{t}{1+ts}\,d\nu(s),}      \tag{14}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 R_t=\int\frac{d\nu(s)}{(1+ts)^2},
 \qquad
 C_t=\int\frac{s\,d\nu(s)}{(1+ts)^3}.}           \tag{15}
\]

In particular, (2) holds.  Moreover \(R_t\) is completely monotone:

\[
 \boxed{
 (-1)^kR_t^{(k)}
 =(k+1)!\int
   \frac{s^k}{(1+ts)^{k+2}}\,d\nu(s)\ge0
 \qquad(k\ge0).}                                  \tag{16}
\]

#### Proof

The Woodbury identity gives

\[
 I-tU(A+tU^*U)^{-1}U^*=(I+tB)^{-1}.               \tag{17}
\]

Since

\[
 a_t-a=tA_t^{-1}U^*r,
\]

equation (17) proves (13).  Functional calculus then gives the first
formula in (15).  Also

\[
 UA_t^{-1}U^*=B(I+tB)^{-1},                       \tag{18}
\]

so the definition of \(C_t\), followed by (13), gives the second formula
in (15).  Integrating \(\sigma_t'=R_t\) yields

\[
 \int_0^t(1+us)^{-2}\,du=\frac{t}{1+ts},
\]

which proves (14).  Repeated differentiation of the first integral in
(15) proves (16). \(\square\)

At \(t=1\), (14) is exactly the augmented Kalman gain

\[
 \Delta_{\mathcal B}
 =r^*(I+B)^{-1}r
 =\int\frac{d\nu(s)}{1+s}.                        \tag{19}
\]

Thus \(\nu\) records how the new adaptive response is distributed among
the old regression channels.  Large \(s\) means that a response can be
removed cheaply by changing old coefficients; small \(s\) is genuine
new-direction energy.

## 3. Optimal two-moment lower bound

### Theorem 3 — Sharpness of the directional certificate

For every finite positive measure \(\nu\) with

\[
 R=\int d\nu>0,
 \qquad C=\int s\,d\nu<\infty,                    \tag{20}
\]

one has

\[
 \boxed{
 \int\frac{d\nu(s)}{1+s}\ge\frac{R^2}{R+C}.}      \tag{21}
\]

Equality holds if and only if \(\nu\) is supported at one point, apart
from the vacuous zero-mass case.  Conversely, every pair \(R>0\),
\(C\ge0\) is realized by a one-dimensional adaptive block for which
equality holds.  Hence no universally larger lower bound can be deduced
from \((R,C)\) alone.

#### Proof

Normalize \(d\pi=R^{-1}d\nu\).  The function

\[
 f(s)=\frac1{1+s}
\]

is strictly convex on \([0,\infty)\).  Jensen's inequality gives

\[
 \frac1R\int f(s)\,d\nu(s)
 \ge f\!\left(\frac CR\right)
 =\frac{R}{R+C},
\]

which is (21).  Strict convexity gives the equality statement.

For sharpness, take a one-dimensional old space with \(A=1\), a
one-dimensional observation space, \(r=\sqrt R\), and

\[
 U=\sqrt{C/R}.
\]

Then \(B=C/R\), the spectral measure is
\(R\delta_{C/R}\), and its gain is exactly \(R^2/(R+C)\). \(\square\)

Combining (19) and (21), a negative pivot \(\sigma_0=-\delta\) crosses
whenever (4) holds.  More precisely, (4) is necessary and sufficient for
crossing to be guaranteed uniformly over all adaptive blocks sharing the
three scalar data \((\delta,R,C)\).  It is sufficient, but need not be
necessary, for a particular block whose measure \(\nu\) has more than one
spectral point.

## 4. Literal von Mangoldt expansion

Let \(\mathcal B\) be a finite set of prime powers and put

\[
 w_m=\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\sqrt m}>0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_{\mathcal B}
 =\bigoplus_{m\in\mathcal B}\sqrt{w_m}\,D_m.       \tag{22}
\]

For the adaptive residual \(q^*\), define

\[
 d_m=D_mq^*,
 \qquad
 e_m=\|d_m\|^2,                                    \tag{23}
\]

and, in the coefficient space of the old modes,

\[
 z_m=(D_m|_{V_{M-1}})^*d_m.                       \tag{24}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{R=\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}w_me_m,}           \tag{25}
\]

whereas

\[
\boxed{
 C=
 \left\|\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}
     w_mA^{-1/2}z_m\right\|^2
 =\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal B}w_mw_n
   \langle z_m,A^{-1}z_n\rangle.}                 \tag{26}
\]

The double sum in (26) is real because it is a squared norm; equivalently
one may place a real part around its displayed Hermitian sum.

Substitution in (4) gives the exact literal scalar polynomial

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal F_{\mathcal B}(q^*)
 :={}&R^2-\delta(R+C)\\
 ={}&\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal B}w_mw_n
 \left{
 e_me_n-\delta\langle z_m,A^{-1}z_n\rangle
 \right}
 -\delta\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}w_me_m.
\end{aligned}}                                     \tag{27}
\]

This is the scalar adaptive object whose positivity is observed in the
stable finite-head diagnostics.  Unlike the failed matrix polarization,
the cross term in (27) is a Hermitian Gram sum.  Its sign inside
\(\mathcal F_{\mathcal B}\), however, is negative.

## 5. Why this is not the positive jet \(j_2\)

The arithmetic convolution has the schematic form

\[
 j_2(k)
 =(\delta_{\log}\Lambda)(k)
 +\sum_{mn=k}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n).                \tag{28}
\]

After grouping the quadratic part of (27) by \(k=mn\), one instead gets

\[
 \sum_k\frac1{\sqrt k}
 \sum_{mn=k}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)
 \mathcal K_{q^*,A}(m,n),                         \tag{29}
\]

where

\[
 \mathcal K_{q^*,A}(m,n)
 =e_me_n-\delta\langle z_m,A^{-1}z_n\rangle.       \tag{30}
\]

There are three exact mismatches.

1.  The kernel in (30) depends separately on the ordered factor pair
    \((m,n)\), not only on its product \(mn\).  It therefore cannot be
    extracted from a Dirichlet-convolution fibre.

2.  The inverse \(A^{-1}\) contains the previously accumulated Gamma,
    polar, and prime contributions.  It is adaptive and changes from one
    staircase row to the next.

3.  The linear term in (27) is

    \[
     -\delta\sum_m\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\sqrt m}e_m,   \tag{31}
    \]

    not a lift of \((\log m)\Lambda(m)\).  The Schur deficit \(\delta\)
    is a global regression scalar, not the logarithmic derivation.

Therefore the curvature identity

\[
 \sigma_t''=-2C_t                                  \tag{32}
\]

is a positive two-index Gram identity, but it is not a realization of
\(\Lambda*\Lambda\), and adding \(\sigma_t'\) does not turn it into
\(j_2\).  This corrects the possible interpretation left open at the end
of 106.83.  It also agrees with the established gates:

* 106.52 shows that the projected Riccati identity pays a position-leakage
  term;
* 106.54 shows that the primitive \(j_2\) composition overcounts the
  physical two-step kernel by a positive intermediate-position defect;
* 106.55 shows that the primitive term and that defect have no separate
  cutoff-free limits;
* 106.60 gives a literal one-tower negative polarization and proves that an
  independent positive \(j_2\) square cannot be the missing curvature.

No matrix positivity is inferred from coefficient positivity here.

## 6. Explicit counterexample to coefficientwise closure

The failure is visible before any limiting issue.  Let

\[
 H_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.        \tag{33}
\]

Then \(A=1\), \(q^*=e_2\), and \(\delta=1\).  Label one positive atom by
the ordinary prime \(2\), with its literal coefficient

\[
 w_2=\frac{\log2}{\sqrt2}.                         \tag{34}
\]

Choose its one-dimensional feature to vanish on the old coordinate and
to satisfy

\[
 D_2e_1=0,
 \qquad
 D_2e_2=(2w_2)^{-1/2}.                             \tag{35}
\]

Thus the positive block is

\[
 w_2D_2^*D_2
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix}.         \tag{36}
\]

For this block,

\[
 R=\frac12,
 \qquad C=0,
 \qquad
 R^2-\delta(R+C)=-\frac14<0,                      \tag{37}
\]

and

\[
 \sigma_1=-1+\frac12=-\frac12<0.                 \tag{38}
\]

Nevertheless the ordinary arithmetic coefficient satisfies

\[
 j_2(2)=(\log2)^2>0.                               \tag{39}
\]

This example does not model the full Riemann theta feature, and it is not
a counterexample to the desired ordinary-prime inequality.  It proves the
precise algebraic point: the sign \(j_2(n)\ge0\), even with a literal
ordinary-prime coefficient and a positive atom, does not imply the
adaptive crossing inequality.  Any successful proof must use the actual
theta placement and the adaptive relation with the preceding Gamma--prime
head.

## 7. The surviving arithmetic target

For each negative adaptive pivot \((A,q^*,\delta)\), the exact scalar
target is to find a finite block \(\mathcal B\) such that

\[
\boxed{
 \int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{d\nu_{\mathcal B}(s)}{1+s}
 >\delta.}                                         \tag{40}
\]

The sharp universal two-moment sufficient form is

\[
\boxed{
 \left(\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}w_me_m\right)^2
 >\delta\left[
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal B}w_me_m
 +\left\|\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}
       w_mA^{-1/2}z_m\right\|^2
 \right].}                                         \tag{41}
\]

Equation (41) is exactly \(\mathcal F_{\mathcal B}(q^*)>0\).  It keeps
together the real von Mangoldt weights, literal theta displacement
features, signed prime--Gamma deficit, adaptive old-mode regression, and
all cross-atom interference terms.

It also identifies the required quantitative balance.  By the
large-prime estimate of 106.73--106.81, blocks starting at \(P\) have at
most

\[
 C_{M,X_0}(\log P)P^{2+b}e^{-2\pi P}              \tag{42}
\]

of unregularized response energy.  Hence a proof must establish (40) or
(41) in a finite block before this theta envelope falls below the deficit.
Qualitative nonvanishing at infinitely many primes is insufficient.

The Stieltjes theorem removes one ambiguity: no further scalar
rearrangement of \((R,C)\) can improve (3).  Progress must now come from
additional arithmetic information about the adaptive spectral measure
\(\nu_{\mathcal B}\), equivalently from a lower bound for the literal
quantity in (41).  Coefficientwise \(j_2\) positivity is not that
information.

## 8. Verification status

Equations (9)--(19) are finite-dimensional identities and use only the
spectral theorem for the positive matrix \(B\).  The optimality example
in Theorem 3 realizes equality exactly.  The counterexample
(33)--(39) is an exact rational matrix calculation apart from the displayed
positive normalization \(w_2\), which cancels in (36).
