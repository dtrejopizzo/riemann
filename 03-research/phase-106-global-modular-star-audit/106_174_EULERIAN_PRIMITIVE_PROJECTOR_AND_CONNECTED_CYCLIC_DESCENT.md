# 106.174 — Eulerian primitive projector and connected cyclic descent

## 1. Purpose

Document 106.173 constructs the finite-place localization on the
first-order spherical tangent.  The CCM object is a cyclic complex, so a
comparison must also explain why tensors involving several local
deviations do not create missing degree-one prime terms.

The answer is algebraic.  Normalized chains of a commutative spherical
Euler algebra carry the shuffle Hopf structure.  Its first Eulerian
idempotent is the convolution logarithm of the identity and projects
exactly onto connected, primitive chains.  The logarithmic derivative in
the explicit formula is the weighted character of this primitive part.
Consequently cross-prime products are removed before any completion or
positivity argument.

This note constructs the projector, proves its range, and extends the
local map of 106.173 from the tangent to the complete connected
finite-place cyclic sector.  The noncommutative CCM restriction cone still
has to be compared with this connected spherical quotient.

## 2. Completed Euler Hopf algebra

Let

\[
 \mathscr P
 =\bigoplus_p\bigoplus_{k\ge1}\mathscr T_{p,k}             \tag{1}
\]

be the algebraic direct sum of copies of the spherical tangent
\(\mathscr T_p\), one for each prime orbit and return number.  Return
number is a holonomy label, not an additional transverse degree.

Form the augmentation-completed symmetric algebra

\[
 \widehat{\mathscr E}
 =\prod_{m\ge0}\mathrm{Sym}^m(\mathscr P).             \tag{2}
\]

Give it the standard connected commutative and cocommutative Hopf
structure determined by

\[
 \Delta x=x\otimes1+1\otimes x,
 \qquad x\in\mathscr P.                                     \tag{3}
\]

Let \(\eta\), \(\epsilon\), and \(I\) denote unit, counit, and identity,
and put

\[
 N=I-\eta\epsilon.                                          \tag{4}
\]

For endomorphisms of \(\widehat{\mathscr E}\), convolution is

\[
 A\star B=m(A\otimes B)\Delta.                              \tag{5}
\]

The series

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak e_1=\log^\star I
 =\sum_{r\ge1}{(-1)^{r-1}\over r}N^{\star r}}               \tag{6}
\]

is well defined in the augmentation topology: on homogeneous degree
\(m\), only \(r\le m\) contributes.

### Theorem 2.1 — Primitive projection

On \(\widehat{\mathscr E}\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak e_1^2=\mathfrak e_1,
 \qquad
 \mathrm{Ran}\,\mathfrak e_1
 =\mathrm{Prim}(\widehat{\mathscr E})=\mathscr P.}    \tag{7}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \mathfrak e_1x=x\quad(x\in\mathscr P),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak e_1(x_1\cdots x_m)=0
 \quad(m\ge2, x_j\in\mathscr P).                           \tag{8}
\]

#### Proof

It is enough to work on a finite-dimensional subspace generated by the
finitely many primitive vectors occurring in a given homogeneous
polynomial.  There the Hopf algebra is a polynomial algebra.  For a
primitive \(x\),

\[
 \Delta^{(r-1)}x
 =\sum_{j=1}^r1^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes x\otimes
      1^{\otimes(r-j)},                                      \tag{9}
\]

so \(N^{\star r}x=0\) for \(r\ge2\), and (6) gives
\(\mathfrak e_1x=x\).

For the exponential group-like element

\[
 G(v)=\exp(v),\qquad v\in\mathscr P,                         \tag{10}
\]

one has \(\Delta G(v)=G(v)\otimes G(v)\).  Therefore convolution
of endomorphisms evaluated on \(G(v)\) becomes ordinary multiplication,
and

\[
 \mathfrak e_1G(v)
 =\log G(v)=v.                                               \tag{11}
\]

Comparing homogeneous coefficients in \(v\) proves (8).  Thus the range
is \(\mathscr P\), on which \(\mathfrak e_1\) is the identity; (7)
follows.  Passing to the augmentation completion is valid degree by
degree. \(\square\)

## 3. Relation with normalized cyclic chains

For a commutative algebra in characteristic zero, normalized Hochschild
chains carry the shuffle product and deconcatenation coproduct.  The
Eulerian idempotents give the Hodge decomposition.  Their precise
interaction with the mixed differential is

\[
 b e_n^{(i)}=e_{n-1}^{(i)}b,
 \qquad
 B e_n^{(i)}=e_{n+1}^{(i+1)}B.                              \tag{12}
\]

Thus the first Eulerian idempotent is a Hochschild/Harrison projector, but
it is not by itself a projector of the complete mixed complex: Connes'
operator raises the Hodge index.  In this construction \(\mathfrak e_1\)
is therefore applied **after** the CCM cyclic diagonal trace has produced
the commutative Hopf algebra of orbit coefficients.  The phase/Bott mixed
complex remains untouched.  On finite-place orbit symbols the range is
exactly the primitive module (1).

This order is essential.  No claim is made that the first Eulerian
idempotent is a mixed-complex projector on the entire noncommutative
crossed product before passing to cyclic trace and fixed orbits.

## 4. The Euler product is group-like and its logarithm is primitive

Introduce formal orbit variables \(X_{p,k}\), primitive in the sense of
(3), and define the Euler partition element

\[
 \mathcal Z
 =\exp\left(\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}{X_{p,k}\over k}\right).
                                                                    \tag{13}
\]

It is group-like.  The Eulerian projector gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak e_1\mathcal Z
 =\log\mathcal Z
 =\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}{X_{p,k}\over k}.}                      \tag{14}
\]

Here \(X_{p,k}\) is the connected cycle corresponding to the \(k\)-fold
return; it is not the Hopf-algebra product \(X_{p,1}^k\).  The latter is a
disconnected product and is killed by \(\mathfrak e_1\).  Holonomy
evaluation sends the connected label \(X_{p,k}\) to the actual power
\(U_{\log p}^k\).  This is the standard connected-cycle/exponential
distinction and prevents an incorrect claim that ordinary powers of a
primitive element remain primitive.

Let \(D\) be the orbit-length derivation

\[
 DX_{p,k}=k\log p\,X_{p,k}.                                  \tag{15}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 D\mathfrak e_1\mathcal Z
 =\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\log p\,X_{p,k}.}                      \tag{16}
\]

Evaluating the orbit character of 106.154,

\[
 X_{p,k}\longmapsto
 \tau(U_{\log p}^k)\widehat h(k\log p)
 =p^{-k/2}\widehat h(k\log p),                              \tag{17}
\]

turns (16) into

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}{\log p\over p^{k/2}}
 \widehat h(k\log p).}                                     \tag{18}
\]

After the common Toeplitz orientation of 106.170, (18) occurs with the
degree-one minus sign.  Thus the complete von Mangoldt channel is the
weighted character of the primitive cyclic sector.  Products involving
two different primes belong to disconnected symmetric degree and are
annihilated by \(\mathfrak e_1\).

## 5. Extension of the local Hodge map

Let \(L_{p,k}\) be the map (9) of 106.173 on the copy
\(\mathscr T_{p,k}\), with the same Tate Hodge plane for every return and
with holonomy \(U_{\log p}^k\).  Define on the complete Euler algebra

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal L_{\rm conn}
 =\left(\bigoplus_{p,k}L_{p,k}\right)\mathfrak e_1.}          \tag{19}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — Connected finite-place orbit descent

After cyclic diagonal trace, the map (19):

1. projects the Euler coefficient Hopf algebra onto its connected
   primitive orbit sector;
2. commutes with Fourier on the coefficient module and acts trivially on
   the separate common phase/Bott factor;
3. is surjective on every finite collection of primitive orbit
   coefficients;
4. has a complex-linear right inverse obtained by summing the sections
   \(s_p\) of 106.173 on primitive generators;
5. reproduces the complete prime term (18) after equivariant index.

#### Proof

The first assertion is Theorem 2.1.  The Eulerian projector is natural
for Hopf automorphisms; local Fourier transform acts linearly on
primitives and hence commutes with \(\mathfrak e_1\).  The phase/Bott
operator acts on a separate tensor factor and is unchanged.
Theorem 3.1 of 106.173 gives a complex-linear split surjection on every
primitive summand, and algebraic direct sums preserve it.  Finally,
(14)--(18) prove the trace statement. \(\square\)

## 6. Why no cross-prime metric estimate is required

The passage from an Euler partition function to its fixed-orbit trace is
often expanded into products and then bounded term by term.  That is not
the operation in the explicit formula.  The explicit formula uses the
logarithmic derivative, hence the connected projector (6) followed by
the length derivation (15).  Cross-prime products disappear by the exact
identity (8), not by orthogonality or cancellation in an infinite sum.

This also explains why a common regression across all primes is required:
all primitive generators land in one coefficient object, while
disconnected products are removed by one global Hopf logarithm rather
than by separate prime-wise projections.

## 7. Remaining comparison with CCM

Let \(\mathrm{Tr}_{\rm orb}\rho^\natural\) denote the CCM cyclic
restriction followed by diagonal trace and fixed-orbit localization.  The
new construction supplies the complete right-hand side of a comparison

\[
 \begin{CD}
 \mathrm{Cone}(\rho^\natural)
   @>{\mathrm{Tr}_{\rm orb}}>>
  \widehat{\mathscr E}\\
 @. @VV{\mathfrak e_1}V\\
 @. \mathscr P
   @>{\mathcal L_{\rm conn}}>>
 \mathfrak C_{\rm Tate}^{\rm mid}.
 \end{CD}                                                     \tag{20}
\]

The top arrow is the established CCM restriction/trace morphism.  The
right and bottom arrows are now explicit.  What remains is to prove that
the induced map on the degree-one CCM cokernel is faithful after the
Gamma and polar identity sector is attached:

\[
 \boxed{
 \ker H^1(\mathcal L_{\rm conn}\mathfrak e_1
       \mathrm{Tr}_{\rm orb})
 =\overline{\mathrm{Ran}\,\rho^\natural}.}              \tag{21}
\]

Equation (21), together with the metric identity, is not a consequence of
the Hopf projection: the projector identifies the complete connected
prime channel, whereas faithfulness must also retain the global
archimedean and polar extension data.

## 8. Falsification controls

* A Dirichlet series without an Euler product has no group-like element
  (13) indexed by local prime orbits, so the construction fails before
  (14).
* The projector is applied before absolute values or Hilbert completion.
* No density, sampling, or zero-location statement is used.
* The theorem concerns the connected spherical fixed-orbit quotient, not
  the full noncommutative CCM cone.

## 9. Status

Proved without RH or zero input:

* the augmentation-convergent Eulerian projector;
* its exact primitive range;
* removal of every disconnected cross-prime product;
* identification of the von Mangoldt distribution as the length-weighted
  primitive character;
* extension of 106.173 to the complete connected fixed-orbit coefficient
  localization after cyclic trace;
* compatibility with Fourier Hodge structure and Toeplitz parity.

Still required:

* the faithful derived comparison (21) for the CCM restriction cone;
* complete Gamma/polar attachment at chain level;
* equality of the transferred positive metric with the CCM Rosati trace
  pairing.

## 10. Standard algebraic inputs

The convolution logarithm and primitive projection are the classical
first Eulerian idempotent in a connected commutative Hopf algebra; the
Hodge identities (12) are the standard Eulerian decomposition of cyclic
homology.  The new use here is their placement after the CCM orbit trace
and before the Tate coefficient map.  Standard references are:

* J.-L. Loday, *Cyclic Homology*, second edition, Springer, 1998;
* C. Reutenauer, *Free Lie Algebras*, Oxford University Press, 1993.
