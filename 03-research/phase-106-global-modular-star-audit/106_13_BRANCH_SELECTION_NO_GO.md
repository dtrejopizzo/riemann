# 106.13 — Branch-selection no-go: the completed semigroup and coupled radical models

## Purpose

After 106.11, parity is no longer an obstruction: failure of RH produces a
fixed compactly supported negative test in each parity sector.  The remaining
problem is genuinely angular:

\[
 \frac{\|A_L^+q_L\|}
 {|\langle\phi_L^+,q_L\rangle|}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{1}
\]

This note tests two proposed ways of controlling the denominator in (1)
against the **actual completed ordinary-prime Weil operator**:

1. positivity improvement and a Perron--Frobenius/Harnack argument;
2. coupling the even model \(K\) to the odd model \(K'\), or more generally
   using finitely many derivatives of Riemann's full kernel.

Both mechanisms fail for exact structural reasons.

- The completed semilocal Weil semigroup is not positivity preserving, even
  in the even sector with its natural pointwise cone.  The obstruction is the
  positive long-range kernel of the pole term.
- Every off-line evaluation mode is asymptotically orthogonal to every fixed
  finite family generated by \(K,K',K'',\ldots\), because all their transforms
  contain the common factor \(\Xi\).

These are no-go theorems for the two mechanisms.  They do not refute the
anti-orthogonality target (1), and they do not prove RH.

## 1. The completed form in the additive coordinate

Let

\[
 I_L=[-L/2,L/2],
 \tag{2}
\]

and extend functions in \(L^2(I_L)\) by zero to the real line.  For real
compactly supported smooth \(f,g\), put

\[
 H_{f,g}(u)
 :=(f^**g)(u)
 =\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)g(x+u)\,dx.
 \tag{3}
\]

Polarizing the completed explicit formula gives

\[
 QW_L(f,g)=P(f,g)+G(f,g)+E(f,g)+D(f,g),
 \tag{4}
\]

where \(D(f,g)\) contains only terms proportional to
\(H_{f,g}(0)=\langle f,g\rangle\).  The other three blocks are as follows.

### 1.1 The pole block

The evaluations at \(\pm i/2\) give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(f,g)
 &=\widehat H_{f,g}(i/2)+\widehat H_{f,g}(-i/2)\\
 &=\iint_{\mathbb R^2}
 f(x)g(y)
 \left(e^{(y-x)/2}+e^{-(y-x)/2}\right)\,dx\,dy\\
 &=\iint_{\mathbb R^2}
 2\cosh\!\left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)f(x)g(y)\,dx\,dy.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]

Thus the pole kernel is strictly positive away from the diagonal and grows
as \(e^{|x-y|/2}\).

### 1.2 The Gamma block

Writing \(u=\log x>0\) in the archimedean distribution gives the off-diagonal
density

\[
 c_\infty(u)
 :=\frac{e^{u/2}}{e^u-e^{-u}}
 =\frac{e^{-u/2}}{1-e^{-2u}}.
 \tag{6}
\]

If \(f\) and \(g\) have disjoint supports, then \(H_{f,g}(0)=0\), and the
Gamma contribution is exactly

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G(f,g)
 &=-\int_0^\infty
 \bigl(H_{f,g}(u)+H_{f,g}(-u)\bigr)c_\infty(u)\,du\\
 &=-\iint_{\mathbb R^2}
 c_\infty(|x-y|)f(x)g(y)\,dx\,dy.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]

In particular, its off-diagonal kernel is negative and decays as
\(e^{-|x-y|/2}\).

### 1.3 The ordinary-prime block

The Euler term is

\[
 E(f,g)
 =-\sum_{n\ge2}
 \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 \left(
 H_{f,g}(\log n)+H_{f,g}(-\log n)
 \right),
 \tag{8}
\]

with only the finitely many terms allowed by the support window being
nonzero.  Hence its off-diagonal support is the discrete set

\[
 \mathscr P
 :=\{\log n:n\ge2,\ \Lambda(n)>0\}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Equations (5)--(9) retain the pole, Gamma factor, primes and prime powers
jointly.  No zero representation is used.

## 2. Positivity preservation fails for the actual completed operator

Define

\[
 h(u)
 :=2\cosh(u/2)-c_\infty(u),
 \qquad u>0.
 \tag{10}
\]

Since

\[
 2\cosh(u/2)\asymp e^{u/2},
 \qquad
 c_\infty(u)\asymp e^{-u/2},
 \tag{11}
\]

one has \(h(u)>0\) for every sufficiently large \(u\); in fact \(h(1)>0\).
The set \(\mathscr P\) is locally finite and \(1\notin\mathscr P\).
Choose \(\delta>0\) so small that

\[
 [1-4\delta,1+4\delta]\cap\mathscr P=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 h(u)>0\quad(1-4\delta\le u\le1+4\delta).
 \tag{12}
\]

Let \(f,g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\) be nonzero, nonnegative bumps
supported respectively in

\[
 (-\delta,\delta),
 \qquad
 (1-\delta,1+\delta).
 \tag{13}
\]

They are disjoint, so \(D(f,g)=0\).  Condition (12) and (8) give
\(E(f,g)=0\).  Equations (5) and (7) therefore yield

\[
 \boxed{
 QW_L(f,g)
 =\iint f(x)g(y)h(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy>0
 }
 \tag{14}
\]

for every interval \(I_L\) containing their supports.

### Theorem 1 — The completed Weil heat semigroup is not positive

Let \(A_L\) be the self-adjoint operator associated with the completed
ordinary-prime semilocal Weil form.  For every sufficiently large \(L\),

\[
 \boxed{e^{-tA_L}\text{ is not positivity preserving}.}
 \tag{15}
\]

#### Proof

The first Beurling--Deny criterion says that positivity preservation of the
semigroup associated with a real lower-semibounded closed form requires

\[
 QW_L(u,v)\le0
 \tag{16}
\]

for all nonnegative form-domain functions \(u,v\) with disjoint supports.
The smooth compactly supported functions in (13) belong to the form core,
and (14) violates (16). \(\square\)

The same conclusion can be seen by differentiating at \(t=0\): if the
semigroup were positive, then

\[
 \langle e^{-tA_L}f,g\rangle\ge0,
 \qquad
 \langle f,g\rangle=0,
 \tag{17}
\]

would force

\[
 -QW_L(f,g)
 =\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0+}
 \langle e^{-tA_L}f,g\rangle\ge0,
 \tag{18}
\]

again contradicting (14).

### Theorem 2 — Positivity also fails in the even block

The restriction of the completed semigroup to the even subspace is not
positivity preserving for its natural pointwise cone.

#### Proof

Choose \(0<r_1<r_2\) such that

\[
 d_1=r_2-r_1,\qquad d_2=r_1+r_2
 \tag{19}
\]

both lie in the open set where \(h>0\) and outside \(\mathscr P\).  This is
possible because \(\mathscr P\) is locally finite.  Take four sufficiently
narrow nonnegative bumps centered at

\[
 \pm r_1,\qquad \pm r_2,
 \tag{20}
\]

and let \(F\) be the even sum of the first pair and \(G\) the even sum of the
second pair.  Their supports are disjoint.  Every cross-support difference
lies close to either \(d_1\) or \(d_2\), so the widths may be chosen such
that no prime-power logarithm occurs and \(h\) remains positive.  The same
calculation as (14) gives

\[
 QW_L(F,G)>0.
 \tag{21}
\]

Applying the first Beurling--Deny criterion inside the even subspace proves
the claim. \(\square\)

Adding a scalar multiple of the identity does not change (14) or (21),
because the test pairs are orthogonal.  Thus no spectral shift repairs this
failure.  The pole-free Gamma--Euler operator may possess a Markov
semigroup; the **completed** zeta operator used in the CCM construction
does not.

## 3. Exact pole decomposition by parity

The same obstruction is visible directly in the pole quadratic form.  Put

\[
 c(x)=\cosh(x/2),
 \qquad
 s(x)=\sinh(x/2).
 \tag{22}
\]

For real \(f\),

\[
 P(f,f)
 =2\left(
 |\langle c,f\rangle|^2-|\langle s,f\rangle|^2
 \right).
 \tag{23}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 f\text{ even}:&
 P(f,f)=2|\langle c,f\rangle|^2\ge0,\\[1mm]
 f\text{ odd}:&
 P(f,f)=-2|\langle s,f\rangle|^2\le0.
 \end{array}
 \tag{24}
\]

The pole raises one rank-one even direction and lowers one rank-one odd
direction.  Therefore neither parity comparison nor an even positive cone
can be inferred by simply importing the pole-free jump-process argument.

## 4. Coupling \(K\) and \(K'\) does not select the branch

Let \(K\) be Riemann's full additive kernel, normalized by

\[
 \widehat K(z)=\Xi(z),
 \tag{25}
\]

and put

\[
 K_j=\partial_x^jK.
 \tag{26}
\]

Then

\[
 \widehat{K_j}(z)=(iz)^j\Xi(z).
 \tag{27}
\]

Fix a hypothetical off-line zero

\[
 z_0=x_0+ia,\qquad a\ne0,\qquad\Xi(z_0)=0,
 \tag{28}
\]

and let \(P_L\) denote restriction to \(I_L\).  Its Paley--Wiener evaluation
vector is

\[
 E_{L,z_0}(x)=\mathbf1_{I_L}(x)e^{i\overline{z_0}x},
 \tag{29}
\]

with

\[
 \|E_{L,z_0}\|_2^2
 =\frac{\sinh(|a|L)}{|a|}.
 \tag{30}
\]

For every fixed \(j\), dominated convergence and the quartet symmetry of
\(\Xi\) give

\[
 \langle P_LK_j,E_{L,z_0}\rangle
 \longrightarrow
 \widehat{K_j}(-\overline{z_0})
 =(-i\overline{z_0})^j\Xi(-\overline{z_0})
 =0,
 \tag{31}
\]

because \(-\overline{z_0}\) belongs to the same zero quartet as \(z_0\).
Since \(\|P_LK_j\|_2\to\|K_j\|_2>0\) while (30) grows exponentially,

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\langle
 \frac{P_LK_j}{\|P_LK_j\|_2},
 \frac{E_{L,z_0}}{\|E_{L,z_0}\|_2}
 \right\rangle
 \longrightarrow0.
 }
 \tag{32}
\]

The statement is parity-resolved.  Define

\[
 E_{L,z_0}^+(x)
 =\mathbf1_{I_L}(x)\cos(\overline{z_0}x),
 \qquad
 E_{L,z_0}^-(x)
 =\mathbf1_{I_L}(x)\sin(\overline{z_0}x).
 \tag{33}
\]

The even derivatives \(K_{2j}\) satisfy (32) against \(E_{L,z_0}^+\), and
the odd derivatives \(K_{2j+1}\) satisfy it against \(E_{L,z_0}^-\).  The
norms of both parity evaluation vectors grow as \(e^{|a|L/2}\).

### Theorem 3 — Every fixed derivative model misses the off-line channel

For fixed \(M\), let

\[
 V_L=\operatorname{span}\{P_LK_0,\ldots,P_LK_M\}.
 \tag{34}
\]

If \(\Pi_{V_L}\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(V_L\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\|
 \Pi_{V_L}
 \frac{E_{L,z_0}}{\|E_{L,z_0}\|_2}
 \right\|_2\longrightarrow0.
 }
 \tag{35}
\]

The analogous statement holds in each parity sector with the corresponding
derivatives and evaluation vector from (33).

#### Proof

The functions \(K_0,\ldots,K_M\) are linearly independent: a linear
relation would transform into

\[
 p(z)\Xi(z)\equiv0
 \tag{36}
\]

for a nonzero polynomial \(p\), which is impossible.  Hence the Gram matrix
of \(P_LK_0,\ldots,P_LK_M\) converges to a positive-definite matrix.  Its
inverse is uniformly bounded for large \(L\).  Every coordinate of the
normalized evaluation vector against this basis tends to zero by (31)--(32).
The standard Gram-matrix formula for an orthogonal projection now proves
(35).  Parity follows by restricting to the even or odd indices. \(\square\)

In particular, replacing the model \(K\) by the coupled model

\[
 (K,K')
 \tag{37}
\]

does not repair branch selection.  Both channels contain the common
vanishing factor \(\Xi(z_0)\), so the hypothetical off-line evaluation mode
escapes them simultaneously.

## 5. Real-rootedness does not prevent boundary escape

The finite CCM ground transform has only real zeros once the simple-even
hypothesis is available.  That property still does not supply the missing
tightness.

Let

\[
 g_\delta(x)=(2\delta)^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{[-\delta,\delta]}(x),
 \qquad
 \widehat g_\delta(z)
 =\left(\frac{2}{\delta}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\sin(\delta z)}{z}.
 \tag{38}
\]

For \(a>\delta\), put

\[
 \psi_a(x)
 =2^{-1/2}\bigl(g_\delta(x-a)+g_\delta(x+a)\bigr).
 \tag{39}
\]

Then \(\psi_a\) is nonnegative, even and normalized in \(L^2\), while

\[
 \widehat\psi_a(z)
 =\sqrt2\cos(az)\widehat g_\delta(z).
 \tag{40}
\]

All zeros in (40) are real.  Nevertheless,

\[
 \psi_a\rightharpoonup0
 \quad(a\to\infty),
 \qquad
 \langle\psi_a,K_j\rangle\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{for every fixed }j.
 \tag{41}
\]

Thus positivity, evenness, normalization and real-rootedness of the entire
transform are simultaneously compatible with complete escape from every
fixed radical model.  The example is kinematic rather than an eigenfunction
of \(A_L\); its role is to show that the Carathéodory--Fejér/Suzuki
real-rootedness theorem cannot by itself furnish the overlap denominator in
(1).

## 6. Binding consequence

Documents 106.11 and 106.13 separate two logically different facts.

1. **Detection is complete.**  Under failure of RH, each parity sector has
   a fixed negative branch bounded away from zero.
2. **Selection is still open.**  The completed operator has no
   positivity-preserving heat semigroup in the natural cone, and every fixed
   coupled radical model is orthogonal to the off-line evaluation channel.

Thus neither a Perron--Frobenius/Harnack theorem nor the finite coupling
\(K\oplus K'\) can prove (1).  A successful branch-selection theorem must
use a genuinely signed ordinary-prime estimate which fails for the
hypothetical evaluation channel; it cannot follow from the pole-free Markov
structure or from membership in the full Weil radical.

## Status

Proved here, from the source-side completed explicit formula:

1. the full completed Weil semigroup is not positivity preserving;
2. the same failure occurs in the even parity block;
3. the pole contributes with opposite rank-one signs in the two parities;
4. every fixed finite model generated by \(K,K',K'',\ldots\) is
   asymptotically orthogonal to an off-line evaluation mode;
5. even positive normalized boundary-escaping vectors can have
   real-rooted entire transforms.

Not proved here:

- the ordinary-prime residual/overlap estimate (1);
- exclusion of the off-line branch;
- RH.
