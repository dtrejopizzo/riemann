# 106.167 — The Hilbert--Schmidt Euler determinant and the primitive trace channel

## 1. Purpose

The rooted-Jacobian module realizes all midpoint Euler coefficients, but
its critical product representation becomes white light.  This note tests
a different completion which is genuinely available in the full
half-strip: the Carleman--Fredholm determinant of the prime operator.

The determinant exists and is nonzero for every \(\operatorname {Re}s>1/2\).
It absorbs all repeated prime powers \(p^k\), \(k\ge2\).  The complete
failure of trace class is carried by the single primitive-prime trace
\(\sum_p p^{-s}\).  Consequently any completion of the polarization must
renormalize one explicit linear channel; there is no remaining operator
determinant ambiguity.

## 2. The prime operator

Let

\[
 \mathscr P=\ell^2(\{p:p\text{ prime}\})
\]

and, for \(s=\sigma+it\), define the diagonal operator

\[
 D_s e_p=p^{-s}e_p.                                          \tag{1}
\]

For \(\sigma>1/2\),

\[
 \|D_s\|_{\mathfrak S_2}^2=\sum_p p^{-2\sigma}<\infty,       \tag{2}
\]

while \(D_s\) is trace class only for \(\sigma>1\).

### Theorem 2.1 — Exact determinant in the critical half-strip

For every \(\operatorname {Re}s>1/2\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \det{}_2(I-D_s)
 =\prod_p(1-p^{-s})e^{p^{-s}}
 =\exp\!\left[-\sum_p\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{p^{-ks}}k\right].}   \tag{3}
\]

The product is holomorphic and has no zero in that half-plane.

#### Proof

Equation (2) gives \(D_s\in\mathfrak S_2\), so the regularized determinant
is defined.  Since \(\|D_s\|=2^{-\sigma}<1\), no eigenvalue of \(D_s\)
equals one.  The standard eigenvalue formula for \(\det_2\) gives the
first product in (3).  Expanding

\[
 \log(1-z)+z=-\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{z^k}{k}
\]

and using absolute local uniform convergence, because
\(\sum_p p^{-2\sigma}<\infty\), gives the exponential formula.  An
exponential of a holomorphic function is nonzero. \(\square\)

Thus all higher windings of every prime orbit already form a regularized
determinant on the correct half-strip.

## 3. Exact separation of the primitive trace

For \(\operatorname {Re}s>1\), let

\[
 P(s)=\operatorname {Tr}D_s=\sum_p p^{-s}.                   \tag{4}
\]

The Euler product and (3) give

\[
 \boxed{
 \det{}_2(I-D_s)=\frac{e^{P(s)}}{\zeta(s)},
 \qquad
 \frac1{\zeta(s)}=e^{-P(s)}\det{}_2(I-D_s).}                \tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) is initially an identity in \(\operatorname {Re}s>1\).  Its
importance is that the determinant factor on the right already extends
holomorphically and without zeros to \(\operatorname {Re}s>1/2\).
Therefore every obstruction to extending the literal Euler
factorization across that half-strip is concentrated in the scalar
primitive trace \(P(s)\).

### Corollary 3.1 — No higher-winding obstruction remains

Let

\[
 H(s)=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{p^{-ks}}k.                    \tag{6}
\]

Then \(H\) is holomorphic for \(\operatorname {Re}s>1/2\), and

\[
 \log\zeta(s)=P(s)+H(s)                                     \tag{7}
\]

wherever the Euler logarithm is defined.  Hence any singularity or branch
obstruction of a continued prime logarithm in the critical half-strip is
entirely in \(P(s)\), not in the prime-power tail.

## 4. The compatible positive fermionic realization

Every finite-prime truncation of (3) is the exterior-algebra determinant
of the diagonal contraction \(D_s\).  Passing to \(\det_2\) is legitimate
because (2) is exactly the Hilbert--Schmidt threshold for the
Carleman--Fredholm determinant.  This supplies a Hilbert-space fermionic
realization of the local factors; it does not assert that the generally
complex scalar determinant is positive.

This is the parity-correct local construction: primitive prime modes are
odd, repeated windings are generated by the exterior determinant, and no
ordinary trace-class assertion is made at the critical exponent.

It does **not** yet give the desired arithmetic polarization.  The
counterterm in \(\det_2\) is \(+P(s)\), while the Euler factor needs the
opposite scalar \(e^{-P(s)}\) in (5).  A Hilbert Fock realization fixes
the determinant but does not fix the finite part of this primitive trace.

## 5. Why Gamma and the pole must enter at this exact point

Let

\[
 \xi(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).          \tag{8}
\]

The Gamma and polar factors are holomorphic and nonzero at every
nontrivial zero.  They can prescribe a canonical regularization of the
large-\(p\) continuum and impose the functional equation, but they cannot
alter the nonvanishing determinant in (3).  Consequently a successful
global construction must provide a scalar renormalized primitive trace

\[
 P_{\rm rel}(s)
 =\operatorname {FP}\!\left(\sum_p p^{-s}
   -\text{generic-orbit continuum}
   +\text{Gamma/polar boundary term}\right)                 \tag{9}
\]

such that the *relative determinant*

\[
 \mathcal D_{\rm rel}(s)
 =e^{-P_{\rm rel}(s)}\det{}_2(I-D_s)                         \tag{10}
\]

is produced before analytic continuation by a polarized relative
complex.  Merely defining \(P_{\rm rel}\) from
\(-\log\zeta-H\) would insert the desired answer and is not admissible.

The semilocal CCM term \(2h(1)\log\lambda\) is precisely the trace of the
generic orbit which must be removed in (9).  Thus (9), not another local
prime factorization, is the intersection map required for the pair
\((\operatorname {Pic}_{\rm ar},\eta)\).

## 6. Status

Proved:

* a genuine Hilbert--Schmidt prime operator on the whole half-strip
  \(\operatorname {Re}s>1/2\);
* its exact nonvanishing Carleman--Fredholm determinant;
* holomorphic absorption of every prime-power winding \(k\ge2\);
* localization of the complete non-trace-class obstruction in the single
  primitive trace \(P(s)\).

Still required:

* an arithmetic, source-defined finite-part identity for (9), coupling
  the generic orbit, Gamma, the pole, and the ordinary primes before
  analytic continuation;
* proof that the resulting relative intersection form agrees with the CCM
  Rosati form and is positive.

The construction narrows the missing polarization from an infinite Euler
bank to one scalar primitive-orbit intersection channel.
