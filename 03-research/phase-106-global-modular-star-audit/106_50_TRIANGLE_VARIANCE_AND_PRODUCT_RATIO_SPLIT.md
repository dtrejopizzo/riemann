# 106.50 — Triangle variance and the product--ratio split

## Purpose

Document 106.49 identifies the missing sign as alignment of all increments
leaving a common spatial point. This note removes the probabilistic
notation and rewrites the alignment defect as an exact energy on triangles
of source edges. It then separates, algebraically but without estimating,
the product, ratio and continuous mixed geometries.

This is the first place where the two-prime convolution
\(\Lambda*\Lambda\) appears literally in the cluster trace. It also shows
why \(j_2\) is not the whole double-current coefficient: the ratio triangles
and the prime--Gamma triangles survive.

## 1. Exact triangle identity

Work first with the finite-rate cutoff (10) of 106.49. Let

\[
 \mathcal S_{\varepsilon,N}
 =\{(u,\sigma):u\in\operatorname {supp}\nu_{\varepsilon,N},
 \ \sigma=\pm1\},
\]

with the integration convention

\[
 \int_{\mathcal S_{\varepsilon,N}}F(s)\,d\omega_x(s)
 =\int\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}
 K(x+\sigma u)F(u,\sigma)\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,N}(u).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

Put \(y_s=x+\sigma u\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 W(x)&=\int d\omega_x(s),\\
 S_P(x)&=\int\|\mathbf Q(x)-\mathbf Q(y_s)\|^2d\omega_x(s),\\
 \Theta_P(x)&=\iint
 \|\mathbf Q(y_s)-\mathbf Q(y_t)\|^2
 d\omega_x(s)d\omega_x(t).                           \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]

### Theorem 1 — Star-to-triangle conversion

The joint current of 106.49 satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathbf B_P(x)\|^2
 =W(x)S_P(x)-\frac12\Theta_P(x).}                    \tag{3}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak T_{\varepsilon,N}(P)
 =\int_{\mathbb R}K(x)\Bigg[
 \left(\frac{c_KW(x)}{h(x)}-\frac14\right)S_P(x)
 -\frac{c_K}{2h(x)}\Theta_P(x)
 \Bigg]dx.                                           \tag{4}
\end{aligned}}
\]

#### Proof

Apply the Hilbert-space variance identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &W\int\|v_s\|^2d\omega(s)
 -\left\|\int v_s\,d\omega(s)\right\|^2\\
 &\qquad=\frac12\iint\|v_s-v_t\|^2d\omega(s)d\omega(t) \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]

to \(v_s=\mathbf Q(x)-\mathbf Q(y_s)\). This gives (3).
Substitute (3) in 106.49(6) and use the symmetrized version of
106.49(7),

\[
 \operatorname {Tr}(PL)
 =\frac12\int K(x)S_P(x)\,dx .
\]

The result is (4). \(\square\)

Thus the cluster-current inequality is exactly a domination of the
neighbor-to-neighbor triangle energy by the coherent outgoing surplus:

\[
\boxed{
 \frac{c_K}{2}\int\frac K h\,\Theta_P
 \le\int K\left(\frac{c_KW}{h}-\frac14\right)S_P.}   \tag{6}
\]

The two sides of (6) must remain coupled when the Gamma cutoff is removed.

## 2. Prime--prime triangles

Write \(a_n=\log n\) and \(w_n=\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n\). The prime--prime
part of \(\Theta_P\) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Theta_{pp}(x)
 =\sum_{m,n\le N}w_mw_n
 \sum_{\sigma,\tau=\pm1}
 &K(x+\sigma a_m)K(x+\tau a_n)\\
 &\times
 \|\mathbf Q(x+\sigma a_m)
 -\mathbf Q(x+\tau a_n)\|^2.                         \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]

There are two different geometries.

### Opposite orientations: product channel

If \(\tau=-\sigma\), the two neighboring points are separated by

\[
 a_m+a_n=\log(mn).                                    \tag{8}
\]

Grouping ordered pairs by \(r=mn\) produces exactly

\[
 \sum_{mn=r}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)
 =(\Lambda*\Lambda)(r).                              \tag{9}
\]

The theta factors and the center \(x\) remain inside the grouped spatial
integral; (9) is an exact coefficient identity, not yet a sign estimate.

### Equal orientations: ratio channel

If \(\tau=\sigma\), the separation is

\[
 |a_m-a_n|=\left|\log\frac mn\right|.                 \tag{10}
\]

This channel cannot be grouped by Dirichlet convolution. It contains the
literal ratios \(m/n\) and retains the interference between different
prime towers. Dropping it is precisely the kind of lossy operation excluded
by the radical saturation theorem 106.36.

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\Theta_{pp}=\Theta_{\rm prod}+\Theta_{\rm rat},} \tag{11}
\]

where both terms are nonnegative but only the first has coefficient
\(\Lambda*\Lambda\).

## 3. Mixed and continuous triangles

Similarly,

\[
 \Theta_P
 =\Theta_{pp}+2\Theta_{p\Gamma}+\Theta_{\Gamma\Gamma}. \tag{12}
\]

The mixed term contains the four separations

\[
 |\log n-u|,\qquad \log n+u,                         \tag{13}
\]

with the exact Gamma density \(g(u)\) and all theta factors retained.
The continuous term contains \(|u-v|\) and \(u+v\).

The logarithmic coefficient

\[
 \delta\Lambda(n)=\Lambda(n)\log n                   \tag{14}
\]

does not arise from (9). In the spatial Riccati calculation it is the
rate-variation term generated by translating the center through the
prime displacement \(\log n\). Accordingly, the nonnegative jet

\[
 j_2=\delta\Lambda+\Lambda*\Lambda                   \tag{15}
\]

can only emerge after the product triangles (9), the movement of the
center in (4), and the mixed continuous terms (13) have been combined.

## 4. Use of the reducing-projection equation

For the cluster feature, there is a Hermitian matrix
\(\boldsymbol\Lambda=\operatorname {diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m)\)
such that

\[
 \frac{c_K}{h(x)}\mathbf B_P(x)
 =\mathbf Q(x)\boldsymbol\Lambda.                    \tag{16}
\]

This is the vector form of \(L_x\Pi=L_y\Pi\). It gives the exact coherent
current

\[
 \overline V_x
 =\frac{h(x)}{c_KW(x)}
 \mathbf Q(x)\boldsymbol\Lambda                      \tag{17}
\]

at every finite cutoff, up to the graph-norm cutoff error.

Equation (17) is the additional information absent from the unrestricted
Hankel counterexample. It does not by itself determine the triangle
variance. The remaining calculation is to insert (16) at each of the three
possible triangle centers and add the resulting identities. That cyclic
sum is the only available operation which simultaneously

1. converts center variation into \(\delta\Lambda\);
2. groups opposite prime orientations into \(\Lambda*\Lambda\);
3. retains the ratio and prime--Gamma channels; and
4. vanishes on the exact threshold radical.

## 5. Exact next identity

For a triangle with vertices \(x,y,z\), define the cyclic current defect

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal C_P(x,y,z)
 :=&\frac{K(x)K(y)K(z)}{h(x)h(y)h(z)}\\
 &\times\operatorname {Re}\Big[
 \langle h(x)\mathbf Q(x)-h(y)\mathbf Q(y),
          \mathbf Q(y)-\mathbf Q(z)\rangle\\
 &\hspace{35mm}
 +\text{the two cyclic rotations}\Big].              \tag{18}
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(h\) is replaced by a constant, the real part of the cyclic bracket in
(18) is not zero; the exact polarization identity is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname {Re}\{&
 \langle Q_x-Q_y,Q_y-Q_z\rangle
 +\langle Q_y-Q_z,Q_z-Q_x\rangle\\
 &+\langle Q_z-Q_x,Q_x-Q_y\rangle\}\\
 &=-\frac12\bigl(
 \|Q_x-Q_y\|^2+\|Q_y-Q_z\|^2+\|Q_z-Q_x\|^2\bigr).
                                                               \tag{19}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the cyclic sum produces exactly the negative triangle perimeter,
while the nonconstant \(h\)- and rate weights add a center-variation
correction. Those correction terms are the only possible source of
\(\delta\Lambda\), Gamma and the polar contribution.

The next theorem to prove is therefore a **weighted cyclic triangle
identity**: after integration against the literal two-step edge measure,
(19) must reproduce the triangle term of (4), while the variation of
\(h\), \(K\), and the rates supplies the compensating part. Its
prime-product coefficient must assemble to \(j_2\). Establishing the sign
of the remaining weighted perimeter, with the ratio terms included, is
equivalent to (6).

This formulation is narrower than an arbitrary curvature inequality: it is
restricted to a finite-rank reducing projection and to the exact cyclic
weight produced by \(h=\cosh(x/2)\) and Riemann's theta kernel.
