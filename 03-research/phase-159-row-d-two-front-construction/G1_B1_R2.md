## §2 Theorem H′ — Krasner coefficient absorption

**Setting.** `R` is a commutative ring, `G ⊆ R^×` a subgroup, and `R/G` the Krasner hyperring of orbits `[a] = aG`, with
`[a] ⊕ [b] = {[ag + bh] : g,h ∈ G}` and `[a][b] = [ab]`. Iterated hyper-sums are
`[x_1] ⊕ … ⊕ [x_k] = {[Σ_i x_ig_i] : g_i ∈ G}`. (For Krasner quotients this is associative; it is the standard definition.)
CC's adèle-class hyperring is `R = A_Q`, `G = Q^×` (CC, *The hyperring of adèle classes*, JNT 131 (2011); only the Krasner
definition, stated here in full, is used).

A *Frobenius combination* with coefficients `c = (c_1,…,c_k) ∈ (Q^×)^k` and Frobenius elements `λ_1,…,λ_k ∈ A_Q^×` is the
multivalued map

  S_c : [a] ↦ [c_1λ_1a] ⊕ … ⊕ [c_kλ_ka] = {[a·Σ_i c_ig_iλ_i] : g_i ∈ G}.

A *hyper-invariant* is any function `N` of the family of sets `S_c = ([a] ↦ S_c([a]))`. This covers every "kernel",
"cardinality", "renormalized measure of `{[a] : 0 ∈ S_c([a])}`", "graph of `S_c`", and so on.

**Theorem H′ (`PROVED`).**
 (i) *(absorption)* `S_{h·c} = S_c` for every `h = (h_1,…,h_k) ∈ (G ∩ Q^×)^k`, where `(h·c)_i = h_ic_i`. Hence
  `N(h·c) = N(c)` for every hyper-invariant `N`.
 (ii) *(vanishing)* Suppose some `h_0 ∈ G ∩ Q^×` has `h_0² ≠ 1`, and a hyper-invariant agrees with a real quadratic form
  `Q(c) = Σ_{i,j}A_{ij}c_ic_j` (with `A` symmetric) on a set `Ω ⊆ Q^k` that is stable under `c ↦ h_0c` and Zariski-dense in
  `R^k`. Then `A = 0`. The same holds with "quadratic form" replaced by any nonzero real form that is homogeneous of degree
  `m ≠ 0`.
 (iii) *(diagonality)* Suppose `G ∩ Q^× = {±1}`, and a hyper-invariant agrees with `Q(c)` on a set `Ω` that is stable under
  independent sign changes and Zariski-dense. Then `A_{ij} = 0` for all `i ≠ j`: `Q` has no cross term.
 (iv) *(CC's choice)* For `G = Q^×` case (ii) applies with `h_0 = 2`. Every hyper-invariant of Frobenius combinations on
  `A_Q/Q^×` is a function of the support `{λ_i}` alone: it forgets both the magnitudes and the signs of all coefficients.

*Proof.* (i) `S_{h·c}([a]) = {[a·Σ_ic_i(h_ig_i)λ_i] : g_i ∈ G}`. The map `g_i ↦ h_ig_i` is a bijection of `G`, so the set
is `S_c([a])`, for every `[a]`.
(ii) On `Ω`, `Q(h_0c) = N(h_0c) = N(c) = Q(c)`, where the middle equality is (i) applied to the diagonal vector
`h = (h_0,…,h_0)`. So `(h_0² − 1)Q(c) = 0` on `Ω`, hence `Q = 0` on `Ω`, and by density `Q ≡ 0`. For degree `m` the factor
is `h_0^m − 1`, which is nonzero because `|h_0| ≠ 1`.
(iii) Flip the sign of coordinate `i` only. Invariance gives `Q(c) − Q(c^{(i)}) = 4c_iΣ_{j≠i}A_{ij}c_j = 0` on `Ω`. By
density the polynomial `c_iΣ_{j≠i}A_{ij}c_j` vanishes identically, so `A_{ij} = 0` for `j ≠ i`.
(iv) `2 ∈ Q^×`. By (i), `N` depends on `c` only through the orbit `(Q^×)^k·c`. For `c ∈ (Q^×)^k` there is exactly one such
orbit. ∎

**Corollary H′.1 (the round-1 lead is dead; `PROVED`).** Let `𝒞` be any cone of primitive classes realised by Frobenius
combinations on `A_Q/Q^×`, on which `W(G) = −I_∞(G,G)` is not identically zero. Then no hyper-invariant equals `W/2` on
`𝒞`. In particular, the programme of G1_B1_R1 §8 is excluded: it asked for a renormalized size `#_ren ker(Σc_nFrob_n)`
equal to `W(G)/2` for the class `G` of `Σc_nδ_{log n}`. The requirement "`W` not identically zero on `𝒞`" is necessary for
the count to carry any information at all. If `W ≡ 0` on `𝒞`, then `𝒞` lies in the isotropic cone, and Theorem E's slice
(SG) over `𝒞` is empty of content.
*Proof.* `W(tG) = t²W(G)`, and `G ↦ tG` corresponds to `c ↦ tc`. Apply Theorem H′(ii) with `h_0 = 2`. ∎

**Remark H′.2 (what Weil's count has that the hyperring loses; `PROVED` for the model).** In the model `E/F_q`,
`#ker(k·(m+nπ)) = k²·#ker(m+nπ)`. The kernel of multiplication by `k` on `E(F̄_q)` has `k²` points (`p ∤ k`). Degree is
2-homogeneous **because multiplication by `k` has a large kernel.** On `A_Q/Q^×`, multiplication by a rational `k` is the
identity on orbits (`[ka] = [a]`). On the additive solenoid `A_Q/Q` it is an automorphism: `Q` is divisible, so `x ∈ A_Q`
with `kx ∈ Q` already lies in `Q`. The kernel of `k` is trivial in both quotients, and the product formula
`∏_v|k|_v = 1` records this: the adelic module of `k` is `1`. This is the arithmetic core of "no `+` over `Spec Z`". Any
carrier whose integer multiplications have `k^{2}`-point kernels must be **integral** (like `Ẑ`, where `[Ẑ : kẐ] = k`), and
must not be a quotient by `Q^×`. But on `Ẑ` the index of multiplication by `m + np` is `|m + np|`. That is linear, not
quadratic: `p ∈ Z` gives `Z[Frob_p] = Z`, and there is no rank-2 endomorphism ring whose trace could carry a zero.
(`HEURISTIC` as a reading. The two index computations are `PROVED`.)

**Scope.** Theorem H′ is about Krasner quotients. A quotient by a group with `G ∩ Q^× = {1}` (for example
`G = Ẑ^× × R_{>0}`) escapes (ii)–(iii). But then `R/G` is not a quotient of the adèle class space: the idèle class action,
and with it the periodic orbits of length `log p` of Connes' trace formula, is lost. Whether such a quotient carries a
2-homogeneous count with a cross term is `OPEN`. Remark H′.2 indicates that its index count is linear.

## §3 Theorem J — the standard-part principle, and `Id_ε`

### 3.1 The abstract principle

Let `𝕂 ⊇ R` be an ordered field in which `R` is **not** cofinal on the infinitesimal side. That is, `𝕂` has infinitesimals,
and every `x ∈ 𝕂` of finite size (`|x| < r` for some real `r`) has a unique standard part `st(x) ∈ R` with `x − st(x)`
infinitesimal. Examples: Hahn series `R((ε^Γ))`, Hardy fields of germs at `0⁺`, and the ultrapower `*R`. A *𝕂-class* is a
`𝕂`-linear map `ℓ : D^W ⊗ 𝕂 → 𝕂`. It is *finite* if `ℓ(G)` is finite for every real `G`, and then its standard part
`st ℓ : G ↦ st(ℓ(G))` is a real class.

**Theorem J (`PROVED`, given Cor C.1).** Let `𝔈_𝕂` be any set of finite 𝕂-classes whose standard parts lie in `𝓜^ext_+`.
Then no `ℓ ∈ 𝔈_𝕂` agrees with `ℓ_{E_w}` on real tests. More generally, none satisfies a witness inequality with a
**real** gap, `|ℓ(F)| ≥ (C_F + r)·deg ℓ` for some real `r > 0` and non-infinitesimal `deg ℓ > 0`. (A strict inequality with
only an infinitesimal gap can occur at the boundary `|st ℓ(F)| = C_F deg st ℓ`, and it is useless: the witness needs the
real gap `tW_F − C_F d > 0`.) Hence no infinitesimal enrichment of the cone escapes Cor 19.25b, and the same holds for
`N·E_w`.

*Proof.* If `ℓ(G) = ℓ_{E_w}(G)` for all real `G`, taking standard parts gives `st ℓ = ℓ_{E_w}` with `st ℓ ∈ 𝓜^ext_+`,
against Cor C.1. For the inequality, standard parts preserve weak inequalities, so
`|st ℓ(F)| ≥ (C_F + r)·deg(st ℓ)` with `deg(st ℓ) = st(deg ℓ) > 0`. Theorem C(a) for `st ℓ ∈ 𝓜^ext_+` gives
`|st ℓ(F)| ≤ C_F·deg(st ℓ)`, a contradiction since `r > 0`. ∎

**Reading.** Cor C.1 is an *open* condition on standard parts, with a gap supplied by Lemma 19.25a(v). Infinitesimal data can
never create the gap. An escape needs standard real classes outside `𝓜^ext_+`. This is the precise form of G1_B1_R1
Cor C.3, "distributions of positive order", for all deformation-type enrichments.

### 3.2 `Id_ε` in the arithmetic site (CC 1405.4527 Def 4.2, Thm 4.3; quoted from the source)

From the source: `Germ_{ε=0}(R_+^max)` is "the semiring of germs of continuous functions from a neighborhood of `0 ∈ R` to
`R_+^max`, endowed with the pointwise operations". `N̄_ε` is the sub-semiring generated, for fixed `q ∈ (0,1)`, by `q` and
`Fr_{1+ε}(q) = q^{1+ε}`. The tangential deformation of the identity is the triple `Id_ε = (N̄_ε, ℓ_ε, r_ε)`, with
`ℓ_ε(q^n) = Fr_{1+ε}(q^n)` and `r_ε(q^n) = q^n`. Thm 4.3: `Ψ(λ)∘Ψ(λ′) = Ψ(λλ′)` unless `λ, λ′ ∉ Q` and `λλ′ ∈ Q`, in which
case the composition is `Ψ(λλ′)∘Id_ε`.

`Id_ε` is the only correspondence of the arithmetic site that is not a Frobenius ray. It is also the only place where a
"tangent" (a class of positive order, `w_G′`) could enter the CC square. Theorem J already shows that, as an infinitesimal,
it cannot escape. The finer structure is as follows.

**Proposition J.1 (parity; `PROVED`).** Let `σ` be the map on germs given by `(σf)(ε) = f(−ε)`. It is an automorphism of
the semiring `Germ_{ε=0}(R_+^max)`, and it induces an isomorphism of reduced correspondences (CC Def 4.1)
`Id_ε ≅ Id_{−ε} := (σN̄_ε, σ∘ℓ_ε, r)`, where `σ∘ℓ_ε(q^n) = q^{n(1−ε)}`.
*Proof.* The operations are pointwise (`max`, `×`), and `ε ↦ −ε` maps neighbourhoods of `0` to neighbourhoods of `0`, so
`σ` is a semiring automorphism. It fixes the constant germs, so `σ∘r_ε = r_ε`. It maps `N̄_ε` isomorphically onto
`σN̄_ε`, which is multiplicatively cancellative, and `σ^{−1}({0}) = {0}`. The conditions of Def 4.1 transfer. ∎

**Corollary J.2 (`PROVED`).** Let `cl` be any map from reduced correspondences (with germ coefficients) to germ-valued classes
that is invariant under isomorphism of triples, and suppose that on germs of rays it is `cl(Fr_{λ(ε)}) = [ε ↦ ψ_{λ(ε)}]`.
Then `cl(Id_ε)` is an **even** germ: `cl(Id_ε)(ε) = cl(Id_ε)(−ε)`. So the `ε`-linear jet of `ψ_{1+ε}`, namely
`∂_λψ_λ|_{λ=1}`, does not appear in `cl(Id_ε)`.
*Proof.* Proposition J.1 gives `cl(Id_ε) = cl(Id_{−ε}) = σ(cl(Id_ε))`. ∎

**Proposition J.3 (the parity-invariant first-order jets have degree ≤ 0; `PROVED`).** Let `𝒜` be the smallest set of
germs `ε ↦ Fr_{λ(ε)}` that contains the constant rays `Fr_λ` (`λ > 0`) and `Fr_{1±ε}`, and is closed under composition
`Fr_λ ∘ Fr_μ = Fr_{λμ}` and under `⊕` (pointwise `max` in `R_+^max`). Then:
 (i) Every element is a ray germ `Fr_{λ(ε)}`, where `λ` is continuous and piecewise smooth near `0`, and has one-sided
  derivatives `λ′_±(0)`.
 (ii) Its kink `κ := ½(λ′_+(0) − λ′_−(0))` satisfies `κ ≤ 0`.
 (iii) The class germ `ε ↦ ψ_{λ(ε)}` has the first-order expansion
  `ψ_{λ(0)} + [½(λ′_+ + λ′_−)ε + κ|ε|]·∂_λψ_λ|_{λ(0)} + o(ε)`. The parity-invariant first-order part is
  `κ|ε|·∂_λψ`, and by (1.1) its degree is `κ ≤ 0`.
 (iv) In particular `Id_ε ⊕ Id_{−ε} = Fr_{1−|ε|}`: its parity-invariant jet is `−|ε|∂_λψ_λ|_1`, which has degree `−1`.
So the only first-order information that survives parity comes with **nonpositive degree**. Under the degree gate
(P46 (21.12): a nonzero effective class has `deg > 0`) no such jet is an effective class. Jets of order `k ≥ 2` have degree
`0` by (1.1), so they are excluded by the same gate.

*Proof.* (i) Induction on the construction. `Fr_λ∘Fr_μ(x) = (x^μ)^λ = x^{λμ}`. For `x = q^n` with `q ∈ (0,1)`,
`max(q^{nλ}, q^{nμ}) = q^{n·min(λ,μ)}`, so `Fr_λ ⊕ Fr_μ = Fr_{min(λ,μ)}` on the generators `q^n`. (This is G1_B1_R1
Thm A(iii) in multiplicative notation.) Products and minima of continuous, piecewise smooth germs are again of that kind.
(ii) The generators have `κ = 0`.
* For a product `λμ`, one-sided derivatives obey the Leibniz rule, so `κ_{λμ} = κ_λμ(0) + λ(0)κ_μ ≤ 0`, since
  `λ(0), μ(0) > 0`.
* For a minimum `min(λ,μ)` with `λ(0) ≠ μ(0)`, the germ coincides with one of the two.
* If `λ(0) = μ(0)`, then `min(λ,μ)′_+ = min(λ′_+, μ′_+)` and `min(λ,μ)′_− = max(λ′_−, μ′_−)`. So
  `κ_{min} ≤ ½(λ′_+ − λ′_−) = κ_λ ≤ 0`.

(iii) `λ ↦ ψ_λ(G) = λ^{1/2}w_G(log λ)` is `C¹` for fixed `G` (`w_G` is `C¹`: the prime and archimedean parts are smooth
convolutions of `G ∈ C²`, P46 Thm 11.14; this is `CITED` from record). Compose it with the one-sided expansion of `λ(ε)`.
The odd part `½(λ′_+ + λ′_−)ε` is killed by Cor J.2. The even part is `κ|ε|`. By (1.1),
`deg ∂_λψ_λ = d/dλ(1 + λ) = 1`.
(iv) `max(q^{n(1+ε)}, q^{n(1−ε)}) = q^{n(1−|ε|)}` for `q < 1`. ∎

**Corollary J.4 (the arithmetic site's correspondence algebra is numerically the Frobenius cone; `PROVED`, given the
dictionary).** Every element of the `⊕,∘`-closure of `{Ψ(λ), Id_{±ε}}` in CC's reduced square has:
* standard class in `{ψ_λ} ⊂ 𝓜_+`, and more precisely in the closed Frobenius cone;
* parity-invariant first-order jet of degree `≤ 0`;
* jets of order `≥ 2` of degree `0`.

By Theorem J and the degree gate, no class generated in this way escapes Cor 19.25b. CC's Theorem 4.3 is therefore
numerically multiplicative, `ψ_λ ⋆ ψ_{λ′} = ψ_{λλ′}`. This is C24 of record, now including the exceptional case with `Id_ε`.

## §4 Proposition M — Frobenius additivity under dequantization

For `ħ > 0` put `x ⊕_ħ y := (x^{1/ħ} + y^{1/ħ})^ħ` on `R_+ = [0,∞)`, and `x ⊕_0 y := max(x,y)`. Multiplication is the usual
one. (Maslov–Litvinov dequantization; `φ_ħ(x) = x^{1/ħ}` is a semiring isomorphism `(R_+, ⊕_ħ, ×) → (R_+, +, ×)`.)
Frobenius: `Fr_λ(x) = x^λ`, `λ > 0`.

**Proposition M (`PROVED`).**
 (i) For `ħ > 0`, `(R_+, ⊕_ħ, ×)` is isomorphic to `(R_+, +, ×)`. It is cancellative and non-idempotent, and its
  Grothendieck ring is `R`.
 (ii) `Fr_λ` is a `⊕_ħ`-endomorphism **iff `ħ = 0` or `λ = 1`.**
 (iii) The endomorphisms of `(R_+, ⊕_ħ, ×)` that are continuous and nonzero are only the identity when `ħ > 0`. When
  `ħ = 0` they are `{Fr_λ : λ > 0}` (CC: `Gal(R_+^max/B) = R_+^*`, CITED).

*Proof.* (i) `φ_ħ(x ⊕_ħ y) = φ_ħ(x) + φ_ħ(y)` and `φ_ħ(xy) = φ_ħ(x)φ_ħ(y)`.
(ii) `Fr_λ(x ⊕_ħ y) = (x^{1/ħ} + y^{1/ħ})^{ħλ}` and `Fr_λx ⊕_ħ Fr_λy = (x^{λ/ħ} + y^{λ/ħ})^ħ`. At `x = y = 1` these are
`2^{ħλ}` and `2^ħ`, which are equal iff `ħ = 0` or `λ = 1`. Conversely `ħ = 0` gives
`max(x,y)^λ = max(x^λ,y^λ)`.
(iii) Transport by `φ_ħ`: a continuous, nonzero, unital endomorphism of `(R_+, +, ×)` is additive and continuous, hence
linear, hence the identity. ∎

**Reading (`PROVED` as a method constraint; `HEURISTIC` as a design statement).** Weil's `m + nπ` needs an endomorphism
ring containing `Z` and Frobenius, with a cancellative `+` for which Frobenius is additive. Over `F_q` this holds because
Frobenius is additive in characteristic `p`. In the dequantized family, Frobenius is additive **only** in characteristic
one (`ħ = 0`), and there `+` is idempotent (G1_B1_R1 Thm A: no cross term). Moving to `ħ > 0` restores `+`, but the only
remaining Frobenius-compatible endomorphism is the identity. So a sum graph `Γ_{Fr_λ ⊕_ħ Fr_μ}` at `ħ > 0` is not
equivariant for the scaling flow. This is why G1_B1_R1's Remark A.1 cycle is non-homogeneous: it is Prop M(ii), not an
accident of the chart. Combined with G1_B1_R1 Thm B, **no class produced by a `+` of this type lies in the correspondence
module on which the CC corner pairing is defined.** A new pairing is needed, and none exists (`OPEN`).

*Relation to the literature (`CITED`, not re-read this phase).* CC's "universal thickening" / Witt construction in
characteristic one recovers a characteristic-zero ring from `R_+^max` through an entropy deformation. Prop M(ii) says that
in any such deformation the Frobenius action survives only at the idempotent point. The proof above is self-contained.

## §5 Theorem K — synthesis for the CC square

**Theorem K (`PROVED`, conditional on the dictionary of G1_B1_R1 §1 and the imports (X19), (E2), (E3d) of Cor C.1).**
Let `ℓ` be a real class with `ℓ = ℓ_{E_w}`, i.e. an escaping class for the witness of Cor 19.25b. Then `ℓ` is not in any of
the following:
 (a) the standard parts of infinitesimal/germ/jet enrichments of `𝓜^ext_+` (Thm J);
 (b) `𝓜^ext_+` itself: measures on `[−∞,∞]`, rulings included (G1_B1_R1 Cor C.1);
 (c) the numerical image of the homogeneous effective cone of the scaling-site square (G1_B1_R1 Thm B);
 (d) the numerical image of the `⊕,∘`-algebra generated by the Frobenius correspondences and `Id_{±ε}` of the arithmetic
  site (Cor J.4);
 (e) the value set of a hyper-invariant of Frobenius combinations on `A_Q/Q^×`, if the escape is to be supplied by the
  count of G1_B1_R1 Thm E (Thm H′, Cor H′.1).
Any escaping class must come from a `+` that is not Frobenius-equivariant (Prop M) and must be standard, real, and not
bounded by `‖w̃_G‖_∞` (Thm C). Furthermore, by G1_B1_R1 Thm E, the effectivity of such classes in the quantity needed
(`(SG)` under the degree gate) is **equivalent to RH**.

*Proof.* Collect the cited statements. (d) uses Cor J.4 together with (b). (e) is Cor H′.1. ∎

**What Theorem K does not say.** It does not exclude an as-yet undefined intersection theory on non-homogeneous tropical
