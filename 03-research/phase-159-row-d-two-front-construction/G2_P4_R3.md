## 1. Step check (audit; the steps the literature verdict relies on)

**Lemma L, direct statement (both forms). Re-derived; correct. PROVED.**
- *Step 1.* `θv` and `θβ` are tempered and supported in `[0.1,∞)`. The Laplace transform of a tempered distribution
  supported in a half-line is holomorphic on `Re s > 0`. This is CITED correctly (Schwartz Ch. VIII; Hörmander I §7.4;
  also Vladimirov, *Methods of the theory of generalized functions*, §9).
- *Step 2, the memo's weakest step.* The support bookkeeping is right:
  - `[θ,(−∂²+¼)^k]` is a differential operator whose coefficients are derivatives of `θ`, so `r` is supported in
  `supp θ′ ⊂ [0.1,0.5]`, and `ℒr` is entire.
  - `(−∂²+¼)^k(χe^{−su}) = (¼−s²)^k χ e^{−su} + (terms supported in supp χ′ ⊂ [0.02,0.05])`, and `θu_Λ = θβ·0 = 0` there.
  - One point the memo leaves implicit. `θu_Λ` is **not** tempered unless RH holds, so `⟨(−∂²+¼)^k(θu_Λ), χe^{−su}⟩` is
  not an `𝒮′` pairing. It is the Laplace pairing for a distribution `w` with `e^{−σu}w ∈ 𝒮′` for all `σ > ½`, and it is
  well defined for `Re s > ½`. By uniqueness of the Laplace transform on the common half-plane, it agrees with the
  `𝒮′` pairing on the tempered pieces. Linearity then gives the displayed identity on `Re s > ½`.
  - This is a presentational gap, not a fatal one. It should be one sentence in the memo.
- *Step 3.* The measure on `(0.1,∞)` is computed correctly. The Laplace transform of `2cosh(u/2)` beyond `0.5` is
  `e^{−(s−½)/2}/(s−½) + e^{−(s+½)/2}/(s+½)`, with poles only at `±½`. The Dirichlet series identity is correct.
- *Step 4.* The domain `Re s > 0` is connected. `(¼−s²)^{−k}` has only `s = ½` there. A zero `ρ` with `Re ρ > ½` would
  give a pole at `s = ρ−½ ≠ ½`, and the functional equation then gives RH. Correct.

**Lemma L, converse.** Weil, plus Lemma 1.2 (the odd part is annihilated), plus Bochner–Schwartz. Correct.

**Theorem N(i). Correct. PROVED.** The proof reduces to Lemma L once one notes the following:
- `λ^A_T(a) ≥ −C` for all `T` means that `(−∂²+¼)²(u_a + Cδ)` is positive definite on `C_c^∞`.
- `c_F = (−∂²+¼)²(G⋆G̃)`, because the operator is even and commutes with convolution.
- On `P_T`, `𝔞^{(a)}_T(F) = ⟨u_a, c_F⟩`, because the polar term `2A(F)B(F)` vanishes.

There is a harmless notational slip in the memo: "`a = Λ − β` with `β := −β_b`" mixes a comb with a distribution. By
Lemma 1.1, `u_{Λ+b} = u_Λ − β_b`. The sign does not matter, since Lemma L accepts any tempered slack.

**Theorem R′(i). Correct, and logically immediate.** Suppose `Λ+b ∈ 𝒮^A`. Then RH holds by N(i), and `b = 0` by the R2
Theorem R, which is proved under RH and was verified 5/5 in round 2. The contradiction is unconditional. This is a
two-line combination of N(i) with R2's Theorem R; it contains no new analysis.

**Theorem R′(ii). Correct.**
- Under ¬RH: N(i) gives `inf_T = −∞`, and `lem:monoT` gives `lim = −∞`.
- Under RH: R2 gives `L^A ≤ −sup D_b`.
- `sup D_b > 0` for `b ≠ 0`, because `D_b` is a nonzero almost-periodic function with mean `0`.

**Remark 3.4 (the proportional case). Correct.** A tempered `σ̌` cannot match `ε·2cosh(u/2)` off `±log ℕ`.

Conclusion of the step check: I found **no fatal step**. All load-bearing steps are PROVED or correctly CITED.

## 2. Literature and corpus: what is known

### 2.1 Lemma L ("`u_Λ` tempered ⟺ RH")
- **Classical content.**
  - von Koch (1901): RH ⟺ `ψ(x) = x + O(√x log²x)`.
  - Landau: the Laplace/Mellin transform of a half-line object, and its first singularity.
  - The memo says this himself (Remark 2.1). I agree.
- **Closest published analogue I could verify (CITED, text read).** M. Suzuki, *Aspects of the screw function
  corresponding to the Riemann zeta-function*, arXiv:2206.03682 (J. London Math. Soc. 2023).
  - Theorem 1.6: *RH ⟺ Ψ(t) = O(1) on [0,∞)*, where `Ψ(t) = Σ_γ (1−cos γt)/γ²` is defined through the primes, by his
  eq. (1.1).
  - `Ψ` is a second primitive of the zero side of the explicit formula. In our notation, it is a second primitive of `u_Λ`
  modulo its archimedean and polar parts.
  - "Bounded second primitive ⟺ RH" and "tempered distribution ⟺ RH" are the same von Koch–Landau fact at two levels of
  regularity.
  - The same paper, Theorem 1.2 via Kreĭn–Langer: *RH ⟺ g is a screw function*. This is the Bochner–Schwartz half of
  Lemma L in Kreĭn's language.
- **Genuinely new in Lemma L:** only the packaging with `(−∂²+¼)^k` and a tempered slack `β`. This packaging is exactly
  what the primitive form and comb perturbations need. It is a useful record lemma, **not a new theorem about ζ**.

### 2.2 Theorem N(i) ("bounded below uniformly in T suffices")
- **Literature (folklore).** It follows from any proof of Weil's criterion that goes through Bochner–Schwartz and
  temperedness. Adding `Cδ` adds the constant `C` (a multiple of Lebesgue measure) to the Fourier side, and temperedness
  is unaffected.
- **Li-coefficient analogue.** An off-line zero forces exponentially growing oscillation of `λ_n`, so "`λ_n ≥ −C`" or
  even "`λ_n ≥ −e^{o(n)}`" already implies RH.
  - This is CITED from memory: Bombieri–Lagarias, *J. Number Theory* 77 (1999), the multiset theorem. I did not re-verify
  the exact theorem number.
  - It is in the corpus as `phase-105/105_02` ("off-line zeros force exponential Li modes … cannot be cancelled").
- **Consistency with Bombieri.** E. Bombieri, *Remarks on Weil's quadratic functional in the theory of prime numbers I*,
  Rend. Mat. Acc. Lincei (2000), eudml 252338, abstract read. With finitely many off-line zeros, the number of negative
  eigenvalues on a large enough window is half the number of off-line zeros. So at each fixed window the negative index
  is finite, while N(i) says the bottom goes to `−∞` as `T → ∞`. There is no conflict, and the contrast is informative:
  *finite negative index does not imply RH, but a uniformly bounded bottom does.*
- **Corpus: the memo's corpus check missed this.** The memo says that "bounded below" occurs in P47 only for symbols,
  and that Theorem N(i) is "new to the corpus". That is inaccurate.
  - `03-research/phase-16-surf-witness-variety/09-self-adjoint-construction.md` l.41–46 records "the form is semibounded **iff RH**
  (Phase-4 B-2)".
  - `phase-4-handoff/proofs/B2-semiboundedness.md` §5.4: with the `L²` norm, (SB) "**plausibly collapses to RH**".
  - `phase-4-handoff/PHASE4-WORK-PROGRAM.md` §3.3 asserted the opposite: "an **entire interval** `C ∈ (−∞,0)` of worlds
  where B holds, RH fails".
- **The genuine corpus value of N(i).**
  - It **proves** the §5.4 collapse: with the `L²` norm and all windows, semiboundedness ⟹ RH.
  - It **refutes** PHASE4 §3.3 in that setting. More generally, a lower bound `−C⟨σ,|F̂|²⟩` with any tempered positive
  `σ` also implies RH, which covers the log-weighted form norm.
  - It does *not* contradict `B2-THEOREM.md`. That theorem works on a band-limited class of fixed width `d` (one fixed
  window), and there finite bottoms are unconditional and harmless.
  - This cross-link should be recorded. It is the most useful corpus consequence of the memo.

### 2.3 Theorem R′ and the dichotomy `{Λ}` / `∅`
- **Literature.** I found no open-literature statement of the form "no `ℓ¹_w`-perturbation of the von Mangoldt comb
  keeps Weil positivity".
  - Searches covered Weil positivity together with perturbation, Beurling, the screw function, and crystalline measures.
  - The mechanism is short: positive definiteness makes `2πμ_Λ − D_b` a positive measure; an atomic measure absorbs no
  continuous density; a nonzero almost-periodic function with mean `0` takes positive values.
  - Novelty as a *statement* is plausible. Depth is modest, and the unconditional upgrade over R2 is logically a
  two-line corollary of N(i).
- **Related literature the record should cite.**
  1. Fourier quasicrystals and crystalline measures, which bear on Remark 3.4 ("a Fourier pair between `±log ℕ` and
  `Γ_ζ` other than 0"):
  - Y. Meyer, PNAS 113 (2016). CITED from memory, as in the memo.
  - P. Kurasov, P. Sarnak, *Stable polynomials and crystalline measures*, arXiv:2004.05678, abstract verified.
  - The Olevskii–Ulanovskii uniqueness theorems for Fourier quasicrystals. CITED from memory, not re-verified.

  Remark 3.4's OPEN question is a positivity-constrained uniqueness question in exactly this area. A nonzero `b` with
  `D_b ≤ 2πμ_Λ` would make `u_{Λ+b}` a positive-definite distribution supported on `{0}∪±log ℕ` away from the smooth
  archimedean part. That is a question about crystalline pairs perturbed off the Guinand–Weil pair, and it deserves a
  check against Olevskii–Ulanovskii before anyone attacks it.
  2. Beurling systems satisfying RH: F. Broucke, G. Debruyne, J. Vindas, *Beurling integers with RH and large
  oscillation*, arXiv:2004.11501 (Adv. Math. 370, 2020). These are Weil-positive "combs" (with their own polar term)
  whose difference from `Λ` is not supported on `log ℕ`, so they lie outside `𝒯_meas`. They confirm, from the
  literature side, that the memo's F4 line is correctly left OPEN: Theorem R′ does not exclude DMV/BDV-type systems,
  and it could not, since such systems are Weil-positive.

### 2.4 Is the construction known to be equivalent to RH?
Yes, and the memo says so.
- Theorem N(iii), `RH ⟺ (Λ+ℓ¹_w)∩𝒮^A ≠ ∅ ⟺ ∃a ∈ Λ+𝒯 : inf_T λ^A_T(a) > −∞`, is an equivalent form of RH.
- Its right-hand condition is the "bounded-below Weil criterion" of §2.2, relaxed by a tempered slack. This is folklore
  strength, not a new route.
- The mechanism reading in §4 of the memo, "FE ⟹ ∀T positivity on `{ζ}`", is RH verbatim. The "only rigid axiom is the
  FE" step uses Hamburger's theorem (CITED in G2_B3_R2). That does not change the verdict.

## 5. Final verdict on G2_B9_R3
**Restatement (as a G2 mechanism).**
- **Fatal step for the mechanism:** none in the proofs. The only arrow the memo produces toward RH is Theorem N(i),
  "bounded below ⟹ RH", which is the known direction of Weil's criterion. The arrow G2 needs, prime data ⟹ `∀T`
  positivity, is shown to be rigid and is left exactly as hard as RH. The resulting equivalence N(iii) is a restatement.
- **Record value (keep):** Lemma L in the `(−∂²+¼)^k`-with-slack form; Theorem N(i) as the proof of the phase-4 collapse
  conjecture; Theorem R′ and Cor R1′ as unconditional obstructions (a comb-variable analogue of R157_002 row 17);
  Remark 3.3; Remark 3.4 (PROVED part).
- **Salvageable idea:** Remark 3.4's OPEN question, whether a nonzero comb perturbation exists whose positive part is
  carried by the ordinates, as a crystalline-measure uniqueness problem. It is not a route to a sign. A positive answer
  would give a second ∀T-positive comb under RH, and that is the only way the rigidity could fail.
