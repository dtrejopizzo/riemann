## §0 Verdict

Borger's premise fixes the square: "the space `Spec Z ×_{Spec F_1} Spec Z` must be defined to be the Witt space
`W^*(Spec Z)`" (0906.3146 §1, read this phase). I take that carrier seriously and build on it. I get four theorems.

* **Theorem A** (`PROVED`): the Witt descent datum forces the positive form. Its Frobenius lifts `F_n` and
  Verschiebungen `V_n` are adjoint, `F_n^† = V_n`, for **exactly one** positive form up to a scalar: the weight `1/m` on
  the ghost set `N`, and `x^{−2}dx` on its continuous hull. That form is conformal with factor `n = e^{log n}`. So
  Deninger's axiom (D3) with `α = 1`, which R158_001 Lemma T lists as a *postulate*, is here *derived*: it follows from
  `F_n V_n = n`, from the ring-homomorphism property of `F_n`, and from the `H²` eigenvalue `n` of the orientation
  functional. That half of N-a holds, with no zeros used.
* **Theorem B** (`PROVED`, new to the corpus): the arithmetic intersection lengths of the ghost components
  `Γ_n ⊂ W^*(Spec Z)` are
  `Γ_m · Γ_n = (1 + v_p(min(m,n)))·log p` if `max/min = p^k` (`k ≥ 1`), and `0` otherwise.
  In particular **`Γ_1 · Γ_n = Λ(n)` exactly**. Borger's square therefore produces the exact von Mangoldt weights as
  "`Γ_{Frob^n} · Δ`". This is the finite-prime half of OT-2, geometric and rigid.
  The pairing is **not translation-invariant**: `Γ_2 · Γ_4 = 2 log 2 ≠ Γ_1 · Γ_2 = log 2`. That is a non-radial,
  `p`-adic-depth datum which the frame does not have.
* **Theorem C** (`PROVED`, blindness): on the canonical carrier `H_W = L²(R_+, x^{−2}dx)` the Frobenius flow is
  unitary with Lebesgue spectrum. The Euler element `E^♭ = Σ_n n^{−1}V_n` (Connes' map in Witt coordinates) has symbol
  `ζ(½ − iγ)` **exactly on the unitary line**, so the Witt–Rosati form places Serre's line `Re = α/2 = ½` on the
  critical line. But `E^♭` has dense range, so its cokernel is `0`: no subquotient of `H_W` carries a single zero.
* **Theorem D** (`PROVED`, content CLASSICAL, a restatement): *every* continuous positive semidefinite Λ-Rosati form
  on `C_c^∞(R_+)` has the form `∫|Ĝ|²dν`, with `ν ≥ 0` tempered (Bochner–Schwartz). The cokernel of `E^♭` in its
  completion is `⊕_{γ ∈ Z_c, ν{γ} > 0} C`, where `Z_c` is the set of critical zeros. So the cokernel sees only critical
  zeros, each with multiplicity `1`, and it sees a zero only if `ν` was given an atom there. An H¹ realising all the
  zeros exists ⟺ RH ∧ simple zeros, and its form reads the zeros (OT-3 fails). The only prime-side choice of `ν` is
  Weil's distribution, whose positivity is E1.

**Conclusion.** Borger's descent supplies a Rosati involution (`F ↦ V`), a forced positive form and derived
conformality, all before the spectrum and without zeros, and it supplies the exact `Λ(n)` as intersection numbers.
It does **not** supply an `H¹`. The unique Λ-Rosati form is zero-blind (Thm C), and every Λ-Rosati form that is not
zero-blind reads the zeros or is Weil's (Thm D). The object test fails at **OT-1/OT-2** (archimedean place absent,
which is Borger's own caveat) and at **OT-5′** (the forced form lives on the wrong space). The angle closes as

## §1 The carrier

**1.1 Borger's square** (`CITED`, 0906.3146 §1, read). `v : Sp_Z → Sp_{F_1}`. The base change `v^*` forgets the
Λ-structure. Its left adjoint `v_!` sends `X` to its Witt space `W^*(X)`, and its right adjoint `v_*` gives arithmetic
jets. "In particular, if one accepts the premise of this paper, the space `Spec Z ×_{Spec F_1} Spec Z` must be defined
to be the Witt space `W^*(Spec Z)`." The author's caveat: "since the current version of our theory says nothing about
the archimedean place of `Q`, it is hard to imagine this succeeding without further ideas."

**1.2 Coordinates** (`CITED`, classical; Hazewinkel, *Witt vectors I*, arXiv:0804.3888, §§9–16). `W(A)` is the big
Witt ring. Its Witt coordinates are `x = (x_d)_{d ≥ 1}`, and its ghost components are the ring homomorphisms
`gh_n(x) = Σ_{d|n} d·x_d^{n/d}`. For torsion-free `A`, `gh : W(A) → A^N` is an injective ring homomorphism, and it is
bijective when `Q ⊂ A`. The operators:
* Frobenius `F_n` (ring endomorphism): `gh_m(F_n x) = gh_{nm}(x)`;
* Verschiebung `V_n` (additive): `gh_m(V_n x) = n·gh_{m/n}(x)` if `n|m`, and `0` otherwise;
* Teichmüller `[a]`: `gh_m([a]) = a^m`.

Relations (Cartier): `F_mF_n = F_{mn}`, `V_mV_n = V_{mn}`, `F_nV_n = n`, `F_mV_n = V_nF_m` for `(m,n) = 1`,
`F_n[a] = [a^n]F_n`, `[a]V_n = V_n[a^n]`. Each one is checked on ghost components in one line. The Λ-structure of
`W(A)` is `ψ_n = F_n`.

**1.3 Ghost components as Frobenius graphs** (`PROVED`, by definition). `gh_n : W(Z) → Z` defines a closed point-curve
`Γ_n := Spec Z ↪ W^*(Spec Z)`. From `gh_n = gh_1 ∘ F_n`, the map `Spec(F_n)` sends `Γ_1` onto `Γ_n`, so
`Γ_n = (F_n)_*Γ_1`. Take `Δ := Γ_1`, the canonical section `gh_1`. Then `Γ_n` plays the role of `Γ_{π^n}` on `C × C`
(`HEURISTIC` as a dictionary; `PROVED` as the identity `Γ_n = (F_n)_*Δ`). Over `Q` the `Γ_n` are disjoint:
`W^*(Spec Z)_Q = ⊔_{n∈N} Spec Q`, the generic fibre is the ghost set `N`, and the components are glued over `F_p` by the
Dwork congruences.

## §2 Theorem A: the descent datum forces the positive form

**Theorem A** (`PROVED`).
(i) *(discrete ghost carrier)* Let `w : N → (0,∞)` and `⟨a,b⟩_w = Σ_m a(m) b̄(m) w(m)` on finitely supported
`a, b : N → C` (the ghost image of `W(Q)` ⊗ C, finitely supported part). Then `⟨F_na, b⟩_w = ⟨a, V_nb⟩_w` for all `n`
⟺ `w(m) = w(1)/m`.
(ii) *(continuous hull)* Let `μ` be a positive Radon measure on `(0,∞)`, with `F_q g(x) = g(qx)` and
`V_q g(x) = q·g(x/q)` for `q ∈ N`, acting on `C_c(0,∞)`. Then `⟨F_q g,h⟩_μ = ⟨g,V_q h⟩_μ` for all `q ∈ N` ⟺
`μ = c·x^{−2}dx` with `c > 0`.
(iii) *(derived conformality)* On `H_W := L²((0,∞), x^{−2}dx)`: `⟨F_q g, F_q h⟩ = q⟨g,h⟩` for all `q ∈ Q_{>0}`. So
`U_t := e^{−t/2}φ^t`, with `φ^t g(x) = g(e^t x)`, is a strongly continuous unitary group. This is Deninger's (D3)
with `α = 1` (R158_001 Lemma T), derived and not postulated. Moreover `⟨g,h⟩ = ℓ(g·h̄)` with `ℓ(k) = ∫k x^{−2}dx`,
`ℓ(F_q k) = q·ℓ(k)`. Conformality is therefore (ring homomorphism `F_q`) + (the orientation `ℓ` is an `F`-eigenvector
of eigenvalue `q`, "`H²` has `Θ = 1`"), and `1` is the `F`-fixed vector (`H⁰`, `Θ = 0`).
(iv) *(Rosati anti-involution)* `†` realises `F_n ↦ V_n`, `V_n ↦ F_n`, `[a] ↦ [a]` (real `a`) as the Hilbert adjoint
on `ℓ²(N, 1/m)`. It is an anti-involution of the Cartier ring `Cart(Z)` acting on the ghost space, compatible with all
the relations of 1.2.

*Proof.* (i) `⟨F_na,b⟩ = Σ_m a(nm) b̄(m) w(m)` and `⟨a,V_nb⟩ = Σ_k a(k) n b̄(k/n) 1_{n|k} w(k) = Σ_m a(nm) n b̄(m) w(nm)`.
Testing on `a = δ_{nm}`, `b = δ_m` gives `w(m) = n·w(nm)` for all `m, n`, i.e. `w(m) = w(1)/m`. The converse is the
same computation.
(ii) Substituting `y = qx`, `⟨F_q g,h⟩ = ∫ g(y) h̄(y/q) d(σ_qμ)(y)`, with `σ_q` the push-forward by `x ↦ qx`, and
`⟨g,V_qh⟩ = q∫ g(y) h̄(y/q) dμ(y)`. Since `g(y)h̄(y/q)` runs through a set whose span is dense in `C_c`, this forces
`σ_qμ = qμ`. Put `ν := x·μ`. Then `σ_qν = ν`: for `φ ∈ C_c`,
`∫φ d σ_q(xμ) = ∫φ(qx) x dμ = q^{−1}∫ φ(y) y dσ_qμ = ∫ φ y dμ`. So `ν` is a Radon measure on the group `(0,∞)`,
invariant under the dense subgroup `Q_{>0}`. For `φ ∈ C_c`, `s ↦ ∫φ(sx)dν(x)` is continuous (uniform continuity with
compact support, and `ν` is locally finite), so `ν` is invariant under all of `(0,∞)`, i.e. `ν = c·dx/x` (uniqueness of
Haar measure). Hence `μ = c x^{−2}dx`, and `c > 0` by positivity. The converse is direct.
(iii) `∫ |g(qx)|² x^{−2}dx = q∫ |g(y)|² y^{−2}dy`, and `ℓ(F_q k) = ∫ k(qx) x^{−2}dx = q ℓ(k)`. The unitary group
extends from `q ∈ Q_{>0}` to `t ∈ R` by strong continuity of dilations on `L²`.
(iv) Adjointness is (i). The Hilbert adjoint is an anti-involution on the algebra of operators that preserve finitely
supported vectors. `F_n`, `V_n` and `[a]` preserve them, and `[a]` is multiplication by the real sequence `a^m`, which
is self-adjoint. The relations are carried to their `†`-images, e.g. `(F_n[a])^† = [a]V_n = V_n[a^n] = ([a^n]F_n)^†`. ∎

**Remark A.1** (`HEURISTIC`). `F_nF_n^† = F_nV_n = n` is the formal analogue of Weil's `ππ^† = q`. On `ℓ²(N,1/m)`,
`n^{−1/2}V_n` is an isometry and `n^{−1/2}F_n` a co-isometry. On `H_W` both are unitary. Weil's Rosati argument
needs two things: `ππ^† = q` (present here), and **`Tr(ff^†) > 0` on a finite-dimensional `H¹` whose trace is computed
geometrically by Lefschetz**. Here the second is absent. `Tr(ff^†)` is the trivial Hilbert–Schmidt positivity of an
operator on a function space, not a Lefschetz number (§4).

**Remark A.2** (`PROVED`, scope). Theorem A is about forms of *multiplication type*, `⟨g,h⟩ = ℓ(g h̄)` ("cup product
then trace"). Theorem D below treats *all* continuous Λ-Rosati forms, which are the translation-invariant ones.

## §3 Theorem B: intersection lengths on Borger's square

Define `Γ_m · Γ_n := log #(Z ⊗_{W(Z)} Z)`, where the two structure maps are `gh_m, gh_n`, i.e. the log-length of the
scheme-theoretic intersection `Γ_m ∩ Γ_n ⊂ W^*(Spec Z)` (`m ≠ n`). This is the finite part of an Arakelov-type
intersection number. There is no archimedean contribution (1.1).

**Theorem B** (`PROVED`). For `m ≠ n`:
`Γ_m · Γ_n = (1 + v_p(min(m,n)))·log p` if `max(m,n)/min(m,n) = p^k` with `p` prime and `k ≥ 1`, and `Γ_m·Γ_n = 0`
otherwise. Equivalently, `Γ_m · Γ_{mp^k} = Λ(p^k)·(1 + v_p(m))`. In particular **`Γ_1 · Γ_n = Λ(n)`** for all `n ≥ 2`.

*Proof.* `Z ⊗_{W} Z = W/(ker gh_m + ker gh_n) ≅ Z/I`, with `I = (gh_n − gh_m)(W(Z))`. This image is a subgroup of `Z`
because `gh_n − gh_m` is additive (both are ring homomorphisms), hence an ideal. It can be computed on truncated Witt
vectors `W_S(Z)`, where `S` is the set of divisors of `m` and `n` and `W_S(Z) = Z^S` as sets via Witt coordinates:
`P(x) := gh_n(x) − gh_m(x) = Σ_{d|n} d x_d^{n/d} − Σ_{d|m} d x_d^{m/d}`.
*Case `m ∤ n`, `n ∤ m`.* There are primes `p ≠ q` with `v_p(m) > v_p(n)` and `v_q(n) > v_q(m)`. Take
`d = q^{v_q(n)}`, so `d | n` and `d ∤ m`; then `P(e_d) = d`. Take `d' = p^{v_p(m)}`; then `P(e_{d'}) = −d'`. Since
`gcd(d,d') = 1`, `I = Z`.
*Case `m | n`, `n/m` divisible by two primes `p, q`.* `d = p^{v_p(n)}` and `d' = q^{v_q(n)}` divide `n` and not `m`,
and give `P = d, d'`, coprime. So `I = Z`.
*Case `n = mp^k`, `k ≥ 1`, `a := v_p(m)`.* Divisors `d | n` with `d ∤ m` are `d = p^j e` with `j > a`, `e | m`. They
contribute `d x_d^{n/d} ∈ p^{a+1}Z`, and `x_{p^{a+1}} = 1`, all other coordinates `0`, gives `P = p^{a+1}`. Divisors
`d = p^j e | m` (`j ≤ a`) contribute `d·(y^{p^{a−j+k}} − y^{p^{a−j}})` with `y = x_d^{(m/d)p^{−(a−j)}}`, an integer.
By the classical congruence `y^{p^{r+s}} ≡ y^{p^r} (mod p^{r+1})` (`s ≥ 1`), obtained by induction from `y^{p} ≡ y`
(mod `p`) and `u ≡ v (p^t) ⟹ u^p ≡ v^p (p^{t+1})`, each term lies in `p^j·p^{a−j+1}Z = p^{a+1}Z`. Hence
`I = p^{a+1}Z`, and `log #Z/I = (a+1)log p`. The case `m = 1` gives `a = 0` and `I = pZ` for `n = p^k`. ∎

**Corollary B.1** (`PROVED`). With the unitary normalisation of Thm A (`n^{−1/2}` per unit of conformal weight),
`n^{−1/2}(Γ_1 · Γ_n) = Λ(n)/√n = ½v_n`, the P47 comb weight (`eq:symbol`). The prime side of ζ's explicit formula is
`Σ_n (n^{−1/2} Γ_n · Δ)(h(log n) + h(−log n))`: a Lefschetz-shaped sum of fixed-point contributions, exact and
computed geometrically. Only the finite part is computed; the archimedean term is not.

**Corollary B.2** (`PROVED`; ghost relation, cf. P44 Cor 5.3a). `Σ_{d|n, d>1} Γ_1·Γ_d = log n`.

**3.3 Non-radiality** (`PROVED`). `Γ_{m}·Γ_{mp}` depends on `m`, not only on the ratio `mp/m`:
`Γ_1·Γ_2 = log 2`, `Γ_2·Γ_4 = 2 log 2`, `Γ_4·Γ_8 = 3 log 2`, while `Γ_3·Γ_6 = log 2`. The frame's contacts
`I(Frob_m, Frob_n)` depend only on `log(n/m)` (translation invariance; P46 §21.7, the `⋆`-structure). So this is the
first datum of a Borger-type carrier that does **not** commute with the radial projection. It passes the R147_005
test (§C) in the literal sense. *Caveat* (`HEURISTIC`, via C16): the extra factor `1 + v_p(m)` is `p`-adic valuation
data, i.e. local, and C16 (P46 Prop 22.5, Thm 26.13) says forms computed from local data are unchanged by
`ξ → Ξ_a`. No sign can be expected from it alone.

**3.4 Rigidity at object level** (`PROVED` by inspection). `W^*(Spec Z)` and the numbers `Γ_m·Γ_n` are defined from
the ring `Z` alone: its additive structure through the Dwork congruences, and `+`/`×` through the Witt polynomials.
* `Λ + εδ_{n_0}` and `Λ·1_{n ≥ N}` are not intersection numbers of any Witt square, since Theorem B pins the values.
* A Beurling system has no ring, so no Witt square exists for it. This is the "additive structure of `Z`" that C14 /
  Rem 27.16a say BE1–BE8 lack.
* `ζ·E`, `Ξ_Q` and `Ξ_a` are not produced.

So the *carrier* passes OT-4's substitution list. OT-4 would still have to be checked on the positivity proof, and
there is none.

## §4 Theorem C: the canonical form is blind

**4.1 The Euler element.** For `g ∈ C_c^∞(0,∞)` with `∫ g(y) y^{−2}dy = 0` (the pole constraint), put
`E^♭g(x) := Σ_{n≥1} n^{−1}(V_ng)(x) = Σ_{n≥1} g(x/n)`.
Formally `E^♭ = Σ_n n^{−1}V_n = Π_p (1 − p^{−1}V_p)^{−1}`: this is the Euler product in the completed Cartier ring,
i.e. Connes' map `f ↦ Σ_n f(n·)` written in Witt coordinates (`HEURISTIC` as the identification; the definition is
exact).

**Theorem C** (`PROVED`).
(i) `E^♭g ∈ H_W`. It vanishes on `(0, min supp g)` and is `O(x^{−N})` for every `N` as `x → ∞`.
(ii) In unitary coordinates `G(u) = e^{−u/2}g(e^u)` (so `‖g‖_{H_W} = ‖G‖_{L²(R)}`, and `U_t` becomes the translation
`G ↦ G(· + t)`), `(E^♭g)^∧(γ) = ζ(½ − iγ)·Ĝ(γ)`, with `Ĝ(γ) := ∫ G(u) e^{iγu} du`. So on the unitary line of the
Witt–Rosati form, the symbol of the Euler element is ζ on the critical line.
(iii) The spectrum of `U` on `H_W` is Lebesgue (absolutely continuous, all of `R`). No nonzero closed `U`-invariant
subspace and no quotient by one has an eigenvector.
(iv) `E^♭` has dense range in `H_W`. Its cokernel is `0`.

*Proof.* (i) Support: `g(x/n) ≠ 0` needs `x/n ≥ a := min supp g`, so `x ≥ a`. Decay: `t ↦ g(x/t)` is smooth with
support in `[x/b, x/a]`, and its `k`-th derivative is `O_k(x^{−k})` there. Euler–Maclaurin of order `k` gives
`Σ_n g(x/n) = ∫_0^∞ g(x/t)dt + O_k(x^{1−k}) = x∫ g(y)y^{−2}dy + O_k(x^{1−k}) = O_k(x^{1−k})`.
(ii) Write `M_k(s) := ∫_0^∞ k(x) x^{s} dx/x`. Then `Ĝ(γ) = M_g(iγ − ½)`. For `Re s < −1`,
`M_{E^♭g}(s) = Σ_n ∫ g(x/n) x^{s} dx/x = Σ_n n^{s} M_g(s) = ζ(−s) M_g(s)`. Both sides are entire:
the left by (i), the right because `ζ(−s)` has its only pole at `s = −1`, where `M_g(−1) = ∫ g y^{−2}dy = 0`. By
analytic continuation they agree at `s = iγ − ½`, which gives `(E^♭g)^∧(γ) = ζ(½ − iγ) Ĝ(γ)`. (With the opposite
Fourier sign convention the factor is `ζ(½ + iγ)`; the zero set `Z_c` is symmetric, so nothing below depends on it.)
(iii) `U` is unitarily equivalent to translation on `L²(R)`, i.e. multiplication by `e^{iγt}` on `L²(R, dγ)`, whose
spectral measure is Lebesgue. An eigenvector of a restriction or of a compression to an invariant complement (the
complement is invariant because `U` is unitary) is an eigenvector of `U`, and there are none.
(iv) By Wiener's theorem (closed translation-invariant subspaces of `L²(R)` are `{Ĝ : supp Ĝ ⊂ S}` for measurable
`S`), the closure of the range is `L²(S)` with `S ⊇ {γ : ζ(½+iγ) ≠ 0 and Ĝ(γ) ≠ 0 for some admissible g}`. This set is
`R` minus a discrete set, because the admissible `Ĝ` include functions nonvanishing off any prescribed point (the
constraint `M_g(−1) = 0` removes one complex point). So the closure is all of `L²`. ∎

**Proposition C.3** (`PROVED`; discrete ghost carrier). On `ℓ²(N, 1/m)` the vectors `a_s(m) = m^{s}` (`Re s < 0`)
are eigenvectors of `F_n` with eigenvalue `n^s`. Their Gram kernel is
`K(s,t) = ⟨a_s, a_t⟩ = Σ_m m^{s + t̄ − 1} = ζ(1 − s − t̄)`, which is positive definite on `{Re s < 0}`. Its meromorphic
continuation is **not** positive on any point of `0 < Re s < ½`: `K(s,s) = ζ(1 − 2σ) < 0` there, since
`ζ(x) = η(x)/(1 − 2^{1−x})` with `η(x) > 0` and `1 − 2^{1−x} < 0` for `0 < x < 1`. The positivity of the Rosati Gram
stops at the pole `s + t̄ = 0`. The zeros of `K` (`1 − s − t̄ = ρ`) lie on `Re(s + t̄) = ½` if RH holds, on the far side
of the pole. *Proof.* Direct summation for `Re(s + t̄) < 0`, and the classical sign of ζ on `(0,1)`. ∎
*Reading* (`HEURISTIC`): this is the §28.4 (2)/(3) geometry, a polar divisor fixed by local data with the zeros to its
left. The Witt Gram positivity sits entirely on the polar side, like Bost–Connes (R157_002 row 33).

## §5 Theorem D: every Λ-Rosati form either sees no zero or is built from them (restatement)

**Theorem D** (`PROVED`; content CLASSICAL: Bochner–Schwartz, Wiener, Serre; no novelty claimed for it).
Let `B` be a Hermitian form on `C_c^∞(R)` (unitary coordinates, pole constraint imposed where needed) that is continuous
in the `C_c^∞` topology, positive semidefinite, and Λ-Rosati, i.e. `B(F_q G, H) = B(G, V_q H)` in the normalisation of
Thm A. Equivalently, `U_{log q}` is `B`-isometric for `q ∈ Q_{>0}`.
(i) `B(G,H) = ∫ Ĝ \overline{Ĥ} dν` for a unique positive tempered measure `ν` on `R`.
(ii) In the completion `H_B`, the closure of `E^♭`'s range is `L²(ν|_{R∖Z_c})` and the cokernel is
`L²(ν|_{Z_c}) = ⊕_{γ ∈ Z_c, ν{γ} > 0} C`, where `Z_c := {γ ∈ R : ζ(½ + iγ) = 0}`. Every eigenvalue of `U` on any
subquotient of `H_B` is an atom of `ν`, hence real, i.e. its zero is on the critical line, and the cokernel carries
each such zero with multiplicity exactly `1`.
(iii) Hence: there is a Λ-Rosati form whose `E^♭`-cokernel carries all non-trivial zeros with their multiplicities
(Lemma T's (D1)–(D3) plus the trace formula) **⟺ RH ∧ all zeros simple**. Such a form necessarily has `ν ≥ Σ_ρ c_ρδ_{γ_ρ}`
with atoms placed at the zeros, so OT-3 fails. The one prime-side candidate for `ν` is the Fourier transform of Weil's
distribution, whose positive-definiteness is E1 (Weil 1952; Bombieri 2000; P47 `prop:finuniv` for its finite
analogue).

*Proof.* (i) Continuity of `B`, the kernel theorem and invariance under the dense subgroup `log Q_{>0}` (upgraded to
all of `R` by continuity of translation on `C_c^∞`) give `B(G,H) = D(G ⋆ H̃)` for a distribution `D`. `D` is
positive-definite, so Bochner–Schwartz (Reed–Simon II Thm IX.10) gives `D = ν̂` with `ν ≥ 0` tempered.
(ii) Let `h ∈ L²(ν)` with `h ⊥ ζ(½+i·)Ĝ` for all admissible `G`. The complex measure `μ' := h̄ ζ(½ + i·) ν` is
tempered (Cauchy–Schwarz, and ζ is polynomially bounded on the line). It annihilates every Paley–Wiener `Ĝ` that
vanishes at the one complex point `γ_*` imposed by the pole constraint. So `∫Ĝ dμ' = c Ĝ(γ_*)` for all `G ∈ C_c^∞`,
and the Fourier transform of `μ'` is `c·e^{iγ_* u}` (exponential growth `e^{±u/2}`). A tempered distribution of that
form has `c = 0`. So `μ' = 0`, and `h = 0` ν-a.e. off `Z_c`. `Z_c` is countable, so `ν|_{Z_c}` is atomic, which gives the
cokernel. A `U`-eigenvector in `L²(ν)` is supported on an atom. (iii) (⇒) is (ii). (⇐) Under RH ∧ SZ take
`ν = Σ_γ δ_γ` (tempered, since `N(T) ≪ T log T`). This is Lemma T(⇐) and uses the zeros. ∎

**Remark D.1** (`HEURISTIC`). Thm D is R158_005's "any definite Θ-compatible form ⟹ RH ∧ simple zeros" transported
to Borger's carrier. What the Witt angle adds is Thm A: there the Rosati *structure* is canonical, but the canonical
*form* is Lebesgue `ν` (Thm C), which is blind. Every other Rosati form is a choice of `ν`, i.e. of spectral data. The
Witt descent datum does not select `ν`.

## §7 What is new, what is open, and the weakest step

**New (`PROVED`, not in the corpus):**
1. Thm A: the Witt descent datum *forces* the positive form (`1/m`, `x^{−2}dx`) and *derives* conformality `α = 1`
  from `F_nV_n = n`, the ring structure and the `H²` eigenvalue. (D3) is no longer only an axiom: on Borger's carrier it
  is a theorem. It is a theorem on the wrong space.
2. Thm B: the intersection lengths of Frobenius graphs on Borger's square, `Γ_1·Γ_n = Λ(n)`, and the non-radial depth
  factor `1 + v_p(m)`. This is the first computation on the square that P36 l.9856 names ("right structure, no
  engine"). It also answers the finite half of phase 114 gap G-4 (an intersection pairing on the Witt object).
3. Thm C: the Euler element `E^♭ = Σ n^{−1}V_n` has symbol `ζ(½ − iγ)` on the Witt–Rosati unitary line, i.e. the Witt
  form places Serre's line on the critical line, and its cokernel is `0` there. This is the exact reason the canonical
  Rosati form is blind.

**Restatement (Thm D):** Λ-Rosati forms = positive tempered spectral measures. Zero-seeing ⟺ atoms at the zeros ⟹
OT-3 fails, and realising all the zeros ⟺ RH ∧ SZ. **EQUIVALENT**, classical content.

**Open:**
* **O-B5.1** (`OPEN`). Complete the Borger square's pairing on `span{Γ_n}` to an Arakelov-type form: self-intersections
  `Γ_n·Γ_n` (adjunction) plus an archimedean correction. Then decide whether its Hodge-index form, restricted to
  translation-invariant combinations, is P46's `I_∞`, and what the depth factor `1 + v_p(m)` does to its signature. By
  C16 the depth factor alone is zero-blind. Any sign would have to come from the archimedean completion, which is where
  Borger's theory is silent. I rate this RH-strength if it succeeds (by P46 Cor 17.11 / E6) and most likely a
  restatement.
* **O-B5.2** (`OPEN`). OT-10: Borger's `Λ_{O_K}`-variant and whether Thm B holds with `Λ_K(𝔫)`.

**Weakest step.** The scope of Theorem D. It covers every H¹ obtained as a subquotient of a (continuous, Λ-Rosati)
Hilbert completion of the Witt *function* space. It does not cover an H¹ built as a genuinely new cohomology of
`W^*(Spec Z)` whose inner product is not a form on functions (e.g. from O-B5.1 via intersection theory on cycles). The
exclusion of the Borger/Rosati route is therefore complete on the function carrier only. The cycle-level route O-B5.1 is
not excluded, and it is not built either. Secondary: Thm C(ii) uses a sign convention for the Fourier variable; the
statement is convention-independent because `Z_c` is symmetric.

**Status for the panel.** No object passing OT-5′ has been constructed. The angle yields three proved structural
theorems (A, B, C) and one classical restatement (D). The Borger–Rosati route to OT-5′ is **excluded on the function
carrier** (Thms C + D). The cycle route (O-B5.1) is open and unbuilt. Row D: no label, no sign.
