  * the Blaschke division `F ↦ F·(z−w̄)/(z−w)` at a zero `w` of `F`;

  positivity is everything.
2. **Theorem J (no type-zero stretch; PROVED modulo CITED Romanov).** Let the window form be coercive in the log-Sobolev
  norm (Lemma P′ gives this for every `χ` of large conductor). Then every canonical system realising the window has
  strictly increasing type `τ` on its window segment:
  * no indivisible interval;
  * no interval with `det H = 0` a.e.

  realisation.
3. **Theorem U (Kreĭn rigidity; PROVED modulo CITED Romanov Thm 10, 13 and Cor 19).** Under the same hypothesis, a

  * monotone reparametrisation;
  * one constant unipotent gauge `H ↦ S_c^T H S_c`, with `S_c = [[1,c],[0,1]]`.

  Its chain is `{𝒳_ρ}_{ρ≤r}`, where `𝒳_ρ` is the FT image of the tests supported in `[−ρ, ρ]`, and `τ(y_ρ) = ρ`.
4. **Theorem K⁺ (divergence law; PROVED modulo CITED Chebotarev and Romanov; rule-independent).** Take a Galois-conjugate
  pair of cubic characters `χ, χ̄` of prime conductor `N ≥ N₀(r)`. Suppose they agree at every prime `< e^{2r}` except
  `q` (Chebotarev supplies such pairs). Then:
  * their Kreĭn Hamiltonians are gauge-equivalent **exactly** up to type `½ log q`;
  * they are gauge-inequivalent on every longer initial segment.

  So **a prime `q` is heard by the Hamiltonian at depth exactly `½ log q`, not earlier and not later.** Round 4's
  Theorem K was the inequality `s_q ≤ ½ log q` for sequential data-local (SDL) rules. K⁺ is the equality, for the

5. **Proposition G (gauge-local rules; PROVED).** Consider an "impedance / Goupillaud" rule: a common background
  conjugated by `SL₂(R)` jumps `g_{p,b}` at data-independent positions. Each jump is then visible at type exactly
  `½ log q`, and the jump sits at `τ_q < ½ log q` only in one case: the relative jump is elliptic **and** the Hamiltonian

6. **Consequence (EQUIV, declared).** Cor U.1 below: a de Branges carrier of the window `T = 2r` exists iff the complete

## §3 The window forms: automatic axioms, coercivity, support spaces

**Def 3.1.**
* 𝔉 is the set of even primitive Dirichlet characters of prime conductor `N`. For `χ ∈ 𝔉` and `f ∈ C_c^∞(−r,r)`, put
  `g = f * f̃` and `F(z) = ∫ f(x)e^{ixz}dx`.
* On the real line `ĝ = |F|²` and `g(x) = (1/2π)∫|F(t)|²e^{−ixt}dt`. So the prime-side Weil form of round 4, Def 3.1,
  becomes **exactly**
  ```
  Q_χ(f) = (1/2π) ∫ |F(t)|² ω_{χ,r}(t) dt,
  ω_{χ,r}(t) := log(N/π) + Re ψ(¼ + it/2) − 2 Re Σ_{n<e^{2r}} Λ(n) n^{−½} χ(n) n^{−it}. (3.1)
  ```
  This is a finite sum, valid for every `f` supported in `(−r, r)`, because `supp g ⊂ (−2r, 2r)`. The weight `ω` is
  real, and it is not claimed to be positive.
* The distribution is `D_χ = log(N/π)δ₀ + (arch) − Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−½}(χ(n)δ_{log n} + \barχ(n)δ_{−log n})`, as before.
* The log-Sobolev weight is `w(t) := 1 + Re ψ(¼+it/2) − ψ(¼) ≥ 1`, and `‖f‖²_ℓ := (1/2π)∫|F|² w`.
  * `w(t) = log|t| + O(1)`.
  * `w(t/λ) ≤ w(t) + 1 + log 2` for `λ ∈ [½, 1]`.
* The support spaces are `X_ρ := {f ∈ L²(R) : supp f ⊂ [−ρ, ρ], ‖f‖_ℓ < ∞}` and `𝒳_ρ := {F : f ∈ X_ρ}`.

**Lemma A′ (automatic de Branges axioms; PROVED).**

(a) Let `f ∈ X_r` and `F(w) = 0`. Then `F·b_w`, with `b_w(z) = (z − \bar w)/(z − w)`, lies in `𝒳_r`, and
`Q_χ(F b_w) = Q_χ(F)`. Also `F^# ∈ 𝒳_r` and `Q_χ(F^#) = Q_χ(F)`.

(b) The same holds for any Hermitian form `Σ_{γ∈Γ} ĝ(γ)`, taken as an absolutely convergent or distributional pairing,
over a multiset `Γ ⊂ C` stable under `γ ↦ \barγ`. This includes the complete Weil form of ζ with its polar pair
`±i/2`.

*Proof.*
1. **(a), division.**
  * `h(x) := −ie^{−iwx}∫_{−∞}^x f(s)e^{iws}ds` vanishes for `x < −r`. It vanishes for `x > r` because
  `∫f e^{iws} = F(w) = 0`. It lies in `L²`.
  * `\hat h = F/(z−w)`, so `F b_w = F + (w−\bar w)\hat h` is the FT of an `L²` function supported in `[−r, r]`.
  * On `R`, `|b_w| = 1`, so `|F b_w| = |F|`. The ℓ-norm and (3.1) are unchanged.
  * (3.1) extends from `C_c^∞` to `X_r` by continuity, since `|ω| ≤ C_r w`.
2. **(a), the `#` operation.** `F^#(t) = \overline{F(t)}` on `R`, and it is the FT of `\bar f(−·)`, which is supported
  in `[−r,r]`.
3. **(b).** `ĝ(γ) = F(γ)\overline{F(\barγ)}`. Replacing `F` by `F b_w` multiplies this by
  `b_w(γ)\overline{b_w(\barγ)} = \frac{γ−\bar w}{γ−w}\cdot\frac{γ − w}{γ − \bar w} = 1`. The case `F^#` is the same
  computation. ∎

*Reading.* This is Weil's `ĝ(γ) = F(γ)\overline{F(\barγ)}` structure seen through de Branges: any explicit-formula form
is formally a de Branges norm. What is not automatic is that it be a norm at all.

**Lemma P′ (log-coercivity at large conductor; PROVED).** Let
`C(r) = log π − ψ(¼) + 2Σ_{n<e^{2r}}Λ(n)n^{−½}`. If `log N > C(r) + 1`, then for `f ∈ X_r`
```
‖f‖²_ℓ ≤ Q_χ(f) ≤ K_N(r) ‖f‖²_ℓ, K_N(r) := max(1, log(N/π) + ψ(¼) + 2Σ_{n<e^{2r}}Λ(n)n^{−½}).
```
*Proof.* Split `ω = (Re ψ − ψ(¼)) + (log(N/π) + ψ(¼)) − (prime part)`. The prime part has modulus
`≤ 2ΣΛ(n)n^{−½}` pointwise. Also `(1/2π)∫|F|² = ‖f‖²₂` and `Re ψ(σ+iu) ≥ ψ(σ)` (series; round 4, Lemma P). Hence
`Q ≥ (1/2π)∫|F|²(w − 1) + (log N − C(r))‖f‖²₂ ≥ ‖f‖²_ℓ`. The upper bound is the same computation. ∎

This is a large-conductor statement. It proves nothing about ζ and nothing for any fixed `χ` beyond its window.

**Lemma S (support spaces; PROVED).** For `0 < ρ ≤ r`:
* (a) `X_ρ` is complete in `‖·‖_ℓ`.
* (b) `X_ρ` is the ℓ-closure of `C_c^∞(−ρ, ρ)`.
* (c) `∩_{ρ′>ρ} X_{ρ′} = X_ρ`, and the closure of `∪_{ρ′<ρ} X_{ρ′}` is `X_ρ`.
* (d) `∩_{ρ>0} X_ρ = {0}`.

*Proof.*
1. **(a).** `‖·‖₂ ≤ ‖·‖_ℓ`, `L²(w dt)` is complete, and the support condition is closed under `L²` limits.
2. **(b).** Given `f ∈ X_ρ`, put `f_λ(x) = f(x/λ)` with `λ < 1`, supported in `[−λρ, λρ]`. Then mollify.
  * Mollification multiplies `F` by `\hat φ_ε → 1` with `|\hat φ_ε| ≤ 1`, so it converges in `L²(w)` by dominated
  convergence.
  * Dilation `F ↦ λF(λ·)` has norm `≤ (1 + (1+log 2)/1)^{1/2}` on `L²(w)`, uniformly in `λ ∈ [½,1]`, by the tameness
  of `w`. It is strongly continuous on the dense set `C_c(R)`, hence on `L²(w)`.
3. **(c).** The intersection statement is trivial on supports. The closure of the union contains the ℓ-closure of
  `C_c^∞(−ρ,ρ)`, which is `X_ρ` by (b).
4. **(d).** A function in `L²` supported in `{0}` is 0. ∎

**Proposition A″ (the window space is a regular de Branges space; PROVED).** Under Lemma P′, `(𝒳_r, Q_χ)` is a regular
de Branges space without real zeros, and each `(𝒳_ρ, Q_χ)`, `ρ ≤ r`, is a de Branges subspace of it.

*Proof.*
1. It is a Hilbert space by Lemma P′ and Lemma S(a).
2. (H1): `|F(w)| ≤ (2r)^{½}e^{r|Im w|}‖f‖₂ ≤ (2r)^{½}e^{r|Im w|}Q_χ(f)^{½}`.
3. (H2) and (H3) are Lemma A′(a), applied with `r` replaced by `ρ` for the subspaces.
4. By (F5) these are de Branges spaces.
5. No real zeros: a bump `f` has `F(t) ≠ 0` at any given `t`.
6. Regularity: `(F(z) − F(w))/(z − w)` is the FT of an `L²` function supported in `[−r, r]`, by the same primitive as in
  Lemma A′. On `R` it is `O(|F(t)| + |F(w)|)/(1+|t|)` away from `w` and bounded near `w`. Since `∫ w/(1+t²) < ∞`, it
  lies in `X_r`. ∎

## §4 The theorems

Throughout, `χ ∈ 𝔉` with `log N > C(r) + 1`. A **realisation** of the window `r` is an (L)-system `H` on some `[0, L]`
with `𝒟_r := {F : f ∈ C_c^∞(−r,r)} ⊂ H_L` isometrically for `Q_χ` (round 4, Def 3.2). By Lemma P′ and Lemma S(b), the
closure of `𝒟_r` in `H_L` is `𝒳_r`, and evaluations pass to the limit. By (F4) and Prop A″, each `𝒳_ρ = H_{y_ρ}` for
some `y_ρ ∈ M`. By round-4 Lemma B and (R11), `τ(y_ρ) = ρ`: `≤ ρ` by Lemma B, and `≥ ρ` because `𝒳_ρ` contains
functions of type arbitrarily close to `ρ` (Paley–Wiener).

**Theorem J (no type-zero stretch; PROVED modulo (R5), (R11), (R12), (F4)).** In every realisation, `τ` is strictly
increasing on `[0, y_r]`. In particular, `(0, y_r)` contains no indivisible interval and no interval on which
`det H = 0` a.e.

*Proof.*
1. **Set-up.** Suppose `τ ≡ s` on a maximal interval `[a, b] ⊂ [0, y_r]` with `a < b`. By maximality neither `a` nor `b`
  is interior to an indivisible interval (on which `τ` is constant), so `a, b ∈ M`.
2. **Case `s > 0`.**
  * For `ρ < s`, `τ(y_ρ) = ρ < s` gives `y_ρ < a`, hence `𝒳_ρ ⊂ H_a`.
  * For `ρ ∈ (s, r]` (when `s < r`), `y_ρ > b`, hence `H_b ⊂ 𝒳_ρ`.
  * So `\overline{∪_{ρ<s}𝒳_ρ} ⊂ H_a ⊂ H_b ⊂ ∩_{ρ>s}𝒳_ρ`. By Lemma S(c) both ends equal `𝒳_s`.
  * If `s = r`, use `H_b ⊂ H_{y_r} = 𝒳_r` instead.
  * Either way `H_a = H_b`.
3. **Case `s = 0`.** Here `a = 0` and `H_b ⊂ ∩_{ρ>0}𝒳_ρ = {0}` by Lemma S(d), while `H_0 = {0}`. So again `H_a = H_b`.
4. **Contradiction.** By (R5), `H_b ⊖ H_a` is the `U`-image of the elements of `𝓗` supported in `[a, b]`. Since
  `tr H = 1`, some `v ∈ {e₁, e₂}` has `∫_a^b⟨Hv, v⟩ > 0`. The function `v·1_{(a,b)}` is constant on every indivisible
  interval inside `(a,b)`, none of which crosses `a` or `b`. So it is a nonzero element of `𝓗`. Hence `H_a ⊊ H_b`, a
  contradiction. ∎

**Theorem U (Kreĭn rigidity; PROVED modulo (R10), (R13), (SL)).**

(i) **Existence.** A realisation of the window `r` exists.

(ii) **Uniqueness.** Let `H¹, H²` be two realisations. Then there are a constant `c ∈ R` and a monotone absolutely
continuous bijection `x : [0, y¹_r] → [0, y²_r]` such that `x′(t) H²(x(t)) = S_c^T H¹(t) S_c` a.e., with
`S_c = [[1,c],[0,1]]` — after trace-normalising `S_c^T H¹ S_c` by the reparametrisation. Write `H¹ ≅_c H²`.

(iii) **Uniqueness of the gauge.** `c` is unique.

(iv) **Invariance.** Every `H ≅_c H¹` is again a realisation.

*Proof.*
1. **(i).** Normalise the HB function of `𝒳_r` by `E(0) = 1`, which is possible since there are no real zeros. Then
  (R10) gives an (L)-system with `B(E_L) = 𝒳_r`.
2. **(ii), same chains.** By Theorem J, both `τ^i` are strictly increasing homeomorphisms onto `[0, r]`, and there is no
  indivisible interval, so `M = [0, y^i_r]`. By the argument before Theorem J, `H^i_x = 𝒳_{τ^i(x)}`. So the two chains
  coincide as families of de Branges spaces, with `x(t) := (τ²)^{−1}(τ¹(t))`.
3. **(ii), the gauge.** (R13) gives (41) with `a_t` constant, say `a_t ≡ c`. Put `H³ := S_c^{−T} H¹ S_c^{−1}`.
  * By (SL), its `Θ`-solution is `S_cΘ¹`, and `Θ³(0) = (1,0)^T`.
  * (L) is preserved, since `S_c^T e₂ = e₂`.
  * Then `Θ²(x(t)) = Θ³(t)` for all `t`. The last clause of (R13), applied to the trace-normalisations of `H²` and
  `H³`, gives equality up to the reparametrisation.
4. **(iii).** Suppose `≅_c` and `≅_{c′}` both hold with `c ≠ c′`. Then `H¹ ≅_{c−c′} H¹`. The bijection is forced to be
  `x(t) = t`, because the chain is strictly increasing (Theorem J). So `S^T H¹ S = H¹` a.e. with
  `S = [[1,d],[0,1]]`, `d ≠ 0`.
  * Expanding, the `(1,2)` entry gives `d·h₁₁ = 0`, so `h₁₁ = 0`. Then `h₁₂ = 0` by positivity, and
  `H¹ = h₂₂e₂e₂^T`.
  * This has `det H¹ = 0` a.e., which contradicts Theorem J.
5. **(iv).** This is (SL): `B(E)` is unchanged. ∎

**Corollary U.1 (the angle is the positivity; EQUIV, declared).** Let `L` be ζ or an L-function of 𝔉, and let `T = 2r`.

(a) A canonical system realising the complete window form of `L` on `(−r, r)` exists **iff** that form is positive
definite. For existence, coercivity is also needed on the ⇐ side: (⇒) is trivial, and (⇐) is Prop A″ with Lemma A′(b),
followed by (R10).

(b) When it exists, the realisation is unique in the sense of Theorem U. The "Hamiltonian built from Γ and the Euler
product" is therefore **not a construction but a computation**: Kreĭn's inverse map applied to `D_L|_{(−2r,2r)}`.

(c) "`H ≥ 0` structurally, for all windows" is thus the statement that the complete form is positive for every `T`. That
is Weil's criterion (P46 `thm:Z`), so it is **EQUIV to RH**, respectively GRH for `χ`. This is R157_002 rows 38–39 and
P155 round 5 (Kreĭn indeterminacy), now with the construction side shown to carry **no freedom** beyond `(x, c)`.

**Theorem K⁺ (divergence law; PROVED modulo CITED Chebotarev and the above; independent of any rule).**
* Let `q < e^{2r}` be prime.
* Let `N ≡ 1 (3)` be prime, with `log N > C(r) + 1`, such that every prime `p < e^{2r}`, `p ≠ q`, is a cube mod `N` and
  `q` is not. Chebotarev in `Q(ζ₃, p^{1/3}, q^{1/3})` supplies infinitely many such `N` (round 4, Theorem K, step 2).
* Let `χ` be a cubic character mod `N`, and let `H_χ, H_χ̄` be realisations of the window `r`.

Then there is a unique `c` such that `H_χ ≅_c H_χ̄` on the initial segments of type `½ log q`. For no `c′` and no `ε > 0`
does `≅_{c′}` hold on the segments of type `½ log q + ε`.

*Proof.*
1. **Agreement.** Every `n < q` has all its prime factors cubes mod `N`, so `χ(n) = \barχ(n) = 1`. The conductor is
  common. Hence `ω_{χ,ρ} = ω_{χ̄,ρ}` for every `ρ ≤ ½ log q`, since `n < e^{2ρ} ≤ q`. The two forms coincide on the
  window `ρ`.
2. The initial segments `[0, y_ρ]` of `H_χ` and `H_χ̄` are realisations of that common window. Theorem U(ii) gives
  `≅_{c_ρ}`, and (iii) makes `c_ρ` independent of `ρ`. Let `ρ ↑ ½ log q`; the relation holds a.e., hence on the closed
  segment.
3. **Divergence.** Suppose `H_χ ≅_{c′} H_χ̄` up to type `ρ = ½ log q + ε`. By (iv), the two systems have the same chain
  up to type `ρ`, so `(𝒳_ρ, Q_χ) = (𝒳_ρ, Q_χ̄)` isometrically. By polarisation and round-4 Lemma C,
  `D_χ = D_χ̄` on `(−2ρ, 2ρ) ∋ log q`.
4. But the coefficient of `δ_{log q}` in `D_χ − D_χ̄` is `−Λ(q)q^{−½}(ω − ω²) = −i√3·log q/√q ≠ 0`. ∎

*Reading.* The Hamiltonian's dependence on the local datum at `q` switches on at depth **exactly** `½ log q`. Any
construction of `H` from prime data must therefore install each prime's information at the single depth `½ log q`, and
nowhere earlier. The type budget up to `X` is `½ log X`, while the number of primes to install is `π(X)`. This is the
exponential mismatch of round 4, now as an identity about Kreĭn's Hamiltonian rather than a constraint on a rule class.

**Proposition G (gauge-local rules; PROVED).**

*Setting.* A gauge-local rule assigns:
* a common (L)-background `K` on `[0, L]`, depending on Γ and `N` only;
* data-independent positions `x_p`;
* jumps `g_{p,b} ∈ SL₂(R)`, depending only on `p` and its local datum `b`.

It sets `H_χ(x) = G_χ(x)^T K(x) G_χ(x)`, where `G_χ(x)` is the ordered product of the `g_{p,b_p(χ)}` with `x_p ≤ x`.
This is the Goupillaud / impedance-jump shape: constant gauge jumps between common layers. It is not a concatenation
of blocks, so round-4 Theorem K does not apply to it.

*Statement.* Suppose such a rule realises the window `r` for every `χ ∈ 𝔉` of large conductor. Take `q < e^{2r}`, with
`N, χ` as in K⁺, and write `τ_q = τ(x_q)`. Then `τ_q ≤ ½ log q`. If `τ_q < ½ log q`, then:
* the relative jump `A := G_0^{−1}g_{q,\barω}^{−1}g_{q,ω}G_0` is elliptic;
* `H_χ̄ = h(x)S_A` a.e. on `(x_q, x_{½ log q})`, where `S_A > 0` is the `A`-invariant form, `G_0` is the product of the
  jumps before `x_q`, and `ω = χ(q)`.

In words: the jump is invisible until type `½ log q` exactly.

*Proof.*
1. **Structure of the two Hamiltonians.** All data other than `q` coincide for `χ` and `χ̄`. So `H_χ = H_χ̄` on
  `[0, x_q]`, and `H_χ = A^T H_χ̄ A` on `(x_q, L]` with `A` constant. Since `det A = 1`, `τ_χ = τ_χ̄` pointwise.
2. **`τ_q ≤ ½ log q`.** If `τ_q > ½ log q`, the systems coincide past type `½ log q`, which contradicts the divergence
  part of K⁺.
3. **The gauge constant is 0 and the reparametrisation is trivial.** By K⁺, `H_χ ≅_c H_χ̄` up to type `½ log q`.
  * The reparametrisation matches equal `τ`-levels (proof of Theorem U(ii)), and `τ_χ = τ_χ̄` is strictly increasing,
  so `x(t) = t`.
  * If `τ_q > 0`, the two Hamiltonians are already equal on `[0, x_q]`, so `c = 0` by U(iii).
  * If `τ_q = 0`, the same conclusion follows from uniqueness applied to the segment `[0, x_q]`, which is empty of type.
  Then `c` is the unique gauge on the whole segment. Absorbing `S_c` into `G_0` reduces this to `c = 0`.
4. **Consequence.** `A^T H_χ̄ A = H_χ̄` a.e. on `(x_q, x_{½log q})`. This interval has positive type when
  `τ_q < ½ log q`.
5. **Case analysis on `A`.**
  * **Hyperbolic or parabolic `A ≠ ±I`.** Its PSD fixed forms have rank `≤ 1` (the computation of U(iii), and for
  hyperbolic `A` its diagonal version). This contradicts `det H > 0` on a set of positive measure (Theorem J).
  * **`A = ±I`.** The systems would coincide on the whole window, which contradicts K⁺.
  * **Elliptic `A`.** It fixes exactly `R_{≥0}S_A` and has no real eigenvector, so no rank-one fixed form exists.
  Hence `H_χ̄ ∝ S_A` a.e. ∎

## §5 What remains, and why the angle is closed

* **(E4) echo chain: CLOSED (Theorem J).** Type-zero point interactions do not occur in any realisation of a coercive
  window.
* **Concatenated local chains: CLOSED** (round-4 Theorem K and Cor K.1; K⁺ is the sharp form).
* **(E4′) Gauge-local rules: reduced by Proposition G.** Every jump is effectively at type exactly `½ log q`. What remains
  is the multiple-echo test: does a Goupillaud medium, with jumps effectively at `½ log q` over the archimedean
  background `K`, produce a `D` whose atoms lie only at `±log(prime power)`?
  * For a free background, the internal multiple between reflectors at `½ log 3` and `½ log 2` arrives at
  `log(9/2) ∉ log Z`, so free backgrounds fail. That is the classical travel-time count (Bube–Burridge, SIAM Rev. 25
  (1983)): `HEURISTIC` as applied here.
  * For the actual `K`, whose `D_∞` has a `|x|^{−1}`-type finite-part singularity at 0, a singularity-propagation
  theorem for canonical systems with non-`L¹` accelerant is needed. **OPEN.**
  * By K⁺, however, any gauge rule that passes is simply Kreĭn's Hamiltonian written in gauge form. It would carry no
  positivity beyond what Corollary U.1 already equates with RH. Passing (E4′) would give a **structure theorem** for
  Kreĭn's Hamiltonian of `D_χ`, not a sign.
* **(E5) Non-sequential / matrix-valued coupling.** RH-strength by record (phase 120 P3). Unchanged.
* **The one global route: EQUIV** (Corollary U.1). No construction freedom remains that could make `H ≥ 0` hold "for a
  reason that precedes the spectrum". The Hamiltonian is determined by `D` window by window, and `H ≥ 0` on the window
  `T` is exactly `W_T ≥ 0`.

**Conclusion for G1.** The de Branges / canonical-system angle cannot produce an OT-5′ object. Every realisation is
Kreĭn's, and its positivity is the window positivity itself. I recommend closing the angle in the map with
{A′, J, U, K⁺, G} as its closing theorems.

## §7 What is genuinely new, and what is not

**Not new.**
* Uniqueness of the Hamiltonian given the chain (Kreĭn; de Branges 1968 Thms 38–40; Romanov Thm 13).
* The equivalence "de Branges realisation exists ⟺ positivity".
* The echo localisation.

Theorem U is their assembly for window forms. Its only non-routine inputs are Theorem J (which guarantees that chains
coincide as families) and the observation, read from Romanov's own proof, that the gauge constant `a_t` is constant
without an endpoint hypothesis.

**New (to my knowledge; corpus grep for "Blaschke", "log-Sobolev", "type-zero", "gauge" and "Goupillaud" gives no prior
hit in phases 150–159).**
1. **Lemma A′.** The identity `b_w(γ)\overline{b_w(\barγ)} = 1` makes every explicit-formula form satisfy the de Branges
  axioms (H2) and (H3) identically, polar pair included. On a window, de Branges structure costs nothing; only
  positivity does. This is elementary, but it pinpoints why the angle cannot outsource positivity to structure.

  round 4 had only a HEURISTIC multiple-echo sketch.

  the local datum at `q`. It is rule-independent.
