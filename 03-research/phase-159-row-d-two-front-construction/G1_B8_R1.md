directly from `Γ` and the Euler product, so that `H(t) ≥ 0` holds by construction, and to take the associated de Branges
chain as the carrier.

## §0 Verdict

1. **Suzuki has already built this object in the literature** (CITED: arXiv 1606.05726v3; Tohoku Math. J. 74 (2022),
  arXiv 1907.07838; Adv. Stud. Pure Math. 84 (2020), arXiv 1907.07302; Ann. Inst. Fourier 75 (2025), arXiv 2012.11121).
  * The Hamiltonian is `H(t) = diag(1/γ(t), γ(t))` with `γ = m(t)²` and `m(t) = det(1+K[t])/det(1−K[t])`.
  * `K[t]` is a Hankel-type operator whose kernel is built from Γ and from the prime data. For the exponential
  version, the prime data enter through Ihara's coefficients `λ_θ(n) ≥ 0`, which are generated by `Λ`.
  * So `H(t) ≥ 0` holds structurally (`γ` is a square), which is exactly what the angle asked for. It holds wherever
  `H` is defined.
2. **What is actually decided is whether the chain survives.** The Fredholm determinants must not vanish for any
  `t ∈ [0, ∞)`. Suzuki (Tohoku Thm 1.2 and 1.3) proves that survival to `∞` is equivalent to the scattering function
  being inner, which is RH. The structural positivity therefore moves the sign into the *existence* of the object. This
  is the de Branges form of Lemma T and of OT-5′, and it happens in every canonical-system construction (§4, Lemma 4.1).
3. **New in this memo (PROVED; not found in the Suzuki texts read).** Define the **window Nehari norm**
  `N_t(η)` of the exponentiated scattering function `Θ_η = exp(−2η ξ′/ξ(½−iz))` (§2). I prove the following
  (Theorem B8.A).
  * (i) Dichotomy: `sup_t N_t(η)` is either `≤ 1` (RH) or `= ∞` (¬RH), for every `η > 0`. So RH is equivalent to
  *uniform boundedness* of a family of finite-window quantities. No margin is needed and no sharp constant is needed.
  * (ii) `η ↦ log N_t(η)` is subadditive: `N_t(η₁+η₂) ≤ N_t(η₁) N_t(η₂)`.
  * (iii) Log-Lipschitz stability under changes of the weights:
  `N_t(η; a) ≤ N_t(η; Λ)·exp(2η Σ|a(n)−Λ(n)| n^{−1/2})`. Compare O3, where a change of size `e^{−14T}` flips the
  Weil sign.
  * (iv) Linearisation: `N_t(0) = 1` and `N_t(η) ≥ 1 − η λ^W_t + o(η)`. This ties the family to the **complete**
  Weil window of P47. The constant was checked against the exact atom normalisation `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n` (§3.4).
  * (v) Causality: `N_t(η)` depends only on `Γ` and on `Λ(n)` for `n < e^{2t}`.
4. **What this does and does not change.**
  * The criterion (i) is **EQUIV**: it is a restatement of RH.
  * Properties (iii) and (i) show that the proved obstructions C8–C10 (`thm:finmod`, `cor:void`, O3) and C18
  (`thm:margin`) are obstructions to deriving the **sign of `W_T`**. They are **not** obstructions to this criterion,
  which is invariant, up to bounded factors, under the modified combs those theorems use.
  * The object test is not passed. OT-5′ fails in the de Branges way: the positive form (the Hardy/de Branges–Rovnyak
  norm) is prior and automatic, but the object exists for all `t` iff RH.
  * The remaining content is one uniform bound, `sup_t N_t(η) < ∞`. Its likely size under ¬RH is the lead for round 2
  (§6, HEURISTIC).

## §2 The construction (the carrier of this memo)

Notation. `s = ½ − iz` and `z = i(s − ½)`, so that `Im z > 0 ⟺ Re s > ½`. Put `G(s) = ½s(s−1)π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)`, so
`ξ = Gζ`. Define `ψ(z) := (ξ′/ξ)(½ − iz)`. For a weight sequence `a = (a(n))_{n≥2}` with `|a(n)| ≪ n^θ`, put

```
ψ_a(z) := (G′/G)(s) − Σ_n a(n) n^{−s}, ψ_Λ = ψ,
Θ_{η,a}(z) := exp(−2η ψ_a(z)), η > 0.
```

**Def 2.1 (the kernel).** `Θ_{η,a}` is analytic on `{Im z > c}` for some `c = c(a) > ½`, with polynomial growth there.
Indeed `|ψ_a| ≪ log(2+|z|)` on `Im z ≥ c`, so `|Θ_{η,a}| ≤ (2+|z|)^{Cη}`. By the Paley–Wiener–Schwartz half-plane theorem
(CITED) there is a unique distribution `K_{η,a}` with the following properties:
* its support lies in `[0,∞)`;
* `e^{−c′x}K_{η,a}` is tempered for `c′ > c`;
* `Θ_{η,a}(z) = ⟨K_{η,a}, e^{ixz}⟩` for `Im z > c`, which is Suzuki's convention (L3 (1.10)).

For `θ = η > 1` and `a = Λ`, this is Suzuki's continuous kernel `K_θ` (L3 Thm 1.2).

**Prop 2.2 (arithmetic–archimedean factorisation; PROVED).** For `a = Λ`:
`Θ_η = exp(−2η G′/G(s)) · Σ_n b_η(n) n^{−s}`, where
`b_η(n) = Σ_{k≥0} ((2η)^k/k!) Λ^{*k}(n) ≥ 0`. Here `Λ^{*k}` is the k-fold Dirichlet convolution, `Λ^{*0} = δ_1`, and
`b_η` is Ihara's `λ_η`, which is multiplicative. Hence

```
K_η = Σ_{n≥1} b_η(n) n^{−1/2} · g_η(· − log n), g_η := Laplace⁻¹[exp(−2η G′/G)] ∈ D′_+ ,
```

and on `(−∞, 2t)` only the terms `n < e^{2t}` contribute. Each `b_η(n)` with `n < e^{2t}` depends only on `Λ(m)` for
`m | n`.

*Proof.* Since `−ζ′/ζ = Σ Λ(n)n^{−s}` for `Re s > 1`, `exp(−2ηζ′/ζ) = Σ_k (2η)^k/k! (ΣΛ(n)n^{−s})^k`. This series
converges absolutely for `Re s > 1`, and its coefficients are `≥ 0` because `Λ ≥ 0`. We have
`n^{−s} = n^{−1/2}e^{iz log n}`, and the Laplace transform turns multiplication by `e^{iz log n}` into translation by
`log n`. The distribution `g_η` is supported in `[0,∞)`, by Paley–Wiener–Schwartz again: `exp(−2ηG′/G)` is analytic
with polynomial growth on `Im z > ½`, where `G` has no zeros, since its zeros are at `s = 1` and `s ∈ −2N`. Hence a
translate by `log n ≥ 2t` vanishes on `(−∞, 2t)`. ∎

**Def 2.3 (window Nehari norm).** For `t > 0`, put

```
N_t(η; a) := inf { ‖χ‖_{L^∞(R)} : χ ∈ L^∞(R), (F⁻¹χ)|_{(−∞,2t)} = K_{η,a}|_{(−∞,2t)} } ∈ [0, ∞],
```

where `F⁻¹χ(x) = (1/2π)∫χ(u)e^{−iux}du`, taken in `S′`. Write `N_t(η) := N_t(η; Λ)`. An admissible `χ` has
`F⁻¹χ = 0` on `(−∞,0)`, so it is the boundary value of a function in `H^∞(C₊)`, still denoted `χ`.

**Prop 2.4 (link to Suzuki's Hamiltonian; PROVED given CITED Nehari).** Suppose `K_η` is a continuous kernel, as for
`η > 1` by L3 Thm 1.2. Let `K_η[t]` be Suzuki's operator on `L²(−∞,t)` with kernel `K_η(x+y)`. Then:
* `‖K_η[t]‖ = N_t(η)`;
* `t ↦ N_t(η)` is nondecreasing;
* Suzuki's condition (K5) on `[0,τ)` holds ⟺ `N_s(η) < 1` for all `s < τ`.

On that range `H_η(t) = diag(1/γ,γ)`, `γ = m²`, is a Hamiltonian with `H_η ≥ 0`, by construction.

*Proof.*
1. The substitution `x ↦ t − x` turns `K_η[t]` into the Hankel operator on `L²(0,∞)` with kernel `k_t(σ) = K_η(2t − σ)`.
  Nehari's theorem (CITED) gives `‖Γ_{k_t}‖ = inf{‖φ‖_∞ : F⁻¹φ = k_t on (0,∞)}`. Reflecting and modulating `φ` turns
  this into Def 2.3.
2. Monotonicity: `K_η[s] = P_s K_η[t] P_s` for `s < t`, so the norms are nondecreasing.
3. (K5): `K_η[0] = 0`, and `s ↦ K_η[s]` is continuous in Hilbert–Schmidt norm (L2 §1). Hence `s ↦ ‖K_η[s]‖` is
  continuous and nondecreasing. `K_η[s]` is compact and self-adjoint, so `±‖K_η[s]‖` is an eigenvalue. Therefore
  "no eigenvalue ±1 on `[0,τ)`" ⟺ `‖K_η[s]‖ < 1` on `[0,τ)`.
4. `H ≥ 0`: `γ = m²` with `m` real and nonzero (L2 (1.4)). ∎

**The carrier (definition, for the object test).** For each `t` with `N_t(η) ≤ 1` and each admissible `χ` with
`‖χ‖_∞ ≤ 1`:
* take the de Branges–Rovnyak space `𝓗(χ)`, with kernel `(1 − χ(z)\overline{χ(w)})/(−2πi(z − w̄))`, which is `≥ 0`;
* the positive form is the `𝓗(χ)` norm. It is **defined for every Schur function, prior to and independent of any
  spectrum**.

When the chain exists up to `∞`, it is Suzuki's chain `𝔉(V_t) ⊂ K(Θ_η)` (L2 §6), a chain of subspaces of the model
space `H² ⊖ Θ_η H²`.

## §3 Theorem B8.A (the exponential window) — statements and proofs

**Theorem B8.A.** Let `η > 0` and `t > 0`. Every item is `PROVED`, using only Def 2.1, 2.3 and the classical tools of
§1. Nehari is not used.

* **(A1) RH ⟹ `N_t(η) ≤ 1` for all `t`.**
* **(A2) Dichotomy.** If `sup_t N_t(η) < ∞` for **one** `η > 0`, then RH holds. Hence
  `sup_t N_t(η) ∈ [0,1] ∪ {∞}` for every `η`, and RH ⟺ `sup_t N_t(η) < ∞`. — **EQUIV** (a restatement).
* **(A3) Submultiplicativity.** `N_t(η₁+η₂) ≤ N_t(η₁)·N_t(η₂)` (with `∞·x = ∞` for `x > 0`).
* **(A4) Stability under weight changes.** If `Σ_n |a(n) − Λ(n)| n^{−1/2} =: D(a) < ∞`, then
  `e^{−2ηD(a)} N_t(η;Λ) ≤ N_t(η;a) ≤ e^{2ηD(a)} N_t(η;Λ)`.
* **(A5) Linearisation at η = 0.** `N_t(0) = 1` (with `K_0 = δ_0`), and `N_t(η) ≥ 1 − η λ^W_t + o(η)` as `η ↓ 0`, where
  `λ^W_t = inf{W_t(F) : F ∈ L²(I_t), ‖F‖ = 1}` is the **complete** form of P47 `eq:W` on `I_t = (−t,t)`. In particular,
  if `λ^W_t < 0` then `N_t(η) > 1` for all sufficiently small `η > 0`.
* **(A6) Causality.** `N_t(η)` is a function of `Γ` and of `(Λ(n))_{n<e^{2t}}` only.

### 3.1 Proof of (A1)

Assume RH. By Hadamard's product (CITED), `Re ξ′/ξ(s) = Σ_ρ Re (s−ρ)^{−1}`, with the conjugate zeros paired. Under RH,
`Re(s−ρ)^{−1} = (σ−½)/|s−ρ|² > 0` for `σ > ½`. So `ψ` is analytic on `C₊`, since its poles are the zeros of `ξ`, all on
`z ∈ R`, and `Re ψ ≥ 0` on `C₊`. Hence `Θ_η = exp(−2ηψ) ∈ H^∞(C₊)` with `|Θ_η| ≤ 1`.

Its boundary function `χ₀ ∈ L^∞(R)` has `F⁻¹χ₀` supported in `[0,∞)`. The Fourier–Laplace transform of `F⁻¹χ₀` is the
Poisson extension, namely `Θ_η` on `C₊`. That transform agrees with `⟨K_η, e^{ixz}⟩` on `Im z > c`. The Fourier–Laplace
transform is injective on `D′_+` with exponential growth, so `F⁻¹χ₀ = K_η` on all of `R`. Thus `χ₀` is admissible for
every `t`, and `N_t(η) ≤ ‖χ₀‖_∞ ≤ 1`. ∎

### 3.2 Proof of (A2)

Suppose `N_{t_k}(η) ≤ M` along a sequence `t_k → ∞`. Since `N_t` is nondecreasing in `t` (an admissible `χ` for `t` is
admissible for `s < t`), this holds for every `t`.
1. Choose admissible `χ_k` with `‖χ_k‖ ≤ M + 1`. By Banach–Alaoglu, a subsequence converges weak-* in `L^∞(R)` to some
  `χ`, with `‖χ‖ ≤ M+1`. Weak-* convergence in `L^∞` implies convergence in `S′` of `F⁻¹χ_k → F⁻¹χ`.
2. For `φ ∈ C_c^∞(R)` we have `supp φ ⊂ (−∞, 2t_k)` for large `k`, so `⟨F⁻¹χ_k, φ⟩ = ⟨K_η, φ⟩`. Hence
  `F⁻¹χ = K_η` in `D′(R)`.
3. So `K_η` is tempered and supported in `[0,∞)`. Its Fourier–Laplace transform is the Poisson extension of `χ`, which
  is analytic and bounded by `M+1` on `C₊`, and on `Im z > c` it equals `Θ_η`. So **`Θ_η` extends to a bounded
  analytic function on `C₊`**, and this extension equals `exp(−2ηψ)` on `C₊` minus the poles of `ψ`, which is
  connected.
4. Suppose `ρ₀ = β₀ + iγ₀` with `β₀ > ½` were a zero of `ξ`, of multiplicity `m ≥ 1`. Put `z₀ = i(ρ₀ − ½)`, so
  `Im z₀ = β₀ − ½ > 0`. Near `z₀`, `ψ(z) = im/(z − z₀) + O(1)`. Along `z = z₀ − ir` (`0 < r < Im z₀`, inside `C₊`),
  `−2ηψ = 2ηm/r + O(1)`, so `|Θ_η| → ∞`. This contradicts boundedness.
5. Hence there is no zero with `β > ½`. By the functional equation there is none with `β < ½`, and RH holds.

The dichotomy follows from (A1). ∎

*Remark.* The mechanism is that exponentiation turns an off-line **pole** of `ψ` into an **essential singularity** of
`Θ_η`. No bounded function tolerates one, so *any* uniform bound forces the sharp one. The comparison is
`ψ(x) − x ≪ x^{θ}`, which only yields a zero-free region `β ≤ θ`, with no dichotomy.

### 3.3 Proof of (A3)

Take admissible `χ_i` for `(η_i, t)`. Both lie in `H^∞(C₊)`, so `χ₁χ₂ ∈ H^∞` and `‖χ₁χ₂‖ ≤ ‖χ₁‖‖χ₂‖`.
* The distributions `D_i := F⁻¹χ_i ∈ D′_+` have exponential-type Laplace transforms. By the Laplace convolution
  theorem on `D′_+` (CITED, Vladimirov §9), `F⁻¹(χ₁χ₂) = D₁ ∗ D₂`.
* For `x < 2t`, `(D₁∗D₂)|_{(−∞,2t)}` depends only on `D_i|_{(−∞,2t)}`, since the supports lie in `[0,∞)`. So it
  equals `(K_{η₁} ∗ K_{η₂})|_{(−∞,2t)}`.
* The Laplace transform of `K_{η₁} ∗ K_{η₂}` on `Im z > c` is `Θ_{η₁}Θ_{η₂} = Θ_{η₁+η₂}`, which gives
  `K_{η₁}∗K_{η₂} = K_{η₁+η₂}`.

So `χ₁χ₂` is admissible for `(η₁+η₂, t)`. Taking the infimum proves the claim. ∎

### 3.4 Proof of (A4)

Write `Θ_{η,a} = Θ_{η,Λ}·B`, with `B(z) = exp(2η Σ_n (a−Λ)(n) n^{−1/2} e^{iz log n})`.
* On `C₊`, `|e^{iz log n}| ≤ 1`, so `B ∈ H^∞(C₊)` and `‖B‖_∞ ≤ e^{2ηD(a)}`.
* Expanding the exponential gives `F⁻¹B = Σ_m c_m δ_{log m}`, a discrete measure supported in `[0,∞)` with
  `Σ|c_m|m^{−1/2} < ∞`.
* As in §3.3, if `χ` is admissible for `(η,Λ,t)` then `χB` is admissible for `(η,a,t)`, so
  `N_t(η;a) ≤ ‖B‖_∞ N_t(η;Λ)`.
* The reverse inequality follows from `B^{−1} = exp(−…)`, which has the same bound. ∎

**Consistency check of the constant (PROVED; it cross-checks (A5)).** In P47, a weight change `δa(n)` changes `v_n` by
`2δa(n)/√n`. It changes `W_t(F)` by `−(2δa(n)/√n)·Re c_F(log n)`, with `|c_F(u)| ≤ ‖F‖²`. So
`|Δλ^W_t| ≤ 2D(a)`. Linearising (A4) in `η` gives the same factor, `e^{±2ηD} = 1 ± 2ηD + O(η²)`. The two first-order
constants agree.

### 3.5 Proof of (A5)

Let `f ∈ C_c^∞(−t,t)` and put `P := f ∗ f̄`, with `P(σ) = ∫f(σ−x)\overline{f(x)}dx`. Then `P ∈ C_c^∞(−2t,2t)`.

**Upper bound on the form.** For any admissible `χ`,
`q_η(f) := ⟨K_η, P⟩ = ⟨F⁻¹χ, P⟩ = (1/2π)∫χ(u) f̂(u)\hat{\bar f}(u) du`. Here `f̂(u) = ∫f e^{−iux}`, so the
Fourier transform of `P` is `f̂·\hat{\bar f}`. Therefore `|q_η(f)| ≤ ‖χ‖_∞‖f‖²_2` by Cauchy–Schwarz and Plancherel,
`‖f̂‖² = 2π‖f‖²`, and `|q_η(f)| ≤ N_t(η)‖f‖²`.

**Evaluation of the form.** By Laplace–Parseval on `D′_+` (CITED),
`q_η(f) = (1/2π)∫_{Im z = v} Θ_η(z) P̂(z) dz` for `v > c`, where `P̂(z) = ∫P(x)e^{−ixz}dx = Φ_P(s)` and
`Φ_P(s) = ∫P e^{(s−½)x}dx` (P47 notation). The function `P̂` is entire and rapidly decreasing on horizontal lines.

* **At `η = 0`.** `Θ = 1`. Shifting the line to `v = 0` gives `(1/2π)∫_R P̂ = P(0) = ⟨Jf, f⟩`, where
  `Jf(x) = f(−x)`.
* **First order.** On `Im z = v`, `|ψ| ≪ log(2+|u|)`. So `(Θ_η − 1)/η → −2ψ`, dominated by
  `2|ψ|(2+|u|)^{2Cη}|P̂|`, which is integrable. Hence
  `q_η(f) = ⟨Jf,f⟩ − 2η·(1/2π)∫_{Im z=v} ψ P̂ dz + o(η)`.
  With `du = i ds` along `Re s = ½+v`, which is traversed downward, `(1/2π)∫ψP̂ dz = I(Φ_P)`, where
  `I(Φ) := (1/2πi)∫_{(½+v)}(ξ′/ξ)(s)Φ(s)ds`.
* **The explicit formula.** Shifting to `Re s = ½ − v` and using `ξ′/ξ(s) = −ξ′/ξ(1−s)` gives
  `I(Φ) + I(Φ∘(1−·)) = Σ_ρ Φ(ρ)` (CITED, classical, P47 `eq:W`/Paper 45 Thm 9.4 for `C_c^∞` tests). Also
  `Φ_P(1−s) = Φ_{JP}(s)`.
* **Parities.** If `f` is even or odd then `P` is even. In both cases `I(Φ_P) = ½Σ_ρΦ_P(ρ)`.
  * Let `c_f(σ) = ∫f(σ+x)\overline{f(x)}dx`, so that `Σ_ρ Φ_{c_f}(ρ) = W_t(f)`, which is P47 `eq:W` in its hermitian
  form (R158_001 A1′).
  * For even `f`, `P = c_f`. For odd `f`, `P = −c_f`, because `\bar f(−x) = −\bar f(x)`.
  * Hence, for unit `f` of parity `ε ∈ {±1}`: **`ε·q_η(f) = 1 − η W_t(f) + o_f(η)`.**

**Conclusion.** Since `|q_η(f)| ≤ N_t(η)` for unit `f`, we get `N_t(η) ≥ 1 − ηW_t(f) + o_f(η)` for every unit `f` of
definite parity. Parity sectors are `W`-orthogonal: the cross terms vanish by the symmetry `ρ ↔ 1−ρ` of the zero set,
using `Φ_f(1−s) = εΦ_f(s)`. So the infimum over parity-definite unit `f` equals the infimum over all unit `f` in the
form core `C_c^∞(I_t)`, which is P47 `thm:KEP2` (proof). That infimum is `λ^W_t`. Hence
`liminf_{η↓0}(N_t(η) − 1)/η ≥ −λ^W_t`.
For `N_t(0) = 1`: `χ = 1` is admissible for `K_0 = δ_0`. Any admissible `χ` has `F⁻¹χ − δ_0` supported in
`[2t,∞)`, so `χ(iy) → 1` as `y → ∞` and `‖χ‖ ≥ 1`. **Not proved:** `limsup_{η↓0} N_t(η) ≤ 1`, i.e. continuity of
`N_t` at `η = 0` from above. Under RH it holds trivially by (A1); unconditionally it is part of (Q2). ∎

*Normalisation check.* The sign is forced. With the opposite sign, (A1) would give `W_t ≤ 0` on even tests under RH,
contradicting Yoshida's window (`W_t ≻ 0` for `t ≤ ½log2`, CITED in the record). The size `1·η` matches §3.4.

### 3.6 (A6)

This follows from Prop 2.2 and Def 2.3, because `N_t` reads `K_η` only on `(−∞, 2t)`. ∎

## §4 What the construction cannot do: obstructions (PROVED unless labelled)

**Lemma 4.1 (in a canonical system, positivity is free and the sign lies in survival; PROVED, elementary).**
* Every Hamiltonian is `≥ 0` by definition. On any interval where a chain of de Branges or RKHS spaces is defined, the
  kernels are positive (the Gram identity, as in phase 64 §3).
* For a construction `a ↦ H_a` from prime data, "structural positivity" is therefore empty. The whole content is the
  survival time `τ(a) := sup{t : H_a defined on [0,t)}`.
* In Suzuki's framework (Prop 2.4), `τ(a) = sup{t : N_t(η;a) < 1}`, and `τ = ∞` ⟺ `Θ_{η,a}` is inner (L2 Thm 1.2
  and 1.3; (K6) holds because `Θ_η` has essential singularities under ¬RH and is not a ratio of entire functions of
  exponential type).

This is Lemma T and OT-5′ in de Branges coordinates: the prior positive form exists, and the **existence** of the object
for all `t` is RH.

**Lemma 4.2 (rigidity of the framework, OT-4/OT-5; PROVED).** Definitions 2.1–2.3 apply without change to the following:
* `Ξ_a = ξ(s+a)ξ(s−a)`: the log-derivative has nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients `Λ(n)(n^{a}+n^{−a})`, and the
  functional equation holds;
* Epstein `Ξ_Q`: `−Z′/Z` is a Dirichlet series for `Re s` large, and the functional equation holds;
* any Beurling system with a meromorphic `ζ_B`.

For each of these that has an off-line zero, `sup_t N_t = ∞` by the proof of (A2), and the criterion answers correctly.
But **the definition does not break for them**. So a proof of `sup_t N_t(η) < ∞` for ζ must use a property that fails
for `Ξ_a` (A3 of P46), for `Ξ_Q` (A7/A8/A9, T2) and for DMV/Zhang Beurling systems (T3, modulo H-DMV). The framework
passes OT-4 only in the weak sense: the break must sit in a line of the proof (R158_003 A14).

**Lemma 4.3 (what (A4) does to C8–C10 and C18; PROVED).**
* `thm:finmod`, `cor:void` and O3 exclude derivations of the **Weil sign** `λ^W_T ≥ 0` from class properties. They do so
  because modified combs `a = Λ + εδ_{n₀}`, `Λ·1_{n≥N}` break that sign. By (A4), those combs change `N_t(η)` by at most
  the factor `e^{2ηD(a)}`, uniformly in `t`.
* So, under RH for ζ, `sup_t N_t(η;a) ≤ e^{2ηD(a)} < ∞`. The modified combs satisfy the **boundedness** criterion
  exactly when ζ does, as they must: their `Θ` has the same singularities as ζ's.
* **Hence C8–C10 do not obstruct a derivation of `sup_t N_t < ∞`.**
* By (A2), C18 (no uniform margin) does not bite either. Under RH, `sup_t N_t(η) = 1` exactly: `N_t → ‖Θ_η‖_∞ = 1` by
  weak-* lower semicontinuity. So there is no margin below 1, and none is needed.
* What is **not** changed: C12–C15 (the `Ξ_a`, `Ξ_Q`, Beurling and field-uniformity gates), by Lemma 4.2. Also C1: the
  quantity is infinite-dimensional at each window.

**Lemma 4.4 (the Euler product does not give the bound; PROVED at the level stated, HEURISTIC beyond it).**
* The local factor `exp(2η Σ_k log p·p^{−ks})` lies in `H^∞(C₊)`, with norm `≤ exp(2η log p/(√p − 1))`.
* At window `t`, factors with `p ≥ e^{2t}` have trivial data on `(−∞,2t)`, so their `N_t` equals 1.
* So (A3) gives `N_t(η) ≲ N_t(archimedean·polar part) · exp(2η Σ_{p<e^{2t}} log p/(√p−1))`, which grows like
  `exp(O(η e^{t}))`.
* **Caveat.** The split must keep the factor `s−1` with the prime part, because `G′/G` and `ζ′/ζ` each have a pole at
  `s = 1` (`z = i/2 ∈ C₊`) and these cancel only in the product. The honest statement is for the grouped factors.
  Beyond that, the bound is HEURISTIC.

This is the "no finite-S" constraint (C17) in the new coordinates. Place-by-place bounds diverge like `e^{√X}`, the
divergence of the Euler product at `σ = ½`, and the criterion needs `O(1)`.

## §6 Lead for round 2 (HEURISTIC / OPEN) and the weakest step

* **(Q1, OPEN, the main lead) Size of `N_t(η)` under ¬RH.**
  * Heuristic: an off-line zero of multiplicity `m` makes `Θ_η` behave like `exp(2iηm/(z−z₀))` near `z₀`. Window data up
  to `2t` pin `χ` down to resolution `~1/t` above `z₀`. Balancing `e^{2ηm/ε}` against `e^{−2tε}` suggests
  `log N_t(η) ≍ √(ηm t)`, with the height `γ₀` entering only through the time at which the zero becomes visible.
  * If a rigorous lower bound `log N_t(η) ≫ √t` under ¬RH held, RH would be equivalent to `log N_t(η) = o(√t)`. That is
  an estimate with a **gap**, which the Weil sign never has (C18, O3).
  * Status: HEURISTIC. No proof. The obstacle is controlling admissible `χ` from data on `[0,2t)` alone (§3.2 gives
  only qualitative unboundedness).
* **(Q2, OPEN)** The reverse linearisation `N_t(η) ≤ 1 − ηλ^W_t + o(η)`. With (A5), it would make `η ↦ N_t(η)` an exact
  deformation of the complete Weil window. It needs uniformity of the `o(η)` term over the unit ball of the form domain,
  and the log-order symbol makes this delicate.
* **(Q3, OPEN)** Whether (A3) together with (A5) constrains `t ↦ N_t` at all. Subadditivity works *upward* in `η` and
  the linearisation at `η → 0`; neither gives anything *in `t`*. No uniformity in `t` is obtained here. **None of
  (A1)–(A6) proves any sign at any window.**

**Weakest step.**
* Theorem B8.A is proved, but its content is a restatement (A2 is EQUIV), and the carrier fails OT-5′ (Lemma 4.1).
* The one genuinely new lever is (A4) with Lemma 4.3: the modified-comb obstructions do not apply to the boundedness
  criterion. It carries **no proof weight** toward the bound itself. Proving `sup_t N_t(η) < ∞` still has to separate ζ
  from `Ξ_a`, `Ξ_Q` and Beurling systems (Lemma 4.2), and nothing here does that.
* Technically, the least-checked link is §3.5. It uses the Laplace–Parseval identity for distributional `K_η` when
  `η ≤ 1`, where Suzuki's continuity of `K_θ` (θ>1) is unavailable. The identity is standard for `D′_+` with
  polynomially bounded Laplace transform, but the panel should verify it.
* Lemma 4.3 overrides nothing in the record. It says only that C8–C10 are statements about the sign functional. The
  panel should confirm that reading against the exact statements of `thm:finmod`/`cor:void` (P47 l.3452, l.3563).
