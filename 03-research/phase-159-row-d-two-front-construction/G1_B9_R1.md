## §0 Verdict

The transport can be made fully precise, and it is a **restatement**. Its one new structural fact (Theorem 4, the
*closure theorem*) is this: close up the Weil distributions of all curves over all finite fields, allowing dilations
and positive multiples. The result is **exactly** the cone `𝒫_sym` of symmetric positive-definite tempered
distributions, i.e. Bochner's cone. So Castelnuovo positivity, summed over all curves, carries no information beyond
positive-definiteness. On this cone, "ζ belongs" is RH. At a window it is `λ^W_T ≥ 0`, up to the boundary set
`{λ^W_T = 0}`, the same sandwich as KEP (P47 `thm:KEP3`). **The positivity of the limit object is derived from
geometry, and it is defined before any spectrum. But ζ's membership is the sign itself.** So the object fails OT-5′
at the one place that matters.

The undilated limit, the literal "`q → 1`", fails outright and unconditionally (Theorem 2). Every limit of undilated
curve data is supported on `{0} ∪ {±log m : m a prime power}`, while `𝔚_ζ` has a nonzero smooth density on
`(0.3, log 2)`. So no such limit contains ζ, and none has an archimedean place. Weil's normalisation ties the lattice
step to the pole pair: step `log q ≥ log 2` exactly when the poles sit at `{0,1}` (Theorem 3). The step can go to 0
only by dilating, and dilating moves the pole pair to `½ ± λ/2`. So the approximants never carry ζ's hyperbolic plane
(the rulings `F_1, F_2`). ζ's polar term comes out of their zero sides.

**What survives the limit:** the carrier (the lattice becomes the line, by Riemann sums and Poisson), the explicit
formula, the Toeplitz/Gram form of the Frobenius graphs, the degree and ampleness, and the model constant as a
continuum function `s(γ,θ)` (Theorem 6).
**What does not survive:** finite genus, integrality of `NS(C×C)`, the surface itself, and hence the proof of the
Hodge index (Riemann–Roch on `S` plus ampleness).
**The constant `√(7/11)` does not survive.** It is a lattice under-resolution effect (`γ·log q = θ_u ≈ 1.93`). As the
step goes to 0 the model constant converges to the arithmetic one, and `1 − min_θ s(γ,θ) ≍ γ^{−2}` (Theorem 6).

## §1 Setting and notation

`C/F_q` is a smooth projective geometrically irreducible curve of genus `g`. Write `η := log q`, and let
`α_1,…,α_{2g}` be the Frobenius eigenvalues, `u_j = α_j/√q = e^{iθ_j}` with `|u_j| = 1` (Weil; P46 (X27), `CITED`),
`N_m = #C(F_{q^m}) = q^m + 1 − Σ_j α_j^m`, and `s_n := Σ_j u_j^n` (real, `s_{−n} = s_n`, `s_0 = 2g`).

**Fourier convention (P47 `thm:KEP2`).** For a positive tempered measure `ν` on `R_τ`, the distribution `ν̂` on `R_u` is
`⟨ν̂, h⟩ := ∫ ĥ dν`, with `ĥ(τ) = ∫ h(u)e^{iτu}du`. P47 `def:KEP`: `g` is the window datum (`eq:gdatum`), built from
`C_0`, `K`, `2cosh(u/2)` and the atoms `−(v_n/2)(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n})`, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`. **It is defined from `Λ` and
Γ only.** Set `𝔚_ζ := g`. By `thm:KEP2`, `⟨𝔚_ζ, F⋆F̃⟩ = W_T(F)` for `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`.

**Definition 1.1 (the Weil distribution of a curve).** Let `μ_C := Σ_j Σ_{k∈Z} δ_{(θ_j + 2πk)/η}` be the zero ordinates of
`ξ_C` (P46 Def 21.1) with all their periodic translates, and set `𝔚_C := μ̂_C`. For `λ > 0` define the dilate by
`⟨D_λ𝔚, h⟩ := ⟨𝔚, h(·/λ)⟩/λ`, i.e. `D_λ𝔚(u) = 𝔚(λu)`. Its spectral measure is `μ_C` pushed forward by `τ ↦ λτ`.

**Lemma 1.2 (Poisson form and prime-side form; `PROVED` from `CLASSICAL`).**
(i) `𝔚_C = η Σ_{n∈Z} s_n δ_{nη}`.
(ii) (prime side) `s_n = q^{|n|/2} + q^{−|n|/2} − q^{−|n|/2}N_{|n|}` for `n ≠ 0`, and `s_0 = 2g`. Hence
`𝔚_C = 𝔓_C := η Σ_n p_n δ_{nη}`, where `p_n` is computed from the point counts alone.
(iii) Unpacking `N_m = Σ_{d|m} d B_d`, the negative part of `𝔓_C` is the measure
`η Σ_{n≠0} q^{−|n|/2}N_{|n|} δ_{nη} = Σ_{P closed} Σ_{k≥1} log NP · NP^{−k/2}(δ_{k log NP} + δ_{−k log NP})`. This is exactly
the shape of ζ's prime atoms `(v_n/2)(δ_{±log n})`, with `Λ(P^k) = log NP`. The `n = 0` term is `2gη δ_0 = η(2 − N_0)δ_0`, with
`N_0 = 2 − 2g` (P46 Thm 21.5): a polar sample `2η` minus the conductor term `(2−2g)η`. With the polar density `η Σ_n 2cosh(nη/2)δ_{nη} → 2cosh(u/2)du` it reproduces the three-part
shape of `eq:gdatum`, except that the archimedean Lévy pair `C_0δ_0 − ½Pf K(|u|)` is replaced by the single atom
`(2−2g)ηδ_0` with the sign absorbed (P46 mismatch 5).
(iv) (Weil for `C`) `𝔚_C` is positive definite: `⟨𝔚_C, F⋆F̃⟩ = Σ_{j,k}|F̂((θ_j+2πk)/η)|² ≥ 0`. This is the **complete**
Castelnuovo form (P46 Thm 21.7(ii) and the identity `I_∞ = 2Φ(0)Φ(1) − W`).

*Proof.* (i) Poisson summation: `Σ_k ĥ(γ + 2πk/η) = η Σ_n h(nη)e^{iγnη}`; sum over `j`. (ii) is
`N_m = q^m + 1 − q^{m/2}s_m`. (iii) is a regrouping. (iv): `μ_C ≥ 0` because `|u_j| = 1`, which is Weil's theorem.
Equivalently, it is P46 Thm 21.7 with `E(n) = ηF(nη)`, which is what P46 (21.5) becomes after Poisson. ∎

Remark. (iv) is where the geometry sits. The positivity is the Hodge index on `C×C`, proved from Riemann–Roch on the
surface before, and without, any knowledge of the `α_j`. **Per member of the family, OT-5′ is passed** (`CITED`,
classical).

## §2 The undilated limit: the literal "q → 1" (Theorem 2)

**Theorem 2 (support obstruction; `PROVED`, unconditional).** Let `𝒱_0` be the real linear span of
`{𝔚_C : C a curve over some finite field}`, and let `\overline{𝒱_0}` be its sequential closure in `𝒟'(R)`. Then:
(a) every element of `\overline{𝒱_0}` is supported in `L := {0} ∪ {±log m : m ≥ 2 a prime power}`, a closed discrete
set;
(b) `𝔚_ζ ∉ \overline{𝒱_0}`;
(c) no element of `\overline{𝒱_0}` restricts on `(−log 2, log 2)` to anything but a finite sum `Σ c_j δ^{(j)}_0`. So no
limit of undilated curve data has an archimedean place in the sense of OT-12 (a non-atomic Lévy kernel near 0).

*Proof.* (a) By Lemma 1.2(i), `supp 𝔚_C ⊆ ηZ`, and `nη = log(q^n)` with `q^n` a prime power. So every element of `𝒱_0`
is supported in `L`. `L` is closed and locally finite, since `log m ∈ [a,b]` for only finitely many `m`. If
`𝔚_k → 𝔚` in `𝒟'` and `φ ∈ C_c^∞(R∖L)`, then `⟨𝔚,φ⟩ = lim⟨𝔚_k,φ⟩ = 0`.
(b) `(0.3, log 2) ∩ L = ∅`. On that interval `𝔚_ζ = g` has no atoms and equals the absolutely continuous density
`d_*(u) = 2cosh(u/2) − ½K(u)`, which is `> 0` on `[0.3, ∞)`. That fact is established in the proof of P47 `thm:Zexh`
and quoted in `thm:KEP1(ii)`, `PROVED` there. So `supp 𝔚_ζ ⊄ L`.
(c) `L ∩ (−log 2, log 2) = {0}`, and a distribution supported at a point is a finite sum of derivatives of `δ`
(Schwartz). ∎

**Consequence (P46 mismatch 5, upgraded).** "The model has no archimedean place" is not a model artefact that a limit
could repair. Without rescaling time, no limit of function-field data has one. `PROVED`.

## §3 Step and weight rigidity (Theorem 3)

**Theorem 3 (`PROVED`).** Consider a dilated curve datum `D_λ𝔚_C`. Its prime-side expression is
`(η/λ)Σ_n p_n δ_{nη/λ}`, its lattice step is `η/λ`, and its polar density is locally `λ^{−1}·2cosh(λu/2)`. Its
"hyperbolic plane" is therefore the pair of evaluations at `s = ½ ± λ/2`, not at `{0,1}`. In particular:
(i) the datum has ζ's polar normalisation (pole pair at `{0,1}`, polar density `2cosh(u/2)`) **iff `λ = 1`**, and then
its step is `log q ≥ log 2`. So the sub-family with ζ's hyperbolic plane is `𝒱_0`, excluded by Theorem 2;
(ii) a family whose steps `η_k/λ_k → 0` must have `λ_k → ∞`, because `η_k ≥ log 2`. Its pole pairs then run off to
`½ ± ∞`. In any limit of such a family, ζ's polar term `2cosh(u/2)` cannot be the limit of the approximants' polar
terms `λ_k^{−1}2cosh(λ_ku/2)`, which diverge on every window. It arises from the cancellation of polar term against
prime atoms inside each approximant, i.e. from its zero side `η Σ s_n δ_{nη}`.

*Proof.* Substitute `δ(λu − a) = λ^{−1}δ(u − a/λ)` into Lemma 1.2(ii). The polar sum
`(η/λ)Σ_n 2cosh(nη/2)δ_{nη/λ}` is a Riemann sum of `λ^{−1}2cosh(λu/2)du` at mesh `η/λ`. `e^{±λu/2} = e^{(s−½)u}` at
`s = ½ ± λ/2`. (i) and (ii) follow. ∎

**Remark 3.1 (the genus in the continuum; `PROVED` + `HEURISTIC`).** At `λ = 1` the conductor atom is `(2−2g)ηδ_0`. A
limit `η → 0` with a finite archimedean-like constant would need `(2g−2)η → L < ∞`, i.e. `g ≍ L/η → ∞`. This is the
precise sense of P46 Rem 21.23's "infinite genus" (`PROVED` as arithmetic). That the Riemann–Roch threshold
`deg(nD) > deg K_S = 4g − 4` in the proof of the Hodge index then diverges is `HEURISTIC`: it is the reason the
*proof* does not pass to the limit, though the *conclusion* does, by closedness (§4).

**Remark 3.2 (Kapranov–Smirnov / Manin; `HEURISTIC`).** Formal `q`-interpolation keeps identities that are polynomial
in `q`, such as the explicit formula and `Γ_α·Γ_β = deg(α−β)`. It does not keep inequalities obtained from
integrality, such as `deg(m + nπ) = #ker ≥ 0`, i.e. `a² ≤ 4q` (P46 (21.14)). Connes–Consani's counting function
`N(q)`, `q ∈ [1,∞)`, for `\overline{Spec Z}` is of the first kind. Its positivity is A8 (`Λ ≥ 0`), not the Hodge index
(C13–C14). No formal interpolation carries a derived inequality to `q = 1`.

## §4 The dilated limit: the closure theorem (Theorem 4)

Let `𝒦 := {c·D_λ𝔚_C : c > 0, λ > 0, C a curve over a finite field}`, let `\overline{𝒦}` be its sequential closure in
`𝒮'(R)`, and let `𝒫_sym` be the cone of even positive-definite tempered distributions, i.e. `ν̂` with `ν` a positive
symmetric tempered measure (Bochner–Schwartz).

**Theorem 4 (closure theorem; `PROVED` modulo the `CITED` realisation input (KS) below).**
(a) `\overline{𝒦} = 𝒫_sym`.
(b) (global) **RH ⟺ `𝔚_ζ ∈ \overline{𝒦}`.** Equivalently, in prime-side form: RH ⟺ the datum `g` of `eq:gdatum`,
built from `Λ` and Γ only, is an `𝒮'`-limit of `c_k D_{λ_k}𝔓_{C_k}`, i.e. of rescaled point-count distributions of
curves. `EQUIVALENT`.
(c) (window) Say `T` is *transportable* if `g|_{(−2T,2T)}` is a `𝒟'((−2T,2T))`-limit of restrictions of elements of
`𝒦`. Then
`λ^W_T > 0 ⟹ KEP(T) solvable ⟹ T transportable ⟹ λ^W_T ≥ 0`.
This is the **complete** form, and the same sandwich as `thm:KEP3(a)`.

**(KS), the realisation input (`CITED`, secondary: Katz–Sarnak 1999 Ch. 10, via Kedlaya–Sutherland 0803.4462).** For
fixed `g ≥ 1`, as `q → ∞` the unitarised Frobenius classes of hyperelliptic curves of genus `g` over `F_q` equidistribute
in the conjugacy classes of `USp(2g)` with respect to Haar measure. The Weyl density is positive off a null set, so
Haar charges every nonempty open set. Hence for every `(θ_1,…,θ_g) ∈ [0,π]^g` and every `ε > 0` there is `q` and a curve
`C/F_q` of genus `g` whose angles satisfy `|θ_j(C) − θ_j| < ε` after reordering.

*Proof of (a).* (⊆) By Lemma 1.2(iv), each `D_λ𝔚_C` is the Fourier transform of a positive symmetric tempered measure,
and so is every positive multiple. `𝒫_sym` is sequentially closed in `𝒮'`: `⟨𝔚_k, φ⋆φ̃⟩ ≥ 0` passes to the limit for
`φ ∈ 𝒮`, and Bochner–Schwartz gives back a positive measure. So `\overline{𝒦} ⊆ 𝒫_sym`.
(⊇) Let `ν` be positive, symmetric and tempered, with `ν([−R,R]) ≤ C_ν(1+R)^N`, and fix `φ ∈ 𝒮(R_τ)`. It suffices to
make `|∫φ dν_P − ∫φ dν| → 0` for spectral measures `ν_P` of elements of `𝒦`. Fix `P > 0` large and write
`M_P := ν([−P/2,P/2]) ≤ C_ν(1+P)^N`.
*Quantise.* Choose a cell length `δ_P` and a weight `c_P`. On `[0,P/2)`, put `⌊ν(cell)/c_P⌋` atoms at the centre of
each cell (the cell at 0 split half and half between `±`), and reflect. This gives a symmetric multiset `Z_P ⊂
(−P/2,P/2)` of even size `2g_P`, with
`|c_PΣ_{z∈Z_P}φ(z) − ∫_{[−P/2,P/2]}φ dν| ≤ M_Pδ_P‖φ′‖_∞ + (P/δ_P)c_P‖φ‖_∞`.
*Realise.* Put `θ_j := 2πz_j/P ∈ [0,π]` for the nonnegative half of `Z_P`. By (KS) pick `C_P` of genus `g_P` with angles
within `ε_P`. Take `λ_P := ηP/(2π)`, `η = log q(C_P)`. Then `D_{λ_P}𝔚_{C_P}` has spectral measure
`Σ_j Σ_k (δ_{±θ_j(C_P)P/2π + kP})`.
*Error.* There are three terms:
- main copy `k = 0`: at most `c_P·2g_P·‖φ′‖_∞·ε_PP/2π ≤ M_P‖φ′‖_∞ε_PP`;
- copies `k ≠ 0`: at most `c_P·2g_P·Σ_{k≠0} sup_{|τ|≥(|k|−½)P}|φ| ≤ 2M_P·C_{φ,m}Σ_{k≥1}((k−½)P)^{−m}`;
- the tail of `ν`: `∫_{|τ|>P/2}|φ|dν`.
Take `m = N+2`, `δ_P = P^{−N−2}`, `c_P = δ_P²P^{−N−2}`, `ε_P = P^{−N−3}`. Then all terms go to 0 as `P → ∞`, and
`c_P·D_{λ_P}𝔚_{C_P} → ν̂` in `𝒮'`. ∎
*Proof of (b).* (⇐) `𝔚_ζ ∈ 𝒫_sym` by (a). Then `W_T(F) = ⟨𝔚_ζ, F⋆F̃⟩ ≥ 0` for all `T` and all `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, which
extends to the form domain as in `thm:KEP2`, and Weil's criterion gives RH. (⇒) Under RH, `𝔚_ζ = μ̂_ζ ∈ 𝒫_sym`
(`thm:KEP1(iii)`); apply (a). The prime-side form is Lemma 1.2(ii) applied to each approximant. ∎
*Proof of (c).* The first arrow is `thm:KEP3(a)`. For the second, let `ν ∈ 𝔖(T)`. Then `ν̂ = g` on `(−2T,2T)`, and (a)
gives `𝒦 ∋ 𝔚_k → ν̂` in `𝒮'`, hence in `𝒟'((−2T,2T))`. For the third, each `𝔚_k ∈ 𝒦` has `⟨𝔚_k, F⋆F̃⟩ ≥ 0` for
`F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, where `supp F⋆F̃ ⊂ (−2T,2T)`. In the limit `W_T(F) = ⟨g, F⋆F̃⟩ ≥ 0`, and the form-core argument of
`thm:KEP2` gives `λ^W_T ≥ 0`. ∎

**Reading.** Theorem 4 is the exact content of "the limit of the Castelnuovo forms".
(i) Every member's positivity is a geometric theorem, so the positive form on `\overline{𝒦}` is defined before the
spectrum and without it.
(ii) The cone is Bochner's cone, so it is complete, and membership is tested only by positive-definiteness itself.
(iii) The (⇒) approximants of (b) are built from the zeros: `Z_P` quantises `μ_ζ`. The approximants of (c) are built
from a KEP solution. A prime-side construction of the `C_k` (point counts `N_m(C_k)` chosen from `Λ` alone) is the
whole problem. By (c) its existence at a window is `λ^W_T ≥ 0` up to `{λ^W_T = 0}`, so it has the full strength of the
sign.

## §5 Zero-blindness of genus normalisation (Theorem 5)

**Theorem 5 (`PROVED`, elementary).** Let `N_k → ∞`, and let `Q_k` be quadratic forms of the shape
`Q_k(F) = Σ_{ρ∈Z_k}Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`. Suppose the normalised limit `Q(F) := lim N_k^{−1}Q_k(F)` exists (this is the
Tsfasman–Vlăduţ-type normalisation by the genus). Then `Q` is unchanged if each `Z_k` is modified in at most
`o(N_k)` points, where each modified point has `|Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)|` bounded uniformly in `k` (for fixed `F`). In
particular, finitely many off-line zeros are invisible to `Q`.

*Proof.* The modification changes `Q_k(F)` by `o(N_k)·O_F(1)`. ∎

Consequence: a "limit of Castelnuovo forms normalised by the genus" is zero-blind in the sense of C16, and it cannot
carry the sign. `PROVED`.

## §6 The constant `√(7/11)` and what survives of it (Theorem 6)

For `γ > 0`, `θ ∈ R` and step `η > 0`, let `s_η(γ,θ) := sup_{n∈Z}|cos(γnη+θ)|/cosh(nη/2)` (P46 (21.9) with
`γ log q → γη`), and `s(γ,θ) := sup_{u∈R}|cos(γu+θ)|/cosh(u/2)` (the arithmetic constant of P46 §19).

**Theorem 6 (`PROVED`).**
(i) `0 ≤ s(γ,θ) − s_η(γ,θ) ≤ (γ+½)η/2` uniformly in `θ`. Hence `min_θ s_η(γ,·) → min_θ s(γ,·)` as `η → 0`.
(ii) For `γ > 1/2.4`: `1/cosh(π/(4γ)) ≤ min_θ s(γ,θ) ≤ s(γ,π/2) ≤ 1/cosh(1/(2γ))`. So
`1 − min_θ s(γ,θ) ∈ [1 − sech(1/(2γ)), 1 − sech(π/(4γ))] ≈ [1/(8γ²), π²/(32γ²)]`, and the witness degree
`d_0 = (1 − s²)^{−1} ≍ γ²`.
(iii) At `γ_1 = 14.1347…` the bracket is `[6.26·10^{−4}, 1.544·10^{−3}]`. It contains the value of record
`1 − s_1 = 1.5398·10^{−3}` (P46 §19). The curve of record has `γη = θ_u = 1.9322`, where the lattice does not resolve
the oscillation. `√(7/11)` is a value of `s_η` at that coarse mesh, and no limit retains it.

*Proof.* (i) `f_θ(u) = |cos(γu+θ)|/cosh(u/2)` is Lipschitz with constant `≤ γ + ½`, uniformly in `θ`. Every `u` lies
within `η/2` of `ηZ`. The minimum over `θ` of functions that are uniformly close is close.
(ii) Lower bound: for any `θ` there is `u = (kπ−θ)/γ` with `|u| ≤ π/(2γ)` and `|cos(γu+θ)| = 1`.
Upper bound at `θ = π/2`: `|sin γu| ≤ γ|u|`, and `γu/cosh(u/2)` increases on `[0,1/γ]` (because
`(u/2)tanh(u/2) < 1` there), with value `sech(1/(2γ))` at `u = 1/γ`. For `|u| ≥ 1/γ`, the ratio is
`≤ sech(u/2) ≤ sech(1/(2γ))`.
(iii) is arithmetic. ∎

**What survives (`PROVED`).** Of P46 §21's constant, only the continuum function `s(γ,θ)` survives, with the law
`1 − s ≍ γ^{−2}`. The frame defect therefore tends to 0 at high zeros, and so it has no uniform size. This sharpens
C24 in one direction ("a property of the cone"): the defect is also a property of the **sampling mesh versus the
height**. The 131× ratio of P46 §21.3 comes from `n = 0` (mismatch 11) together with `γη = O(1)`. No sign.

## §8 What is genuinely new

1. **Theorem 4(a)** (`PROVED` modulo (KS), secondary citation). The closed cone of dilated, scaled Weil distributions of
  curves over finite fields is exactly Bochner's cone `𝒫_sym`. So "RH for all curves, closed up" is dense in
  positive-definiteness, and it is a complete, information-free transport. Theorem 4(b) gives the prime-side
  equivalence: RH ⟺ `g` is a limit of rescaled curve point-count distributions (`EQUIVALENT`). Theorem 4(c) is the
  per-window sandwich.
2. **Theorem 2** (`PROVED`). No undilated limit of function-field data contains `𝔚_ζ` or has an archimedean place.
  This is the literal `q → 1` answer.
3. **Theorem 3** (`PROVED`). Step ↔ pole-pair rigidity. Approximants never carry ζ's hyperbolic plane.
4. **Theorem 6** (`PROVED`). `s_η → s` uniformly in `θ`, and `1 − min_θ s(γ,·) ≍ γ^{−2}`. `√(7/11)` is a coarse-mesh
  artefact.
5. **Theorem 5** (`PROVED`). Genus-normalised limits are zero-blind.
