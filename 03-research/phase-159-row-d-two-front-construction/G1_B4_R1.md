## §0 Verdict

**No object is constructed.** The angle is closed in every realisation I could state, and it is closed by proofs, not
by failed attempts. Four points:

1. **What Faltings–Hriljac means in Weil's own case** (Thm 2.1; `CITED` (std.) for the dictionary, `PROVED` for the
  computation). Take the constant fibration `C × C → C`. Its Mordell–Weil lattice is `End(J_C)`, and the Néron–Tate
  height on it is the Rosati trace form. The Frobenius graph is a section, and Weil's inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz
  for the height of the *Frobenius point*. The mechanism therefore splits into five ingredients, FH-1 to FH-5:
  * FH-1: a group law;
  * FH-2: a height that is bounded below for arithmetic reasons;
  * FH-3: a Frobenius that is conformal for the height, `ĥ(π x) = q ĥ(x)`;
  * FH-4: a fixed-point count;
  * FH-5: an extraction over all `k`.
2. **The abstract mechanism is RH-equivalent as axioms** (Thm 3.2 and Cor 3.3; `PROVED`, elementary). Tate's
  quadratic-scaling trick turns a lossy lower bound `h ≥ −C` on a *group* into exact positivity, with no margin. That
  is exactly the shape C18 asks for. But on a group inside a vector space of test functions the trick is vacuous. The
  whole content is a **lower bound on an integral structure**. The (⇐) witness reads the zeros, as in Lemma T.
3. **Every realisation I can write down fails for a proved reason:**
  * **The scheme-theoretic square** (Thm 4.1, from P47 Thm 22.17): its Mordell–Weil group is `{0}`.
  * **Genuine arithmetic surfaces `X → Spec O_K`** (Prop 4.2): the positive space is `J(K)⊗R`, which has finite rank.
  It carries heights and not zeros (phase 15 M3 Attempt 6).
  * **The infinite-level limit `J(Q̄)⊗R`** (Prop 4.3, `PROVED`): it is infinite-dimensional and positive definite,
  but every flow that comes from its arithmetic symmetries is **trivial**. A connected `R` has only the trivial
  image in the profinite Galois group. So OT-1 and OT-2 fail.
  * **A conformal Frobenius family made of endomorphisms or correspondences** (Lemma 4.4, `PROVED`, new here): no
  nonzero abelian variety in characteristic `0` carries commuting `φ_p` with `φ_p†φ_p = p` for a set of primes
  whose complement has density `0`. So FH-3 cannot be realised for ζ's full prime side (C8).
  * **Genus 0** (`G_m`, with `x ↦ x^n` of degree `n`): the conformal family exists, but the global Faltings–Hriljac
  term vanishes (Chambert-Loir, via R157_006). The place decomposition of `W` is not of Faltings–Hriljac shape,
  because every local summand is indefinite on `P_T` for `T ≥ 0.60` (Prop 4.5, a restatement of P46 `cor:Qp` and
  `cor:Tarch`).
  * **Modular curves**: the sector in which ζ enters, through the scattering of the Eisenstein series, is torsion in
  `J_0(N)(Q)` by Manin–Drinfeld. So Faltings–Hriljac positivity is identically zero there (Prop 4.6). The resonance
  reading is R157_002 rows 46 and 58.
4. **What an object on this angle would have to be.** It must be an integral structure (a group `M`) carrying:
  * (FH-1) a group law, which is **N-b** of R158_005;
  * (FH-2) an arithmetic lower bound proved through sections that avoid the Frobenius points, which is **N-c**, and
  the step that the frame defect P47 Thm 19.25 blocks on the frame's cone;
  * (FH-3) a conformal Frobenius of factor `n` for **every** `n`, which Lemma 4.4 forbids for endomorphisms of
  abelian varieties and Prop 4.3 forbids for Galois.

  This sharpens R158_001 §4 row "Arakelov / Faltings–Hriljac", which read "OT-2; import excluded". It is **not** a
  construction.

## §2 The Faltings–Hriljac mechanism in Weil's own case

**Setting** (`CITED` (std.): Weil 1948; Mattuck–Tate 1958; Grothendieck 1958; Mumford, *Abelian Varieties*, §§19–21;
Lang–Néron 1959; Shioda, *On the Mordell–Weil lattices*, Comment. Math. Univ. St. Pauli 39 (1990)).
* `C/F_q` is a smooth projective geometrically connected curve of genus `g ≥ 1`, and `J = J_C`.
* `S = C × C`, with `pr_2 : S → C` viewed as a fibration over the base `B = C`. Its generic fibre is `C_K`, where
  `K = F_q(C)`.
* Sections of `pr_2` are graphs of morphisms `f : C → C`.
* By Lang–Néron, the `K/F_q`-trace of `J_K` is `J`, and `J_K(K)/J(F_q) ≅ Hom(J,J) = End(J)`.
* `†` is the Rosati involution of the canonical polarisation. Rosati positivity holds: `Tr(αα†) > 0` for
  `α ∈ End(J)⊗R` with `α ≠ 0`, where `Tr` is the trace on `H¹` (dimension `2g`).

**Theorem 2.1 (Weil's positivity is Faltings–Hriljac positivity of the Frobenius point; `CITED` (std.) for (i)–(ii),
`PROVED` for (iii)–(iv)).**

 (i) `NS(S)⊗R = R f_1 ⊕ R f_2 ⊕ E`, with `f_i` the two fibre classes and `E ≅ End(J)⊗R`. The intersection form on `E`
  is `(D_α·D_β) = −Tr(αβ†)` (Weil; Mumford §21). Check: `Δ = f_1 + f_2 + D_{id}` gives
  `Δ² = 2 − 2g = 2 + D_{id}²`, so `D_{id}² = −2g = −Tr(id)`.
 (ii) (Shioda–Tate for the fibration `pr_2`.) The Mordell–Weil lattice `J_K(K)/J(F_q) ⊗ R` is isometric to `E`, with
  the Néron–Tate height equal to minus the intersection form, up to the positive normalising factor of the chosen
  height. So `ĥ(α) = Tr(αα†)`, and the Hodge index theorem on `S`, restricted to `E`, is the positive definiteness
  of `ĥ`. This is the function-field Faltings–Hriljac theorem for the constant family.
 (iii) The Frobenius section `Γ_π` has Mordell–Weil class `π`. Its Verschiebung is `π† = V`, with `πV = q`. Hence
  `ĥ(π∘x) = Tr(πxx†π†) = q·ĥ(x)`: **the Frobenius is conformal for the height with factor `q`**, and this follows
  from `deg π = q`, a degree identity proved before any eigenvalue is known.
 (iv) Put `a = Tr(π|H¹) = 1 + q − N_1`. Since `{q/α_i} = {α_i}`, also `a = Tr(V|H¹)`. Then
  `0 ≤ ĥ(m + nπ) = 2g m² + 2mn·a + 2gq n²` for all `m, n ∈ Z`. The discriminant gives `a² ≤ 4g²q`. Applied over
  `F_{q^k}` this gives `|Σ_i α_i^k| ≤ 2g q^{k/2}` for all `k`, hence `|α_i| = √q`.

*Proof of (iii)–(iv).* (iii): `x ↦ π∘x` acts on `E` as left multiplication by `π`. So
`ĥ(πx) = Tr(πxx†π†) = Tr(x†π†πx) = Tr(x†(Vπ)x) = q Tr(xx†)`, using `Vπ = q` and cyclicity of the trace. (iv):
expand `Tr((m+nπ)(m+nV)) = m²Tr(1) + mn(Tr π + Tr V) + n²Tr(πV)`, with `Tr 1 = 2g`, `Tr π = Tr V = a` and
`Tr(πV) = 2gq`. The form in `(m,n)` is positive semidefinite on `Z²`, hence on `R²` by scaling and continuity. The rest
is the classical extraction: the power series `Σ_k (Σ_i α_i^k) z^k` converges for `|z| < q^{−1/2}`. ∎

**The five ingredients, and which of them carries the sign.**

| # | ingredient | in Weil's case | carries the sign? |
|---|---|---|---|
| FH-1 | a **group** of points, so that `m + nπ` is an element | `J_K(K)`, through the group law of `J` | no, but without it there is nothing to be positive on |
| FH-2 | a height, positive definite, proved **before** the spectrum | Hodge index on `S` (Riemann–Roch), or `ĥ = lim h_Θ(2^k x)/4^k` with `h_Θ ≥ −C` from ampleness of `Θ` (Tate) | **yes** |
| FH-3 | a Frobenius that is conformal for `ĥ` with factor `q` | `π†π = q`, from `deg π = q` | yes: it fixes the weight |
| FH-4 | a fixed-point count on the pairing `⟨π^k, 1⟩` | `N_k = #Fix(π^k)` | no; this is rows A–C for ζ |
| FH-5 | extraction over all `k` | radius of convergence | no; this is "∀T" (P46 `prop:uniform`) |

For ζ, FH-4 and FH-5 are available: they are rows A–C and the reduced statement E1–E5. What is missing is FH-1, FH-2
and FH-3, and they must be realised simultaneously, on one integral structure. `HEURISTIC` (the reading); the table
entries are `CITED`.

**Remark 2.2 (infinite genus; `PROVED` from the record).** In (iv), `ĥ(1) = Tr(id|H¹) = 2g`. For ζ, "`2g = ∞`" is
P47 Rem 21.23 (`Δ·Δ = 2−2g → −∞`) together with Rem 22.4a (the diagonal `I_A(Frob_n,Frob_n)` does not exist). So the

integral structure on a **space of test functions**, not on the formal `Z⟨Frob_n⟩`. This is where §3 applies.

## §3 The abstract Tate–Hriljac mechanism, and why it is RH-equivalent as axioms

Let `𝒟` be the real test space (real `C_c^∞(R)`, or `D^W`). Let `Q` be a quadratic form on `𝒟` with closure `(Q, 𝒟[Q])`
in its form norm. Here `Q` is either `W_T` on `L²(I_T)` (complete form) or `𝔞_T` on `P_T` (primitive form), and the form
norm is the one of P46 `def:W` (m(τ)-weighted).

**Definition 3.1 (Faltings–Hriljac datum).** An FH datum for `(Q, 𝒟)` is a triple `(M, ι, h)` such that:
* (a) `M` is an abelian group, and `ι : M → 𝒟` is an additive map whose `R`-span is dense in `𝒟[Q]` for the form norm;
* (b) `h : M → R` satisfies `Q(ιx) ≥ h(x) − C` for all `x ∈ M` (quasi-quadraticity, one-sided);
* (c) `h(x) ≥ −C_1` for all `x ∈ M` (the arithmetic lower bound).

In Faltings–Hriljac, `M = J(K)`, `ι` is the Faltings correction `D ↦ D + Φ(D)`, and `h = h_Θ` is the Weil height of an
ample class. (b) is Néron–Tate's `ĥ = h + O(1)`. (c) is `h_Θ ≥ −C` off the base locus, proved from global sections: a
proper intersection has local contribution `log #(O/𝔞) ≥ 0`, and the archimedean part is bounded below by
`−log sup‖s‖`. `CITED` (std.): Faltings, Ann. Math. 119 (1984); Hriljac, Amer. J. Math. 107 (1985); Hindry–Silverman
Part B.

**Theorem 3.2 (Tate–Hriljac positivity; `PROVED`, elementary).** If `(M, ι, h)` is an FH datum, then `Q ≥ 0` on
`𝒟[Q]`.

*Proof.* For `x ∈ M` and `n ∈ Z`, additivity of `ι` gives `ι(nx) = nι(x)`, so `Q(ι(nx)) = n²Q(ιx)`. By (b) and (c),
`n²Q(ιx) ≥ −C − C_1` for every `n`, so `Q(ιx) ≥ 0`. Now let `y = Σ_j r_j ιx_j` be a finite real combination. On the
finite-dimensional span of the `ιx_j`, `Q` is continuous. Take rational `r_j^{(N)} = a_j^{(N)}/N^{(N)} → r_j`. Then
`Q(Σ r_j^{(N)} ιx_j) = (N^{(N)})^{−2} Q(ι(Σ a_j^{(N)} x_j)) ≥ 0`, and letting `N → ∞` gives `Q(y) ≥ 0`. By (a) and
continuity of the closed form in its own norm, `Q ≥ 0` on `𝒟[Q]`. ∎

**Corollary 3.3 (FH data are RH as axioms; `PROVED`).**
 (i) If for every `T` there is an FH datum for the complete form `W_T` on `L²(I_T)`, then E1 holds, i.e. RH. The same
  holds for the primitive `𝔞_T` on `P_T`, giving E2 (globally; P46 `thm:parityschur` for the per-window caveat, C22).
 (ii) Conversely, under RH, take any countable subgroup `M ⊂ 𝒟` with dense span, `ι` the inclusion and `h := Q|_M`. This
  is an FH datum with `C = C_1 = 0`. The witness uses `Q ≥ 0`, i.e. the zeros.
 (iii) **Tate's step is vacuous on test spaces.** Let `ι(M)` sit in a vector space and let `Q∘ι` be bounded below on
  `M`. Then `Q∘ι ≥ 0` on `M` already (proof of Thm 3.2 with `h = Q∘ι`). So (b) buys nothing unless `h` is
  *computed* by a mechanism different from `Q`. The content of an FH datum is exactly: an **integral structure `M`
  and a proof of (c) for an `h` within `O(1)` of `Q` from below**, obtained from a positivity of integrality type
  (lengths, cardinalities) and not from the eigenvalues.

*Proof.* (i) is Thm 3.2 plus E1/E2 (reduced statement §1). (ii) is immediate. (iii) is shown in the statement. ∎

**Reading (`HEURISTIC`).** Cor 3.3 is Lemma T (R158_001 §3.1) transposed to the height language. Theorem 3.2 is the one
mechanism in this programme's lists that turns a **lossy** bound into an **exact** sign without a margin: C18 and
`thm:margin` do not bind it, because the loss `C + C_1` is killed by `n² → ∞`, not by a window-uniform constant. The
price is that the bound must hold on a group. On a group inside a vector space that is the sign itself, unless `h` has
an independent arithmetic origin. In Faltings–Hriljac that origin is **integrality**: local intersection numbers are
`log`s of indices of `Z`-lattices, hence `≥ 0`.

**Remark 3.4 (integrality as a rigidity test; `HEURISTIC`, checked against OT-4).** Suppose (c) came from integrality
of the shape `h(x) = Σ_p n_p(x) log p + a(x)`, with `n_p(x) ∈ Z_{≥0}` and `a` bounded below. Run it against the
record's falsifiers:
* **Amplification** `Λ + cδ_{n_0}` (P46 `thm:finmod`(ii)) generically breaks it: `c ∉ Z·log p`.
* **Deletion** `Λ·1_{n≥N}` (`thm:finmod`(i)) keeps it: weights in `{0, log p}`. So an integrality bound must also use
  the archimedean place and the functional equation (C11), since deletion keeps the zeros and the pole but breaks
  `E(s) = E(1−s)`.
* **Beurling systems** (C14, T3) have integral multiplicities at generalised primes `log p_j`. So "integrality" must
  mean the index of a `Z`-lattice: `log #(Z/p) = log p` with `p` an actual integer. That uses `+` and `×` of `Z`, which
  is P47 §28.4 clause (5).

No such `h` is constructed. **(Q-FH), `OPEN`, RH-strength by Cor 3.3:** is there a subgroup `M ⊂ D^W` with dense span
and a decomposition `W(ιx) = Σ_p n_p(x) log p + a(x) + (≥ 0)`, with `n_p(x) ∈ Z_{≥0}` given by lengths of finite
`Z`-modules and `a ≥ −C_1`?

## §4 The carriers, one by one

### 4.1 The scheme-theoretic square

**Theorem 4.1 (the constant family over `Spec Z` has trivial Mordell–Weil group; `PROVED` from P47 Thm 22.17).** In
the category of schemes, the constant family "`Spec Z × Spec Z → Spec Z`" is the identity `Spec Z → Spec Z`. Its
generic fibre is `Spec Q`, its sections form `Hom(Spec Z, Spec Z) = {id}`, and the analogue of `J_K(K)/trace` is `0`.
So FH-1 is empty and Theorem 3.2 has nothing to act on.

*Proof.* `Spec Z` is terminal (P47 Thm 22.17(ii)), so `Spec Z ×_{Spec Z} Spec Z = Spec Z`. The generic fibre is
`Spec Z ⊗ Q = Spec Q`. Sections are `Hom_{Spec Z}(Spec Z, Spec Z) = {id}`. A zero-dimensional fibre has trivial
Picard/Albanese variety. ∎

The formal replacement `Z⟨Frob_n⟩` of P47 §22 is an abelian group, but its only form `Q_0` violates (c): it takes the
value `−10` on `5Frob_6 − 4Frob_{10}` (Thm 22.4(ii)), so `Q_0(n·x) = −10n² → −∞`. It is also zero-blind and
`Ξ_a`-uniform (Prop 22.5). `PROVED` (record).

### 4.2 Genuine arithmetic surfaces

**Proposition 4.2 (finite rank; `PROVED` given `CITED` (std.) Faltings–Hriljac and Mordell–Weil).** Let
`X → Spec O_K` be a regular proper arithmetic surface with generic fibre of genus `g ≥ 1`. Faltings–Hriljac's positive
space is the quotient of `\hat{CH}^1(X)_R`, restricted to the orthogonal complement of the vertical components and the
fibre classes, by the kernel of the form. That space is `J(K)⊗R`, of dimension `rk J(K) < ∞`. So:
* for any window `T`, the image of any linear map `j : J(K)⊗R → L²(I_T)` is finite-dimensional;
* whether or not `W_T ∘ j = ĥ`, the positivity of `W_T` on `j(J(K)⊗R)` says nothing about `λ^W_T` or `λ^A_T`. By C1
  and `thm:arena`, no finite-dimensional compression decides the even Schur complement.

The eigenvalue data this space carries are heights (phase 15 M3 Attempt 6). `PROVED` (elementary given the citations).

### 4.3 The infinite-level limit

Let `V := J(Q̄)⊗_Z R = ∪_K J(K)⊗R` for a curve `C/Q` of genus `≥ 1`. It is infinite-dimensional (rank grows along
suitable towers; `CITED` (std.)). `ĥ` is positive definite on each `J(K)⊗R` (`CITED` (std.)), hence on `V`. This is the
first candidate on this angle with an **infinite-dimensional space and a positivity defined before any spectrum**,
passing C1's dimension clause.

**Proposition 4.3 (no flow from the arithmetic symmetries of `V`; `PROVED`).**
 (i) `Gal(Q̄/Q)` acts on `(V, ĥ)` by isometries, and every vector has a finite orbit.
 (ii) Every continuous homomorphism `R → Gal(Q̄/Q)` is trivial. In particular, the connected component `R_{>0}` of the
  idèle class group, and the norm-flow of the Weil group, act trivially on `V` through Galois.
 (iii) Hence the "flow" `U_t` induced on `V` by its arithmetic symmetries is `U_t = id`. The Lefschetz distribution
  `h ↦ Tr_V ∫h(t)U_t dt` (on any finite-dimensional Galois-stable piece) is `(∫h)·dim`, a multiple of the zero-frequency
  evaluation. This cannot equal ζ's explicit formula, whose zero side `Σ_ρ ĥ(γ_ρ)` has infinitely many non-zero
  frequencies and whose prime side is supported on `{±log n}`.

*Proof.* (i): heights are Galois invariant (`CITED` (std.)), and `x ∈ J(K)` is fixed by `Gal(Q̄/K)`, which has finite
index. (ii): `Gal(Q̄/Q)` is profinite, hence totally disconnected, and the continuous image of the connected group `R`
is connected, hence a point. The Weil group acts on Galois modules through `W_Q → Gal(Q̄/Q)`. Its norm part `R_{>0}`
lies in the connected component, which maps to `1` (Artin reciprocity; `CITED` (std.)); (ii) alone suffices. (iii): by
(ii), `U_t = id`. On a Galois-stable finite-dimensional piece `V_0`, `∫h(t)U_t dt = (∫h)·id_{V_0}`, whose trace is
`(∫h) dim V_0 = ĥ_{Fourier}(0)·dim V_0`. The explicit formula (P46 `thm:Z`(i)) is not of this form for all `h ∈ C_c^∞`:
take `h` with `∫h = 0`, the left side vanishes, and the right side is `Σ_ρ Φ_h(ρ)`, which is not identically zero on
`{∫h = 0}` because the zeros are infinitely many and `Φ_h(ρ)` separates them. ∎

**Consequence.** On `V`, FH positivity is **isometric** (`α = 0` in Deninger's normalisation) and has no flow. This
parallels C5, where Deninger–Singhof's derived positivity also forces `α = 0`. Any flow on `V` would be extra structure,
and its conformality `ĥ(U_t x) = e^t ĥ(x)` would have to be **proved**: that is OT-5′, with Faltings–Hriljac
contributing only the definiteness. `HEURISTIC` (the parallel); `PROVED` (the proposition).

### 4.4 A conformal Frobenius family made of endomorphisms or correspondences

In Weil's case FH-3 is `π†π = q` (Thm 2.1(iii)). For ζ, the prime side needs a commuting family `φ_n` with
`φ_n†φ_n = n` for every `n`, in particular for **every** prime, since deleting a single prime is fatal (C8, `thm:finmod`).
Any correspondence on a curve acts on `J(K̄)⊗R` through `End(J)`. So the following settles both endomorphisms and
correspondences.

**Lemma 4.4 (no conformal Frobenius family in characteristic 0; `PROVED`; standard facts `CITED` (std.): Mumford §21,
Chebotarev).** Let `A ≠ 0` be an abelian variety over a field of characteristic `0`, with a polarisation and Rosati
involution `†`. Let `P` be a set of primes, and let `φ_p ∈ End(A)` (`p ∈ P`) be pairwise commuting with `φ_p†φ_p = p`.
Then there is a set of primes `P_A`, disjoint from `P`, which is either cofinite or of Dirichlet density
`≥ 1/[M:Q] > 0` for a number field `M` depending on `A`. In particular `P` is not the set of all primes, nor any set of
density `1`.

*Proof.*
1. **The algebra.** Since `φ_p†φ_p = p ≠ 0`, `φ_p` is invertible in `End⁰(A) = End(A)⊗Q` and `φ_p† = pφ_p^{−1}`, which
  commutes with every `φ_q`. So `R := Q[φ_p, φ_p† : p ∈ P] ⊂ End⁰(A)` is commutative, finite-dimensional and
  `†`-stable, and `†` is positive on it: `Tr(xx†) > 0` for `x ≠ 0` (Rosati positivity, Mumford §21).
2. **`R` is reduced.** Let `x ∈ R` be nilpotent. Then `y := xx†` is nilpotent (commutativity) and `y† = y`, so
  `Tr(yy†) = Tr(y²) = 0`, since `y²` is nilpotent. So `y = 0`, and then `Tr(xx†) = Tr(y) = 0`, so `x = 0`. Hence
  `R = Π_i L_i` is a product of number fields, and `†` preserves each factor with a positive involution. So each `L_i`
  is either totally real with `† = id`, or CM with `†` the complex conjugation (`CITED` (std.)).
3. **Reduction to one factor.** Fix one factor `L := L_1` (`R ≠ 0` since `1 ∈ R`). The image `α_p` of `φ_p` in `L`
  satisfies `α_p ᾱ_p = p`, and `α_p` is integral, since `φ_p` is integral over `Z` as an element of the finitely
  generated `Z`-module `End(A)`. So `α_p ∈ O_L`.
4. **`L` totally real.** Then `α_p² = p`, so `Q(√p) ⊂ L`, which holds for at most finitely many `p`. Take `P_A` to be
  the cofinite complement.
5. **`L` CM, with maximal totally real subfield `L⁺`.** Let `M` be the Galois closure of `L`; it is CM, and complex
  conjugation `c` is central in `G = Gal(M/Q)` (`CITED` (std.)). By Chebotarev, the primes `p` unramified in `M` with
  `Frob_p = c` have density `1/|G|`. For such `p`:
  * `c` acts trivially on every conjugate of `L⁺` (all real), so `p` splits completely in `L⁺`;
  * `c` acts nontrivially on every conjugate of `L` (none is real), so each prime `𝔭 | p` of `L⁺` is inert in `L`.
  Put `𝔓 = 𝔭O_L`, which is `c`-stable.
  Then `v_𝔓(α_pᾱ_p) = 2v_𝔓(α_p)` is even, while `v_𝔓(p) = v_𝔭(p) = 1` (`p` unramified). Contradiction. So no such
  `p` lies in `P`, and we take `P_A := {p : Frob_p = c}`. ∎

**Consequence (`PROVED`).** An FH carrier whose Frobenius is realised by endomorphisms or correspondences of an abelian
variety (a Jacobian, a Mordell–Weil group) misses a positive-density set of primes. Its Lefschetz formula cannot carry
ζ's prime side with exact `Λ(p)` at every `p`, so it fails OT-2 (C8, C11). Weil's `π` escapes because it is **one**
element, whose powers give all `k` (FH-5). ζ needs infinitely many independent generators `log p`. This is the
height-theoretic form of the infinite generation in C5 (Deninger Cor 3.5 Rem 3). *Scope:* the lemma is about
endomorphisms. It says nothing about a conformal flow that is not an endomorphism, which is where §4.3 left it.

### 4.5 Genus 0, and the place decomposition of `W`

On `G_m/Z ⊂ P¹_Z`, the maps `[n] : x ↦ x^n` form a commuting family of degree-`n` endomorphisms lifting Frobenius at
every `p`. This is the one arithmetic surface carrying a full conformal-by-degree Frobenius family, and it is where Lemma
4.4 does not apply (`dim J = 0`). But in genus 0 the global Faltings–Hriljac term is `0`, and the arithmetic
self-intersection is a sum of local energies (Chambert-Loir 1001.2517 §3.4.1, read in R157_006 l.73–78). So only a
place-by-place positivity remains, and that is excluded as a derivation (C17). The analytic counterpart is the
following.

**Proposition 4.5 (the place decomposition of `𝔞_T` is not of Faltings–Hriljac shape; `PROVED` = restatement of P46).**
The FH shape is "global term `≥ 0` plus local terms each `≥ 0` on the primitive space" (after the sign flip
`I_∞ = −𝔞` on primitive real tests, R158_001 A2). For `T ≥ 0.60`, on `P_T` (primitive form):
* `𝔞_T = A_∞ + Σ_{p<e^{2T}} Q_p`;
* the archimedean summand `A_∞` is indefinite (negative somewhere by `cor:Tarch`; positive on high-frequency tests since
  `m(τ) → +∞`);
* each `Q_p` with `½log p < T` is indefinite (`cor:Qp`);
* the primes with `log p ≥ 2T` contribute `0` on `I_T`.

So no nonzero summand has a definite sign, and any FH-type representation must put **all** of the positivity in a
global term. By Cor 3.3(iii), that global term is `𝔞_T` itself, unless an independent integral `h` is supplied (Q-FH).

*Proof.* P46 `cor:Tarch` (`λ^∞_P(T) < 0` for `T ≥ 0.60`) and `cor:Qp`. That P46 records "for `T ≥ 0.60` both summands
are indefinite" is its own remark after `cor:Qp`. Positivity of `A_∞` somewhere on `P_T`: `A_∞(F) = (2π)^{−1}∫m(τ)|F̂(τ)|²dτ`
on `P_T` (pole term `0`), with `m(τ) = log|τ| + O(1)` (P46 `lem:mbounds`), and `P_T` has finite codimension, so it
contains tests with `F̂` concentrated at `|τ| ≥ τ_0` for any `τ_0`. ∎

### 4.6 Modular curves and the Eisenstein sector

ζ enters the Arakelov geometry of `X_0(N)` through the Eisenstein series: the scattering matrix of `SL_2(Z)` is
`ξ(2s−1)/ξ(2s)`, so the zeros appear as resonances at `ρ/2` (R157_002 rows 46 and 58: EXCL (listed instances) and
EQUIV). Phase 16 Doc 05 proved the resonance kernel definite for every configuration.

**Proposition 4.6 (Faltings–Hriljac is blind to the Eisenstein sector; `PROVED` given `CITED` (std.) Manin–Drinfeld
1972–73).** Every degree-zero cuspidal divisor on `X_0(N)` has finite order in `J_0(N)(Q)`. So its Néron–Tate height is
`0`, and the Faltings–Hriljac positive form vanishes identically on the span of the cuspidal classes: the one part of
`\hat{Pic}(X_0(N))` built from the Eisenstein data.

*Proof.* Manin–Drinfeld: the classes are torsion, `ĥ` vanishes on torsion, and on `J(Q)⊗R` torsion is `0`. ∎

So on modular curves, the FH positivity (on cusp forms, i.e. heights of Heegner-type points; Gross–Zagier) and the
location of ζ's zeros (Eisenstein, continuous spectrum) live in orthogonal sectors. `HEURISTIC` beyond the proposition.
