## §0 Verdict

1. **The loophole is closed. Its three branches each end in a proved obstruction or a proved circularity (Theorem L,
  §6).** A reciprocity (Ancona-type) proof of Weil positivity on `Spec Z` needs three things:
  (R) a rational quadratic structure;
  (S) small-rank orthogonal pieces;
  (I) local invariants of those pieces, accessible independently of the real Gram matrix.
  Branch by branch:
  * **Theorem B (`PROVED`, via Baker's theorem, `CITED` std).** Frobenius-compatible rational structures fail. The
  atomic Lefschetz numbers `c(n) = Λ(n)/√n` of the Frobenius correspondences in window `T` span a `Q̄`-module of rank
  `π(e^{2T})` (exactly), or `≥ π(e^{2T}) − 1` after an arbitrary real rescaling. So any intersection theory whose
  Lefschetz numbers lie in a period module of rank `m` has `m ≥ π(e^{2T}) − 1 → ∞`. A finite period matrix is
  impossible. For a curve over `F_q` the corresponding rank is `1` (the single period `log q`). This replaces and
  quantifies M5.
  * **Theorem Q1 (`PROVED`, Hasse–Minkowski existence, `CITED` std).** Rational structures not compatible with
  Frobenius are *free*. Every finite-rank real piece admits rational structures realizing *every* system of local
  invariants compatible with its real signature, so a structure chosen without canonical origin carries no
  information. If such a structure is fed by local data, then C16 and the `Ξ_a` gate move all of the content into
  proving the real-realization clause (Prop X).
  * **Prop N + Cor R (`PROVED`; consistent with Bombieri 2000 Thm 8, `CITED` via P46).** On real-even tests, each
  distinct off-line quadruple of zeros contributes exactly one negative square. Real tests give two per quadruple;
  complex tests give two. Reciprocity (M3) therefore detects `#{off-line quadruples} mod 4` and nothing more.
  Ancona's small-rank requirement (S) is equivalent to the a priori bound "at most 3 off-line quadruples". That bound
  is `OPEN` for ζ; not even finiteness of the off-line zero set is known.
2. **Status: dead end for the motivic object (honest).** Round 1 plus round 2 close the motivic angle as a source of
  OT-5′:
  * the Hodge-type standard conjecture of `𝔐_Z` is RH (M1);
  * the one mechanism that proves such conjectures without a real realization (S4) is excluded in every branch
  (Theorem L).
  The new content is exclusionary and elementary. It is stated so that the loophole cannot be resubmitted.
3. **Weakest step.** Theorem L(b) is a *circularity* statement, not an impossibility. A rational structure with
  independent local invariants whose source is neither motivic (a) nor local data (C16) is not excluded. It is
  excluded only in the sense that no such source is known or described anywhere (`HEURISTIC`).

## §2 Setting and definitions

Notation of P46 (checked against `eq:symbol`, `eq:form`, `eq:W`, l.115–145):
* `Φ_F(s) = ∫F(t)e^{(s−½)t}dt`, so `Φ_F(½ + iz) = F̂(z)` with `F̂(z) = ∫F(t)e^{izt}dt`.
* `W_T(F) = 𝔞_T(F) + 2Re(A(F)\overline{B(F)}) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)` for real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, the sum running over
  non-trivial zeros with multiplicity.
* `c(n) := Λ(n)/√n = v_n/2`.
* `K(u) = 2e^{3u/2}/(e^{2u} − 1)` (P46 l.364), `C_0 = ψ(¼) − log π`.

**Definition 2.1 (the Lefschetz distribution and its atomic Lefschetz numbers).** Put

  `𝓛 := Σ_{n≥2} c(n)(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n}) − κ_∞ + (e^{u/2} + e^{−u/2})`,

where `κ_∞ := (1/2π)∫m(τ)e^{iτu}dτ` in the sense of distributions. This is the "fixed-point side" of (P2) of R1:
`L(G) = ⟨𝓛, G⟩` up to the polar terms written in R1 Def 2.5.

*Structure of `κ_∞` (`PROVED` from P46 l.369).* `m(τ) = C_0 + ∫_0^∞(1 − cos τu)K(u)du`. Since `K` is integrable away from
`0`, `κ_∞` restricted to `R∖{0}` is the smooth function `−½K(|u|)`.

Consequence: on `R∖{0}`, `𝓛` is a Radon measure `= (atomic part) + (smooth density)`. Its atoms are exactly at
`u = ±log n` with `Λ(n) ≠ 0`, of mass `c(n)`. The smooth density is `½K(|u|) + 2cosh(u/2)`. The **atomic Lefschetz
number** of the Frobenius correspondence `Frob_n = δ_{log n}` is `Lef(Frob_n) := 𝓛({log n}) = c(n)`. It is canonical:
it is the mass of an atom of a measure, and it needs no smooth test.

*Analogue over `F_q`.* For a smooth projective curve `C/F_q`, the Weil distribution of `Z_C(q^{−s})` in the variable `u`
has atoms at `u = k log q` of mass `log q · N_k(C)·q^{−k/2}`, up to the normalization of the polar terms. The only
transcendental period is `log q`; the numbers `N_k ∈ Z` are the intersection numbers `Frob^k · Δ`.

**Definition 2.2 (intersection theory with period module of rank `m`).** Fix a real scale `λ ≠ 0`. A *Lefschetz
structure of period rank `m` on window `T`* consists of:
* a `Q`-vector space `𝒵` of "cycles", containing elements `Z_n` for `n ∈ N_T` and `Z_1 = Δ`;
* a `Q`-bilinear pairing `(·,·) : 𝒵 × 𝒵 → M`, where `M = Q̄ + Q̄ω_1 + … + Q̄ω_m ⊂ C`;
* the compatibility `(Z_n, Δ) = λ·c(n) + a_n` with `a_n ∈ Q̄`, for all prime `n = p ∈ N_T`.

The `a_n` allow algebraic polar or archimedean corrections. For example, `K(log n) = 2n^{3/2}/(n² − 1)` and
`2cosh(½log n)` are algebraic. For `F_q`-curves one has `m = 0` with `λ = 1/log q`.

**Definition 2.3 (reciprocity certificate, Ancona form, window `T`, real-even sector).** Let
`D_T^{ev} := {F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T; R) : F(−t) = F(t)}`. A certificate is `(V_Q, q, π, (V_i)_i, (λ_{i,ℓ})_{i,ℓ})` with:
* **(R1)** `V_Q` is a `Q`-vector space and `q : V_Q × V_Q → Q` is symmetric bilinear.
* **(R2)** `π : V_Q ⊗ R → D_T^{ev}` is `R`-linear and injective with `W_T(πx, πy) = q(x, y)`. Its image is dense in
  `D_T^{ev}` for the form norm `‖F‖²_{W} := W_T(F) + C‖F‖²₂` (with `C` large; `W_T` is bounded below and closable,
  P46 §1).
* **(R3)** `V_Q = ⊕_i V_i`, a `q`-orthogonal sum with `dim V_i ≤ r₀`.
* **(R4)** for each `i` and each prime `ℓ`, an isometry class `λ_{i,ℓ}` of quadratic spaces over `Q_ℓ`, together with a
  proof that `λ_{i,ℓ} = [q|V_i ⊗ Q_ℓ]`, obtained **without** computing the real Gram matrix of `q|V_i`.

*Lemma 2.4 (what a certificate proves; `PROVED`).* If (R1)–(R3) hold, then `W_T ≥ 0` on `D_T^{ev}` iff `s₋(q|V_i) = 0` for
every `i`. If also `r₀ ≤ 3`, each `s₋(q|V_i)` is determined by `sign d(q|V_i)` and the `λ_{i,ℓ}` (M3).

*Proof.* `(⇒)` is restriction. `(⇐)`: by orthogonality, `W_T ≥ 0` on `π(V_Q ⊗ R)`. This subspace is dense for `‖·‖_W`, and
`W_T` is continuous for that norm, so `W_T ≥ 0` on the closure.
For the second claim: `s₋ ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}` is fixed by `s₋ mod 4`, and `s₋ mod 4` is fixed by `sign d` together with the
Hasse invariant. By Hilbert reciprocity the Hasse invariant is `ε_∞ = ∏_ℓ ε_ℓ` (M3 of R1). ∎

Remark: `sign d` is itself a real-place invariant. In the one-sided use of reciprocity it is read off the discriminant,
which is local data at every `ℓ` by M3's first step.

## §3 Theorem Q1 — rational structures are free (`PROVED`; Hasse–Minkowski existence `CITED` std)

**Theorem Q1.** Let `(U, Q)` be a nondegenerate real quadratic space of dimension `r` and signature `(s₊, s₋)`. Suppose
we are given:
* `d ∈ Q^×/Q^{×2}` with `sign d = (−1)^{s₋}`;
* for each prime `ℓ`, a Hasse invariant `ε_ℓ ∈ {±1}`, with `ε_ℓ = 1` for almost all `ℓ`;
* `∏_ℓ ε_ℓ = (−1)^{s₋(s₋−1)/2}`;
* `ε_ℓ = 1` whenever `r = 1`, or `r = 2` and `d = −1` in `Q_ℓ^×/Q_ℓ^{×2}`.

Then there is a `Q`-structure `V_Q ⊂ U` (i.e. `V_Q ⊗ R = U`) such that `Q|V_Q` is `Q`-valued with discriminant `d` and
Hasse invariants `ε_ℓ`. Conversely, every `Q`-structure on which `Q` is rational has invariants satisfying these
conditions.

*Proof.* Existence of a rational form with the prescribed data: Serre, *Cours d'arithmétique*, Ch. IV §3.3, Prop. 7
(`CITED` std). The conditions listed are exactly its hypotheses at the finite places, with `(r_∞, s_∞) = (s₊, s₋)`,
`d_∞ = (−1)^{s₋}` and `ε_∞ = (−1)^{s₋(s₋−1)/2}` at `∞`.

Call that form `q` on `Q^r`. Then `q ⊗ R` has signature `(s₊, s₋)`, so by Sylvester there is an `R`-isometry
`φ : (R^r, q ⊗ R) → (U, Q)`. Put `V_Q := φ(Q^r)`.

The converse is Hilbert reciprocity (M3 of R1) together with the sign of the discriminant, plus the classical
restrictions in ranks 1 and 2. ∎

**Corollary Q1′ (rank-one certificates exist iff the sign holds; `PROVED`).** Let `(f_j)_{j≥1}` be a countable
`‖·‖_W`-total family in `D_T^{ev}`, and suppose `W_T` is nondegenerate on every flag `span(f_1, …, f_J)`. This is a
generic condition; Bombieri's "finite truncations never have eigenvalue 0" is the analogous statement (via P46 l.286).
Then `W_T`-Gram–Schmidt gives a `W_T`-orthogonal family `(g_j)`. The data
* `e_j := g_j/√|W_T(g_j)|`,
* `V_Q := ⊕ Q e_j`,
* `q(e_j, e_k) = ±δ_{jk}`,
* pieces `V_j = Qe_j` (so `r₀ = 1`)

satisfy (R1)–(R3). Its local invariants are `d_j = W_T(g_j)/|W_T(g_j)|`, the sign itself. So a certificate of rank 1
exists iff `W_T ≥ 0`, but writing it down requires the signs `sign W_T(g_j)`. Without (R4), a certificate is a
bookkeeping of the answer.

*Reading.* Q1 is the precise sense in which a rational structure "chosen" on the real form carries no information. Its
local invariants are whatever the chooser put in, subject only to consistency with the real signature. What reciprocity
needs is (R4). The rest of the memo classifies the possible sources of (R4).

## §4 Theorem B — the period rank of the Frobenius Lefschetz numbers (`PROVED`; Baker `CITED` std)

**Baker's theorem** (A. Baker, *Mathematika* 13 (1966) 204–216; *Transcendental Number Theory*, CUP 1975, Thm 2.1; `CITED`
std). Let `α_1, …, α_r` be nonzero algebraic numbers such that `log α_1, …, log α_r` are linearly independent over `Q`.
Then `1, log α_1, …, log α_r` are linearly independent over `Q̄`.

For distinct primes `p_1, …, p_r`, `log p_1, …, log p_r` are `Q`-linearly independent, by unique factorisation.

**Theorem B.** Let `N ≥ 2` and `𝒫_N = {p prime : p ≤ N}`. Let `M = Q̄ + Q̄ω_1 + … + Q̄ω_m ⊂ C`, `λ ∈ C^×`, and
`(a_p)_{p ∈ 𝒫_N} ⊂ Q̄`, with `λ c(p) + a_p ∈ M` for every `p ∈ 𝒫_N`. Then:

  (i) `m ≥ π(N) − 1`;
  (ii) if `λ ∈ Q̄^×`, then `m ≥ π(N)`;
  (iii) the `Q̄`-span of `{c(n) : 2 ≤ n ≤ N}` has dimension exactly `π(N)`, and together with `1` exactly `π(N) + 1`.

*Proof.* (iii) Since `c(p^k) = p^{−(k−1)/2}·p^{−1/2}·log p`, the span of `{c(n)}_{n≤N}` equals the span of
`{log p}_{p≤N}`. So the span of `{1} ∪ {c(n)}` equals the span of `{1} ∪ {log p}_{p≤N}`, which has dimension `π(N) + 1`
by Baker.

(i) `Q̄ ⊂ M`, so `λc(p) ∈ M` for every `p`. If `Σ_p β_p λc(p) = 0` with `β_p ∈ Q̄`, then dividing by `λ` gives
`Σ_p (β_p p^{−1/2}) log p = 0`. The coefficients `β_p p^{−1/2}` are algebraic, so Baker gives `β_p = 0`. Hence the
`λc(p)` are `Q̄`-independent, so `π(N) ≤ dim_{Q̄} M ≤ m + 1`.

(ii) Now `λ ∈ Q̄^×`, so a relation `β_0 + Σ β_p λc(p) = 0` has algebraic coefficients `β_0` and `β_pλp^{−1/2}`. The
inhomogeneous form of Baker gives `β_0 = β_p = 0`. So `{1} ∪ {λc(p)}` is independent in `M`, and `π(N) + 1 ≤ m + 1`. ∎

**Corollary B1 (no finite-period intersection theory on `Spec Z`; `PROVED`).** A Lefschetz structure of period rank `m`
on window `T` (Def 2.2) has `m ≥ π(e^{2T}) − 1`, and `m ≥ π(e^{2T})` when `λ` is algebraic. Consequences:
* **Rational intersection numbers** (`m = 0`, the Weil / Tate / Ancona situation) are impossible as soon as two primes
  are contacts, i.e. for `T > ½log 3`. With the Weil normalization (`λ = ±1`) they are impossible already for
  `T > ½log 2`.
* For the "for all `T`" statement, `m = ∞`: at least one new period per prime. By the prime number theorem,
  `π(e^{2T}) ~ e^{2T}/(2T)`.
* By contrast, a curve over `F_q` has rank `1` for all `k`: the single period `log q`, and after dividing by it,
  `N_k ∈ Z`.

*What this closes.* R1 §3.5 excluded only one real scale (Gelfond–Schneider for two primes). The loophole was a rational
cycle lattice whose intersection numbers reach the `c(n)` through a transcendental period matrix. Theorem B covers any
finite period matrix and any algebraic polar/archimedean corrections. A Frobenius-compatible period-matrix carrier needs
a period module whose rank grows like `e^{2T}/2T`. The periods can be taken to be the `log p` themselves, and then the
rational structure is the tautological prime-by-prime one: divide the atoms at `p^k` by `log p`, leaving
`p^{−k/2} ∈ Q̄`. That is branch (c) of Theorem L.

*Scope (`PROVED` / `HEURISTIC` split).* The theorem is about the atomic Lefschetz numbers, which Def 2.1 makes
canonical. It does not constrain intersection numbers of cycles that are not Frobenius correspondences, nor pairings
whose Lefschetz numbers are not read off the atoms of `𝓛`. Any carrier passing OT-2 (C8) must reproduce `𝓛`, and hence
its atoms. That its "Frobenius–diagonal intersection numbers" *are* those atoms is the defining property of a Lefschetz
formula over `F_q` (localisation at fixed points). For a carrier whose trace formula is not localised on closed orbits
this identification is `HEURISTIC`. OT-12 requires a fixed-point-type formula, which localises (R158_001 OT-12 row).

## §5 The negative index of the Weil form, by sector, and what reciprocity can see

**Proposition N (`PROVED`; the equality is consistent with Bombieri 2000 Thm 8, `CITED` via P46 l.286, not re-read).**
Assume the multiset of non-trivial zeros off the critical line consists of finitely many distinct quadruples
`𝒬_j = {ρ_j, \bar ρ_j, 1 − ρ_j, 1 − \bar ρ_j}`, `j = 1, …, k`, with `ρ_j = ½ + δ_j + iγ_j`, `δ_j ∈ (0, ½)`,
`γ_j > 0`, and multiplicities `m_j ≥ 1`. Assume further that there are no real zeros in `(0,1)`; for ζ this holds,
since `ζ(σ) < 0` on `(0,1)`. Let `W` be the complete Weil form on real test functions in `⋃_T C_c^∞(I_T)`. Then:

  (i) on the **real-even** sector, `n₋(W) = k`;
  (ii) on the **real-odd** sector, `n₋(W) = k`;
  (iii) on **real** tests, `n₋(W) = 2k`; on **complex** tests (hermitian extension), `n₋ = 2k`;
  (iv) for every `T`, `n₋(W_T | D_T^{ev}) ≤ k`, and `T ↦ n₋(W_T | D_T^{ev})` is nondecreasing.

If there are infinitely many off-line zeros, then `n₋ = ∞` in each sector. This follows from the lower-bound
construction applied to any `k` of them.

*Proof.* Sector (i). For real even `F`: `Φ_F(1 − s) = Φ_F(s)` (substitute `t → −t`) and `Φ_F(\bar s) = \overline{Φ_F(s)}`.

On the line, `ρ = ½ + iγ`, the term is `Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1 − ρ) = F̂(γ)²`. This is `≥ 0`, because `F̂(γ) ∈ R` for real even `F`.

On a quadruple, put `a_j := Φ_F(ρ_j) = F̂(z_j)` with `z_j = γ_j − iδ_j`. The four values are
`Φ_F(ρ_j) = Φ_F(1 − ρ_j) = a_j` and `Φ_F(\bar ρ_j) = Φ_F(1 − \bar ρ_j) = \bar a_j`. The four terms sum to
`m_j(a_j² + \bar a_j² + a_j² + \bar a_j²) = 4m_j Re(a_j²)`.

So `W(F) = P(F) + H(F)`, where:
* `P(F) = Σ_{line} m_ρ F̂(γ)² ≥ 0`;
* `H(F) = Σ_j 4m_j Re(F̂(z_j)²)`.

For `F ∈ C_c^∞`, `F̂` is rapidly decreasing in every horizontal strip, so both sums converge absolutely.

Upper bound. `H = H₀ ∘ E`, where `E(F) = (F̂(z_j))_j ∈ C^k ≅ R^{2k}` and `H₀(x + iy) = Σ 4m_j(x_j² − y_j²)`. So
`n₋(H) ≤ n₋(H₀) = k`. For `P ≥ 0`, `n₋(P + H) ≤ n₋(H)`: a subspace `U` on which `P + H < 0` has `H < 0` on `U`, so
`dim U ≤ n₋(H)`. Hence `n₋(W) ≤ k`.

Lower bound. For `ε > 0`, `ε ≤ 1`, we construct `k` real even tests `G_1, …, G_k` in Weil's class (smooth,
`G(t)e^{(½+η)|t|} ∈ L¹` with derivatives, `Ĝ` entire). The requirements are `E(G_i) = i e_i + O(ε)` and
`P(G_i, G_i) ≤ ε`.
1. Let `q_j(z) := Π_{i≠j}(z − z_i)(z − \bar z_i)(z + z_i)(z + \bar z_i)`. It has real coefficients, is even, and vanishes
  at every `±z_i`, `±\bar z_i` with `i ≠ j`.
2. For `σ ∈ (0, 1]` put
  `ĝ_{j,σ}(z) := q_j(z)[(z − γ_j)e^{−(z−γ_j)²/2σ²} − (z + γ_j)e^{−(z+γ_j)²/2σ²}]`.
  It is entire, even, and real on `R`. It is the Fourier transform of a real even Schwartz function whose
  super-exponential decay puts it in Weil's class.
3. At `z_j`, `(z_j − γ_j)e^{−(z_j−γ_j)²/2σ²} = −iδ_j e^{δ_j²/2σ²}`, and the second bracket term is
  `O(e^{(δ_j² − 4γ_j²)/2σ²})`. At `z_i` (`i ≠ j`), `ĝ_{j,σ}(z_i) = 0` exactly, by the factor `q_j`.
4. Hence `E(ĝ_{j,σ}) = −iδ_j q_j(z_j)e^{δ_j²/2σ²}(1 + o(1)) e_j`, with `q_j(z_j) ≠ 0` since the `z_i` are distinct and
  `γ_i > 0`.
5. The companion `ĝ′_{j,σ}` without the linear factors `(z ∓ γ_j)` has value `q_j(z_j)e^{δ_j²/2σ²}(1 + o(1))` at `z_j`.
  A real combination of `ĝ_{j,σ}` and `ĝ′_{j,σ}` therefore reaches `i·e^{δ_j²/2σ²}(1 + o(1))e_j` with bounded real
  coefficients.
6. On the real line, `|ĝ_{j,σ}(x)| ≤ |q_j(x)|(1 + |x|)(e^{−(x−γ_j)²/2σ²} + e^{−(x+γ_j)²/2σ²})`. The line zeros satisfy
  `Σ_{|γ − x| ≤ 1} m_ρ = O(log(2 + |x|))`. So `P(ĝ_{j,σ}) ≤ C_j`, uniformly for `σ ≤ 1`.
7. Normalise by `e^{δ_j²/2σ²}`. The normalised tests `G_j` satisfy `E(G_j) → i e_j` and
  `P(G_j) ≤ C_j e^{−δ_j²/σ²} → 0`.
8. On `span(G_j)`, `W = P + H ≤ o(1)|c|² − Σ 4m_j|c_j|²(1 + o(1)) < 0` for small `σ`. So `n₋(W) ≥ k` on Weil's class.

Passage to `C_c^∞`. Cut off: `G_j χ(t/R)` with `χ ∈ C_c^∞`, `χ = 1` near `0`. As `R → ∞`, `(G_jχ(·/R))^` converges to
`Ĝ_j` uniformly on the strip `|Im z| ≤ ½ + η/2`, and the prime and archimedean sides converge, since
`Σ_n c(n)|G(log n)|` and `∫(1 + |t|)|G|e^{|t|/2}` are controlled by the weighted `L¹` norm. So `W` converges on the
`k`-dimensional span, and strict negativity persists. This passage is the standard continuity of Weil's functional on
Weil's class; I state it without writing every estimate, and that is the one step of Prop N at "sketch" level.

(ii) Odd real `F`: `Φ_F(1 − s) = −Φ_F(s)`, and the quadruple term is `−4m_j Re(a_j²)`, again of signature `(1, 1)`.
The line terms are `−Φ_F(ρ)²`. There `Φ_F(½ + iγ) = F̂(γ) ∈ iR`, so `−F̂(γ)² = |F̂(γ)|² ≥ 0`. The same proof applies,
with the odd symmetrisation.

(iii) `W` commutes with `t → −t`, so the even and odd sectors are `W`-orthogonal, and (iii) for real tests is the sum of
(i) and (ii). For complex tests the hermitian form is `Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)\overline{Φ_F(1 − \bar ρ)}`. Each reflection pair
`{ρ, 1 − \bar ρ}` gives `2Re(a\bar b)`, which is hyperbolic over `C`, and a quadruple contains two reflection pairs:
`2k`.

(iv) `D_T^{ev}` is contained in the even sector, which gives the bound. `D_T^{ev} ⊂ D_{T′}^{ev}` for `T < T′` and
`W_{T′}` restricts to `W_T` (P46 §1), which gives monotonicity. ∎

*Remark N1 (real zeros; `PROVED`, field-uniformity note).* For a self-dual `L`-function with a real zero
`β ∈ (½, 1)` (a Siegel-type zero), the pair `{β, 1 − β}` contributes `2m a²` with `a = Φ_F(β) ∈ R` on even tests,
which is `≥ 0`, and `−2m a²` on odd tests. So a real off-line zero is **invisible in the even sector** and visible only
in the odd sector. Any OT-10 statement made with even tests only, as P46 does ("`v_n` on even tests"), cannot detect
Siegel zeros. For ζ this is void, since ζ has no real zeros in `(0,1)`.

**Corollary R (what reciprocity can conclude; `PROVED` from M3, Prop N, Lemma 2.4).** Let `(V_Q, q, π)` satisfy (R1)–(R2)
on window `T`, real-even sector, and let `k` be as in Prop N (possibly `∞`).
  (a) Every finite-rank subspace `V′ ⊂ V_Q` has `s₋(q|V′) ≤ n₋(W_T|D_T^{ev}) ≤ k`.
  (b) Local invariants determine `s₋(q|V′) mod 4` and nothing more (M3, sharp).
  Suppose `V′` captures all negative directions, i.e. `s₋(q|V′) = n₋(W_T|D_T^{ev}) =: k_T`. Then local data give
  `k_T mod 4`. A reciprocity argument on such a `V′`, without an a priori bound, can prove at most "the number of
  off-line quadruples seen by window `T` is `≡ 0 mod 4`". It can never prove `k_T = 0`.
  (c) The small-rank clause (S) of Ancona's method is equivalent to an a priori bound. Precisely:
  * `r₀ ≤ 3` gives the bound `s₋(q|V_i) ≤ 3` for free on each piece.
  * For a single piece capturing all negative directions, local data decide `s₋` iff `k_T ≤ 3` is known in advance.
  * The global a priori statement "`k ≤ 3`" (at most three off-line quadruples) is `OPEN` for ζ. Finiteness of the
  off-line zero set is itself unknown; zero-density theorems give `N(σ, R) = o(N(R))`, not finiteness.

*Reading.* On `Spec Z`, reciprocity is a detector of the number of off-line quadruples mod 4. It turns into a
definiteness proof only through small orthogonal pieces with independently known local invariants, i.e. through
(R3) + (R4). By Q1′, (R3) with `r₀ = 1` always exists and is circular. So all the weight falls on (R4).

## §6 Theorem L — the period-matrix loophole, closed branch by branch

**Theorem L (`PROVED` in branches (a) and (c-i); a proved circularity in (b); `HEURISTIC` only in the completeness
of the list of sources, see the weakest step).** Let a reciprocity certificate (Def 2.3) be given on a window `T` with
`π(e^{2T}) ≥ 2`, i.e. `T > ½log 3`. Its independent local data (R4) must come from one of the following sources.

**(a) A Frobenius-compatible rational intersection theory.** This is the source in Tate and in Ancona: the pieces are
motivic, the correspondences preserve `V_Q`, and `q ⊗ Q_ℓ` comes from an `ℓ`-adic realization via comparison. It
requires a Lefschetz structure of finite period rank `m` (Def 2.2). **Excluded by Corollary B1**:
`m ≥ π(e^{2T}) − 1 ≥ 1`, and `m = ∞` for the all-`T` statement. With the Weil normalization, `m = 0` is impossible
already for `T > ½log 2`. In addition, no correspondence-stable finite-rank piece exists inside `𝒜` (Cor M4′ of R1,
using Conrey or Levinson + Heath-Brown). So Ancona's CM decomposition has no analogue even if rationality were granted.

**(b) A rational structure on `H¹` not preserved by the correspondences, with local invariants from local data.**
Local data means Euler factors, `log p`, `p^{−1/2}`, Γ, conductors: the C16 list. **A proved circularity.**
* By Q1 the structure is free: its local invariants are constrained only by the real signature, so they are
  informative only if (R4) is proved independently.
* **Proposition X (`PROVED`, given C16 and Prop N).** Suppose the invariants `(λ_{i,ℓ})` are produced by a procedure
  that reads only C16-type local data. Then any proof of the real-realization clause (R2) for the resulting
  `(V_Q, q, π)` must contain a step that fails for `Ξ_a(s) = ξ(s + a)ξ(s − a)`, `0 < a < ½`.

  *Proof.* By C16 (P47 Prop 22.5, Thm 26.13; `PROVED` of record) the procedure's output is unchanged under
  `ξ → Ξ_a`. So the same `(V_Q, q, (V_i), (λ_{i,ℓ}))` is produced for `Ξ_a`.

  If (R2) were proved by a `Ξ_a`-valid argument, then (R2) would hold for `Ξ_a`'s form `W_T^{Ξ_a}`. Lemma 2.4 with
  `r₀ ≤ 3`, or Cor R(b)–(c), would then give `W_T^{Ξ_a} ≥ 0` on `D_T^{ev}` for all `T` at which the certificate is
  produced.

  But the zeros of `Ξ_a` are `ρ ∓ a`: infinitely many off-line quadruples `{½ ± a + iγ, ½ ± a − iγ}`. By Prop N
  (lower bound, `k = ∞`), and monotonicity in `T`, `n₋(W_T^{Ξ_a}|D_T^{ev}) > 0` for all large `T`. This is a
  contradiction for such `T`. ∎

  *Reading.* The content is then entirely in (R2), the equality `q = π^*W_T` on the pieces. That is the computation of
  the real Gram matrix, per window. By `prop:finite` it is decidable, and by Sylvester it already contains the
  signature. For the all-`T` arrow it is the OT-5 gate, i.e. OT-5′ unsupplied. Reciprocity adds nothing.

**(c) A period-matrix carrier with infinitely many periods (at least one per prime).**
* **(c-i) `PROVED`.** The field `K_∞ := Q(c(n) : n ≥ 2)` is not a number field, since `log 2 / √2` is transcendental
  (Lindemann: `log 2` transcendental). So `K_∞` is not a global field (global = finite over `Q` or over `F_q(t)`). The
  Hilbert reciprocity law `∏_v ε_v = 1` behind M3 is a theorem about quadratic forms over global fields. It does not
  apply to forms whose Gram entries lie in `K_∞`. Either:
  * the form is rational over `Q`, which is branch (a) with `m = 0`, excluded; or
  * the periods are carried as independent data, one per prime. The tautological rational structure is then the
  prime-by-prime one (divide by `log p`). Its "local pieces" are the local prime forms `Q_p`, which are indefinite on
  `P_T` for `T > ½log p` (`cor:Qp`, C17). **Per A9 (R158_003) this excludes only a derivation of the sign place by
  place, not a positive global sum.**
* **(c-ii) `HEURISTIC`.** A reciprocity law over `K_∞`, or over a field of transcendence degree `≥ 1`, relating one
  real ordering to "finite places" would be needed. The transcendence degree of `Q(log 2, log 3)` is itself `OPEN`: it
  is `2` under Schanuel, and algebraic independence of `log 2, log 3` is unknown. No such law is known, and the
  orderings of such fields form a continuum.

**Conclusion of Theorem L.**
* S4 (Ancona reciprocity) is excluded for `Spec Z` in branches (a) and (c-i), both `PROVED`.
* In branch (b) it is reduced to computing the real Gram matrix, which is circular (`PROVED` via Prop X).
* The period-matrix loophole of R1 §3.5 is closed as asked: finite period matrices are impossible (Theorem B);
  infinite ones are prime-by-prime (c).

## §8 What is new, what is not, the weakest step

**Not new.**
* Weil's criterion and CCM Cor 6.3 (M1, R1).
* Bombieri's count of negative eigenvalues (Prop N as an equality is consistent with his Thm 8, cited via P46). The
  sector-wise bookkeeping (i)–(iv) and Remark N1 are elementary and probably folklore.
* Baker's theorem, Hasse–Minkowski existence, Lindemann.

**New to the record (elementary, `PROVED`).**
1. **Theorem B / Cor B1.** The atomic Lefschetz numbers of the Frobenius correspondences in window `T` span a `Q̄`-module
  of rank exactly `π(e^{2T})` (`π(e^{2T}) + 1` with `1`). So no intersection theory with finite period rank has the
  explicit formula as its Lefschetz formula. Rational intersection numbers, the Weil/Tate/Ancona input, die at
  `T > ½log 3` for any real rescaling, and at `T > ½log 2` in the Weil normalization. A curve over `F_q` has rank `1`.
  This is the object-side counterpart of `G2_B4_R1` S3(a), which is mechanism-side; both rest on Baker.
2. **Theorem Q1 / Cor Q1′.** Rational structures on the real form are free and realize every compatible local system.
  A rank-one reciprocity certificate exists iff `W_T ≥ 0`, and writing it down requires the signs.
3. **Cor R.** Reciprocity on `Spec Z` detects `#{off-line quadruples} mod 4` (real-even sector). Ancona's small-rank
  clause is equivalent to the `OPEN` a priori bound "at most three off-line quadruples".
4. **Prop X.** A certificate fed by local data must prove its real-realization clause by a `Ξ_a`-failing step, so all of
  its content sits in the Gram computation.
5. **Theorem L.** The period-matrix loophole of R1 §3.5 is closed. The motivic angle (S1–S8 of R1 plus this memo) supplies
  no OT-5′ object. It should be recorded as closed, with M1 as `EQUIVALENT` and S4 as `EXCLUDED`.

**Weakest step.**
* *Completeness of the source list in Theorem L.* Suppose (R4) is supplied by something that is neither a
  Frobenius-compatible finite-period intersection theory (a) nor a procedure on C16 local data (b). An example would be
  an `ℓ`-adic realization of a non-Frobenius-stable rational structure obtained from some unknown global object. Such a
  source is not excluded; it is only unknown. That the list is exhaustive is `HEURISTIC`.
* *Prop N lower bound, passage from Weil's class to `C_c^∞`.* This is standard continuity, not every estimate is written
  here, and the equality is also covered by Bombieri Thm 8 (`CITED`, not re-read). Theorem L uses only the upper bound
  (elementary, fully proved) and the `k = ∞` lower bound for `Ξ_a`. The latter is produced by the same Gaussian
  construction on finitely many chosen quadruples.
* *Scope of Theorem B.* It concerns the atomic Lefschetz numbers. Identifying these with the Frobenius–diagonal
  intersection numbers of a putative carrier is `HEURISTIC` for carriers whose trace formula does not localise on
  closed orbits.
* *Prop X* inherits C16's exact scope: the list of admissible local data in R158_001 C16 and P47 Prop 22.5.
