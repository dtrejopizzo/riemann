## §2 Theorem A — the idempotent collapse

**Theorem A (`PROVED`, elementary).** Let `(M, ⊕, 0)` be a commutative monoid with `x ⊕ x = x` for all `x`.
 (i) Every monoid homomorphism `h : M → (A,+)` into an abelian group is `0`. In particular the Grothendieck group
  `K_0(M) = 0`.
 (ii) Every `Q : M → R` with `Q(x ⊕ x) = 4Q(x)` (a quadratic-type invariant) is `0`.
 (iii) On the scaling-site chart `[0,∞)`, with Frobenius `Fr_λ(x) = λx` (`λ > 0`) and pointwise `⊕ = max`,
  `Fr_λ ⊕ Fr_μ = Fr_{max(λ,μ)}`. So the `⊕`-closure of `{Fr_λ}` is `{Fr_λ}` itself, and its graphs are the rays
  `L_λ`.
 (iv) Consequently, in the scaling-site square, the "sum-graph" classes `Γ_{Fr_λ ⊕ Fr_μ}` are Frobenius rays, and every
  intersection number formed from them is a function of `(λ, μ)` alone. The characteristic-one analogue of
  `deg(m+nπ) = m² + amn + qn²` has no cross term: the trace `a = Σα_i`, which carries the spectral information, is
  identically absent.

*Proof.* (i) `h(x) = h(x ⊕ x) = 2h(x)`, so `h(x) = 0`. The universal map `M → K_0(M)` is such a homomorphism with
generating image, so `K_0(M) = 0`. (ii) `Q(x) = Q(x ⊕ x) = 4Q(x)`. (iii) For `x ≥ 0`,
`max(λx, μx) = max(λ,μ)x`. (iv) is (iii) combined with the ray description. ∎

**Reading (`PROVED` as a method constraint, schema of P47 Restatement 12.4a / C16).** The construction in (iii)–(iv)
never references `ζ`. Any cone or form built from the `⊕`-structure of the scaling site alone is therefore the same for
`ξ` and for `Ξ_a`. By Restatement 12.4a (C12), no positivity derivation from it alone can be sound. The model's `+`
(Rem 21.23) is a **ring** addition with cancellation, whose degree form has the cross term `amn`. Characteristic one
offers only an idempotent `+`, whose group completion vanishes. Over `Z`, the base `Spec Z` is terminal and
`m + n·Frob` has no realization by self-maps (P47 Thm 22.17(iii), C3). So neither base carries the ring `Z[Frob]`. A
genuine `+` must come from a structure that is neither idempotent nor a self-map of the base. Lemma H (§7) is the one
such structure in the CC school.

**Remark A.1 (`HEURISTIC`).** On the log-chart of `R_+^max` (`Fr_λ(x) = x^λ`, `X = log x ∈ R`), the pointwise sum
`max(λX, μX)` (`λ > μ`) is not linear. Its graph becomes a balanced (real-weight) tropical cycle only after adding the
half-ray `{X = 0, Y < 0}` with weight `λ − μ`. Balancing at `0`: `(1,λ) + (−1,−μ) + (λ−μ)(0,−1) = 0`. So the "sum"
appears together with a **piece of a fibre**, exactly the pattern of Rem 21.23 ("the graphs of the sums, together with
the fibres"). This cycle is not scaling-equivariant, so it is outside the correspondence module on which `I_∂` is
defined (107_239). Pairing it with anything requires a new intersection theory (§6, §9).

## §3 Theorem B — the homogeneous effective cone of the square

**Definition 3.1.** Let `Q = (0,∞)²` be the quadrant chart of 107_237. An *effective divisor* on `Q` is the Hessian
measure `Div U := ∇²U` (a positive-semidefinite matrix-valued Radon measure) of a continuous convex `U : Q → R`, taken
modulo affine `U`. For a finite max of affine functions this is the tropical curve of `U` with its weights; this is the
convention of 107_237 (2.4). `Div U` is *homogeneous* if `U(sx,sy) = sU(x,y)` for all `s > 0`, i.e. `U` is
equivariant for the diagonal scaling flow, which is the condition for `Div U` to lie in the correspondence module of
107_237–107_240.

**Theorem B (`PROVED`).**
 (i) `U` is convex and 1-homogeneous on `Q` if and only if `U(x,y) = x·u(y/x)` with `u : (0,∞) → R` convex.
 (ii) In that case `Div U = ∫_{(0,∞)}[L_r]dν(r)` with `ν = u''` (a positive Radon measure on `(0,∞)`), where `[L_r]` is
  the ray current `y = rx` normalised as in 107_237 (2.4). `ν` determines `U` modulo linear functions.
 (iii) Conversely every positive Radon measure `ν` on `(0,∞)` arises in this way.
 (iv) Hence the homogeneous effective cone of the square is `{∫[L_r]dν(r) : ν ≥ 0}`. Under the dictionary of §1 its
  elements of finite degree form the cone `𝓜_+` of positive Radon measures `μ` on `R` with
  `∫2cosh(t/2)dμ < ∞`, and the class of `μ` is `ℓ_μ(G) := ∫w_G dμ`. The cone `𝓜_+` contains `𝓔` (as
  `F(t)dt`, `F ≥ 0`), and every element of `𝓜_+` is a weak limit of elements of `𝓔`.
 (v) The two coordinate axes, which are the rulings, are the limits of the degree-normalised rays: as a functional,
  `Ψ_λ/deg Ψ_λ → e_0` as `λ → +∞` and `→ e_1` as `λ → 0⁺`. Adjoining them gives
  `𝓜^ext_+ := {ℓ_μ + αe_0 + βe_1 : μ ∈ 𝓜_+, α,β ≥ 0}`, the cone of positive Radon measures `ν` on `[−∞,+∞]` acting
  by `ℓ_ν(G) = ∫_{[−∞,∞]} w̃_G dν`, with `deg ℓ_ν = ν([−∞,∞])`.

*Proof.* (i) (⇒) Put `u(r) := U(1,r)`. It is convex as the restriction of a convex function to a line, and
`U(x,y) = xU(1,y/x) = xu(y/x)`. (⇐) `xu(y/x)` is the perspective of the convex function `u`, which is jointly convex on
`x > 0` (standard: for `θ ∈ [0,1]`, write `x = θx_1 + (1−θ)x_2` and `y/x = (θx_1/x)(y_1/x_1) + ((1−θ)x_2/x)(y_2/x_2)`,
and apply convexity of `u` with those weights). It is 1-homogeneous by inspection.
(ii) For smooth `u`, direct differentiation gives `∇²U = (u''(r)/x)·v_rv_r^T`, `r = y/x`, `v_r = (r,−1)^T`
(107_238 (1.2)). This is the ray current `[L_r]` with density `u''(r)dr` in the normalisation of 107_237 (2.3)–(2.4),
where `u_f'' = f(r)/r` and `D_f = ∫f[L_λ]d^*λ`, so that `f d^*r = u''dr`. For general convex `u`, mollify `u` in `r`:
this preserves convexity, the homogeneous extensions converge locally uniformly, and Hessian measures converge weakly.
`u''` is determined by `U` modulo the kernel of `u ↦ u''`, the affine `u`, i.e. linear `U`.
(iii) Given `ν ≥ 0`, let `u` be a second antiderivative of `ν`. It is convex, and (i)–(ii) apply.
(iv) Push `ν` through the dictionary: the ray at `r` goes to `r^{1/2}δ_{log r}` (§1), so `ν` goes to the positive
measure `μ = (log)_*(r^{1/2}ν)`. Positivity is preserved. `𝓔 ⊆ 𝓜_+` is the case `ν = f d^*r`, `f ≥ 0`, and weak
density of absolutely continuous positive measures is standard. That `ℓ_μ(G) = ∫w_G dμ` extends `ℓ_E` is Thm 11.14(i)
for `dμ = E dt`. It converges absolutely because `|w_G| ≤ C_G·2cosh(u/2)`.
(v) `Ψ_λ` has class `λ^{1/2}w_G(log λ)` and degree `1+λ`. Their ratio is `w̃_G(log λ)`, since
`λ^{1/2}/(1+λ) = 1/2cosh(½log λ)`. Lemma 1.1(ii) gives the limits. The measure description is the rewriting
`ν = 2cosh(t/2)μ` on `R`, `ν({+∞}) = α`, `ν({−∞}) = β`, with `w̃_{2K} ≡ 1` (Lemma 1.1(i)). ∎

**Corollary B.1 (`PROVED`).** No homogeneous effective divisor on the square is a "sum graph": by the rank-one Hessian,

## §4 Theorem C — Riesz characterization, and the frame defect on the whole measure cone

**Theorem C (`PROVED`).** Let `ℓ : D^W → R` be linear with `deg ℓ = ℓ(2K) ≥ 0`. The following are equivalent:
 (a) `|ℓ(G)| ≤ C_G·deg ℓ` for every `G ∈ D^W` (bounded density with the frame's constants, Thm 11.14(ii));
 (b) `ℓ ∈ 𝓜^ext_+`, i.e. `ℓ(G) = ∫_{[−∞,∞]}w̃_G dν` for a positive Radon measure `ν` on `[−∞,∞]`, of total mass
  `deg ℓ`.

*Proof.* (b)⇒(a): `|∫w̃_G dν| ≤ ‖w̃_G‖_∞ν([−∞,∞]) = C_G deg ℓ` (Lemma 1.1(ii)).
(a)⇒(b): Let `X = [−∞,∞]` and `V := {w̃_G : G ∈ D^W} ⊆ C(X)` (Lemma 1.1(ii)). `V` is a linear subspace, since `G ↦ w_G`
is linear, and `1 = w̃_{2K} ∈ V`. Define `Λ(w̃_G) := ℓ(G)`. This is well defined: if `w̃_G = w̃_{G'}` then
`C_{G−G'} = 0`, so `ℓ(G−G') = 0` by (a). `Λ` is linear, with `|Λ(v)| ≤ Λ(1)‖v‖_∞` and `Λ(1) = deg ℓ`. By Hahn–Banach,
extend it to `Λ̃` on `C(X)` with `‖Λ̃‖ = Λ̃(1)`. Such a functional is positive: for `0 ≤ v ≤ 1`, `‖1−v‖_∞ ≤ 1` gives
`Λ̃(1) − Λ̃(v) ≤ Λ̃(1)`, so `Λ̃(v) ≥ 0`. By the Riesz–Markov theorem, `Λ̃(v) = ∫v dν` with `ν ≥ 0`. Restricting to `V`

## §5 Proposition D — the missing classes in the model, computed

**Proposition D (`PROVED`, from (X27) = P47 Prop 21.22).** Let `E/F_q` be an ordinary elliptic curve, `End(E) = Z[π]`
of rank 2, `a = π + π̄`, and `S = E × E` with `F_1, F_2`, `Δ = Γ_1`, `Γ_π`, where `Γ_α·F_1 = 1`,
`Γ_α·F_2 = deg α`, `Γ_α·Γ_β = deg(α−β)` (21.13). Then in `NS(S)_Q`, for all `(m,n) ∈ Z²∖{0}`:

  Γ_{m+nπ} ≡ mΔ + nΓ_π + (m² + amn + qn² − m − nq)·F_1 + (1 − m − n)·F_2 .

*Proof.* `F_1, F_2, Δ, Γ_π` span `NS(S)_Q` (rank `2 + rk End = 4`, (X27)), and the intersection form is nondegenerate
on `NS ⊗ Q`. So it suffices to match intersections with the four classes. Write `R` for the right side and use
`F_i² = 0`, `F_1·F_2 = 1`, `Δ·F_i = Γ_π·F_1 = 1`, `Γ_π·F_2 = q`, `Δ² = Γ_π² = 0`, `Δ·Γ_π = deg(1−π) = 1 − a + q`.
`R·F_1 = m + n + (1−m−n) = 1`. `R·F_2 = m + nq + (m² + amn + qn² − m − nq) = deg(m+nπ)`.
`R·Δ = n(1−a+q) + (m²+amn+qn²−m−nq) + (1−m−n) = (m−1)² + a(m−1)n + qn² = deg(m−1+nπ)`.
`R·Γ_π = m(1−a+q) + (m²+amn+qn²−m−nq) + q(1−m−n) = m² + am(n−1) + q(n−1)² = deg(m+(n−1)π)`.
These are exactly `Γ_{m+nπ}·F_1, ·F_2, ·Δ, ·Γ_π`. ∎

**Reading (`PROVED` for the model; the transcription is `HEURISTIC`).**
* The **primitive part** of `Γ_{m+nπ}` (its projection orthogonal to the hyperbolic plane `⟨F_1,F_2⟩`) is
  `m·prim(Δ) + n·prim(Γ_π)`, an **arbitrary signed** integer combination. The Frobenius cone `𝓔_C` contains only
  nonnegative combinations. This is the whole of "the frame has no `+`": the sum graphs make signed Frobenius

* The **ruling part** is fixed by `deg(m+nπ)`, a **kernel count** `#ker(m+nπ)` (for separable `m+nπ`). It can lower the
  degree below that of the formal combination. For example `H·Γ_{1+π} = 2 + a + q < 3 + q = H·(Δ+Γ_π)` when `a < 1`,
  with the same primitive part. This raises the density ratio beyond the Frobenius cone, which is exactly how
  `σ(Eff) = ½ > ½s_C = σ(𝓔_C)` arises in Thm 21.17.
* The effectivity of `Γ_{m+nπ}` is **geometric** (it is a subvariety). Its degree is a count. Neither uses `|α| = √q`.
  This is Weil's direction (W-6), and it is the direction N-a asks for.

## §6 Theorem E — the arithmetic sum-graph axiom is RH

Transcribe Proposition D to the frame. In the extended space `D^W ⊕ Re_0 ⊕ Re_1` with the form of §1, a *graph class*
of a primitive `G ∈ P_c^∞` is `Γ_{G;α,β} := G + αe_0 + βe_1` with `α, β ≥ 0`. Since `G` is primitive,
`⟨Γ,Γ⟩ = −W(G) + 2αβ`, where `W(G) := −I_∞(G,G)`, and `deg Γ = α + β`. The graph normalisation `Γ² = 0`
(P47 §21.7: a difference-only form, diagonal subtracted) is `2αβ = W(G)`. With `α = 1` (`Γ·F_1 = 1`) the "count" is
`β = W(G)/2`.

**(SG) (sum-graph axiom for a cone `𝔈` of classes).** For every primitive real `G ∈ P_c^∞` and all `α, β ≥ 0` with
`2αβ ≥ W(G)`, the class `G + αe_0 + βe_1` lies in `𝔈`.

**Theorem E (`PROVED`; RH-strength).** Let `𝔈` be a convex cone of classes satisfying the degree gate
(`ℓ ∈ 𝔈`, `ℓ ≠ 0` implies `deg ℓ > 0`; P47 (21.12)).
 (i) If `𝔈` satisfies (SG), then RH holds.
 (ii) Conversely, under RH the cone `𝔈_RH := 𝓜^ext_+ + {G + αe_0 + βe_1 : G ∈ P_c^∞, α,β ≥ 0, 2αβ ≥ W(G), (α,β) ≠ 0}`
  satisfies (SG) and the degree gate.
 So (SG) under the degree gate is **equivalent to RH**. It is the restriction of Prop 21.21 to the 3-dimensional slices
 `span(G, e_0, e_1)`, i.e. to the isotropic boundary of `Pos` through the two rulings.

*Proof.* (i) If RH fails, then by P45 Thm 10.4 there is a primitive real `P ∈ P_c^∞`, `P ∉ 𝓝_∞`, with `W(P) < 0`.
(SG) with `α = β = 0` (allowed, since `0 ≥ W(P)`) puts `P ∈ 𝔈`. But `P ≠ 0` as a class (it is not in the radical) and
`deg P = 0`, against the gate. (ii) Under RH, `W(G) = Σ_γ|Φ_G(½+iγ)|² ≥ 0` for primitive real `G` (Rem 19.25c(i)).
(SG) holds by construction. Gate: a nonzero sum of elements has `deg = ν(X) + Σ(α_j + β_j)`. If this is `0`, then
`ν = 0` and all `α_j = β_j = 0`. The summands are then `G_j` with `W(G_j) ≤ 0`, hence `W(G_j) = 0`. Under RH this forces
`Φ_{G_j}(ρ) = 0` at every zero, i.e. `G_j ∈ 𝓝_∞`, and the sum is the zero class. ∎

**Reading (`HEURISTIC`, sharpening N-a).** The count a carrier must supply is precise: for each signed primitive `G`, an
honest "graph" subobject `Γ_G` meeting a ruling in a set whose size (cardinality, dimension, renormalized measure) is
`W(G)/2` **by a counting identity**, and whose effectivity is a matter of geometry, not of positivity. In the model the
count is `#ker(m+nπ)`. A proof that `W(G)/2` is such a count **is** RH. It cannot be assumed, and it has to come from
the carrier (N-a, P47 §21.7 "deliver the count, never the positivity").

## §7 Proposition F — the Cartwright transfer (107_243) fails at its step (ii)

107_243 proposed to transfer Cartwright's tropical Hodge index (`1506.02023` Thm 1.1: at most one positive eigenvalue),
whose proof is (i) block-diagonal locality, (ii) one positive eigenvalue per local block, (iii) a Sylvester count. The
target (P1) asked whether the local block at a finite `p` has exactly one positive eigenvalue.

**Proposition F (`PROVED`; content of record C17 / `cor:Qp` / NG-E1, applied here to 107_243).** Let
`q_p(F) := (log p)Σ_{k≥1}p^{−k/2}[c_F(k log p) + c_F(−k log p)]`, `c_F = F ⋆ F^∨`, be the `p`-part of the prime side
of the Weil form on real `F ∈ C_c^∞(R)`. Then, for every `J ≥ 1`, `q_p` restricted to a `J`-dimensional space of tests
supported in an interval of length `(J−1)log p + 1` is the Gram of `(log p)‖g‖²_2·(T_J(P_r) − I)`. Here `T_J(P_r)` is
the `J×J` Toeplitz matrix of the Poisson kernel `P_r(θ) = Σ_k r^{|k|}e^{ikθ}`, `r = p^{−1/2}`. The numbers of positive
and of negative eigenvalues both tend to `+∞` with `J`. So the `p`-local block has `n_+ = n_− = ∞` on `C_c^∞`, which
violates (ii). Moreover all the local forms `q_p` and the archimedean form act on the **same** test space, so the
decomposition is not an orthogonal direct sum, and step (iii) has no block structure to count.

*Proof.* Fix `g ≥ 0`, `g ≢ 0`, `g ∈ C_c^∞(−δ,δ)` with `4δ < log p`. Put `F_φ := Σ_{j=0}^{J−1}φ_j g(· − j log p)`. Then
`c_{F_φ}(a) = Σ_{j,j'}φ_jφ_{j'}c_g(a − (j−j')log p)`, and `supp c_g ⊆ (−2δ,2δ)`. So
`c_{F_φ}(k log p) = ‖g‖²Σ_jφ_jφ_{j−k}`, and `q_p(F_φ) = (log p)‖g‖²⟨φ,(T_J(P_r) − I)φ⟩`. The symbol `P_r − 1` takes the
value `2r/(1−r) > 0` at `θ = 0` and `−2r/(1+r) < 0` at `θ = π`, and it is continuous. By Szegő's first limit theorem
(CLASSICAL: Grenander–Szegő, *Toeplitz Forms*, Ch. 5), the proportion of eigenvalues of `T_J(P_r) − I` in `(0,∞)` tends
to `|{θ : P_r(θ) > 1}|/2π > 0`, and likewise for `(−∞,0)`. The map `φ ↦ F_φ` is injective, so the counts transfer. ∎

**Consequence.** (P1) of 107_243 is answered negatively. (P3), a renormalized count, would have to compare `∞ − ∞` local
excesses. The Cartwright architecture does not transfer to the Weil form's place decomposition. This is consistent with
C17: `cor:Qp` already gives indefinite local forms and warns that this does **not** forbid a positive sum. Proposition F
excludes only the *Cartwright-type derivation*, not the sign.

## §8 Lemma H — a non-idempotent "+" with a kernel: the adèle-class hyperring (round-2 lead)

Krasner's construction: for a commutative ring `R` and a subgroup `G ⊆ R^×`, the set `R/G` of orbits `[a] = aG` is a
hyperring with `[a] ⊕ [b] := {[ag + bh] : g,h ∈ G}` and `[a]·[b] = [ab]`. Connes–Consani consider `K_Q := A_Q/Q^×`
(Krasner quotient by the units `Q^× ⊂ A_Q^×`), on which the idèle class group `C_Q = A_Q^×/Q^×` acts by multiplication
(CC, *The hyperring of adèle classes*, J. Number Theory 131 (2011); **not read this phase**. Only the Krasner
definition, stated here in full, is used).

**Lemma H (`PROVED`, elementary).** Let `λ ∈ A_Q^×` and `[a] ∈ K_Q`. Then `0 ∈ [a] ⊕ [λa]` if and only if there is
`q ∈ Q^×` with `a_v = 0` at every place `v` with `λ_v ≠ −q^{−1}`. So
`ker(1 ⊕ λ) = ⋃_{q∈Q^×}{[a] : supp(a) ⊆ S_q(λ)}`, where `S_q(λ) := {v : λ_v = −1/q}`. If the class of `λ` in `C_Q` is
not that of a rational number (up to sign), then every `S_q(λ)` is a proper subset of the places. The kernel then lies in
the locus of adèle classes with a vanishing component, which is where the idèle-class action has nontrivial isotropy
(Connes 1999, the periodic-orbit locus of the trace formula; CITED as in R158_001 §4).

*Proof.* `0 ∈ {[ag + λah]}` iff `ag + λah = 0` for some `g, h ∈ Q^×`, iff `a(1 + qλ) = 0` with `q = h/g`, iff
`a_v(1 + qλ_v) = 0` for all `v`. If `S_q(λ)` were all places for some `q`, then `λ = −1/q ∈ Q^×`, which is trivial in
`C_Q`. ∎

**Continuation (`HEURISTIC` / `OPEN`).** This is the first structure in the CC school found in this round with all three
of the following:
 (a) a non-idempotent addition. `[a] ⊕ [−a] ∋ 0` and `⊕` is multivalued, so Theorem A(i) does not apply: `⊕` is not a
  monoid law.
 (b) "sums of Frobenius" `1 ⊕ λ` as correspondences on `K_Q × K_Q`, graphs `{([a],[z]) : [z] ∈ [a] ⊕ [λa]}`.
 (c) a kernel sitting on the fixed-point loci whose local contributions are the terms `∫'h(u^{−1})/|1−u|_v d^*u` of the
  semilocal trace formula (107_239 (2.1)–(2.2)).
The round-2 question is precise. Is there a renormalized size `#_ren ker(Σ_n c_n·Frob_n)` for finite signed combinations,
defined from `K_Q` and the cutoff of 107_239 and **not** from the zeros, whose value is `W(G)/2` for the class `G` of
`Σc_nδ_{log n}`? If so, the graphs of those hyper-sums would supply (SG) geometrically (Theorem E(i)). If not, which step
fails? Two obstructions must be checked first. (1) R158_002/R158_004 prove that every flow-compatible definite form on
the adèle class space is a restatement. A kernel count is not a form, but the renormalization may reintroduce one.
(2) N-a demands that the count's nonnegativity be automatic (cardinality/measure), while the renormalization subtracts
`2h(1)logΛ`. The sign of the renormalized count is then exactly Connes' positivity (EQUIV, R157_002 row 32). **I expect
(2) to bite. The lead is recorded because the corpus has never examined hyperring kernels, not because it is
promising.**
