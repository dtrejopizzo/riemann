## 2. Theorem L: the Laplace transform in `T` of the certificate value

Decompose `G_τ = A − P + Π` according to `eq:fw`:
```
A(T) := C₀ + ∫_0^∞(1 − w_T(u)cos τu)K(u)du, P(T) := Σ_{n≥2} v_n w_T(log n)cos(τ log n),
Π(T) := 2∫_ℝ w_T(u)cosh(u/2)cos(τu)du .
```

**Lemma 2.1 (archimedean part; PROVED).** `A(T) = m(τ) + a(T)` with `a(T) := ∫_{2T}^∞(1 − φ(u−2T))cos(τu)K(u)du`,
`a` bounded on `(0,∞)` and `|a(T)| ≤ (1 + N_δ)∫_{2T}^∞K = O(e^{−T})` for `T ≥ 1`. Hence `L_A(s) := ∫_0^∞A e^{−2sT}dT`
is meromorphic in `Re s > −½` with a single simple pole, at `s = 0`, of residue `m(τ)/2`:
`L_A(s) = m(τ)/(2s) + ∫_0^∞ a(T)e^{−2sT}dT`.

*Proof.* On `[0,2T]`, `w_T = 1`, so `C₀ + ∫_0^{2T}(1 − cos τu)K = m(τ) − ∫_{2T}^∞(1 − cos τu)K`; adding
`∫_{2T}^∞(1 − φ(u−2T)cos τu)K` gives `m(τ) + a(T)`. For `T ≥ 1`, `|1 − φ| ≤ 1 + N_δ` and `∫_{2T}^∞K ≤ 5e^{−T}` by `lem:K`.
For small `T`: `φ` is Lipschitz on compacts (`φ′` locally bounded), so `|1 − φ(u−2T)| ≤ C(u − 2T)` on `[2T, 2T+1]`,
and `(u−2T)K(u) ≤ (u−2T)/u + O(1) ≤ C` there; the rest is `≤ (1+N_δ)∫_{1}^∞K`. So `a` is bounded, and
`∫_0^∞a(T)e^{−2sT}dT` converges absolutely for `Re s > −½`. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (polar part; PROVED, exact).** With `Φ_L(a) := ∫_0^∞φ(v)e^{av}dv` (absolutely convergent for `Re a < ½ + δ`),
```
Π(T) = 2Re[ e^{(1+2iτ)T}(1/(½+iτ) + Φ_L(½+iτ)) ] + 2Re[ e^{(−1+2iτ)T}(1/(−½+iτ) + Φ_L(−½+iτ)) ] .
```
There is **no constant term**. Hence `L_Π(s)` is a rational combination of `1/(2s − (±1 ± 2iτ))`: meromorphic on ℂ, with
simple poles at `s = ½ ± iτ` and `s = −½ ± iτ` only, and analytic at `s = 0`. The residue of `L_Π` at `s = ½ + iτ` is
`½[1/(½+iτ) + Φ_L(½+iτ)]`, and at `s = ½ − iτ` its complex conjugate.

*Proof.* `Π(T) = 4∫_0^∞w_T cosh(u/2)cos(τu)du` (evenness). Write `cosh(u/2)cos(τu) = ½Re[e^{(½+iτ)u} + e^{(−½+iτ)u}]`.
On `[0,2T]`: `4·½Re[(e^{(1+2iτ)T} − 1)/(½+iτ) + (e^{(−1+2iτ)T} − 1)/(−½+iτ)]`. The constant is
`−2Re[1/(½+iτ) + 1/(−½+iτ)] = −2Re[2iτ/((½+iτ)(−½+iτ))] = −2Re[2iτ/(−¼−τ²)] = 0`. On `(2T,∞)`, substitute `u = 2T + v`:
`4∫_0^∞φ(v)·½Re[e^{(½+iτ)(2T+v)} + e^{(−½+iτ)(2T+v)}]dv = 2Re[e^{(1+2iτ)T}Φ_L(½+iτ) + e^{(−1+2iτ)T}Φ_L(−½+iτ)]`,
the integrals converging absolutely since `|φ(v)| ≤ N_δe^{−(½+δ)v}`. Laplace transforms: `∫_0^∞e^{aT}e^{−2sT}dT = 1/(2s − a)`
for `Re(2s) > Re a`, and `2Re[e^{aT}z] = e^{aT}z + e^{āT}z̄`. ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (prime part; PROVED).** Put
```
c(s) := ½[1/s + Φ_L(s)] (meromorphic in Re s < ½+δ, simple pole at 0 with residue ½),
S_τ(s) := Σ_{n≥2} v_n cos(τ log n) n^{−s} = −[ζ′/ζ(½+s+iτ) + ζ′/ζ(½+s−iτ)] (Re s > ½; meromorphic on ℂ),
R(s) := ½Σ_{n≥2} v_n cos(τ log n) n^{−s}∫_{log n}^∞φ(v)e^{sv}dv (holomorphic in Re s < ½+δ).
```
Then for `Re s > ½`: `L_P(s) := ∫_0^∞P(T)e^{−2sT}dT = c(s)S_τ(s) − R(s)`. Moreover `c(s) = ½Ψ(−is)`, so the zeros of `c`
in `−(½+δ) < Re s`, `s ≠ 0`, are exactly the points `s = −iz` with `Φ₁(z) = 0`.

*Proof.* Fix `n ≥ 2`. Since `w_T(log n) = φ̃(log n − 2T)` with `φ̃ = 1` on `(−∞,0]`, `= φ` on `(0,∞)`, the substitution
`v = log n − 2T` gives, for `Re s > 0`,
```
∫_0^∞ w_T(log n)e^{−2sT}dT = ½n^{−s}∫_{−∞}^{log n}φ̃(v)e^{sv}dv = ½n^{−s}[1/s + ∫_0^{log n}φ(v)e^{sv}dv]
  = n^{−s}c(s) − ½n^{−s}∫_{log n}^∞φ(v)e^{sv}dv .
```
Termwise integration is justified for `Re s = σ > ½`: `Σ_n v_n∫_0^∞|w_T(log n)|e^{−2σT}dT ≤ Σ_n v_n·½n^{−σ}[1/σ + Φ_L^{|φ|}(σ)]`
and `Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−½−σ} < ∞`. The tail term: `|n^{−s}∫_{log n}^∞φ(v)e^{sv}dv| ≤ N_δ n^{−σ}·n^{σ−½−δ}/(½+δ−σ) = N_δ n^{−½−δ}/(½+δ−σ)`,
so `R` converges absolutely and locally uniformly on `Re s < ½ + δ` (with `Σ 2Λ(n)n^{−1−δ} < ∞`), hence is holomorphic
there. The Dirichlet series identity for `S_τ`: `v_n n^{−s}cos(τ log n) = Λ(n)n^{−½−s}(n^{−iτ} + n^{iτ})` and
`−ζ′/ζ(w) = ΣΛ(n)n^{−w}` for `Re w > 1`. Finally `Φ_L(s) = Φ(−is)` with `Φ(x) := ∫_0^∞φe^{ixv}dv`; B4's integration by
parts `Φ(z) = i/z + iΦ₁(z)/z` gives `Φ_L(s) = −1/s − Φ₁(−is)/s`, so `c(s) = −Φ₁(−is)/(2s) = ½Ψ(−is)`. ∎

**Theorem L (PROVED; exact identity, complete form).** Let `φ` be admissible and `τ ∈ ℝ`. Then
```
L(s) = L_A(s) − c(s)S_τ(s) + R(s) + L_Π(s) (Re s > ½), (L)
```
and the right-hand side continues `L` meromorphically to the half-plane `Re s > −½`. In that half-plane:
1. **(pole/polar cancellation, exact)** the poles of `−c S_τ` at `s = ½ ∓ iτ` (from the pole of ζ at `1`) cancel exactly
  against the poles of `L_Π`; `L` is holomorphic at `s = ½ ∓ iτ`;
2. **(the mean is the critical-line functional equation, exact)** if `τ ∉ Ord := {γ_ρ real}`, the poles of `L_A` and of
  `−cS_τ` at `s = 0` cancel exactly: `L` is holomorphic at `0`, because
  `½m(τ) + Re ζ′/ζ(½+iτ) = Re ξ′/ξ(½+iτ) = 0`;
3. **(the zeros)** the remaining singularities of `L` in `Re s > −½` are simple poles at the points
  `s_ρ^± := ρ − ½ ∓ iτ` (each non-trivial zero `ρ`, both signs), with
  `Res_{s = s_ρ^±} L = m_ρ·c(s_ρ^±)`, `m_ρ` the multiplicity, and `c(s_ρ^±) = ½Ψ(−is_ρ^±)`; when `s_ρ^− = s_{ρ′}^+`
  (i.e. `ρ′ = ρ − 2iτ`) the two residues add. In particular `L` has a genuine pole at `s_ρ^−` unless
  `Φ₁(γ_ρ − τ) = 0`, equivalently (since `φ` is real) unless `Φ₁(τ − γ̄_ρ) = 0`.

*Proof.* (L) is Lemmas 2.1–2.3 plus linearity. The meromorphic continuation: `L_A` to `Re s > −½` (Lemma 2.1), `L_Π` to
ℂ (Lemma 2.2), `R` to `Re s < ½+δ` (Lemma 2.3), `c` to `Re s < ½+δ` with the single pole at `0`, `S_τ` to ℂ with simple
poles at `½+s±iτ = 1` (residue `+1` each, from `−ζ′/ζ`) and at `½+s±iτ = ρ` (residue `−m_ρ` each), and no others
(trivial zeros of ζ lie at `Re(½+s±iτ) < 0`, i.e. `Re s < −½`).
1. At `s = ½+iτ`: the pole comes from the branch `½+s−iτ = 1` of `S_τ`, residue `+1`, so `−cS_τ` has residue `−c(½+iτ)
  = −½[1/(½+iτ) + Φ_L(½+iτ)]`, which is minus the residue of `L_Π` there (Lemma 2.2). Same at `½ − iτ` by conjugation.
2. At `s = 0`: `L_A` contributes `m(τ)/2`. `S_τ` is holomorphic at `0` iff `½ ± iτ` is not a zero, i.e. `τ ∉ Ord`; then
  `−cS_τ` contributes `−½S_τ(0) = ½[ζ′/ζ(½+iτ) + ζ′/ζ(½−iτ)] = Re ζ′/ζ(½+iτ)` (reflection `ζ′/ζ(s̄) = conj ζ′/ζ(s)`).
  Now `ξ(s) = ½s(s−1)π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ(s)` gives `ξ′/ξ(s) = 1/s + 1/(s−1) − ½log π + ½ψ(s/2) + ζ′/ζ(s)`. At `s = ½+iτ`:
  `Re[1/s + 1/(s−1)] = Re[(½−iτ) + (−½−iτ)]/(¼+τ²) = 0`, and `Re[½ψ(¼+iτ/2)] − ½log π = ½m(τ)` by `eq:m`. So
  `Re ξ′/ξ(½+iτ) = ½m(τ) + Re ζ′/ζ(½+iτ)`. The functional equation `ξ(s) = ξ(1−s)` gives `ξ′/ξ(s) = −ξ′/ξ(1−s)`; at
  `s = ½+iτ`, `1 − s = s̄`, and `ξ′/ξ(s̄) = conj ξ′/ξ(s)` (ξ is real on ℝ). Hence `ξ′/ξ(s) = −conj ξ′/ξ(s)`, i.e.
  `Re ξ′/ξ(½+iτ) = 0` (`ξ(½+iτ) ≠ 0` as `τ ∉ Ord`). The total residue of `L` at `0` is `0`. `L_Π` and `R` are holomorphic
  at `0`.
3. At `s_ρ^− = ρ−½−iτ`: the branch `½+s+iτ = ρ` of `S_τ` has residue `−m_ρ`, so `−cS_τ` has residue `+m_ρ c(s_ρ^−)`
  provided `c` is holomorphic there; `c` is holomorphic on `Re s < ½+δ` except at `0`, and `s_ρ^− = 0` only if `τ ∈ Ord`
  (excluded in 2.). Since `ρ − ½ = iγ_ρ`, `−is_ρ^− = −i(iγ_ρ − iτ) = γ_ρ − τ`, and `c(s) = ½Ψ(−is)` vanishes at `s ≠ 0`
  iff `Φ₁(−is) = 0`; so the pole at `s_ρ^−` is genuine iff `Φ₁(γ_ρ − τ) ≠ 0`. As `φ` is real, `Φ₁(−z̄) = conj Φ₁(z)`, so
  this is `Φ₁(τ − γ̄_ρ) ≠ 0`; the zero set is symmetric under `ρ ↦ ρ̄`, so the condition over all `ρ` reads
  `Φ₁(τ − γ_ρ) ≠ 0` for all `ρ` (Definition 3.1). The same computation at `s_ρ^+` uses the branch `½+s−iτ = ρ`. If two poles coincide,
  `s_ρ^− = s_{ρ′}^+`, both residues are positive multiples (`m_ρ`, `m_{ρ′}`) of the same number `c(s_ρ^−)`, so they add
  and do not cancel. No other term of (L) has a pole at `s_ρ^±` (`Re s_ρ^± ∈ (−½, ½)`, and `L_A`, `R`, `L_Π` are
  holomorphic there except `L_A` at `0`, covered by 2.). ∎

**Remark 2.4 (what Theorem L is).** It is the Mellin transform, in the window length `x = e^{2T}`, of the prime side of
the explicit formula on the modulated flat-top test `w_T(u)cos(τu)`. Points 1 and 2 are the two "cancellations of
record" seen in the `T`-variable: the polar/pole cancellation (`lem:K`, `thm:Z`) and the archimedean/prime cancellation
on the critical line, which is Riemann's functional equation in its real-part form `Re ξ′/ξ(½+iτ) = 0`. This is the
identity that G2_B2_R2 Prop Ψ4 (`K(s)ζ(½+s) = −ζ′(½+s)`) and G2_B4_R2 Lemma 3.2 (the flat top cancels `2sin(2Tx)/x`) both
express; here it appears as the vanishing of the Bohr mean of `G_τ` in `T`, computed **on the prime side and
unconditionally**. Point 3 is the only ℕ-specific content beyond the functional equation, and it is the zero set.

## 3. Theorem T: the growth rate in `T` is `2(Θ − ½)`

**Definition 3.1 (exceptional set).** `E_φ := Ord ∪ {τ ∈ ℝ : Φ₁(τ − γ_ρ) = 0 for some non-trivial zero ρ}`.

**Lemma 3.2 (PROVED).** `E_φ` is countable.

*Proof.* `Ord` is countable. For each `ρ`, `z ↦ Φ₁(z)` is holomorphic on `Im z > −(½+δ)`, which contains the horizontal
line `Im z = Im(τ − γ_ρ) = β_ρ − ½ ∈ (−½, ½)`; `Φ₁ ≢ 0` (`Φ₁(0) = −1`), so its zeros on that line are isolated, hence
countable. A countable union of countable sets. ∎

**Theorem T (PROVED; complete form).** Let `φ` be an admissible profile and `τ ∉ E_φ`. Define the growth exponent
`κ_τ := inf{a ∈ ℝ : G_τ(T) = O(e^{2aT}) as T → ∞}`. Then
```
κ_τ = Θ − ½ .
```
Consequently the following are equivalent:
(a) RH;
(b) `G_τ(T) = O(T^A)` for some `A` (polynomial growth in the window), or merely `G_τ(T) = O(e^{2aT})` for every `a > 0`;
(c) `sup_{T ≥ 1}|G_τ(T)| < ∞`;
(d) `T ↦ G_τ(T)` is Bohr almost periodic on `[1,∞)`.
If any of these holds for **one** `τ ∉ E_φ`, all of them hold for **every** `τ ∉ E_φ`.

*Proof.* **Upper bound `κ_τ ≤ Θ − ½`.** By Lemma Z′ (unconditional), `G_τ(T) = Σ_ρ ŵ_T(τ − γ_ρ)`. For each `ρ`,
`|Im(τ − γ_ρ)| = |β_ρ − ½| ≤ Θ − ½ < ½`, so Lemma 3.2 of B4 applies in the strip `|Im z| ≤ ½ + δ/2`: for
`|τ − γ_ρ| ≥ 1`, `|ŵ_T(τ−γ_ρ)| ≤ CN_δ e^{2T(Θ−½)}|τ − γ_ρ|^{−2}`; the finitely many `ρ` with `|τ − γ_ρ| < 1` contribute
`O_τ(e^{2T(Θ−½)})` trivially (`|ŵ_T(z)| ≤ ∫|w_T|e^{|Im z||u|}du ≤ Ce^{2T|Im z|}`). Since `Σ_ρ(1 + |γ_ρ|)^{−2} < ∞`,
`G_τ(T) = O_τ(e^{2(Θ−½)T})`.

**Lower bound `κ_τ ≥ Θ − ½`.** Suppose `G_τ(T) = O(e^{2aT})` for some `a < Θ − ½`. Then `∫_0^∞G_τ(T)e^{−2sT}dT`
converges absolutely and is holomorphic in `Re s > a`; by uniqueness of analytic continuation it coincides with the
meromorphic function `L` of Theorem L on `Re s > a`, so `L` has no pole in `Re s > a`. Pick a zero `ρ₀` with
`β₀ := Re ρ₀ > ½ + a` (exists by definition of `Θ`). Then `s₀ := ρ₀ − ½ − iτ` has `Re s₀ = β₀ − ½ > a`, and by Theorem L(3)
`L` has a genuine pole at `s₀`, because `τ ∉ E_φ` gives `Φ₁(τ − γ_{ρ₀}) ≠ 0`, and coincident poles add. Contradiction.

**Equivalences.** (a) ⟹ (c),(d): under RH, B4 Theorem R Step 1 (from Lemma Z′ and Lemma 3.2): `G_τ(T) =
Σ_γ m_γ 2Re[Ψ(τ−γ)e^{2iT(τ−γ)}]` converges absolutely and uniformly in `T` (`τ ∉ Ord`, `|Ψ(x)| ≤ CN_δ x^{−2}`), so `G_τ`
is a uniform limit of trigonometric polynomials in `T`: Bohr almost periodic and bounded. (d) ⟹ (c) ⟹ (b) trivial.
(b) ⟹ (a): (b) gives `κ_τ ≤ 0`, so `Θ − ½ ≤ 0`, i.e. `Θ = ½`. The last sentence: (a) does not depend on `τ`. ∎

**Corollary T1 (PROVED).** For `τ ∉ E_φ`, `limsup_{T→∞} (log|G_τ(T)|)/(2T) = Θ − ½`. The certificate value of a fixed
profile, as a function of the window, has exactly the growth of `(ψ(x) − x)/√x` in `x = e^{2T}` (CITED for the
classical statement: von Koch 1901 for RH ⟹ `O(x^{½}log²x)`; Ingham, *The Distribution of Prime Numbers*, Ch. IV,
Thm 30 for `ψ(x) − x = O(x^Θ log²x)` and the converse via Landau's theorem; theorem number from memory).

**Corollary T2 (PROVED; the Bohr mean on the prime side).** If `G_τ` is bounded (equivalently under RH) and `τ ∉ Ord`,
then its Bohr mean `M(G_τ) := lim_{U→∞}(1/U)∫_0^U G_τ(T)dT` exists and equals `0`; on the prime side this is
`m(τ) + 2Re ζ′/ζ(½+iτ) = 2Re ξ′/ξ(½+iτ) = 0`.

*Proof.* Under boundedness, RH holds (Theorem T), so `G_τ` is Bohr a.p. and its mean exists; the Abelian theorem for
Laplace transforms then gives `M(G_τ) = lim_{s→0+}2sL(s)`. By Theorem L(2), `L` is holomorphic at `0`, so the limit is
`0`. The displayed identity is the computation of the residue in Theorem L(2). ∎

**Remark 3.3 (consistency with B4).** Theorem R of G2_B4_R2 says: under RH, `G_τ` is a mean-zero Bohr a.p. function of
`T`, hence changes sign for arbitrarily large `T`. Theorem T and Corollary T2 read the same two facts on the prime
side: *mean zero* is `Re ξ′/ξ(½+iτ) = 0`, the functional equation on the critical line, unconditional and independent
of the Λ-values beyond the FE; *almost periodicity* is `Θ = ½`, i.e. RH. B4's Remark R3 (zero-tuning: `Φ₁(γ) = 0` at
every ordinate is necessary for `G_0 ≥ 0`) is Theorem L(3) at `τ = 0`: the poles of `L` on `Re s = 0` are cancelled
exactly when `Φ₁(−γ_ρ) = 0` for every `ρ`. The two memos are two sides of one identity; neither produces a sign.

## 4. Why the `T`-axis of the angle cannot produce the "for all `T`" sign

**Proposition 4.1 (PROVED; the angle sees growth, not sign).** Let `a: ℕ_{≥2} → ℝ` with `Σ|a(n)|n^{−1−ε} < ∞` for all
`ε > 0` and let `G^a_τ` be defined by `eq:fw` with `v_n` replaced by `v_n + a(n)/√n`-type data (any finite or absolutely
convergent perturbation `Λ ↦ Λ + a` of the comb, e.g. the `thm:finmod` perturbation `εδ_{n₀}`). Then
`L^a(s) = L(s) − c(s)·A_τ(s) + R^a(s)`, where `A_τ(s) := Σ_n (2a(n)/√n)cos(τ log n)n^{−s}` is holomorphic in `Re s > ½ + ε`
for every `ε > 0`, i.e. in `Re s > ½`, and entire when `a` has finite support. Hence, for finitely supported `a`
(`thm:finmod`, `rem:resweights`): `κ^a_τ = κ_τ` for all `τ ∉ E_φ`, i.e. **the growth exponent of the certificate value in
`T` is invariant under every finite modification of the comb**, while the sign of `G^a_τ(T)` at a given window `T`
changes by `−(2a(n₀)/√n₀)cos(τ log n₀)w_T(log n₀)`, which by `rem:resweights` is enough to flip `λ^W_T` when
`|a(n₀)| ≈ e^{−14.4T}`.

*Proof.* Lemma 2.3 applied to the perturbation; a finite Dirichlet polynomial is entire; `c(s)A_τ(s)` has poles only at
`s = 0` (from `c`), which lies on `Re s = 0` and does not affect the exponent `κ` (it is a bounded contribution, the
constant `−½A_τ(0)` in the mean). ∎

**Reading (PROVED content, HEURISTIC phrasing).** The almost-periodic content of the `T`-axis is a statement about
singularities of the Laplace transform, hence about exponential growth rates. Growth rates are blind to entire
perturbations; the "for all `T`" sign is not (O3). So the `T`-axis of the angle detects `Θ` (RH as `Θ = ½`) and is
structurally unable to detect a sign at any finite `T`. This is the exact `T`-axis twin of round 2's Theorem K: there
the Bohr topology in `τ` was blind to the Γ-matching; here the Laplace/Bohr analysis in `T` is blind to finite
modifications and sees only the abscissa `Θ`. In both cases what remains after the blind part is removed is the zero
set itself.

**Corollary 4.2 (PROVED; the sign along the `T`-hull).** Assume RH and `τ ∉ E_φ`. The Bohr hull of `G_τ` in `T`
(sup-norm closure of its translates) consists of the functions `G^θ_τ(T) = Σ_γ m_γ 2Re[Ψ(τ−γ)e^{iθ_γ}e^{2iT(τ−γ)}]`,
`θ ∈ (ℝ/2πℤ)^{Ord}` in the closure of the Kronecker line `θ_γ = 2t(τ−γ)`; every element has mean zero and, unless
identically zero, takes negative values. A "for all `T ≥ T₀`" sign for `G_τ` is therefore impossible (B4 Thm R), and a
fortiori no hull-type input in `T` (mean value, ergodic average, value distribution) can certify one.

*Proof.* Standard Bohr theory (as in Theorem H of round 2, now in the variable `T`): the hull of a Bohr a.p. function is
the closure of its translates, each translate is `G_τ(· + t)`, which multiplies the coefficient of `e^{2iT(τ−γ)}` by
`e^{2it(τ−γ)}`; uniform limits preserve the mean (`0`) and the spectrum. A nonneg a.p. function of mean zero is `≡ 0`. ∎

## 7. Weakest step

* **The exceptional set `E_φ`.** Theorem T needs `Φ₁(τ − γ_ρ) ≠ 0` for every `ρ`, so that the pole at `s_ρ^−` is genuine.
  For a fixed `τ` this can fail for a specific profile (B4's zero-tuning); Lemma 3.2 only shows the bad `τ` form a
  countable set. The equivalences are therefore "for all `τ` outside a countable set", not for each `τ`. This is
  harmless for the conclusions (RH is `τ`-free) but should be kept in the statement.
* **Reliance on record lemmas.** The upper bound in Theorem T and (a) ⟹ (d) use Lemma Z′ and Lemma 3.2 of G2_B4_R2 as
  PROVED (five panelists verified them). The lower bound and Theorem L are self-contained. If Lemma Z′ were withdrawn,
  the lower bound `κ_τ ≥ Θ − ½` and hence "(b) ⟹ RH" would still stand (they use only the prime side and Theorem L);
  only the upper bound and the almost-periodicity under RH would need another proof.
* **The residue bookkeeping in Theorem L(3).** `Res S_τ = −m_ρ` at a zero, hence `Res(−cS_τ) = +m_ρc`; only the
  non-vanishing of `c(s_ρ^±)` and the equal signs of coincident residues are used. A reader should check this once,
  together with the reflection `Φ₁(−z̄) = conj Φ₁(z)` that converts `Φ₁(γ_ρ − τ) ≠ 0` into the form of Definition 3.1.
* **Citations from memory.** Ingham Ch. IV Thm 30 (theorem number unverified in the primary text); von Koch 1901. Neither
  is load-bearing: Theorem T is proved here directly.
* **The reading of §4** ("the `T`-axis sees growth, not sign") is a PROVED statement about this family of transforms
  (Prop. 4.1); the claim that *every* almost-periodic argument in `T` has this blindness is HEURISTIC, supported by the
  fact that almost periodicity in `T` is, by Theorem T, a growth statement.
