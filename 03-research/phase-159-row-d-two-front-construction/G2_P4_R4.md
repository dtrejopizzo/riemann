### 1.2 Literature: what the graded converse is

- **Classical content (CITED).**
  - Landau's theorem on Laplace integrals of one-signed functions.
  - von Koch (1901): RH ⟺ `ψ(x) = x + O(x^{½+ε})`.
  - Landau and Ingham: `ψ(x) − x = Ω_±(x^{Θ−ε})` (Ingham, *Distribution of Prime Numbers*, Ch. V).

  B2 Lemma T(d) and B3 Lemma 2 make the dictionary explicit. The off-diagonal value of the form on two translated bumps is the smoothed Chebyshev error at `e^x`, with the main term killed by the moment conditions.

  Theorem Q is therefore "graded von Koch" plus one Cauchy–Schwarz (or one two-bump identity) that turns a *spectral* lower bound into a *pointwise* bound on the kernel. That Cauchy–Schwarz step is the Kreĭn–Bochner inequality `|f(x)| ≤ f(0)` for positive-definite functions on an interval, shifted by `Mδ`. It is classical.
- **Li-coefficient analogue: verified this round in the primary text.** Lagarias, *Li coefficients for automorphic L-functions*, Ann. Inst. Fourier 57 (2007) 1689–1740, p. 1695 of the numdam PDF: "If the Riemann hypothesis does not hold … `λ_n(√n, π)` will sometimes be very large, of size exponential in `n`. This fact was already observed for the Riemann zeta function in [Bombieri–Lagarias, JNT 77 (1999)], Theorem 1(c)." So the phenomenon "an off-line zero forces exponential negativity of the positivity functional" is published for Li coefficients. The Weil-bottom version is its window analogue.
- **Weil-form literature.** Bombieri, *Remarks on Weil's quadratic functional I*, Rend. Lincei 2000 (eudml 252338, abstract): with finitely many off-line zeros, the number of negative eigenvalues equals half their number. That is a count, not a size.
  - I found no published statement of the rate law for the bottoms of the truncated Weil form.
  - Kim et al., arXiv:2607.24830 (2026, abstract read) describe "bounded residual growth ↔ all zeros on the line, an injected off-line zero causes exponential blow-up" for a discretised Suzuki operator. This is numerical only, and the authors state that nothing is proved.
  - Suzuki, arXiv:2206.03682 (Thm 1.6, bounded screw function ⟺ RH; read in round 3) is the ungraded sibling.
- **Corpus.**
  - G1_B8_R2 Theorem G (l.19–31) already proves the *two-sided exact* law `limsup (2t)^{−1}log⁺N_t = Θ* − ½` for Suzuki's window Hankel norms, and records the dictionary to Landau's `limsup log|ψ − x|/log x = Θ*` as EQUIV family.
  - The Weil-bottom lower half is new to the corpus *as a statement about `λ^A_T`, `λ^W_T`*.
  - The phenomenon itself (the "EQUIV family" under the Landau dictionary) is not new.

**Novelty verdict.** A correct and useful record lemma, of folklore depth. B1 §1 ("folklore-level; no literature novelty claimed") and B3 §0 ("classical in flavour") assess this accurately. B5 §0 ("new to the corpus", priority not claimed) is acceptable. The claim in B5 §7 that the Gram-inequality bridge is "new to the corpus" is literally true only up to this round's five concurrent memos and G1_B8_R2's Schur-test lower half.

### 1.3 Theorem U (B2) and the Carleson condition

The device (subharmonic mean over discs, plus Plancherel on horizontal lines, bounding `Σ_k|Φ(λ_k)|²` by `‖F‖²`) is the **Plancherel–Pólya inequality**. The hypothesis (BD) is exactly the classical **Carleson-measure condition for Paley–Wiener spaces**: a measure `μ` in a horizontal strip is Carleson for `PW` iff `sup_x μ(unit square at x) < ∞`.
- Sources: Plancherel–Pólya (1937); R. M. Young, *An Introduction to Nonharmonic Fourier Series*, Ch. 2 §3 and Ch. 4; Seip, *Interpolation and Sampling in Spaces of Analytic Functions* (AMS ULS 33), Ch. 2.
- These are CITED from memory and were not re-read this round, but the inequality is textbook.

What follows from this:
1. Theorem U is correct, and its method is classical. Its content is the observation that the off-line zeros enter the Weil bottom exactly as a Paley–Wiener Carleson measure. The explicit `T`-dependence (`T²` from the radius `1/(2T)`) is B2's own bookkeeping and is fine.
2. The obstruction recorded as OPEN in B2 Rem U-b, B3 Q2 and B5 Q4 is *precisely* the failure of the Carleson condition. The on-line zero measure has local mass `≍ log t` (Riemann–von Mangoldt), so it is not a PW-Carleson measure. An unbounded local off-line count would not be one either. The literature confirms that without (BD) this route to the upper half cannot work.
3. B1 Prop Q2 is the special case of U with finitely many off-line zeros. B2's claim to "the first upper bound on the negativity in terms of the zeros" should say "concurrently with B1 Prop Q2", and should cite Plancherel–Pólya for the method.
4. (BD) is OPEN. B2 is right that zero-density theorems do not give it, since they bound averages and not local counts.
