## §2 The FE door: Levinson's operations on window tests (Prop. L1) and the exact FE step (Prop. L2)

For a real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` let `Φ_F(s) = ∫F(t)e^{(s−½)t}dt`. By `thm:Z` (explicit formula, PROVED in the record), the
complete form is
```
W(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ), absolutely convergent, W(F) = W_T(F) for supp F ⊂ I_T.
```
Put `w = ρ − ½`. Then `Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ) = φ(w)φ(−w)` with `φ(w) := Φ_F(½+w)`, an entire function of exponential type,
real on the real axis (`F` real).

**Proposition L1 (the three Levinson operations on tests; PROVED).**

(i) *Mollifier (translations).* This is round-1 Prop. W-M: `F_M = Σa_n n^{−½}F(·+log n)` has `Φ_{F_M} = MΦ_F`, and
`W(F_M) = Σ_ρ M(ρ)M(1−ρ)Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`. The support moves to `I_{T+½log N}` after centring.

(ii) *FE-symmetric mollifier.* Let `M^∨(s) := M(1−s)`, realised by `F ↦ Σa_n n^{−½}F(·−log n)`. For
`N := M + M^∨`, `N(1−s) = N(s)`, and
```
W(F_N) = Σ_ρ N(ρ)² Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ).
```
On the line `N(½+iγ) = 2Re M(½+iγ)` is real, and `F_N` is the image of `F` under an operator that commutes with the
reflection `t ↦ −t`.

(iii) *Levinson's differential combination.* For a real polynomial `P`, `Φ_{P(∂)F}(s) = P(−(s−½))Φ_F(s)`, and
`supp P(∂)F ⊂ supp F`. So
```
W(P(∂)F) = Σ_ρ P(−w)P(w) φ(w)φ(−w).
```
In particular `W((1+λ∂)F) = W(F) + λ²W(F′)` for real `λ`: the cross term vanishes.

(iv) *Consequence.* Each of (i)–(iii) is the Weil form of a single window, evaluated on the image of a linear map of
the test space into a test space of a (possibly larger) window, or a positive combination of such evaluations. Hence
```
inf{W(LF)/‖LF‖² : F} ≥ λ^W_{T′} for every such L with range in L²(I_{T′}),
```
and no statement of the form "`W(LF) ≥ 0` for all `F`" is stronger than `λ^W_{T′} ≥ 0`. At fixed `T′`, the union over
`L` of the ranges contains `C_c^∞(I_{T′})` (take `L = id`), so positivity on all of them for all `T′` is exactly E1.

*Proof.*
* (i) is round 1.
* (ii): `Φ_{F(·−log n)}(s) = n^{s−½}Φ_F(s)`, so `Φ_{F_{M^∨}} = n^{−½}n^{s−½}` summed with `a_n`, which is
  `M(1−s)Φ_F`. Then `N(1−s) = M(1−s) + M(s) = N(s)`, so `N(ρ)N(1−ρ) = N(ρ)²`. On the line,
  `M(½−iγ) = conj M(½+iγ)` because the `a_n` are real.
* (iii): integrate by parts, `∫F′e^{(s−½)t} = −(s−½)Φ_F`. The cross term of `(1+λ∂)F` is `λ·Σ_ρ [(−w) + w]φ(w)φ(−w)`
  after symmetrising the pair `{ρ, 1−ρ}` (`w ↦ −w`), which is `0`. The absolute convergence of the rearranged sum
  follows from that of `W(F)` and `W(F′)`, since `|w|·|φ(w)φ(−w)| ≤ ½(|φ(w)φ(−w)| + |w|²|φ(w)φ(−w)|)`.
* (iv) is the variational principle for `λ^W_{T′}` on `L²(I_{T′})`. ∎

**Reading.** Levinson's gain in the density problem comes from the argument principle for `ζ + λζ′` (with the FE
making `ζ(½+it)` and `ζ(½−it)` equal in modulus), not from positivity. In the window problem the same operations
produce only restrictions of the form. Mollification can **hide** off-line zeros (round 1, Rem. W-M′: `M(ρ₀) = 0` kills
the quadruple). The Levinson multiplier `P(−w)P(w)` can hide them too (take `P(w₀) = 0` with `w₀ = ρ₀ − ½`). Neither
certifies. **No Levinson-type multiplier is a positivity amplifier: PROVED (L1(iv)).**

**Proposition L2 (the exact FE step of Levinson's method is Speiser; CITED, EQUIVALENT).**
* Levinson's method (Levinson 1974; Conrey 1989) uses the FE at one load-bearing line: on `σ = ½`,
  `|ζ(½+it)| = |ζ(½−it)|`. This turns zeros of `ζ` on the line into zeros of `Re(e^{iθ(t)}(ζ+λζ′)(½+it))`, and the
  argument principle then counts the zeros of `ζ + λζ′` in `σ < ½` (Littlewood's lemma on `σ = ½ − R/L`, with a
  mollifier).
* The 100 % form of the counting step, with the density loss removed, is Speiser's theorem (Math. Ann. 110 (1934)):
  RH ⟺ `ζ′ ≠ 0` on `0 < σ < ½`. Levinson–Montgomery (Acta Math. 133 (1974)) gives `N₁⁻(T) = N⁻(T) + O(log T)`.
* Both are **CITED** and **EQUIVALENT** (R157_002 row 49, NG-A5 family).
* The density form is **EXCLUDED** by O2 (round-1 Prop. M4).
* *What passes the twist test:* Speiser's argument uses the FE, so it is inapplicable to `L_2` and to `ζ·E`. It is
  correct for `Ξ_a`, where the zeros are off the line and the argument detects them.
* *What fails:* it is a statement about zeros (detection side, §3). It is never an inequality on a window form.

## §3 Theorem H — harmonic rigidity (closes O-B10.1)

**Definition H0 (explicit-formula zero functional).**
* Let `S = {w : |Re w| < ½}`.
* An *admissible kernel* is a function `G` holomorphic on a neighbourhood of `S̄`, even (`G(−w) = G(w)`), and real on
  the real axis (`G(w̄) = conj G(w)`), such that `Σ_ρ |G(ρ−½)| < ∞`.
* The associated functional of the zero multiset `Z` is `L_G(Z) := Σ_ρ m_ρ G(ρ − ½)`.
* This covers every Weil test, `G(w) = φ(w)φ(−w)` with `φ = Φ_F(½+·)` and `F` real, compactly supported (`thm:Z`). It
  also covers every even test `ĝ` of the Guinand–Weil explicit formula with enough decay, and every finite linear
  combination of these.
* The orbit of a zero `ρ = ½ + a + iγ` is `{±(a+iγ), ±(a−iγ)}`.
  * Off the line (`a ≠ 0`), the orbit contributes `4u(a,γ)`, where `u := Re G` in the coordinates `w = a+iγ`.
  * On the line (`a = 0`), it contributes `2u(0,γ) = 2G(iγ)`, which is real.

**Theorem H (PROVED).** Let `G` be admissible, and put `u(a,γ) = Re G(a+iγ)`.

(i) `u` is harmonic on `S`, and even in `a` and in `γ` separately.

(ii) *(No termwise detector.)* Suppose `u ≥ 0` on a connected open set `U ⊂ S` that meets the line `a = 0`, and
`u(0,γ) = 0` for the points `(0,γ)` of `U`. Then `G ≡ 0`.

(iii) *(Nodal penalty, Lemma H2.)* Let `G = φ(w)φ(−w)` with `φ` entire and real on the real axis. Suppose `φ(iγ₀) = 0`
for some real `γ₀`, with a zero of exact order `k ≥ 1`. Then for all real `a ≠ 0` small enough,
```
u(a, γ₀) = (−1)^k |c_k|² a^{2k} (1 + O(a)), c_k := φ^{(k)}(iγ₀)/k! ≠ 0.
```
In particular, at every simple on-line node of a Weil test, the off-line kernel is **strictly negative** at the same
height:
```
u(a,γ₀) = −|φ′(iγ₀)|² a² + O(a³) < 0.
```

(iv) *(Jensen is not explicit-formula.)* The round-1 Jensen detector has orbit kernel `ℓ*(a,γ) = |ℓ(a,γ)|`, where
`ℓ(a,γ) := log|(w+½)/(w−½)|`. It is continuous, `≥ 0`, `= 0` exactly on `a = 0`, and **strictly subharmonic across the
line**: its distributional Laplacian is `2|∂_aℓ(0,γ)| dγ ⊗ δ_{a=0} > 0`. By (ii) it is not `Re G` for any admissible
`G`. More strongly, it is not `Re G` on any neighbourhood of any point of the line. The same holds for every termwise
detector, that is, every kernel that is `≥ 0`, `= 0` on the line, and not `≡ 0` near the line.

*Proof.*
* (i): `u` is the real part of a holomorphic function. Evenness: `G(−w) = G(w)` gives `u(−a,−γ) = u(a,γ)`, and
  `G(w̄) = conj G(w)` gives `u(a,−γ) = u(a,γ)`. Combining the two, `u(−a,γ) = u(a,γ)`.
* (ii): At a point `p = (0,γ) ∈ U`, `u(p) = 0 = min_U u`. By the strong minimum principle for harmonic functions,
  `u ≡ 0` on `U`. Then `G` is holomorphic with vanishing real part on `U`, so `G ≡ ic` on `U` for a real constant `c`.
  By the identity theorem, `G ≡ ic` on `S`. Since `G` is real on the real axis (which meets `S`), `c = 0`.
* (iii): Put `ψ(w) := φ(−w)`. Because `φ` is real on the real axis, `φ(z̄) = conj φ(z)`, and so
  `ψ(a+iγ₀) = φ(−a−iγ₀) = conj φ(−a+iγ₀)`. Near `iγ₀`, `φ(iγ₀+h) = c_k h^k(1+O(h))`. Hence
  * `φ(a+iγ₀) = c_k a^k(1+O(a))`;
  * `φ(−a+iγ₀) = c_k(−a)^k(1+O(a))`.

  Therefore `G(a+iγ₀) = φ(a+iγ₀)·conj φ(−a+iγ₀) = (−1)^k|c_k|²a^{2k}(1+O(a))`. This is real to leading order, so
  `u(a,γ₀) = Re G = (−1)^k|c_k|²a^{2k}(1+O(a))`.
* (iv):
  * `ℓ = log|w+½| − log|w−½|` is harmonic on `S`, since the singularities `±½` lie on `∂S`.
  * `ℓ` is odd in `a`: `|−ā+½| = |w̄ − ½|`, so `ℓ(−a,γ) = −ℓ(a,γ)`. It is positive for `a > 0`.
  * `∂_aℓ(0,γ) = Re[1/(iγ+½) − 1/(iγ−½)] = 1/(¼+γ²) > 0`.
  * `|ℓ|` is continuous, harmonic off the line, and `C¹` from each side. Its normal derivative jumps by
  `2∂_aℓ(0,γ) = 2/(¼+γ²)`. The Green formula gives `Δ|ℓ| = 2/(¼+γ²)·dγ ⊗ δ_{a=0}`, a positive measure, so `|ℓ|` is
  subharmonic and not harmonic near any point of the line.
  * If `|ℓ| = Re G` near a point of the line, it would be harmonic there, which is a contradiction.
  * The general detector statement is (ii) applied locally. ∎

**Corollary H3 (what Theorem H says about the "100 random zeros"; PROVED).**
* A termwise detector certifies any subset of zeros by itself: each on-line zero contributes `0`. So the Jensen
  functional is additive over random subsets, and a finite set of on-line zeros "certifies" itself.
* By Theorem H, no functional with that property is linear in the zero measure with a holomorphic (explicit-formula)
  kernel.
* Every Weil functional is harmonic. Its off-line kernel therefore inherits the on-line structure (Lemma H2): where
  the test is blind on the line, it is negative off the line. The sign is carried by **cancellation between on-line
  heights**. This is the off-line mechanism behind `lem:Rrig` / `cor:nontermwise` and `thm:finZ`.
* The intuition "certify from a random subset" is realisable only on the detection side, and there the prime
  side is not a linear explicit formula but a mean square `‖ζM‖²` or `∫log|ζ|dμ`, which is nonlinear in `ζ`.

**Remark H4 (why Lemma H2 is the "Weil detects" mechanism; HEURISTIC as interpretation, the formula is PROVED).**
* Weil's converse direction (a negative value forced by an off-line zero `ρ₀ = ½+a₀+iγ₀`) picks tests whose `φ` is
  small at the on-line heights near `γ₀` and not small at `w₀`.
* H2 quantifies the local version: a simple on-line node at `γ₀` gives the off-line orbit at height `γ₀` the weight
  `−|φ′(iγ₀)|²a₀² + O(a₀³)`.
* The window restriction `supp F ⊂ I_T` bounds the exponential type of `φ` by `T`. So a node can be placed at `γ₀`,
  but by Jensen's formula for `φ` it cannot be isolated from the on-line mass on a scale finer than `≈ 1/T`.
* This is the rigorous content behind "the margin decays with `T`" (`thm:margin`) as seen from the zero side. As an
  estimate it is not proved here, and it is not needed.

## §4 Theorem N± — the mollifier closure passes no twist test (falsifiers 4 and 5)

**Definition.** `𝒞_E^± := { real combs a : ζ_a = ζ·exp(D_a) with D_a ∈ A_½ }`. There is no sign condition. The round-1
class `𝒞_E` required `a ≥ 0`. That condition was never used in the proof, and it is dropped here.

**Lemma N0 (membership; PROVED).**
* (a) Deletion, `a = Λ1_{n≥N}`, and amplification, `a = Λ + c1_{n=n₀}` (any real `c`), lie in `𝒞_E^±` (round 1).
* (b) `a = Λχ_2`, with `χ_2(2^k) = (−1)^k` and `χ_2 = 1` on odd prime powers, lies in `𝒞_E^±`, and `ζ_a = L_2`.
* (c) More generally, `a = Λ·ε` lies in `𝒞_E^±` for every finite set of primes `P₀` and every choice of complex
  unimodular `ε_p` with `ε(p^k) = ε_p^k` real, and `ε = 1` off `P₀`. Here `ζ_a = ζ·Π_{p∈P₀}(1−p^{−s})/(1−ε_p p^{−s})`.

*Proof.* (b) is the case `P₀ = {2}`, `ε_2 = −1` of (c). For (c), the comb difference is
`D_a = Σ_{p∈P₀}Σ_k (ε_p^k − 1)p^{−ks}/k`, with `‖D_a‖ ≤ Σ_{p∈P₀}Σ_k 2p^{−k/2}/k < ∞`. Then
`exp(D_a) = Π_{p∈P₀}(1−p^{−s})/(1−ε_p p^{−s})`, by `log(1−x)^{−1} = Σx^k/k` for `|x| ≤ p^{−½} < 1`. For `ε_2 = −1` this
is `(1−2^{−s})/(1+2^{−s})`. The coefficient of `2^{−ks}/k` in `log L_2` is `1 − 1 + (−1)^k = (−1)^k`, so
`a(2^k) = (−1)^k log 2 = Λ(2^k)χ_2(2^k)`. ∎

**Theorem N± (PROVED).** For every `a ∈ 𝒞_E^±`, `ζ_a𝒟₀ ⊂ H²(μ)` and `[ζ_a𝒟₀] = [ζ𝒟₀]` in `H²(μ)`. Consequently the
following coincide for all `a ∈ 𝒞_E^±`:
* `d_∞(a) = dist(1, [ζ_a𝒟₀])`;
* `inf Q_a`;
* `S_{ζ_a} = S_ζ`, the Jensen mass of the zeros in `Ω`;
* the whole exact mollifier/NB/BSY datum of round-1 §2.

*Proof.* Round-1 Theorem N, verbatim. The proof uses only:
* `E^{±1} = exp(±D_a) ∈ A_½`, bounded analytic and continuous on `Re s ≥ ½`;
* uniform approximation of `E^{±1}M ∈ A_½` (which vanishes at `1`) by elements of `𝒟₀`;
* `‖ζ‖_{L²(μ)} < ∞`, from `∫_0^X|ζ(½+it)|²dt ≪ X log X` and partial summation against `(¼+t²)^{−1}`;
* for the reverse inclusion, `‖ζ_a‖_{L²(μ)} ≤ ‖E‖_∞‖ζ‖_{L²(μ)}`.

No sign of `a` is used. `E` has no zeros in `Re s ≥ ½`, where `D_a` converges absolutely, so `ζ_a` and `ζ` have the
same zeros in `Ω`. For `L_2`, the extra zeros `s ∈ 2πiℤ/log 2` and the poles `s ∈ iπ(2ℤ+1)/log 2` lie on `Re s = 0`,
outside `Ω̄`. ∎

**Corollary N±1 (the twist test is failed by the whole mollifier route below the FE line; PROVED, with the citations
shown).**
* Let `𝔐` be any statement or number that depends on the comb only through `[ζ_a𝒟₀]`. Then `𝔐(Λχ_2) = 𝔐(Λ)`.
* *Complete form.* For every `T ∈ [0.38, 0.8]`: `λ^W_T(Λ) ≥ 0` (Zhu 2026, **CITED**, not re-verified), while
  `λ^W_T(Λχ_2) < 0` (G2_B3_R1 Thm B, PROVED and panel-verified 5/5: witness `F = t`, value
  `≤ −7.13·10⁻⁴·‖F‖⁻²` at `0.38`, extended by zero).
* *Primitive form.* For `T ∈ (½log2, log2]`, the amplification comb of Lemma N0(a) plays the same role
  (`thm:finmod`(ii), Paper 42).
* Hence `𝔐` decides no window in these ranges. For `T > 0.8` it decides nothing unless the sign for `Λ` is already
  known, and it is not.
* Unlike round 1, the complete-form clause needs **no amplification of size `c > c_*`**. It uses a comb with exact
  moduli `|a(n)| = Λ(n)` and real coefficients (falsifier 5). The pair `(Λ, Λχ_2)` has the same moduli, the same pole
  and the same zeros in `Ω`, and opposite window signs.

**Corollary N±2 (where the FE is load-bearing, and that it cannot be skipped; PROVED).**
* The only derivation of a window sign from mollifier data is
  ```
  [ζ𝒟₀] ∋ 1 ⟹ S_ζ = 0 (no zeros in Ω) ⟹ (FE) RH ⟹ (Weil) ∀T: λ^W_T ≥ 0.
  ```
* The passage marked "(FE)" reflects the zeros from `Ω` to `{Re s < ½}` and excludes further singularities in
  `Re s ≤ ½`. It is load-bearing, and not only formally so. For `a = Λχ_2`, the first two steps hold verbatim whenever
  they hold for `Λ` (Theorem N±), and the conclusion fails from `T = 0.38` on. The unconditional form of this
  statement is:
  * **either** `S_ζ > 0` (so RH is false),
  * **or** `L_2` is a comb with `S_{L_2} = 0` and `λ^W_T(Λχ_2) < 0` for all `T ≥ 0.38`.
* Either way, "no zeros in `Ω` ⟹ `∀T` sign" is false for combs without FE. The failure for `L_2` comes from its
  zeros and poles on `Re s = 0`, which are off-line relative to `½` and are not mirrored in `Ω`.
* The FE line of the mollifier route is exactly Weil's easy direction. The hard step (cyclicity) sits entirely on
  the comb-blind side.

## §5 Theorem D — the dichotomy, and where 100 % is forced

**Theorem D (PROVED as a conjunction of Theorems H and N± and Props L1–L2, with the record items cited).** Split the
positive-proportion/mollifier toolkit into two sides.

| | detection side (Jensen, NB, BSY, Speiser, mollified mean squares) | Weil side (window forms `A_T`, `W_T`) |

| kernel in the zero position | termwise, `≥ 0`, `= 0` on the line; subharmonic, not harmonic (H(iv)) | harmonic (H(i)); never a termwise detector (H(ii)); nodal penalty (H(iii)); never termwise on the line either (`lem:Rrig`) |

| prime side | nonlinear in `ζ` (`‖ζM‖²`, `∫log|ζ|dμ`) | linear (explicit formula) |
| 100 % statement | cyclicity of `ζ𝒟₀` / Speiser: **EQUIVALENT** | `∀T λ ≥ 0`: E1/E2, **EQUIVALENT** |

The toolkit's operations on window tests (L1) stay on the Weil side and add no positivity. Its 100 % statements stay
on the detection side. The only bridge between the sides is RH together with the FE (N±2).

**The step where 100 % would be forced (answer to the angle).** It is one statement, in two equivalent forms:
* (α) `1 ∈ [ζ𝒟₀]` in `H²(μ)` (Báez-Duarte, **CITED**). In round 1's four-term identity

Both are **EQUIVALENT** to RH.
* (α) is comb-blind on `𝒞_E^±` (N±), so it uses no exact value of `Λ` beyond membership in that class. It therefore
  cannot be the F1 step of a G2 mechanism. If proved for `ζ`, it would also be proved for `L_2`, whose window forms

* (β) uses the FE, so it passes the twist test, but it is a zero-location statement with no window content.

## §7 What is genuinely new (round 2)

1. **Theorem H** (PROVED): explicit-formula zero functionals have harmonic orbit kernels. There is no nonzero
  termwise detector among them, and there is a nodal penalty `u(a,γ₀) = −|φ′(iγ₀)|²a² + O(a³)` at every simple on-line
  node of a Weil test. The Jensen detector is strictly subharmonic across the line. This closes O-B10.1 and is the
  off-line counterpart of `lem:Rrig`.
2. **Theorem N±** (PROVED): the mollifier closure is invariant on `𝒞_E^±`, which contains `L_2` (falsifier 5) and
  every finite-prime unimodular real twist. With B3 Thm B and Zhu, mollifier data cannot see a complete-form sign
  change at any `T ∈ [0.38, 0.8]`, without amplification.
3. **Prop. L1** (PROVED): the Levinson operations (translation mollifiers, FE-symmetric mollifiers, derivative
  combinations `P(∂)`) are restrictions or positive combinations of the window form. `W((1+λ∂)F) = W(F) + λ²W(F′)`.
4. **Cor. N±2** (PROVED, unconditional dichotomy): "no zeros in `Ω` ⟹ `∀T` window sign" fails for a comb with exact
  moduli and no FE, either because RH is false or through `L_2`. So the FE is load-bearing exactly at Weil's easy
  direction.
5. **Theorem D**: the detection/Weil dichotomy table. The 100 % step is (α) or (β), both EQUIVALENT.

No sign is claimed at any window. Row D keeps no label.
