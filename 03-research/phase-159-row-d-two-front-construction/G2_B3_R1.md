## Verdict, stated first

**No Hermitian-square mechanism is obtained, and no sign is claimed at any window.** The round proves a
new obstruction that applies to the whole angle and to more:

* **Theorem A (twist averaging), PROVED.** Let the group `G_T` of completely multiplicative unimodular
  functions supported on the primes below `e^{2T}` act on the window comb by `v_n ↦ χ(n)v_n`. The Haar mean of
  the twisted forms is the archimedean form `A_∞`, exactly. So for **every** `T ≥ 0.4608` (complete form) and
  **every** `T ≥ 0.60` (primitive form), a set of twists of positive Haar measure makes the form negative, and
  `inf_χ λ(T,χ) ≤ λ_{∞,P}(T) → C_0 = −5.37`.
* **Theorem B (the sign flip at 2), PROVED, unconditional, with an explicit witness.** Take the weight function
  `a_2(n) = (−1)^k Λ(n)` for `n = 2^k` and `a_2(n) = Λ(n)` otherwise. It is `−L′/L` for
  `L(s) = ζ(s)(1−2^{−s})/(1+2^{−s})`. It has exactly the moduli `|a_2(n)| = Λ(n)` and real coefficients. `L` has an
  Euler product with unimodular local roots, the pole at `s = 1`, and exactly the zeros of `ζ` in `0 < Re s < 1`. All
  the twisted convolution identities hold for it. Yet its complete window form is **negative for every
  `T ≥ 0.38`**, and the witness is `F(t) = t` on `I_{0.38}`.
* **Theorem C (no separated arithmetic square), PROVED.** Let `D_j` be any operators that are sums of translations
  (Dirichlet-shift operators, with arbitrary real shifts). Suppose `W_T = Σ_j‖D_jF‖² + R` with a remainder `R` that
  has no atoms in `0 < |u| < 2T`. Then `R ≥ 0` forces `A_∞ ≥ β_0‖·‖²` with `β_0 > 0`, which is false for
  `T ≥ 0.4608`. The primitive analogue is false for `T ≥ 0.60`.
* **Corollary D (method constraint), PROVED in the sense of `cor:void`.** Consider a derivation of `W_T ≥ 0` or
  `𝔞_T|P_T ≥ 0` whose prime-side inputs are all invariant under the twist. These inputs include all Dirichlet-ring
  identities, the exact moduli `|Λ(n)|`, the Euler product with unimodular roots, the pole, every statement about
  the zeros in the open strip, and `ψ_a(x) = x + O(log x)`. Such a derivation is invalid at every `T ≥ 0.38`
  (complete) and at every `T ≥ 0.60` (primitive).

Consequence for the programme: F1 ("exact values of `Λ(n)`") is **not strong enough as stated**. The exact
**moduli**, together with every exact convolution identity, provably decide nothing at any window past `0.38`. What
must be load-bearing is the **phase** `arg Λ(p) = 0`, that is, reality and nonnegativity of the local atoms, coupled
to the archimedean place, or the functional equation. This proves the *necessity* half of Paper 46's open item (36)
("a class carrying an explicit **phase** datum … rather than a magnitude"). It also shows that *unimodularity* of the
local roots, the datum (36) names, is **not** sufficient: every twist keeps the roots unimodular.

## §1 What the identities produce: holomorphic products, not Hermitian squares

**Lemma 1 (identities are ring statements). PROVED.** Let `𝒟` be the ring of arithmetic functions under Dirichlet
convolution, with the derivation `∂f(n) = f(n)log n`, the absolute value `|f|`, and the size truncations
`f ↦ f·1_{n≤x}`. For every completely multiplicative `χ : ℕ → S¹`, the map `ι_χ : f ↦ fχ` is a ring automorphism
of `𝒟`. It commutes with `∂`, with `|·|` and with every truncation.

*Proof.* `(fχ)∗(gχ)(n) = Σ_{de=n}f(d)g(e)χ(d)χ(e) = χ(n)(f∗g)(n)` by complete multiplicativity; `ι_χ` is invertible
with inverse `ι_{χ̄}`; `∂` multiplies by `log n` and `χ` multiplies pointwise, so they commute; `|fχ| = |f|`; and
truncation is pointwise multiplication by `1_{n≤x}`. ∎

Consequently every exact identity used in the theory of primes holds verbatim after twisting all functions, with `1`
replaced by `χ`. The list: `Λχ = (μχ)∗(χlog)`, `(Λχ)log + (Λχ)∗(Λχ) = (μχ)∗(χlog²)` (Selberg), and Vaughan's identity
`Λχ = (Λχ)_{≤V} + (μχ)_{≤U}∗(χlog) − (μχ)_{≤U}∗(Λχ)_{≤V}∗χ + (μχ)_{>U}∗(Λχ)_{>V}∗χ` on `n > V`. Heath-Brown's and
Linnik's identities follow in the same way. **This is the whole content of Lemma 1: identity-level arithmetic is
`G`-covariant.**

**Lemma 2 (the Selberg–Riccati identity has signature (1,1)). PROVED.** With `G(s) = Σ Λ(n)n^{−s}` (`Re s > 1`),
Selberg's identity is `G² − G′ = ζ″/ζ`. On any vertical line,
`Re(G²) = (Re G)² − (Im G)²`. So the identity writes `Re G′` as `(Re G)² − (Im G)² − Re(ζ″/ζ)`: a difference of two
squares. In general, a Dirichlet-ring identity multiplies holomorphic functions, `D_1(s)D_2(s)`. A Hermitian square
`|D(s)|² = D(s)\overline{D(s)}` requires the anti-holomorphic partner `\overline{D(\bar s)}`, and no ring identity
supplies it.

*Proof.* Algebra. ∎ On the critical line, `\overline{D(s)} = \bar D(1−s)` for `s = ½ + iτ`, where `\bar D` has the
conjugated coefficients. For `ζ`, reality (`\bar ζ = ζ`) and the functional equation turn `ζ(1−s)` back into `ζ(s)`
times the Γ-ratio. So the only route from a holomorphic identity to a Hermitian square on the line passes through
**reality and the functional equation** (`HEURISTIC` as a statement about all routes; §7 makes a precise version of
it). This is P37 `ng:m5-gram` ("the true adjoint reintroduces the comb of quotients") seen from the symbol side.

## §2 The twist action on the window

**Definition 1.** Fix `T > 0`, let `S_T = {p prime : p < e^{2T}}`, and let
`G_T = {χ : ℕ → S¹ completely multiplicative, χ(p) = 1 for p ∉ S_T} ≅ (S¹)^{S_T}` (a compact torus, with Haar
probability `dχ`). For `χ ∈ G_T` put `v^χ_n = χ(n)v_n` (complex weights) and define, on complex `F` in the form
domain,
```
Π^χ_T(F) = −Σ_{n∈N_T} Re( v^χ_n c_F(log n) ), W^χ_T = A_∞ + Π^χ_T, 𝔞^χ_T = W^χ_T|_{P_T}.
```
These are the Hermitian forms whose kernel on `(−2T,2T)` is the archimedean-plus-polar kernel of `W_T` together with
the atoms `−½(v^χ_nδ_{log n} + \overline{v^χ_n}δ_{−log n})`. They are the forms of `eq:W` and `eq:Aform` with the weights
`v^χ`: use `c_F(−u) = \overline{c_F(u)}`. `W^{1}_T = W_T`. Write `λ^W_T(χ)` and `λ^A_T(χ)` for their bottoms.

**Lemma 4 (what the twisted object keeps). PROVED.** Put `a_χ = Λχ` and
`L_χ(s) = ζ(s)E_χ(s)` with `E_χ(s) = Π_{p∈S_T}(1 − p^{−s})/(1 − χ(p)p^{−s})`. Then:
(i) `−L_χ′/L_χ = Σ a_χ(n)n^{−s}` and `|a_χ(n)| = Λ(n)` for all `n`;
(ii) `L_χ = Π_p(1 − χ(p)p^{−s})^{−1}` is an Euler product with unimodular local roots;
(iii) `E_χ` is holomorphic and zero-free on `Re s > 0`, so `L_χ` has the simple pole at `s = 1` (residue
`E_χ(1) ≠ 0`), is zero-free on `Re s ≥ 1`, and has **exactly** the zeros of `ζ` in `0 < Re s < 1`, with
multiplicity. Every zero-free region, zero-density bound or ordinate statement for `ζ` in the open strip holds for
`L_χ`;
(iv) `ψ_{a_χ}(x) = ψ(x) + O_T(log x)`, so `ψ_{a_χ}(x) − x ≪ x^{½+ε}` holds if and only if it holds for `ψ`;
(v) every twisted identity of Lemma 1 holds for `(a_χ, μχ, χ)`;
(vi) the window form depends on `a_χ` only through `v^χ_T` (`prop:finite`(i), same proof).
What `L_χ` loses when `χ ≠ 1`: the reality `a_χ(n) ∈ ℝ` (for non-real `χ`), the nonnegativity `a_χ ≥ 0`, the
functional equation, and freedom from zeros and poles on `Re s = 0`. `E_χ` has zeros where `p^{−s} = 1` and poles
where `χ(p)p^{−s} = 1`, all on `Re s = 0`.

*Proof.* (i) `−(d/ds)log(1−χ(p)p^{−s})^{−1} = Σ_k χ(p)^k log p·p^{−ks}`. (ii) Definition. (iii) For `Re s > 0` one has
`|p^{−s}| < 1`, so no factor vanishes or has a pole, and `E_χ(1) = Π(1−1/p)/(1−χ(p)/p) ≠ 0`. (iv)
`ψ_{a_χ}(x) − ψ(x) = Σ_{p∈S_T}Σ_{p^k≤x}(χ(p)^k − 1)log p`, of size `≤ 2|S_T|·log x`. (v) Lemma 1. (vi) Definition. ∎

## §3 Theorem A — the Haar mean of the twisted forms is the archimedean form

**Theorem A. PROVED.** For every `T > 0` and every `F` in the form domain,
```
∫_{G_T} W^χ_T(F) dχ = A_∞(F), ∫_{G_T} 𝔞^χ_T(F) dχ = C_0‖F‖² + Lev(c_F) (F ∈ P_T).
```
Consequently:
(a) `inf_{χ∈G_T} λ^W_T(χ) ≤ λ_∞(T)` and `inf_{χ∈G_T} λ^A_T(χ) ≤ λ_{∞,P}(T)`;
(b) for `T ≥ 0.4608`, the set `{χ : λ^W_T(χ) < 0}` is a nonempty open subset of `G_T`, hence of positive Haar measure.
For `T ≥ 0.60` the same holds for `{χ : λ^A_T(χ) < 0}`;
(c) `inf_χ λ^A_T(χ) ≤ C_0 + φ((3π/2T)²) → C_0 = −5.372…` as `T → ∞`;
(d) in the language of `prop:finite`: the closed convex set `𝒱_T ⊂ ℂ^{N_T}` (primitive) contains the orbit
`G_T·v_T` for **no** `T ≥ 0.60`. The analogous set `𝒱^W_T` of the complete form contains the orbit for no
`T ≥ 0.4608`.

*Proof.* For `n ∈ N_T` with `v_n ≠ 0`, `n = p^k` with `p ∈ S_T` and `k ≥ 1`, and `χ(n) = χ(p)^k`. Its Haar integral over
the factor `S¹` of `p` is `∫_0^{2π}e^{ikθ}dθ/2π = 0`. The sum over `N_T` is finite, so
`∫Π^χ_T(F)dχ = −Σ_n Re(v_n c_F(log n)∫χ(n)dχ) = 0`. On `P_T` the polar term vanishes. (a) Take a unit `F` with
`A_∞(F) < λ_∞(T) + ε`. A mean does not exceed the supremum, and here it is at least the infimum, so some `χ` has
`W^χ_T(F) ≤ A_∞(F)`. (b) `λ_∞(T) < 0` for `T ≥ 0.4608` (`prop:archneg`) and `λ_{∞,P}(T) < 0` for `T ≥ 0.60`
(`cor:Tarch`). Take a unit `F` with `A_∞(F) < 0`. The map `χ ↦ W^χ_T(F)` is a trigonometric polynomial on the torus,
so `{χ : W^χ_T(F) < 0}` is open, and it is nonempty by the mean. A nonempty open set in a compact group has positive
Haar measure. (c) `prop:arch`(ii). (d) The forms are affine in the weight vector `v ∈ ℂ^{N_T} ≅ ℝ^{2|N_T|}` (the kernel
atoms are `−½(v_nδ_{log n} + \bar v_nδ_{−log n})`). So `𝒱_T = ∩_F{v : 𝔞^{(v)}_T(F) ≥ 0}` is closed and convex, as in
`prop:finite`(ii). If it contained the orbit, it would contain the Haar barycentre, the vector `0`. But `0 ∉ 𝒱_T` for
`T ≥ 0.60` (`prop:finite`(iii)), and `0 ∉ 𝒱^W_T` for `T ≥ 0.4608` (`prop:archneg`). ∎

*Form.* Both forms are stated separately. *Scope.* This is a statement about the orbit, not about `ζ`: the point
`χ = 1` is not touched.

**Remark A1 (the point `χ = 1` is an outlier).** By (c), for large `T` the orbit of the window comb has bottoms
reaching `≤ −5.37 + o(1)`. Under RH, `λ^A_T(1) ≥ 0` for every `T`. So `ζ` sits at a point of its own orbit
`G_T·v_T` whose value exceeds the orbit infimum by at least `|C_0| − o(1)`. This is not a margin statement:
`thm:margin` concerns `λ^A_T(1)` alone.

## §4 Theorem B — the sign flip at 2: real coefficients, exact moduli, negative from `T = 0.38`

Let `χ_2 ∈ G_T` (for any `T > ½log2`) be `χ_2(2) = −1` and `χ_2(p) = 1` for `p ≠ 2`. Then
`a_2 := Λχ_2` is real, `|a_2| = Λ`, `a_2(2^k) = (−1)^k log 2`, and it belongs to
`L_2(s) = ζ(s)(1−2^{−s})/(1+2^{−s})`. That function is real on `ℝ` and positive on `(1,∞)`. It has the pole at `1`
with residue `1/3`, exactly `ζ`'s zeros in the open strip, and zeros and poles only on `Re s = 0` besides.

**Theorem B. PROVED** (unconditional; one interval evaluation). For every `T ≥ 0.38`, `λ^W_T(χ_2) < 0`. Explicitly, with
`F(t) = t` on `I_{0.38}` (extended by zero),
```
W^{χ_2}_{0.38}(F) = A_∞(F) + v_2 c_F(log 2) ≤ U(0.38) + v_2 c_F(log 2) ∈ [−7.13042·10⁻⁴, −7.13041·10⁻⁴] < 0,
```
where `U` is the upper bound of `prop:archneg`, `c_F(log2) = (4T³ − 6T²log2 + log³2)/6` and `v_2 = √2·log2`.

*Proof.* `F` is real, odd and bounded, with `|F̂(τ)| = O(1/|τ|)`, so it lies in the form domain. The proof of
`prop:archneg` gives `A_∞(F) ≤ U(T)` for every `T > 0`. It uses only `K(u) ≤ 1/u + ½`, the tail formula
`thm:S`(iii) and the pole term `−2s²`, and it never uses `T = 0.4608`. For `0 ≤ u ≤ 2T`,
`c_F(u) = (4T³ − 6T²u + u³)/6`, and `log 2 < 0.76 = 2·0.38`. The only contact of `N_{0.38}` is `n = 2`
(`e^{0.76} < 3`), and `χ_2(2)v_2 = −v_2`, so `Π^{χ_2}(F) = +v_2c_F(log 2)`. Interval arithmetic
(`mpmath.iv`, 30 digits, `C_0 = −γ − π/2 − 3log2 − logπ`, `arctan` via `atan2`) gives
`U(0.38) ∈ [0.0071339356, 0.0071339357]` and `c_F(log2) ∈ [−0.0080050109, −0.0080050108]`, with the sum in the
displayed interval. For `T > 0.38`: extend `F` by zero. The norm, the Tate moments and `c_F` are unchanged, and new
contacts have `log n ≥ 0.76`, where `c_F = 0`. This is the argument of `lem:monoT`, which does not use the sign of the
weights. So `λ^W_T(χ_2) ≤ W^{χ_2}_{0.38}(F)/‖F‖² < 0`. ∎

For comparison: the same `F` gives `U(0.38) − v_2c_F(log2) = +0.01498` for `ζ`. That is consistent with Zhu's positivity
at `T ≤ 0.8` (`CITED`), and it proves nothing. The threshold for this `F` is `T ≈ 0.37739` (the root of
`U + v_2c = 0`, `DIAGNOSTIC`).

**Corollary B1 (primitive form, Liouville on the window). PROVED, modulo Paper 42's certified result.** For
`T ∈ [0.60, log 2]` the contacts are `{2, 3}`, both primes, and `χ_λ := (χ(2), χ(3)) = (−1, −1)` sends `v_T` to
`−v_T`. Paper 42 gives `v_T ∈ 𝒱_T`. If also `−v_T ∈ 𝒱_T`, convexity would put `0 ∈ 𝒱_T`, contradicting
`prop:finite`(iii). Hence `λ^A_T(χ_λ) < 0` on `[0.60, log 2]`: the window form of `ζ(s)Π_{p=2,3}(1−p^{−s})/(1+p^{−s})`
is negative there. ∎

**Remark B2 (the general "reflection" principle).** For every `T ≥ 0.60` and every weight vector `u`, at most one of
`u, −u` lies in `𝒱_T`. For `T ≥ 0.4608` the same holds for `𝒱^W_T`. So positivity of the window form is never a
property of `|u|`: `𝒱_T` is convex and does not contain `0`, and a nonempty convex set that is symmetric under
`u ↦ −u` contains `0`. `PROVED` (two lines). Under RH, `v_T ∈ 𝒱_T` for all `T`, so `−v_T ∉ 𝒱_T` for all `T ≥ 0.60`.

## §5 Theorem C — separated arithmetic squares cannot carry the sign

**Definition 2 (Dirichlet-shift operator).** For a countable set `X ⊂ ℝ` and `b : X → ℂ` with
`‖b‖_1 = Σ_x|b(x)| < ∞`, put `(D_bF)(t) = Σ_{x∈X}b(x)F(t − x)`, a bounded operator `L²(I_T) → L²(ℝ)`. A *separated
arithmetic square* for `W_T` is a family `(b_j)` with `Σ_j‖b_j‖_1² < ∞`, together with `Q(F) := Σ_j‖D_{b_j}F‖²`, such
that `R := W_T − Q` is `≥ 0` on the form domain of `L²(I_T)` and has **no atoms** in `0 < |u| < 2T`. The last
condition means that `Q` reproduces the prime atoms exactly and creates no others in the window. For the primitive
form, replace `L²(I_T)` by `P_T` and `W_T` by `𝔞_T`.

**Theorem C. PROVED.** A separated arithmetic square for `W_T` exists only if `λ_∞(T) ≥ β_0 := Σ_jΣ_x|b_j(x)|² > 0`. So
none exists for `T ≥ T*_∞`, in particular for any `T ≥ 0.4608`. For the primitive form one exists only if
`λ_{∞,P}(T) ≥ β_0 > 0`, so none exists for `T ≥ 0.60`. The shifts are arbitrary reals, so `X = log ℚ_+` (Dirichlet
polynomials in `n^{iτ}` and their ratios) is included.

*Proof.* Since `⟨F(·−x), F(·−y)⟩_{L²(ℝ)} = c_F(y − x)` and `|c_F| ≤ ‖F‖²`, the double sum converges absolutely, and
`Q(F) = Σ_d β_d c_F(d)` with `β_d = Σ_jΣ_{y−x=d}b_j(x)\overline{b_j(y)}` and `Σ_d|β_d| ≤ Σ_j‖b_j‖_1²`. Only
`|d| < 2T` contribute. The term `d = 0` is `β_0‖F‖²`. So `Q` is a Toeplitz form with purely atomic kernel. `W_T` is
Toeplitz, with kernel equal to the archimedean-plus-polar kernel plus the comb atoms. So `R` is Toeplitz. By the
density of `span{φ∗ψ̃ : φ, ψ ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)}` in `C_c^∞(−2T,2T)`, a Toeplitz form determines its kernel on `(−2T,2T)`:
for a test function supported near `u_k` with `|u_k| < 2T`, take `φ` supported near `u_k/2` and `ψ` an approximate
identity near `−u_k/2`. On `0 < |u| < 2T` the kernel of `A_∞` is the continuous function `−½K(|u|) + 2cosh(u/2)`. So
"no atoms of `R` there" says that `β_d` equals the comb atom at `d = ±log n` and vanishes at every other `d` with
`0 < |d| < 2T`. Then `Q = β_0‖·‖² − Π_T`, so `R = W_T − Q = A_∞ − β_0‖·‖²`, and `R ≥ 0` is `A_∞ ≥ β_0` on the unit
sphere. Apply `prop:archneg` and `cor:Tarch`. `β_0 > 0` unless `Q ≡ 0`, and `Q ≡ 0` cannot reproduce a nonempty comb
(`N_T ∋ 2` for `T > ½log2`). ∎

*Twist reading.* Theorem C is the special case of Theorem A in which the construction is visibly covariant. Replace
`b_j(x)` by `χ(x)b_j(x)`, where `χ` extends a character of `log ℚ_+` to the subgroup generated by `X`; this is
possible because `S¹` is divisible. The atoms `β_d` then become `χ(d)β_d`, so the twisted square reproduces
`W^χ_T − R`, and `W^χ_T ≥ R ≥ 0` for every `χ`, which contradicts Theorem A(b).

## §6 Corollary D — twist-covariant derivations decide no window past `0.38`

**Corollary D. PROVED** (a statement about derivations, in the sense of `cor:void` and P46 Thm 18.1(ii)). Let
`𝓗(a)` be a hypothesis on complex weight functions `a` with `𝓗(Λ)`. Suppose `𝓗(Λ) ⟹ 𝓗(Λχ)` for every `χ ∈ G_T`, or
for `χ ∈ {1, χ_2}`. Then no derivation `𝓗(a) ⟹ W^{(a)}_T ≥ 0` is valid at any `T ≥ 0.38`. With `G_T`-invariance, no
derivation `𝓗(a) ⟹ 𝔞^{(a)}_T|P_T ≥ 0` is valid at any `T ≥ 0.60`. Such an `𝓗` can include any conjunction of the

## §7 What survives in the angle (stated as `OPEN`, with the constraint it must meet)

1. **Dressed squares.** Theorem C excludes squares in which the prime shifts and the archimedean part are
  separated. What remains is operators `B_j = Σ_x S_x C_{j,x}` whose translation parts are *dressed* by archimedean
  convolutions `C_{j,x}`. Under the twist, their cross terms `C_{j,x}^*…C_{j,y}` get the factor `χ(y−x)`, and the
  archimedean kernel does not. So a dressed identity `W_T = ΣB_j^*B_j + R` is not automatically covariant, and Theorem A
  does not exclude it by itself. By Corollary D, its *proof* must use A8, A9 or the functional equation. The one
  dressed square on record is the zero-side square `Σ_γ|F̂(γ)|²` (rank-one `B_γ`), which is the RH SOS (`cor:RH`,
  R157 row 51: EQUIV). **`OPEN`:** a dressed square built from `Λ = μ∗log` whose proof uses the functional equation
  through the involution `s ↦ 1 − \bar s` of Lemma 2. No candidate is proposed here.
2. **Anchoring (`HEURISTIC`).** For `χ = 1`, the three points where the comb is fully aligned (`τ = 0`), where the pole
  sits (`s = 1`, i.e. `τ = −i/2`, which is what `P_T` annihilates to second order), and where the archimedean
  symbol is centred (the minimum `m(0) = C_0`) coincide. For a generic twist the comb's alignment points move to
  Kronecker points, and the pole and `P_T` stay where they are. Theorem A says that the coincidence is what keeps
  `χ = 1` above the orbit average. The primitive subspace is *defined* by the pole. A mechanism in this angle would
  have to exploit the coincidence exactly, not estimate around it, because of O3. This is the "polar/Frobenius
  coupling" of MEMORY (phase 136), now with a proved necessity statement (Thm A, Cor D) and no construction.

## §10 Weakest step

Corollary D and F1′ are statements about **derivations**. They depend on the reading that a hypothesis "used only
through" identities and moduli is `G_T`-invariant. A derivation that silently uses reality or positivity of the
coefficients (for instance by taking real parts, or by `Λ ≥ 0` inside an inequality) is **not** covered. Such a
derivation is not excluded, and the memo does not claim otherwise. On the mathematical side, Theorem C's "no atoms of
`R`" hypothesis is essential. If `R` may carry atoms in the window, the decomposition is vacuous, since
`R = W_T, Q = 0` always works when `W_T ≥ 0`. The theorem therefore excludes a specific architecture and not every
square. Theorem A(b) for the complete form relies on `prop:archneg`, which is interval-certified in P47; Theorem B's
threshold `0.38` relies on one interval evaluation, reproduced here and easy to rerun. No mechanism is constructed: the
"for all `T`" arrow is **not** built in this round.
