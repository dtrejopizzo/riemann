# G2_B4_R4: Group G2 (the mechanism), round 4. The primitive gap of the SOS no-go is closed: the two polar multipliers cannot rescue a dilated certificate rule (Theorem R′_prim), through an edge-atom rigidity lemma

Angle: sums of squares / Positivstellensatz. The target is an explicit certificate family indexed by `T`, with
coefficients built from the exact `Λ(n)`, that exists for all `T` by an identity.

**Status on entry.** In round 3 I reported the angle as a dead end (G2_B4_R3 §8). The round-3 panel reviewed only
G2_B9_R3, and none of its reports mentions G2_B4_R3. G2_B4_R3 §8 left one gap in its own no-go: **the primitive form
was not covered.** Theorems R and R′ are complete-form statements. A primitive certificate carries two free
multipliers `β = (β_0, β_1)` chosen per window (`thm:Zexh(ii)`), and I had not excluded that they rescue a dilated
rule. This round closes that gap. **It does not supply a mechanism.** Nothing below proves `λ^A_T ≥ 0` or
`λ^W_T ≥ 0` for any `T > 0.8`.

Notation follows Paper 46 (`main.tex`): `s_T` (eq:symbol), `f_w` (eq:fw, def:W), `ĝ_0, ĝ_1` (eq:g, lem:gbounds), and
the dual (D) of def:PD. `ĥ(τ) = ∫h(u)e^{iτu}du`. **[compl]** means complete form and **[prim]** primitive form. Every
statement carries one of the labels PROVED, CITED, HEURISTIC, OPEN.

## §2 Two lemmas on flat tops

**Lemma E1 (edge-atom rigidity; PROVED).** Let `μ` be a finite real even Borel measure on `ℝ` whose restriction to
`(−1,1)` is Lebesgue measure. Then `μ̂(x) = ∫e^{ixv}dμ(v)` takes a negative value.

*Proof.* Suppose `μ̂ ≥ 0`. Fix an even `ρ ∈ C_c^∞(−¼,¼)` with `ρ ≥ 0` and `∫ρ = 1`, and put `k = ρ⋆ρ`. Then
`supp k ⊂ [−½,½]`, `∫k = 1`, `k̂ = ρ̂²` is Schwartz, and `k̂ ≥ 0`. Let `ψ := k⋆μ`. It is continuous and bounded,
and `ψ ∈ L¹` because `‖ψ‖_1 ≤ ‖k‖_1|μ|(ℝ)`. Its transform `ψ̂ = ρ̂²μ̂` is `≥ 0` and lies in `L¹`, since
`|μ̂| ≤ |μ|(ℝ)`. So the inversion formula holds pointwise and `ψ` is positive definite, with
`ψ(0) = (2π)^{−1}∫ψ̂`.

Take `|v| < ½`. Then `ψ(v) = ∫k(v−w)dμ(w)`, and the integrand vanishes unless `|w| ≤ |v|+½ < 1`. On that region
`μ = dw`, so `ψ(v) = ∫k = 1`. In particular `ψ(0) = 1`. The Gram inequality for positive-definite functions,
`|ψ(u+a)−ψ(u)|² ≤ 2ψ(0)(ψ(0) − Re ψ(a))` (as in the proof of `lem:Rrig`), gives `ψ(u+a) = ψ(u)` for all `u` and
all `|a| < ½`. A continuous function with every period in `(0,½)` is constant, so `ψ ≡ 1`. That contradicts
`ψ ∈ L¹`. ∎

*Reading.* Put the flat top at `(−2T,2T)` (rescale `v = u/2T`). The lemma says that no finite measure placed on
`{|u| ≥ 2T}` makes the transform of `1_{(−2T,2T)}du + (that measure)` termwise nonnegative. That measure may be a
flat-top tail, the atoms of `ν_w`, or atoms exactly at the window edge `±2T`. This is `lem:Rrig` for the full dual
feasible class of def:PD at `β = (2,0)`.

**Lemma E2 (a cosine-annihilating negative average; PROVED).** Let `Φ ∈ L¹(ℝ)` be even with `Φ ≡ 1` on `[−1,1]`.
Then there exist `X < ∞` and a probability measure `ν` supported on at most four points of `[−X,X]` and symmetric
under `x ↦ −x`, such that

`∫e^{ix}dν(x) = 0` and `∫Φ̂ dν = −κ < 0`.

*Proof.* `Φ̂` is continuous and even. Three cases.

(1) If `Φ̂(x) < 0` at some `x` with `cos x = 0`, take `ν = ½(δ_x + δ_{−x})`.

(2) Suppose there are `x_1, x_2` with `cos x_1 > 0 > cos x_2` and `−cos x_2·Φ̂(x_1) + cos x_1·Φ̂(x_2) < 0`. Put
`α = −cos x_2/(cos x_1 − cos x_2) ∈ (0,1)` and `ν_0 = αδ_{x_1} + (1−α)δ_{x_2}`. Then `∫cos x dν_0 = 0` and
`∫Φ̂ dν_0 = (cos x_1 − cos x_2)^{−1}(−cos x_2Φ̂(x_1) + cos x_1Φ̂(x_2)) < 0`. Take
`ν = ½(ν_0 + ν_0∘(−·))`. The sine moment then vanishes too, and `∫Φ̂dν = ∫Φ̂dν_0` because `Φ̂` is even.

(3) Suppose neither (1) nor (2) applies. Put `b⁻ := sup_{cos x<0} Φ̂(x)/cos x` and
`b⁺ := inf_{cos x>0} Φ̂(x)/cos x`. The failure of (2) says `b⁻ ≤ b⁺`, and both index sets are nonempty, so both
numbers are finite. Pick `b ∈ [b⁻, b⁺]`. For `cos x > 0`, `Φ̂(x) ≥ b cos x`. For `cos x < 0`,
`Φ̂(x)/cos x ≤ b` gives `Φ̂(x) ≥ b cos x`. For `cos x = 0`, the failure of (1) gives `Φ̂(x) ≥ 0 = b cos x`. Hence
`Φ̂(x) − b cos x ≥ 0` for every `x`. But `Φ̂ − b cos x` is the transform of `μ := Φ dv − (b/2)(δ_1 + δ_{−1})`, a
finite even measure equal to `dv` on `(−1,1)`. That contradicts Lemma E1. ∎

## §3 The multipliers near a zero are a window-edge oscillation

By lem:gbounds and eq:g, with `s = ½ + iτ` and `a = 2T`,

`ĝ_0(τ) = ∫_{−a}^{a}cosh(u/2)e^{iτu}du = 2Re[sinh(sa)/s]`,
`ĝ_1(τ) = ∂_σ|_{σ=½} 2Re[sinh((σ+iτ)a)/(σ+iτ)] = 2Re[a cosh(sa)/s − sinh(sa)/s²]`.

**Lemma E3 (PROVED).** [prim] Put `ζ_τ := e^{2iτT}`, `L(y_0,y_1) := (y_0, y_1 − 2Ty_0)`, and, for `s ∉ ℝ`, let
`N_s: ℂ → ℝ²` be `N_s(ζ) := (Re(ζ/s), −Re(ζ/s²))`. Then:

(i) `e^{−T}L(ĝ_0(τ), ĝ_1(τ)) = N_{½+iτ}(ζ_τ) + ε(τ)`, with `|ε(τ)| ≤ 12(T+1)e^{−2T}` for all real `τ`.
(ii) `N_s` is ℝ-linear with `det N_s = −Im s/|s|^4`, which is nonzero for `τ ≠ 0`.
(iii) Fix `γ_1 > 0`, put `s_* = ½ + iγ_1`, and fix `X`. For `τ = γ_1 + x/2T` with `|x| ≤ X`:
`e^{−T}L(ĝ_0, ĝ_1)(τ) = N_{s_*}(e^{iφ}e^{ix}) + O_X(1/T)`, where `φ := 2γ_1T` and the constant is uniform in `φ`.

*Proof.* (i) Write `e^{sa} = e^Tζ_τ` and `|e^{−sa}| = e^{−T}`, and note `|s| ≥ ½`. Then
`ĝ_0 = e^T Re(ζ_τ/s) + ε_0` with `|ε_0| ≤ 2e^{−T}`, and
`ĝ_1 = e^T(2T Re(ζ_τ/s) − Re(ζ_τ/s²)) + ε_1` with `|ε_1| ≤ (4T+4)e^{−T}`. Hence
`ĝ_1 − 2Tĝ_0 = −e^T Re(ζ_τ/s²) + ε_1 − 2Tε_0`. Multiplying by `e^{−T}` gives (i).
(ii) Put `w = 1/s`. The rows of `N_s` are `(Re w, −Im w)` and `(−Re w², Im w²)`, so
`det = Re w·Im w² − Im w·Re w² = Im(w̄w²) = |w|²Im w = −Im s/|s|^4`.
(iii) Here `ζ_τ = e^{iφ}e^{ix}` and `s = s_* + ix/2T`. The maps `s ↦ 1/s` and `s ↦ 1/s²` are Lipschitz on `|s| ≥ ½`,
so `‖N_s − N_{s_*}‖ ≤ C X/T`. Add (i). ∎

*Reading.* Near a zero, in the resolved variable `x = 2T(τ − γ_1)`, every primitive correction `b_0ĝ_0 + b_1ĝ_1`
equals `e^T·Re(c·e^{ix})` up to `O(|b|e^T/T)`, for some `c ∈ ℂ` depending on `b` and `φ`. So it is a pure
frequency-1 oscillation, which is the transform of two atoms at the edge `±1` of the rescaled flat top. Lemma E1
says that such atoms cannot make the rescaled kernel termwise positive. §4 turns this into an exact statement
(Farkas), so that the unbounded size of `b` does no harm.

## §4 Theorem R′_prim

Let `Φ` be as in G2_B4_R3 Def. 2.1: even, `≡ 1` on `[−1,1]`, `Φ′ ∈ BV`, with superexponential decay. Let
`w_T = Φ(·/2T) ∈ 𝒲_T` (G2_B4_R3 Lemma 2.2). A primitive certificate built on `w_T` has objective
`F_{T,b}(τ) := f_{w_T}(τ) + b_0ĝ_0(τ) + b_1ĝ_1(τ)`, where `b = (β_0 − 2, β_1) ∈ ℝ²` is arbitrary (def:PD,
`thm:Zexh(ii)`).

**Theorem R′_prim (PROVED).** **[prim]**
(a) *(Under RH.)* Let `γ_1` be an ordinate with multiplicity `m_1`, and let `κ = κ_Φ > 0` and `X` be as in Lemma E2.
There is `T_3 = T_3(Φ, γ_1)` such that for every `T ≥ T_3`,

`sup_{b∈ℝ²} inf_{τ∈ℝ} F_{T,b}(τ) ≤ −m_1κT.`

(b) *(Unconditional.)* There is `T* < ∞` such that for every `T ≥ T*` and every `b ∈ ℝ²`, `inf_τ F_{T,b} < 0`. The
existence of `T*` is proved, but the value of `T*` is not effective. So the dilated rule is not a primitive
certificate family on any half-line `[T_0, ∞)`, **whatever the per-window choice of the two polar multipliers.**

*Proof.* (a) Assume RH. **Step 1 (exact annihilating configuration).** Let `ν = Σ_k p_kδ_{x_k}` be as in Lemma E2,
with `|x_k| ≤ X`. Add the auxiliary points `y_j = 2πj/3` (`j = 0,1,2`). For a finite set of `x`'s put
`τ(x) := γ_1 + x/2T` and `V_T(x) := e^{−T}L(ĝ_0,ĝ_1)(τ(x))`. By Lemma E3(iii),
`V_T(x) = N_{s_*}(e^{i(φ+x)}) + O(1/T)`, uniformly in `φ`. Since `Σp_ke^{ix_k} = 0` and `N_{s_*}` is linear,
`E := Σ_kp_kV_T(x_k) = O(1/T)`.

*Positive-spanning claim.* The vectors `u_j := N_{s_*}(e^{i(φ+y_j)})` satisfy `Σu_j = 0`, and `u_0, u_1` are linearly
independent because `N_{s_*}` is invertible. Let `B_φ = [u_0 u_1]`. Then `‖B_φ^{−1}‖ ≤ β_*` uniformly in `φ`, by
continuity on the circle, and `B_φ^{−1}(−u_2) = (1,1)`. For `T` large, the perturbed matrix `[V_T(y_0) V_T(y_1)]`
has inverse of norm `≤ 2β_*`, and its inverse applied to `−V_T(y_2)` is `(1,1) + O(1/T) ≥ (½,½)`. So every
`e ∈ ℝ²` can be written `e = Σ_jc_jV_T(y_j)` with `c_j ≥ 0` and `Σc_j ≤ C|e|`. To see this, take `c_2 = 4β_*|e|`
and solve for `(c_0,c_1)`. Apply this with `e = −E` to get `c_j ≥ 0` with `Σ_jc_j = O(1/T)` and

`Σ_kp_kV_T(x_k) + Σ_jc_jV_T(y_j) = 0` **exactly.**

Put `ν_T := (Σ_kp_kδ_{x_k} + Σ_jc_jδ_{y_j})/(1 + Σc_j)`, a probability measure. `L` is invertible and `e^{−T}` is a
nonzero scalar, so `Σ_xν_T(x)ĝ_i(τ(x)) = 0` for `i = 0,1`.

**Step 2 (the multipliers drop out).** For every `b ∈ ℝ²`,
`Σ_xν_T(x)F_{T,b}(τ(x)) = Σ_xν_T(x)f_{w_T}(τ(x))`. Hence `inf_τF_{T,b} ≤ Σ_xν_T(x)f_{w_T}(τ(x))`, uniformly in `b`.

**Step 3 (the dip).** Let `d = min{|γ−γ_1| : γ ≠ γ_1} > 0` and `S_1 := Σ_{γ≠γ_1}m_γ(γ−γ_1)^{−2} < ∞`. Take
`T ≥ max(X, 2π)/d`. Then for every support point `x`, `|x|/2T ≤ d/2`, and the argument of G2_B4_R3 Theorem R′(b)
applies (Lemma Z′ amended, Lemma 2.2(iii)):
`f_{w_T}(τ(x)) = 2Tm_1Φ̂(x) + R_T(x)` with `|R_T(x)| ≤ 2‖Φ′‖_{BV}S_1/T`. Therefore

`Σν_Tf_{w_T}(τ(·)) ≤ (1+Σc)^{−1}[−2Tm_1κ + 2Tm_1‖Φ‖_1Σc_j] + 2‖Φ′‖_{BV}S_1/T = −2m_1κT + O(1)`,

using `Σc_j = O(1/T)` and `|Φ̂| ≤ ‖Φ‖_1`. The right side is `≤ −m_1κT` for `T ≥ T_3`.

(b) If RH holds, (a) gives the claim with `T* = T_3`. If RH fails, the primitive form of Weil's criterion
(P46 l.140–146, CITED: Paper 45 Thm 10.4, Bombieri 2000 Thm 1) gives `T_1` with `λ^A_{T_1} < 0`. By `lem:monoT`,
`λ^A_T < 0` for all `T ≥ T_1`. By weak duality (`prop:weak`), every primitive certificate value is `≤ λ^A_T`, so
`inf_τF_{T,b} ≤ λ^A_T < 0` for every `b`. Take `T* = T_1`. ∎

**Cor. R′+ (PROVED).** [compl] The same case split upgrades G2_B4_R3 Theorem R′(a) from "arbitrarily large `T`" to
"all `T ≥ T*`". Under ¬RH, `λ^W_T < 0` for every `T ≥ T_1`, by `lem:monoT` and weak duality. Under RH,
Theorem R′(b) of R3 applies. Theorem R′_prim contains this: set `b = (0,0)`. It also sharpens it, since the bound
holds uniformly over all `b`.

*Remarks.* (1) The configuration `ν_T` sits within `O(X/T)` of a single zero, so it is a local statement at one
ordinate. Under RH, `m_1 = 1` for `γ_1 = 14.13…` (CITED, numerical verification of simple low zeros). The bound needs
only `m_1 ≥ 1`. (2) What made the complete-form proof work was the resolution scale; the primitive proof needs
Lemma E1 on top of it. Lemma E3 exhibits the two multipliers as window-edge atoms, and Lemma E1 says that edge
atoms cannot remove a flat-top negativity. (3) **Open.** Theorem R (bounded edge scale) and Cor. R′2 with
`a_T = o(T)` are not covered in [prim]. When `a_T = o(T)`, the correction oscillates at frequency `2T/a_T → ∞` in
the resolved variable `a_T(τ−γ)`. It is then not a fixed `cos x`, and Lemma E2 does not apply as stated. In the
bounded-scale case, `β(T)` is free and can break the almost-periodicity argument of Theorem R. **Label: OPEN.**

## §5 Remark PS: what a Positivstellensatz certificate for all `T` would have to be (bookkeeping)

Treat the comb `a = (a(n))` as the variable. A *relative certificate* is an identity
`W_T(a)[F] = σ_0(a,F) + Σ_iσ_i(a,F)g_i(a) + Σ_jr_j(a,F)h_j(a)`. Here the `σ`'s are sums of squares in `F`, and
`S = {g_i ≥ 0, h_j = 0}` is a constraint set of combs containing `Λ`. By definition, such an identity proves
`W_T(a) ≥ 0` for every `a ∈ S` (or `λ^A_T ≥ 0` on `P_T` in [prim]). Hence, PROVED from the record:

* `S ∩ (Λ + 𝒯_meas) = {Λ}` is necessary. Otherwise `S` contains some `Λ + b` with `b ≠ 0`, and G2_B9_R3 Theorem R′(i)
  (unconditional, both forms) says that `Λ + b` is not ∀T-positive.
* `S` must avoid the DMV, Epstein and finmod falsifiers (`cor:void`, `thm:finmod`, P47 Thm 18.1(ii)). This excludes
  the Smith/GCD identity of G2_B4_R3 Rem. G, whose only constraint is `Λ ≥ 0`.
* The one natural arithmetic `S` that passes both tests is the Chebyshev system `(a⋆1)(n) = log n`. For
  `n < e^{2T}` it fixes every coordinate that `W_T` reads. The relative certificate then degenerates: it is an SOS
  decomposition of the single form `W_T(Λ)`, i.e. `λ^W_T ≥ 0` itself (`prop:finite` per window). Its existence for all
  `T` by a *uniform formula* is exactly what Theorems R and R′ and R′_prim exclude for rules with a scale. What
  remains is `cor:RH`, which is **EQUIVALENT**.

So the Positivstellensatz language adds no leverage. Constraint sets loose enough to leave room for an algebraic
identity are killed by rigidity or by the falsifiers. The tight constraint set turns the question back into
Weil's criterion.

## §7 What is genuinely new

1. **Theorem R′_prim (PROVED, [prim]).** It closes the gap declared in G2_B4_R3 §8: a dilated certificate rule
  fails in the primitive form too, at depth `≥ κ_Φm_1T`, uniformly over the two polar multipliers.
2. **Lemma E3 (PROVED).** Near a zero, `ĝ_0` and `ĝ_1` are, up to `O(1/T)`, an invertible linear image of `e^{2iτT}`:
  the primitivity constraints act at the resolution scale as atoms at the window edge. This explains *why* the
  primitive multipliers cannot help a resolving kernel.
3. **Lemma E1 and Lemma E2 (PROVED).** `lem:Rrig` extends to measures that equal `du` on the flat top (edge atoms
  allowed). The dual form is an explicit 4-point negative average that annihilates `e^{ix}`. E1 is probably
  folklore, and I claim no priority for it.
4. **Cor. R′+.** The failure of dilated rules holds at every `T ≥ T*` unconditionally (non-effective `T*`), in both
  forms.
5. **Rem. PS.** This is the Positivstellensatz form of the "asymmetry lesson" of the round-3 panel.

Not new: Weil's criterion (primitive form), `lem:monoT`, `prop:weak`, the explicit formula, the Gram inequality.

## §8 Weakest step, and the honest verdict

* **Weakest step: Step 1 of Theorem R′_prim.** The perturbation argument that turns the approximate annihilation
  `Σp_ke^{ix_k} = 0` into an exact one needs Lemma E3(iii) to hold uniformly in the phase `φ = 2γ_1T`, and needs
  `‖B_φ^{−1}‖` bounded on the circle. Both follow from continuity and compactness, as written, but the constant
  `T_3` is not tracked. The tail bound `O(1/T)` is inherited from R3, where it needs `Φ′ ∈ BV`. A profile with a
  jump is not covered.
* **[prim] OPEN cases:** bounded edge scale (Theorem R) and `a_T = o(T)` (Cor. R′2), where `β(T)` is free.
* **Verdict for G2.** The SOS/Positivstellensatz angle is now closed in both forms on every certificate rule that
  has a scale. It is also closed for every relative certificate whose constraint set does not already pin `Λ`
  (Rem. PS). What remains is the free, scale-less per-window choice of `cor:RH`, and that is RH (EQUIVALENT).
  **No mechanism. Dead end, reported as such.** For the record I recommend Lemma E1 (next to `lem:Rrig`) and
  Theorem R′_prim with Lemma E3 (next to `thm:Zexh(ii)`), as proved obstructions.
