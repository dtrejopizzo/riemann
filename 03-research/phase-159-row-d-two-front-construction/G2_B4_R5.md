# G2_B4_R5: Group G2 (the mechanism), round 5. The polar multipliers cannot be localised: a phase-window inequality for the primitive dual (Theorem PT), and the resolution no-go for every resolving certificate rule in both forms

Angle: sums of squares / Positivstellensatz. The target is an explicit certificate family `T ↦ (w_T, β(T))` with
coefficients built from the exact `Λ(n)`, existing for all `T` by an identity.

**Status on entry.** The round-4 panel did not review G2_B4_R4; none of the five reports names it. G2_B4_R4 left two
cases of its own no-go OPEN in the primitive form: the bounded edge scale (Theorem R of R2) and the edge scale
`a_T = o(T)` (Cor. R′2 of R3). Its primitive proof (Lemmas E1–E3, a Farkas configuration near one zero) was also
specific to dilations.

**This round.** I replace that machinery with one elementary inequality, **Theorem PT**. It holds for *every* dual
objective at every window, with no hypothesis on `f` and none on the zeros. The two primitive multipliers act, up to
`O(1/T)`, as a single global oscillation `Re[e^{2iTτ} r_β(½+iτ)]` whose amplitude varies slowly. So they can lift a
dip of depth `A` at one point only by paying `≥ A/K` on **every** phase window (length `π/T`) of the line. Under RH
this gives a resolution criterion (Cor. RC). The criterion closes both OPEN cases, re-proves Theorem R′_prim of R4
in two lines, and upgrades Theorem R of R2 from "arbitrarily large `T`" to "all `T ≥ T*`, depth `≍ T`".
**No mechanism.** Nothing below proves `λ^A_T ≥ 0` or `λ^W_T ≥ 0` for any `T > 0.8`.

Notation follows Paper 46 (`main.tex`): `s_T`, `f_w` (def:W, eq:fw), `ĝ_0, ĝ_1` (eq:g, lem:gbounds), (D) of
def:PD, `thm:Zexh(ii)`, `prop:weak`, `lem:monoT`, `lem:Rrig`. `ĥ(x) = ∫h(u)e^{ixu}du`, `s = ½ + iτ`,
`ζ_τ := e^{2iTτ}`. **[compl]** means complete form and **[prim]** means primitive form.

## §2 Theorem PT: the multipliers are a global phase oscillation

### 2.1 Exact form of the multipliers

Put `a = 2T`. From eq:g, writing `cosh(u/2)e^{iτu} = ½(e^{su} + e^{−s̄u})` and integrating over `[−a,a]` gives
`ĝ_0(τ) = 2Re[sinh(sa)/s]`, and differentiating in `σ` at `σ = ½` gives `ĝ_1(τ) = 2Re[a cosh(sa)/s − sinh(sa)/s²]`.
With `e^{sa} = e^Tζ_τ` and `|e^{−sa}| = e^{−T}`:

**Lemma 2.1 (PROVED).** For all real `τ`, put `y(τ) := e^{−T}(ĝ_0, ĝ_1 − 2Tĝ_0)(τ)`. Then
`y(τ) = (Re(ζ_τ/s), −Re(ζ_τ/s²)) + ε(τ)` with `|ε(τ)| ≤ 12(T+1)e^{−2T}`. Hence, for `b ∈ ℝ²` and
`β_0 := b_0 + 2Tb_1`, `β_1 := −b_1` (a bijection of `ℝ²` for fixed `T`),

`c_b(τ) := b_0ĝ_0(τ) + b_1ĝ_1(τ) = e^T Re[ζ_τ r_β(s)] + e^Tθ(τ)`, with `r_β(s) := β_0/s + β_1/s²` and
`|θ(τ)| ≤ 12(T+1)e^{−2T}|β|`.

*Proof.* `ĝ_0 = e^TRe(ζ/s) − e^{−T}Re(ζ̄/s)`, and `|s| ≥ ½` gives an error `≤ 2e^{−T}`.
`ĝ_1 = e^T[2TRe(ζ/s) − Re(ζ/s²)] + ε_1` with `|ε_1| ≤ (4T+4)e^{−T}`. Subtract `2Tĝ_0` and multiply by `e^{−T}`:
the errors are `≤ 2e^{−2T}` and `≤ (8T+4)e^{−2T}`, and their Euclidean norm is `≤ 12(T+1)e^{−2T}`. Then
`b_0ĝ_0 + b_1ĝ_1 = (b_0+2Tb_1)ĝ_0 + b_1(ĝ_1 − 2Tĝ_0) = e^T[β_0y_0 − β_1y_1]`. Substitute. ∎

**Constants.** For `τ ≠ 0` put `q(τ) := min_{|β|=1}|r_β(½+iτ)|`. Since
`|β_0s + β_1|² = β_0²(¼+τ²) + β_0β_1 + β_1² = βᵀQ_τβ` with `det Q_τ = τ² > 0`, we get
`q(τ) = √λ_min(Q_τ)/|s|² > 0`. For `τ_1, τ_F ≠ 0` put `K(τ_1,τ_F) := sup_{β≠0}|r_β(s_1)|/|r_β(s_F)|`. This is
`≤ (|s_F|²/|s_1|²)√(λ_max(Q_{τ_1})/λ_min(Q_{τ_F})) < ∞`, because `r_β(s_F) = 0` would force `s_F = −β_1/β_0 ∈ ℝ`.
Both are continuous on `{τ ≠ 0}`. Also `|∂_τ r_β(½+iτ)| = |β_0/s² + 2β_1/s³| ≤ 4√17|β|`.

### 2.2 The inequality

**Theorem PT (PROVED; unconditional).** Fix `τ_1, τ_F ≠ 0`. Let `I := [τ_F − π/2T, τ_F + π/2T]` and define

`θ_T := 1/K(τ_1,τ_F) − (26/T + 12(T+1)e^{−2T})/q(τ_1)`, `η_T := 12(T+1)e^{−2T}/q(τ_1)`, `k_T := θ_T/(1+η_T)`.

Assume `k_T > 0`, which holds for `T ≥ T_0(τ_1,τ_F)`. Then for **every** function `f: ℝ → ℝ` and every `b ∈ ℝ²`,

`μ := inf_τ [f(τ) + b_0ĝ_0(τ) + b_1ĝ_1(τ)] ≤ (sup_I f + k_T f(τ_1)) / (1 + k_T).`

*Proof.* Write `c = c_b` and `R := e^T|r_β(s_1)|`, so that `|β| ≤ e^{−T}R/q(τ_1)`.
(1) By Lemma 2.1, `c(τ_1) ≤ R + e^T|θ(τ_1)| ≤ R(1+η_T)`.
(2) If `β = 0`, then `c ≡ 0` and `μ ≤ min(f(τ_1), sup_I f)`, which is below the right-hand side. Otherwise
`r_β(s_F) ≠ 0`. As `τ` runs over `I`, `2Tτ` runs over an interval of length `2π`, so some `τ* ∈ I` has
`Re[ζ_{τ*}r_β(s_F)] = −|r_β(s_F)|`. Then `|r_β(s*) − r_β(s_F)| ≤ 4√17|β|·π/2T ≤ 26|β|/T`, and Lemma 2.1 gives
`c(τ*) ≤ −e^T|r_β(s_F)| + e^T|β|(26/T + 12(T+1)e^{−2T}) ≤ −R·θ_T`,
using `|r_β(s_F)| ≥ |r_β(s_1)|/K`.
*Case `μ ≥ f(τ_1)`.* From (1), `R(1+η_T) ≥ c(τ_1) ≥ μ − f(τ_1) ≥ 0`. So
`μ ≤ F(τ*) ≤ sup_I f − Rθ_T ≤ sup_I f − k_T(μ − f(τ_1))`, which rearranges to the claim.
*Case `μ < f(τ_1)`.* Here `μ ≤ F(τ*) ≤ sup_I f`, because `c(τ*) ≤ 0`. So `μ ≤ min(f(τ_1), sup_I f)`, which is
`≤` the convex combination on the right. ∎

*Reading.* The two primitive multipliers of (D) are, up to `O(1/T)`, a single oscillation `e^{2iTτ}` with a slowly
varying complex amplitude `e^T r_β(s)` drawn from a 2-real-parameter family that never vanishes off the real axis.
They cannot be localised. Lifting a dip of depth `A` at `τ_1` costs a trough of depth `≳ A/K` inside every phase
window `I`, anywhere on the line. So a primitive certificate needs `sup_I f ≳ A/K` on **every** window of length
`π/T`. This is the exact content of G2_B4_R4 Lemma E3 ("the multipliers are window-edge atoms"), now with no
locality assumption. Theorem PT applies verbatim to the full dual (D), since `f = s_T + ν̂ + 2ĝ_0` is arbitrary.

## §3 The resolution criterion and the no-go for resolving rules

**Setting (under RH).** Let `γ_1 > 0` be an ordinate of multiplicity `m_1 ≥ 1`. (Under RH ordinates exist
(Hardy) and are discrete; numerically `γ_1 = 14.1347…`, `m_1 = 1`, CITED, but only `m_1 ≥ 1` is used.) Let
`d_1 := min{|γ − γ_1| : γ ∈ Ord ∖ {γ_1}}`; the multiset `Ord` is symmetric, so `d_1 ≤ 2γ_1`. Let `d_1^+` be the
gap to the next ordinate above `γ_1`, and put `τ_F := γ_1 + d_1^+/2` and `δ_F := dist(τ_F, Ord) = d_1^+/2 ≥ d_1/2`.
The compact set `J_1 := [γ_1 − d_1/4, γ_1 + d_1/4]` lies in `[γ_1/2, ∞)`. By continuity,
`q_* := min_{J_1}q > 0` and `K_* := max_{τ_1∈J_1}K(τ_1,τ_F) < ∞`, so `k_T ≥ k_* := 1/(2K_*)` for `T ≥ T_0`,
uniformly in `τ_1 ∈ J_1`. Put
`S_1′ := (16/9)Σ_{γ≠γ_1}m_γ(γ−γ_1)^{−2}` and `S_F′ := 4Σ_γ m_γ(τ_F−γ)^{−2}`. Both are finite because
`N(t) ≪ t log t`.

**Corollary RC (PROVED under RH).** Let `T ≥ max(T_0, π/δ_F)`. Let `w ∈ 𝒲_T` satisfy the zero-side
representation `f_w(τ) = Σ_γ ŵ(τ−γ)`, absolutely convergent: this holds for `w ∈ 𝒲_T^{reg}` (thm:Z(ii)) and for
the class of Lemma Z′ (G2_B4_R3 §4). Assume
(N) `ŵ(x_1) ≤ −N` for some `|x_1| ≤ d_1/4`, and
(D) `|ŵ(x)| ≤ D x^{−2}` for all `|x| ≥ d_1/4`.
If `k_* m_1 N > D(S_F′ + k_*S_1′)`, then for every `β ∈ ℝ²`,
`inf_τ[f_w + (β_0−2)ĝ_0 + β_1ĝ_1] ≤ [D S_F′ − k_*(m_1N − DS_1′)]/(1+k_*) < 0`.
So `(w, β)` is not a primitive certificate of value `≥ 0`, and a fortiori (at `β = (2,0)`) not a complete one.

*Proof.* Take `τ_1 := γ_1 + x_1 ∈ J_1`. For `γ ≠ γ_1`: `|τ_1−γ| ≥ |γ−γ_1| − d_1/4 ≥ ¾|γ−γ_1| ≥ ¾d_1`. So by (D),
`f_w(τ_1) ≤ m_1ŵ(x_1) + DS_1′ ≤ −m_1N + DS_1′`. For `τ ∈ I`: `|τ−γ| ≥ |τ_F−γ| − π/2T ≥ |τ_F−γ|/2 ≥ δ_F/2 ≥ d_1/4`,
so `sup_I f_w ≤ DS_F′`. Apply Theorem PT with `b = (β_0−2, β_1)`, and write `B(k) := (s + k f_1)/(1+k)` with
`s = sup_I f_w` and `f_1 = f_w(τ_1)`. If `f_1 ≤ s`, then `B` is nonincreasing in `k`, so
`μ ≤ B(k_T) ≤ B(k_*)`. If `f_1 > s`, then `μ ≤ s ≤ B(k_*)` (proof of PT, second case). Finally `B(k_*)` is
increasing in `s` and in `f_1`, so
`B(k_*) ≤ (DS_F′ + k_*(−m_1N + DS_1′))/(1+k_*)`. ∎

**Definition 3.1 (resolving rule).** A rule `T ↦ w_T ∈ 𝒲_T` (with the zero-side representation for each `T`) is
*resolving* if there are points `x_T → 0` such that, with `N_T := −ŵ_T(x_T)` and
`D_T(ε) := sup_{|x|≥ε}x²|ŵ_T(x)|`, we have `N_T/D_T(ε) → +∞` for every fixed `ε > 0`.
The definition refers only to the kernels `ŵ_T`. It is prime-free and zero-free.

**Theorem RN (PROVED; unconditional).** For a resolving rule there is `T* < ∞` such that for every `T ≥ T*` and
every `β ∈ ℝ²`, `inf_τ[f_{w_T} + (β_0−2)ĝ_0 + β_1ĝ_1] < 0`. So the rule is not a primitive certificate family on
any half-line, and hence not a complete one either. `T*` is not effective.

*Proof.* Under RH: take `ε = d_1/4`, `N = N_T`, `D = D_T(d_1/4)`. For large `T`, `|x_T| ≤ d_1/4` and
`N_T/D_T > (S_F′ + k_*S_1′)/(k_*m_1)`, so apply Cor. RC. Under ¬RH: the primitive form of Weil's criterion
(P46 l.140–146; CITED: Paper 45 Thm 10.4, Bombieri 2000 Thm 1) gives `T_1` with `λ^A_{T_1} < 0`. By `lem:monoT`,
`λ^A_T < 0` for all `T ≥ T_1`, and by `prop:weak` every certificate value is `≤ λ^P_T ≤ λ^A_T < 0`. ∎

**Corollary N (PROVED under RH; a necessary condition).** For `T ≥ max(T_0, π/δ_F)`, every primitive certificate
`(w, β)` of value `≥ 0` with the zero-side representation satisfies
`m_1 N(w) ≤ D(w)(S_F′ + k_*S_1′)/k_*`, where `N(w) := −min_{|x|≤d_1/4}ŵ` and `D(w) := sup_{|x|≥d_1/4}x²|ŵ|`.
*Proof.* Contrapositive of Cor. RC. ∎
Under RH the certificates of `cor:RH`/`thm:Zexh(ii)` exist for every `T`, so they are necessarily
**non-resolving**: the near-zero negativity of their kernel, which `lem:Rrig` forces somewhere, is bounded by a
fixed multiple of its far-field size. This constrains *what* the flat-kernel form of RH asks for. It does not
decide it.

## §4 Applications: every rule with a finite-order scale is resolving

### 4.1 Edge rules

Let `φ: [0,∞) → ℝ` with `φ(0) = 1`, `φ` absolutely continuous, `φ′` of bounded variation, and superexponential
decay of `φ, φ′, dφ′` (compact support suffices). Then every dilate `φ(·/a)` satisfies def:W and Lemma Z′. Put
`V_φ := |φ′(0+)| + |dφ′|((0,∞))`, `M_1 := ∫_0^∞t|φ′(t)|dt`, `Φ_1(z) := ∫_0^∞φ′(t)e^{izt}dt` (so `Φ_1(0) = −1`),
and `J(z) := ∫_{(0,∞)}e^{izt}dφ′(t)`. The edge rule is `w_T(u) = 1` for `|u| ≤ 2T` and `φ((|u|−2T)/a_T)`
otherwise, with `a_T > 0`.

**Lemma 4.1 (PROVED).** (i) `ŵ_T(x) = (2/x)Re[ie^{2iTx}Φ_1(a_Tx)] = −(2/(a_Tx²))Re[e^{2iTx}(φ′(0+) + J(a_Tx))]`.
(ii) `|ŵ_T(x)| ≤ 2V_φ/(a_Tx²)` for all `x ≠ 0`.
(iii) With `ρ := a_T/2T` and `Φ̂_ρ(ξ) := (2/ξ)Re[ie^{iξ}Φ_1(ρξ)]`, we have `ŵ_T(ξ/2T) = 2TΦ̂_ρ(ξ)`. Here `Φ̂_ρ` is
the transform of `Φ_ρ(v) = 1_{|v|≤1} + φ((|v|−1)/ρ)1_{|v|>1}`, and `Φ̂_0(ξ) = 2sinξ/ξ`.
(iv) `‖Φ̂_ρ − Φ̂_{ρ′}‖_∞ ≤ 2M_1|ρ−ρ′|`.

*Proof.* (i) `ŵ_T = 2∫_0^∞w_T cos(ux)du`. Integrate by parts once (the boundary terms vanish):
`= −(2/x)∫_{2T}^∞a^{−1}φ′((u−2T)/a)sin(ux)du = −(2/x)Im[e^{2iTx}Φ_1(ax)]`. For the second form, integrate
`Φ_1` by parts: `Φ_1(z) = (i/z)(φ′(0+) + J(z))`. (ii) follows from `|J| ≤ |dφ′|`. (iii) is the change of
variable `u = 2Tv` and the same integration by parts. (iv) follows from `|Φ_1(z) − Φ_1(z′)| ≤ M_1|z−z′|`. ∎

**Corollary E1 (PROVED).** If `T·a_T → ∞` and `a_T ≤ 2RT` for some `R`, the edge rule is resolving. Hence
(Theorem RN) it fails in both forms at all `T ≥ T*`, whatever `β(T)`. Under RH, if also `a_T ≥ a_0 > 0`, the
depth is `≥ cT` for large `T` (by the RC bound `≥ [k_*(2Tm_1κ_R − DS_1′) − DS_F′]/(1+k_*)` with `D = 2V_φ/a_0`). This covers the fixed profile (`a_T ≡ 1`, G2_B4_R2 Theorem R, now upgraded to
all large `T`), every bounded edge scale, and `a_T = o(T)`. Those were the two OPEN primitive cases of R4.

*Proof.* The function `Φ̂_ρ` is the transform of a finite even measure equal to `dv` on `(−1,1)`. By R4 Lemma E1
(or `lem:Rrig` for `ρ > 0`; for `ρ = 0` directly, since `2sinξ/ξ < 0` at `ξ = 3π/2`), `κ(ρ) := −min Φ̂_ρ > 0`.
By (iv), `κ` is `2M_1`-Lipschitz, so `κ_R := min_{[0,R]}κ > 0`. At a minimiser `ξ_ρ`,
`κ_R ≤ |Φ̂_ρ(ξ_ρ)| ≤ 2‖φ′‖_1/|ξ_ρ|`, so `|ξ_ρ| ≤ X_R := 2‖φ′‖_1/κ_R`. Take `x_T := ξ_{ρ_T}/2T → 0`. Then
`N_T ≥ 2Tκ_R`, and by (ii) `D_T(ε) ≤ 2V_φ/a_T` for every `ε`. So `N_T/D_T(ε) ≥ κ_R T a_T/V_φ → ∞`. ∎

**Corollary E2 (PROVED; finite-order joins, no upper bound on `a_T`).** Suppose that for some `k ≥ 1`,
`φ^{(j)}(0+) = 0` for `1 ≤ j < k`, `c_k := φ^{(k)}(0+) ≠ 0`, and `φ^{(k)}` has an absolutely continuous derivative
on `(0,∞)` with `φ^{(k+1)} ∈ L¹` and superexponential decay of `φ^{(j)}`, `j ≤ k+1`. Then the edge rule is
resolving for **every** `a_T` with `T a_T → ∞`.

*Proof.* Integrating by parts `k` times gives `Φ_1(z) = (i/z)^k(c_k + J_k(z))`, with
`J_k(z) = ∫_0^∞φ^{(k+1)}(t)e^{izt}dt → 0` (Riemann–Lebesgue) and `|J_k| ≤ ‖φ^{(k+1)}‖_1`. So
`ŵ_T(x) = (2/(a^kx^{k+1}))Re[i^{k+1}e^{2iTx}(c_k + J_k(ax))]`, and
`|ŵ_T(x)| ≤ 2V_k/(a^k|x|^{k+1})` with `V_k := |c_k| + ‖φ^{(k+1)}‖_1`. Choose `z_0` with `|J_k(z)| ≤ |c_k|/2` for
`z ≥ z_0`, and put `R := 2z_0/π`.
*If `ρ_T ≤ R`:* use Corollary E1.
*If `ρ_T > R`:* pick `j ∈ {1,2,3,4}` with `i^{k+1+j} = −sgn c_k`, and put `x_T := jπ/4T ≤ π/T`. Then `e^{2iTx_T} = i^j`
and `a x_T ≥ πρ_T/2 ≥ z_0`, so `ŵ_T(x_T) ≤ −|c_k|/(a^kx_T^{k+1}) ≤ −|c_k|(T/π)^{k+1}/a^k`. Also
`D_T(ε) ≤ 2V_kε^{1−k}/a^k`. So `N_T/D_T(ε) ≥ |c_k|ε^{k−1}T^{k+1}/(2π^{k+1}V_k) → ∞`, independently of `a_T`.
Both cases hold along any sequence, so the rule is resolving. ∎

### 4.2 Dilated rules (re-proof of R4 Theorem R′_prim)

**Corollary D (PROVED).** Let `Φ` be as in G2_B4_R3 Def. 2.1 and `w_T = Φ(·/2T)`. Then
`ŵ_T(x) = 2TΦ̂(2Tx)`. Take `x_T := x_0/2T`, where `Φ̂(x_0) = −κ_Φ < 0` (`lem:Rrig`); this gives `N_T = 2Tκ_Φ`.
From `|Φ̂(ξ)| ≤ ‖Φ′‖_BV ξ^{−2}` we get `D_T(ε) ≤ ‖Φ′‖_BV/(2T)`. So `N_T/D_T ≍ T² → ∞`: the rule is resolving, and
Theorem R′_prim follows from Theorem RN with depth `≍ T`. Lemmas E2 and E3 of R4 are no longer needed.

### 4.3 What is not covered (OPEN)

* **`C^∞`-flat joins with `a_T/T` unbounded.** Then `Φ_1(z)` decays faster than every power. Both `N_T` and
  `D_T` are super-polynomially small, and the location of the negativity forced by `lem:Rrig` is not controlled.
  **OPEN.** HEURISTIC: `N_T` is sampled at `|x| ≍ 1/T` and `D_T` at `|x| ≥ ε`. For monotonically decaying `|Φ_1|`
  the ratio should still diverge, but a sign on the near lobe is needed, and I have none.
* **Degenerate edge scale `a_T = O(1/T)`.** The kernel approaches the sharp cut, and the far-field bound
  `2V_φ/(a_Tx²)` is too weak. **OPEN** (thm:arena/prop:finite territory; not claimed).
* **Rules without a scale.** A free per-window `w_T` is `thm:Zexh`/`cor:RH`: **EQUIVALENT**. By Cor. N, under RH
  such certificates are non-resolving at every large `T`.

## §5 Positivstellensatz reading (bookkeeping)

In SOS language, a primitive certificate at `T` is `(w_T, β(T))` with `f_{w_T} + (β−2e_0)·ĝ ≥ 0` on `ℝ`. That is a
univariate positivity certificate in `τ` whose "multipliers" `ĝ_0, ĝ_1` are the images of the two primitivity
constraints (def:PD). Theorem PT says that these multipliers carry exactly one degree of freedom that matters at
scale `π/T`, a complex amplitude of a fixed carrier `e^{2iTτ}`. Because the amplitude is slowly varying, the
multipliers are **global**. Hence:

* An SOS certificate family defined by a formula with a scale (dilation, edge profile of finite order) is
  resolving and is excluded in both forms (Thm RN).
* The Chebyshev-constrained relative certificates of R4 Rem. PS reduce to the per-window problem, and the per-window
  problem with a free non-resolving choice is `cor:RH` (EQUIVALENT).
* The Smith/GCD identity (R3 Rem. G) remains the only exact all-`N` SOS identity with coefficients `Λ(d)`, and it is
  Beurling-generic (F4, `cor:void`).

So the angle's target, an explicit `Λ`-built SOS family valid for all `T` by an identity, would have to be
non-resolving and phase-balanced on the whole line at every `T`. No construction of that kind is known that does
not use the zeros (OT-5′ shadow).

## §7 What is genuinely new

1. **Theorem PT (PROVED, unconditional).** The primitive multipliers of (D) cannot be localised: for any
  objective, the primitive bottom is at most an explicit convex combination of the value at one point and the sup
  over any phase window of length `π/T`. The constants are explicit (`q`, `K`, `k_T`).
2. **Cor. RC, Def. 3.1, Thm RN.** A prime-free, zero-free kernel condition ("resolving") that excludes a certificate
  rule in both forms for all `T ≥ T*`. This is the theorem form of P46 `subsec:where`'s DIAGNOSTIC on individual
  zeros, now including the primitive multipliers.
3. **Cor. E1/E2/D.** These close the two OPEN primitive cases of R4 (bounded edge scale; `a_T = o(T)`). They
  extend the no-go to finite-order joins with arbitrary `a_T`, upgrade R2 Theorem R to permanent failure at depth
  `≍ T`, and re-prove R4 Theorem R′_prim in two lines.
4. **Cor. N.** Under RH, every primitive certificate of value `≥ 0` at large `T` is non-resolving. This is a
  structural necessary condition on the certificates of `cor:RH`.

Not new: Weil's criterion, `lem:monoT`, `prop:weak`, `lem:Rrig`, the explicit formula, Farkas/Riemann–Lebesgue.

## §8 Weakest step, and the honest verdict

* **Weakest step: the zero-side representation for the rules of §4.** RC needs `f_{w_T} = Σ_γŵ_T(τ−γ)`, absolutely
  convergent, for non-smooth edge weights. It rests on Lemma Z′ (G2_B4_R3 §4, accepted by the round-3 panel),
  applied to `φ(·/a_T)` for each fixed `T`. Its weighted total-variation hypothesis holds under superexponential
  decay, but the bound is not uniform in `a_T`. Uniformity is not needed, because RC is applied at each `T`
  separately. Second-weakest: the continuity of `q` and `K` on `J_1` and the resulting `T_0`. These are elementary,
  but the constants are not tracked numerically.
* **OPEN:** `C^∞`-flat joins with `a_T/T` unbounded; `a_T = O(1/T)`.
* **Verdict for G2.** No mechanism. The SOS/Positivstellensatz angle is closed in both forms for every resolving
  rule, and that includes every rule defined by a formula with a finite-order scale. What survives is the
  non-resolving, scale-free per-window choice of `cor:RH`, which is RH itself (**EQUIVALENT**). **Dead end for the
  mechanism, reported as such.** For the record I recommend Theorem PT next to `lem:gbounds`/def:PD, and
  Cor. RC with Thm RN next to `thm:Zexh(ii)`, as proved obstructions. They supersede R4 Lemmas E2–E3 and the
  almost-periodicity proof of R2 Theorem R.
