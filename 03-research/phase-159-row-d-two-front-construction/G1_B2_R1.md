## §0 Verdict in one paragraph

On the Deninger route, **OT-5′ turns out to be the cheap part**. In leaf dimension 2, the leafwise L² pairing of
1-forms depends only on the conformal structure of the leaves and on a transverse measure. Any leaf-preserving
conformal action that scales the transverse measure is therefore conformally unitary on leafwise harmonic 1-forms,
with the conformal factor equal to the transverse modulus (Prop 1, `PROVED`, elementary). No spectral input is
involved. Applied to Deninger's elliptic example (math/0204194, re-read in source), this shows that the "Hasse input"
Doc 130 found is a removable normalisation. The measure `μ̃` is `H`-invariant unconditionally, by a lattice index.
With the metric rescaled, the positivity holds with an intrinsic factor `β = log|ξ|²/log q`. `|ξ|² = q` is then needed
only to match the `H²` eigenvalue with the pole, i.e. in **OT-2**, and there it is a degree identity (Prop 2, `PROVED`
from the source + `CITED` classical facts). I then build the global arithmetic analogue: the **adelic Riemann-surface
solenoid** `Y_K = A_K/K` for `K` imaginary quadratic (Construction 3). It is compact, its leaves are copies of `C`, and
`K^×` acts on it by holomorphic automorphisms. The prior positivity is proved there from Haar measure and the product
formula alone, so OT-5′ holds, and OT-3 holds too. But the **dynamical suspension** along `log N(K^×)`, which is the
step that should turn Frobenius data into a flow with the zeros as spectrum, keeps only the **R-continuous part** of
the Frobenius representation. For every p-adic (adelic) realisation of Frobenius, that part is killed by mixing
(Theorem D, `PROVED`). The suspension of `Y_K` has exactly `H⁰ = C` (`Θ = 0`), `H¹ = 0`, `H² = C` (`Θ = 1`): the
polar part of ζ and no zero. The same computation for `Y_Q = A_Q/Q` gives the polar pair only. So on this carrier,
**OT-1 and OT-2 fail by a proved mechanism, not for lack of effort**. The mechanism gives a new necessary condition
**N-d**: the Hilbert space that carries the prior positivity must carry the Frobenius family as the restriction of a
strongly continuous R-representation, i.e. Frobenius must act *archimedeanly*. Connes' quotient by `Q^×` achieves
exactly this, and it pays with an infinite measure, continuous spectrum and a relative `H¹` (R158_002 Lemma A, M8 F1).
**No object is constructed. Status: partial new result, a dead end for the Deninger–solenoid class, with a sharper wall.**

## §1 Prior positivity is free in leaf dimension 2

**Setting.** `(Y, 𝓕)` is a lamination (a foliated space in the sense of Moore–Schochet) with oriented leaves of real
dimension 2, each carrying a conformal structure `c` that varies continuously transversally. There is **no metric**.
`ν` is a holonomy-invariant transverse measure, with Ruelle–Sullivan current `C_ν` (for a leafwise 2-form `β` that is
compactly supported and transversally continuous, `C_ν(β) = Σ_i ∫_{T_i} (∫_{plaque} ψ_iβ) dν`, with `ψ_i` a partition
of unity subordinate to flow boxes). A group `G` acts on `Y` by homeomorphisms `m_g` that map leaves to leaves by
orientation-preserving conformal diffeomorphisms, and `(m_g)_*C_ν := C_ν ∘ m_g^* = δ(g)C_ν` for a homomorphism
`δ: G → R_{>0}` (the **transverse modulus**).

**Definition.** For leafwise 1-forms `ω, η` (complex, transversally continuous, leafwise smooth, L²), set
`⟨ω, η⟩ := C_ν(ω ∧ ⋆\bar η)`. Here `⋆` is the leafwise Hodge star on 1-forms, which in dimension 2 depends only on
`c` (rotation by `+90°`). Complete to `L²Ω¹_𝓕`. Let `Harm¹ := ker d_𝓕,max ∩ ker d*_𝓕,max`.

**Proposition 1 (conformal relocation; `PROVED`, elementary).**
(a) `⟨·,·⟩` is a positive-definite hermitian form that depends only on `(c, ν)`.
(b) `⟨m_g^*ω, m_g^*η⟩ = δ(g)⟨ω, η⟩` for all `g`.
(c) `m_g^*` preserves `Harm¹`, and `U(g) := δ(g)^{-1/2}m_g^*` is a unitary representation of `G` on `Harm¹`. By the
weak Kodaira decomposition `L²Ω¹ = Harm¹ ⊕ cl(d Ω⁰) ⊕ cl(d*Ω²)`, `Harm¹` is the reduced L² leafwise cohomology
`H̄¹_{(2)}`.
(d) If `G = R` acts as a flow, with `δ(t) = e^{αt}`, then the generator `Θ` of `m_t^*` on `H̄¹_{(2)}` is
`α/2 + (skew-adjoint)`. So every eigenvalue of `Θ` on `H̄¹_{(2)}` has real part `α/2`.

*Proof.* (a) Pointwise, for a real 2-dimensional oriented conformal vector space and a covector `ω`,
`ω ∧ ⋆\bar ω = |ω|²_g vol_g` for any metric `g ∈ c`. The right side is invariant under `g ↦ λg`: `|ω|²` scales by
`λ^{-1}` and `vol` by `λ`. So `ω ∧ ⋆\bar ω` is a nonnegative 2-form, it depends only on `c`, and it is zero only where
`ω = 0`. `C_ν` of a nonnegative leafwise 2-form is `≥ 0`, and it is `> 0` unless the form vanishes `ν`-almost
everywhere on leaves. (b) An orientation-preserving conformal map commutes with `⋆` on 1-forms, so
`m_g^*ω ∧ ⋆ m_g^*\bar η = m_g^*(ω ∧ ⋆\bar η)`. Then `C_ν(m_g^*β) = δ(g)C_ν(β)` by the definition of `δ`. (c) For 1-forms in leaf dimension 2, `Harm¹ = {ω : d_𝓕ω = 0, d_𝓕⋆ω = 0 weakly}`, since `d* = −⋆d⋆` on 1-forms and the pairing of (a) is conformally invariant. `m_g^*` commutes with `d_𝓕` and with `⋆`, so it preserves `Harm¹`. Unitarity of `U` is (b). The identification with reduced L² cohomology uses the weak Kodaira decomposition. That needs a leafwise metric on 0- and 2-forms, which can be chosen freely in `c`, because `Harm¹` does not depend on the choice. (d) By (c), `e^{-αt/2}m_t^*` is a
strongly continuous unitary group, and Stone's theorem gives the rest. ∎

**Relation to Deninger's normalisation (`PROVED` by rewriting).** Deninger (math/0204110 Thm 2.1; math/0204194 §3)
uses a leafwise metric `g` with `g(Tφ^t v, Tφ^t w) = e^{αt}g(v,w)` and a fixed measure `μ` on the ambient space. For
1-forms, `|ω|²_g dμ = (ω ∧ ⋆\bar ω) ⊗ ν_g`, where `ν_g := dμ / vol_g` is the induced transverse measure. So Deninger's
conformal factor on the leaf **is** a transverse modulus. In leaf dimension 2 the leafwise metric can be discarded, and
the factor lives entirely in `ν`.

**Reading (`HEURISTIC`).** For a Deninger-type object with 2-dimensional leaves, (D3) of Lemma T (R158_001 §3.1) is not
an axiom about the spectrum. It is the existence of a transverse measure with modulus `e^t`, i.e. a Ruelle–Sullivan
class `[C_ν] ∈ H̄²` with `φ^t_*[C_ν] = e^{t}[C_ν]`: **the `H²` eigenvalue, which is the pole.** Leichtnam's axiom 5]
with (15) is thus implied by "`H²` is spanned by a transverse-measure class with eigenvalue `1`" plus conformality of
the leaves. That is geometric data, defined before the spectrum. OT-5′ is met *by design* on any such carrier. The
whole content of Lemma T(⇒) moves to OT-1 + OT-2: an `H̄¹_{(2)}` with discrete spectrum equal to the zeros, and a
trace formula equal to ζ's explicit formula.

**Proposition B (the modulus is forced by the trace formula; `PROVED` modulo Lemma U).** In the setting of Prop 1(d),
suppose `H⁰ = C` (`Θ = 0`), `H² = C[C_ν]` (`Θ = α`), and that `Θ` on `H̄¹_{(2)}` has discrete spectrum `{s_j}` with
multiplicities `n_j ≥ 1`. Suppose the Lefschetz identity
`Φ_h(0) − Σ_j n_j Φ_h(s_j) + Φ_h(α) = Φ_h(0) + Φ_h(1) − Σ_ρ m_ρ Φ_h(ρ)` holds for all `h ∈ C_c^∞(R)`, with
`Φ_h(s) = ∫h e^{ts}dt` (the explicit formula, OT-2). Then `α = 1` and `{(s_j, n_j)} = {(ρ, m_ρ)}`. In particular RH
holds.
*Proof.* By **Lemma U** (uniqueness of exponential-sum expansions: if `Σ_s c_s Φ_h(s) = 0` for all `h`, where `s`
ranges over a discrete set in a vertical strip and `Σ|c_s|(1+|s|)^{-N} < ∞`, then every `c_s = 0`), compare
coefficients. If `α ≠ 1`, then `s = 1` has coefficient `+1` on the right. On the left it can only come from `−n_j`,
with `n_j ≥ 1`, which is impossible. So `α = 1`, and the rest is coefficient comparison. RH then follows from
Prop 1(d). ∎
*Label of Lemma U:* standard (it follows from the injectivity of the Laplace transform of such exponential sums, as
used to recover the zeros from the Weil distribution); **not re-proved here**. So Prop B is `PROVED` conditional on
Lemma U. It carries no new content beyond Lemma T(⇒). It is recorded because it shows *where* the pole enters: in
OT-2, never in the definition of the form.

## §2 Deninger's elliptic example re-read: the "Hasse input" is removable

Source: C. Deninger, *On the nature of the explicit formulas in analytic number theory — a simple example*,
math/0204194 (pdftotext, this phase), §3. Notation as in the source: `Γ ⊂ C` is the period lattice of the lift,
`ξ ∈ K ⊂ C` is the lift of Frobenius, `X̃ = C × V_ξΓ × R`, and `H = V ⋊ Λ` with `Λ = (log q)Z`, acting by
`(z, v̂, t)·λ = (ξ^{−ν}z, ξ^{−ν}v̂, t + ν log q)`. The measure is `μ̃ = dx dy ⊗ μ_ξ ⊗ dt`, and the metric is
`g̃ = e^t Re(ξ\barη)` on tangent vectors.

**Proposition 2 (`PROVED` from the source text; the classical facts in (iii) are `CITED`).**
(i) **`μ̃` is `H`-invariant without Hasse.** The source proves `μ_ξ(ξ^νA) = |ξ|^{−2ν}μ_ξ(A)` from
`#(T_ξΓ/ξ^νT_ξΓ) = #(Γ/ξ^νΓ) = |ξ|^{2ν}`. This is a lattice index, the determinant of multiplication by `ξ` on
`Γ ⊗ R = C`. Under `λ`, `dx dy` scales by `|ξ|^{−2ν}` and `μ_ξ` by `|ξ|^{2ν}`, and `dt` is translation-invariant. So
`μ̃` is invariant. The second equality in the source, `= q^{−ν}μ_ξ(A)`, is where Hasse is quoted. The invariance of
`μ̃` does not use it. (This corrects Doc 130 §2.2/§5 and R158_004 row 107 on `μ̃`.)
(ii) **Only `g̃` uses `|ξ|² = q`, and a rescaling removes it.** Define `β := log|ξ|²/log q` (intrinsic: the lattice
index of Frobenius, measured against the orbit length `log q`) and `g̃_β := e^{βt}Re(ξ\barη)`. Then `g̃_β` is
`H`-invariant unconditionally, since `|ξ^{−ν}|²e^{βν log q} = 1`. The source's own computation, with `e^t` replaced by
`e^{βt}`, shows that `exp(−½jβt)φ^{t*}` is orthogonal on `Harm^j_{L²}(X)`. That is Prop 1(b)–(c) with
`δ(t) = e^{βt}`. So **the positivity (D3) holds, with conformal factor `e^{βt}`, before anything is known about
`|ξ|`**.
(iii) **The spectrum is computed without positivity, and Hasse enters only through `H²`.** The source (Thm 3.1 and its
proof) finds the `H¹` eigenvalues `Log_q ξ + 2πiν/log q` and `Log_q \barξ + 2πiν/log q`, with real part
`log|ξ|/log q = β/2`. This part does not use the metric. With `g̃_β`, the `H²` eigenvalues become
`β + 2πiν/log q`. The transversal index equals the explicit formula of `ζ̂_E` (Cor 3.2) exactly when `β = 1`, i.e.
`|ξ|² = q`. And `|ξ|² = q` is the degree identity `deg Θ(ξ) = [Γ : ξΓ] = |ξ|²`, combined with `deg(Frob_q) = q` and
invariance of the degree under the lift (Deuring/Serre–Tate canonical lift; `CITED`, classical). That is a proof of
Hasse in this setting, not an assumption of it.

**Consequence (`HEURISTIC`, but the steps are the proved (i)–(iii)).** In the only realised arithmetic case, the
positive form is prior to the spectrum. It is the Euclidean area structure of the lift's Lie algebra `C` (the Riemann
form of `Γ`) together with a lattice index. It passes OT-5′. The identification of its conformal factor with the pole
is an OT-2 statement, and it is proved there by geometry (degree). So Doc 130's "biased to CIRCULAR" concerns a
normalisation choice of the source and should be revised: **the example is Weil-shaped**. This does *not* transfer to
`Spec Z`: §3–§4 show what breaks.

## §3 The global carrier: the adelic Riemann-surface solenoid `Y_K`

**Construction 3 (`PROVED` as a construction).** Let `K` be `Q` or an imaginary quadratic field, with `d = [K:Q]` and
`K_∞ = K ⊗ R` (`= R`, resp. `C`). Set
`Y_K := A_K / K` (the additive adèle class group, compact; Tate's thesis: `K` is discrete and cocompact in `A_K`, and
the Pontryagin dual of `A_K/K` is `K` via a standard character `ψ`; `CITED`, classical).
* **Leaves.** The cosets `y + K_∞`. The map `K_∞ → Y_K`, `z ↦ y + z`, is injective, because `K ∩ ({0} × A_{K,f}) = {0}`.
  So every leaf is a copy of `K_∞`, of real dimension `d` (a Riemann surface `≅ C` when `d = 2`). Every leaf is dense,
  since `K_∞ + K` is dense in `A_K` by strong approximation. The transversals are open subsets of `A_{K,f}` modulo
  `O_K`-type lattices: totally disconnected.
* **Transverse measure.** Haar measure on `A_{K,f}`, which is holonomy-invariant because holonomy acts by translations.
  Haar measure on `Y_K` is (leaf Lebesgue) ⊗ (transverse Haar), up to normalisation.
* **Frobenius action.** `K^×` acts by `m_κ(y) = κy`. Each `m_κ` is an automorphism of the compact group (`κK = K`),
  hence preserves normalised Haar measure on `Y_K`. It maps leaves to leaves by `z ↦ κ_∞z`, which is holomorphic for
  `d = 2` (and a dilation for `d = 1`). Its modulus on leafwise top-degree forms is
  `C_ν ∘ m_κ^* = N(κ)·C_ν`: for `β = f·vol` one has `m_κ^*β = (f∘m_κ)|κ_∞|^{(d)}vol`, with `|κ_∞|^{(2)} = |κ|²`,
  `|κ_∞|^{(1)} = |κ|`, and `∫f∘m_κ = ∫f` by Haar invariance. Equivalently, the product formula
  `|κ|_∞·|κ|_{A_f} = 1` moves the leafwise dilation into the transverse Haar measure.
* **Prior positive form.** The flat conformal structure on `K_∞` is canonical (the complex structure of `C`). Define
  `⟨ω, η⟩ := ∫_{Y_K} ω ∧ ⋆\barη` (for `d = 2`; for `d = 1`, the L² norm of 1-forms `f dx` with the canonical `dx`).
  **No zero, no `Λ(n)` and no spectral datum enters.**

**Theorem C (cohomology and Frobenius on `Y_K`; `PROVED`).**
(i) `L²Ω^j_𝓕(Y_K) = ⊕_{v∈K} Cχ_v ⊗ Λ^j(K_∞⊗C)^*`, with `χ_v(y) = ψ(vy)`. The leafwise differential acts on
`χ_v` by `∂_z χ_v = c v χ_v`, `∂_{\bar z}χ_v = c\bar v χ_v` (`c = −2πi`, `d = 2`), resp. `∂_x χ_v = c v χ_v`
(`d = 1`).
(ii) `Harm^j = C·1 ⊗ Λ^j(K_∞⊗C)^*`: only the constant forms. For `d = 2`, `H̄⁰ = C`, `H̄¹ = C dz ⊕ C d\bar z`,
`H̄² = C dz∧d\bar z`. For `d = 1`, `H̄⁰ = C`, `H̄¹ = C dx` (top degree). The unreduced `H¹` is non-Hausdorff, because
`K` is dense in `K_∞` for `d = 2` and in `R` for `d = 1`, so `im d_0` is not closed.
(iii) The action of `κ ∈ K^×` is: `m_κ^* = 1` on `H̄⁰`; `m_κ^* = diag(κ, \barκ)` on `H̄¹` (`d = 2`); `m_κ^* = N(κ)`
on `H̄^{top}`. By Prop 1, `U(κ) = N(κ)^{−j/2}m_κ^*` is unitary on `H̄^j` (`d = 2`), and
`|eigenvalue of m_κ^* on H̄¹|² = N(κ)`.
*Proof.* (i) Pontryagin duality for the compact group `Y_K`, tensored with the trivial bundle `Λ^j T*_𝓕`, which is
trivialised by the constant forms. `χ_v` restricted to a leaf is `z ↦ χ_v(y)ψ_∞(vz)`, and `ψ_∞(vz) = e^{−2πi Tr(vz)}`
gives the derivatives. (ii) For `ω = Σ(a_v dz + b_v d\bar z)χ_v`: `dω = 0` gives `v b_v = \bar v a_v`, and `d*ω = 0`
gives `\bar v a_v + v b_v = 0`. Hence `\bar v a_v = 0`, so `a_v = b_v = 0` for `v ≠ 0`. For 2-forms and 0-forms the
argument is the same, and the `d = 1` case is analogous. Non-closedness of `im d_0`: `‖dχ_v‖ ≍ |v| → 0` along `v ∈ K`,
`v → 0`. (iii) `m_κ^*χ_v = χ_{κv}`, `m_κ^*dz = κ dz`. ∎

**Reading (`PROVED` content, `HEURISTIC` name).** For `d = 2`, `H̄¹(Y_K)` with its `K^×`-action is the algebraic
character `κ ↦ κ` of infinity type `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, i.e. the `H¹` of a CM elliptic curve, on principal ideals.
Prior positivity gives `|κ|² = N(κ)`, a tautology. **On the compact solenoid the prior positivity controls the local
Frobenius parameters, not the zeros.** This is the global form of the elliptic example. There, the single Frobenius `ξ`
made the parameters and the zeros the same thing, by suspension along `Λ = Z log q`.

## §4 The suspension wall

For ζ the flow has to be produced from the Frobenius family: time `log N𝔭` must act as Frobenius at `𝔭`. The Deninger
recipe is the suspension, and the elliptic example is `X = (Y × R)/Λ` with `Λ ≅ Z`. For a number field, the length
group `ℓ(K^×) = log N(K^×) ⊂ R` is **dense**: it contains `log 4` and `log 9`, whose ratio is irrational. So the
geometric suspension is not Hausdorff, which is C5 of the record in its dynamical form. The best one can do is the
Hilbert-space suspension. Theorem D computes it.

**Theorem D (suspension along a dense length group; `PROVED`).** Let `G` be a countable abelian group, `ℓ: G → R` a
homomorphism, and `u` a unitary representation of `G` on a separable Hilbert space `H`. Set
`𝒮 := {f ∈ C(R, H) : f(t + ℓ(g)) = u(g)f(t) for all g ∈ G, t ∈ R}`, with the translation flow `(τ_s f)(t) = f(t+s)`.
(i) `f(t) ∈ H^{ker ℓ}` for every `f ∈ 𝒮` and every `t`.
(ii) Let `V_c := {w ∈ H^{ker ℓ} : ℓ(g) ↦ u(g)w is continuous at 0 for the topology of R on ℓ(G)}`. Then `V_c` is a
closed `u(G)`-invariant subspace. On it, `u` extends uniquely to a strongly continuous unitary representation `ũ` of
`R`, and `ev_0: 𝒮 → V_c` is a bijection intertwining `τ_s` with `ũ(s)`. So the spectrum of the suspended flow is the
spectrum of the generator of `ũ` on `V_c`.
(iii) **Mixing kills.** If `M ⊂ H` is a closed invariant subspace and there are `g_n` with `ℓ(g_n) → 0` and
`⟨u(g_n)x, y⟩ → 0` for all `x, y ∈ M`, then `V_c ⊥ M`.
(iv) The same conclusions hold for locally Bochner-integrable equivariant `f`, by mollification: `f_φ := ∫φ(s)f(·+s)ds`
is continuous and equivariant, and `f = 0` a.e. if every `f_φ = 0`.
*Proof.* (i) Take `g ∈ ker ℓ`. (ii) `f(ℓ(g)) = u(g)f(0)`, and `ℓ(G)` is dense if `ℓ ≠ 0` (the discrete case is the
elliptic example and is trivial), so `f` is determined by `w = f(0)`, and `ℓ(g) ↦ u(g)w = f(ℓ(g))` is continuous.
Since `‖u(g)w − u(g′)w‖ = ‖u(g−g′)w − w‖`, continuity at 0 is uniform continuity, so it extends to `R`. Closedness:
`‖u(g)w − w‖ ≤ ‖u(g)w_n − w_n‖ + 2‖w − w_n‖`. Invariance is clear, because the group is abelian. Conversely, every
`w ∈ V_c` gives `f(t) = ũ(t)w ∈ 𝒮`. (iii) For `w ∈ V_c`, write `w = w_M + w_⊥`, where `M^⊥` is invariant.
`u(g_n)w → w` strongly, so `u(g_n)w_M → w_M` strongly. But `u(g_n)w_M → 0` weakly, so `w_M = 0`. (iv) is standard. ∎

**Corollary D1 (the suspension of `Y_K` sees only the pole; `PROVED`).** Take `H = L²Ω^j_𝓕(Y_K)` (the full L²
complex, not only its harmonic part), `G = K^×`, `ℓ = log N`, and `u_j(κ) = N(κ)^{−j/d}m_κ^*` (unitary by Theorem C
and Haar invariance). Then:
* `V_c ⊂ C·1 ⊗ Λ^j`, the `v = 0` sector. *Proof:* on `M = ⊕_{v≠0}Cχ_v ⊗ Λ^j`, `⟨u(κ)χ_v e, χ_{v′}e′⟩ ≠ 0` only if
  `κv = v′`. So for pairwise distinct `κ_n` with `log N(κ_n) → 0` (they exist by density), the matrix coefficients
  vanish eventually on a dense set, hence everywhere by uniform boundedness. Apply (iii).
* On the `v = 0` sector, for `d = 2`: `j = 0`: `u = 1`, so `V_c = C` and the suspended `Θ = 0`. `j = 2`: `u = 1`, so
  `V_c = C` and, restoring the factor `e^{t}`, `Θ = 1`. `j = 1`: `u(κ) = diag(κ/|κ|, \barκ/|κ|)`, and `κ = −1 ∈ ker ℓ`
  acts by `−1`, so `H^{ker ℓ} = 0` and **`V_c = 0`**.
* For `d = 1` (`K = Q`): `V_c = C` in degrees 0 and 1 (top), with `Θ = 0, 1`.
So the suspended object has `H⁰ = C`, `H¹ = 0`, `H² = C`, with `Θ = 0, 1`: exactly the polar pair `P = {0, 1}` of
ζ (A3), and **no zero**. Its Lefschetz formula is `Φ_h(0) + Φ_h(1)`. That is the polar part of the explicit formula,
and it is zero-blind (C16). ∎

**Necessary condition N-d (proposed; `PROVED` for the Koopman class below, `HEURISTIC` in general).** *The Hilbert
space on which the prior positivity lives must carry the Frobenius family `{Frob_p}` as the restriction to
`log Q_{>0}` of a strongly continuous unitary representation of `R`, on the part where the zeros are to appear.*
Theorem D(ii) makes this necessary for any suspension. Theorem D(iii) shows that it fails wherever Frobenius acts
through p-adic geometry and the action is mixing. This covers every Koopman representation of `Q^×_{>0}` (resp.
`K^×`) on L² of a compact abelian solenoid with infinite dual orbits, with or without leafwise forms. The sequences
`2^{a}3^{−b} → 1` in `R` are p-adically huge, and that is the mixing.
**How N-d relates to the record (`HEURISTIC`).** Connes' multiplicative quotient `A_Q/Q^×` satisfies N-d by fiat: in
`C_Q = R_+^* × Ẑ^×` the idèle of `p` at `p` becomes archimedean scaling by `p^{−1}` times a compact rotation (the
product formula moves the p-adic size to `∞`). It pays for this with an infinite transverse measure (type II_∞),
continuous spectrum on `L²`, and zeros only in a relative cokernel (R158_002 Lemma A, §4.5; M8 F1/F5; D.191). So the
two routes of record sit on either side of N-d:

| route | prior positivity (OT-5′) | Frobenius R-continuous (N-d) | zeros in the positive space |
|---|---|---|---|
| compact adelic solenoid `Y_K` + suspension (this memo) | **yes** (Prop 1, Thm C) | **no** (Thm D, mixing) | no: `H¹ = 0` (Cor D1) |
| adèle class space `A_Q/Q^×` (R158_002) | no carrier for leafwise forms (Lemma A); definite forms ⟺ RH ∧ SZ | yes | only in the relative `H¹`; positivity = Weil (EQUIV) |
| Deninger elliptic (math/0204194) | yes (Prop 2) | yes: `Λ ≅ Z` discrete | yes (function field) |

An object for ζ would need the first column of row 1 **and** the second column of row 2 at once. No construction on
record, including this one, has both. Whether N-d and a finite-measure, discrete-spectrum prior positivity are
compatible over `Q` is `OPEN`. I do not claim an impossibility theorem.

## §6 What is new, and the weakest step

**New (to the corpus; literature status noted).**
1. Prop 1: in leaf dimension 2, the conformal factor of Deninger's (D3) is a transverse modulus (a Ruelle–Sullivan
  eigenvalue), so OT-5′ is met by design on any conformal lamination with a modular transverse measure. The
  ingredients are classical (conformal invariance of the 1-form Dirichlet form in dimension 2). I have not found this
  stated in math/0204110 or math/0204194, but I do not claim it is new to the literature.
2. Prop 2: correction of Doc 130 / R158_004 row 107. `μ̃` is `H`-invariant by a lattice index. The metric's use of
  Hasse is a normalisation, removable by `β`. Hasse enters only in OT-2 via `H²`, as a degree identity. The elliptic
  example is Weil-shaped.
3. Construction 3 + Theorem C: the adelic Riemann-surface solenoid `Y_K = A_K/K` as the global analogue of Deninger's
  example, with prior positivity from Haar measure and the product formula, and its full leafwise L² cohomology.
4. Theorem D + Cor D1: suspension along a dense length group keeps only the R-continuous part, and adelic mixing kills
  it. So the suspension of `Y_K` is exactly the polar pair. This yields the proposed necessary condition **N-d**
  (archimedean realisation of Frobenius on the positive space), which separates the two routes of record.

**Not new / not claimed.** No object for ζ, no trace formula with zeros, no sign at any window, no progress on the
"for all `T`" arrow. RH is not claimed and is not approached by any step here.

**Weakest step.** Two points. (a) N-d is proved necessary only for suspension-type constructions (Theorem D(ii)), and
shown to fail only for Koopman representations on compact abelian solenoids (Theorem D(iii), Cor D1). A non-abelian
carrier (a Shimura/modular solenoid with Hecke *correspondences* instead of automorphisms) is not covered. There the
leafwise `H̄¹` is infinite-dimensional (cusp forms), but Frobenius acts by non-unitary correspondences. My expectation
(`HEURISTIC`) is that the prior positivity there again controls Satake parameters (Ramanujan-type) and not zeros, and
that its suspension meets the same continuity wall. That is the natural round-2 test. (b) Prop 2(iii) relies on the
canonical-lift facts for the curves treated in math/0204194 (`CITED`, classical, not re-verified against the source's
hypotheses on `E`). Prop B relies on Lemma U (standard, not re-proved).

**Suggested round 2.** (1) Test N-d on the modular solenoid `GL_2(Q)\(H^± × GL_2(A_f))` with Hecke
correspondences: does the suspension of its leafwise `H̄¹_{(2)}` (weight-2 forms, Petersson norm, prior) retain
anything R-continuous? (2) Try to prove or refute the general dichotomy: *a unitary `Q^×_{>0}`-representation that is
R-continuous and arises from p-adic geometry on a finite-measure space is trivial on the mixing part*. This would turn
the §4 table into a theorem.
