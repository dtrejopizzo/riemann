## 2. Lemma T: the renormalisation identity between windows

Fix a real `h ∈ C_c^∞(−½,½)`, `h ≠ 0`, put `D := −∂² + ¼` and `Γ(y) := ∫Dh(t)Dh(t−y)dt` (even, real,
`supp Γ ⊂ (−1,1)`). Write `H(s) := ∫h(t)e^{−st}dt`, which is entire. For `x ≥ 2` put
```
f_x := Dh(· − x/2), g_x := Dh(· + x/2).
```

**Lemma T. PROVED; primitive form.** Let `x ≥ 2` and `T > x/2 + ½`. Then:
(a) `f_x, g_x ∈ P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`, `⟨f_x,g_x⟩ = 0`, `‖f_x‖ = ‖g_x‖ = ‖Dh‖`, and `𝔞_T(f_x) = 𝔞_T(g_x) = a₀ := 𝔞_1(Dh)`.
(b) The bilinear value `U(x) := 𝔞_T(f_x, g_x)` does not depend on `T`, and
```
U(x) = −½ ∫_1^∞ K(u) Γ(u − x) du − Σ_{n ≥ 2} w_n Γ(log n − x). (2.1)
```
(c) `|U(x)| ≤ a₀ + ‖Dh‖² · K⁻(x/2 + 1)`, where `K⁻(T) := max(0, −λ^A_T)`.
(d) `Γ̃(s) := ∫Γ(y)e^{−sy}dy = (¼ − s²)² H(s)H(−s)`. In particular `Γ̃(±½) = 0`, and
`U(x) = −∫Γ(y−x)e^{−y/2}d(ψ(e^y) − e^y) + O(e^{−x/2})`: `U` is the `Γ`-smoothed Chebyshev error at `e^x`.

*Proof.* (a) The supports lie in `(x/2 − ½, x/2 + ½)` and `(−x/2 − ½, −x/2 + ½)`. These are disjoint for `x ≥ 1` and
contained in `I_T`. Integrating by parts, `A(Dh(·−c)) = ∫h(t−c)De^{t/2}dt = 0`, since `De^{±t/2} = 0`. `𝔞_T(F)` depends
on `F` only through `c_F` (by (1.1)), `c_{f_x} = c_{Dh}` is supported in `(−1,1)`, and the only contact with
`log n < 1` is `n = 2`, which lies in `𝒩_1 ⊂ 𝒩_T`. So `𝔞_T(f_x) = 𝔞_1(Dh)`. The same holds for `g_x`.
(b) Polarising (1.1) (a real quadratic form, with `c_{f+g} − c_{f−g} = 2(f⋆g + g⋆f)` and `(g⋆f)(u) = (f⋆g)(−u)` for real
`f,g`, where `(f⋆g)(u) := ∫f(t)g(t−u)dt`) gives the bilinear form
`𝔞_T(f,g) = C_0⟨f,g⟩ + ∫_0^∞(⟨f,g⟩ − ½[(f⋆g)(u) + (f⋆g)(−u)])K du − Σ_{𝒩_T} w_n[(f⋆g)(log n) + (f⋆g)(−log n)]`.
Substituting `t' = t − x/2` gives `(f_x⋆g_x)(u) = Γ(u − x)`, which is supported in `(x−1, x+1) ⊂ (1, ∞)`. So the terms at
`−u` and `−log n` vanish, `⟨f_x,g_x⟩ = 0`, and every contact with `Γ(log n − x) ≠ 0` has `log n < x+1 < 2T`, so it lies in
`𝒩_T`. This is (2.1), which contains no `T`.
(c) `B_K(f,g) := 𝔞_T(f,g) + K⟨f,g⟩` with `K := K⁻(T)` is positive semidefinite on real `P_T ∩ C_c^∞`, by the
definition of `λ^A_T` and (D). Cauchy–Schwarz gives `|U(x)|² = |B_K(f_x,g_x)|² ≤ B_K(f_x,f_x)B_K(g_x,g_x) =
(a₀ + K‖Dh‖²)²`. Take `T = x/2 + 1`.
(d) With `y = t − r`, `Γ̃(s) = [∫Dh(t)e^{−st}dt][∫Dh(r)e^{sr}dr]`, and two integrations by parts give
`∫Dh e^{−st} = (¼ − s²)H(s)`. For the last formula, write `Σ w_nΓ(log n − x) = ∫Γ(y − x)e^{−y/2}dψ(e^y)`. Subtract
`∫Γ(y−x)e^{y/2}dy = e^{x/2}Γ̃(−½) = 0`. The `K`-integral is `O(e^{−x/2})` because `K(u) ≤ 2.4e^{−u/2}` for `u ≥ 1`. ∎

*Reading (the renormalisation relation).* This is the functional equation between windows that the angle asked for.
The new contacts that window `T+δ` adds (`log n ∈ [2T, 2T+2δ)`) enter the fixed, `T`-free profile `U` only at
`x ∈ (2T−1, 2T+2δ+1)`, and (c) says that the bottom at the window which first sees them bounds them. The profile is
*not* propagated from `T` to `T+δ`, which would be W15. It is read off once, at every scale, from the family of
bottoms.

## 3. Theorem G1: the lower half, two-sided transport proof

**Theorem G1. PROVED; primitive form (hence complete, since `λ^W ≤ λ^A`).** Let `α ≥ 0`, let `b` be real with
`b(1) = 0` and `Σ_n |b(n)| n^{−½−α} < ∞`, and let `a = Λ + b`. If there are `M, T₁` with `λ^A_T(a) ≥ −Me^{2αT}` for all
`T ≥ T₁`, then `ζ(z) ≠ 0` for `Re z > ½ + α`.

*Proof.* We may assume `α < ½`. Suppose `ζ(½ + s₀) = 0` with `Re s₀ > α`. Then `Re s₀ < ½` and `s₀ ≠ ±½`. The real-linear
functionals `h ↦ H(±s₀)` on real `C_c^∞(−½,½)` are nonzero, so we can choose `h` with `H(s₀)H(−s₀) ≠ 0`. A real vector
space is not a union of two proper subspaces.

*Growth.* Lemma T holds verbatim for `a`, with `w_n` replaced by `a(n)/√n` in (2.1) and `λ^A(a)` in (c). For
`T ≤ T₁` the trivial bound `λ^A_T(a) ≥ C_0 − Σ_{𝒩_T}2|a(n)|/√n` (P47 `lem:degen`, CITED) is finite and nonincreasing in
`T`. So `|U(x)| ≤ C e^{αx}` for all `x ≥ 2`. `U` is continuous, being a locally finite sum of continuous functions plus
a continuous integral. Hence `L(s) := ∫_2^∞ U(x)e^{−sx}dx` converges absolutely and is holomorphic on `Re s > α`.

*Identification on `Re s > ½`.* Here `Σ|a(n)|n^{−½−σ} < ∞` for `σ > ½`, so Fubini applies. For `n ≥ 21` (`log n > 3`),
`∫_2^∞Γ(log n − x)e^{−sx}dx = n^{−s}Γ̃(s)` (Γ even). For `n ≤ 20` the integral is over a compact set, so it is entire.
The `K`-term is holomorphic on `Re s > −½` by the bound in Lemma T(d). Hence
```
L(s) = Γ̃(s)·[ (ζ′/ζ)(½ + s) − Σ_n b(n) n^{−½−s} ] + E(s), Re s > ½, (3.1)
```
with `E` holomorphic on `Re s > −½`. The `b`-series converges absolutely, so it is holomorphic, on `Re s > α`.

*Continuation.* The right side of (3.1) is meromorphic on `Re s > α`, and the left side is holomorphic there. The two
agree on `Re s > ½`, so by the identity theorem `Γ̃(s)(ζ′/ζ)(½+s)` is holomorphic on `Re s > α`. The pole of `ζ′/ζ` at
`s = ½` is cancelled by `(¼ − s²)²`. At `s₀`, `(ζ′/ζ)(½+s)` has a simple pole whose residue is the multiplicity, while
`Γ̃(s₀) = (¼ − s₀²)²H(s₀)H(−s₀) ≠ 0`. Contradiction. ∎

**Corollary G2 (gap and coset form). PROVED.** (i) `κ^A(a) ≥ Θ − ½` for every `a ∈ Λ + ℓ¹_w`
(`ℓ¹_w := {Σ|b|n^{−½} < ∞}`). (ii) For such `a`: `RH ⟺ κ^A(a) = 0 ⟺ inf_T λ^A_T(a) > −∞`. For `a = Λ` this is also
`⟺ κ^W = 0 ⟺ ∀T λ^W_T ≥ 0`. **EQUIVALENT.** (iii) Scale gap: under RH, `0 ≤ λ^W_T ≤ λ^A_T ≤ 𝓑(T,k)` (`thm:margin`,
CITED). Under ¬RH, `sup_{T ≥ T′}(−λ^A_T)e^{−2αT} = ∞` for every `α < Θ − ½` and every `T′`. So nothing lies strictly
between "`≥ 0`" and "exponentially negative along a sequence".
*Proof.* (i) If `κ^A(a) < α < Θ − ½`, the hypothesis of G1 holds with `M = 1`. Here `Σ|b|n^{−½−α} ≤ Σ|b|n^{−½}`, and G1
contradicts the existence of a zero with `Re > ½ + α`. (ii) (⟸) by (i), since `Θ ≤ ½` is RH (use the functional
equation). (⟹) Weil gives `λ^W_T(Λ) ≥ 0`, and `|λ^A_T(a) − λ^A_T(Λ)| ≤ 2Σ|b|n^{−½}` (B3_R4 Prop I; `|c_F| ≤ ‖F‖²`).
The `κ^W` clause holds because `κ^A ≤ κ^W`. (iii) is (i) restated with (Mono). ∎

G1 with `b = 0` is G2_B3_R4 Theorem Q. With `α = 0` it is G2_B9_R3 Theorem N(i) for `b ∈ ℓ¹_w`. What is new here is
only the route (a two-sided bound, no Landau positivity theorem, no Bochner–Schwartz) and the perturbation class
`Σ|b|n^{−½−α} < ∞`, which allows `S_b(X)` as large as `X^{α}`.

## 4. Theorem U: the upper half of the growth law

**Theorem U. PROVED, conditional on (BD); complete form (hence primitive).** Assume (BD). For every `T ≥ 1`,
```
λ^A_T ≥ λ^W_T ≥ −16e · D_off · (β* + 1/(2T)) · T² · e^{2β*T} .
```

*Proof.* By (D) it suffices to take real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` with `‖F‖ = 1`. By (E), with absolute convergence,
```
W_T(F) = Σ_{Re ρ = ½} Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ) + Σ_{ρ ∈ Z_off} Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ).
```
*On-line terms.* For `ρ = ½ + iγ` with `γ` real and `F` real, `Φ_F(ρ) = F̂(γ)` and `Φ_F(1−ρ) = F̂(−γ) = conj F̂(γ)`. So each
term is `|F̂(γ)|² ≥ 0`.
*Off-line terms.* `Z_off` is invariant under `ρ ↦ 1 − ρ` (functional equation), with multiplicities. So by
`|xy| ≤ ½(|x|² + |y|²)`,
`|Σ_{Z_off}Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)| ≤ Σ_{Z_off}|Φ_F(ρ)|²`.
*Subharmonic Bessel bound.* `Φ_F` is entire and `|Φ_F|²` is subharmonic, so for `r := 1/(2T) ≤ ½`,
`|Φ_F(ρ)|² ≤ (πr²)^{−1}∫_{D(ρ,r)}|Φ_F|²dA`. Summing with multiplicity, the discs `D(ρ,r)`, `ρ ∈ Z_off`, cover each point
at most `sup_z #{ρ ∈ Z_off : |ρ−z| < r} ≤ D_off` times. All of them lie in the strip `S := {|Re z − ½| ≤ β* + r}`. Hence
```
Σ_{Z_off}|Φ_F(ρ)|² ≤ D_off (πr²)^{−1} ∫_S |Φ_F|² dA .
```
*Plancherel on vertical lines.* For `z = σ + iγ`, `Φ_F(z) = ∫F(t)e^{(σ−½)t}e^{iγt}dt`, so
`∫_ℝ|Φ_F(σ+iγ)|²dγ = 2π∫|F(t)|²e^{2(σ−½)t}dt ≤ 2πe^{2|σ−½|T}`. Integrating over `|σ−½| ≤ b := β* + r`:
`∫_S|Φ_F|²dA ≤ 4πb·e^{2bT}`.
*Assembly.* `W_T(F) ≥ −D_off(πr²)^{−1}·4πb·e^{2bT} = −16 D_off T²(β* + 1/(2T))e^{2β*T}·e^{2rT}`, and `e^{2rT} = e`. Take
the infimum over `F`, then use `λ^A_T ≥ λ^W_T` (P47 §1.1). ∎

**Corollary U1 (two-sided growth law). PROVED under (BD).** `κ^A = κ^W = Θ − ½`. More precisely,
`limsup_T (2T)^{−1}log(−λ^A_T) = Θ − ½` when `Θ > ½`, and `−λ^W_T ≤ C T² e^{2(Θ−½)T}` for every `T`. This holds in
particular if the set of off-line zeros is finite (then `D_off ≤ #Z_off`).
*Proof.* Upper: Theorem U. Lower: Cor G2(i) (or B3_R4 Q1). ∎

**Remarks.**
- *(U-a) Sanity.* Under RH, `Z_off = ∅` and Theorem U gives `λ^W_T ≥ 0`: Weil's direction, re-derived from the
  on-line/off-line split. The theorem does not use RH anywhere, only (BD).
- *(U-b) Where (BD) enters, and why it is not removable by this argument.* Without (BD) the multiplicity bound becomes
  the local off-line count `N_off(z, r)`. If off-line zeros had density comparable to `log|γ|` (as for `Ξ_a`), the
  bound would be `∫_S log(2+|Im z|)|Φ_F|²`, which is not controlled by `‖F‖²` uniformly on the unit ball of `L²(I_T)`.
  The positive on-line terms and the archimedean growth `m(τ) ~ ½log|τ|` cannot be used to absorb this, since the
  on-line zeros are not known to lie near the off-line ones. So for `Ξ_a` the upper half is **OPEN**. The lower half
  gives `κ^A(Ξ_a) ≥ a` (B3_R4 Rem Q2).
- *(U-c) Status of (BD).* It is implied by RH (`D_off = 0`) and by finiteness of `Z_off`. It is strictly weaker than
  RH and not known. Zero-density theorems (`N(σ,T) = o(T)` for `σ > ½`) are averaged statements and do not give it.
  **OPEN**, and not claimed.
- *(U-d) Two-sided local law for `U`.* Under (BD), Lemma T(c) and Theorem U give
  `|U(x)| ≤ a₀ + C‖Dh‖²x²e^{(Θ−½)x}`, the smoothed von Koch bound. So the `T`-free profile of §2 and the bottoms have
  the same exponent. Without (BD), the same bound for `U` follows from the classical explicit formula for `ψ`
  (CITED, Ingham Ch. IV) and is weaker than a bound on the bottoms.

## 5. How W15 is avoided, and why the mechanism still lands on RH

**W15 (P46 §21.7, `cor:mono`)** kills every argument that proves the sign at `T+δ` from the sign at `T`. The
invariant here is not a sign and is not propagated.
- Its definition, `κ = limsup(2T)^{−1}log⁺(−λ_T)`, is a statement at `T = ∞`.
- Its lower bound (G1) comes from **one** analytic continuation of the `T`-free profile `U`, which Lemma T reads
  simultaneously from *all* windows, not step by step.
- Its upper bound (Theorem U) is proved at each `T` separately and directly, from the zero side, with no reference to
  `T − δ`.
So the scheme is outside Definition 2.1 of my R3 memo: it is case (c) of Theorem NB, a `T`-free statement. That is
exactly why it escapes (a) and (b). And case (c) is where Theorem NB predicted the landing, EQUIVALENT.

**What the growth law says about a G2 mechanism.** By Cor U1, the rate is the zero displacement *exactly* (under
(BD)). A prime-side proof of `κ^A = 0` is therefore a prime-side proof of `Θ = ½`. The prime side sees `κ` only through
the smoothed error profile `U` (Lemma T(d)), and bounding `U` by `e^{o(x)}` is the von Koch–Landau form of RH. The
slack that the growth target gives (B3_R4 §4: it is blind to O3, `thm:margin`, `cor:void`, `thm:finmod`) is real. But
Theorem U shows that it buys nothing in the exponent: `κ` cannot be made small without making `Θ − ½` small.

## 8. What is genuinely new, and the weakest step

**New.**
1. **Theorem U and Cor U1.** This is the first upper bound on the negativity of the Weil bottoms in terms of the zeros,
  and it gives the two-sided growth law `κ^A = κ^W = Θ − ½` under (BD), in particular when `Z_off` is finite. It
  answers G2_B3_R4 Q2 conditionally. The device is the subharmonic Bessel bound on discs of radius `1/(2T)`, which
  needs only local finiteness of off-line zeros, where the termwise bound needed global summability.
2. **Lemma T**, the two-sided transport. It gives an independent proof of the lower half without Landau's positivity
  theorem, and a perturbation class with `S_b(X)` up to `X^{α}`.
3. For the angle: the exact renormalisation invariant of the window flow is identified, and it is shown to be the zero
  displacement itself. The bootstrap/renormalisation angle is closed in both its sign form (R3, Theorem NB) and its
  growth form (here).

**Not new.** Landau's method, von Koch's theorem, Weil's criterion, the lower half (B3_R4 Q, concurrent), and the
`α = 0` case (B9_R3 N(i)).

**Weakest step.** Theorem U rests on the explicit formula (E) with absolute convergence for smooth tests, and on the
density/real reduction (D). Both are CITED from the record and standard. Its hypothesis (BD) is OPEN, and nothing
here makes it plausible or implausible. Of the unconditional statements, the delicate point is the identification
(3.1): the Fubini exchange on `Re s > ½` and the claim that the finitely many terms with `n ≤ 20` and the `K`-part are
holomorphic on `Re s > −½`. Both are checked in §3. **No sign for ζ is claimed at any `T > 0.8`, and the `∀T` arrow is
not advanced.**
