## §2 Why the angle cannot be repaired: Proposition D

The probe is: take a finite universe `M` of zeros (100 at random), see where the truncated certificate
fails, and find the infinite structure that repairs it. Rounds 1–2 did the first two steps. This section proves that
the third step has only one outcome.

**Definition (zero-universe law).** A *zero-universe law* is a rule that assigns to each window `T` a lower bound
`c_T ≥ 0` for `λ^W_T` (complete) or `λ^A_T` (primitive) using, as its only input about the zeros, a multiset `M`
together with properties `𝒫` of `M` that are stable under (i) passing to sub-multisets of positive density and
(ii) moving the points off the line inside the strip `|Im γ| < ½` while keeping the counting function.

**Proposition D (PROVED).** Let `𝓛` be a zero-universe law.
* (a) If `𝓛` is valid for some finite `M`, then `c_T ≤ 0` for every `T`: no finite universe gives a positive bound.
  (Both forms.)
* (b) If `𝓛` is valid for `M = Z` (the zeros of ζ) with counting bounds `(X-N)` and `ν`, and `𝓛` uses only
  properties stable under (ii), then `c_T ≤ 0` for every `T ≥ T₁` for some `T₁`. (Complete form.)
* (c) Consequently any zero-universe law with `c_T > 0` for all `T` uses a property that is *not* stable under (ii),
  i.e. a property that fails for some configuration with ζ's counts and points off the line. The minimal such
  property is reality of the multiset. Reality of `Z` is RH.

*Proof.*
* (a) For finite `M` and any `T`, Lemma K's counting argument (G2_B6_R2 §3.2) with `X` replaced by `max_M |Re γ|`
  gives `F ∈ P_T`, `F ≠ 0`, with `F̂(γ) = 0` for all `γ ∈ M`. So `Q_M(F) = 0` and the bottom of `Q_M` on `P_T` is
  `≤ 0`. On the certificate side, `thm:finZ` gives `inf f_{w,M} < 0` for every flat-top `w`, so a certificate bound is
  negative. In both readings `c_T ≤ 0`.
* (b) Take the falsifier `Ξ_a` of the record (P46 §21.7, P47 `subsec:finite`): a Dirichlet-series datum with the
  same archimedean factor and a zero multiset `M_a` with the counting function of ζ, but with a zero off the line.
  By Weil's criterion for that datum (P46 Thm 19.25 / P47 §1, the `(⟸)` direction, which holds for every datum with
  a functional equation of this shape), `λ^W_T(M_a) < 0` for `T ≥ T₁`, and by `lem:monoT` it stays negative. A law
  using only (ii)-stable properties gives the same `c_T` for `Z` and for `M_a`, hence `c_T ≤ λ^W_T(M_a) < 0`.
* (c) is the contrapositive of (a) and (b). Reality is the property that distinguishes `Z` from `M_a` while keeping
  all counting data; any weaker property that still forbids `M_a` forbids some off-line configuration, i.e. is a
  partial form of RH for the datum. ∎

*What the probe answers, and what it cannot.* Proposition D is the precise content of the question "which
infinite structure repairs the finite universe". The answer is *infinite density* (against (a)) *and reality*
(against (b)). On the zero side these are exactly the inputs of Proposition T: Beurling sampling needs density above
Nyquist and needs a real set. On the prime side, the only proved structure that ties `Λ` to the zeros is the explicit
formula (`thm:Z`, premise 3), and reality does not appear in it as a hypothesis but as the conclusion (RH). So a
"candidate law" from this probe is either the explicit formula (no sign) or RH (EQUIVALENT). **Dead end, PROVED as a
dichotomy on the class of zero-universe laws; the phrase "minimal property" in (c) is a method-level reading.**

## §3 Panel check (b), part 1: Lemma E re-derived by a second route

**Lemma E′ (PROVED).** With `A := r c_β u`, `u = |Re z|`, `J := ⌊A^α/e⌋ ≥ 1`, and `y = Im z`,

`|ω̂(x+iy)| ≤ e^{|y|} · exp(−(1+β)(2J − 1 − ½ log J))`.

In particular `|ω̂(x+iy)| ≤ e^{|y|}𝖤(u)` with `𝖤(u) = exp(−(1+β)J)` as in Lemma E, since `2J − 1 − ½log J ≥ J` for
all `J ≥ 1`.

*Proof.* Two elementary facts about `sinc(w) = sin w/w`:
* `|sin w| ≤ cosh(Im w) ≤ e^{|Im w|}`, hence `|sinc(w)| ≤ e^{|Im w|}/|w| ≤ e^{|Im w|}/|Re w|` for `Re w ≠ 0`;
* `|sinc(w)| = |∫₀¹ cos(sw) ds| ≤ e^{|Im w|}`.

By Lemma W(3), `ω̂(z) = sinc((1+θ)z/2)·∏_{j≥1} sinc(a_j z)`. Bound the factors `j ≤ J` by the first fact and all
other factors by the second. The imaginary parts add up to `|y|((1+θ)/2 + Σ_j a_j) = |y|`. What remains is

`∏_{j≤J} (a_j u)^{−1} = ∏_{j≤J} j^{1+β}/A = (J!)^{1+β} A^{−J}`.

Now `J ≤ A^α/e` means `A ≥ (eJ)^{1+β}`, so `A^{−J} ≤ (eJ)^{−(1+β)J}`. With Stirling's upper bound
`J! ≤ e√J · J^J e^{−J}`,

`(J!)^{1+β}A^{−J} ≤ (e√J·J^Je^{−J})^{1+β}(eJ)^{−(1+β)J} = exp((1+β)[1 + ½log J − J − J]) = exp(−(1+β)(2J − 1 − ½log J))`.

Finally `J − 1 ≥ ½log J` for `J ≥ 1` (equality at `J = 1`, and the difference is increasing), so the exponent is
`≥ (1+β)J`. ∎

*Comparison with the round-2 proof.* Round 2 bounded each of the first `J` factors by `e^{−(1+β)}` using `a_ju ≥ e^{1+β}`
for `j ≤ J`; that discards the growth of `a_j u` for `j ≪ J`. The second route keeps it and gains a factor `2` in the
exponent. **Theorem M and Corollary M1 stand as written** (they use only `𝖤 ≤ e^{1+β}e^{−κu^α}`, which Lemma E′
implies with room). The improvement is not used anywhere and is recorded only as the independent confirmation the
panel asked for. Lemma W(3) (the infinite product on `ℂ`) enters both proofs identically; its verification by P2 and
P5 (Lévy continuity, `|sinc w − 1| ≤ |w|²e^{|Im w|}/6`, `Σ a_j² < ∞`) is not repeated.

## §5 Panel check (d): where Theorem M sits in the prolate literature

**5.1 The classical statement.** For the time-and-band-limiting operator of `PW_Ω` (functions band-limited to
`[−Ω,Ω]`) restricted to `[−τ₀,τ₀]`, with `c = Ωτ₀`, the eigenvalues `1 > λ₀ > λ₁ > … > 0` satisfy (CITED):
* Slepian–Pollak 1961, Landau–Pollak 1961–62: about `2c/π` eigenvalues are close to `1` (the Shannon number),
  followed by a plunge region of width `O(log c)` and then super-exponential decay.
* Landau–Widom 1980: `#{n : ε < λ_n < 1−ε} = (2/π²)log c · log(1/ε − 1) + o(log c)`.
* Slepian 1965 / Landau–Widom: for fixed `n`, `1 − λ_n(c) ∼ κ_n c^{n+½}e^{−2c}` as `c → ∞`: **below the Shannon
  number, `1 − λ_n` is exponentially small in `c`.**
* Bonami–Karoui 2014 (C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 352, 229–234) and Osipov 2013 (arXiv:1206.4056 / Appl. Comput. Harmon.
  Anal.): explicit non-asymptotic bounds for `λ_n` and `1 − λ_n` across the plunge region.
Sources consulted (abstracts/search summaries ): arXiv:2006.00427, arXiv:1509.02646, arXiv:1012.3881,
ScienceDirect S1631073X1400017X; primary texts of Slepian and Landau–Widom not re-read (CITED-NOT-VERIFIED for the
exact constants, which are not used).

**5.2 The translation.** In Theorem M the roles are: `PW_T` (tests supported in `[−T,T]`, so `F̂` of exponential type
`T`) and the frequency interval `[−X′,X′]`, with `c = TX′`. The Shannon number is `2c/π = 2TX′/π = 2J + 1`, which is
**exactly** the dimension of the span of the Nyquist lattice `{e^{−ijgt}}_{|j|≤J}` on `[−θT,θT]` used in Lemmas K–N.
The quantity bounded is the energy of `F̂` outside `[−X,X]`, i.e. `1 − (concentration inside)`, for `F` in a subspace
of dimension `2J+1 − (2N(X)+2) ≥ 1` of the lattice span. So Theorem M is the statement

> for `F` in a subspace of the Shannon-number span with `2N(X)+2` linear side conditions, the out-of-band energy is
> `≤ exp(−κ(TD)^α)`, `TD ≍ TX`,

which is a Slepian-type `1 − λ` bound below the Shannon number, proved here by a Gevrey window (Lemma W, E) instead of
prolate asymptotics. The exponent `(TX)^{α}` with `α < 1` is weaker than the classical `e^{−2c}`, `c = TX`, by the
Gevrey loss; the loss is what `ε` in `e^{(2−ε)T}` measures.

**5.3 What is new and what is not.**
* **Not new (classical analysis):** exponential smallness of `1 − λ_n` below the Shannon number; the use of an exactly
  orthogonal Nyquist lattice as a substitute for prolate bases.
* **New (application, not found in Zhu 2608.24827 nor in the prolate sources above):** the observation that the
  number of real linear conditions imposed by the zeros below height `X`, `2N(X)+2`, falls below the Shannon number
  `2TX/π` exactly when `X < 2πe^{2T}` (the Nyquist height, since `2N(X) ≈ (X/π)log(X/2π)`), so that **the zeros below
  the Nyquist height can be annihilated by a Shannon-number test without any information about their positions**;
  and the resulting unconditional bound `λ^A_T ≤ exp(−e^{(2−ε)T})`, i.e. the upper half of Zhu's Landau–Widom law up
  to `e^{εT}` without RH.
* **Consequence for the record.** The novelty caveat in G2_B6_R2 §6(v) is resolved as: *the analytic mechanism is
  classical (Slepian plunge), the arithmetic content is the counting comparison, the statement about ζ is new to the
  corpus and, to the extent checked, to the literature.* If Theorem M enters Paper 47 it should be stated with the
  prolate sources beside Zhu.

**5.4 Alternative proof route (not written out).** With the explicit plunge bounds of Bonami–Karoui or Osipov one
could replace Lemmas W, E, I by: take the first `2J+1` prolate functions of `(PW_T, [−X,X])`, impose the `2N(X)+2`
zero conditions, and bound the out-of-band energy by `max_{n ≤ 2J} (1 − λ_n)`. This would give `e^{−2TX(1−o(1))}`,
i.e. `exp(−e^{2T(1−o(1))})` with an effective `T₀`, better than Corollary M1's `e^{(2−ε)T}`. I did not check the
non-asymptotic constants in those papers; HEURISTIC as a route, and not needed for the record statement.

## §6 Proposition T: the simplicity hypothesis, examined

G2_B6_R2 §4.2 proved Proposition T (random infinite thinnings keep the zero-side form coercive at every `T`) under RH
**and all zeros simple**. The panel accepted it as conditional. The natural question is whether simplicity can be
dropped. The answer is: not by this proof, and the reason is instructive.

**6.1 Proposition T″ (the proof's inputs do not exclude density zero) — PROVED as a statement about multisets.**
There exist real symmetric multisets `M ⊂ ℝ` with counting function `N_M(t) = (t/2π)log(t/2πe) + 7/8 + S_M(t) + O(1/t)`
and `S_M(t) = O(log t/log log t)`, such that for every `p ∈ (0,1)` the Bernoulli thinning `S_p(M)` (orbits kept
independently with probability `p`) has Beurling lower uniform density `D⁻(S_p(M)) = 0` almost surely — indeed, for
every `r > 0`, almost surely infinitely many windows `[x, x+r]` with `x → ∞` contain no point of `S_p(M)`.

*Proof.* Choose `M` with all its points at distinct positions `t_k` spaced by `h = 1` (say `t_k = k`), each with
multiplicity `m_k := ⌊(1/2π)log(k/2πe)·1⌋ + O(1)` chosen so that `N_M(k) − N_M(k−1)` matches `(1/2π)log(k/2πe)` to
within `1`; then `S_M(t) = O(1)`, which is well inside `O(log t/log log t)`. The distinct orbits in `[x,x+r]` number at
most `r + 1`. So `P([x,x+r] ∩ S_p(M) = ∅) ≥ (1−p)^{r+1} =: q_r > 0`, independent of `x`. Disjoint windows are
independent (distinct orbits). By the second Borel–Cantelli lemma along `x = 2kr`, almost surely infinitely many are
empty; hence `inf_x #(S_p(M) ∩ [x,x+r])/r = 0` for every `r`, and `D⁻ = 0`. ∎

*Reading.* The RH bound on `S(t)` controls the *counting function with multiplicity*. Beurling sampling needs a
*uniformly discrete* set of positive density, i.e. distinct points. Under RH alone, `m(γ) ≤ S(γ⁺) − S(γ⁻) ≪
log γ/log log γ` (from `N(t) = (t/2π)log(t/2πe) + 7/8 + S(t) + O(1/t)` and Littlewood's bound, CITED Titchmarsh
§14.13), which permits, in principle, a multiset like `M`. So the proof of Proposition T genuinely uses more than RH.
(For ζ itself all known zeros are simple, and simplicity is conjectured; nothing here suggests otherwise.)

**6.2 Proposition T′ (the honest form) — PROVED conditional, from CITED results.** Assume RH and that the multiplicities
are bounded: `m(γ) ≤ m₀` for all `γ`. Then the conclusion of Proposition T holds: for `p ∈ (0,1]`, almost surely, for
every `T > 0` there is `c_T > 0` with `Σ_{γ∈S_p}|F̂(γ)|² ≥ c_T‖F‖²` for all `F ∈ L²(I_T)`.

*Proof.* Under RH and `S(t) = O(log t/log log t)`, for fixed `h` and `t ≥ t₀(h)`, `N(t+h) − N(t) ≥ (h/4π)log t`
(counted with multiplicity), so the number of *distinct* ordinates in `[t,t+h]` is `≥ (h/4πm₀)log t`. Then
`P(I_k ∩ S_p = ∅) ≤ (1−p)^{(h/4πm₀)log(kh)} = (kh)^{−a′h}` with `a′ = log(1/(1−p))/(4πm₀) > 0`, a power decay in `k`.
The block/Borel–Cantelli argument of G2_B6_R2 §4.2 steps (iii)–(iv) goes through verbatim with `a` replaced by `a′`,
giving for every `M ∈ ℕ` an `M^{−1}`-separated `Λ_M ⊂ S_p` with `D⁻(Λ_M) ≥ M/4`, almost surely; Beurling's theorem
(CITED, as in round 2) gives sampling for `PW_T` whenever `M/4 > T/π`. ∎

The hypothesis "bounded multiplicity" can be weakened to "the number of distinct ordinates in `[t,t+1]` is
`≥ c log t` for large `t`" (a positive proportion of distinct zeros). It cannot be weakened to RH alone by this
proof (Proposition T″). Whether the *conclusion* of Proposition T holds under RH alone is OPEN and is not a row-D
question.

**6.3 Reality (P5's remark).** Beurling's theorem needs `Λ ⊂ ℝ`. For `Ξ_a` the multiset is not real and (i) of the
round-2 proof fails; this is the load-bearing role of reality identified in Proposition D(c). No change.
