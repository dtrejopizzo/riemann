## §3 Sharp multiplicity cap and the completeness problem

**Setting (`CITED`, Connes, Selecta Math. 5 (1999), arXiv:math/9811068).** Restrict to the `Ẑ^×`-invariant part, as
the G1_B6_R1 panel required (E3), and write `u = log|x|`. Then `L²_δ = L²(R, (1+u²)^{δ/2} du)`, the normalised flow is
translation `(U_t f)(u) = f(u − t)`, and the dual is `L²_{−δ}` under the `L²(du)` pairing. The dual flow acts on
functionals.

**Lemma S′ (`PROVED`).**
* (i) `‖U_t‖_{L²_{±δ}} ≤ 2^{δ/4}(1+t²)^{δ/4}`. So `U` is tempered of order `N = δ/2`, and R1 Lemma S bounds the chain
  length by `k ≤ 1 + δ/2`.
* (ii) A distribution `ℓ` on `R` with `U_t^* ℓ = e^{λt}ℓ` for all `t` is `ℓ = c·e^{−λu}`. A Jordan chain of length `k`
  at `λ` consists of `e^{−λu}P(u)` with `deg P ≤ k − 1`, and the top vector has `deg P = k − 1`.
* (iii) `e^{−λu}u^{k−1} ∈ L²_{−δ}` iff `Re λ = 0` and `2(k−1) − δ < −1`, i.e. `k < (1+δ)/2`. This is Connes' cap.
  Since `(1+δ)/2 < 1 + δ/2`, the membership cap is strictly sharper than the growth cap.

*Proof.*
* (i) Peetre's inequality: `(1+(u+t)²)/(1+u²) ≤ 2(1+t²)`. Hence
  `‖U_t f‖²_{δ} = ∫|f(v)|²(1+(v+t)²)^{δ/2}dv ≤ 2^{δ/2}(1+t²)^{δ/2}‖f‖²_δ`, and the same argument with `−δ`.
* (ii) Differentiate in `t` at `0`: `ℓ′ = −λℓ` in `𝒟′(R)`. The only solutions are `c e^{−λu}`. That is the standard
  uniqueness for the distributional ODE: `(e^{λu}ℓ)′ = 0` forces `e^{λu}ℓ` to be constant. Chains follow by
  induction from `(d/du + λ)ℓ_j = ℓ_{j−1}`.
* (iii) `∫|e^{−λu}|² u^{2(k−1)}(1+u²)^{−δ/2}du` diverges at `+∞` or at `−∞` if `Re λ ≠ 0`. If `Re λ = 0`, it converges
  iff `2(k−1) − δ < −1`. ∎

**Proposition C_δ (the completeness problem; `PROVED` (⇒), `CITED` (⇐)).** Fix `δ > 1`. Let `(C_δ)` be the statement:
for every non-trivial zero `ρ` of `ζ`, with multiplicity `m_ρ`, the annihilator of `range(E)` in `L²_{−δ}` (on the
`Ẑ^×`-invariant part) contains a Jordan chain of the dual flow of length `m_ρ` at `λ = ρ − ½`. Then
`(C_δ) ⟺ [RH and m_ρ < (1+δ)/2 for every ρ]`.

*Proof.*
* (⇒) Lemma S′(ii)–(iii) applied to the chain at `ρ − ½` gives `Re(ρ − ½) = 0` and `m_ρ < (1+δ)/2`.
* (⇐) This is Connes 1999 Thm 1: the spectrum on the cokernel is the set of critical zeros, with multiplicity equal to
  the largest integer `< (1+δ)/2` that is `≤ m_ρ` (`CITED`). ∎

**Label: EQUIVALENT (RH together with a multiplicity cap).** This is the named RH-equivalent the panel asked to be
recorded. `∀δ: (C_δ)` fails for every fixed `δ` if multiplicities are unbounded. "`(C_δ)` for some `δ`" is equivalent
to RH together with bounded multiplicities.

## §4 The Iwasawa mechanism: what compactness gives, and why the flow over Q has none

### 4.1 Iwasawa over `F_q` is Weil's `H¹` without the sign (`CITED` std.)

Let `C/F_q` be a smooth projective curve of genus `g`, and let `ℓ ∤ q`. Along the constant `Z_ℓ`-extension
`C ⊗ F_{q^{ℓ^∞}}`, the Iwasawa module (the Galois group of the maximal unramified abelian pro-`ℓ` extension, geometric
part) is `T_ℓ(J_C)`. It is free of rank `2g` over `Z_ℓ`, and the Iwasawa generator acts as Frobenius.

The characteristic polynomial of Frobenius on `T_ℓ J_C` is the numerator `P(T)` of `Z(C,T)`. That is the
function-field main conjecture (Iwasawa 1969 analogy; Weil). So Iwasawa theory delivers:
* OT-1 (a rank-`2g` carrier);
* the count (OT-2: rationality and the numerator);
* **integrality**: the eigenvalues are `ℓ`-adic units and algebraic integers.

It does **not** deliver `|α| = q^{1/2}`. That comes from Rosati positivity (Castelnuovo on `C × C`; W-5, W-6 of R158_001).

### 4.2 Compactness gives a lattice, and a lattice gives only `p`-adic units

**Proposition I1 (`PROVED`, elementary).** Let a compact group `K` act continuously on a finite-dimensional `Q_p`-space
`V`. Then:
* (a) there is a `K`-stable `Z_p`-lattice `L`;
* (b) every `k ∈ K` has characteristic polynomial in `Z_p[T]` with unit constant term, so all its eigenvalues `α`
  satisfy `|α|_p = 1`;
* (c) (b) says nothing about `|α|` under any embedding `Q̄_p → C`.

*Proof.*
* (a) Take any lattice `L_0`. The orbit map `K × L_0 → V` is continuous, so `K·L_0` is compact, hence bounded. Its
  `Z_p`-span `L` is a bounded open `Z_p`-submodule, i.e. a lattice, and it is `K`-stable.
* (b) `k` and `k^{−1}` both preserve `L`, so `det k ∈ Z_p^×` and the characteristic polynomial has coefficients in
  `Z_p`.
* (c) Take `α = 1 + p` acting on `Q_p`: `|α|_p = 1`, while its complex absolute value is `1+p`. ∎

**Proposition I2 (Mazur–Wiles; `CITED` std.).** For the cyclotomic `Z_p`-extension of `Q(μ_p)`, the characteristic
ideal of each odd eigenspace of the unramified Iwasawa module equals the ideal generated by the Iwasawa power series of
the corresponding Kubota–Leopoldt `p`-adic `L`-function. References: Mazur–Wiles, Invent. Math. 76 (1984); Washington,
*Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, Thm 15.14; an Euler-system proof in Rubin's appendix to Lang, *Cyclotomic Fields
I and II*.

*Reading.* This is the only unconditional arithmetic result on record in which **an object defined without the zeros
delivers the count**, which is the N-a shape. The zeros it counts are the zeros of `L_p(s, ω^i)` in `s ∈ Z_p`. They
are unrelated to the complex zeros of `ζ`, and `L_p` interpolates `ζ` only at negative integers.

### 4.3 Theorem I

**Theorem I (`PROVED`, given G1_B4_R1 Prop 4.3(ii), Sz.-Nagy / G1_B6_R1 Thm 8, and standard facts on almost periodic
representations).** Let `(H, φ^t)` be a flow carrier on a Banach space, and let `U_t = e^{−t/2}φ^t`. Say `U` has
**Iwasawa-type prior positivity** if there are a compact group `K`, a continuous homomorphism `ι : R → K`, and a
strongly continuous representation `π` of `K` on `H` with `U_t = π(ι(t))`.
* (i) If `K` is an arithmetic compact group of `Q` (a quotient of `Gal(Q̄/Q)`, of `Gal(Q^{ab}/Q) ≅ Ẑ^×`, or of the
  profinite completion of `C_Q/D_Q`) and `ι` comes from the norm flow, then `ι` is trivial. So `U_t = id`, `Θ = ½`
  on `H`, and the Lefschetz distribution is `(∫h)·dim` on each finite-dimensional stable piece. This is zero-blind and
  prime-blind, so OT-2 fails.
* (ii) For any `K`, `U` is uniformly bounded. `H` carries an equivalent `U`-invariant norm. If `H` is Hilbertian, it
  carries an equivalent `U`-invariant inner product, and P-HS holds for it. Every orbit `t ↦ U_t v` is Bohr almost
  periodic. `Θ` is semisimple on each generalised eigenspace, and every eigenvalue has `Re = ½`.
* (iii) Conversely, if `H` is Hilbertian and `U` is uniformly bounded with almost periodic orbits, then `U` has
  Iwasawa-type positivity with `K = bR`, the Bohr compactification.
* (iv) Hence, on a Hilbertian `H¹` satisfying OT-2 (all zeros with multiplicity), Iwasawa-type positivity implies RH,
  with every `ρ`-part semisimple. The only compact group that is not killed by (i) is `bR`, and its use amounts to
  postulating (ii), which is Lemma T. **RH-strength (Sz.-Nagy); no new input.**

*Proof.*
* (i) `K` is profinite, hence totally disconnected, and `ι(R)` is connected, so `ι(R) = {1}` (B4 Prop 4.3(ii); for
  `Ẑ^×` also by Artin reciprocity, `D_Q ↦ 1`, `CITED` std.). The trace statement is B4 Prop 4.3(iii).
* (ii) `π(K)` is strongly continuous on a compact group, so `sup_k ‖π(k)‖ < ∞` by uniform boundedness. The invariant
  norm is `‖v‖′ := sup_k ‖π(k)v‖`. In the Hilbertian case the invariant inner product is
  `⟨v,w⟩′ := ∫_K ⟨π(k)v, π(k)w⟩ dk` (Haar measure), which is equivalent to the original one because `π` is bounded
  above and below. Orbits `t ↦ π(ι(t))v` are continuous functions on `R` pulled back from the compact group `K`, hence
  Bohr almost periodic (`CITED` std., Bohr/Weil). The eigen-statements follow from unitarity in `⟨·,·⟩′` (Lemma T(⇒)).
* (iii) Almost periodicity of all orbits for a bounded group is the condition for the representation to extend
  continuously to `bR` (`CITED` std., de Leeuw–Glicksberg).
* (iv) (ii) and Lemma T(⇒). The remark on `bR` follows from (iii): the extension exists iff the conclusion of (ii)
  already holds. ∎

**Corollary I′ (the two inputs of Weil's proof are independent; `PROVED` by Props I1 and I2 and §4.1).**
* Over `F_q`, Frobenius generates a dense subgroup `Z ⊂ Ẑ`. Compactness (Prop I1) gives the lattice, integrality and
  the finite count.
* The archimedean statement `|α| = q^{1/2}` is not a consequence of compactness (Prop I1(c)). It needs Rosati.
* Over `Q`, the flow `R_{>0} = D_Q` has no arithmetic compact closure (Thm I(i)). So even the first, non-archimedean
  input is unavailable for the flow, and the second is exactly OT-5′.
* This makes R1 §5 Prop W, which was a `HEURISTIC` reading, a theorem for the whole Iwasawa/Galois/FF class: **every
  positivity that p-adic Hodge theory derives is a compactness positivity, and it controls only `p`-adic absolute
  values.**

**What Theorem I adds.** B4 Prop 4.3 proves (i) for the Néron–Tate carrier `J(Q̄)⊗R`. Theorem I states it for every
carrier with any compact-group positivity, adds the non-arithmetic alternative (ii)–(iv), and closes that alternative
too. It also records the Iwasawa main conjecture as the unique arithmetic instance of "the object delivers the count".
In that instance the count is `p`-adic.

## §5 Weil–Arakelov cohomology (Flach–Morin) is zero-blind

**Setting (`CITED`: Flach–Morin, *Deninger's conjectures and Weil–Arakelov cohomology*, Münster J. Math. 13 (2020);
text read from the authors' PDF this phase).**
* Conjecture 2.3 (Deninger): cohomology `H^i_{dyn}(X)` with an `R`-action `φ^t`, where `ζ(X̄, s)` is an alternating
  product of `det_∞((s − Θ)/2π | H^i_{dyn,c})`, and (4) gives a Hodge `∗` producing a hermitian scalar product.
* Conjecture 4.1: for every `n ∈ Z`, a long exact sequence
  `… → H^i_{ar,c}(X, C̃(n)) → H^i_{dyn,c}(X) −(Θ−n)→ H^i_{dyn,c}(X) → H^{i+1}_{ar,c}(X, C̃(n)) → …`.
  Equivalently (their Rem. 4.12), `RΓ_{ar,c}(X, C̃(n))` is the homotopy fibre of `Θ − n`.
* The Weil–Arakelov side is **defined unconditionally**, from Bloch's cycle complexes `Z(n)` and Deligne cohomology.
  It exists only for `n ∈ Z`.
* Their Theorem 4.2 proves Conjecture 4.1 over `F_q` (with finite generation). Their Prop. 4.4 shows that 4.1 forces
  `Θ` to be semisimple at `n`.

**Proposition WA (`PROVED` conditional on Conj. 2.3 and 4.1).** Let `X̄ = Spec Z̄`. For every `n ∈ Z`, the groups
`H^i_{ar,c}(X̄, C̃(n))` are determined by `ker` and `coker` of `Θ − n` on `H^0_{dyn,c}` and `H^2_{dyn,c}`. `H^1_{dyn,c}`
contributes nothing. So the unconditionally defined Weil–Arakelov cohomology of `Spec Z̄` carries no zero of `ζ`.
OT-1 fails for `H¹`, and the Lefschetz content is polar/special-value only.

*Proof.*
* By Conj. 2.3(2), the eigenvalues of `Θ` on `H^1_{dyn,c}(X̄)`, with algebraic multiplicity, are the zeros of the
  completed `ξ`. These are the non-trivial zeros, with `0 < Re ρ < 1` (`CITED` std.: Hadamard–de la Vallée-Poussin
  on `Re s = 1`, and the functional equation).
* So no integer is an eigenvalue. Since `det_∞` is defined (Def 2.1(1): direct sum of finite-dimensional invariant
  subspaces), `Θ − n` is bijective on `H^1_{dyn,c}` for every `n ∈ Z`.
* In the long exact sequence, the terms from `H¹` then have zero kernel and cokernel, and only `H⁰`, `H²` (eigenvalues
  `0` and `1`) contribute. ∎

**Remark WA′ (interpolating the weight; `HEURISTIC`, with the facts `CITED` std.).** To make the unconditional side
see a zero `ρ`, one would need coefficients `C̃(s)` with `s ∉ Z`. Motivic twists `Z(n)` are indexed by `n ∈ Z`, and no
motivic `Z(s)` is known.
* `p`-adically, twists interpolate over weight space: `Z_p(s)` via `κ^s`, `s ∈ Z_p`, with `κ` the cyclotomic
  character. This is exactly Iwasawa's variable (§4). It interpolates along the **compact** factor `Z_p^× ⊂ Ẑ^×` of
  `C_Q`.
* The archimedean analogue of "twist by `s`" is the character `|·|^s` of `D_Q = R_{>0}`, i.e. the flow itself. By
  Conj. 4.1, "Weil–Arakelov with coefficients `C̃(s)`" would be the fibre of `Θ − s` on Deninger's complex. That is
  Deninger's cohomology read at `s`, and it needs OT-1 first. So interpolating the weight archimedeanly is circular,
  and `p`-adically it goes along the wrong direction.

## §7 Bridge to G2: compactness positivity = temperedness of the prime side

**Proposition AP (`PROVED`, given standard facts, `CITED` std.).** Let
`D := e^{−t/2}(dψ(e^t) − e^t dt)`, a measure on `(0, ∞)`, extended by `0` to `t ≤ 0`. Then **D is a tempered
distribution on `R` iff RH.**

*Proof.*
* (⇐) Under RH, `ψ(x) − x = O(x^{1/2}log²x)` (von Koch, `CITED` std.). Write `D = e^{−t/2}dE(t)`, where
  `E(t) = ψ(e^t) − e^t`. Integrating by parts, `D = d(e^{−t/2}E) + ½e^{−t/2}E dt`, and `e^{−t/2}E(t) = O(t²)`. So `D`
  is a derivative of a polynomially bounded function plus a polynomially bounded function, hence tempered.
* (⇒) `D` is supported on `[0,∞)`. By Paley–Wiener–Schwartz for Laplace transforms (`CITED` std., Schwartz), a tempered
  `D` has Laplace transform `L(z) = ∫e^{−zt}dD` holomorphic in `Re z > 0`. For `Re z > ½` one computes
  `L(z) = −ζ′/ζ(z+½) − 1/(z − ½)`. Its continuation has poles exactly at `z = ρ − ½`, apart from the cancelled
  `z = ½`. Holomorphy in `Re z > 0` forces `Re ρ ≤ ½`, and the functional equation gives RH. ∎

**Label: EQUIVALENT to RH** (the box `ψ(x) − x ≪ x^{1/2+ε}` in distributional form). *Reading* (`HEURISTIC`):
by Theorem I(ii), compactness positivity for the flow means bounded, almost periodic orbits. On the prime side of the
explicit formula that is temperedness, and a Bohr/Besicovitch almost periodicity, of `D`. A G2 mechanism would have to
prove it from exact `Λ(n)`, uniformly in `t`. So G1 in its compactness form and G2 are **one** statement. No step toward
proving it is supplied here.

## §9 What is genuinely new, and what is not

**New to the record:**
* The Iwasawa row: Props I1 and I2 and Corollary I′. The two inputs of Weil's proof (compact-Galois integrality and
  count; Rosati sign) are independent, and over `Q` the flow lacks both.
* Theorem I as a class statement. Its (i) is B4 Prop 4.3(ii), generalised; (ii)–(iv) close the non-arithmetic
  alternative.
* The Weil–Arakelov row (Prop WA, Rem WA′): the only unconditional Deninger-shaped cohomology sees integers only.
  Interpolating the weight is `p`-adic along the compact direction, and archimedeanly it is circular.
* The sharp cap, Lemma S′, and the named RH-equivalent `(C_δ)`.
* Prop AP as the explicit identification of G1-compactness with G2-temperedness.

**Not new:** Sz.-Nagy/Dixmier boundedness, Bohr and de Leeuw–Glicksberg, von Koch, Paley–Wiener–Schwartz, Mazur–Wiles,
and Connes Thm 1 are all classical. Everything is structural. **No sign, no object.**

## §10 Weakest step

* **Prop WA depends on the conjectural Flach–Morin long exact sequence** (Conj. 4.1, proved only over `F_q`). Without
  it, the zero-blindness of Weil–Arakelov cohomology at the level of `H¹` is only `HEURISTIC`. The unconditional side
  is still indexed by `n ∈ Z`.
* Theorem I(iii) uses de Leeuw–Glicksberg (`CITED` std., not re-read). The "only `bR` survives" clause of (iv) is
  proved only for compact groups receiving the flow through a continuous homomorphism. A carrier with an **amenable,
  non-compact** symmetry larger than `R`, where positivity comes from an invariant mean, is covered only via Sz.-Nagy
  for `R` itself (G1_B6_R1 Thm 8). It is not treated separately.
* Prop AP (⇒): the Laplace transform identity and the Paley–Wiener–Schwartz characterisation are standard. The
  normalisation of `D` near `t = 0` (`ψ(1) = 0`, the extension by `0`) was checked by hand and not otherwise.
* §6 is at abstract level and carries no weight.
