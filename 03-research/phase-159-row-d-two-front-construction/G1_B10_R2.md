## §0 What changed since round 1, and the line the panel asked for

Round 1 (Cauchy damping `e^{−ε|u|}g`) was judged RESTATEMENT by 5/5. The reason: every rung below `ε = ½` is a
zero-free half-plane. That makes it quasi-RH, blocked by T3, and the only unconditional rung is RH-silent. This round
changes the damping from `e^{−ε|u|}` to `e^{−εu²}`, and the structure changes with it.
* **On the spectral side the damping is the heat semigroup.** Its rungs are *not* half-plane statements. Each rung is
  strictly weaker than RH; heuristically it is "RH up to a finite height, plus local density" (H1).
* **Every rung is uniform in T and reduces to a compact interval** (Thm 4). The archimedean `log τ` growth does this;
  the Γ-factor carries the load.
* **One rung is proved unconditionally, uniformly in T: ε = 1** (Thm 6). The proof uses the Platt–Trudgian zero
  verification and Trudgian's `S(t)` bound, both CITED. A prime-side proof that reads no zero at all gives ε = 150
  (Thm 5).
* **RH ⟺ ε_G = 0**, where `ε_G` is the least rung (Thm 2). This is a de Bruijn–Newman-type constant for the *Weil
  distribution*, not for Ξ. The trivial bound is `ε_G ≥ 0`, and this memo proves `ε_G ≤ 1`.

**The line the panel asked for (P1 §9: "which of OT-2 / OT-5′ is proved from non-RH input, by which line"):**
* **OT-5′** is proved at rungs ε ≥ 150 by Thm 5: a prime + Γ inequality that reads no zero.
* A T-uniform positivity at rungs ε ≥ 1 is proved by Thm 6. It uses zero *counting* and zero *verification* (CITED),
  so it is not OT-5′ in the strict sense.
* **OT-2 is not proved.** The Lefschetz identity at rung ε is the Gaussian-damped explicit formula. ζ's formula is
  reached only as ε → 0, which is RH (Thm 2).

**Verdict in advance:** a partial new result together with an honest reformulation. The "for all T" wall becomes the
**backward heat equation in ε**. It does not disappear.

## §1 Definitions (P47 normalisation)

* `g` is the Weil distribution `eq:gdatum`:
  `⟨g,h⟩ = C_0h(0) + ∫_0^∞(h(0) − h_e(u))K(u)du − Σ_{n≥2} v_n h_e(log n) + 2∫h(u)cosh(u/2)du`.
  * Here `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`, `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`, and `C_0 = ψ(¼) − log π = −5.37218…`.
  * The pairing is extended to Hermitian `h` through `h_e`.
* The explicit formula (P47 `thm:Z`, `lem:roughEF`) is `⟨g,φ⟩ = Σ_ρ φ̂(γ_ρ)` for compactly supported Lipschitz `φ`.
  * `φ̂(z) = ∫φ(u)e^{izu}du`, `ρ = ½ + iγ_ρ`, and the sum runs over all nontrivial zeros with multiplicity.
  * Write `γ_ρ = γ′_ρ − iδ_ρ`, so that `δ_ρ = β_ρ − ½`.
* **Gaussian-damped Weil distribution.** For `ε > 0`, `⟨G^ε,h⟩ := ⟨g, e^{−ε(·)²}h⟩`. It reads `Λ(n)` (with the
  weights `e^{−ε log² n}`), `C_0` and `K`, and **no zero**.
* **Rung ε (complete form).** `R(ε)`: `⟨G^ε, F⋆F̃⟩ ≥ 0` for every `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)`. The window version is `R_T(ε)`, the
  same statement for `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`. Here `(F⋆F̃)(u) = ∫F(u+t)F̄(t)dt` and `(F⋆F̃)^ = |F̂|²`.
* **Symbol.** `σ_ε(τ) := C_0 + ∫_0^∞(1 − e^{−εu²}cos τu)K(u)du − Σ_n v_n e^{−ε log² n}cos(τ log n) + P_ε(τ)`, where
  `P_ε(τ) := 2√(π/ε)·e^{(1/4 − τ²)/4ε}·cos(τ/4ε)`.
  * The Lévy integral converges: its integrand is `O(u)` at 0 and `O(e^{−u/2})` at ∞.
  * `σ_ε` is continuous, even, and `O(log(2+|τ|))`.
* **Heat kernel.** `H_s(x) = (4πs)^{−1/2}e^{−x²/4s}`. Note `(e^{−su²})^ = 2πH_s`.

## §2 Theorems

### Theorem 1 (spectral identity and PD criterion) — PROVED

(a) For `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)`: `⟨G^ε, F⋆F̃⟩ = (1/2π)∫|F̂(τ)|²σ_ε(τ)dτ`.
(b) `σ_ε(τ) = √(π/ε)·Σ_ρ e^{−(γ_ρ−τ)²/4ε} = √(π/ε)·Σ_ρ e^{(δ_ρ² − x_ρ²)/4ε}·cos(x_ρδ_ρ/2ε)`, with `x_ρ = γ′_ρ − τ`.
The sum converges absolutely and locally uniformly.
(c) `R(ε) ⟺ σ_ε ≥ 0 on ℝ`.

*Proof.*
(a) Pair `G^ε` with `h = F⋆F̃ = (2π)^{−1}∫|F̂(τ)|²e^{−iτu}dτ` term by term. Each piece of `G^ε` is a finite measure
with Gaussian decay, or the Lévy piece whose integrand is `O(u)` at 0 and `O(e^{−u/2})` at ∞. So Fubini applies, and
`⟨G^ε, e^{−iτ·}⟩ = ⟨G^ε, cos τ·⟩ = σ_ε(τ)`, since `G^ε` is even. For the polar piece,
`2∫cosh(u/2)e^{−εu²}cos(τu)du = 2 Re ∫e^{−εu² + (½+iτ)u}du = 2√(π/ε)·Re e^{(½+iτ)²/4ε} = P_ε(τ)`.

(b) Put `φ = e^{−εu²}h`, which is compactly supported and smooth. Then `⟨G^ε,h⟩ = ⟨g,φ⟩ = Σ_ρ φ̂(γ_ρ)`.
* `φ̂ = (2π)^{−1}ĥ ∗ ŵ` with `ŵ(z) = √(π/ε)e^{−z²/4ε}`, which is entire. The convolution identity holds for complex `z`
  because `ĥ` is Schwartz on ℝ and `ŵ` is entire with Gaussian decay on horizontal lines.
* `|ŵ(γ_ρ − τ)| ≤ √(π/ε)·e^{1/16ε}·e^{−x_ρ²/4ε}`, using `|δ_ρ| < ½` (CITED: de la Vallée Poussin). The number of
  zeros with `γ′ ∈ [t, t+1]` is `O(log(2+|t|))` (Riemann–von Mangoldt, CITED). So Fubini applies and
  `⟨G^ε,h⟩ = (2π)^{−1}∫|F̂|²·Σ_ρŵ(γ_ρ − τ)dτ`.
* Both `σ_ε` and the zero sum are continuous with logarithmic growth. Their integrals against every `|F̂|²` agree, and
  these functions separate continuous functions of polynomial growth (the approximate-identity argument in (c)). So the
  two are equal pointwise.
* The second form of (b) comes from `(x − iδ)² = x² − δ² − 2ixδ`. The imaginary parts cancel between ρ and `1 − ρ̄`,
  whose `γ` is `γ̄`.

(c) (⇐) follows from (a). (⇒) Suppose `σ_ε(τ_0) < 0`.
* Take `F_R(t) = R^{−1/2}χ(t/R)e^{−iτ_0t}` with `χ ∈ C_c^∞` real and `‖χ‖_2 = 1`.
* Then `|F̂_R(τ)|² = R|χ̂(R(τ − τ_0))|²` (up to the sign convention of the modulation), with Schwartz tails.
* Hence `(1/2π)∫|F̂_R|²σ_ε → ‖χ‖²σ_ε(τ_0) < 0`, because `σ_ε` is continuous with logarithmic growth. ∎

*Check (numerical, used only to validate normalisations).* Using the first 300 zeros and the primes below 3000, the
prime-side `σ_ε(τ)` and the zero-side sum agree to 10–15 digits at `ε ∈ {1, ½, 0.2}` and `τ ∈ {14.13, 20, 30, 40}`
(mpmath, 20 digits). The exception is the `ε = 0.2` rows: there the truncation of the prime sum at 3000 still shows,
at about 10^{−5} in `σ` and at 10^{−4} at `τ = 0` (the §2 margin remark).

### Theorem 2 (heat semigroup; the constant ε_G) — PROVED

(a) For `ε′ > ε > 0`: `σ_{ε′} = H_{ε′−ε} ∗ σ_ε`. In words, `ε ↦ σ_ε` is the heat flow `∂_εσ = ∂_τ²σ`, and formally
`σ_ε = 2π·e^{ε∂²}μ`, where `μ = Σ_ρ δ_{γ_ρ}` is continued analytically at the complex points.
(b) `{ε > 0: R(ε)}` is an up-set, and it is closed in `(0,∞)`. Put `ε_G := inf{ε > 0: R(ε)} ∈ [0, ∞]`.
(c) `RH ⟺ ε_G = 0`, and then `R(ε)` holds for every ε > 0.
(d) For `ε > ε_G`, `m(ε) := min_τ σ_ε(τ) > 0`. So `⟨G^ε, F⋆F̃⟩ ≥ m(ε)‖F‖²` for every F: a **uniform-in-T margin**
holds at every rung strictly above `ε_G`.

*Proof.*
(a) `e^{−ε′u²}g = e^{−(ε′−ε)u²}·G^ε`, and the Fourier transform of a product is `(2π)^{−1}` times the convolution. Both
factors are tempered and one of them is Schwartz.

(b) The heat kernel is positive, so `σ_ε ≥ 0 ⟹ σ_{ε′} ≥ 0`. For closedness: each `⟨G^ε, F⋆F̃⟩` is continuous in ε, by
dominated convergence on each piece.

(c)
* (⇒) Under RH every term of Thm 1(b) is `√(π/ε)e^{−x²/4ε} > 0`.
* (⇐) Suppose `ε_G = 0`. Then `R(ε)` holds for every ε > 0, by (b). Fix `F ∈ C_c^∞`. Then
  `⟨G^ε, F⋆F̃⟩ → ⟨g, F⋆F̃⟩` as ε ↓ 0: the atoms in the support are finitely many, the Lévy integrand is dominated by
  `C·u·K(u)` near 0, and the polar term converges by dominated convergence. So `⟨g, F⋆F̃⟩ ≥ 0` for every `F`, i.e.
  `W_T ≥ 0` for every `T` (`eq:W`; complete form). Weil's criterion gives RH.

(d) Write `σ_ε = H_{ε−ε′} ∗ σ_{ε′}` with `ε_G ≤ ε′ < ε`, so that `σ_{ε′} ≥ 0` by closedness. Also `σ_{ε′} ≢ 0`, since
it grows like `log|τ|` (Thm 4). A positive kernel convolved with a nonnegative function that is not identically zero
is strictly positive everywhere. The growth at infinity (Thm 4) then gives a positive minimum. Plancherel,
`‖F̂‖² = 2π‖F‖²`, finishes the argument. ∎

*Remark 2.3 (why this is not the round-1 ladder).* At the Cauchy rung ε the pair kernel is `P_{ε−δ} + P_{ε+δ}`, which
is nonnegative **iff** `|δ| ≤ ε`, and that condition is a half-plane. At a Gaussian rung, every single zero term
`e^{(δ²−x²)/4ε}cos(xδ/2ε)` is negative for some `x` as soon as `δ ≠ 0`. Positivity is therefore **never** termwise
(the Gaussian analogue of `cor:nontermwise`, now for zeros *off* the line), and a rung decides no half-plane. One
off-line zero at height γ is hidden at rung ε whenever the local background dominates it. The background is
`≍ log γ`; the zero's dip is `e^{δ²/4ε − π²ε/δ²}` (H1 below).

*Remark 2.4 (positive temperatures).* By Widder's theorem (CITED: Widder 1944, *Trans. AMS* 55), the nonnegative
solutions of the heat equation on a strip `ℝ × (0, t_1)` are exactly the Gauss–Weierstrass transforms of positive
measures. So `ε_G = 0` says that the "arithmetic temperature" `σ` is a positive temperature down to time 0, and its
initial measure is then the zero measure `2πμ`. That is the Gaussian-side form of `thm:Z` / Bochner, and it is
**equivalent to RH**; it adds no content. It is recorded only as the correct name of the object.

### Theorem 3 (probabilistic reading; window thresholds) — PROVED, plus CITED for `T ≤ 0.8`

(a) `⟨G^ε, F⋆F̃⟩ = E_{c∼N(0,2ε)}[W_T(M_cF)]`, where `(M_cF)(t) = e^{ict}F(t)` and `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`. The rung-ε form
is the complete Weil form averaged over **Gaussian random frequency shifts** of the test function.
(b) Put `ε_G(T) := inf{ε: R_T(ε)}`. Then:
* `ε_G(T)` is nondecreasing in T, and `R_T(ε)` holds exactly for `ε ≥ ε_G(T)`;
* `ε_G(T) = 0` ⟺ `λ^W_T ≥ 0`;
* `sup_T ε_G(T) = ε_G`;
* `ε_G(T) = 0` for `T ≤ ½log 2` (Yoshida, Bombieri; CITED) and for `T ≤ 0.8` (Zhu, CITED, not re-verified).

*Proof.*
(a) `E e^{icu} = e^{−εu²}`, and `(M_cF ⋆ \widetilde{M_cF})(u) = e^{icu}(F⋆F̃)(u)`. The map `c ↦ e^{icu}(F⋆F̃)` is
continuous into `C¹(I_{2T})`, with norm `≤ C(1+|c|)`. The Gaussian has a first moment, so the Bochner integral
converges in the topology in which `g` (order ≤ 1 on compacts) is continuous. This repairs P3's objection for the
Gaussian case.

(b)
* Up-set per window: `e^{−(ε′−ε)u²} = E e^{ic′u}` with `M_{c′}` preserving `C_c^∞(I_T)`, exactly as in Thm 2(b).
* `W_T ≥ 0 ⟹ R_T(ε)` for every ε, by (a).
* The converse holds by continuity as ε ↓ 0 for fixed F.
* The sup: every `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)` is a translate of an element of some `C_c^∞(I_T)`, and the form is translation
  invariant. ∎

### Theorem 4 (finite reduction of each rung: uniformity in T is free at every ε > 0) — PROVED

For every `ε > 0` and every `τ` with `|τ| ≥ 1`:

`σ_ε(τ) ≥ C_0 + e^{−ε}(log|τ| − 2)/cosh(½) − M(ε) − |P_ε(τ)|`, where `M(ε) := Σ_n v_n e^{−ε log² n} < ∞`.

Hence `σ_ε(τ) > 0` for `|τ| ≥ τ_1(ε)`, where
`τ_1(ε) := max{ exp(2 + cosh(½)e^{ε}(M(ε) + |C_0| + 1)), τ_P(ε) }` and `τ_P(ε)` is defined by
`2√(π/ε)e^{(1/4−τ_P²)/4ε} = ½`. Consequently:

**`R(ε)` — the damped complete Weil positivity for all T at once — holds iff the continuous function `σ_ε` is ≥ 0 on the
compact interval `[0, τ_1(ε)]`.** `σ_ε` is given there by an absolutely convergent prime-side expression with an
explicit tail bound. So if `σ_ε > 0` on that interval, a finite computation certifies `R(ε)`. That happens for every
ε > ε_G, by Thm 2(d), and under RH for every ε > 0.

*Proof.*
* Split `1 − e^{−εu²}cos τu = (1 − e^{−εu²}) + e^{−εu²}(1 − cos τu)`. Both parts are ≥ 0.
* For `0 < u ≤ 1`: `sinh(u/2) ≤ (u/2)cosh(½)`, so `K(u) ≥ 1/(u·cosh ½)`, and `e^{−εu²} ≥ e^{−ε}`.
* `∫_0^1 (1 − cos τu)/u du = ∫_0^{|τ|}(1 − cos v)/v dv = log|τ| + γ_E − Ci(|τ|) ≥ log|τ| − 2` for `|τ| ≥ 1`, since
  `|Ci(x)| ≤ 2/x` and `γ_E > 0`.
* The prime part has modulus at most `M(ε)`, and `M(ε) < ∞` because `e^{−ε log² n} = n^{−ε log n}`.
* With `log|τ| ≥ 2 + cosh(½)e^{ε}(M + |C_0| + 1)` the lower bound is `≥ 1 − |P_ε| ≥ ½`. ∎

*Reading.* This is where **OT-12 is load-bearing**. The Γ-factor's `log τ` growth beats the prime sum, which is bounded
at each rung. Without an archimedean term every rung fails (Thm 8(b)). The size is `τ_1(ε) ≈ exp(C·ε^{−1/2}e^{1/(16ε)})`,
using `M(ε) ≍ √(π/ε)e^{1/(16ε)}` (from `ψ(x) ≍ x`, CITED). The compact interval therefore grows doubly exponentially as
ε ↓ 0. This matches H1.

### Theorem 5 (an unconditional rung with a zero-free proof: OT-5′ at the rung) — PROVED (the constant 150 by numerical quadrature)

For every τ, `σ_ε(τ) ≥ L(ε) := C_0 + c_ε − M(ε) − 2√(π/ε)e^{1/(16ε)}`, where `c_ε := ∫_0^∞(1 − e^{−εu²})K(u)du`.
* `L(ε) → +∞` as ε → ∞ (PROVED): `c_ε ≥ (1 − e^{−1})·(1/cosh ½)·½ log ε` for `ε ≥ 1`, by integrating over
  `[ε^{−1/2}, 1]`; `M(ε) → 0`; and the polar bound tends to 0.
* So there is `ε_P < ∞` with `R(ε)` for all `ε ≥ ε_P`, uniformly in T, with margin `≥ L(ε)`.
* Numerically (mpmath quadrature, 30 digits): `L(150) = 0.053` and `L(200) = 0.241`, so `ε_P ≤ 150`. This is a routine
  interval-arithmetic task, not done here; the constant 150 is `HEURISTIC-numerical`, while existence is PROVED.

*The proof reads no zero.* It uses `Λ(n)` only through the size of `M(ε)`, plus `C_0` and `K`. This is a positive form
defined before the spectrum, compatible with the flow (the Gaussian space `H_ε` below carries a unitary translation
group). So it is **OT-5′ at the rung ε ≥ 150**. It is also a small-effective-support statement: `e^{−εu²}` concentrates
the test on `|u| ≲ ε^{−1/2} ≪ ½log 2`, where Yoshida-type archimedean dominance is classical. **It is RH-silent** (see
§3, OT-4).

### Theorem 6 (unconditional rung ε = 1, uniform in T) — PROVED modulo CITED inputs (Z1)–(Z3)

`σ_1(τ) > 0` for every real τ. Hence `ε_G ≤ 1`, and for every `ε ≥ 1`, every T and every `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`:

`E_{c∼N(0,2ε)} W_T(M_cF) = ⟨G^ε, F⋆F̃⟩ ≥ m(ε)‖F‖² > 0` (complete form).

Inputs (CITED, not re-derived):
* (Z1) `|δ_ρ| < ½` (de la Vallée Poussin).
* (Z2) All zeros with `0 < γ′ ≤ H_0 := 3·10^{12}` are simple and on the line (Platt–Trudgian, *Bull. LMS* 53 (2021)).
* (Z3) For `t ≥ e`: `N(t) = (t/2π)log(t/2πe) + 7/8 + S(t) + E(t)`, with
  `|S(t)| ≤ 0.112 log t + 0.278 log log t + 2.510` and `|E(t)| ≤ 0.2/t` (Trudgian, *J. Number Theory* 134 (2014)).

Write `R(t) := 0.112 log t + 0.278 log log t + 2.51 + 0.2/t`.

*Proof.* σ is even, so take `τ ≥ 0`. Put `t_ρ := e^{(δ²−x²)/4}cos(xδ/2)` (ε = 1), so `σ_1 = √π·Σ_ρ t_ρ`.

*A crude count.* For `s ≥ 1`, `#{ρ: γ′ ∈ [s, s+1]} ≤ U(s) := 0.4 log(s+2) + 0.56 log log(s+3) + 6`. This follows from
(Z3), since `(1/2π)log((s+1)/2π) ≤ 0.16 log(s+2)`. For `|γ′| < 14` there are no zeros (classical, CITED).

*Case A: `0 ≤ τ ≤ H_0 − 10³`.*
* Every zero with `|γ′| ≤ H_0` has `δ = 0`, so `t_ρ = e^{−x²/4} > 0`.
* There is a zero with `|x| ≤ 100`.
  * For `τ < 14`, take `γ_1 = 14.13…`.
  * For `τ ≥ 14`, (Z3) gives `N(τ+100) − N(τ) ≥ (100/2π)log(τ/2π) − 2R(τ+100) ≥ 12.8 − 7.0 > 0` at τ = 14.
  * The left side increases faster than `2R` for τ ≥ 14: the derivative of the main term is `(100/2π)/τ·…`, which
  exceeds `2·0.112/(τ+100)` and the log log term.
  * So the positive part is `≥ e^{−2500}`.
* Zeros with `|γ′| > H_0` have `|x| ≥ 10³`. Their total is at most
  `2e^{1/16}Σ_{k≥10³}U(τ+k)e^{−k²/4} < e^{−10^5}`.
* Hence `σ_1(τ) > 0`.
* The same argument works verbatim for every `ε ∈ (0, 1]`: the positive part is `≥ e^{−10⁴/4ε}` and the negative
  part is `≤ e^{1/16ε − 10⁶/4ε}·poly(τ)`.

*Case B: `τ > H_0 − 10³`, so `log τ ≥ 28.7`.*
* (i) Zeros with `|x| ≤ 2π` have `|xδ/2| < π/2`, so `t_ρ ≥ 0`.
* (ii) Zeros with `|x| ≤ 1.8` have `t_ρ ≥ e^{−0.81}cos(0.45) ≥ 0.40`, using `e^{δ²/4} ≥ 1`.
* (iii) By (Z3), the number of zeros in (ii) is at least
  `N(τ+1.8) − N(τ−1.8) ≥ (3.6/2π)log((τ−1.8)/2π) − 2R(τ+1.8) =: D(τ)`.
  * At `log τ = 28.7`: `0.573·26.86 − 2·6.66 = 2.08`.
  * `dD/d log τ ≥ 0.573 − 0.224 − 0.56/log τ > 0`.
  * So there are at least 3 zeros for all τ in Case B, and the count grows like `0.349 log τ`.
* (iv) Zeros with `|x| > 2π` satisfy `|t_ρ| ≤ e^{1/16}e^{−x²/4}`. Their total is at most
  `2e^{1/16}Σ_{k≥6}U(τ+k+1)e^{−k²/4} ≤ 2.75·10^{−4}(U(τ) + 1)`, which is `≤ 5.3·10^{−3}` at `log τ = 28.7`.
  It grows like `1.1·10^{−4}log τ`.
* (v) The positive part is `≥ 0.40·max(3, D(τ))`. That is `≥ 1.2` at `log τ = 28.7`, and it grows like `0.14 log τ`.
  So (v) > (iv) throughout Case B. ∎

### Theorem 7 (the Gaussian margin law; the identity at τ = 0) — PROVED (using the classical "no zero with |γ′| < 14.13", CITED)

(a) For every ε > 0, the prime-side identity is

`C_0 + c_ε − Σ_n v_n e^{−ε log² n} + 2√(π/ε)e^{1/(16ε)} = σ_ε(0) = √(π/ε)Σ_ρ e^{(δ_ρ² − γ′_ρ²)/4ε}cos(γ′_ρδ_ρ/2ε)`.

(b) `min_τ σ_ε ≤ σ_ε(0) ≤ C·ε^{−1/2}·e^{(1/4 − 14.13²)/(4ε)} ≈ C·e^{−49.9/ε}` for `0 < ε ≤ 1`.

So the certificate of rung ε requires the prime sum `M(ε)`, whose size is `≍ ε^{−1/2}e^{1/16ε}`, to cancel against
`C_0 + c_ε + P_ε(0)` to absolute precision `e^{−50/ε}`. This is the Gaussian counterpart of `thm:margin`
(`λ^A_T ≤ 4·10⁹Te^{−15.449T}`). Finite decidability (Thm 4) is not feasibility: the relative precision needed is
`e^{−50/ε − 1/16ε}`.

*Proof.* (a) is Thm 1(b) at τ = 0 together with the definition of `σ_ε`. (b) follows from (a), `|δ| < ½`, and the
count `Σ_ρ e^{−γ′²/4ε} ≤ C·e^{−14.13²/4ε}` for ε ≤ 1. ∎

### Theorem 8 (obstructions at every rung) — PROVED

(a) **Amplification (the `cor:void` analogue at each rung).** Let `a = Λ + c·1_{n=n_0}`. Then
`σ^{(a)}_ε(0) = σ_ε(0) − (2c/√n_0)e^{−ε log² n_0}`, which is `< 0` for `c > c_*(ε, n_0)`.
* `𝒞_PNT(ε)` and `𝒞_zeros` of P47 `cor:void` are closed under amplification.
* Hence **no rung `R(ε)`, for any ε > 0, has a derivation whose only prime input is membership in such a class**
  (P46 Thm 18.1(ii)).
* Every rung genuinely uses the exact `Λ(n)`. Thm 5 escapes this only because at ε ≥ 150 the amplification threshold
  `c_*` is astronomically large; it is still a class statement for the class `{a: M_a(ε) small}`.

(b) **No archimedean term ⟹ every rung fails** (Beurling / T3 data). Take a datum
`σ^P_ε(τ) = P_ε(τ) − Σ_k a_k e^{−ε ℓ_k²}cos(τℓ_k)` with `ℓ_k > 0`, `a_k ≥ 0` not all 0, and
`Σ a_k e^{−εℓ_k²} < ∞`.
* The sum `f` is a uniformly almost periodic function with mean 0 and `f ≢ 0`.
* A real Bohr-AP function of mean 0 that is not identically 0 satisfies `f(τ) ≤ −η < 0` on a relatively dense set of
  τ. Otherwise `f ≥ −η` for every η outside a set of zero density, and mean 0 would force `f ≡ 0` (CITED: Besicovitch,
  *Almost periodic functions*, ch. I).
* Since `P_ε(τ) → 0`, `σ^P_ε(τ) < 0` for some large τ.
* So DMV-type systems, which have no Γ-term, fail **every** rung. T3 therefore does not obstruct the Gaussian ladder as
  it did the Cauchy one. The ladder is instead tied to the Γ-factor (OT-12).

(c) **`Ξ_a` gate.** For `Ξ_a`, where every zero is displaced by a > 0 (P47 falsifier), Weil's criterion for its datum
together with Thm 2(c) gives `ε_G(Ξ_a) > 0`. The ladder detects the falsifier. The high rungs hold for `Ξ_a` as class
statements: Thm 6's proof goes through with δ ≤ ½ + a, once the verification input (Z2) is replaced by its own.

## §4 Heuristics (clearly labelled) on what the rungs mean

**H1 (HEURISTIC): rung ε ≈ "RH up to height exp exp(δ²/4ε)" plus local density.**
* An isolated off-line zero with displacement δ at height γ contributes a dip of size about `2e^{δ²/4ε − π²ε/δ²}` at
  `x = 2πε/δ`.
* The on-line background at scale √ε is `≈ log(γ/2π)` (when ε ≫ (mean gap)²).
* So rung ε roughly forbids zeros with `δ² > 4ε log log γ + O(ε log(1/ε))`: a *zero-free region that widens with
  height*. It is the opposite shape to the classical regions, and it amounts to RH up to height about
  `exp exp(δ²/4ε)` for each δ.
* This matches the size `τ_1(ε) ≈ exp(Cε^{−1/2}e^{1/16ε})` of Thm 4's compact interval. Descending the ladder
  (ε ↓ 0) is verifying RH to doubly-exponentially larger heights, plus local density conditions above them.
* Uniformly displaced configurations (Ξ_a-like, dense) are *not* detected at scales where they look like a density:
  `∫e^{−x²/4ε}cos(xa/2ε)dx·e^{a²/4ε} = √(4πε) > 0`. Detection happens only at `ε ≲ a·gap/4π`.
* H1 is not proved in either direction. Other off-line zeros can mask a dip, and unconditional local density lower
  bounds exist only at window length ≳ 1.41 (Z3).

**H2 (HEURISTIC): the unconditional descent is governed by the constant in `S(t) ≪ log t`.**
* Thm 6's method reaches rung ε only while `ε ≳ (2π·2a)²/c` for a moderate constant c, where
  `a = limsup|S(t)|/log t` in the proven bound (a = 0.112, or 0.1038).
* So pushing `ε_G` below about ½ unconditionally needs (i) a smaller `a`, together with (ii) verification to height
  about `exp exp(1/16ε)`.
* Neither is RH, so each rung `ε ∈ (0, 1)` is, heuristically, **strictly weaker than RH and not a half-plane
  statement**, and no proved obstruction of the record is known to cover it (Thm 8(a) excludes only class-membership
  derivations).
* `ε_G = 0` would need `a → 0` in a quantitative form together with infinite verification, i.e. RH.

**The wall, stated honestly (PROVED part and OPEN part).** By Thm 2(c), "for all T at ε = 0" ⟺ `ε_G = 0` ⟺ RH. What
this memo moves is where the wall sits.
* At each fixed ε > 0, "for all T" is **free**: Thm 4 reduces it to a compact interval, and Thm 6 proves it for ε ≥ 1.
* The remaining arrow is **ε ↓ 0**, i.e. the **backward heat equation** for the arithmetic temperature `σ`.
* Backward heat flow does not preserve positivity (Widder), so no rung implies a lower one. That is the exact analogue
  of W15 ("a certificate at T says nothing at T′ > T"), and it is OPEN at RH strength.

## §5 What is genuinely new, and what is not

**New (not found in the corpus; literature novelty `HEURISTIC`):**
1. The Gaussian ladder `G^ε = e^{−εu²}g`. Its symbol `σ_ε` is the heat flow of the zero measure, with prime side
  `Λ(n)e^{−ε log² n}` (Thms 1, 2). The constant `ε_G` satisfies `RH ⟺ ε_G = 0`.
2. **An unconditional, uniform-in-T positivity of a damped complete Weil form: `ε_G ≤ 1`** (Thm 6, modulo CITED
  (Z1)–(Z3)). A zero-free prime-side proof gives `ε_G ≤ 150` (Thm 5). In words: for every window T and every
  `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, the complete Weil form averaged over Gaussian frequency shifts `c ∼ N(0, 2)` is positive.
3. Finite reduction of each rung to a compact interval through archimedean growth (Thm 4). Every rung is decidable in
  the sense that failure is detectable and strict success is certifiable.
4. The Gaussian margin law `min σ_ε ≲ e^{−49.9/ε}` and the τ = 0 identity (Thm 7).
5. Obstructions at every rung: amplification (`cor:void` analogue), and failure of all rungs without a Γ-term
  (Thm 8(a), (b)). Together they show that the Gaussian ladder evades T3 and is tied to OT-12.

**Not new / reformulation:**
* `RH ⟺ ε_G = 0` is Weil's criterion passed through a positivity-preserving semigroup. It is an EQUIVALENT
  reformulation.
* Widder's reading adds no content.
* Thm 5 is small-support archimedean dominance (Yoshida-type) in Gaussian dress.
* Thm 6 is a zero-side computation of the kind used for de Bruijn–Newman upper bounds (Polymath 15), transplanted to
  the zero measure. The analogy is structural: `ε_G ≥ 0` is trivial, `ε_G = 0 ⟺ RH`, and upper bounds come from
  verification plus counting. It is the Weil-side analogue of `Λ_dBN ≤ 0.2`. It is **not** the dBN flow, which deforms
  Ξ and is arithmetic-blind (R157_002 row 41); this flow deforms the prime weights.

**Status for the success criterion of R159_000:** no object passes OT-5′ ∧ OT-1 ∧ OT-2 for ζ. The G1 objective is
**not** met.
