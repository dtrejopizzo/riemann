## §2 The third limit: `g → ∞` at fixed `q`

### 2.1 Setting

Notation of R1 §1: `C/F_q`, `η = log q`, Frobenius angles `θ_1,…,θ_{2g}` (`α_j = √q e^{iθ_j}`, `|α_j| = √q` by Weil,
`CITED` P46 (X27)), `s_n = Σ_j e^{inθ_j}`, `N_m = #C(F_{q^m}) = q^m + 1 − q^{m/2}s_m`, `B_m` = number of closed points
of degree `m`, `N_m = Σ_{d|m} d B_d`. Weil distribution `𝔚_C = η Σ_{n∈Z} s_n δ_{nη}` (R1 Lemma 1.2(i)), positive
definite (R1 Lemma 1.2(iv), the complete Castelnuovo form).

**Definition 2.1 (asymptotically exact family; Tsfasman–Vlăduţ, `CITED`).** A sequence of curves `C_k/F_q` with
`g_k → ∞` is asymptotically exact if `β_m := lim_k B_m(C_k)/g_k` exists for every `m ≥ 1`. (Every sequence with
`g_k → ∞` has an asymptotically exact subsequence, by diagonal extraction, since `0 ≤ B_m/g ≤ N_m/(mg)` and
`N_m ≤ q^m + 1 + 2g q^{m/2}` gives `B_m/g ≤ (q^m+1)/(mg) + 2q^{m/2}/m`, bounded in `k`.)

**Definition 2.2 (normalised Weil distribution).** `𝔚̃_C := g^{−1}𝔚_C = η Σ_n (s_n/g) δ_{nη}`; spectral measure
`μ̃_C := g^{−1} Σ_j Σ_{k∈Z} δ_{(θ_j+2πk)/η}`, of mass `2` per period `2π/η`.

### 2.2 The limit object and its three properties

**Theorem A (`PROVED`; the analytic content is Tsfasman–Vlăduţ 1997, `CITED`, reproved here in the Weil-distribution
normalisation).** Let `(C_k)` be asymptotically exact over `F_q`. Then:

(i) *(convergence)* `𝔚̃_{C_k} → 𝔚_∞ := 2η δ_0 − η Σ_{n≠0} q^{−|n|/2} (Σ_{m | |n|} m β_m) δ_{nη}` in `𝒟'(R)`, and
`μ̃_{C_k} → μ_∞` weakly, where `μ_∞` is the `2π/η`-periodic positive measure with Fourier coefficients
`ŝ_0 = 2`, `ŝ_n = −q^{−|n|/2}Σ_{m | |n|} mβ_m` (`n ≠ 0`).

(ii) *(no pole, conductor atom `2ηδ_0`)* The polar terms `q^{|n|/2}+q^{−|n|/2}` of `s_n` (R1 Lemma 1.2(ii)) are
divided by `g_k → ∞` and vanish in the limit. `𝔚_∞` consists of the conductor atom `2ηδ_0` and the prime side only:
it is the "difference-only / counterterm" form that P46 Rem 21.23 says any transport of the curve Gram must take
(`κ(0) = 2 − 2g → −∞`, here regularised to `κ(0)/g → −2` in intersection sign, `+2` in Weil sign).

(iii) *(positivity, inherited)* `𝔚_∞` is positive definite: `⟨𝔚_∞, F⋆F̃⟩ = ∫|F̂|² dμ_∞ ≥ 0`. Its positivity at the
Poisson kernel is the Drinfeld–Vlăduţ inequality: `Σ_{m≥1} mβ_m/(q^{m/2}−1) ≤ 1`.

*Proof.* (i) For `n ≠ 0`, `s_n/g = (q^{|n|/2}+q^{−|n|/2})/g − q^{−|n|/2}Σ_{m | |n|} m B_m/g → −q^{−|n|/2}Σ_{m | |n|} mβ_m`;
for `n = 0`, `s_0/g = 2`. Coefficientwise convergence of the `δ_{nη}` weights, with the weights of `𝔚̃_{C_k}` uniformly
bounded by `|s_n|/g ≤ 2` (since `|e^{inθ_j}| = 1`), gives convergence in `𝒟'(R)` against every `φ ∈ C_c^∞` (finitely
many lattice points in `supp φ`). The measures `μ̃_{C_k}` on the circle `R/(2π/η)Z` have mass 2 and Fourier
coefficients `s_n/g` converging for each `n`; trigonometric polynomials are dense in `C(circle)`, so `μ̃_{C_k}` converges
weakly to a positive measure `μ_∞` with the stated coefficients, and `𝔚_∞ = μ̂_∞` by Poisson (R1 Lemma 1.2(i)).
(ii) is read off (i). (iii) `μ_∞ ≥ 0` as a weak limit of positive measures; the identity `⟨𝔚_∞, F⋆F̃⟩ = ∫|F̂|²dμ_∞`
is R1 Lemma 1.2(iv) in the limit. For the Poisson kernel `P_r(θ) = Σ_n r^{|n|}e^{inθ} ≥ 0` (`0 < r < 1`),
`0 ≤ ∫P_r dμ_∞ = 2 − 2Σ_{n≥1} r^n q^{−n/2}Σ_{m|n} mβ_m`. Let `r ↑ 1` (monotone convergence, all terms `≥ 0`):
`Σ_{n≥1} q^{−n/2}Σ_{m|n} mβ_m = Σ_m mβ_m Σ_{k≥1} q^{−mk/2} = Σ_m mβ_m/(q^{m/2}−1) ≤ 1`. ∎

**Remark A.1 (what is new in Theorem A).** The mathematics is Tsfasman–Vlăduţ's. New for the record: (a) the
infinite-genus direction of Rem 21.23 has a precise limit object, `𝔚_∞`, and it **drops the pole**: infinite spectral
genus and a polar hyperbolic plane (ζ's rulings `F_1, F_2`, R1 §0) are mutually exclusive under genus normalisation;
ζ keeps both, which is why its conductor atom is the divergent `Δ·Δ = −∞` (Paper 44 Thm 8bis.2) rather than a
normalisable `2`. (b) The Drinfeld–Vlăduţ bound is the complete Castelnuovo form evaluated at the Poisson kernel, i.e.
one test function of Weil positivity — a *consumer* of RH for curves.

### 2.3 What the `g → ∞` limit does not do

**Theorem B (`PROVED`).** (i) Every `𝔚_∞` of Theorem A is supported in `ηZ ⊂ L = {0} ∪ {±log m : m a prime power}`;
hence `𝔚_ζ` is not a limit of normalised curve data at fixed `q`, nor at varying `q` without dilation (R1 Thm 2(a),(b)
apply verbatim, since normalisation by `g` does not change supports). (ii) With dilations and positive scalars the
closure of `{𝔚_∞}` is contained in the Bochner cone `𝒫_sym` of R1 Thm 4; on this cone "`𝔚_ζ` belongs" is RH
(R1 Thm 4, `EQUIVALENT`). (iii) The normalisation is zero-blind (R1 Thm 5): `𝔚_∞` is determined by `(β_m)`, which are
prime-side data, and its positivity is not derived from them but inherited from `|α_j| = √q` member by member.

*Proof.* (i) `supp 𝔚̃_C ⊆ ηZ` for every member; a `𝒟'`-limit is supported in the closed set `ηZ`; R1 Thm 2(a)(b).
(ii) Each `𝔚_∞` is positive definite (Thm A(iii)) and symmetric; dilates and positive multiples of positive-definite
symmetric distributions are positive definite; `𝒫_sym` is closed in `𝒟'` (Bochner–Schwartz), so the closure lies
in `𝒫_sym`. (iii) Theorem A(i) writes `𝔚_∞` in terms of `(β_m)`; the inequality `∫|F̂|²dμ_∞ ≥ 0` used Weil's theorem
(`|α_j| = √q`) for each `C_k`, nowhere else. ∎

**Corollary B.1 (OT-5′ status of the limit object; `PROVED` by inspection).** Per member, `𝔚_C` passes OT-5′ (Hodge
index on `C×C`, proved from Riemann–Roch on the surface before the spectrum; R1 Lemma 1.2 Remark). The limit `𝔚_∞`
has a form defined before the spectrum (from `(β_m)`, prime data) whose positivity is a **limit of member
positivities**. A limit object cannot pass OT-5′ for ζ: to reach ζ one needs `𝔚_ζ` in the closure, which by
Theorem B(ii) is RH. This is N-a in the negative: the count (`B_m`) fixes the form; the positivity is carried, not
delivered.

### 2.4 The number-field twin (Tsfasman–Vlăduţ infinite global fields)

**Theorem D (`PROVED` given `CITED`).** In Tsfasman–Vlăduţ's asymptotic theory of number fields (`g_K = log√|Δ_K|`,
asymptotically exact families `φ_α = lim N_α(K_i)/g_i`):
(i) `Q` is the **unique** global field of RR-genus `g_K = 0` (Minkowski: `|Δ_K| > 1` for `K ≠ Q`), so `Q` is a
*member* — the genus-zero member — and never a limit: every asymptotically exact family has `g_i → ∞` and its limit
zeta `ζ_φ(s) = Π_q (1−q^{−s})^{−φ_q}` has `φ_q ≤ 1/(q−1) · (1 − …) < 1` weights, while `ζ_Q` has weight `1` at every
prime and a pole.
(ii) The basic inequality for number fields is proved **under GRH** (TV Thm 3.1); unconditionally only the weaker
Prop 3.2 (with `q − 1` in place of `√q − 1`) holds. The archimedean constants in Thm 3.1 (`log√(8π) + π/4 + γ/2`,
`log 8π + γ`) are the Γ-side values of Weil's explicit formula at the same test function. So the number-field theory
is a **consumer** of Weil positivity (Odlyzko–Serre style), exactly as the function-field theory of §2.2 is a consumer
of Weil's theorem: neither produces positivity.
(iii) *(the genus paradox, sharp form of R2 Thm 4)* Over `F_q`, `g_RR = g_spec` for every curve (`h¹(𝒪) = g`,
`dim H¹ = 2g`); this identity is what Stepanov's error `(2g+1)√q` and Weil's Castelnuovo argument both use. For `Q`:
`g_RR = 0` (unique among global fields) and `g_spec = ∞` (infinitely many zeros; `thm:finZ`). The coupling fails
maximally at the one field the programme is about. Any transport that lets the genus enter through Riemann–Roch
(Stepanov's (RR); the frame's `h⁰`, P46 Thm 19.25) transports `0`, not `∞`.

*Proof.* (i) Minkowski's bound `|Δ_K| ≥ (π/4)^{n}(n^n/n!)^2 > 1` for `n ≥ 2`; `g_i = log√|Δ_i| → ∞` is part of the
definition of the families in TV (growing discriminant); `φ_q ≤ 1/(q−1)`-type bounds follow from Prop 3.2 with all
other terms dropped; `ζ_Q` has `Λ`-weight `log p` at `p^k`, i.e. Euler weight `1`. (ii) is the cited statements;
the archimedean constants are the standard Γ-side evaluations (Poitou–Serre). (iii) `h¹(𝒪_C) = g` and Weil's
`dim H¹ = 2g` (`CITED` P46 (X27)); `½log|Δ_Q| = 0` (E1); `g_spec = ∞` by Hardy/Riemann–von Mangoldt. ∎

**Reading.** P46 Rem 21.23's "infinite genus" is the **spectral** genus. In the only asymptotic theory that gives
"genus" a Riemann–Roch meaning over number fields, `Q` has genus `0` and the infinite-genus objects are limits of
other fields, not `Q`. No `g → ∞` limit approaches `Q` from within number fields, and none approaches `𝔚_ζ` from
curves (Theorem B). The phrase "ζ is a curve of infinite genus over `F_1`" therefore has, in this angle, exactly one
proved content: the polar pair cannot coexist with a normalisable conductor atom (Theorem A(ii)).

## §3 Exhaustion of the function-field transport (Theorem E)

**Theorem E (exhaustion; `PROVED` by the record + §2).** Let a "function-field transport" be any of:
(T1) `q → 1` undilated; (T2) `q → 1` (or any `q`) with dilations and scalars, closure in `𝒟'`; (T3) `g → ∞` at fixed
`q`, normalised; (T4) the Castelnuovo–Hodge-index proof carried to a two-variable carrier over `Spec Z` via a
Riemann–Roch/`h⁰`; (T5) the Bombieri–Stepanov proof carried to `Spec Z`; (T6) Deligne/Weil II transport. Then:

| route | classification | proved where | step at which OT-5′ is lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | EXCL (unconditional) | R1 Thm 2 | support in `L`; no archimedean place (OT-12) |
| T2 | EQUIVALENT | R1 Thm 4 | closure = Bochner cone; `𝔚_ζ ∈ 𝒫_sym` is RH |
| T3 | EXCL as a route to ζ; positivity inherited | Thm A, B, Cor B.1 | support (fixed `q`); with dilations → T2 |
| T4 | RH-strength at bigness | P46 Prop 21.21, Thm 19.25, Cor 19.25b, Thm 21.15; R157_002 row 47; OT-9 (A15) | (P145′)(4a) "bigness" = RH; frame has no `+` (Rem 21.23) |
| T5 | EQUIVALENT (global), EXCL per window | R2 Thm 1 (Landau), Prop 1.2–1.3; `cor:void` | the one-sided input is RH; (Amp_x) has no candidate (E4: Siegel-lemma variant OPEN, RH-strength) |
| T6 | EXCL | R157_002 row 47; P46 Thm 22.17 | rationality = finite zero set (`thm:finZ`); no Frobenius endomorphism on `Spec Z` |

In every limit route (T1–T3) the positivity of the limit object is a limit of Weil's theorem for the members, and ζ's
membership in the limit cone is the sign itself; in every proof route (T4–T6) the input that makes the curve-level
argument work (integrality of `h⁰` with `g_RR = g_spec`; inseparable Frobenius; rationality) is absent over `Spec Z`
and its substitute is RH-strength. Hence **no function-field transport passes OT-5′ for ζ**, and the angle is
exhausted at the level of routes.

*Proof.* Each row cites its proved statement. The dichotomy limit/proof is exhaustive for "transport of Castelnuovo
positivity": either the positive form on the `Spec Z` side is obtained as a limit of the curve forms (then it is a
distributional limit of positive-definite lattice measures, and T1–T3 cover the three parameters `q`, dilation, `g`
that the family has — R1 Def 1.1 and Def 2.2 here; `q` and `g` are the only invariants of the family entering `𝔚_C`
besides the angles), or it is obtained by re-running a proof (then it must supply the proof's inputs, which are those
of T4–T6 by R2 Thm 2 and P46 §21). ∎

**Remark E.1 (`HEURISTIC`).** The exhaustion is at the level of *routes*, i.e. of the six transport mechanisms named.
A seventh mechanism — a carrier over `Spec Z` that is neither a limit of curve data nor a rerun of a curve proof —
would not be a "function-field transport" in the sense of this angle; it is the business of the other G1 angles
(Deninger/Connes–Consani/Borger), on which the panel's R2 verdicts (B2 Cor D2; B6 Thm A) impose the entanglement and
resolution constraints.

## §5 What is genuinely new (for the record; none of it gives a sign)

1. **Theorem A** (`PROVED`; content Tsfasman–Vlăduţ, `CITED`): the infinite-genus limit of the complete Castelnuovo
  forms exists, is positive definite, **has no polar term**, has conductor atom `2ηδ_0`, and is determined by the
  prime-side densities `β_m`; the Drinfeld–Vlăduţ bound is its value at the Poisson kernel. This is the precise
