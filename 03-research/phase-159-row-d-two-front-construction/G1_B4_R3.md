## §2 Theorem W: the tail wall

### 2.1 Setting

Partition the primes as `P = P_0 ⊔ ⨆_{j≥1} B_j`, with each `B_j` finite and nonempty, infinitely many `j`, and `P_0`
arbitrary (it may be empty or infinite).

**Spaces.**
* `H_0` is a Hilbert space. Only its Hilbert topology is used, so it may carry any additional Krein or Pontryagin form.
* For `j ≥ 1`, `H_j` is a Hilbert space with a unit vector `ξ_j`.
* `X := H_0 ⊗ ⊗'_{j≥1}(H_j, ξ_j)` is von Neumann's incomplete tensor product: the completion of the span `𝒟` of
  elementary vectors `v_0 ⊗ v_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ v_K ⊗ ξ_{K+1} ⊗ ξ_{K+2} ⊗ ⋯`, with
  `⟨⊗x_j, ⊗y_j⟩ = Π_j ⟨x_j, y_j⟩` (a finite product on `𝒟`). `CITED` (std.): J. von Neumann, *On infinite direct
  products*, Compositio Math. 6 (1939); A. Guichardet, *Symmetric Hilbert spaces…*, LNM 261, Ch. 1.

**Frobenius.**
* For `p ∈ B_j`, `S_p := T_{j,p}` acting on the `j`-th factor, where `T_{j,p}` is a bounded operator on `H_j`.
* For `p ∈ P_0`, `S_p := A_p ⊗ 1`, with `A_p` bounded on `H_0` and otherwise **arbitrary**.
* No commutation, unitarity, positivity or continuity is assumed.

Two **tail hypotheses** are used, one or the other:
* **(C)** every `T_{j,p}` is a contraction;
* **(N)** every `T_{j,p}` is normal, of any norm, and the `T_{j,p}` with `p ∈ B_j` commute.

**Notation.**
* For `γ ∈ C`: `E_j(γ) := {x ∈ H_j : T_{j,p}x = p^{iγ}x ∀p ∈ B_j}`, a closed subspace, with orthogonal projection
  `P_j(γ)`.
* `a_j(γ) := ‖P_j(γ)ξ_j‖² ∈ [0,1]`.
* `ι_{B}(γ) := (p^{iγ})_{p∈B}`.

`p^{iγ}` means `e^{iγ log p}`. For real `γ` it is unimodular; this is the critical line in the normalisation where the zero
`ρ = ½ + iγ` corresponds to the normalised Frobenius eigenvalue `p^{ρ−½}`.

**The critical joint spectrum** is `Γ(X) := {γ : ∃ w ∈ X∖0, S_p w = p^{iγ}w for all p ∈ P}`. **The tail criterion set** is
`A(ξ) := {γ : ∃ j_0 with a_j(γ) > 0 for j ≥ j_0 and Σ_{j≥j_0}(1 − a_j(γ)) < ∞}`. It depends only on
`(T_{j,·}, ξ_j)_{j≥j_0}` for any `j_0`, i.e. only on the tail.

### 2.2 Four lemmas

**Lemma W1 (separating one factor; `CITED` (std.), von Neumann 1939, associativity).** For each `j` there is a unitary
`X ≅ H_j ⊗ X^{(j)}`, where `X^{(j)}` is the incomplete tensor product of the other factors. It is the identity on
elementary vectors, and it carries `S_p` (`p ∈ B_j`) to `T_{j,p} ⊗ 1`.

**Lemma W2 (kernels of amplified operators; `PROVED`).** Let `H` and `K` be Hilbert spaces and `B` bounded on `H`. Then
`ker(B ⊗ 1_K) = ker(B) ⊗ K`.

*Proof.* Choose an orthonormal basis `(e_k)` of `K`, which may be uncountable. Every `w ∈ H ⊗ K` is uniquely
`w = Σ_k w_k ⊗ e_k`, with `Σ‖w_k‖² = ‖w‖²`, and `(B⊗1)w = Σ_k Bw_k ⊗ e_k`. So `(B⊗1)w = 0` iff `Bw_k = 0` for all `k`,
iff `w ∈ ker B ⊗ K`. ∎

**Lemma W3 (orthogonality of unimodular eigenspaces; `PROVED`).**
* (a) If `T` is a contraction, `|μ| = 1` and `Tx = μx`, then `T^*x = μ̄x`.
* (b) Under (C), if `γ, γ′ ∈ R` and `ι_{B_j}(γ) ≠ ι_{B_j}(γ′)`, then `E_j(γ) ⊥ E_j(γ′)`.
* (b′) Under (N), the same holds for all `γ, γ′ ∈ C`.

*Proof.*
* (a) `‖T^*x − μ̄x‖² = ‖T^*x‖² − 2Re(μ⟨T^*x, x⟩) + ‖x‖²`. Here `⟨T^*x, x⟩ = ⟨x, Tx⟩ = μ̄‖x‖²`, so the middle term is
  `−2‖x‖²`, and the whole is `‖T^*x‖² − ‖x‖² ≤ 0`, because `‖T^*‖ ≤ 1`.
* (b) Pick `p ∈ B_j` with `μ := p^{iγ} ≠ ν := p^{iγ′}`. For `x ∈ E_j(γ)` and `y ∈ E_j(γ′)`, (a) applied to `y` gives
  `μ⟨x,y⟩ = ⟨Tx, y⟩ = ⟨x, T^*y⟩ = ⟨x, ν̄y⟩ = ν⟨x, y⟩`. So `⟨x, y⟩ = 0`.
* (b′) For normal `T`, `Ty = νy` implies `T^*y = ν̄y` (`CITED` (std.): `‖(T−ν)y‖ = ‖(T−ν)^*y‖`), and the same line
  applies. ∎

**Lemma W4 (infinite products of factor projections; `PROVED`).** Let `P_j` be orthogonal projections on `H_j` for
`j ≥ 1`, and let `R_j` be `P_j` acting on the `j`-th factor of `X`. Suppose that for every `j_0`,
`Π_{j≥j_0}‖P_jξ_j‖² = 0`. Then `⋂_j ran R_j = {0}`.

*Proof.*
* The `R_j` are commuting orthogonal projections, because they act on different factors.
* So `Q_J := R_1⋯R_J` is a decreasing sequence of projections. Its strong limit `Q` is the projection onto `⋂_j ran R_j`.
* For an elementary `v = v_0 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ v_K ⊗ ξ_{K+1} ⊗ ⋯` and `J > K`,
  `Q_J v = v_0 ⊗ P_1v_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ P_Kv_K ⊗ P_{K+1}ξ_{K+1} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ P_Jξ_J ⊗ ξ_{J+1} ⊗ ⋯`.
  This is again elementary, so `‖Q_J v‖² = ‖v_0‖² Π_{j≤K}‖P_jv_j‖² Π_{K<j≤J}‖P_jξ_j‖²`.
* By hypothesis with `j_0 = K+1`, this tends to 0, so `Qv = 0` for all `v ∈ 𝒟`.
* `𝒟` spans a dense subspace and `Q` is bounded, so `Q = 0`. ∎

### 2.3 The theorem

**Theorem W (`PROVED`).** Assume (C) and let `γ ∈ R`, or assume (N) and let `γ ∈ C`.
* (i) If some `w ≠ 0` satisfies `S_p w = p^{iγ} w` for all **tail** primes `p ∈ ⨆B_j`, then `γ ∈ A(ξ)`.
* (ii) `A(ξ)` has at most one element (in `R` under (C), in `C` under (N)). Hence `|Γ(X)| ≤ 1`, and even the weaker
  tail-only joint spectrum has at most one point. That point is determined by the tail `(T_{j,·}, ξ_j)_{j≥j_0}`, for any
  `j_0`.
* (iii) **Any interpolating flow.** Let `(V_t)` be any family of operators on `X` indexed by `t ∈ R` or `t ≥ 0`, with
  `V_{log p} = S_p` for the tail primes. No semigroup law, continuity, boundedness, contractivity or positivity is assumed.
  If `w ≠ 0` satisfies `V_t w = e^{iγt} w` for `t ∈ {log p}`, then `γ` is the unique element of `A(ξ)`. This applies in
  particular to eigenvectors `Θw = iγw` of the generator of a `C_0` semigroup or group with `V_{log p} = S_p`.
* (iv) **Direct sums.** If `X = ⊕_k X^{(k)}` is a countable orthogonal sum of `S`-invariant summands, each
  tail-factorizable in the above sense (with its own partition and tail), then `Γ(X) ⊆ ⋃_k A^{(k)}`, with **at most one
  point per summand**.

*Proof.*
* (i) Fix `j`, and apply W1 and W2 to `B = T_{j,p} − p^{iγ}` for each `p ∈ B_j`. Then `w ∈ E_j(γ) ⊗ X^{(j)}`, i.e.
  `R_j w = w` with `R_j := P_j(γ)` on the `j`-th factor. So `0 ≠ w ∈ ⋂_j ran R_j`. By W4 (contrapositive) there is a
  `j_0` with `Π_{j≥j_0} a_j(γ) > 0`. For numbers in `[0,1]`, this means all factors are positive and
  `Σ(1 − a_j) < ∞` (`CITED` (std.)). So `γ ∈ A(ξ)`.
* (ii) Let `γ ≠ γ′` both lie in `A(ξ)`.
  * `ι_{B_j}(γ) = ι_{B_j}(γ′)` means `(γ−γ′)log p ∈ 2πZ` for all `p ∈ B_j`, which forces `γ − γ′ ∈ R`. If this held for
  two different `j`, with `p ∈ B_j` and `q ∈ B_{j′}`, then `log p/log q ∈ Q`, i.e. `p^a = q^b` with `a, b ≥ 1`. That
  is impossible. So for all but at most one `j`, `ι_{B_j}(γ) ≠ ι_{B_j}(γ′)`.
  * For those `j`, W3 gives `P_j(γ)P_j(γ′) = 0`, so `a_j(γ) + a_j(γ′) ≤ ‖ξ_j‖² = 1`.
  * But membership in `A(ξ)` forces `a_j(γ) → 1` and `a_j(γ′) → 1`. This is a contradiction.
  * Tail dependence: the definition of `A(ξ)` is insensitive to finitely many `j`.
* (iii) `S_p w = V_{log p}w = p^{iγ}w` for all tail primes. Apply (i) and (ii). For a generator eigenvector,
  `V_t w = e^{iγt}w` for all `t` (`CITED` (std.), Engel–Nagel II.3.1(ii)'s spectral inclusion
  `e^{tσ_p(Θ)} ⊆ σ_p(V_t)`, with the same eigenvector).
* (iv) If `w = Σ_k w_k` is a joint eigenvector, then each `w_k` is one, because the orthogonal projections onto the
  summands commute with every `S_p`. Invariance of `X^{(k)}` and of its orthogonal complement is part of "orthogonal sum
  of invariant summands". Apply (ii) to each `k` with `w_k ≠ 0`. ∎

**Remark W5 (relation to the record).**
* B2 Theorem E is the special case "`T_{j,p}` unitary and `ξ_j` fixed", with the conclusion restricted to the suspended
  part. Its repair by the round-2 panel, `Γ_tail`, is exactly the set `A(ξ)`.
* The proof here does not pass through Theorem S, Bochner or Lusin–Souslin.
* It also shows the P1/P5 gap (`P_0 = ∅`) is immaterial: `H_0` plays no role in W4.

**Remark W6 (what Theorem W does not use, `PROVED` by inspection; its scope).** Nothing on `H_0` is used: not the form
(P-HS or P-HI), not the operators `A_p`, not the flow. The hypothesis is the tail only: infinitely many independent
finite blocks of primes, each acting on its own Hilbert factor by contractions (or normal operators), and the eigenvector
living in the incomplete tensor product. In particular:
* **P-HI (Hodge-index) carriers.** If the indefinite part sits in finitely many factors, these can be put into `H_0`, and
  Theorem W applies verbatim. That it *must* sit in finitely many factors, for a finite Pontryagin index, is `HEURISTIC`:
  for finite-dimensional factors with signature `(π_j, κ_j)`, `π_j, κ_j ≥ 1`, the negative index of the product of `J` of
  them is at least `2^{J−1}` (induction: `κ(H⊗H′) = πκ′ + κπ′`). A theory of incomplete tensor products of Krein spaces is
  not developed here.
* **Correspondence-valued Frobenius.** A normalised correspondence acting contractively (a Markov-type or Hecke-type
  operator of norm `≤ 1` after normalisation) is covered under (C). No dilation is needed.
* **Weil's case (`HEURISTIC` reading).** A curve over `F_q` has one prime, `P_0 = {p}` and no tail, so the wall is vacuous.
  This is why a single summand `H¹(C)` can carry `2g` eigenvalues. For ζ the wall bites because there are infinitely many
  primes and a single `Frob_p` determines `γ` only modulo `2π/log p`.

**Corollary W7 (N-e₁ extended; `PROVED` as a filter).** Let a candidate object have a carrier `X` on which some flow
interpolates Frobenius at `t = log p` (in the sense of W(iii)), and whose critical-line eigenvalues are to be the zero
ordinates. Suppose `X` is a countable orthogonal sum of tail-factorizable summands under (C) or (N). Then it needs **at
least one summand per zero**, i.e. installation (B2 Prop R). With (N), the same holds for all zeros, on or off the line.
By Lemma T and C16 such an object fails OT-3 or OT-4. So in any object that passes, Frobenius on the zero-carrying part
must be entangled across infinitely many primes (E1), or be neither contractive nor normal on the tail (E2), or the flow
must not interpolate Frobenius (E3).

## §3 Proposition BW: the Bost–Connes / Witt Frobenius

**Setting (`CITED` (std.)).**
* On `ℓ²(N^×)`, with basis `(ε_n)`, the Bost–Connes isometries are `μ_n ε_m = ε_{nm}` (Bost–Connes, Selecta 1995, §3).
* In the ghost coordinates of the big Witt vectors `W(Z) → Π_{m≥1} Z`, the Verschiebung and Frobenius act by
  `(V_n a)(m) = n·a(m/n)` (0 if `n ∤ m`) and `(F_n a)(m) = a(nm)`, so `F_nV_n = n` (Hesselholt, *Lecture notes on Witt
  vectors*, §1).
* After the unitary rescaling `a(m) ↦ a(m)m^{−1/2}`… precisely, on the weighted space `ℓ²(N^×, m^{−1})`, the operators
  `V_n/√n` and `F_n/√n` are unitarily equivalent to `μ_n` and `μ_n^*`. (`PROVED`, direct: with `b(m) = a(m)/√m`,
  `(V_n a)(m)/(√n·√m) = √n·a(m/n)/√(nm) = b(m/n)`.)
* Borger's λ-ring Frobenius lifts on `W(Z)` restrict to these operators on ghost components.

**Proposition BW (`PROVED`).**
* (i) `ℓ²(N^×) = ⊗'_p(ℓ²(N_0), δ_0)` via `ε_n ↔ ⊗_p δ_{v_p(n)}`, and `μ_p` is the unilateral shift on the `p`-th factor.
  So the family is Euler-factorizable (`P_0 = ∅`, `B_j = {p_j}`) with contractive tail: hypothesis (C).
* (ii) The `μ_p` have **no** eigenvectors, so `Γ = ∅` for the Frobenius direction.
* (iii) The joint eigenvectors of the adjoints `μ_p^*` are exactly the multiples of `w_λ := Σ_n λ(n)ε_n` with `λ`
  completely multiplicative, `|λ_p| < 1` and `Σ_p|λ_p|² < ∞`. Here `‖w_λ‖² = Π_p(1 − |λ_p|²)^{−1}`. On the Kronecker
  slice `λ_p = p^{−s̄}`: `w_s = Σ n^{−s̄}ε_n` exists **iff `Re s > ½`**, with `‖w_s‖² = ζ(2 Re s)` and `μ_p^*w_s = p^{−s̄}w_s`.
* (iv) For `F = Σ a_nε_n ∈ ℓ²`, `⟨F, w_s⟩ = Σ a_n n^{−s} =: D_F(s)` for `Re s > ½`. So the carrier is the Hardy space `H²`
  of Dirichlet series (Hedenmalm–Lindqvist–Seip), the `μ_n` are multiplication by `n^{−s}`, and invariant subspaces are
  where ζ's zeros could enter. RH is equivalent to a cyclicity/density statement in this `H²` (Noor, Adv. Math. 350
  (2019), arXiv:1809.09577, the H² form of Báez-Duarte; `CITED`, abstract read this phase). That is EQUIV, R157_002
  row 43.

*Proof.*
* (i) The map is unitary, since it sends basis to basis. `μ_p` raises `v_p` by one.
* (ii) `μ_p x = cx` with `x ≠ 0` is impossible for the unilateral shift: the lowest nonzero coordinate moves up.
* (iii) The condition is `μ_p^* w = λ_p w` with `w = Σ f(n)ε_n`. Since `μ_p^* ε_{pm} = ε_m` and `μ_p^*ε_n = 0` for
  `p ∤ n`, this reads `f(pm) = λ_p f(m)` for all `m`. So `f(n) = f(1)Π_pλ_p^{v_p(n)}`, which is completely
  multiplicative, and `f(1) ≠ 0` for `w ≠ 0`. Then
  `Σ|f(n)|² = |f(1)|² Π_p Σ_k|λ_p|^{2k} = |f(1)|²Π_p(1 − |λ_p|²)^{−1}` (Euler product of nonnegative terms, Tonelli).
  This is finite iff every `|λ_p| < 1` and `Σ|λ_p|² < ∞`. For `λ_p = p^{−s̄}`, `|λ_p|² = p^{−2σ}`, and `Σp^{−2σ} < ∞` iff
  `σ > ½`; the product is then `ζ(2σ)`.
* (iv) The inner product is immediate. ∎

**Reading (`PROVED` for the facts; the verdict is inspection).** The BC/Witt carrier is the cleanest Frobenius object over
`F_1` on record.
* Its critical line is the boundary `Re s = ½` of the Frobenius point spectrum, and it is produced by the **pole**
  `ζ(2σ) → ∞` as `σ ↓ ½`, not by the zeros. It is zero-blind (consistent with R158_004 row 17(ii)).
* Its Frobenius is Euler-factorizable, so Theorem W allows at most one critical-line eigenvalue, and there is none.
* The only place zeros live is the lattice of invariant subspaces, and that route is Báez-Duarte, EQUIV.

So the Bost–Connes and Borger objects that the panel asked to be tested against N-e **satisfy the wall and do not escape
it**. They escape B2's unitary class only formally (isometries, not unitaries). Theorem W covers them.

## §4 Theorem H and Proposition G: the Frobenius that carries a `+` is weight 0

### 4.1 Theorem H: the group ring of Rem 22.18

**Setting.**
* `K/Q` is finite Galois with `G = Gal(K/Q)`, and `S` is the set of ramified primes.
* For `p ∉ S`, `C_p ⊂ G` is the Frobenius class.
* `Z[G]` carries the involution `x† = Σ x̄_g g^{−1}` and the trace `τ(x) = x_1`. Then `τ(xx†) = Σ|x_g|²` (`PROVED`,
  direct), so `C[G]` is a finite-dimensional C*-algebra.

**Theorem H (`PROVED`).**
* (i) **(Degree identity: the `+` is realised.)** For `x ∈ Z[G]` with `L_x` (left multiplication) injective,
  `#(Z[G]/xZ[G]) = |det L_x| = Π_ρ |det ρ(x)|^{dim ρ}`, the product running over the irreducible complex representations.
  For `x = m + n g` (`m, n ∈ Z`, `g ∈ G`) this is `Π_ρ Π_{λ∈spec ρ(g)}|m + nλ|^{dim ρ}`, a nonnegative integer: an actual
  count. **P46 Thm 22.17(iii)'s additive structure is present in `Z[G]`, as Rem 22.18 anticipated.**
* (ii) **(Weight 0.)** Each factor is `|m + nλ|² = m² + 2(Re λ)mn + n²` with `λ` a root of unity. The Hasse-type inequality
  obtained from nonnegativity of all degrees, i.e. positive semidefiniteness of every binary factor, is `|Re λ| ≤ 1`,
  which is true for all unimodular `λ`. This is Weil's argument at `q = 1`. For the permutation representation on `G/D`,
  `Tr ρ(g_p^k) = #X_p(F_{p^k})` with `X_p = Spec(O_K/pO_K)` (`CITED` (std.), the Lefschetz formula in degree 0). So
  `Z[G]` realises `H⁰` of zero-dimensional `F_p`-schemes, of weight 0, and not an `H¹`.
* (iii) **(Assignment blindness.)** Let `𝒫` be the set of all statements "`φ(xx†) ≥ 0`" (with `φ` a positive functional
  on `C[G]`, e.g. `τ` or `Tr_ρ`) and all statements (i)–(ii), for `x` in the subring generated by a family `(g_p)_{p∉S}`.
  Then every statement of `𝒫` holds for **every** family `(g_p)` in `G`, not only for `g_p ∈ C_p`.
* (iv) **(A falsifier.)** Take `K = Q(i)`, `G = {1, c}`, sign character `ρ`. The true family gives
  `L(s, ρ) = L(s, χ_{−4})`. Fix a prime `p_* ≡ 1 mod 4` and flip it: `g′_{p_*} = c`, `g′_p = g_p` otherwise. The family
  `g′` satisfies all of `𝒫` by (iii), and
  `L(s, ρ, g′) = L(s, χ_{−4})·(1 − p_*^{−s})/(1 + p_*^{−s})`
  has zeros at `s = 2πik/log p_*` for every `k ∈ Z`: infinitely many zeros on `Re s = 0`, off the critical line.

  So **no derivation whose only inputs are `𝒫` and the membership `g_p ∈ G` proves GRH for `L(s, ρ)`, nor RH for
  `ζ_K = ζ·L(s, χ_{−4})`.** This is the schema of P46 Thm 18.1(ii) and `cor:void`.

*Proof.*
* (i) `xZ[G]` is the image of the lattice `Z[G] ≅ Z^{|G|}` under the integral matrix `L_x`. Its index is `|det L_x|` when
  that is nonzero (`CITED` (std.), Smith normal form). Over `C`, the left regular representation is `⊕_ρ ρ^{⊕ dim ρ}`
  (`CITED` (std.), Serre, *Linear representations*, §2.4), so `det L_x = Π_ρ det ρ(x)^{dim ρ}`. For `x = m + ng`,
  `ρ(x) = m + nρ(g)` has eigenvalues `m + nλ`, and `ρ(g)` is diagonalisable with root-of-unity eigenvalues because `g`
  has finite order. The eigenvalues of `ρ(g)` come in conjugate pairs with equal multiplicity (`ρ(g)` is conjugate to
  its complex conjugate on the character level, `χ(g^{−1}) = χ̄(g)`), which makes the product a nonnegative integer.
* (ii) The factors are as stated. A binary form `m² + 2bmn + n²` is positive semidefinite iff `|b| ≤ 1`. The fixed-point
  identity is the standard degree-0 Lefschetz count: `F_{p^k}`-points of `X_p` are the fixed points of `Frob^k` on
  `Hom(O_K, F̄_p) ≅ G/D_p`.
* (iii) Every statement of `𝒫` is a statement about elements of the finite group `G` and the C*-algebra `C[G]`, in which
  `φ(xx†) ≥ 0` holds for all `x`. None refers to which element `g_p` is.
* (iv) `L(s, ρ, g)` is the Euler product `Π_{p odd}(1 − ρ(g_p)p^{−s})^{−1}`, so flipping `p_*` multiplies it by
  `(1 − p_*^{−s})/(1 + p_*^{−s})`, since `χ_{−4}(p_*) = 1`. The numerator vanishes exactly at `p_*^{−s} = 1`, i.e.
  `s = 2πik/log p_*`. The denominator vanishes at `p_*^{−s} = −1`, a disjoint set.
  * `L(s, χ_{−4})` is entire, and it is nonzero on `Re s = 0`. For odd primitive `χ`,
  `Λ(s) = (4/π)^{(s+1)/2}Γ((s+1)/2)L(s,χ)` satisfies `Λ(s) = Λ(1−s)`.
  * At `s = it`, `Γ((1+it)/2) ≠ 0, ∞`, and `Λ(1−it)` is nonzero because `L(1−it, χ) ≠ 0` (de la Vallée Poussin) and
  `Γ(1 − it/2)` is finite and nonzero.
  * So the zeros at `s = 2πik/log p_*` are not cancelled. (`CITED` (std.) for the functional equation and non-vanishing
  on `Re s = 1`.) ∎

**Closing Rem 22.18 (`PROVED` as a method constraint; `HEURISTIC` as the reading "the + exists only at weight 0").**
Rem 22.18 asked whether `Z[Gal(K/Q)]` supplies the missing `+`.
* It does supply a genuine `+`, with a genuine degree identity (H(i)).
* The positivity that `+` carries is weight 0 (H(ii)) and assignment-blind (H(iii)). The only arithmetic content is the
  map `p ↦ C_p` (Artin reciprocity / Chebotarev), which is external to the positivity. The falsifier H(iv) satisfies all
  of it.
* So `Z[G]` does not supply the `+` "in the sense Theorem 22.17(iii) shows to be absent", i.e. one that would carry a sign.

### 4.2 Proposition G: Galois on Mordell–Weil

**Proposition G (`PROVED`, with `CITED` (std.) inputs).** Let `C/Q` have genus `g ≥ 1`, `J` its Jacobian, `ĥ` the
Néron–Tate height, and `V = J(Q̄)⊗_Z R` with the positive definite form `ĥ`. Positive definiteness of `ĥ` on `J(K)⊗R`
is Néron–Tate (`CITED` (std.), Hindry–Silverman B.5). Faltings–Hriljac identifies `−ĥ` with the arithmetic intersection
pairing on the orthogonal complement of the fibres (`CITED` (std.)).

For any choice of `σ_p ∈ Gal(Q̄/Q)` (a Frobenius lift at `p`: any element of any decomposition group at `p` mapping to
Frobenius):
* (i) `σ_p` acts on `V` as an isometry of `ĥ`;
* (ii) every `v ∈ V` has a finite `σ_p`-orbit;
* (iii) if `σ_p v = p^{iγ}v` and `σ_q v = q^{iγ}v` for two distinct primes and some `v ≠ 0` in `V⊗C`, then `γ = 0`.

*Proof.*
* (i) The Weil height is Galois-invariant, because Galois permutes the places of a Galois closure. The Néron–Tate height
  is the Tate limit of a Galois-invariant Weil height attached to a Galois-stable (defined over `Q`) symmetric ample
  class. So `ĥ(σP) = ĥ(P)`. The pairing is `Z`-bilinear, so `σ_p` is an isometry of `V`.
* (ii) Any `v` lies in `J(K)⊗R` for some finite Galois `K/Q`, because `J(Q̄) = ⋃_K J(K)`. `J(K)` is Galois-stable, and
  `Gal(Q̄/Q)` acts on it through the finite group `Gal(K/Q)`.
* (iii) On `J(K)⊗C`, `σ_p^N = 1` with `N = [K:Q]`, so `p^{iγN} = 1` and `q^{iγN} = 1`, i.e.
  `γ log p, γ log q ∈ (2π/N)Z`. If `γ ≠ 0`, then `log p/log q ∈ Q`, which is impossible. ∎

**Consequence (`PROVED` for the facts; the verdict is inspection).** On the FH carrier, the positivity (`ĥ`), the `+`
(the group law of `J`) and a Frobenius at every prime (Galois) do all coexist. But:
* the Frobenius is weight 0, i.e. of finite order on each finite-dimensional piece;
* its joint critical spectrum is `{0}` (G(iii));
* its Lefschetz numbers are Artin characters, so the positivity is Theorem H's, realised on `J(Q̄)`. `ĥ ≥ 0` controls
  `|χ(Frob_p)| ≤ dim`, which is automatic, and nothing about zeros. The equivariant heights of the Mordell–Weil Artin
  representations (equivariant BSD) are regulators, not Frobenius spectra.

R2 Thm A showed that no **flow** preserves Mordell–Weil. Prop G shows that the **discrete** Frobenius which does preserve
it is weight 0. This completes the FH angle: in the arithmetic surface over `Z`, the Frobenius of `Spec Z` itself (the
weight-`½` action whose eigenvalues would be `p^{ρ}`) has no realisation on the Hodge-index carrier. That is P46 Thm
22.17 read on the Faltings–Hriljac side.

## §6 What is genuinely new

1. **Theorem W.** The tail wall for contraction-valued or normal Frobenius on incomplete tensor products. It needs no
  unitarity, no suspension and no flow continuity, and places no condition on `H_0` or on the form. It supersedes B2
  Theorem E (with its `Γ_tail` repair), answers the round-2 panel's dilation/compression question directly, and covers
  P-HI carriers whose indefiniteness sits in finitely many factors.
2. **Prop BW.** The exact joint point spectrum of the Bost–Connes / Witt Frobenius: the `ℓ²`-polydisc. On the Kronecker
  slice it is `Re s > ½`, with norm `ζ(2σ)`: the critical line as the boundary produced by the pole. The facts are
  classical Hardy-space-of-Dirichlet-series theory. The formulation as a Frobenius test is new to the corpus.
3. **Theorem H.** It resolves P46 Rem 22.18 for the positivity it carries. The group ring realises Weil's `+` with an
  honest degree identity, but only at weight 0, and assignment-blind, with an explicit off-line falsifier. It is a
  proved instance showing that N-b (a correspondence algebra with `+`) is not sufficient.
4. **Prop G.** The Frobenius available on the Faltings–Hriljac carrier is Galois, weight 0, with joint critical spectrum
  `{0}`. With R2 Thm A, this closes the FH angle in both the flow reading and the discrete reading.

None of these is a sign, and none reopens row D.

## §7 Weakest step

* **Theorem W's scope hypothesis.** It needs the zero-carrying part to be an incomplete tensor product with the Frobenius
  of infinitely many independent blocks acting factorwise by contractions or normal operators. Escapes (E1)–(E3) remain,
  and (E2) is the least explored: tail operators that are non-normal and non-contractive, with unimodular eigenspaces at
  angles tending to 0. The power-bounded case with a uniform bound `M` looks provable by mean-ergodic idempotents
  (`‖P_μ‖ ≤ M`, `P_μP_ν = 0`), but the infinite product of non-orthogonal idempotents in W4 is not controlled. `OPEN`.
* **Remark W6's P-HI clause** (finite Pontryagin index forces finitely many indefinite factors) is `HEURISTIC`: Krein
  incomplete tensor products are not developed.
* **Lemma W1** (associativity of incomplete tensor products) is `CITED` (std.), not re-proved.
* **Theorem H(iv)** excludes only derivations whose inputs are `𝒫` and membership. As with R2 Thm C and `cor:void`, it is
  a schema-type method constraint. A construction that uses the Chebotarev map `p ↦ C_p` analytically is outside it, and
  then the content is ζ_K's analytic theory, not the ring.
* The G1 objective is **not** met by this memo.
