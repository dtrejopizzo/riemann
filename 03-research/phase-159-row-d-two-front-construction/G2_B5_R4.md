## 1. Set-up

Notation is that of P46 (`eq:symbol`, `eq:form`, `eq:W`): `I_T = (−T,T)`, `F̂(τ) = ∫F(t)e^{iτt}dt`,
`c_F(u) = ∫F(t)F(t−u)dt` for real `F`, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`, and `P_T = {A(F) = B(F) = 0}`. `λ^A_T` is the bottom of `𝔞_T`
on the unit sphere of `P_T`, and `λ^W_T` is the bottom of `W_T` on the unit sphere of `L²(I_T)`. Also `P := −∂² + ¼`.

**The Weil distribution** (G2_B9_R3 §1, verified by the panel, re-read against P46 `thm:S`/`eq:A`):
`u_Λ = k_∞ + 2cosh(u/2) − Σ_{n≥2} (Λ(n)/√n)(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n})`. It is real and even. On `|u| > 0`, `k_∞` is the
function `−½K(|u|)`, with `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2) = O(e^{−u/2})` as `u → ∞`. For real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`,

`(1.1) W_T(F) = ⟨u_Λ, c_F⟩`, and `𝔞_T(F) = ⟨u_Λ, c_F⟩` if moreover `F ∈ P_T`. **PROVED** (B9 R3 §1).

**Monotonicity.** `λ^W_T` and `λ^A_T` are nonincreasing in `T` (`lem:monoT`: `W_T` and `𝔞_T|_{P_T}` are restrictions
of the forms on larger windows). So `M_A` and `M_W` are nondecreasing. **PROVED** (record).

**Lemma 1.2 (trivial bound; PROVED).** `λ^A_T ≥ C_0 − Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n = C_0 − (4 + o(1))e^T`, and
`λ^W_T ≥ C_0 − (6 + o(1))e^T`, where `C_0 = ψ(¼) − log π`. Hence `κ_A ≤ κ_W ≤ ½`.
*Proof.* By P46 `eq:A`, `𝔞_T(F) = C_0‖F‖² + ∫_0^∞(‖F‖² − c_F(u))K(u)du − Σ_{N_T} v_n c_F(log n)`. For real `F`,
`|c_F| ≤ ‖F‖²` (Cauchy–Schwarz), so the middle term is `≥ 0` and the last term is `≥ −(Σ_{N_T} v_n)‖F‖²`. By the prime
number theorem and partial summation, `Σ_{n<X}Λ(n)n^{−1/2} = (2 + o(1))√X`, and `X = e^{2T}`. For the polar term,
`|A(F)|, |B(F)| ≤ ‖F‖(∫_{−T}^T e^{t}dt)^{1/2} = ‖F‖(2 sinh T)^{1/2}`, so `|2A(F)B(F)| ≤ 4 sinh T ‖F‖² ≤ 2e^T‖F‖²`. ∎

## 2. The key lemma: a bottom bound controls the mollified Weil distribution

Fix a real, even `g ∈ C_c^∞(−r, r)` with `r ≤ 1/8`, and put `k := g ⋆ g̃ = g ∗ g`. Then `k` is even, supported in
`(−¼, ¼)`, and `k(0) = ‖g‖²`. Set

`U(y) := ⟨u_Λ, k(· − y)⟩` and `V(y) := ⟨u_Λ, (P²k)(· − y)⟩`.

Both are smooth functions of `y ∈ ℝ`.

**Lemma 2.1 (PROVED).** Let `y ∈ ℝ` with `|y| ≥ ¼`, and let `T > |y|/2 + r`. Then
* (complete) `|U(y)| ≤ U(0) + M_W(T)‖g‖²`;
* (primitive) `|V(y)| ≤ V(0) + M_A(T)·(P²k)(0)`.

*Proof.* (Complete.) For real `φ, ψ ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` put `B(φ, ψ) := ⟨u_Λ, φ ⋆ ψ̃⟩ + M_W(T)⟨φ, ψ⟩`. The form `B` is
bilinear. It is symmetric because `ψ ⋆ φ̃` is the reflection of `φ ⋆ ψ̃` and `u_Λ` is even. It is positive
semidefinite because, by (1.1), `B(φ, φ) = W_T(φ) + M_W(T)‖φ‖² ≥ (λ^W_T + M_W(T))‖φ‖² ≥ 0`. Hence
`|B(φ, ψ)|² ≤ B(φ, φ)B(ψ, ψ)`. Take `φ := g(· − y/2)` and `ψ := g(· + y/2)`. Their supports lie in
`(±y/2 − r, ±y/2 + r) ⊂ I_T`, because `|y|/2 + r < T`. A change of variables with `g` even gives
`(φ ⋆ ψ̃)(w) = ∫g(t − y/2)g(t − w + y/2)dt = k(w − y)`, and `⟨φ, ψ⟩ = k(y) = 0` since `|y| ≥ ¼`. By translation
invariance, `B(φ, φ) = B(ψ, ψ) = ⟨u_Λ, k⟩ + M_W(T)k(0) = U(0) + M_W(T)‖g‖²`. So `|U(y)| ≤ U(0) + M_W(T)‖g‖²`.

(Primitive.) For real `G ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, the function `PG` lies in `P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`: integrating by parts twice,
`A(PG) = ∫G·(−¼ + ¼)e^{t/2}dt = 0`, and likewise `B(PG) = 0` (B9 R3 Lemma 1.3). Also `c_{PG} = P²c_G`, because `P`
has constant coefficients and is even. By (1.1),
`⟨P²u_Λ, c_G⟩ = 𝔞_T(PG) ≥ −M_A(T)‖PG‖² = −M_A(T)⟨P²δ, c_G⟩`.
So `B_A(G, H) := ⟨P²(u_Λ + M_A(T)δ), G ⋆ H̃⟩` is a symmetric positive-semidefinite bilinear form on real
`C_c^∞(I_T)`. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz with the same `G = g(· − y/2)`, `H = g(· + y/2)`. The δ-term at the shift `y`
equals `M_A(T)(P²k)(y) = 0`, since `supp P²k ⊂ (−¼, ¼)`. ∎

**Reading in the canonical-system angle.** `B` is the inner product of the de Branges space of the shifted datum
`u_Λ + M_W(T)δ` at time `σ(T)`. The shifted datum has a chain on `[0, σ(T)]` by G2_B5_R1 Theorem B, applied with the
comb unchanged and the constant `C_0` raised by `M_W(T)` (plus `ε`, then let `ε ↓ 0`). Lemma 2.1 is the Gram inequality
`|⟨e_a, e_b⟩|² ≤ ‖e_a‖²‖e_b‖²` for the translated bumps `e_a = g(· − a)`. This is how a lower bound on the bottom of
the form (a spectral quantity) becomes a pointwise bound on the mollified comb, **without** passing through `c_F′`.
It is the step that G2_B9_R1 §3 lacked.

## 3. Theorem Q

**Theorem Q (graded Weil converse; PROVED).** Let `η ≥ 0`.
* (primitive) If `M_A(T) ≤ C e^{2ηT}` for all `T ≥ T₀`, then `ζ(ρ) ≠ 0` for `Re ρ > ½ + η`.
* (complete) If `M_W(T) ≤ C e^{2ηT}` for all `T ≥ T₀`, then the same conclusion holds. (This follows from the
  primitive case, because `M_A ≤ M_W`. The direct proof with `U` in place of `V` is shorter, and I give both.)

*Proof.* For `η ≥ ½` there is nothing to prove, since `ζ ≠ 0` on `Re s ≥ 1`. So let `η < ½`. Fix a zero `ρ₀` with
`Re ρ₀ > ½ + η` and put `s₀ := ρ₀ − ½`. Then `Re s₀ > η ≥ 0` and `s₀ ≠ ½`, since `ρ₀ ≠ 1`. We derive a contradiction.

*Step 0 (choice of the bump).* Fix a real, even `g₁ ∈ C_c^∞(−1/8, 1/8)` with `g₁ ≥ 0` and `∫g₁ = 1`, and put
`G₁(s) := ∫g₁(w)e^{−sw}dw`, an entire function. The map `c ↦ G₁(cs₀)` is entire in `c` and equals `1` at `c = 0`, so it
vanishes only on a discrete set. Choose `c ∈ (0, 1]` with `G₁(cs₀) ≠ 0`, and let `g(w) := c^{−1}g₁(w/c)`, supported
in `(−1/8, 1/8)`. Then `G(s) := ∫g e^{−sw} = G₁(cs)` and `G(s₀) ≠ 0`. Put
`κ(s) := ∫k(w)e^{−sw}dw = G(s)²` (since `k = g ∗ g`) and `κ₂(s) := ∫(P²k)(w)e^{−sw}dw = (¼ − s²)²κ(s)` (integrate by
parts: `∫k″e^{−sw} = s²κ(s)`). Both are entire. `κ(s₀) ≠ 0`, and `κ₂(s₀) ≠ 0` because `s₀ ≠ ±½`.

*Step 1 (growth).* Primitive case: apply Lemma 2.1 with `T = T(y) := |y|/2 + 1/4`. Since `M_A` is nondecreasing and
finite (Lemma 1.2), for all `|y| ≥ ¼` we get `|V(y)| ≤ V(0) + (P²k)(0)·max(M_A(T₀), C e^{2ηT(y)})
= O(e^{η|y|})`. Complete case: the same with `U`, `‖g‖²` and `M_W`.

*Step 2 (the half-line Laplace transform).* Let `f ∈ {k, P²k}`, with Laplace transform `κ_f ∈ {κ, κ₂}`, and
`X_f(y) := ⟨u_Λ, f(· − y)⟩ ∈ {U, V}`. For `y > 1`, `supp f(· − y) ⊂ (¾, ∞)`. There `u_Λ` is the locally finite measure
`h(u)du − Σ_{n≥2}(Λ(n)/√n)δ_{log n}` with `h(u) := 2cosh(u/2) − ½K(u)`. The negative-side atoms `δ_{−log n}` and the
singular part of `k_∞` at `0` do not meet `(¾, ∞)`. So

`X_f(y) = ∫f(y − u)h(u)du − Σ_{n≥2}(Λ(n)/√n)f(y − log n)` for `y > 1`.

For `Re s > ½`, `∫_1^∞|X_f(y)|e^{−(Re s)y}dy < ∞`, and Fubini applies to both pieces:
`Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−1/2}∫|f(y − log n)|e^{−σy}dy ≪ Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−1/2−σ} < ∞` for `σ > ½`, and `h(u) = O(e^{u/2})`. Define
`L_f(s) := ∫_1^∞ X_f(y)e^{−sy}dy`. By Step 1 the integral converges absolutely and locally uniformly on `Re s > η`, so
`L_f` is holomorphic there.

We compute `L_f` for `Re s > ½`. (a) *Primes.* For `n ≥ 4` (`log 4 > 1 + ¼`), `∫_1^∞ f(y − log n)e^{−sy}dy
= n^{−s}κ_f(s)`. For `n = 2, 3` the integral is over a compact set, so it is an entire function `I_n(s)`. Hence the prime
part equals `κ_f(s)[−(ζ′/ζ)(½ + s) − Λ(2)2^{−1/2−s} − Λ(3)3^{−1/2−s}] + (Λ(2)/√2)I_2(s) + (Λ(3)/√3)I_3(s)`, using
`Σ_{n≥2}Λ(n)n^{−1/2−s} = −(ζ′/ζ)(½ + s)` for `Re s > ½`. (b) *Continuous part.* By Fubini,
`∫_{3/4}^∞ h(u)[∫_1^∞ f(y − u)e^{−sy}dy]du`. For `u ≥ 5/4` the inner integral is `e^{−su}κ_f(s)`. For
`u ∈ (¾, 5/4)` it is entire in `s`, and the `u`-integral is over a compact set. Moreover
`∫_{5/4}^∞ 2cosh(u/2)e^{−su}du = e^{−(5/4)(s−½)}/(s − ½) + e^{−(5/4)(s+½)}/(s + ½)`, and
`∫_{5/4}^∞ K(u)e^{−su}du` is holomorphic on `Re s > −½`. Collecting terms, for `Re s > ½`:

`(3.1) κ_f(s)·(ζ′/ζ)(½ + s) = L_f(s) − R_f(s)`,

where `R_f` is meromorphic on `Re s > −½` with at most a simple pole at `s = ½`. (The signs are right: `X_f` is the
continuous part minus the prime part, so `L_f = κ_f·ζ′/ζ(½+s) + (continuous and correction terms) =: κ_f ζ′/ζ + R_f`.)

*Step 3 (continuation and contradiction).* The left side of (3.1) is meromorphic on `ℂ`. The right side is
meromorphic on `{Re s > η}`, which is connected, with at most a pole at `s = ½`. By uniqueness of continuation, (3.1)
holds on `{Re s > η} ∖ {½, poles of ζ′/ζ(½ + ·)}`, and the right side is holomorphic at `s₀`. But `ζ′/ζ(½ + s)` has a
simple pole at `s₀` with residue equal to the multiplicity of `ρ₀`, and `κ_f(s₀) ≠ 0`, so the left side has a pole
at `s₀`. This is a contradiction. ∎

**Remarks.**
* **Sanity checks.** (i) With `η = ½`, Lemma 1.2 always supplies the hypothesis, and the conclusion `Θ ≤ 1` is true. The
  theorem is consistent at the trivial end. (ii) With `η = 0`, it recovers G2_B9_R3 Theorem N(i) for `a = Λ`, and
  for `a ∈ Λ + ℓ¹_w` via Proposition Q2. (iii) Under RH, `M ≡ 0` (Weil), so the hypothesis holds with `η = 0`, as it
  must.
* **Only the hypothesis for all large `T` is used.** A bound along a sequence `T_j → ∞` does not give Step 1 for
  every `y`. I do not know whether a sequential version holds. **OPEN.**
* **Beurling-genericity.** Steps 0–3 use only the Dirichlet-series identity of the comb and the fixed archimedean
  part. For any admissible datum `a` (`Σ_{n<x}|a(n)| = O(x)`), the same proof shows: if `M_•(a; T) = O(e^{2ηT})`, then
  `−Σ a(n)n^{−s}` has no singularity on `Re s > ½ + η` other than `s = 1`. **PROVED**, with the same proof. For the
  falsifiers of the record (DMV/Zhang Beurling systems, `Ξ_Q`, `ζ·E` with off-line zeros), this says their negativity
  exponent is at least the distance of their rightmost zero from `½`. This is consistent with their proved negative
  windows, and it is a lower bound on negativity, not a sign.

## 4. Consequences

**Corollary Q1 (EQUIVALENT to RH; primitive and complete).**
`RH ⟺ κ_A(Λ) = 0 ⟺ κ_W(Λ) = 0 ⟺ ∀ε > 0: −λ^A_T ≤ C_ε e^{εT}`.
*Proof.* (⟸) Apply Theorem Q with every `η > 0`: `Θ ≤ ½`. (⟹) Under RH, `λ^W_T ≥ 0` for all `T` (Weil, P46
l.140–146), so `M_W ≡ M_A ≡ 0`. ∎
It is an **equivalent reformulation** (Lens-1 class (ii)), the window-bottom form of von Koch's `ψ(x) = x + O(x^{½+ε})`.
I do not offer it as a mechanism.

**Proposition Q2 (κ is a coarse invariant of the comb; PROVED).** For a real perturbation `b`, let
`β(x) := Σ_{n<x} 2|b(n)|/√n`. Then, for both forms and all `T`, `|M_•(Λ + b; T) − M_•(Λ; T)| ≤ β(e^{2T})`. Consequently:
if `b ∈ ℓ¹_w` (`β` bounded), then `κ_•(Λ + b) = κ_•(Λ)`; and if `β(x) = O(x^{η′})`, then
`κ_•(Λ + b) ≤ max(κ_•(Λ), η′)`.
*Proof.* The complete forms differ by `Σ_{n∈N_T}(2b(n)/√n)c_F(log n)`, which is at most `β(e^{2T})‖F‖²` in absolute
value because `|c_F| ≤ ‖F‖²`. The same holds on `P_T`. Taking bottoms: `|λ_•(Λ + b) − λ_•(Λ)| ≤ β`, and
`x ↦ max(0, −x)` is 1-Lipschitz. ∎

**What Q2 does to O3 (`rem:resweights`).** O3 says that changing one `Λ(n₀)` by `≈ e^{−14.4T}` flips the **sign** at
window `T`. By Q2 it changes `M_•(T)` by at most `2e^{−14.4T}/√n₀` and leaves `κ` unchanged. So "`κ = 0`" is a target
equivalent to RH (Q1) that is **not** subject to O3, `cor:void` or `thm:margin`. All three concern the sign on a
finite window; `κ` is an asymptotic growth rate. F1 (exact values of `Λ(n)`) is also not forced by the target
`κ = 0`, since `κ` does not see `ℓ¹_w` changes. This is the one structural novelty of this round for G2. It also
turns the panel's "asymmetry" (B9 R3: the direct arrow is rigid, the converse robust) into a precise statement: the
**sign** target is rigid, while the **growth-rate** target is robust. Both are RH.

**Corollary Q3 (calibration; PROVED).** Unconditionally, `κ_A ≤ κ_W ≤ ½` (Lemma 1.2). If some argument proves
`−λ^A_T ≤ C e^{(1−δ)T}` for all large `T`, with `δ ∈ (0, 1]`, then `Θ ≤ 1 − δ/2` (Theorem Q with `2η = 1 − δ`).
No zero-free strip `Re s > 1 − δ/2` is known for any `δ > 0` (OPEN in the literature; the best known zero-free regions
are of Vinogradov–Korobov type and shrink to `Re s = 1`). So **any estimate-level progress on the bottoms beyond the
trivial bound is at least a quasi-RH theorem.** This is the output-side complement of the record's O2 (R157_000,
which excludes quasi-RH inputs). The target `κ = 0` escapes O3, but it does not escape O2: every point of the scale
`κ ≤ η < ½` is a zero-free strip.

**Conjecture Q4 (the reverse inequality; OPEN).** `κ_A(Λ) = κ_W(Λ) = Θ − ½`.
*What is missing.* The natural proof goes through the zero side: `W_T(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1 − ρ)`. The terms from zeros
on the critical line are `|F̂(γ)|² ≥ 0`. A quadruple of zeros off the line contributes `2Re(F̂(t − iδ)F̂(−t − iδ)) +
(conj.)`, bounded by `e^{2δT}`-multiples of `|F̂|²` near height `t`. Summing needs a Carleson-type bound
`Σ_{off-line ρ}|F̂(γ_ρ)|² ≤ C_ε e^{2(Θ−½+ε)T}·(norm)`. The zero count per unit height is `≍ log t`, so the available
norm is the log-energy `∫log(2 + |τ|)|F̂|²`, not `‖F‖²`. That log-energy is unbounded on the `L²` unit sphere, and
bounding it by `W_T(F)` itself runs in a circle. On the prime side the obstruction is the same: bounding the comb
remainder paired with `c_F` needs `c_F′`. I record the conjecture and do not claim it. Under RH it holds, since both
sides are `0`.

## 5. Relation to `thm:KEP3`, and what is not a restatement

`thm:KEP3`(b): RH ⟺ `KEP(T)` is solvable for every `T` ⟺ indeterminate for every `T`. For a fixed `T`, `λ^W_T ≥ 0`
(i.e. `M_W(T) = 0`) is the per-window positivity underlying `KEP(T)`. So `thm:KEP3`(b) is the statement `M_W ≡ 0 ⟺ RH`.
G2_B5_R3 Theorem `H∞` is the same statement in chain form.

What Theorem Q adds, and why it is not a restatement:
* It is a theorem about the **defect** `M(T) > 0`, which `thm:KEP3` does not quantify. For every `η ∈ (0, ½)` it gives
  an implication "defect growth `≤ e^{2ηT}` ⟹ zero-free half-plane `Re s > ½ + η`". None of these is equivalent to RH,
  and none is in the record.
* Its mechanism is new to the corpus. A spectral bound (the bottom) becomes a pointwise bound on the mollified comb
  through the Gram inequality of the shifted de Branges space (Lemma 2.1). This bypasses `c_F′`, which blocked
  G2_B9_R1 §3.
* Proposition Q2 separates the robust invariant `κ` from the rigid sign. That is a statement about the structure of
  the G2 target, not a reformulation of RH.

What **is** a restatement: Corollary Q1 (`κ = 0 ⟺ RH`), which is Weil plus von Koch. It is labelled EQUIVALENT.
