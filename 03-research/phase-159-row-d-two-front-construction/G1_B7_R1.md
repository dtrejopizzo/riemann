## §2 The construction: the numerical motive `𝔐_Z`

Notation from P46 (checked against `eq:W`, `eq:form`, l.120–145): `Φ_F(s) = ∫F(t)e^{(s−½)t}dt`,
`A(F) = Φ_F(1)`, `B(F) = Φ_F(0)`, `m(τ) = Re ψ(¼ + iτ/2) − log π`, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`, `F̂(τ) = ∫F(t)e^{iτt}dt`.

**Definition 2.1 (correspondences).** `𝒜 := C_c^∞(R; C)` with convolution `★`.
* The Frobenius correspondences are the Dirac masses `Frob_n := δ_{log n}` in the multiplier algebra, so that
  `Frob_m ★ Frob_n = Frob_{mn}`.
* `𝒜` has both `+` and `★`, but the `+` is formal on `{Frob_n}` (C3, N9). This is recorded, not hidden.

**Definition 2.2 (Rosati involution = transpose).** `F^†(t) := \overline{F(−t)}`.
* It is an anti-linear anti-automorphism: `(F★G)^† = G^†★F^†` (the algebra is commutative).
* `Φ_{F^†}(s) = \overline{Φ_F(1−\bar s)}`.
* It is CCM's `f^♯(x) = |x|^{−1}f(x^{−1})` (R158_002 (6.9)) under `F(t) = e^{t/2}f(e^t)`, together with complex
  conjugation. `PROVED` by substitution: `e^{t/2}·e^{−t}f(e^{−t}) = F(−t)`.

**Definition 2.3 (`H⁰`, `H²` as characters).** `B, A : 𝒜 → C` are algebra characters, since `Φ_{F★G} = Φ_F Φ_G`,
evaluated at `s = 0` and `s = 1`.
* Weights: `Θ = 0` on `H⁰` and `Θ = 1` on `H²`, as in OT-1's rank-one clause. They are defined by the characters, not
  by a spectrum. This meets the R-SIG requirement of Doc 119 §2.3.
* **Primitive ideal:** `𝒜⁰ := ker A ∩ ker B`. It is a **two-sided ideal**, being the intersection of the kernels of two
  characters. `𝒜⁰ ∩ C_c^∞(I_T) = P_T ∩ C_c^∞`.

**Definition 2.4 (`H¹`-trace from the prime side).** For `G ∈ 𝒜` set

  `τ₁(G) := Φ_G(0) + Φ_G(1) + (1/2π)∫ m(τ) Ĝ(τ) dτ − Σ_{n≥2} (Λ(n)/√n)·(G(log n) + G(−log n))`.

It converges absolutely: `Ĝ` is Schwartz, `m(τ) = O(log(2+|τ|))`, and the sum is finite on compact support.

**Definition 2.5 (the motive).** `𝔐_Z := (𝒜, ★, †, A, B, τ₁)`.
* The intersection pairing is `⟨F, G⟩ := τ₁(F ★ G^†)`.
* The Lefschetz number is `L(G) := Φ_G(0) − τ₁(G) + Φ_G(1)`, i.e. `Tr H⁰ − Tr H¹ + Tr H²`.
* The field family `𝔐_K` replaces `Λ` by `Λ_K` (von Mangoldt of `K`) and `m` by the Γ-factor of `ξ_K`.

### Properties (all `PROVED`, elementary; the trace identity is `CITED`)

**(P1) Definitional independence.** Definitions 2.1–2.5 use `Λ(n)`, `ψ` and `π` only. No zero, and no catalogued
RH-equivalent, enters them. This is OT-3 by inspection.

**(P2) Lefschetz formula = explicit formula.** For every `G ∈ 𝒜`, `τ₁(G) = Σ_ρ Φ_G(ρ)` (symmetric sum, with
multiplicity). Equivalently,

  `L(G) = Σ_n (Λ(n)/√n)(G(log n) + G(−log n)) − (1/2π)∫ m Ĝ`,

the "fixed-point side". This is Weil 1952, as in P45 Thm 9.4 and P46 `eq:W` (`CITED`).
*Check against P46:* take `G = F★F^†` with `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`.
* `Ĝ = |F̂|²`, and `G(−u) = \overline{G(u)}`, so `(1/2π)∫cos(τ log n)|F̂|²dτ = ½(G(log n) + G(−log n))`.
* `Φ_G(1) = A(F)\overline{B(F)}` and `Φ_G(0) = B(F)\overline{A(F)}`.

Hence `⟨F,F⟩ = 𝔞_T(F) + 2Re(A\bar B) = W_T(F)`, the **complete** form (for complex `F`, read per R158_001 A1′).

**(P3) Poincaré duality.** With `G^∨(t) = G(−t)`, `τ₁(G^∨) = τ₁(G)`, since every term of Definition 2.4 is invariant.
On the zero side this is `ρ ↔ 1 − ρ`, the functional equation. This is the analogue of `H¹ × H¹ → H²` pairing weight
`ρ` against weight `1 − ρ`.

**(P4) Lefschetz decomposition.** On `𝒜⁰`, `⟨F,F⟩ = 𝔞_T(F)`, the **primitive** form.
* `span{e^{±t/2}}` is dual to `(A, B)` and supplies the hyperbolic plane of signature `(1,1)` (P47 A6, `Λ = span(K, K_odd)`).
* So `𝔐_Z` has Grothendieck's standard conjecture B (Lefschetz type) **by construction**: the Lefschetz involution is
  the projection onto the primitive ideal. This is André's device (§4, S5) and is automatic here.

**(P5) Field uniformity.** `𝔐_K` exists for every `K`, with the same proofs (P1)–(P4); P2 uses the explicit formula for
`ξ_K`. This is OT-10, and its price (GRH for every `K`) is stated.

### Lemma M1 (the Hodge-type standard conjecture of `𝔐_Z` is RH; `PROVED`, content `CLASSICAL` — EQUIVALENT)

Define `HT(𝔐_Z)`: `⟨F,F⟩ ≥ 0` for every `F ∈ 𝒜⁰`, the Hodge index on the primitive part. Then:

  (i) `HT(𝔐_Z) ⟺ RH` (primitive form, E2);
  (ii) `⟨F,F⟩ ≥ 0` on all of `𝒜` (Rosati positivity `τ₁(F★F^†) ≥ 0`) `⟺ RH` (complete form, E1).

*Proof.* By (P2) and (P4), for `supp F ⊂ I_T` the two statements are `𝔞_T ≥ 0` on `P_T ∩ C_c^∞` and `W_T ≥ 0` on
`C_c^∞(I_T)`, for every `T`. Both extend from the smooth core to the form domain by density and lower semicontinuity
(P46 §1; `lem:roughEF`). Weil's criterion and P45 Thm 10.4 / Bombieri 2000 Thm 1 give ⟺ RH. ∎

*Reading.* Every piece of the motivic dictionary can be built on `Spec Z` without the zeros: the correspondences, the
transpose, the grading by `H⁰`/`H²`, the primitive decomposition, the trace, and Poincaré duality. The one exception is
the Hodge-type standard conjecture, and that is RH exactly, in both forms. This is Lemma T in the motivic language
(R158_001 §3.1). The object test's OT-5′ is untouched, since nothing here supplies a *reason* for the sign. **`𝔐_Z` is
an equivalent reformulation; not a new object.** (Rosati ⟺ RH is already on record as CCM Cor 6.3; I list it only to fix
the dictionary.)

*What `H¹` and the polarization are.*
* `H¹` is the completion of `𝒜 / rad⟨·,·⟩` for the pairing `⟨,⟩`. By M1(ii) it is a pre-Hilbert space iff RH.
  Otherwise it is an indefinite inner-product space. I do not claim a Kreĭn completion exists unconditionally (`OPEN`).
* The polarization is the Rosati trace form itself.
* Under RH, `H¹ ≅ ℓ²`-type space on the zeros, with multiplicity invisible: the form is `Σ_ρ m_ρ|Φ_F(ρ)|²`. This is
  `HEURISTIC` (not proved here; cf. R158_002 on Jordan tails being null for semidefinite forms).

## §3 The reciprocity mechanism (S4) and three new elementary statements

### 3.1 The mechanism in the one place it proves a new case of the Hodge standard conjecture (`CITED`, read)

G. Ancona, *Standard conjectures for abelian fourfolds*, arXiv:1806.03216 (Invent. Math. 2021), Thm 1.3: the Hodge-type
standard conjecture holds for abelian fourfolds in characteristic `p`. Idea of the proof, quoted from the introduction
(pdftotext, this phase): *"let q be a Q-quadratic form, if we know q ⊗ Q_ℓ for all prime numbers ℓ then we have
information on the signature of q, more precisely we know the difference s₊ − s₋ modulo 8 … In order to deduce the
full signature from the information modulo 8 one needs to be sure that the rank of q is small. Hence … one first
decomposes it into smaller quadratic subspaces"* (by complex multiplication, via Tate/Honda–Tate). The local forms
`q ⊗ Q_ℓ` are computed via `ℓ`-adic cohomology of a CM lift, and `q ⊗ Q_p` via crystalline comparison. The pieces have
rank 2.

This is the only mechanism in the literature I know of that proves **positivity at the real place of a form that has
no real realization** in a Weil cohomology. In characteristic `p` no Weil cohomology is real. That is exactly the
situation of `Spec Z`, whose `H¹` has no known realization. It is therefore the natural motivic candidate for OT-5′: a
form defined before the spectrum (algebraic cycles with rational intersection numbers), whose sign is decided without
eigenvalues. It must be checked.

### 3.2 Proposition M3 (reciprocity determines exactly `n₋ mod 4`; `PROVED`, classical)

Let `q` be a nondegenerate quadratic form over `Q` of rank `r`, with signature `(s₊, s₋)` over `R`. The isometry classes
of `q ⊗ Q_ℓ` for all primes `ℓ` determine `s₋ mod 4`, and in general nothing more.

*Proof.* The discriminant.
* `d(q) ∈ Q^×/Q^{×2}` is determined by its images in all `Q_ℓ^×/Q_ℓ^{×2}`: an element of `Q^×` that is a square in
  every `Q_ℓ` is a square in `Q`. Look at `ord_ℓ` for the positive part. `−1` is not a square in `Q_3`, and `−2`, `2`
  are not squares in `Q_5`, which handles the sign.
* Hence `sign d = (−1)^{s₋}` is determined.

The Hasse invariant.
* By Hilbert reciprocity `∏_v ε_v(q) = 1`, so `ε_∞ = ∏_ℓ ε_ℓ` is determined.
* For `q ⊗ R ≅ ⟨1⟩^{s₊} ⊕ ⟨−1⟩^{s₋}`, `ε_∞ = (−1,−1)_∞^{s₋(s₋−1)/2} = (−1)^{s₋(s₋−1)/2}`.

Conclusion.
* Knowing `s₋ mod 2` and `s₋(s₋−1)/2 mod 2` is knowing `s₋ mod 4`.
* Sharpness: `⟨1,1,1,1⟩` and `⟨−1,−1,−1,−1⟩` are isometric over every `Q_ℓ` (both have `d = 1` and the same `ε_ℓ`),
  and they differ by `s₋ = 4`.

(Serre, *Cours d'arithmétique*, Ch. III–IV, std; consistent with Ancona's "modulo 8" for `s₊ − s₋ = r − 2s₋`.) ∎

**Consequence.** Reciprocity can prove definiteness only for a **rational**, **finite-rank** form with an a priori
bound `s₋ ≤ 3` on each piece, and only given **independent** access to all the non-archimedean completions. For
`𝔐_Z`, each of the three fails, as follows.

### 3.3 Lemma M5 (no rational structure on the Frobenius intersection numbers; `PROVED` modulo Gelfond–Schneider, `CITED` std)

Let `c(n) := Λ(n)/√n`. This is the prime-side Lefschetz contribution of `Frob_n` in (P2), the analogue of `N_n` over
`F_q`. For distinct primes `p ≠ q`, `c(p)/c(q)` is transcendental. So there is no real `λ ≠ 0` with
`λ·c(p) ∈ Q` for two distinct primes: the Frobenius intersection numbers span no `Q`-structure, even after rescaling.
On the other hand, `c(p^k)/c(p) = p^{−(k−1)/2}` is algebraic. The numbers at a single prime do carry a `Q(√p)`-structure.

*Proof.* Set `β = log p / log q`.
* `β ∉ Q`, since `p^a = q^b` with `a, b ≥ 1` contradicts unique factorisation.
* If `β` were algebraic, Gelfond–Schneider (`q ∉ {0,1}` algebraic, `β` algebraic irrational) would make `q^β = p`
  transcendental, which is absurd. So `β` is transcendental.
* `c(p)/c(q) = β·√(q/p)`, with `√(q/p)` algebraic and nonzero, is therefore transcendental. ∎

*Reading (`HEURISTIC`).* The only rational structure available lives prime by prime, the local Euler factor being
algebraic in `p^{−s}`. That is the place-by-place situation, whose local forms are indefinite on `P_T` for
`T > ½log p` (C17, NG-E1; `cor:Qp`). Over `F_q`, `N_n ∈ Z` supplies the integrality on which Ancona and Tate rely.
Over `Spec Z` the corresponding numbers are `Q`-linearly independent logarithms. In addition, the diagonal
self-intersection of any Frobenius lattice is infinite (NG-113-O2; R158_001 Rem 1.1), so there is not even a real
finite Gram matrix on the `Frob_n`.

### 3.4 Lemma M4 (no finite-rank isotypic pieces; `PROVED`, using Riemann–von Mangoldt and Conrey 1989, `CITED` std)

Let `F ∈ 𝒜 = C_c^∞(R)` and let `S` be a finite set of non-trivial zeros. If `Φ_F(ρ) = 0` for every non-trivial zero
`ρ ∉ S` (distinct zeros, no multiplicity condition), then `F = 0`.

*Proof.* Suppose `supp F ⊂ [−T, T]` and `F ≠ 0`, and put `g(z) := Φ_F(½ + iz) = ∫F(t)e^{izt}dt`.
* `g` is entire, of exponential type `≤ T`, with `|g(z)| ≤ ‖F‖₁ e^{T|Im z|}`, and `g ≢ 0`. Pick `z₀ ∈ R` with
  `g(z₀) ≠ 0`.
* Jensen's formula on `|z − z₀| ≤ 2R` gives `#{zeros of g in |z − z₀| ≤ R} ≤ log(‖F‖₁e^{2TR}/|g(z₀)|)/log 2 =
  O_F(1 + R)`.
* A zero `ρ = β + iγ` of ζ corresponds to `z = γ − i(β − ½)`, with `|z| ≤ |γ| + ½`.
* Conrey (J. reine angew. Math. 399 (1989)): more than `2/5` of the zeros up to height `R` are simple (and on the
  line). With Riemann–von Mangoldt, the number of **distinct** zeros with `0 < γ ≤ R` is `≥ (2/5)(R/2π)log R` for
  large `R`.
* All but `|S|` of them are zeros of `g` in `|z − z₀| ≤ R + |z₀| + 1`, so `R log R ≪ R`, a contradiction. ∎

**Corollary M4′ (`PROVED`).** Let `U ⊂ 𝒜` be a linear subspace such that the joint "support" of `U`,
`{ρ : ∃F ∈ U, Φ_F(ρ) ≠ 0}`, is finite. Then `U = 0`.
* In particular, `𝒜` has no nonzero ideal, and no nonzero `★`-stable subspace, on which `⟨,⟩` factors through
  finitely many zeros.
* So the analogue of Ancona's CM decomposition into small isotypic pieces does not exist inside the correspondence
  algebra.
* The only candidates are the zero-eigenvectors `e^{(ρ−½)t}`. They are not in `𝒜`, and they are defined from the
  spectrum, so they fail OT-3.

This is consistent with, and distinct from:
* `thm:finZ`, which concerns flat-top certificates, not tests;
* Prop H of R158_002, which concerns multipliers of infinite type;
* C1.

**Novelty:** elementary, probably folklore. I found no statement of it in the corpus.

### 3.5 Verdict on S4 (`PROVED` as a method exclusion, on the correspondence algebra `𝒜`)

Reciprocity needs three things, and `𝔐_Z` supplies none of them:
1. **A `Q`-structure.** Excluded on the Frobenius correspondences by M5 (and the diagonal is infinite).
2. **Finite-rank pieces with `s₋ ≤ 3`.** Excluded inside `𝒜` by M4′.
3. **Independent non-archimedean realizations of `H¹`.** None exists: ζ has no Frobenius-at-`ℓ` (NG-C1 Wall A), and a
  form built from local data is zero-blind (C16).

Scope: this excludes S4 for `𝔐_Z` and for any carrier whose correspondences are the `Frob_n` with the Lefschetz numbers
of (P2). It does **not** exclude a new carrier with a genuinely rational cycle lattice (for example, integral
intersection numbers on an unbuilt arithmetic square). Such a lattice would have to be a lattice whose Lefschetz
numbers are **not** `Λ(n)/√n`. By OT-2/C8 it must nevertheless reproduce them, and M5 then says the intersection
numbers cannot be those of that lattice. The exact statement: **no `Q`-lattice of cycles whose Lefschetz numbers
against `Δ` include `c(p), c(q)` for two primes, up to a common real scale, exists.** `PROVED` (M5).

* automatic but signless (S5, S7);
* inapplicable, because ζ is degree-1 primitive (S6);

## §6 What is new, what is not, and the weakest step

**Not new.**
* `𝔐_Z` and Lemma M1: Weil's criterion, and CCM's trace/Rosati form, rewritten in the language of standard
  conjectures. This is an EQUIVALENT reformulation of RH. It is recorded so that no later round resubmits "prove the
  Hodge standard conjecture for the motive of `Spec Z`" as a new route. **That route is RH, verbatim.**

**New to the record** (elementary, `PROVED`):
1. The identification of **Ancona's reciprocity mechanism (S4)** as the one known way to prove real-place positivity
  without a real realization, together with its exclusion for the Frobenius correspondence algebra of `Spec Z`:
  * Prop M3: reciprocity fixes only `s₋ mod 4`;
  * Lemma M5: by Gelfond–Schneider, there is no `Q`-structure on the `Λ(n)/√n`, and rationality exists only prime by
  prime, which is the C17 situation;
  * Lemma M4 / Cor M4′: by Jensen plus Conrey, no nonzero compactly supported test sees only finitely many zeros, so
  there are no finite-rank isotypic pieces.
2. The observation that in `𝔐_Z` the primitive part is a **two-sided ideal** (kernel of two characters), and that
  Lefschetz-type B holds automatically. So André's enlargement buys nothing here.
3. The classification S1–S8 of Hodge-type positivity sources against the record.

**Weakest step.**
* The new content is only exclusionary. M4 relies on Conrey's 2/5 theorem (CITED std, not re-read this phase). An
  unconditional elementary replacement would need `#distinct zeros ≤ R` to be `≫ R·ω(R)` with `ω → ∞`.
* The scope sentence of §3.5 excludes only lattices whose Lefschetz numbers are the `c(n)` up to one real scale. A
  carrier whose rational cycle numbers relate to the `c(n)` through a transcendental period matrix (Ancona does use
  `p`-adic periods) is **not** excluded. That loophole is `OPEN`. By M4 it would still need infinitely many
  non-isotypic pieces, i.e. an a priori bound on `s₋` over an infinite-rank form, and nothing of that kind is on
  record. For the archimedean part, `thm:P` gives only upper bounds `n₋ ≤ 5/7/8` at three windows, and they grow with `T`.
* S8's genus sign is unchecked.

**Proposed next step (for round 2, if pursued).** Test the "period-matrix loophole" of §3.5 precisely: is
there any finite-rank rational quadratic space `(V_Q, q)` with a real period map `V_Q ⊗ R → L²(I_T)`, such that
`q ⊗ R` equals `W_T` pulled back, whose finite completions `q ⊗ Q_ℓ` are computable from local data? By C16, forms
computed from local data are zero-blind, which suggests this is excluded too. That would be a proved statement to
write, not an assumption.
