### 1.1 Theorem B(3): the hypotheses of de Branges's ordering theorem are verified (PROVED from CITED)

The claim: for `0 < T < T′ < T*(λ)`, the space `H(E^λ_T)` sits isometrically in `H(E^λ_{T′})`, the function
`E_T/E_{T′}` is of bounded type in `C⁺`, and `E_T` has no real zeros. So de Branges 1968 Thm 35 (ordering) and Thm 40
(the chain is the chain of a canonical system) apply. Both theorems are CITED; the hypotheses are checked here.

*Proof.*

1. **Isometry.** This is G2_B5_R1 Thm B(1).
2. **No real zeros.** This is G2_B5_R1 Thm A.
3. **`E_T` is a linear combination of kernel functions.** Write `Θ = E^#/E`.
  * `Θ` is not constant. If `E^# = cE`, then applying `#` gives `E = c̄E^# = |c|²E`. Since `|c| < 1` (Hermite–Biehler),
  this forces `E = 0`.
  * Choose `w₁, w₂ ∈ C⁺` with `Θ(w₁) ≠ Θ(w₂)`.
  * The kernel identity `E(z)·conj E(w_i) − E^#(z)·conj E^#(w_i) = 2πi(w̄_i − z)K(w_i,z)`, for `i = 1,2`, is then a
  linear system in `E(z), E^#(z)`. Its determinant is `conj(E(w₁)E(w₂))·conj(Θ(w₂) − Θ(w₁)) ≠ 0`, because `E` has no
  zeros in `C⁺`.
  * Hence `E(z) = Σ_i c_i (z − w̄_i) K(w_i, z)` with constants `c_i`.
4. **Bounded type.**
  * `K(w_i,·) ∈ H(E_T) = {F̂ : F ∈ D_T}`, so it is the Fourier transform of an `L²(I_T)` function. It is therefore
  entire, of exponential type `≤ T` and in `L²(R)`, i.e. of Cartwright class.
  * By Kreĭn's theorem (Levin, *Lectures on Entire Functions*, Lecture 16, Thm 1; de Branges 1968 Problem 34; CITED),
  every Cartwright-class function is of bounded type in `C⁺` and in `C⁻`.
  * Polynomial multiples and finite sums of bounded-type functions are of bounded type, so `E_T` is of bounded type in
  `C⁺`, and so is `E_{T′}`.
  * `E_{T′}` has no zeros in `C⁺`. If `E_{T′} = g/h` with `g, h ∈ H^∞`, then `1/E_{T′} = h/g` is also of bounded type.
  * So `E_T/E_{T′}` is of bounded type in `C⁺`. ∎

This fixes the step the panel marked "CITED with unchecked hypotheses" (P2, P3). The label is now **PROVED from
CITED Thms 35/40**.

## 2. Theorem L: no finite Hamiltonian segment carries the sign forward

**Theorem L (localisation; PROVED).** Let `T₀ < T*(0)`, and let `T₁ > T₀` be such that some integer `n₀` has
`2T₀ < log n₀ < 2T₁`. Then there is a comb `v′` with the following properties:

* (a) `v′ ≥ 0`. `v′_n = v_n` for `n < e^{2T₀}`. `ζ_{v′} = ζ·E` with `E` entire and zero-free, so `ζ_{v′}` has exactly
  the zeros and the pole of ζ. `ψ_{v′} − ψ` is eventually constant. `ζ_{v′}` satisfies no functional equation.
* (b) The window-time Hamiltonians of `v` and `v′` (at `λ = 0`) coincide on `[0, σ(T₀)]`.
* (c) `T₀ < T*_{v′}(0) ≤ T₁`.

The same holds with `v′` taken from the `L_2`-type sign flip, or from a Haar-generic twist, of the atoms beyond
`e^{2T₀}` (P5 battery items 5 and 6), whenever those combs fail at some window `≤ T₁`.

*Proof.*

1. **The comb.** Let `v′ = v + c·1_{n = n₀}·(2/√n₀)`, i.e. amplification at `n₀` (`thm:finmod`(ii)). Item (a) is
  `thm:finmod`(iii).
2. **Item (b).**
  * The datum `g′` differs from `g` only by atoms at `±log n₀`, and `log n₀ > 2T₀` lies outside `(−2T₀, 2T₀)`.
  * G2_B5_R1 Thm B(4) (causality) says the Hamiltonian on `[0, σ(T)]` is a function of the datum on `(−2T, 2T)`.
  * Uniqueness of the trace-normed Hamiltonian then gives (b).
3. **Item (c), lower bound.** `λ^W_{T₀}[v′] = λ^W_{T₀}[v] > 0`, since the forms coincide on `D_{T₀}`. By
  `lem:contT`, `T*_{v′} > T₀`.
4. **Item (c), upper bound.** Let `T₂ := ½ log n₀ + δ < T₁`.
  * `thm:finmod`(ii) gives `c_*(T₂, n₀)` such that the primitive form of `v′` fails at `T₂` for `c > c_*`.
  * The complete bottom is at most the primitive bottom, since `W = 𝔞` on `P_T`.
  * Hence `λ^W_{T₂}[v′] < 0`, and so `T*_{v′}(0) ≤ T₂ < T₁`.
5. **The twisted variants** are the same argument, with `thm:finmod`(ii) replaced by the proved negativity of the
  corresponding comb (G2_B3_R1 Thm B, G2_B7_R1 Thm B). ∎

**Corollary L1 (PROVED).** Let `P` be any property of a trace-normed Hamiltonian segment `H|_{[0,s]}`. Suppose that for
some `T₀` and every window `T₁ > T₀` carrying an integer contact,
"`P(H|_{[0,σ(T₀)]})` ⟹ `λ^W_{T₁} ≥ 0`" holds for every comb `v′ ≥ 0` with the zeros and pole of ζ. Then `P` fails for
ζ's own segment `H|_{[0,σ(T₀)]}`.

In words: no forward-propagation law on Hamiltonian segments exists that is valid on the `thm:finmod` class. This is W15
("a certificate at `T` says nothing about `T′ > T`") in canonical-system form, now **with the finite-time dynamics made
explicit**.

**Reading.** Every functional-equation-bearing input is invisible to every finite segment. By (a), the comb `v′` agrees
with `v` on the segment and has no functional equation. So the one door the panel left open, the functional equation at
`θ = 0`, can enter a window-time argument **only through the whole Hamiltonian**, i.e. through its germ at `s → ∞`.
That is the end treated in §3.

**Relation to the record.** This is the Hamiltonian version of `cor:void`, `thm:finmod` and W15. It is **new in form
only**; its content is `thm:finmod` plus causality. It is recorded because it turns Prop E ("the dynamics is
comb-universal") into a quantitative statement: the dynamics is universal **segment by segment**, with the blow-up time
`T*` free in `(T₀, T₁]`.

## 3. The germ at infinity: the phase form, and why it is Lagarias's criterion

### 3.1 The symbol is the derivative of a phase (PROVED)

Let `θ(τ) = Im log Γ(¼ + iτ/2) − (τ/2) log π` be the Riemann–Siegel theta function. Put
`log ζ_T(s) := Σ_{n∈N_T} Λ(n)(log n)^{−1} n^{−s}` and `Φ_T(τ) := θ(τ) + Im log ζ_T(½ + iτ)`.

**Proposition Φ (PROVED).** `s_T(τ) = 2 Φ_T′(τ)` for every real `τ`.

*Proof.*

* `θ′(τ) = ½ Re ψ(¼ + iτ/2) − ½ log π`, so `2θ′ = m` (P47 l.120).
* `Im log ζ_T(½ + iτ) = −Σ_{N_T} Λ(n)(√n log n)^{−1} sin(τ log n)`. Its derivative is
  `−Σ Λ(n)n^{−½} cos(τ log n) = −½ Σ v_n cos(τ log n)`. ∎

So the window form is `𝔞_T(F) = π^{−1} ∫ Φ_T′ |F̂|² dτ`: a Toeplitz form whose symbol is the phase velocity of the
truncated completed Euler product.

**Remark Φ1 (the weight = position identity is vacuous as an identity; PROVED).** For every comb `v′`, the relation
`v′_n = (log n)·(v′_n/log n)` gives `s_T[v′] = 2Φ′_T[v′]`, with `Φ_T[v′] = θ + Im Σ (v′_n/(2 log n)) n^{−iτ}`. So the
phase representation holds for every member of the battery.

What is specific to Λ is that `Λ(n)/log n = 1/k` on `p^k` and 0 elsewhere, i.e. `ζ_T` is a truncated **Euler product**.
That property also holds for DMV/Beurling systems and for `L_2` (with unimodular local factors).

Hence "weight = position" (P4, P5; G2_B4 door 3) passes the battery only through the **functional equation** relating
`θ` to the Euler product. On a finite window that relation is invisible (Thm L). It is visible only at `T = ∞`, where
`ξ(½ + iτ)` is real, i.e. `θ(τ) + arg ζ(½ + iτ) ∈ πZ` off the zeros.

### 3.2 The only positive phase statement at infinity is EQUIVALENT to RH (PROVED from CITED)

Positivity of a Toeplitz form is implied by pointwise positivity of its symbol. Pointwise positivity fails for
`Φ_T′` itself at large `T` (termwise negativity, `cor:Qp`). The natural replacement is to move the abscissa into the
half-plane, where the Euler product converges and the functional equation acts: `Φ_{ε}(τ) := arg ξ(½ + ε + iτ)`.

**Theorem Φ′.**

* (i) **PROVED.** `Φ_ε′(τ) = Re (ξ′/ξ)(½ + ε + iτ) = Σ_ρ (ε − β_ρ)/((ε − β_ρ)² + (τ − γ_ρ)²)`, where
  `ρ = ½ + β_ρ + iγ_ρ`. Proof: the Hadamard product, summed in symmetric pairs `ρ, 1 − ρ̄`.
* (ii) **CITED.** Lagarias, *On a positivity property of the Riemann ξ-function*, Acta Arith. 89 (1999): RH ⟺
  `Re (ξ′/ξ)(s) > 0` for every `s` with `Re s > ½`.
* (iii) **PROVED.** A symmetric pair with `β > 0` makes `Φ_ε′(γ) < 0` for every `ε < β`. Its contribution at `τ = γ` is
  `1/(ε − β) + 1/(ε + β) = 2ε/(ε² − β²) < 0`. The other terms are `O(ε·Σ_{ρ′}(γ − γ_{ρ′})^{−2})` and are negligible for
  `ε` small.

*Consequence.* The functional-equation-bearing positivity at the germ at infinity is the family `Φ_ε′ ≥ 0, ε > 0`,
which is Lagarias's criterion, hence **EQUIVALENT** to RH. It is also the Hermite–Biehler property of
`E_ξ = ξ(½ − iz) + ξ′(½ − iz)` (Lagarias 2006; R157_002 row 39). In window-time language, it is G2_B5_R1 Thm D read
backwards: the spectral measure at `s = ∞` is the zero measure, positive iff RH.

**Theorem Φ′ does not violate O3.** It is an identity (Hadamard), not an estimate, and it depends on the exact `Λ(n)`
only through ξ. But its hypothesis is the conclusion.

Hamiltonian on `[0, ∞)` with spectral data tied to ξ that is defined without the zeros. That is OT-5′ (group G1), not a
G2 mechanism.
