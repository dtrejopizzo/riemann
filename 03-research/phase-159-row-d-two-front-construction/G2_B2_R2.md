## 2. The construction: the sieve flow `Ψ`

**Definition 2.1 (the recursion operator).** For `a : ℕ → ℝ` with `a(1) = 0` and `n ≥ 2`, put
`R(a)(n) := log n − Σ_{d|n, 1<d<n} a(d)`. In comb form (`κ_a := Σ_n (2a(n)/√n) δ_{log n}`), and writing
`Z₁^{≥2} := Σ_{m≥2} m^{−1/2} δ_{log m}`, this reads

  `R(a)(n)·2/√n = 2(log n)n^{−1/2} − (κ_a ⋆ Z₁^{≥2})({log n})`.

**Theorem Ψ1 (short-annulus locality; the functional equation `T → T+δ`).** *PROVED, elementary; form-independent.*
Let `T < T′ ≤ T + ½log 2`, and let `A = {n : 2T ≤ log n < 2T′}` be the annulus of new contacts. For `n ∈ A`, every
divisor `d` of `n` with `1 < d < n` lies in `𝒩_T`, and

  `Λ(n) = R(Λ)(n)`, so `v_n = 2(log n)n^{−1/2} − Σ_{d|n, 1<d<n} v_d (n/d)^{−1/2}` (`n ∈ A`).

Hence the map `Ψ_{T→T′} : (v_n)_{n∈𝒩_T} ↦ (v_n)_{n∈𝒩_{T′}}`, defined by these formulas, is well defined on *arbitrary*
real data. It is affine, and it satisfies `Ψ_{T→T′}(v|_{𝒩_T}) = v|_{𝒩_{T′}}` for `ζ`.

*Proof.* If `d | n`, `1 < d < n`, then `d ≤ n/2 < e^{2T′}/2 ≤ e^{2T}`, so `d ∈ 𝒩_T`. `Σ_{d|n}Λ(d) = log n` is classical
(unique factorisation). ∎

**Theorem Ψ2 (the comb is the unique orbit).** *PROVED.* Start from the empty comb at `T₀ < ½log 2`. Compose `Ψ` along
any partition of `[T₀, T]` into steps of length `≤ ½log 2`. The result is `v|_{𝒩_T}` for `ζ`, whatever the
partition. Conversely, if `a(1) = 0` and `a(n) = R(a)(n)` for all `2 ≤ n ≤ X`, then `a = Λ` on `[1, X]`.

*Proof.* Induction on `n`. `R(a)(n)` involves only `a(d)` with `d < n`. ∎

*Consequence.* The class of `Ψ`-orbits is the singleton `{Λ}`. Both modified combs of `thm:finmod` leave it:
- deletion `a = Λ𝟏_{n≥N}` violates the recursion at `n = 2`;
- amplification `a = Λ + c𝟏_{n=n₀}` violates it at `n₀`.

The same holds for every member of `C_PNT(ε)`, `C_zeros` and `C_BT` other than `Λ`. So an argument whose prime input is
"`a` is a `Ψ`-orbit" is **not** subject to `cor:void`. *PROVED* (from Theorem Ψ2 and the statement of `cor:void`).

## 3. Where `Ψ` lands

### 3.1 F4 placement: `Ψ` exists for every normalised Dirichlet series

**Proposition Ψ3 (generic recursion).** *PROVED, elementary.* Let `L(s) = Σ_{λ} r(λ) λ^{−s}` be a generalised Dirichlet
series whose frequencies `λ ≥ 1` form a multiplicative semigroup, with `r(1) = 1`, absolutely convergent for large
`Re s`. Put `−L′/L(s) = Σ_λ a_L(λ) λ^{−s}`. Then `Σ_{λ=λ₁λ₂} a_L(λ₁) r(λ₂) = r(λ) log λ`, so
`a_L(λ) = r(λ)log λ − Σ_{λ₁λ₂=λ, λ₂≠1} a_L(λ₁) r(λ₂)`. This is a flow `Ψ_L` with the same short-annulus locality as
Theorem Ψ1, and `a_L` is its unique orbit.

*Proof.* `−L′ = L·(−L′/L)`, compared coefficient by coefficient. ∎

Two instances matter for F4 / P46 T2–T3:
- **(DMV)** A discrete Beurling system satisfying (H-DMV′). Here `r` counts generalised integers, and `Ψ_L` has
  nonnegative atoms on prime powers (A8 holds). Its zeta has zeros with `β_n → 1`. There is no functional equation.
  CITED (secondary source) as P46 (X31). The primary text is unread, open item (41).
- **(Epstein)** `Q = x² + 5y²`, discriminant `−20`, `h = 2`. `½Z_Q(s) = 1 + Σ_{n≥2} (r_Q(n)/2) n^{−s}` has leading
  coefficient 1, and it has the functional equation with the `ζ`-type pole structure. It has infinitely many zeros in
  `Re s > 1` (Davenport–Heilbronn, CITED as P46 (X21)). A8 fails: `a_Q` changes sign.

**Corollary Ψ3′ (necessary condition on any `Ψ`-invariant).** *PROVED as a statement about derivations, in the sense of
P46 Thm 18.1(ii); METHOD-level for DMV modulo (H-DMV′).* Let `J` be a quantity built from `(T, r|_{[1,e^{2T}]},
a|_{[1,e^{2T}]})` and the Γ/polar data, together with a proof that "`J(T) ≥ 0` is `Ψ_L`-invariant and implies
`W_T^{(a_L)} ≥ 0`". If that proof uses only the recursion, then it applies to `Ψ_{DMV}` and to `Ψ_{Q}`. For those flows
the conclusion is false at some window (P46 T3 and T2 = Thm 18.16). So the proof must use the specific `r ≡ 1` in both
roles: as an Euler product with nonnegative atoms (A8), which separates `ζ` from `Q`, and as the lattice `ℤ` with its

enter, namely in the coefficients `r(n) = 1` of the flow.

### 3.2 The `ℕ`-specific content of `Ψ` is `ζ(½+it)`: the flow closes on the explicit formula

**Proposition Ψ4 (the integer comb, exactly).** *PROVED (classical Mellin inversion).* For `φ ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)`,

  `Σ_{m≥1} m^{−1/2} φ(log m) = ∫_ℝ φ(u)e^{u/2}du + (1/2π)∫_ℝ φ̂(t) ζ(½+it) dt.`

*Proof.* `Σ m^{−1/2}φ(log m) = (1/2πi)∫_{(c)} Φ(s)ζ(½+s)ds` for `c > ½`, by inverse two-sided Laplace transform and
absolute convergence. Shift the line to `Re s = 0`. `Φ` decays faster than any power of `|Im s|`, uniformly in vertical
strips (integration by parts), while `ζ` grows polynomially there, so the horizontal integrals vanish. The pole of
`ζ(½+s)` at `s = ½` has residue `Φ(½) = ∫φe^{u/2}`. ∎

So in the flow `Ψ` (the case `r ≡ 1`), the "integers" enter as the half polar density `e^{u/2}du` (the A3 part) plus the
boundary values of `ζ` on the critical line. The orbit identity `κ_Λ ⋆ Z₁ = 2uZ₁` is, after Mellin transform,
`K(s)ζ(½+s) = −ζ′(½+s)` with `K(s) = Σ Λ(n)n^{−1/2−s}`, i.e. `K = −ζ′/ζ(½+·)`. Its residue expansion is `thm:Z`/E3.
*PROVED* as an identity. **Consequence (METHOD-level, not a theorem):** any exact `Ψ`-invariant is a functional of
`ζ` on `Re s = ½` and of `−ζ′/ζ`. A proof that it is nonnegative for all `T` is a proof on the zero side (E3). No
formulation of the identity-level use of `Λ = μ*log` leaves the explicit formula. Row 30 of R157_002 is thereby
formulated and placed: **EQUIVALENT** globally unless a genuinely new invariant is supplied (OPEN).

### 3.3 Defect response: what the flow does to one wrong contact

**Theorem Ψ5 (a propagated defect).** *PROVED; the contour step uses a CITED classical bound.* Let `n₀ ≥ 2`, `ε ∈ ℝ`.
Let `a` be the `Ψ`-orbit with one defect at `n₀`: `a(n₀) = Λ(n₀) + ε`, and `a(n) = R(a)(n)` for every `n ≠ n₀`. Then:
1. `a = Λ + ε·b`, where `b(n) = μ(n/n₀)` if `n₀ | n` and `b(n) = 0` otherwise. Equivalently,
  `Σ a(n)n^{−s} = −ζ′/ζ(s) + ε n₀^{−s}/ζ(s)`.
2. For `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, real, and all zeros simple,

  `W_T^{(a)}(F) − W_T(F) = −2ε [ Σ_ρ ĉ_F(γ_ρ) n₀^{−ρ}/ζ′(ρ) + Σ_{k≥1} n₀^{2k} Φ_F(−2k−½)/ζ′(−2k) ]`,

  with `Φ_F(s) = ∫c_F(u)e^{su}du`. The `ρ`-sum is the limit over `|γ| < T_j` along Titchmarsh's sequence `T_j`. The
  `k`-series converges absolutely. With multiple zeros, the `ρ`-term is `Res_{s=ρ−½}[Φ_F(s)n₀^{−1/2−s}/ζ(½+s)]`.
  The left side is a *finite* sum (`n₀m < e^{2T}`), and it vanishes if `log n₀ ≥ 2T`.

*Proof.* (1) `b := (a − Λ)/ε` satisfies `Σ_{d|n} b(d) = 𝟏_{n=n₀}`, so `b = δ_{n₀} ⋆ μ` (Möbius inversion).

(2) The difference of comb parts is `−2ε Σ_n b(n)n^{−1/2}c_F(log n) = −2ε (1/2πi)∫_{(c)} Φ_F(s) D(s) ds`, with
`D(s) = Σ_m μ(m)(n₀m)^{−1/2−s} = n₀^{−1/2−s}/ζ(½+s)` and `c > ½`. Move the line to `Re(½+s) = −2K−1`, closing with
horizontal segments at `±T_j`. Three bounds justify the contour moves:
- *Horizontal segments inside `−1 ≤ Re(½+s) ≤ 2`:* `|ζ(σ+iT_j)|^{−1} = O(T_j^{A})` for suitable `T_j ∈ [j, j+1]`
  (CITED: Titchmarsh, *Theory of the Riemann zeta-function*, 2nd ed., Thm 9.7; the theorem number is to be re-checked
  against the primary text).
- *Horizontal segments left of the strip:* the functional equation gives polynomial bounds.
- *On the segments:* `Φ_F` decays faster than any power of `|t|`.

So the horizontal integrals tend to 0. On the vertical line `σ := Re(½+s) = −2K−1`, the functional equation gives
`ζ(s′) = 2^{s′}π^{s′−1}sin(πs′/2)Γ(1−s′)ζ(1−s′)`, and I bound each factor from below:
- `|sin(πs′/2)| = cosh(πt/2)`;
- `|Γ(x+iy)| ≥ Γ(x)e^{−π|y|/2}` for `x ≥ 1`, from `|Γ(x+iy)|² = Γ(x)² Π_{n≥0}(1 + y²/(x+n)²)^{−1}` and
  `Π_{n≥0}(1 + y²/(x+n)²) ≤ sinh(π|y|)/(π|y|)`;
- `|ζ(1−s′)| ≥ 1/ζ(2)`.

Hence `|1/ζ(s′)| ≤ C(2π)^{|σ|}/Γ(1+|σ|)` uniformly in `t`. Also `|Φ_F(s)| ≤ ‖c_F″‖₁e^{2T|Re s|}/|s|²`, so the vertical
integral is `≤ C′e^{(2T+log n₀+log 2π)|σ|}/(|σ|Γ(1+|σ|)) → 0` as `K → ∞`.

The residues are:
- at `s = ρ−½`: `Φ_F(iγ_ρ)n₀^{−ρ}/ζ′(ρ)`, with `Φ_F(iγ) = ĉ_F(γ)`;
- at `s = −2k−½`: `Φ_F(−2k−½)n₀^{2k}/ζ′(−2k)`;
- no residue at `s = ½`, because `1/ζ(1) = 0`, and none at `s = −½`, because `ζ(0) = −½ ≠ 0`.

The `k`-series converges absolutely because `ζ′(−2k) = (−1)^k(2k)!ζ(2k+1)/(2(2π)^{2k})` and
`|Φ_F(−2k−½)| ≤ ‖c_F‖₁e^{(2k+½)2T}`. ∎

**Reading.** The unpropagated defect of `thm:finmod`(ii) has Dirichlet series `εn₀^{−s}`, which is entire. Its window
effect is the absolute quantity `−2εn₀^{−1/2}c_F(log n₀)`. The flow converts it into `εn₀^{−s}/ζ(s)`, which has no new
singularities: the zero set is unchanged and only the residues are reweighted, `1 ↦ 1 − εn₀^{−ρ}/ζ′(ρ)`. So under RH
with simple zeros, the nontrivial-zero part of the response is *relative* to the form:
`|Σ_ρ ĉ_F(γ_ρ)n₀^{−ρ}/ζ′(ρ)| ≤ n₀^{−1/2}·Q_T(F)`, with `Q_T(F) := Σ_ρ ĉ_F(γ_ρ)/|ζ′(ρ)|`, to be compared with

## 4. How W15 is avoided — this time in substance, and what is still missing

W15 says that a certificate at `T` says nothing about `T′ > T`. The round-1 constructions propagated certificates, and
they failed on it. `Ψ` propagates **data**. The step `T → T′` is a deterministic affine map of the old contacts
(Theorem Ψ1), so the state at `T′` is a function of the state at `T`. A candidate mechanism now has this shape:

> (M-Ψ) a functional `J(T, v|_{𝒩_T})` such that (i) `J(T₀, ∅) ≥ 0`; (ii) `J(T, v) ≥ 0 ⇒ J(T′, Ψ_{T→T′}v) ≥ 0`
> for `T′ ≤ T + ½log 2`; (iii) `J(T, v) ≥ 0 ⇒ λ^W_T(v) ≥ 0`.

- (M-Ψ) is **not** an induction on certificates, because `J` may encode more than any certificate. W15 does not apply.
- It is **not** class-level, because `Ψ`-orbits are `{Λ}` (Theorem Ψ2). `cor:void` does not apply.
- It **is** subject to three constraints:
  - Corollary Ψ3′: (ii) must fail for `Ψ_{DMV}` and `Ψ_Q`.
  - Prop. Ψ6: (ii) must be exact to precision `λ^W_T ≤ 4·10⁹Te^{−15.449T}` (`thm:margin`), even for defects that
  respect the flow.
  - Prop. Ψ4: `J` must be expressible through `ζ(½+it)` and the polar term. Otherwise it would not see the
  `ℕ`-specific content that separates `ζ` from DMV.

**Status of (M-Ψ): OPEN. No candidate `J` is proposed.** The two natural choices fail:
- `J = λ^W` is not preserved; it is monotone *decreasing* (`lem:monoT`).
- `J = ` "`W_T ≥ 0` on a larger cone" is the sign at `T` itself, so (ii) is the problem.

A `J` meeting all three constraints would, by Prop. Ψ4, be a positivity statement about `ζ` on the critical line of
strength RH. I do not have one.
