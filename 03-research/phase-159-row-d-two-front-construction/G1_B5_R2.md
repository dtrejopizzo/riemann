## §2 Theorem E: the pairing is a sum of commuting local Brownian Grams

**Notation.** For a prime `p` and `n = n′p^a` with `p ∤ n′`, let `e^{(p)}_n := Σ_{j=0}^{a} δ_{(n′, j)} ∈ ℓ²(N′_p × Z_{≥0})`.
Here `N′_p` is the set of integers prime to `p`. Put `Gram_p(m,n) := ⟨e^{(p)}_m, e^{(p)}_n⟩`, which equals
`1_{m′=n′}(1 + min(v_p(m), v_p(n)))`.

**Theorem E** (`PROVED`).
(i) For `m ≠ n`: `Γ_m·Γ_n = Σ_p log p·Gram_p(m,n)` (a finite sum: at most one term is nonzero).
(ii) `Σ_p log p·Gram_p(n,n) = Σ_p (1 + v_p(n)) log p = C_∞ + log n`, with `C_∞ := Σ_p log p = +∞`, independent of `n`.
(iii) Under `ℓ²(N) ≅ ⊗′_p ℓ²(Z_{≥0})` (restricted tensor product along `δ_0`, via exponent vectors),
`Gram_p = K_p ⊗ I_{(q≠p)}` with `K(a,b) = 1 + min(a,b)`. The `Gram_p` commute pairwise. As forms `K ≥ ¼`, with
`inf = ¼`, and `K` is unbounded above.
(iv) For `P ≥ 2`, the truncation `Q_P := Σ_{p≤P} log p·Gram_p` is positive semidefinite. It agrees with the pairing on
every pair linked by a prime `≤ P`, and its diagonal is `θ(P) + log(P-smooth part of n)`, with `θ(P) = Σ_{p≤P} log p`.
The **regularised form** `Q_reg := lim_{P→∞}(Q_P − θ(P)·I)` exists entrywise, has diagonal `log n`, and agrees with
the Witt pairing off the diagonal. On `P`-smooth vectors, `Q_reg = Q_P − θ(P)I`, so
`inf_{‖c‖=1} Q_reg(c) = −∞` and `sup = +∞`.

*Proof.* (i) Let `m < n`. If `n/m = p^k` with `k ≥ 1`, then `m′ = n′` for this `p`, and
`Gram_p(m,n) = 1 + min(v_p(m), v_p(n)) = 1 + v_p(m)`, which matches R1 Thm B. For `q ≠ p`, `v_q(m) = v_q(n)` and the
`q`-free parts differ by `p^k ≠ 1`, so `Gram_q(m,n) = 0`. If `n/m` is not a prime power (including `m ∤ n`), then for
every `p` the `p`-free parts of `m` and `n` differ: equality `m′ = n′` would force `n/m = p^{v_p(n)−v_p(m)}`. So all
`Gram_p(m,n) = 0`, which matches Thm B's value `0`.
(ii) `Σ_p v_p(n) log p = log n`.
(iii) `Gram_p(m,n)` depends on the `p`-exponents through `K` and on the other exponents through `δ`. That is the tensor
statement, and operators acting on different tensor factors commute. `K(a,b) = Σ_{j≥0} 1_{j≤a}1_{j≤b}`, so
`⟨Kc,c⟩ = ‖Ec‖²` with `(Ec)_j = Σ_{a≥j} c_a`. Since `c = (I − S^*)Ec` for the shift `S`, with `‖I − S^*‖ = 2`,
`‖Ec‖² ≥ ¼‖c‖²`. Equality is approached by `c` with `Ec` close to the top of the spectrum of `(I−S)(I−S^*)`, e.g.
`(Ec)_j = (−1)^j` on a long block and `0` elsewhere. `⟨Kδ_a, δ_a⟩ = 1 + a` gives unboundedness.
(iv) Positivity: a finite nonnegative combination of Gram forms. The diagonal is (ii) truncated. Entrywise
stabilisation holds once `P` exceeds every prime dividing the entry's indices. For the infimum, take a product vector
`⊗_{p≤P} u_p` with `u_p` finitely supported, `‖u_p‖ = 1` and `⟨K u_p, u_p⟩ ≤ ¼ + ε`. Then
`(Q_P − θ(P)I)(c) ≤ Σ_{p≤P} log p (−¾ + ε) → −∞` as `P → ∞`. The supremum is the diagonal `log n`. ∎

**Reading** (`HEURISTIC` as a dictionary; the identity is `PROVED`). The pairing is literally C16's "form computed from
local data". The entire global content sits in one divergent scalar `C_∞`. Every sign statement about a completion
`Q_d` with `d(n) = c + log n` is a statement about how `C_∞` is renormalised, and nothing in the square fixes it.

## §3 Theorem F: the Dirichlet Gram, and the zeros

**Theorem F** (`PROVED`). For `Re s > 1`, `Re w > 1`, the double series below converges absolutely and
`𝒢(s,w) := Σ_{m,n≥1} m^{−s} n^{−w} Q_reg(Γ_m, Γ_n) = ζ(s+w)·[ −ζ′/ζ(s) − ζ′/ζ(w) + R(s,w) ]`,
with `R(s,w) := Σ_p log p·p^{−s−w} / ((1 − p^{−s})(1 − p^{−w}))`.

*Proof.* Fix `p`. Write `m = m′p^a`, `n = n′p^b`. Then
`Σ_{m,n} m^{−s}n^{−w} Gram_p(m,n) = Σ_{m′⊥p} m′^{−(s+w)} Σ_{a,b≥0} p^{−as−bw}(1 + min(a,b))`.
From `Σ_{a,b}(1+min(a,b))x^a y^b = Σ_{j≥0}(xy)^j/((1−x)(1−y)) = 1/((1−x)(1−y)(1−xy))` and
`Σ_{m′⊥p} m′^{−u} = ζ(u)(1 − p^{−u})`, this equals `ζ(s+w)/((1 − p^{−s})(1 − p^{−w}))`. Now use
`1/((1−x)(1−y)) = 1 + x/(1−x) + y/(1−y) + xy/((1−x)(1−y))`, multiply by `log p` and sum over `p ≤ P`. The constant
term gives `θ(P)ζ(s+w)`, which is the Dirichlet Gram of `θ(P)·I`. So
`Σ_{m,n} m^{−s}n^{−w}(Q_P − θ(P)I)(m,n) = ζ(s+w) Σ_{p≤P} log p [x/(1−x) + y/(1−y) + xy/((1−x)(1−y))]`.
At real `s, w > 1` all entries of `Q_P − θ(P)I` are `≥ 0`: off-diagonal they are pairing values, and the diagonal is
`log(P-smooth part) ≥ 0`. They increase to those of `Q_reg`, so monotone convergence gives the identity with finite
value. The bracket tends to `−ζ′/ζ(s) − ζ′/ζ(w) + R(s,w)`. Absolute convergence for complex `s, w` follows from the
real case. ∎

*Numerical sanity check* (no proof weight): at `(s,w) = (2.5, 3)`, truncating the double sum at `m, n < 3000` gives
`0.4900549`, and the right-hand side is `0.4900594`.

**Corollary F.1** (`PROVED`). The Lefschetz row: letting `Re s → +∞` kills all `m ≥ 2` and gives
`Σ_n n^{−w} Γ_1·Γ_n = −ζ′/ζ(w)`, which is R1 Cor B.1. The whole form is fixed by this row together with local Euler
data. `ζ(s+w)` is the Gram of the Frobenius eigenvectors `a_s(n) = n^{−s}` in `ℓ²(N)`, and `R` is holomorphic on
`{Re s > 0, Re w > 0, Re(s+w) > 1}`.

**Corollary F.2** (`PROVED`). `𝒢` is not of the form `ζ(s+w)·Φ(s+w)`, nor `Φ(s+w)` for any `Φ`. So `Q_reg` is not
invariant under the simultaneous shift of both arguments, i.e. it is not translation-invariant in `log` scale (R1 3.3).
The Weil functional applied to `a_s ⊗ a_w` would be a function of `s + w̄` alone. *Proof.* Suppose
`𝒢(s,w) = Φ(s+w)`. Fix real `s > 1` and let real `w → +∞`. Every term with `n ≥ 2` tends to `0` by dominated
convergence, so `𝒢(s,w) → Σ_m m^{−s} Q_reg(Γ_m,Γ_1) = −ζ′/ζ(s)` (Cor F.1 with the roles exchanged). The limit is
`lim_{u→∞}Φ(u)`, which does not depend on `s`. But `−ζ′/ζ(s)` is non-constant on `(1,∞)`. Contradiction. The same
argument excludes `ζ(s+w)Φ(s+w)`, since `ζ(s+w) → 1`. ∎

**Remark F.3** (`PROVED` for the average, `HEURISTIC` for the reading). *The translation-invariant shadow is a modified
comb.* For `k = p^j`, the density mean over `m` of `Q_reg(Γ_m, Γ_{mk})` is
`lim_X X^{−1}Σ_{m≤X}(1 + v_p(m)) log p = (p/(p−1)) log p`, and for `k` not a prime power it is `0`. So the mean
Toeplitz symbol is the comb `Λ̃(n) := Λ(n)·p/(p−1)` (`n = p^j`). Its zeta is
`ζ_{Λ̃} = ζ·E` with `E(s) = Π_p (1 − p^{−s})^{−1/(p−1)}`, holomorphic and zero-free on `Re s > 0` but not entire. So
`Λ̃` has ζ's zeros in the strip and is *not* `Λ`. The Witt square's translation-invariant shadow is therefore a
modification of the comb in the sense of `thm:finmod`, spread over all prime powers. Its Weil form is `W − E_form` with
`E_form(g) = Σ_p (log p/(p−1)) Σ_{j≥1} p^{−j/2}(g(j log p) + g(−j log p))`. By the O3 margin statement (R157_007 §1:
a change of size `e^{−14.449T}` in one `Λ(n_0)` can flip the primitive sign) one expects `W − E_form` to fail on large

**Proposition F.4** (`PROVED`; the positivity stops at the pole, as in R1 Prop C.3). Deform by `p^{−σ}`:
`Q^{(σ)} := Σ_p log p·p^{−σ}·Gram_p`. For real `σ > 1` it is a convergent PSD form. For `σ ≤ 1` it is *defined* by keeping the off-diagonal entries
(finite sums) and continuing the diagonal analytically in `σ`. Its diagonal is

log p·p^{−kσ}`, and the second sum is holomorphic on `σ > ½`. ∎

## §5 Propositions H and K: no Hodge-index signature, no conformal Frobenius

**Proposition H** (`PROVED`). Let `d : N → R` satisfy `d(n) = c + log n + b(n)` with `c ∈ R` and `|b| ≤ B` bounded.
Then `Q_d` restricted to `span{Γ_n}` has infinite positive index and infinite negative index.
*Proof.* Positive: `n_i = 6^i`. The ratio `6^{k}` is never a prime power, so `Q_d` on `span{Γ_{6^i}}` is
`diag(d(6^i))`, and `d(6^i) → +∞`. Negative: choose distinct primes `q_1 < p_1 < q_2 < p_2 < …` with
`p_i > e^{2|c|+2B} q_i^2`, and put `v_i = Γ_{q_i} − Γ_{q_i p_i}`. Cross terms vanish. For `i ≠ j` the indices
`q_i, q_ip_i, q_j, q_jp_j` pairwise have neither dividing the other with a prime-power ratio, except within one pair
(`q_i | q_ip_i`, ratio `p_i`). So `Q_d` on `span{v_i}` is diagonal, with
`Q_d(v_i) = d(q_i) + d(q_ip_i) − 2 log p_i ≤ 2c + 2B + 2 log q_i − log p_i < 0`. ∎
*Reading.* Weil's `NS(C×C)` has signature `(1, ρ−1)`. On the ghost-cycle lattice no natural completion has a finite
index on either side. Signature `(∞, finite)` requires `d(q) + d(qp) ≥ 2 log p` for almost all such pairs, which forces
`d(n) ≳ C_∞`-type growth. That is the PSD form `Q_full` of Thm E, which is not Hodge-index shaped (Prop K).

**Proposition K** (`PROVED`). There is no function `λ : N → R` and no diagonal `d` such that
`Q_d(F_kx, F_ky) = λ(k)·Q_d(x, y)` for all `x, y ∈ span{Γ_n}` and all `k`. For the local Grams,
`Gram_p(F_p Γ_m, F_p Γ_n) = Gram_p(Γ_m, Γ_n) + 1_{m′=n′}`: `F_p` adds one orthogonal Brownian increment per `p`-class.
*Proof.* Take `x = Γ_1` and `y = Γ_p`: `Q(Γ_k, Γ_{kp}) = (1 + v_p(k)) log p = λ(k) log p`, so `λ(k) = 1 + v_p(k)`
for every prime `p`. At `k = 2`, `p = 2` gives `λ = 2` and `p = 3` gives `λ = 1`. This is independent of `d`, since
only off-diagonal entries were used. The increment formula is `e^{(p)}_{pm} = e^{(p)}_m + δ_{(m′, v_p(m)+1)}`. ∎
*Also* (`PROVED`, same computation): no rescaling `Γ_n ↦ f(n)Γ_n` makes the pairing Toeplitz. `f(m)f(mp^k)(1+v_p(m))`
would have to be independent of `m` and `k`. Comparing bases `m`, `mp`, `mp^2` forces `f(mp) = f(mp^2) = f(mp^3)` and
`(2+a) = (3+a)`, a contradiction. *Reading* (`HEURISTIC`): on `C×C`, `Γ_{π^a}·Γ_{π^b} = q^{min(a,b)}N_{|a−b|}`. Its
multiplicity `q^a` (inseparable degree) is *geometric*, and the normalisation `q^{−(a+b)/2}` makes it Toeplitz. The
Witt multiplicity `1 + v_p(m)` is a *length*, linear and not geometric in the depth. The Witt Frobenius has "log-degree",
not degree, which is the structural reason Weil's Castelnuovo–Severi argument does not transplant.

## §7 New, open, weakest step

**New (`PROVED`, 0 corpus hits):**
1. Thm E: the Witt ghost-cycle pairing is a sum over primes of commuting Brownian (min-kernel) Grams, with a single
  divergent constant `C_∞ = Σ_p log p` on the diagonal.
2. Thm F: the closed Dirichlet-Gram formula `ζ(s+w)[−ζ′/ζ(s) − ζ′/ζ(w) + R(s,w)]`. The zeros enter only through
  separate-argument log-derivatives, so the form is not Weil's (Cor F.2).
3. Thm G: all ghost-cycle invariants are Beurling-transportable. Borger's additive structure reaches them only through
  `v_p`. This corrects R1 §3.4.
4. Props H, K: no Hodge-index signature for natural completions, and no conformal Frobenius (Witt Frobenius has
  log-degree).

**Excluded:** O-B5.1 (the cycle route on Borger's square), for completions that are functions of the norms and local
data. With R1 (function carrier), the Borger/Witt angle is closed for G1.

**Open:**
* **O-B5.1′** (`OPEN`): an archimedean completion of the pairing on `W^*(Spec Z)` built from the geometry of
  `W(R) ≅ R^N` (Green functions of `Γ_m, Γ_n` at `∞`) that is *not* a function of `(log m, log n)` and local data.
  Only such a term escapes Thm G. Borger's theory supplies no metric there, and I have no candidate. A term depending on
  `(m, n)` only through norms transports and is excluded.
* O-B5.2 (`OPEN`): the `O_K` version (OT-10), expected by Thm G's proof to transport likewise.
* Rem F.3's sign failure of `W − E_form` on large windows (`HEURISTIC`).

**Weakest step.** The scope of Thm G(ii). It excludes derivations whose inputs are the ghost-cycle lattice, the
archimedean data and BE1–BE8, and it inherits T3's CITED (secondary-source) import (H-DMV′), whose primary texts are
unread (P47 open item 41). It does not cover cycles on `W^*(Spec Z)` other than ghost components and fibres. I have
not determined `Hom_ring(W(Z), Z)` or the other horizontal subschemes. Such a cycle, meeting the `Γ_n` in a way that
sees additive structure beyond `v_p`, is the one crack in the exclusion (O-B5.1″, `OPEN`). Secondary: Prop H assumes
`d = c + log n + O(1)`; completions with other growth are not classified, apart from the PSD one of Thm E.

**Status.** Dead end for the object. No carrier passing OT-5′ is built. Four proved structural results; O-B5.1 closed
as EXCLUDED; row D: no label, no sign.
