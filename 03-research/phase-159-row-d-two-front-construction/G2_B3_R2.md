## Verdict, stated first

**No mechanism, no square, and no sign are claimed at any window. Status: dead end for the angle.** The round's
content is a structural theorem that closes the angle and says exactly what the G2 target is.

* **Theorem H (Hamburger categoricity of the exact comb). PROVED from CITED (Hamburger 1921).** Let `a` be any
  arithmetic function with `Σ|a(n)|n^{−σ} < ∞` for `σ > 1`, and put `ζ_a = exp(Σ_{n≥2}a(n)n^{−s}/log n)`. Assume
  `(s−1)ζ_a` continues to an entire function of finite order. If `ζ_a` satisfies **Riemann's functional equation with
  ζ's own archimedean factor**, then `a = Λ` identically. In particular `v_T(a) = v_T(Λ)` for **every** `T` at once.
* **Corollary H1 (the target of G2, in exact form). PROVED as a statement about hypotheses.** The datum "the exact
  values `Λ(n)`, for all `n`, uniformly in `T`" is, inside the class of Theorem H, the same hypothesis as "Dirichlet
  series over `ℕ` plus the conductor-1 `Γ_ℝ` functional equation". This conjunction has **no falsifier**: the class it
  defines is `{ζ}`. Every falsifier on record keeps exactly one of its two halves (table in §3). So the
  falsification method of the record (`cor:void`, `thm:finmod`, T1, T2, Cor D of R1) ends at this conjunction, and it
  cannot constrain a derivation that uses both halves.
* **Theorem H0 (twists break the functional equation). PROVED, elementary.** In the twist orbit `G_T·ζ` of R1,
  `L_χ = ζE_χ` satisfies ζ's functional equation only if `χ = 1` on `S_T`.
* **Corollary H2 (closure of the angle). PROVED in the sense of `cor:void`.** Dirichlet-ring identities are
  twist-covariant (R1 Lemma 1), and the functional equation is not (H0). So no derivation whose arithmetic input is
  ring identities, moduli and truncations can reach the only T-uniform exact datum. That datum is **additive**: it is
  Poisson summation on `ℤ ⊂ ℝ`, which is how the functional equation is proved. A mechanism in the angle would need
  a *mixed* identity combining the Dirichlet ring with Poisson summation. Such an identity is `OPEN`.
* **Proposition G (squares linear in ζ carry no sign information). PROVED, trivial.** Poisson/theta produces
  Hermitian squares whose symbol is `|L(½+iτ)|²`, for example Connes' map `E`. Such squares are `≥ 0` for every
  Dirichlet series `L`, falsifiers included. The Weil form is linear in `log ζ`. Going from `|ζ|²` to `log ζ` is
  Jensen's formula, which gives the Balazard–Saias–Yor inequality with the sign pointing the wrong way (R157_002 row
  57, EQUIV).
* **Theorem F (translation-covariant dressed squares). PROVED; the per-window statement is EQUIV and the uniform
  statement is impossible.** A Hermitian square with Fourier-multiplier operators represents `W_T` on `L²(I_T)` if and
  only if it is an absolutely continuous solution of `KEP(T)`. So for each `T` it exists iff `KEP(T)` is solvable by an
  a.c. measure (`thm:KEP1–3`). No single such square works for all `T`, unconditionally. This closes door §7.1 of R1
  for multiplier dressing. It overlaps G2_B4_R1 S1, which is credited.

## §1 Theorem H0: the twist orbit meets the functional-equation locus only at ζ

**Theorem H0. PROVED.** Let `χ ∈ G_T` (R1 Def. 1) and `L_χ = ζE_χ`, where `E_χ(s) = Π_{p∈S_T}(1−p^{−s})/(1−χ(p)p^{−s})`.
Suppose `Λ_χ(s) := π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)L_χ(s)` satisfies `Λ_χ(s) = Λ_χ(1−s)`. Then `χ(p) = 1` for every `p ∈ S_T`.

*Proof.* `ζ` satisfies the equation, so the hypothesis gives `E_χ(s) = E_χ(1−s)` as meromorphic functions. For
`Re s > 0`, `E_χ(s) = Σ_m e_χ(m)m^{−s}`, summed over `S_T`-smooth `m`. The series converges absolutely because it is a
finite product of absolutely convergent geometric series, and `e_χ(1) = 1`, `e_χ(p) = χ(p) − 1`.
For `Re s > 1`, put `u_p = p^{1−s}`, so `|u_p| < 1`. Each factor of `E_χ(1−s)` is
`(1 − u_p^{−1})/(1 − χ(p)u_p^{−1}) = \bar χ(p)(1 − u_p)/(1 − \bar χ(p)u_p)`. Hence
`E_χ(1−s) = \bar χ(P)·Σ_m e_{\bar χ}(m)m·m^{−s}` with `P = Π_{p∈S_T}p`. This series converges absolutely for `Re s > 1`,
since the number of `S_T`-smooth `m ≤ x` is `O((log x)^{|S_T|})` and `|e_{\bar χ}(m)| ≤ 2^{Ω(m)} ≤ m`.
Dirichlet coefficients are unique on a half-plane of absolute convergence. At `m = 1` this gives `1 = \bar χ(P)`. At
`m = p` it gives `χ(p) − 1 = \bar χ(P)·p·(\bar χ(p) − 1) = p(\bar χ(p) − 1)`. Taking absolute values,
`|χ(p) − 1| = p|χ(p) − 1|`. Since `p ≥ 2`, `χ(p) = 1`. ∎

*Reading.* Every falsifier `L_χ` of R1 (Thm A, Thm B: `L_2 = ζ(1−2^{−s})/(1+2^{−s})`) is excluded by the functional
equation, and by nothing weaker that is twist-invariant (R1 Cor D).

## §2 Theorem H: the functional equation pins every `Λ(n)`

**Hamburger's theorem. CITED.** H. Hamburger, *Über die Riemannsche Funktionalgleichung der ζ-Funktion*, Math. Z. 10
(1921) 240–254; Titchmarsh, *The Theory of the Riemann Zeta-Function*, 2nd ed., §2.13. Let
`f(s) = Σ a_n n^{−s}` converge absolutely for `σ > 1`, and let `f = G/P` with `G` entire of finite order and `P` a
polynomial. Suppose `π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)f(s) = π^{−(1−s)/2}Γ((1−s)/2)g(1−s)`, where `g(w) = Σ b_n n^{−w}` converges
absolutely for `Re w > 1 + α` for some `α > 0`. Then `f = a_1ζ`.

**Theorem H. PROVED from Hamburger (CITED).** Let `a : ℕ_{≥2} → ℂ` satisfy `Σ|a(n)|n^{−σ} < ∞` for all `σ > 1`. Put
`D_a(s) = Σ a(n)n^{−s}/log n` and `ζ_a = exp D_a`. Assume `(s−1)ζ_a(s)` extends to an entire function of finite order
and `π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ_a(s) = π^{−(1−s)/2}Γ((1−s)/2)ζ_a(1−s)`. Then `a(n) = Λ(n)` for every `n ≥ 2`.

*Proof.* `D_a` converges absolutely for `σ > 1`. So does `exp D_a = Σ_k D_a^k/k!`, because
`Σ_k(Σ|a(n)|n^{−σ}/log n)^k/k! < ∞`. Its Dirichlet coefficients `b_n` have `b_1 = 1`, since `D_a` has no `n = 1`
term. Apply Hamburger with `f = g = ζ_a` and `P(s) = s − 1` (`g` converges absolutely for `Re w > 1`, so any `α > 0`
works). This gives `ζ_a = b_1ζ = ζ`. Differentiating logarithms on `σ > 1`, where both sides are absolutely convergent
and zero-free, gives `Σ a(n)n^{−s} = −ζ′/ζ(s) = Σ Λ(n)n^{−s}`. Uniqueness of Dirichlet coefficients gives `a = Λ`. ∎

*Form and scope.* This statement is about the arithmetic input, not about any window form. It concerns the exact
factor `E_ξ`, meaning conductor 1, `Γ_ℝ(s)`, and a simple pole at `1` with no other singularity. It does not use the
Euler product, nonnegativity, or any statement about zeros. It is not claimed for generalized (Beurling) Dirichlet
series: whether a Beurling zeta function over non-integer "integers" can satisfy `E_ξ`'s equation is **not
addressed** and not needed below.

**Corollary H1 (what "exact values, uniform in T" is). PROVED, as a statement about hypotheses.** Let `𝓗_FE(a)` be
the hypothesis of Theorem H: ℕ-Dirichlet series, finite-order continuation with the simple pole, and `E_ξ`-equation.
(i) `𝓗_FE(a) ⟺ a = Λ` within the class of Theorem H. So `𝓗_FE` determines the full vector `v_T(Λ)` for every `T`,
with no margin and no truncation. It meets F1 and F2 *as a hypothesis* and meets `rem:twohalves`(a).
(ii) `𝓗_FE` meets `rem:twohalves`(b): it is not shared by any `ζ_K` with `K ≠ ℚ` (their archimedean factor is
`Γ_ℝ^{r_1}Γ_ℂ^{r_2}` with conductor `|d_K|`), nor by Epstein functions (factor `Γ(s)(√D/2π)^{−s}`).
(iii) The class `{a : 𝓗_FE(a)}` is `{Λ}`. So **no falsifier exists** for a derivation whose input includes `𝓗_FE`,
and the method-constraint argument of `cor:void`, T1, T2 and R1 Cor D cannot be applied to such a derivation. This is
not a claim that such a derivation exists. It says that the falsifier programme, which produced every exclusion of
phases 149–159, has no further content here.

*Proof.* (i) is Theorem H plus `prop:finite`(i): the window form depends on `a` only through `v_T(a)`. (ii) The
archimedean factors are those of Neukirch, *ANT* VII §5 (`CITED`). A Dirichlet series satisfying both equations would
equal `cζ` by Hamburger, and `ζ_K ≠ cζ` for `K ≠ ℚ` (for example, compare the coefficient of `p^{−s}` at a prime `p`
that splits in `K`). (iii) is (i). ∎

## §4 What this does to the angle (Hermitian squares from convolution identities)

**Corollary H2. PROVED in the sense of `cor:void` / P46 Thm 18.1(ii).** Let `𝓗` be a hypothesis built only from
Dirichlet-ring identities, derivations, moduli, truncations, the pole, zero-free regions and strip-zero statements.
These are the items of R1 Lemma 1 and Lemma 4, and each is `G_T`-invariant. Then `𝓗` does not imply the
`E_ξ`-equation, because `L_{χ_2}` satisfies `𝓗` and fails the equation (H0). By R1 Cor D, no derivation from such an
`𝓗` proves `W_T ≥ 0` at any `T ≥ 0.38`. So in this angle the functional equation must enter as a separate input.
Its only known proofs go through Poisson summation on `ℤ ⊂ ℝ` (theta modularity) or Tate's adelic Poisson formula.
Both are **additive** identities, invisible to the Dirichlet ring. ∎

**Theorem F (translation-covariant dressed squares). PROVED from `thm:KEP1–3`.** Let `b_j ∈ L^∞_loc(ℝ)` with
`q := Σ_j|b_j|²` of polynomial growth, and set `Q(F) = (1/2π)∫|F̂(τ)|²q(τ)dτ`, the square of the Fourier-multiplier
operators `B_j`.
(i) `Q = W_T` on `C_c^∞(I_T)` if and only if `q dτ/2π ∈ 𝔖(T)`, meaning `q dτ/2π` is an absolutely continuous
solution of `KEP(T)`.
(ii) Such a `q` exists whenever `λ^W_T > 0` (`thm:KEP3`(a)). The existence of such a `q` for every `T` is RH
(`thm:KEP3`(b)(4)). This is EQUIV.
(iii) No single `q` has `Q = W_T` on `C_c^∞(I_T)` for all `T`. This is unconditional.

*Proof.* (i) Take `h = F⋆\tilde F`, polarise, and use density of `span{φ⋆\tilde ψ}` in `C_c^∞(−2T,2T)`, as in R1 Thm C.
Then `Q = W_T` on the window says that `q̌/2π` and `g` agree on `(−2T,2T)`, which is `def:KEP`. Both measures are
symmetric: symmetrise `q`, which does not change `Q` on even `h`. (ii) Cited. (iii) If `q` worked for every `T`, then
`(q dτ/2π)^∨ = g` on all of `ℝ`. Fourier uniqueness for tempered distributions forces `q dτ/2π = ĝ`. By the explicit
formula (`thm:Z`(ii)), `ĝ` is the zero distribution `Σ_ρ δ_{γ_ρ}`. It is atomic under RH, so it is not absolutely
continuous. If RH fails it is not a positive measure on `ℝ`: an off-line zero contributes at a complex `γ`, and the
positive measure `q dτ/2π` would then give `W_T ≥ 0` for every `T`, which is Weil's criterion, a contradiction. ∎
*Credit.* This is G2_B4_R1 S1 ("every covariant SOS realization is the zero side") restated through `KEP`. It is not
new mathematics. It closes R1 §7.1 for multiplier dressing.

**Proposition G (squares linear in L carry no sign information). PROVED.** Let `L` be any Dirichlet series with
`(s−1)L` entire and polynomially bounded on `Re s = ½`. Take the Poisson/Connes map
`(E_Lg)(x) = x^{1/2}Σ_n a_n g(nx)` on `g ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ_+^*)` with `∫_0^∞ g(y)\,dy = 0` (Lebesgue measure, as in Connes). By Mellin–Plancherel
`‖E_Lg‖² = (1/2π)∫|ĝ(τ)|²|L(½+iτ)|²dτ ≥ 0`. This holds for `L = ζ` and for every falsifier in the table.
*Proof.* Mellin transform: `(E_Lg)^∧(τ) = L(½+iτ)ĝ(τ)`. This is legitimate for `Re s > 1` and continues to `Re s = ½`
because `Σ_n a_n g(nx) = x^{−1}(\mathrm{res}_{s=1}L)∫g\,dy + O(x^N)` as `x → 0⁺`, so the zero-mean condition puts `E_Lg` in `L²(ℝ_+^*, dx/x)`. ∎
*Reading, HEURISTIC.* The FE and the Dirichlet structure are linear in `ζ`, while `W_T` is linear in `log ζ`. Hermitian
squares that are linear in `ζ` hold for every `L`, so they are RH-blind. Taking the logarithm is Jensen's formula,
which converts `|ζ|² ≥ 0` into the Balazard–Saias–Yor identity `∫log|ζ(½+it)|dt/(¼+t²) = 2π Σ_{Re ρ>½} log|ρ/(1−ρ)| ≥ 0`
(`CITED`, R157 row 57). There the unconditional inequality has the wrong sign: it permits off-line zeros, and RH is the
case of equality. Subharmonicity, the positivity that Hermitian squares of holomorphic objects give, is positivity of
the Riesz measure of `log|ξ|`. That measure is the zero measure and is always `≥ 0`. RH is a **support** statement
about this positive measure, not a positivity statement. So no square linear in `ζ` or `log|ζ|` can carry it.

**OPEN (the only door left in this angle, stated precisely).** Find operators `B_j` on `P_T`, built from the Dirichlet
ring *and* theta modularity `θ(1/x) = √xθ(x)`, together with an **exact** identity `𝔞_T = Σ_jB_j^*B_j` on `P_T`
valid for all `T`. Constraints proved on record:
(a) the `B_j` are not all Fourier multipliers (Theorem F(iii));
(b) the identity's proof uses the `E_ξ`-equation at a load-bearing step (H2, R1 Cor D);
(c) the identity is linear in `log ζ`, not in `ζ` (Prop G);
(d) it is exact, because of O3 / `rem:resweights`;
(e) it is not termwise in the zeros (`lem:Rrig`, `cor:nontermwise`).
No candidate is proposed. Weil's function-field proof supplies such a square only through the geometry of `C×C`,
which is G1's object. H1 suggests the two fronts meet here: a G2 mechanism is a proof from `𝓗_FE`, and `𝓗_FE` is a
theorem about a carrier with a Poisson formula (Tate). HEURISTIC.

## §7 What is genuinely new

1. Hamburger's theorem enters the record (Theorem H). It says that "the exact values of `Λ(n)`, uniformly in `T`",
  which is the G2 target's input, is **the same hypothesis** as the conductor-1 `Γ_ℝ` functional equation on an
  ℕ-Dirichlet series. The "series equation that gives all results without the full series" therefore has a
  precise form: Riemann–Hamburger's functional equation, not an identity in the Dirichlet ring.
2. The falsifier programme is exhausted at `𝓗_FE` (H1(iii)). Every falsifier on record keeps exactly one half (§3),
  and a new FE-only falsifier `Z_r` completes the table.
3. The twist orbit meets the FE locus only at `ζ` (H0). This is an elementary proof by Dirichlet-coefficient
  comparison, independent of Hamburger.
4. The angle is closed with a reason (H2 and Prop G): convolution identities are twist-covariant and linear in `ζ`,
  while the needed datum is additive and the target is linear in `log ζ`.

## §8 Weakest step

H1(iii) and H2 are statements about **hypotheses and derivations**, not about `ζ`. H1(iii) says the falsifier method
cannot constrain a derivation that uses `𝓗_FE`. It does **not** say such a derivation exists, and it gives no sign.
Hamburger's hypotheses are finite order and absolute convergence on `σ > 1`. They are met by the class of Theorem H as
stated, but the class excludes Beurling systems, so F4's Beurling falsifier (DMV) is covered only in the sense that it
is not an ℕ-series. Whether some Beurling series satisfies `E_ξ`'s equation is not addressed. Theorem F(iii) rests on
the explicit formula's identification `ĝ = Σ_ρδ_{γ_ρ}` as distributions (`thm:Z`(ii)) and on Weil's criterion; both are
on record. The mechanism, the "for all `T`" arrow, is **not** built. The only door left in this angle, a Poisson-and-ring
square linear in `log ζ`, has no candidate.
