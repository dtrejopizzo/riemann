## §1 Setting, and two small repairs to Lemma B

Notation as in G2_B9_R2 §1 (P46 `eq:symbol`, `eq:form`, `eq:W`). For a real comb `a` the **Weil distribution** is
```
u_a := k_∞ + 2cosh(u/2) − Σ_{n≥2} (a(n)/√n)(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n}),
```
where `k_∞` is the inverse Fourier transform of `m` in the convention `φ̂(τ) = ∫φ(u)e^{iτu}du`, `⟨ǧ,φ⟩ = (2π)^{−1}∫gφ̂`.
By P46 `thm:S`/`eq:A`, `m(τ) = C_0 + ∫_0^∞(1−cos τu)K(u)du` with `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`, so
```
⟨k_∞, φ⟩ = C_0 φ(0) + ∫_0^∞ (φ(0) − ½(φ(u)+φ(−u))) K(u) du ;
```
on `|u| > 0`, `k_∞` is the smooth function `−½K(|u|)`, and `K(u) = 2Σ_{k≥0}e^{−(2k+½)u} = O(e^{−u/2})` for `u ≥ 1`.
**PROVED (P46 thm:S, re-read).** For real `F ∈ L²(I_T)` in the form domain, with `c_F` its autocorrelation,
```
W^{(a)}_T(F) = ⟨u_a, c_F⟩, 𝔞^{(a)}_T(F) = ⟨u_a, c_F⟩ for F ∈ P_T,
```
because `(2π)^{−1}∫m|F̂|² = ⟨k_∞, c_F⟩` (`ĉ_F = |F̂|²`), `2A(F)B(F) = ∫2cosh(u/2)c_F(u)du`, and `c_F` is even. **PROVED.**
Recall `𝒮^A`, `𝒮^W`, `ℓ¹_w`, `β_b`, `D_b` from R2 §1, and `Γ_ζ := {γ ∈ ℝ : ζ(½+iγ) = 0}` (well defined with or without RH).

**Lemma 1.1 (perturbation identity; R2).** For `β_b ∈ 𝒮′` and real `F ∈ C_c^∞`: `W^{(Λ+b)}(F) = W(F) − ⟨β_b, c_F⟩`, and
`⟨β_b, c_F⟩ = (2π)^{−1}∫|F̂|² D_b` when `D_b` is a locally finite measure of polynomial growth. PROVED (R2).

**Lemma 1.2 (real tests suffice; PROVED).** Let `u` be a real, even distribution on ℝ. If `⟨u, φ⋆φ̃⟩ ≥ 0` for all real
`φ ∈ C_c^∞`, then for all complex `φ`, so `u` is positive definite in the sense of Bochner–Schwartz.
*Proof.* Write `φ = φ_1 + iφ_2`. Then `φ⋆φ̃ = φ_1⋆φ̃_1 + φ_2⋆φ̃_2 + i(φ_2⋆φ̃_1 − φ_1⋆φ̃_2)`. The bracket is the difference of
a function and its reflection, hence odd, and an even distribution annihilates odd test functions. ∎
All `u_a` and all `(−∂²+¼)^k u_a` are real and even, so Lemma 1.2 applies to every positivity statement below.

**Lemma 1.3 (density in `P_T`; PROVED).** `(−∂²+¼)C_c^∞(I_T) = P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`, and this space is dense in `P_T` for the
form norm `‖F‖²_form := ‖F‖² + ∫log(2+|τ|)|F̂|²`. Consequently Lemma B(iii)(⟸), primitive form, holds in full.
*Proof.* Equality: for `F ∈ P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`, `F̂(±i/2) = A(F), B(F) = 0`, so `Ĝ := F̂/(τ²+¼)` is entire of exponential
type `≤ T` and square integrable on lines, hence `Ĝ = Ĝ` for a `G ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` by Paley–Wiener–Schwartz (CITED,
Hörmander I Thm 7.3.1); conversely two integrations by parts give `A = B = 0` on `(−∂²+¼)G`. Density: fix `F ∈ P_T` in
the form domain. Dilate, `F_δ(t) := F((1+δ)t)`, supported in `I_{T/(1+δ)}`; then mollify, `F_{δ,ε} := F_δ ⋆ η_ε ∈
C_c^∞(I_T)` for `ε < T − T/(1+δ)`. Both operations are continuous in the form norm (`F̂_δ(τ) = (1+δ)^{−1}F̂(τ/(1+δ))`,

## §2 Lemma L: the Laplace criterion (temperedness of the Weil distribution ⟺ RH)

Let `θ ∈ C^∞(ℝ)` with `θ = 0` on `(−∞, 0.1]`, `θ = 1` on `[0.5, ∞)`; note `log 2 = 0.693 > 0.5`, so `θ ≡ 1` on the
support of the positive-side comb. For a distribution `w` with `supp w ⊂ [0.1, ∞)` and `w ∈ 𝒮′`, its **Laplace
transform** is `ℒw(s) := ⟨w, χ e^{−s·}⟩`, `Re s > 0`, where `χ ∈ C^∞`, `χ = 0` on `(−∞, 0.02]`, `χ = 1` on `[0.05, ∞)`.
Since `χe^{−su} ∈ 𝒮` for `Re s > 0` and depends holomorphically on `s` in the topology of `𝒮`, `ℒw` is holomorphic on
`Re s > 0` and independent of `χ`. **PROVED (standard; CITED: L. Schwartz, *Théorie des distributions*, Ch. VIII;
Hörmander I, Thm 7.4.3).** If moreover `w` is a locally finite measure on `(0.1,∞)` with `∫e^{−σu}d|w| < ∞` for
`σ > ½`, then for `Re s > ½`, `ℒw(s) = ∫e^{−su}dw(u)` (truncate `χe^{−su}` by `χ_1(u/R)`; the truncations converge in `𝒮`
and the integrals converge by dominated convergence). **PROVED.**

**Lemma L (Laplace criterion; PROVED; both forms).** Let `β ∈ 𝒮′(ℝ)` be arbitrary and `k ∈ {0,1,2,…}`. If
```
v := (−∂²+¼)^k (u_Λ − β) ∈ 𝒮′(ℝ),
```
then RH. Conversely, under RH, `u_Λ ∈ 𝒮′` (so the case `k = 0`, `β = 0` is an equivalence: **`u_Λ` is tempered iff RH**).

*Proof of the direct statement.*
1. *Localise.* `θv ∈ 𝒮′`, `supp θv ⊂ [0.1,∞)`, so `ℒ(θv)` is holomorphic on `Re s > 0`. Also `θβ ∈ 𝒮′` with the same
  support, so `ℒ(θβ)` is holomorphic on `Re s > 0`.
2. *Commute the cut-off.* `θv = (−∂²+¼)^k(θu_Λ) − (−∂²+¼)^k(θβ) + r`, where `r := [θ,(−∂²+¼)^k](u_Λ − β)` is supported in
  `supp θ′ ⊂ [0.1, 0.5]`, a compact set; so `ℒr` is entire (`r` has finite order, pairing with `e^{−su}` on a compact
  set). For `Re s > ½`, `⟨(−∂²+¼)^k(θu_Λ), χe^{−su}⟩ = ⟨θu_Λ, (−∂²+¼)^k(χe^{−su})⟩ = (¼ − s²)^k ⟨θu_Λ, χe^{−su}⟩`, since
  the commutator of `(−∂²+¼)^k` with `χ` is supported in `[0.02, 0.05]`, where `θu_Λ = 0`. Likewise for `θβ`. Hence, for
  `Re s > ½`,
  ```
  ℒ(θv)(s) = (¼ − s²)^k [ ℒ(θu_Λ)(s) − ℒ(θβ)(s) ] + ℒr(s).
  ```
3. *Evaluate `ℒ(θu_Λ)` classically.* On `(0.1, ∞)`, `θu_Λ` is the measure `θ(u)[−½K(u) + 2cosh(u/2)]du − Σ_{n≥2}
  (Λ(n)/√n)δ_{log n}`, with `∫e^{−σu}d|θu_Λ| < ∞` for `σ > ½` (`K = O(e^{−u/2})`, `Σ Λ(n)n^{−½−σ} < ∞`). So for `Re s > ½`
  ```
  ℒ(θu_Λ)(s) = A(s) + C(s) + (ζ′/ζ)(½ + s),
  ```
  with `A(s) := −½∫θ(u)K(u)e^{−su}du`, holomorphic on `Re s > −½`, and
  `C(s) := ∫_{0.1}^{0.5}θ(u)2cosh(u/2)e^{−su}du + e^{−(s−½)/2}/(s−½) + e^{−(s+½)/2}/(s+½)`, meromorphic on ℂ with simple
  poles at `s = ±½` only. The Dirichlet series identity is `−Σ_{n≥2}Λ(n)n^{−(½+s)} = (ζ′/ζ)(½+s)` for `Re s > ½`.
4. *Continue.* By steps 1–3, on `Re s > ½`,
  ```
  (ζ′/ζ)(½+s) = (¼ − s²)^{−k}[ ℒ(θv)(s) − ℒr(s) ] + ℒ(θβ)(s) − A(s) − C(s).
  ```
  The right side is meromorphic on `Re s > 0`, with poles at most at `s = ½` (from `(¼−s²)^{−k}` and from `C`). So
  `(ζ′/ζ)(½+s)` is meromorphic on `Re s > 0` with no pole off `s = ½`. A zero `ρ` of ζ with `Re ρ > ½`, `ρ ≠ 1`, would give
  a pole of `ζ′/ζ(½+s)` at `s = ρ − ½` with `Re s > 0`, `s ≠ ½`; and `ρ = 1` is a pole of ζ, not a zero. Hence ζ has no
  zero with `Re ρ > ½`, and by the functional equation none with `Re ρ < ½`. RH. ∎

*Proof of the converse.* Under RH, Weil's criterion (CITED, P46 l.140–146: `W_T ≥ 0` for all `T`) gives `⟨u_Λ, φ⋆φ̃⟩ ≥ 0`
for all real `φ ∈ C_c^∞`; Lemma 1.2 extends this to complex `φ`; the Bochner–Schwartz theorem (CITED: Schwartz Ch. VII
§9) makes `u_Λ` tempered with `û_Λ = 2πμ_Λ ≥ 0`. ∎

**Remark 2.1 (what Lemma L is).** With `k = 0`, `β = 0`, Lemma L says: *the Weil distribution of ζ is tempered iff RH*.
Its content is classical — it is von Koch's theorem `RH ⟺ ψ(x) = x + O(x^{½+ε})` read distributionally (the distribution
`2cosh(u/2) − Σ(Λ(n)/√n)δ_{±log n}` is `e^{−u/2}` times `d(e^u − ψ(e^u))` on `u > 0`, and temperedness is polynomial growth
of its smoothings), proved by Landau's method (Laplace transform of a half-line distribution). What is new is only the
formulation with the differential factor `(−∂²+¼)^k` and the tempered slack `β`, which is exactly what the primitive form
and the comb perturbations need. The value of `k` used below is `k = 2`: `(−∂²+¼)²` annihilates the polar term
`2cosh(u/2)` and turns the primitive form on `P_T` into a translation-covariant form on all of `C_c^∞`.

## §3 Theorem N (asymmetry) and Theorem R′ (unconditional rigidity)

**Theorem N (the two arrows of Weil's criterion under comb perturbation). PROVED.**
Let `𝒯 := {b real : β_b ∈ 𝒮′}` and `𝒯_meas := {b ∈ 𝒯 : D_b is a locally finite measure with |D_b|(Γ_ζ) = 0}`; then
`ℓ¹_w ⊂ 𝒯_meas` (for `b ∈ ℓ¹_w`, `D_b` is a bounded continuous function and `Γ_ζ` is countable).
(i) *(Robust converse; primitive and complete.)* If for some `a ∈ Λ + 𝒯` there is `C < ∞` with `λ^A_T(a) ≥ −C` for all
`T`, then RH. A fortiori if `λ^W_T(a) ≥ −C` for all `T`, or if `a ∈ 𝒮^A` or `a ∈ 𝒮^W`.
(ii) *(Rigid direct arrow.)* Under RH, `𝒮^A ∩ (Λ + 𝒯_meas) = 𝒮^W ∩ (Λ + 𝒯_meas) = {Λ}`.
(iii) *(Dichotomy.)* Unconditionally, `𝒮^A ∩ (Λ + 𝒯_meas)` is `{Λ}` if RH holds and `∅` if not; the same for `𝒮^W`.
In particular
```
RH ⟺ (Λ + ℓ¹_w) ∩ 𝒮^A ≠ ∅ ⟺ ∃ a ∈ Λ + 𝒯 : inf_T λ^A_T(a) > −∞ . (EQUIVALENT)
```

*Proof.* (i) Write `a = Λ − β` with `β := −β_b ∈ 𝒮′` (sign chosen to match Lemma L). For real `G ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)` put
`F := (−∂²+¼)G`; then `F ∈ P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)` for every `T` with `supp G ⊂ I_T` (Lemma 1.3), and by §1
`𝔞^{(a)}_T(F) + C‖F‖² = ⟨u_a + Cδ, c_F⟩`. Now `c_F = F⋆F̃ = (−∂²+¼)²(G⋆G̃)` (constant-coefficient operators commute with
convolution and `(−∂²+¼)` is even), so
```
⟨(−∂²+¼)²(u_a + Cδ), G⋆G̃⟩ = 𝔞^{(a)}_T(F) + C‖F‖² ≥ 0 .
```
The distribution `v := (−∂²+¼)²(u_a + Cδ)` is real and even, hence positive definite by Lemma 1.2, hence tempered by
Bochner–Schwartz. Since `(−∂²+¼)²(Cδ) ∈ 𝒮′`, also `(−∂²+¼)²u_a = (−∂²+¼)²(u_Λ − β) ∈ 𝒮′`. Lemma L with `k = 2` gives RH.
For the complete form the same argument with `k = 0`: `W^{(a)}(F) + C‖F‖² = ⟨u_a + Cδ, c_F⟩ ≥ 0` on real `C_c^∞`, so
`u_a + Cδ ∈ 𝒮′`, so `u_Λ − β ∈ 𝒮′`, and Lemma L with `k = 0` gives RH. (`𝒮^A ⊂ 𝒮^W`-type inclusions are not needed:
each form is treated on its own.)
(ii) This is G2_B9_R2 Theorem R(i)–(ii) (PROVED under RH; verified by the panel 5/5). Its hypothesis on `b` is exactly
`b ∈ 𝒯_meas`.
(iii) If RH fails, (i) gives `𝒮^A ∩ (Λ + 𝒯) = ∅` (even the bounded-below set is empty). If RH holds, (ii). The displayed
equivalences follow: `Λ ∈ 𝒮^A` under RH (Weil), so the middle set is nonempty; the right condition implies RH by (i). ∎

**Theorem R′ (unconditional rigidity of the jump weights at `T = ∞`). PROVED; both forms.**
Let `b ∈ 𝒯_meas`, `b ≠ 0` (e.g. `b ∈ ℓ¹_w∖{0}`). Then:
(i) `Λ + b ∉ 𝒮^W` and `Λ + b ∉ 𝒮^A`. No hypothesis on ζ.
(ii) For `b ∈ ℓ¹_w∖{0}`, the limit `L^A(Λ+b) := lim_{T→∞} λ^A_T(Λ+b)` exists in `[−∞, ∞)` and
```
L^W(Λ+b) ≤ L^A(Λ+b) ≤ −sup_τ D_b(τ) < 0 unconditionally,
```
with `L^A(Λ+b) = −∞` if RH fails, and `−‖D_b‖_∞ ≤ L^A(Λ+b)` if RH holds. In particular there is `T_b < ∞` with
`λ^A_T(Λ+b) < 0` for all `T ≥ T_b` (`T_b` not effective: it depends on `dist(τ_0, Γ_ζ)` under RH and on an off-line zero
otherwise).

*Proof.* (i) Suppose `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^A` (or `∈ 𝒮^W`). By Theorem N(i), RH. By Theorem N(ii), `b = 0`. Contradiction.
(ii) Existence of the limit: `lem:monoT` (sign-free). Under RH: R2 Theorem R(iii) (verified). Under ¬RH: Theorem N(i)
in the bounded-below form gives `inf_T λ^A_T(Λ+b) = −∞`, hence the limit is `−∞`. The lower bound under RH is Lemma 1.1:
`𝔞^{(Λ+b)}_T(F) ≥ 𝔞_T(F) − ‖D_b‖_∞‖F‖² ≥ −‖D_b‖_∞‖F‖²` on `P_T`. `sup D_b > 0` for `b ≠ 0`: R2 Theorem R(iii),
almost-periodic mean argument. ∎

**Corollary R1′ (unconditional two-point no-go; PROVED as a statement about derivations, P47 Thm 18.1(ii) / P46

no closure property of `𝒞` and no window-dependence. As in R2 §3, this covers at `T = ∞`: deletion and amplification
