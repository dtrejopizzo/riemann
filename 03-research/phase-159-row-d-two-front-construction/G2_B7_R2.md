## 2. Lemma M: the Kronecker orbit is ζ on modulated tests, up to an explicit Γ-mismatch

For `a ∈ ℝ` let `e_a(x) := e^{iax}`. Multiplication by `e_a` is unitary on `L²(I_T)`, and `(e_aF)^(τ) = F̂(τ+a)`.

**Lemma M (PROVED; exact identity).** Let `t ∈ ℝ` and `θ = tω_T` (the Kronecker orbit). For every `F` in the form domain,
```
𝔞^{tω}_T(F) = 𝔞_T(e_{−t}F) − D_t(F), D_t(F) := (1/2π)∫ (m(τ+t) − m(τ)) |F̂(τ)|² dτ,
W^{tω}_T(F) = W_T(e_{−t}F) − D_t(F) − Π_t(F), Π_t(F) := 2Re(A(e_{−t}F)conj B(e_{−t}F)) − 2Re(A(F)conj B(F)).
```
Here `|D_t(F)| ≤ C log(2+|t|)·‖F‖²`, with `C` absolute. Consequently:
1. **(Γ-matched orbit.)** The Γ- and polar-transported forms `W^{tω}_T + D_t + Π_t = W_T ∘ e_{−t}` are unitarily
  equivalent to `W_T`. They all have bottom exactly `λ^W_T`.
2. The primitive analogue is `𝔞_T` restricted to the rotated codimension-2 space `e_{−t}P_T`. It is not `P_T`, so its
  bottom is not `λ^A_T` in general.

*Proof.* By definition, `σ^{tω}_T(τ) = m(τ) − comb(τ+t) = σ_T(τ+t) − (m(τ+t) − m(τ))`, where
`comb(τ) := Σv_n cos(τ log n)`. By the substitution `τ ↦ τ − t`,
`(1/2π)∫σ_T(τ+t)|F̂(τ)|² = (1/2π)∫σ_T(τ)|F̂(τ−t)|² = 𝔞_T(e_{−t}F)`. For the bound on `D_t`: by Stirling,
`m(τ) = ½log(1+τ²) + O(1)` uniformly in `τ` (more precisely `m(τ) = log|τ/2| − log π + O(1/τ²)` for `|τ| ≥ 1`, and
`m` is bounded on `|τ| ≤ 1`). So `|m(τ+t) − m(τ)| ≤ log(2+|t|) + O(1)` uniformly in `τ`, since
`|log(2+|τ+t|) − log(2+|τ|)| ≤ log(1+|t|)`. Adding the polar terms gives the second identity. Item 1 holds because
`F ↦ e_{−t}F` is a unitary bijection of `L²(I_T)` onto itself. Item 2 holds because `A(e_{−t}F) = ∫F e^{(½−it)x}dx`. ∎

**Reading (PROVED content, HEURISTIC phrasing).** This refines Lemma 1.2 of round 1, which used `ζ(s+it)ζ(s−it)`. The
Kronecker flow acts on ζ's form by a unitary (modulation) *and* by moving the comb against the fixed Γ-symbol. With the
Γ-symbol transported (`m ↦ m(·+t)`), the orbit is **one point**: no new information. Without it, the orbit points carry
the mismatch `D_t + Π_t`, whose sign in `τ` is mixed. Its symbol `m(τ+t) − m(τ)` is, for `t > 0`, positive for `τ > −t/2` and
negative for `τ < −t/2`, because `m` is even and increasing in `|τ|`. So everything the torus adds beyond ζ is the non-transported Γ-symbol. Theorems A_ℂ and B_ℂ show
that this is negative on average and almost surely.

## 3. Theorem H: the Bohr hull of the comb is the twist torus

**Theorem H (PROVED; standard Bohr theory made explicit).** Let `T > ½log 2`, so that `π_T ≠ ∅`.
(i) `θ ↦ f_θ` is an injective, continuous map `Ω_T → C_b(ℝ)` (sup norm), with
`‖f_θ − f_{θ'}‖_∞ ≤ Σ_{p^k∈N_T} k v_{p^k}|θ_p − θ'_p|`.
(ii) `f_θ(· + s) = f_{θ + sω_T}`, where translation is `(τ_s f)(τ) := f(τ+s)`.
(iii) (**Hull.**) `cl_{‖·‖_∞}{τ_s f_0 : s ∈ ℝ} = {f_θ : θ ∈ Ω_T}`. The same holds for the closure of `{τ_s f_0 : s ≥ S}`,
for every `S`.
(iv) (**Totality of closed invariant properties.**) Let `𝒬 ⊆ C_b(ℝ)` be closed in the sup norm and translation invariant
(`τ_s𝒬 ⊆ 𝒬` for all `s`). If `f_0 ∈ 𝒬`, then `f_θ ∈ 𝒬` for **every** `θ ∈ Ω_T`.

*Proof.* (i) Continuity and the Lipschitz bound are termwise, since `|cos a − cos b| ≤ |a−b|`. Injectivity: the Bohr
coefficient `lim (1/2X)∫_{−X}^X f_θ(τ)e^{−iτ log p}dτ = (v_p/2)e^{iθ_p}`. The frequencies `log n` (`n ∈ N_T`) are
distinct, and `v_p ≠ 0`, so `θ_p` mod `2π` is recovered. (ii) Immediate from the definition. (iii) The numbers `log p`,
`p ∈ π_T`, are ℚ-linearly independent (unique factorisation). By Kronecker's theorem (CITED; Hardy–Wright Thm 444), the
half-orbit `{sω_T : s ≥ S}` is dense in `Ω_T`. Then (i), (ii) and compactness of `Ω_T` give "⊇" and "⊆"; the image of a
compact set is closed. (iv) Combine (iii) with closedness and invariance. ∎

**Corollary H′ (the repaired Cor. A′; EXCLUDED as a derivation, in the sense of `cor:void` / P46 Thm 18.1(ii)).** Call an
input about the comb **hull-type** if it has the form `f_0 ∈ 𝒬` with `𝒬` closed and translation invariant, or is a
countable conjunction of such inputs. Examples are:
* Bohr mean values `M(G∘f)` for continuous `G`;
* Bohr–Jessen value-distribution statements at continuity points (a limit of mean values);
* `sup f`, `inf f`;
* the Bohr spectrum together with the *moduli* of the Bohr coefficients;
* upper densities of alignment arcs `{τ : f(τ) ≥ m(τ₀) − η}` defined through mean values;
* ergodic averages along the flow.

A derivation of `λ^A_T ≥ 0` (resp. `λ^W_T ≥ 0`) whose only prime input is hull-type, with `m`, `P_T` and the polar term
fixed, also derives `λ^θ_T ≥ 0` (resp. `λ^{W,θ}_T ≥ 0`) for every `θ ∈ Ω_T`, by Theorem H(iv). By Theorem A_ℂ this is
false for `T ≥ 0.60` (resp. `T ≥ 0.4608`). The same reasoning on `Ω` with Theorem B_ℂ excludes every ∀T derivation from
hull-type inputs *and* from almost-sure inputs (Cor. B′ of round 1). **EXCLUDED.**

*What changed.* In round 1, "holds verbatim at every θ" was asserted through unique ergodicity for the real twist, and
it is false there (P1). Here it is a theorem about the complex twist, with the invariance made exact by (ii). It is also
stronger: it covers every sup-norm-closed invariant property, not only time averages.

## 4. Theorem K: the Γ-transport does not live on the torus

Lemma M shows that the orbit is harmless once `m` is transported. Could a mechanism transport `m` to the whole torus,
i.e. assign each twist θ an archimedean symbol `m_θ` with `m_{tω} = m(·+t)`? No.

**Theorem K (PROVED).** Let `T > ½log 2`. There is no map `θ ↦ m_θ` from `Ω_T` to real functions on ℝ that satisfies
both of the following:
* `m_{tω_T} = m(·+t)` for all `t ∈ ℝ`;
* `θ ↦ m_θ(0)` is continuous at `θ = 0`, or even just bounded on a neighbourhood of `0`.

Moreover, the orbit `ℝω_T` is μ_T-null and meagre in `Ω_T` whenever `|π_T| ≥ 2`, i.e. `T > ½log 3`.

*Proof.* By Dirichlet's simultaneous approximation (or Kronecker, as in Theorem H(iii)), there are `t_k → ∞` with
`t_kω_T → 0` in `Ω_T`. Then `m_{t_kω_T}(0) = m(t_k) = log(t_k/2) − log π + o(1) → ∞`, which contradicts boundedness
near `0`. For the second claim, `ℝω_T = ⋃_N [−N,N]ω_T`. Each piece is a compact `C¹` curve in a torus of dimension
`≥ 2`, so it is closed with empty interior and has Haar measure zero. ∎

**Hamburger's theorem (CITED; H. Hamburger, Math. Z. 10 (1921) 240–254; see Titchmarsh, *Theory of the Riemann zeta
function*, §2.13).** A Dirichlet series `Σa_n n^{−s}` of finite order, with finitely many poles, that satisfies ζ's
functional equation is `a_1ζ(s)`. Applied to `ζ_θ`, where the functional equation fixes `a_p = e^{iθ_p}` to `1`, only
`θ = 0` has ζ's functional equation. The orbit points `θ = tω` have the shifted functional equation, with Γ-factor
`Γ_ℝ(s+it)`.

**Reading (PROVED + CITED).** Among all twists, the Γ-matched ones form the orbit `ℝω`. On that orbit the form is ζ's
own form (Lemma M.1). By Theorem K the orbit is null and meagre, and the matching is discontinuous in the Bohr topology.
Any mechanism that is continuous on the Bohr compactification, which covers all hull-type, ergodic and mean-value
identities, therefore cannot see the Γ-matching. This is the structural reason behind Theorems A_ℂ, B_ℂ and H′.

## 5. Theorem J: the joint hull flattens the archimedean symbol

The last escape is to use the joint behaviour of the pair `(m, comb)` under translation.

**Theorem J (PROVED).** Give `C(ℝ)²` the topology of uniform convergence on compacta. The set of limit points, as
`s → +∞`, of `S_s := (m(·+s) − m(s), τ_s f_0)` is exactly `{(0, f_θ) : θ ∈ Ω_T}`.

*Proof.* `m′(τ) = −½ Im ψ′(¼ + iτ/2) = O(1/(1+|τ|))`, by `ψ′(z) = 1/z + O(1/|z|²)`. So
`sup_{|τ|≤R}|m(τ+s) − m(s)| ≤ R·sup_{|u−s|≤R}|m′(u)| → 0`. The second component converges uniformly on ℝ along any
sequence with `s_kω_T → θ`. Every θ is reached by the half-orbit (Theorem H(iii)), and every limit point has this form
by compactness of `Ω_T`. ∎

**Reading (HEURISTIC, precise as far as it goes).** Asymptotically in τ, the joint hull keeps the comb in full (the whole
torus) but keeps only a **constant** of the archimedean symbol. So the coupling between Γ and the comb that separates ζ
from its twists is not asymptotic. It sits at bounded height `|τ| ≤ τ_last`, in the finite band of `prop:band` /
`prop:align`. There, the round-1 findings (§5.2) and the record give:
* Information at bounded height that uses only positions (Diophantine alignment, Baker bounds on `|Σk_p log p|`) is
  **EXCLUDED** by `cor:void` on `C_supp` (R157_002 row 27, F1).
* Complete knowledge of the symbol on the band does **not** decide the sign (`prop:negtrace`(v), PROVED in P47).

So neither the asymptotic end (Theorems H, J) nor the finite end of the τ-axis carries a sign-deciding almost-periodic
input.

## 6. The endpoint of the angle: Bagchi (EQUIVALENT)

The one Bohr-type statement that uses the point θ = 0 *through ζ itself*, rather than through the comb alone, is
Bagchi's theorem (CITED; B. Bagchi, thesis ISI Calcutta 1981; Math. Z. 181 (1982) 319–334). It says that RH holds iff ζ
is strongly recurrent: for every compact `K ⊂ {½ < Re s < 1}` and `ε > 0`, the set
`{t : max_K|ζ(s+it) − ζ(s)| < ε}` has positive lower density. In torus language, this is the recurrence of the orbit
`tω` to `0`, measured by the *analytic continuation of ζ*, so it uses A1 and A3 of P46 §18.14. It is not measured by the
comb. It is an **EQUIVALENT** of RH. It is already in the corpus (LP-112; phase 55 doc 175, phase 58 docs 181/184:
"LP-112 ⟺ RH modulo a gap", "LP-112 murió como atajo").

Theorem K explains why this is the only door. A recurrence statement that sees θ = 0 must use a quantity that is
discontinuous on the torus, namely the analytic continuation, equivalently the Γ-matching (Hamburger). Every such
quantity known is equivalent to the zero distribution. This is not resubmitted as a mechanism.
