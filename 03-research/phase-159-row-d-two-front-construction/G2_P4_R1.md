## 1. G2_B1_R1 — structure vs randomness (Selberg / Goldston–Yıldırım `Λ_R`)

### Literature

- **`Λ_R`.** The truncated divisor sum `Λ_R(n) = Σ_{d|n, d≤R} μ(d) log(R/d)` goes back to Selberg. Heath-Brown used
  it in "Prime twins and Siegel zeros" (1983), Goldston in 1992, and Goldston–Yıldırım in their correlation papers
  (2003 and later). It is also the standard "structured part" of `Λ` in Green–Tao, together with the W-trick. So the
  split `ζM_R + (−ζ′/ζ − ζM_R)` is the canonical Tao-type decomposition. The novelty is only its insertion into the
  window form.
- **Lemma 2.** On the critical line `Re(ζ′/ζ)(½+iτ) = −½ m(τ)` (equivalently `C_0 = −2ζ′/ζ(½)`). This is the
  classical statement that `ξ′/ξ` is purely imaginary on the critical line. It follows from the functional equation
  and is in Titchmarsh-style treatments. The memo labels it classical, correctly. Its reading as "exact
  criticality of the complete comb at the centre" is a nice remark, but it is the same identity.
- **Thm 3(d).** Sign changes via Landau's theorem on Laplace transforms of eventually one-signed functions, with the
  singularity at `ρ − ½`. This is the Ingham/Pólya method for oscillation of `M(x)`, `ψ(x) − x` and similar functions
  (Ingham, *The Distribution of Prime Numbers*, Ch. V). The method is standard. The application to `g(y)` is correct.

### Step check (audit)

- **Prop 1.** I re-derived the contour shift. It is correct for `D = ζM_R`. The residue is `r_D C(½)` with
  `C(½) = A(F)B(F)`.
- **Defect (non-fatal, but a real error).** The Heath-Brown example violates Def. 1(c). `D_{k,z}` contains
  `ζ′ζ^{j−1}M_z^j`, which has a pole of order `j+1` at `s = 1`. Already for `k = 1`, `D_{1,z} = −ζ′M_z` has a double
  pole. Equivalently, `(ζ′/ζ)(1−ζM_z)^k` has a pole of order `k+1` unless `M_z(1) = 0`. So `D_{k,z}` is **not** a
  zero-free completion in the sense of Def. 1. The representation of Prop 1 then acquires residue terms in
  `C′(½), …, C^{(k)}(½)`, i.e. the moments `∫ t^j F e^{±t/2}`, and these are not killed on `P_T`. The statement "all
  examples PROVED to satisfy (a)–(d)" is false for `D_{k,z}`. Thm 3 uses only `ζM_R`, so it is unaffected.
  **Required correction:** delete `D_{k,z}` from the examples, or extend Def. 1 to poles of higher order at `s = 1`
  and restate Prop 1 with the higher Tate moments.
- **Thm 3(b).** Correct. It is the `prop:finsupp` argument with a `T`-independent symbol. The domination
  `|σ^str_R(s/T)| ≪ (1+|s|)^{1/4+ε}` against Schwartz `ĝ_0` is fine.
- **Thm 3(c)–(d).** Correct. Checks: `g` is continuous (the new term vanishes at `y = log d`); `G` is analytic on the
  real ray `(−5/2, ∞)` (`ζ` has no real zeros in `(−2, 1)`, `ζ(0) = −½`, the pole cancels at `s = 0`); and the pole at
  `s = iγ_1` lies to the right of `−5/2`.

### Novelty / exclusion

The conclusion "the structured part fails for fixed `R`" is what `cor:void` predicts. `Λ_R` agrees with `Λ` on
`n ≤ R`, and beyond `R` it is a modified comb whose symbol at `τ = 0` is `g(log R)`. The mechanism of failure is the
same as in `prop:finsupp`: a probe concentrating at `τ = 0` reads a `T`-independent symbol. What is new is that the
failing comb has infinite support, signed weights and a zero-free Dirichlet series. This is a genuine but small
addition to the record.

Prop 5(ii)–(iii) (estimate branch excluded by O3; Beurling-genericity) is method-level. It coincides with R157_002
row 30 (EXCL per window) and F4.

(Q1) "RH ⟺ `∀T ∃D`: `Re D(½+iτ) ≤ ½m + a_1`" would be EQUIV if proved. (Q2), `R = e^{2θT}`, is open, but by
Prop 4 any such split inherits the margin, so it cannot become a mechanism by estimation.

**Verdict: excluded.**
- Fatal step (as a mechanism): the structured part does not carry the sign (Thm 3), and the pseudorandom part carries
  the entire zero measure (Prop 5(i)). This is exactly E3.
- Salvage: Prop 1 (corrected for `ζM_R`-type completions only) as a class of non-finite-measure dual points, and
  Thm 3 as a record lemma "positivity is collective, even for infinite zero-free combs".

## 2. G2_B2_R1 — bootstrap / renormalisation in `T`

### Literature and corpus

- **B1.** Explicitly a restatement of `thm:Zexh(i)`. The `T`-independence of `f_w` is visible in eq:fw.
- **B2.** The dilation `w ↦ w(·/r)` is a two-line change of variables. Its reading as "the RG acts on `Z` by
  `Z ↦ rZ`" is the standard scaling of Weil/Guinand tests.
- **B3.** Proved correctly: `g(r) ≥ λ^W_{rT} ≥ λ^W_T` for `r ≤ 1`, by the variational principle and `lem:monoT`.
  But it is a triviality once monotonicity is known. The "one-sided Pohozaev inequality" is only the statement that
  `g` has a minimum at `r = 1` on `(0, 1]`.
- **B3′ and the rate formula.** On record (D.133, R153_012), as the memo says.
- **Prop G.** RH ⟺ `∫c < ∞`. This is `log λ^W` finite, i.e. `λ^W` never reaches 0. It is R157_002 row 17 (first
  crossing / clopen, EQUIV).
- **§3.3a.** The LP-143/GUE argument is on record (phase 46, doc 143), and the Conrey–Ghosh–Gonek small-gap bounds
  are classical.
- **Open literature.** The `T`-monotone family of window forms and its first crossing are the same object as in
  Connes–Consani's study of the Weil form on `[λ^{−1}, λ]` ("Weil positivity and trace formula, the archimedean
  place", Selecta 2021) and in Suzuki's screw-function paper (2606.09096). Neither provides a monotone invariant.

### Step check

- Prop G needs (AC), which is OPEN, for its (⇐) direction, and the memo labels it so. As an equivalence it is
  tautological either way.
- Remark B1″ (one-weight sensitivity `≤ |ε|`): correct, because `|w(log n₀)cos| ≤ 1` for flat-top `w` on the window.

**Verdict: restatement.**
- Fatal step: Prop G is RH restated. No structural bound on `c(T)` or `D_r(T)` exists: class-level bounds die by
  `cor:void`, and term-by-term bounds lose `10⁵`.
- Salvage: B1(3) (the exact annulus constraint) as a clean formulation of W15. It is record material only.

## 3. G2_B3_R1 — Hermitian squares and the twist obstruction

### Independent verification of Theorem B

This is the only sign claim in the memo, and it is a sign for a falsifier, not for `ζ`. I recomputed it in mpmath
with 30 digits:
- `U(0.38) = 0.0071339356…`
- `c_F(log 2) = −0.0080050109…`
- `U + √2·log2·c_F(log2) = −7.1304·10⁻⁴`. This matches the memo.

I also computed the **exact** archimedean form by quadrature of `Lev(c_F)` with the true `K`:
`A_∞(F) = 0.0062066`, so the twisted form is `W^{χ_2}_{0.38}(F) = −0.00164`. That is more negative than the memo's
bound (DIAGNOSTIC; the memo's bound is the certified one).

Theorem B holds. Cor B1 (Liouville on `{2,3}`, `[0.60, log2]`) is correct by convexity, as in `prop:finite(iii)`.

### Literature / novelty

- **Theorem A** (Haar mean = archimedean form) is a one-line computation: a trigonometric polynomial on a torus with
  no constant term has mean 0. Its content is entirely `prop:archneg` and `cor:Tarch`.
- **Theorem B's phenomenon is classical folklore.** Keep the Euler product and the strip zeros, drop the functional
  equation, and positivity fails. `L_2 = ζ(1−2^{−s})/(1+2^{−s})` has zeros and poles on `Re s = 0`, that is, off the
  critical line, and they enter its explicit formula. The negativity is the Weil-criterion shadow of those
  off-line singularities. This is the Euler-product half of the dichotomy whose functional-equation half is the
  Davenport–Heilbronn / Epstein phenomenon (P47 `rem:twohalves`; P46 Thm 18.16). Both halves are standard in the
  RH-barrier literature (Bombieri's Clay problem description; Sarnak). Theorem B makes it explicit and certified at a
  window, which is new as a concrete statement. The idea is not new.
- **Theorem C** (no separated arithmetic square). Proved correctly: a Toeplitz form is determined by its kernel on
  `(−2T, 2T)`. By the memo's own admission it excludes one architecture. It refines R157_002 row 51.
- **Corollary D / F1′.** A derivation-level meta-statement in the sense of `cor:void`. F1′ ("the phase `α_p = 1`, not
  only `|α_p| = 1`, or the functional equation, must be load-bearing") is useful. It coincides with F4 and with
  P46 (36), so it is a sharpening, not a new filter.
- **Overlap within phase 159.** B3 Thm A and B7 Thm A are the same theorem (B3 with complex twists, B7 with real
  parts `Re χ_θ`). The panel should merge them and credit one statement.

**Verdict: excluded.**
- No mechanism.
- Fatal step: Thm C together with Cor D closes the separated-square architecture. Dressed squares are left OPEN
  without any candidate.
- Salvage: Thm B's explicit certified falsifier `L_2`, a twist test for all G2 candidates, and F1′ as a wording
  amendment of F1/F4.

## 4. G2_B4_R1 — SOS / Positivstellensatz and the transcendence no-go

### Literature / novelty

- **S1 is textbook.** `W(F) = ⟨g, c_F⟩`. A covariant SOS makes `g` a positive-definite distribution. By
  Bochner–Schwartz its Fourier transform is a unique positive tempered measure. By the explicit formula that
  transform is `μ_Z` under RH, or is not a measure at all otherwise. So "the only covariant SOS is the zero-side SOS"
  and "no a.c. SOS exists" follow directly from Bochner–Schwartz uniqueness plus Weil. The proof is correct
  (temperedness, polarisation, Fourier uniqueness), and the memo himself says "the novelty is the statement, not
  the technique". It upgrades R157_002 row 51 from observation to lemma. That is housekeeping.
- **S2** = `thm:D1`/`thm:Z`/KEP. S2(c) (`lim λ^W_T ≤ 0`) is already implied by `thm:margin`, as the memo notes.
- **Remark S2.4 (causal / Kreĭn factorisation).** This is the same object as B5 (Kreĭn systems, screw functions,
  Suzuki). EQUIV.
- **S3.**
  - The citation of Baker's theorem is correct: if the `log α_i` are `ℚ`-linearly independent, then
  `1, log α_1, …, log α_n` are `ℚ̄`-linearly independent.
  - Hermite–Lindemann for `log 2` is correct, and so is the Schanuel conditional.
  - The theorem is correct for Def. S3.1. But Def. S3.1 makes the weights free indeterminates tied only by `ℚ̄`-algebraic
  relations. So a certificate is an identity valid on a variety that contains the prime-power lattice `L_T`. Then
  Lemma S3.1 (amplify all `2^k` weights) finishes the proof. This is `cor:void` (amplification closure) translated
  into commutative algebra, and transcendence theory only confirms that the variety is big.
  - As a no-go it is correct and mildly novel (first use of Baker in row D). As an obstruction it adds nothing that
  `cor:void`/`thm:finmod(ii)` do not already imply for the relevant class.
- **Door 3** (weight = position, i.e. using `log p` analytically) is correctly identified as the only door left, and
  the explicit formula is the only known identity of that type. That is the premise of record 3.

### Step check

- The primitive temperedness sketch in S1 (construction of `χ_a ∈ P`) is incomplete as written but repairable: the
  functional `F ↦ F̂(ξ₀)` is not in the span of the two moments.
- The S2(c) tail uniformity is standard.

Neither is load-bearing for a sign.

**Verdict: excluded.**
- No mechanism.
- Fatal step: S1 and S2 show every SOS version is the zero side (EQUIV). S3 shows the "algebraic identity for all
  `T`" requested by the angle does not exist under Def. S3.1.
- Salvage: S3 as a remark that "exact values" in F1 must mean analytic use of `log p` (door 3). It overlaps F1′ of B3.

## 5. G2_B5_R1 — window time as de Branges canonical-system time

### Literature

This is precisely Kreĭn's theory of screw functions and continuous Kreĭn systems, applied to the Weil distribution:

- M. Suzuki, *The screw line of the Riemann zeta-function and its applications*, arXiv 2209.04658. It gives necessary
  and sufficient conditions for RH in terms of the screw function.
- M. Suzuki, *On the Hilbert space derived from the Weil distribution*, arXiv 2301.00421. Under RH, the Weil space is
  `H(E_ξ)`.
- M. Suzuki, *Weil's quadratic form via the screw function*, arXiv 2606.09096. De Branges spaces per window `[−a, a]`,
  the shifted operator `A_a − λ`, and a conjectured Hilbert–Pólya limit.
- A numerical follow-up, arXiv 2607.24830.
- Earlier: J. C. Lagarias, *Hilbert spaces of entire functions and Dirichlet L-functions* (2006), and Burnol's
  co-Poisson papers.
- R157_002 row 39 (EQUIV) is the corpus record.

The memo cites Suzuki and claims no priority, which is correct.

### Step check

- **Lemma 1** (polar invariance). Correct. The case `w = ±i/2` also works: both sides vanish.
- **Thm A** (norm equivalence, axioms H1–H3). Correct as sketched.
- **Thm B(3)** (membership in the chain). This is the step the memo flags. It is standard: de Branges's Thm 35
  orders the isometrically embedded de Branges subspaces of `H(E_{T′})`, and those subspaces are the chain. I accept
  it as CITED but not line-checked.
- **Thm C(3).** Plancherel–Pólya for type-`T` functions with an `O(log)` local-mass bound. Standard.
- Nothing here produces a sign. Prop E (the evolution law is comb-universal) is the correct diagnosis: the
  Gelfand–Levitan map `g ↦ H` does not see which comb is arithmetic.

**Verdict: restatement.**
- Fatal step: `T*(0) = ∞ ⟺ RH` is `thm:KEP3(b)` / `rem:nonzero` in canonical-system language. The framework is
  Suzuki's.
- Salvage: Lemma 1 (the complete form, pole included, is de Branges) and Thm C(3) (phantom atomic extensions at a
  degenerate window). Both need checking against Suzuki 2606.09096 in full text before any novelty is claimed.

## 6. G2_B6_R1 — random finite zero universes; the horizon of a certificate

### Literature

- The "sequence equation" is the explicit formula: Riemann 1859; Guinand 1948; Weil 1952. The memo says so.
- **Prop S** (certificate ⟹ lower sampling inequality in `PW_T`). This is Parseval. Its reading as a Beurling–Landau
  sampling statement (Beurling; Landau 1967; Seip's survey) is on record in R153_012 §3.
- Random thinning of zeros and the robustness of positivity: no prior literature found, but mathematically it is a
  perturbation bound (a `Σ g(τ − γ)` majorant over a sparse multiset) around a certificate that is given.

### Step check

- **Theorem H.** I re-derived the region `τ ≥ H/2`: `f ≥ log τ − B_w − ¼ν − G₁/(2G₀) ≥ ½log τ − B_w − 1.35 − G₁/(2G₀)`,
  and (H2) is exactly what makes this `≥ c/2`. The arithmetic is correct.
- **Lemma 1.** Rosser's bound gives `1.32 log(x+1) + 3.18 ≤ ν(x)`. This is correct, and the endpoint bookkeeping for
  `x < 0` is absorbed by the constant.
- **Audit point on the headline.** "Every certificate is blind above `H_*(w) ≥ 2e^{2A_T}`" is misleading. `H_*` is
  defined as the least `H` satisfying the proof's sufficient conditions, so the lower bound `H_* ≥ 2e^{2B_w}` is
  automatic from (H2). It says the proof's horizon is large. It says nothing about where rigidity actually ends. The
  meaningful bound would be an **upper** bound on the true rigidity height, and none is given (the memo concedes
  this in §5(i)). The headline should read "blind above `H_*(w)`", without the lower bound presented as content.
- **Corollary H1.** The Chernoff computation is correct. `q₀ ≈ 10^{−415}` makes it qualitative only.

**Verdict: restatement.**
- Fatal step: the only mechanism candidate, L_seq, is `cor:RH(c)` plus monotonicity, so it is EQUIV. Theorems H and
  H1 transport a given certificate and produce no sign.
- Salvage: a precise answer to the random-100-zeros question. Random finite universes fail on about 40 % of
  the line (DIAGNOSTIC), successive universes fail only above their top (`cor:infinite`), and a certificate needs local
  completeness below its horizon. So "not successive" cannot replace "all zeros".

## 7. G2_B7_R1 — Bohr–Jessen / Kronecker twist torus

### Literature

- **Theorem C is Wintner's theorem.** A. Wintner, *Random factorizations and Riemann's hypothesis*, Duke Math. J. 11
  (1944) 267–275. For random multiplicative functions, partial sums are `O(x^{½+ε})` almost surely, i.e. "RH holds
  almost surely". The Steinhaus / `Λχ_θ` variant, with the same Kolmogorov + Borel–Cantelli proof, is standard (see
  also the Helson/Harper literature on random multiplicative functions). The memo labels Theorem C "PROVED,
  elementary" but does not cite Wintner. **This is a missing attribution and must be added.** It is not new.
- **Bohr–Jessen / Kronecker–Weyl.** The torus lift of `ζ` is classical (Bohr–Jessen 1930–32; Bagchi; Voronin).
- **Known equivalent of RH in this frame.** Bagchi (thesis 1981; Math. Z. 1982) proved RH ⟺ strong recurrence of
  `ζ` (positive lower density of self-approximating shifts in `½ < Re s < 1`). This is the one Bohr-type statement
  that does use the point `θ = 0` through `ζ` itself, not through twist-invariant functionals. It is exactly what
  Theorems A′/B′ leave open, and it is EQUIV. It is already in the corpus (phase 55 docs 112/113/175, phase 58
  doc 184: LP-112 ⟺ RH modulo a gap). **So the "door left open" by B7 is a known equivalent of RH.** B7 should cite
  it.
- **Lemma 1.2.** The Kronecker orbit `θ = tω` gives `ζ(s+it)ζ(s−it)`, which is a standard observation: vertical
  shifts equal twists by `n^{−it}`.
- **Theorem B** (Haar-null positivity, `liminf λ^θ_T/T ≤ −c` a.s.). I found no prior statement of it for the Weil
  form. It is new in this form, and it proves R157_002 row 48 (which was argued, not proved).

### Step check of Theorem B (the memo's flagged weak step)

- **Step 4.**
  - Window of primes: `y = e^{x₀T_j/2}`, so `y < p ≤ y²` means `log p/T_j ∈ (x₀/2, x₀]`, where `r_{T_j} ≥ ½`.
  `y² = e^{x₀T_j} < e^{2T_j}` because `x₀ < 2`. `y ≥ e^{2T_{j−1}}` needs `T_j/T_{j−1} ≥ 4/x₀`, and
  `T_j/T_{j−1} = 2^{2j−1} → ∞`. Both block conditions hold.
  - Variance lower bound: `Var(v_p cos θ_p) = 2log²p/p`. Mertens gives `s_j² ≥ ½ log y (log y + O(1)) ≍ T_j²`.
  - Remainder: orthogonality of `cos(kθ_p)` gives `E R_j² ≪ T_{j−1}² + O(1)`. Chebyshev then gives
  `μ(D_j) ≪ T_{j−1}²/T_j² = 4^{−(2j−1)}`, which is summable.
- **Step 5.**
  - `A_j` depends only on `Y_j`, and the `Y_j` depend on disjoint coordinate blocks, so they are independent and the
  product formula is legitimate.
  - `R_j` shares coordinates with `Y_j` through the prime-power terms, but only Chebyshev is used for `D_j`, so no
  independence is needed there.
  - Berry–Esseen: `Σ E|ξ_p|³ ≤ 1.48 s_j²`, which gives the stated `C/s_j`.
  - `Π μ(A_j) → 0`, since `μ(A_j) → Φ(1) < 0.85`.
- **Step 6.** Second Borel–Cantelli on the independent `Y_j`, first Borel–Cantelli on `R_j`. Correct.
- **Step 1.** `sup_{T≥1} a_T < ∞` is correct.

I accept Theorem B as PROVED.

**Verdict: excluded.**
- Fatal step: Theorems A/B with A′/B′ exclude mean-value, ergodic and almost-sure inputs. The remaining door is
  Bagchi-type use of `θ = 0`, which is a known equivalent of RH.
- Salvage: Theorem B (new, and it turns R157_002 row 48 into a theorem) and the "a.e.-twist test" for G2 candidates.
- Required fixes: cite Wintner 1944 for Thm C, cite Bagchi 1981/1982 in §8, and merge Thm A with G2_B3 Thm A.
