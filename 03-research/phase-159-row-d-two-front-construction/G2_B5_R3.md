## 1. Set-up recalled, and one identity that round 1 used only implicitly

From G2_B5_R1: `F̂(z) = ∫F(t)e^{izt}dt`; `D_T = {F ∈ L²(I_T) : ∫log(2+|τ|)|F̂|² < ∞}`; `S_T(τ) = m(τ) − Σ_{n∈N_T}
v_n cos(τ log n)`, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`; `𝔞_T(F) = (2π)^{−1}∫S_T|F̂|²`; `W_T(F) = 𝔞_T(F) + 2Re(A(F) conj B(F))` with
`A(F) = F̂(−i/2)`, `B(F) = F̂(i/2)`; `T*(λ) = sup{T : λ^W_T > λ}`.

**Lemma 1.1 (the complete form is a Toeplitz form with a distribution kernel; PROVED).** For `F, G ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`,
`W_T(F, G) = ⟨K_T, F ⋆ G̃⟩`, where `(F ⋆ G̃)(u) = ∫F(t) conj G(t − u) dt ∈ C_c^∞(−2T, 2T)` and `K_T` is the
distribution on `R`
`K_T = k_m + 2cosh(u/2) − ½ Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n (δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n})`,
with `k_m` the (tempered, log-singular at 0) inverse Fourier transform of `m`.

*Proof.* Take `F, G` real (the record's forms are evaluated on real test functions, and the complex case follows by
the reduction noted in B9 Lemma B(iii)). The `𝔞_T` part is Plancherel: `(2π)^{−1}∫S_T F̂ conj Ĝ = ⟨Š_T, F ⋆ G̃⟩`, with
`Š_T = k_m − ½Σ v_n(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n})`. For the polar part, `F̂(z) = ∫F(t)e^{izt}dt` gives `A(F) = F̂(−i/2) =
∫F(t)e^{t/2}dt` and `B(F) = F̂(i/2) = ∫F(t)e^{−t/2}dt`, both real. The quadratic form `2Re(A(F) conj B(F)) = 2A(F)B(F)`
polarises to `A(F)B(G) + B(F)A(G) = ∫∫F(t)G(s)[e^{(t−s)/2} + e^{(s−t)/2}] dt ds = ∫∫F(t)G(s)·2cosh((t−s)/2) dt ds`, the
Toeplitz pairing with kernel `2cosh(u/2)`. (The unsymmetrised product `A(F)B(G)` alone has kernel `e^{(t−s)/2}`, also a
function of `t − s`.) ∎

(B9 Lemma B noted the same translation covariance, `2Re(AB̄) = ⟨2cosh(u/2), c_F⟩`. I record it because Lemma G below
depends on it: the **whole** complete form, polar term included, is a Toeplitz form on `I_T`, so it is determined by,
and determines, the distribution `K_T` on `(−2T, 2T)`.)

## 2. Problem `H∞`, and its equivalence with RH

**Definition (Problem `H∞`).** Construct a trace-normed Hamiltonian `H : [0, ∞) → M₂(R)`, `H ≥ 0`, `tr H ≡ 1`, with
the property that the de Branges chain `(H(E_s))_{s ≥ 0}` of the canonical system `J Y′ = zHY` satisfies: for every
`T > 0` there is `s = σ(T)` with `H(E_{σ(T)}) = {F̂ : F ∈ D_T}` as a set, and `‖F̂‖²_{H(E_{σ(T)})} = W_T(F)`.

The phrase "built without the zeros" (the G1/OT-5′ demand) is not part of the definition; the definition is what any
window-time mechanism has to deliver, however it is built.

**Theorem H∞ (PROVED; EQUIVALENT to RH).** Problem `H∞` has a solution ⟺ RH. When it has one, the solution is unique
(up to the gauge of de Branges's Theorem 40 and reparametrisation of time), and `T ↦ σ(T)` is strictly increasing.

*Proof.*
(⟹) Suppose `H` solves `H∞`. A de Branges space carries a positive definite norm, so `W_T(F) = ‖F̂‖² > 0` for every
`F ∈ D_T ∖ {0}`. Hence `λ^W_T ≥ 0` for every `T`, so `W_T ≥ 0` for every `T`, which is Weil's criterion: RH
(`cor:RH`, direction (c) ⟹ (a); P47 §1).
(⟸) Under RH, `λ^W_T > 0` for every `T ≥ ½log 2` (proof of `cor:RH`, `thm:KEP3`(b)), and for `T < ½log 2` the
form is the archimedean-plus-polar form, positive for every `T > 0` (P46 Part I; P47 `thm:KEP3`(b)(2) covers every
`T > 0`). So `T*(0) = ∞`, and G2_B5_R1 Theorem B with the §1.1-verified hypotheses of G2_B5_R2 gives the isometric chain
`H(E_T)`, `T > 0`, and by de Branges Thm 40 (CITED) a trace-normed canonical system on `[0, σ_∞)` realising it,
`σ_∞ = lim σ(T) ≤ ∞`. Since `D_T ⊊ D_{T′}` for `T < T′`, the spaces are strictly increasing, and `σ` is strictly
increasing. If `σ_∞ < ∞`, extend `H` by any trace-normed positive Hamiltonian on `[σ_∞, ∞)` (e.g. a constant one); the
chain up to `σ_∞` is unchanged. Uniqueness up to gauge is de Branges Thm 40 plus Lemma G(ii) below (the
reparametrisation is fixed by `T`). ∎

**Relation to `thm:KEP3`.** `thm:KEP3`(b): RH ⟺ (2) `KEP(T)` solvable ∀`T` ⟺ (3) indeterminate ∀`T`. Theorem `H∞` is
(2) with the per-`T` extension problems glued into one chain. **This is a restatement of `thm:KEP3`(b) in
canonical-system form**, exactly as the round-1 panel said of `T*(0) = ∞`. The only content the chain adds to (2) is
structural (Theorem B: isometric nesting; Lemma G: rigidity), not a sign.

**What is not a restatement.** Theorem `H∞` says that a positive Hamiltonian on `[0,∞)` with the window spaces as
its chain is a **complete** encoding of RH; Lemma G says it is also a complete encoding of `Λ` and of `Γ`. A mechanism
in this angle would therefore have to produce a specific `H ≥ 0` on `[0, ∞)` from `Λ` and `Γ` and prove that its
chain is the window chain. That is precisely OT-5′ (G1), with the Hamiltonian as the carrier: the G2 arrow cannot
be run inside the canonical system because the system's existence is the arrow.

## 3. Lemma G: the Hamiltonian segment determines the datum

**Lemma G (inverse rigidity; PROVED, unconditional).** Let `(m, v)` and `(m′, v′)` be two admissible data: `m, m′`
even real tempered symbols with `m(τ), m′(τ) ≥ log(2+|τ|) − C`, and `v, v′ ≥ 0` combs with `Σ_{n<x} v_n = O(√x)`
(the record's admissibility for `lem:contT`/Theorem A). Suppose `T₀ < min(T*_{(m,v)}(0), T*_{(m′,v′)}(0))` and that the
two window-time Hamiltonians coincide on `[0, ℓ)` for some `ℓ > 0`. Then:

* (i) the time parametrisations coincide: `σ(T) = σ′(T)` for every `T` with `σ(T) < ℓ`;
* (ii) `K_T = K′_T` as distributions on `(−2T, 2T)` for every such `T`; hence `m − m′` has Fourier transform
  supported outside `(−2T, 2T)` — and if `m = m′` (the archimedean part is fixed, as in every G2 candidate), then
  `v_n = v′_n` for every `n < e^{2T}`.
* (iii) In particular, if the Hamiltonians coincide on all of `[0, ∞)` and `m = m′`, then `v = v′`.

*Proof.*
1. **Spaces coincide.** A trace-normed Hamiltonian on `[0, ℓ)` determines, through the transfer matrix `J Y′ = zHY`,
  the Hermite–Biehler functions `E_s` and the spaces `H(E_s)`, `s < ℓ`, **as sets of entire functions with their
  norms** (de Branges 1968 Thm 40 and the construction preceding it; CITED). So for each `s < ℓ` the two chains give
  the same normed space `𝒮_s`.
2. **`T` is determined.** For the first datum, `𝒮_s = {F̂ : F ∈ D_T}` with `T = T(s)`; for the second, with
  `T′ = T′(s)`. The set `{F̂ : F ∈ D_T}` is dense in the Paley–Wiener space `PW_T` and contains no function of
  exponential type `< T` other than… more simply: it contains `F̂` for `F = 1_{[T−ε,T]}·χ` (smooth `χ`), whose
  exponential type is exactly `T`, and every element has type `≤ T`. So `T = sup{type(f) : f ∈ 𝒮_s} = T′`. This is (i).
3. **Norms coincide, hence kernels.** For `F, G ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, `W_T(F,G) = ‖·‖`-polarisation `= W′_T(F,G)`. By Lemma
  1.1, `⟨K_T − K′_T, F ⋆ G̃⟩ = 0` for all such `F, G`. The functions `F ⋆ G̃` span a dense subspace of `C_c^∞(−2T,2T)`
  (translates and dilates of bumps: take `G` a fixed bump at `0` and `F` a bump at `u₀ ∈ (−2T, 2T)`, both inside
  `I_T`, which is possible because `|u₀| < 2T`; convolve to approximate any test function). So `K_T = K′_T` on
  `(−2T, 2T)`. This is (ii): the singular supports of `K_T − K′_T` in `(−2T,2T)` are the atoms at `±log n`, `n <
  e^{2T}`, with weights `−½(v_n − v′_n)`, plus the smooth part `k_m − k_{m′}` (with `2cosh(u/2)` cancelling). If `m
  = m′`, the atoms must vanish: `v_n = v′_n`.
4. (iii) is (ii) for every `T`. ∎

**Remarks.**
* **G1: causality (Theorem B(4)) is the forward direction, Lemma G the inverse.** So the map `datum|_{(−2T,2T)} ↦
  H|_{[0,σ(T)]}` is a bijection onto its image, segment by segment, unconditionally, wherever the chain exists. There is
  no compression: the Hamiltonian is not a "coarse" object that could be positive for reasons weaker than the exact
  comb. This is F1 in the sharpest possible form for this angle, and it is the unconditional companion of B9's
  Theorem R (which says, under RH, that no `ℓ¹_w`-perturbation of `Λ` keeps `∀T` positivity: by Lemma G(iii) such a
  perturbation would have to change `H` somewhere, and B9 says the change always destroys positivity at some time).
* **G2: no lossy encoding.** A frequent hope for canonical systems is that the Hamiltonian "forgets" arithmetic
  detail and keeps only what positivity needs (cf. the phase-64 von Mangoldt system, whose positivity was free). Lemma G
  shows the window-time Hamiltonian forgets nothing. Its positivity for all time is therefore a statement about the
  exact comb — consistent with O3 (`rem:resweights`) and with B6's Corollary M3(ii): a change of one `v_{n₀}` by a
  doubly exponentially small amount changes `H` on `[σ(½log n₀), ·)` and moves `T*` into a window of length `o(1)`.
* **Not new as mathematics.** Lemma G is de Branges's inverse-spectral uniqueness read through Lemma 1.1. It is new
  to the corpus as a statement about the window chain, and it closes a loophole a future attempt might try (a
  "lossy Hamiltonian").

## 4. Theorem Σ: the `∀T` arrow in window time, consolidated

**Theorem Σ (PROVED from the record and from the panel-verified round-2 results; no sign).** Let `(H(E_T))_{T<T*}` be
the window chain of ζ (G2_B5_R1 Thm B, hypotheses verified in G2_B5_R2 §1.1). Then:

* **(Σ1) Segments carry nothing forward** (G2_B5_R2 Theorem L, Cor L1; PROVED). For every `T₀ < T*` and every `T₁ >
  T₀` with an integer contact in `(2T₀, 2T₁)`, there is a comb `v′ ≥ 0` with the zeros and pole of ζ, no functional
  equation, the same Hamiltonian on `[0, σ(T₀)]`, and `T*_{v′} ∈ (T₀, T₁]`. No functional of a finite segment
  implies positivity at any later contact window on the `thm:finmod` class.
* **(Σ2) Uniform rules must carry the ordinates** (G2_B4_R2 Theorem R, Cor R2; PROVED, panel-verified 5/5 as
  correct). Any certificate rule `T ↦ w^φ_T` with a `T`-free edge profile has `T ↦ f_{w_T}(τ)` Bohr almost periodic in
  `T` with spectrum `2(τ − Ord)` and mean zero under RH, hence negative for arbitrarily large `T` (and for all large `T`
  under ¬RH). In chain language: no `T`-independent recipe for the chain's certificate exists; the `T`-dependence must
  encode `e^{2iTγ}` for every ordinate.
* **(Σ3) Per-window verification is exact to doubly exponential precision** (G2_B6_R2 Theorem M, Cor M1; PROVED,
  unconditional, panel-verified). `λ^W_T ≤ λ^A_T ≤ exp(−e^{(2−ε)T})` for `T ≥ T₀(ε)`. In chain language: the distance of
  the chain from blow-up at time `σ(T)` is at most `exp(−e^{(2−ε)T})`, so no estimate-driven argument certifies the
  segment `[0, σ(T)]`.
* **(Σ4) The germ is RH, and it encodes the comb exactly** (Theorem `H∞` and Lemma G, this memo; G2_B5_R2 Theorem Φ′
  with Lagarias 1999 CITED). The existence of the chain on `[0, ∞)` is RH; its Hamiltonian determines `Λ` and `Γ`
  exactly; the only functional-equation-bearing positivity statement at the germ is `Re ξ′/ξ > 0` on `Re s > ½`,
  EQUIVALENT to RH.

*Proof.* Each clause is the cited theorem; the "chain language" sentences are translations via Theorem B (the chain
exists iff `λ^W_T > 0`, `lem:monoT`/`lem:contT`) and Lemma G. ∎

**Reading.** In window time, the `∀T` arrow is: a positive Hamiltonian on `[0,∞)`. (Σ1) says it cannot be built by
continuation from a segment; (Σ2) says it cannot be written by a `T`-free formula; (Σ3) says it cannot be verified

record 3 (positivity must come from the prime side of the explicit formula) with the shape of the requirement proved:
**the prime-side input has to be the whole comb at once, in an exact identity, with the zero ordinates appearing as
the frequencies of its `T`-dependence** — and the record's only such identity is the explicit formula itself (E3).
