## §2 Theorem M1 — mollification is window widening (equality)

Let `J_n := I_T − log n = [−T−log n, T−log n]`, `1 ≤ n ≤ N`, and `U_N(T) := ∪_{n≤N} J_n^∘` (interiors).

**Theorem M1 (PROVED).** Let `𝒮_N(T) := span{F_M : F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T), M real of length ≤ N}`.

(i) `𝒮_N(T) = span{τ_{log n}F : F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T), n ≤ N} = C_c^∞(U_N(T))`.

(ii) If `T > ½log 2` then `U_N(T) = (−T−log N, T)` is connected for every `N`, and
```
inf_{G ∈ 𝒮_N(T), G≠0} W(G)/‖G‖² = λ^W_{T + ½log N} .
```
(iii) For every `T > 0`: `inf_{𝒮_N(T)} W/‖·‖² ≥ λ^W_{T+½log N}`, and the mollified form uses exactly the
weights `Λ(n)` with `n < Ne^{2T}`.

*Proof.*
* (i) `⊆`: each `F_M` is a finite combination of translates `τ_{log n}F` with `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, and
  `supp τ_{log n}F ⊂ J_n^∘`. `⊇`: let `G ∈ C_c^∞(U_N(T))`, `K := supp G` compact in `U_N(T)`. Choose
  `χ_n ∈ C_c^∞(J_n^∘)` with `Σ_n χ_n = 1` on `K` (smooth partition of unity subordinate to the finite open cover
  `{J_n^∘}` of `K`). Then `G = Σ_n χ_nG` and `χ_nG = τ_{log n}F_n` with `F_n := τ_{−log n}(χ_nG) ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`.
  Each `τ_{log n}F_n = n^{½}·(F_n)_{M}` with `M = n^{−s}`, so `G ∈ 𝒮_N(T)`. Also `𝒮_N(T)` contains each
  `τ_{log n}F` (take `M = n^{−s}`), giving the middle equality.
* (ii) Consecutive intervals `J_n^∘, J_{n+1}^∘` overlap iff `log((n+1)/n) < 2T`; the worst case `n = 1` is
  `log 2 < 2T`. So for `T > ½log 2` the union is the open interval `(−T−log N, T)`, of length `2T + log N`. Its
  translate by `+½log N` is `I_{T'}^∘`, `T' = T + ½log N`. `W` and `‖·‖` are translation invariant on `C_c^∞(ℝ)`
  (§1), so the infimum over `C_c^∞(U_N(T))` equals the infimum over `C_c^∞(I_{T'})`, which is `λ^W_{T'}` (form core,
  §1).
* (iii) `U_N(T) ⊂ (−T−log N, T)` always, so `𝒮_N(T) ⊂ τ_{−½log N}C_c^∞(I_{T'})` and the inequality follows. The
  contacts of window `T'` are `n < e^{2T'} = Ne^{2T}`. ∎

**Corollary M1a (no gain; PROVED).** By `lem:monoT`, `λ^W_{T+½log N} ≤ λ^W_T`. A mollifier of length `N` can only
lower the certified bottom, by exactly the amount the window loses in widening by `½log N`. In particular, for
`T > ½log 2` (the range where the classical result stops), **the statement "`W(F_M) ≥ 0` for all `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`
and all `M` of length `≤ N`" is implied by, and (as a statement on the span) equivalent to, `λ^W_{T+½log N} ≥ 0`**.
Letting `N → ∞` at fixed `T` is E1 (`∀T′ λ^W_{T′} ≥ 0`), EQUIVALENT.

**Remark M1b (what Levinson's length really buys).** In the density problem, the mollifier length `N = T^θ`
enters through the mean value theorem for `|ζM|²` (Conrey's `θ = 4/7`). At the Weil level the same `N` enters only
as the window shift `½log N`. There is no analogue of the mean-value gain, because (M1) the mollified family is
*all* of `C_c^∞(I_{T'})` and not a special subspace of it. HEURISTIC as a comparison; the statement it rests on is
Theorem M1.

## §3 Prop M2 — Tate moments, Nyman–Beurling, and Levinson's `λ = ±2`

**Proposition M2 (PROVED).**

(i) `A(F_M) = M(1)·A(F)` and `B(F_M) = M(0)·B(F)`. Hence `M(1) = 0` (the Nyman–Beurling/Báez-Duarte normalisation
`𝒟₀` of round 1) is exactly `A(F_M) = 0` for all `F`; and `M(1) = M(0) = 0` gives `F_M ∈ P_{T'} ∩ C_c^∞`.

(ii) For real `λ ≠ 0`, the range of `1 + λ∂` on `C_c^∞(I_T)` is `{g ∈ C_c^∞(I_T) : ∫g(t)e^{t/λ}dt = 0}`. So the
range of `1 + 2∂` is `{A = 0}`, the range of `1 − 2∂` is `{B = 0}`, and the range of
`(1−2∂)(1+2∂) = 1 − 4∂² = 4Π` (`Π = −∂² + ¼`, P45 `lem:Pi`, CITED) is `P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`, with multiplier
`Φ_{(1−4∂²)F}(s) = 4s(1−s)Φ_F(s)`.

(iii) (round 2, Prop L1(iii)) `W((1+λ∂)F) = W(F) + λ²W(F′)`. At `λ = ±2` this reads: `W` restricted to `{A = 0}`
(resp. `{B = 0}`) is `W(F) + 4W(F′)`, a sum of two window values.

*Proof.* (i) `∫F(t+log n)e^{t/2}dt = n^{−½}A(F)` and `∫F(t+log n)e^{−t/2}dt = n^{½}B(F)`; multiply by `a_n n^{−½}`
and sum. (ii) `(1+λ∂)F = g` has the unique solution `F(t) = λ^{−1}∫_{−∞}^t e^{−(t−s)/λ}g(s)ds` (for `λ > 0`;
for `λ < 0` integrate from `+∞`) among functions vanishing at `−∞`; it vanishes for `t > T` iff
`∫g(s)e^{s/λ}ds = 0`. Conversely each `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` gives `g = (1+λ∂)F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` with
`∫g e^{t/λ} = ∫(F + λF′)e^{t/λ} = ∫Fe^{t/λ} − ∫Fe^{t/λ} = 0`. For `1 − 4∂²`, the Green function is
`G(t) = ¼e^{−|t|/2}` (`(1−4∂²)e^{−|t|/2} = 4δ`), and `F = G*g` vanishes for `t > T` iff `A(g) = 0`, for `t < −T` iff
`B(g) = 0`. The multiplier: `1 − 4(s−½)² = 4s(1−s)`. (iii) is round 2. ∎

**Reading.** Levinson's combination `ζ + λζ′` becomes, at the level of tests, `(1 − λ(s−½))Φ_F(s)`
(Prop L1(iii)). At `λ = ±2` the multiplier `1 ∓ 2(s−½)` vanishes at `s = 0` or `s = 1`: it kills the pole term, i.e.
one Tate moment. **The complete → primitive passage of Paper 46 is Levinson's combination at `λ = ±2`.** No other
`λ` has an arithmetic meaning at the window level; Levinson's `λ ≍ 1/log T` is a density-problem optimisation with
no window counterpart (Theorem M1: there is nothing to optimise, the family is the whole widened window).

## §4 Theorem M3 — the mollified comb (exact prime side)

**Theorem M3 (PROVED).** For real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` and real `M` of length `N`,
```
c_{F_M}(u) = Σ_{m,n≤N} a_m a_n (mn)^{−½} c_F(u + log n − log m), (4.1)
```
and the prime side of `W(F_M)` (the term `−Σ_k v_k c_{F_M}(log k)` of `eq:Aform` at window `T' = T+½log N`, after
centring) is
```
−∫_ℝ c_F dΛ_M, Λ_M := ½Σ_{k≥2} Σ_{m,n≤N} a_m a_n Λ(k)(kmn)^{−½} [δ_{log(kn/m)} + δ_{−log(kn/m)}], (4.2)
```
an even discrete measure of finite total mass on every compact, with two-sided Mellin transform
```
∫_ℝ e^{(s−½)x} dΛ_M(x) = −½[(ζ′/ζ)(s) + (ζ′/ζ)(1−s)]·M(s)M(1−s) (Re s ∈ (0,1) away from poles, as formal
Dirichlet series; absolutely convergent for Re s > 1 on the first term and Re s < 0 on the second). (4.3)
```

*Proof.* (4.1): `c_{F_M}(u) = Σ_{m,n}a_ma_n(mn)^{−½}∫F(t+log m)F(t+u+log n)dt`, substitute `t' = t + log m`.
(4.2): `−Σ_k v_k c_{F_M}(log k) = −Σ_k 2Λ(k)k^{−½}Σ_{m,n}a_ma_n(mn)^{−½}c_F(log(kn/m))`; since `c_F` is even, split
each term into halves at `±log(kn/m)`. Centring by `½log N` does not change `c`. (4.3): the atom at `log(kn/m)`
contributes `(kn/m)^{s−½}(kmn)^{−½}Λ(k)a_ma_n = Λ(k)k^{s−1}·a_n n^{s−1}·a_m m^{−s}`; summing, `−(ζ′/ζ)(1−s)·M(1−s)·M(s)`;
the reflected atom gives `−(ζ′/ζ)(s)M(s)M(1−s)`. Absolute convergence of the `k`-sum needs `Re s > 1` for
`Σ Λ(k)k^{−s}` and `Re s < 0` for `Σ Λ(k)k^{s−1}`; the measure itself is locally finite because the atoms
`log(kn/m)` with `k ≤ X` are finitely many. ∎

**Corollary M3a (the Levinson–Conrey object at the Weil level; PROVED as an identity, HEURISTIC as a comparison).**
Levinson–Conrey mollify `ζ(s)` (or `ζ + λζ′`) by `M(s)`, with `M ≈ 1/ζ` truncated (`a_n = μ(n)P(log(N/n)/log N)`),
so that `ζM ≈ 1` in mean square on the line. At the Weil level, (4.3) shows that the exact object is
`−(ζ′/ζ)·M·M^∨` with `M^∨(s) = M(1−s)`: the **logarithmic derivative** is mollified by the FE-symmetric product. With
`M ≈ 1/ζ`, `−(ζ′/ζ)M ≈ −ζ′/ζ²`, which is not a simplification: the mean-square mechanism (`ζM ≈ 1`) has no
counterpart in the comb. (Compare G2_B2_R2 Prop Ψ4: writing `−ζ′/ζ = (−ζ′)(1/ζ)` and flowing `1/ζ` through Möbius
truncations lands on `ζ(½+it)` — the same conclusion from the Möbius side.)

**Corollary M3b (obstructions transfer; PROVED).** By Theorem M1(ii), for `T > ½log 2` the mollified form is the
window form at `T' = T + ½log N`. Hence:
* O3 (`rem:resweights`): a change of one `Λ(n₀)`, `n₀ < Ne^{2T}`, by `≈ e^{−14.4T'}` flips the sign of the mollified
  form on some `F_M`. The mollified identity must be exact at that precision.

## §5 Theorem B — rank-one (biquadratic) mollified positivity at one window

Theorem M1 concerns the *span* of the mollified tests. The mollified statements that Levinson-type arguments
actually produce are about the tests `F_M` themselves — a set which is not a linear space (it is the set of
"rank-one" elements `F ⊗ a` of `L²(I_T) ⊗ ℝ^N`). The corresponding positivity is biquadratic, and a priori weaker.

**Definition B1.** For `T₀ > 0`:
```
B_N(T₀): ∀F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀}) real, ∀a ∈ ℝ^N: W(F_M) ≥ 0, M = Σ_{n≤N}a_nn^{−s}; B_∞(T₀) := ∀N B_N(T₀).
```
For real `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)` put `β_F(u) := W_bil(F, τ_uF)`, `u ∈ ℝ` (the **shift function** of `F`).

**Lemma B0 (PROVED).**
(a) `β_F(u) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)cosh((ρ−½)u)`, absolutely convergent, locally uniformly in `u`; `β_F` is even and
continuous; `β_F(0) = W(F)`.
(b) For real `c_1,…,c_k` and real `x_1,…,x_k`: `W(Σ_jc_jτ_{x_j}F) = Σ_{i,j}c_ic_jβ_F(x_j − x_i)`.
(c) `W(F_M) = a^ᵀQ_Fa`, `Q_F(m,n) = (mn)^{−½}β_F(log n − log m)`.
(d) For real `ψ` with `∫|ψ(x)|e^{|x|/2}dx < ∞` (so that `F*ψ` has the exponential decay needed by the explicit
formula on the closed strip), `W(F*ψ) = ∫∫ψ(x)ψ(y)β_F(x−y)dxdy`, where `W(F*ψ) := Σ_ρ Φ_{F*ψ}(ρ)Φ_{F*ψ}(1−ρ)` and
`Φ_{F*ψ}(s) = Φ_F(s)·∫ψ(x)e^{−(s−½)x}dx`.

*Proof.* (a) `Φ_{τ_uF}(s) = e^{−(s−½)u}Φ_F(s)`, so `½[Φ_F(ρ)Φ_{τ_uF}(1−ρ) + Φ_{τ_uF}(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)] =
Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)·½[e^{(ρ−½)u} + e^{−(ρ−½)u}]`. Since `|Re ρ − ½| < ½`, `|cosh((ρ−½)u)| ≤ e^{|u|/2}`, and
`Σ_ρ|Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)| < ∞` (`eq:W`, absolute convergence), the series converges uniformly on compacts in `u`; each
term is even and continuous. (b) Bilinearity and translation invariance of `W_bil`: `W_bil(τ_{x_i}F, τ_{x_j}F) =
W_bil(F, τ_{x_j−x_i}F)`. (c) is (b) with `c_n = a_nn^{−½}`, `x_n = log n`. (d) `F*ψ = ∫ψ(x)τ_{−x}F dx`;
`Φ_{F*ψ}(ρ)Φ_{F*ψ}(1−ρ) = Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)·∫∫ψ(x)ψ(y)e^{−(ρ−½)x}e^{(ρ−½)y}dxdy`; symmetrising in `(x,y)` gives
`∫∫ψ(x)ψ(y)cosh((ρ−½)(x−y))`, and the `ρ`-sum may be exchanged with the double integral by Fubini, the dominating
function being `Σ_ρ|Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)|·|ψ(x)||ψ(y)|e^{|x|/2}e^{|y|/2}`. ∎

**Theorem B (PROVED as stated in each clause).**

(i) `RH ⟹ B_∞(T₀)` for every `T₀`.

(ii) `B_∞(T₀) ⟹ λ^W_{T₀} ≥ 0`.

(iii) `B_∞(T₀) ⟺ β_F` is positive definite on `ℝ` for every real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` `⟺ W(F*ψ) ≥ 0` for all such `F`
and all real `ψ` as in Lemma B0(d).

*Proof.* (i) Under RH, `Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ) = |Φ_F(½+iγ)|² ≥ 0` and `cosh(iγu) = cos(γu)`, so `β_F(u) = Σ_γ|Φ_F|²cos(γu)`
is positive definite (Bochner; an absolutely convergent sum of PD functions). Then `[β_F(log n − log m)]_{m,n}` is
PSD, `[(mn)^{−½}]` is PSD of rank one, and their Schur product `Q_F` is PSD (Schur product theorem). By Lemma B0(c),
`W(F_M) ≥ 0`. (Of course (i) also follows from Weil's easy direction; the Schur proof shows *where* RH enters:
`β_F` PD.)
(ii) `M = 1`.
(iii) `⟸` (first): Lemma B0(c) and PD of `β_F` applied to the points `x_n = log n` with weights `a_nn^{−½}`.
`⟹`: fix `F`. `B_∞(T₀)` and Lemma B0(c) give `Σ_{m,n∈S}b_mb_nβ_F(log n − log m) ≥ 0` for every finite `S ⊂ ℕ` and
real `b` (put `a_n = b_nn^{½}` on `S`, `0` off `S`, `N = max S`). Given reals `x_1,…,x_k` and `c_1,…,c_k`, let
`L → ∞` and `n_j(L) := ⌊Le^{x_j}⌉` (nearest integer). Then `log n_j(L) − log n_i(L) → x_j − x_i`. By continuity of
`β_F` (Lemma B0(a)), `Σc_ic_jβ_F(x_j − x_i) = lim_L Σc_ic_jβ_F(log n_j(L) − log n_i(L)) ≥ 0`. So `β_F` is PD on `ℝ`.
Second `⟺`: a continuous function is PD iff `∫∫ψψ β ≥ 0` for all `ψ ∈ C_c(ℝ)` (Riemann sums / Bochner); extend to
`ψ` with `∫|ψ|e^{|x|/2} < ∞` by truncation and dominated convergence (`|β_F(u)| ≤ β_F(0)` for PD `β_F`); then apply
Lemma B0(d). ∎

**Definition B2 (dominant zero).** Write each zero as `ρ = ½ + a_ρ + iγ_ρ`. An off-line zero `ρ₀` (`a₀ := a_{ρ₀} ≠ 0`)
is **dominant** if for every zero `ρ` outside the quadruple `{ρ₀, ρ̄₀, 1−ρ₀, 1−ρ̄₀}`,
```
a_ρ² − (γ_ρ − γ₀)² < a₀² .
```
Since `a_ρ² ≤ ¼`, zeros with `|γ_ρ − γ₀| ≥ ½` satisfy this automatically; the condition concerns the finitely many
zeros with `|γ_ρ − γ₀| < ½`. In particular `ρ₀` is dominant if `|a₀| = max_ρ|a_ρ|` (attained supremum), hence if
there are finitely many off-line zeros.

**Proposition B3 (PROVED).** Suppose RH fails and `ζ` has a dominant off-line zero `ρ₀ = ½ + a₀ + iγ₀`, `a₀ > 0`
(replace `ρ₀` by `1−ρ̄₀` if needed). Then for every `T₀ > 0`, `B_∞(T₀)` fails: there are `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})`, `N`, and
real `M` of length `≤ N` with `W(F_M) < 0`.

*Proof.* By Theorem B(iii) it suffices to find real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` and real `ψ` with `∫|ψ|e^{|x|/2} < ∞` and
`W(F*ψ) < 0`. Put `c_ρ := Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)` and `w_ρ := ρ − ½`.

*Step 1 (choice of `F`).* `Re c_{ρ₀} = Re[φ(w₀)φ(−w₀)]`, `φ := Φ_F(½+·)`, is a real quadratic form in `F` with
symmetric kernel `k(t,s) = Re[e^{w₀t}e^{−w₀s} + e^{w₀s}e^{−w₀t}] = 2cosh(a₀(t−s))cos(γ₀(t−s))`, which is not
identically zero on `I_{T₀}×I_{T₀}`. So there is `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` with `Re c_{ρ₀} ≠ 0`; fix one, and put
`ε := sgn Re c_{ρ₀}`.

*Step 2 (the localiser).* For `σ > 0` and `k ∈ {1,2}` let `ψ(x) := x^k e^{−x²/2σ²}cos(γ₀x)`, with `k = 1` if `ε = +1`
and `k = 2` if `ε = −1`. Let `ĝ_k(z) := ∫x^ke^{−x²/2σ²}e^{−zx}dx`: `ĝ_1(z) = −zσ³√(2π)e^{z²σ²/2}`,
`ĝ_2(z) = σ³√(2π)(1 + z²σ²)e^{z²σ²/2}`. Then `P(w) := ∫ψ(x)e^{−wx}dx = ½[ĝ_k(w+iγ₀) + ĝ_k(w−iγ₀)]`, and
`Φ_{F*ψ}(ρ)Φ_{F*ψ}(1−ρ) = c_ρP(w_ρ)P(−w_ρ)`.

*Step 3 (the quadruple).* At `w₀ = a₀+iγ₀`: `P(w₀) = ½[ĝ_k(a₀+2iγ₀) + ĝ_k(a₀)]` and `P(−w₀) = ½[ĝ_k(−a₀) +
ĝ_k(−a₀−2iγ₀)]`. Since `|ĝ_k(a ± 2iγ₀)| ≤ C_k(σ)e^{(a² − 4γ₀²)σ²/2}` and `γ₀ ≥ 14`, these terms are
`O(e^{−700σ²})` relative to the others. Hence
```
P(w₀)P(−w₀) = ¼ĝ_k(a₀)ĝ_k(−a₀)(1 + O(e^{−700σ²})) = (−1)^k·(π/2)·q_k(a₀,σ)·e^{a₀²σ²}(1 + O(e^{−700σ²})),
q_1 = a₀²σ⁶, q_2 = σ⁶(1 + a₀²σ²)².
```
Write `Q(w) := P(w)P(−w)`; it is even, and `Q(w̄) = conj Q(w)` (`ψ` real). The zeros `ρ₀` and `1−ρ₀` give the
same term `c_{ρ₀}Q(w₀)` (the pairing in (1.1) is symmetric), and `ρ̄₀`, `1−ρ̄₀` give `c_{ρ̄₀}Q(w̄₀) = conj[c_{ρ₀}Q(w₀)]`
(`c_{ρ̄₀} = conj c_{ρ₀}` for real `F`; multiplicities are counted in `c_ρ`). The quadruple's total contribution is
therefore `4Re[c_{ρ₀}Q(w₀)] = 4Re(c_{ρ₀})·(−1)^k(π/2)q_k(a₀,σ)e^{a₀²σ²}(1 + O(e^{−700σ²}))`, which by the choice
of `k` equals `−4|Re c_{ρ₀}|(π/2)q_k(a₀,σ)e^{a₀²σ²}(1 + o(1))`: **strictly negative, of exponential size
`e^{a₀²σ²}`**.

*Step 4 (the other zeros).* For a zero `ρ` with `η := γ_ρ − γ₀`, `|P(w_ρ)| ≤ ½[|ĝ_k(a_ρ + i(η+2γ₀))| + |ĝ_k(a_ρ+iη)|]`
and `|ĝ_k(a+iη)| ≤ σ³√(2π)(1 + (|a|+|η|)σ)^{2}e^{(a²−η²)σ²/2}`. Hence
`|P(w_ρ)P(−w_ρ)| ≤ C σ⁶(1+|η|)⁴(1+σ)⁴e^{(a_ρ²−η²)σ²}` (the `2γ₀`-shifted terms being smaller). Split:
* on-line zeros (`a_ρ = 0`): `|P(iγ)|² = |ψ̂(γ)|² ≤ (∫|ψ|)² ≤ Cσ^{2k+2}`, so their total is `≤ Cσ⁶Σ_ρ|c_ρ| = O(σ⁶)`;
* off-line zeros outside the quadruple: by dominance, `δ_ρ := a₀² − (a_ρ² − η_ρ²) > 0` for each, and
  `δ_ρ ≥ a₀²` when `|η_ρ| ≥ ½`; the finitely many zeros with `|η_ρ| < ½` have `δ := min δ_ρ > 0`. So their total is
  `≤ Cσ¹⁰e^{(a₀²−min(δ,a₀²))σ²}Σ_ρ|c_ρ|(1+|η_ρ|)⁴ = O(σ¹⁰e^{(a₀²−δ')σ²})`, `δ' > 0`, using that `c_ρ` decays faster
  than any power of `|γ_ρ|` (`F ∈ C_c^∞`; `|Φ_F(½+a+iγ)| ≤ C_j|γ|^{−j}` uniformly in `|a| ≤ ½`).

*Step 5.* `W(F*ψ) = −4|Re c_{ρ₀}|(π/2)q_k e^{a₀²σ²}(1+o(1)) + O(σ¹⁰e^{(a₀²−δ')σ²}) + O(σ⁶) < 0` for `σ` large. By
Theorem B(iii) (contrapositive of `⟹`), `B_∞(T₀)` fails, i.e. some `N`, `F`, `M` have `W(F_M) < 0`. ∎

**Remark B3a (on the hypothesis).** The dominance hypothesis is what the localiser `x^ke^{−x²/2σ²}cos(γ₀x)` can
resolve: its weight on a zero is `e^{(a²−η²)σ²}`, so it separates `ρ₀` from every zero except those with larger
`a` at nearly the same height. If the supremum `Θ − ½` of `a_ρ` is not attained, a non-dominant configuration
requires an infinite sequence of zeros `ρ_k` with `a_{k+1}² − a_k² > (γ_{k+1} − γ_k)²`, hence
`Σ_k(γ_{k+1}−γ_k)² < ¼`, `a_k ↑`. Nothing in the record excludes this. **Q-M (OPEN): does `B_∞(T₀)` imply RH
without the dominance hypothesis?** The classical Weil converse uses the full test space (all `F`), where a
node can be placed at each competing zero independently; here `F` is fixed and only `ψ` varies, which is why the
question is not the classical one. I expect the answer to be yes (the natural attempt: choose `F` with `Re c_ρ`
small on the finitely many chain elements below height `Y` and use the decay of `c_ρ` above `Y`; the obstacle is
that the required smallness must beat `e^{(a_ρ² − a₀²)σ²}` for zeros with `a_ρ > a₀`, which no fixed `F` achieves
for all `σ`). I do not claim it.

**Remark B4 (the shift function under RH; PROVED).** Under RH, `β_F(u) = Σ_γ|Φ_F(½+iγ)|²cos(γu)` is a Bohr
almost-periodic positive-definite function of the shift with spectrum `±Ord` and Bochner measure
`Σ_γ|Φ_F(½+iγ)|²(δ_γ + δ_{−γ})/2`: the zero-counting measure weighted by `|Φ_F|²`. This is the shift-variable twin
of G2_B4_R2's Theorem R (a.p. in `T` of the certificate value) and of `thm:Z(ii)` (the certificate as a smoothed
zero measure in `τ`). `B_∞(T₀)` is exactly the statement that, for every `F` supported in `I_{T₀}`, this
weighted zero measure is a **positive** measure — the "reality of the zeros seen through `F`". On the prime side it
is the family of all window forms `W_{T₀+½log N}` on the rank-one tests, by Theorem M1/M3.
