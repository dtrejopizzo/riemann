# G2_B6_R1 — G2 (the mechanism), round 1: finite and random zero universes as a probe; the horizon of a certificate

**Angle.** The idea, done correctly. Take a universe of 100 zeros chosen at random rather than in order. By
`thm:finZ` no such universe carries a certificate. I use it as a probe: I compute where and how the truncated certificate
fails, derive which infinite structure repairs the failure, and ask whether that structure is a law giving every window
from the exact `Λ(n)`.

**Answer in one paragraph.**
* The repairing structure has two parts. Above a height `H_*(w)`, the *horizon* of the certificate, only the growth of
  the zero density is needed. There the certificate survives the deletion, insertion or off-line displacement of a
  sparse set of zeros, and with positive probability it survives a random thinning (Theorem H and Corollary H1, PROVED,
  new). Below the horizon every zero is load-bearing: in the probe, deleting any one of the 29 zeros below height 100
  breaks the certificate (DIAGNOSTIC).
* The only law that fixes the configuration below the horizon from the primes is the explicit formula itself
  (`thm:Z(i)`). That formula already *is* the certificate. So the "sequence equation" asked for exists, is
  classical (Riemann, Guinand, Weil), and supplies no sign.
* The horizon grows at least like `exp(2A_T)`, with `A_T = Σ_{n<e^{2T}} 2Λ(n)/√n ≍ 4e^T`. The "for all `T`" arrow is
  therefore an exhaustion of all heights by horizons, with the exact configuration below each one. That is RH again.

No sign is claimed at any window. Row D keeps no label.

Form conventions: all statements concern the **complete** form `W_T` (flat-top certificates with `β = (2,0)`,
`prop:P`) unless stated otherwise.

## §1 Definitions

Normalisations are those of Paper 47: `F̂(τ) = ∫F(t)e^{iτt}dt`, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`, `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`,
`m(τ) = Re ψ(¼ + iτ/2) − log π`, `𝒲_T` as in `def:W`.

* **Zero multiset.** `Z` is the multiset of `γ` with `ρ = ½ + iγ` a non-trivial zero of `ζ`, counted with multiplicity.
  It is invariant under `γ ↦ −γ` and `γ ↦ γ̄`, and `|Im γ| < ½`. An **orbit** is a class under these two involutions:
  `{γ, −γ}` if `γ` is real, `{γ, γ̄, −γ, −γ̄}` otherwise.
* **Admissible multisets.** `𝔐` is the set of multisets `Z'` of complex numbers with `|Im γ| ≤ ½` that are invariant under
  both involutions. For `Z' ∈ 𝔐` and `w ∈ 𝒲_T ∩ C_c^k`, put `f_{w,Z'}(τ) := Σ_{γ∈Z'} ŵ(τ − γ)`. This is real, because
  `ŵ` is even and real on `ℝ` and `Z'` is conjugation-invariant. It converges absolutely under the counting bound of
  Lemma 2. `f_{w,Z} = f_w` (`thm:Z(ii)`, `lem:smooth(ii)`).
* **Kernel majorant.** Let `w ∈ 𝒲_T` be supported in `[−U,U]`, of class `C^k` (`k ≥ 0`), with `w^{(k+1)}` of bounded
  variation `V`. Put `g(t) := sup_{|y|≤½}|ŵ(t+iy)|`. It is even, and by `lem:smooth(i)` it satisfies
  `g(t) ≤ e^{U/2} min(‖w‖₁, V|t|^{−k−2})`. Put `s_j := sup_{[j,j+1]} g` for `j ∈ ℤ`, and
  `G₀ := Σ_j s_j`, `G₁ := Σ_j s_j log(|j|+5)`. Both are finite because `s_j ≪ |j|^{−2}`.
* **Counting function.** `n_M(x) := #{γ ∈ M : Re γ ∈ [x, x+1)}` for a multiset `M`, and `ν(x) := 2log(|x|+4) + 4`.
* **Weight constants.** `κ_w := ∫_{2T}^∞ |1−w|K`, `A_w := Σ_{n≥2} v_n|w(log n)|`, `P_w := 2∫_ℝ |w(u)|cosh(u/2)du`,
  `β_m := sup_{τ≥1}(log τ − m(τ))`, and `B_w := β_m + κ_w + A_w + P_w`. Stirling gives `β_m < ∞`; numerically
  `β_m = 2.025`, attained at `τ = 1` (DIAGNOSTIC value; only finiteness is used). Because `w ≡ 1` on the window,
  `A_w ≥ A_T := Σ_{n∈𝒩_T} v_n`.
* **Sparse modification.** For `Z' ∈ 𝔐`, let `δ(Z') := (Z∖Z') + (Z'∖Z)` be the symmetric difference as multisets. `Z'` is
  an **`(H, ε)`-modification** of `Z` if `Z'` agrees with `Z` on `{|Re γ| ≤ H}` and `n_{δ(Z')}(x) ≤ ε ν(x)` for every
  real `x`. This covers deletions, insertions of fake zeros, and moves of zeros off the line, all above height `H`.
* **Thinning.** Let `(ξ_O)` be iid Bernoulli(`p`) indexed by orbits, and `S_p := ⋃{O : ξ_O = 1}`.

**What the probe says.**
1. **Random finite universes fail everywhere, not only at the edge.** Each isolated zero exposes the negative lobes of
  `ŵ`, so about 40 % of the line is negative. A successive universe fails only above its top, which is `cor:infinite`.
  The certificate therefore needs **local completeness**: every `τ` must see *all* zeros within the effective reach of
  `ŵ`.
2. **Rigidity is concentrated at low height and fades as `log τ` grows.** Near the minimum, every zero matters. High up,
  the certificate has a surplus of about `log(τ/2π)` against a local perturbation of size `O(1)`.
3. **Random thinning survives if the low zeros are protected** and the deletion rate is small. This is the phenomenon
  that Theorem H proves.

## §3 Theorems

### 3.1 Lemma 1 (local zero count) — PROVED (from the import (X-N) of P47 §`subsec:margin`, Rosser 1941)
For every real `x`, `n_Z(x) ≤ ν(x)`.

*Proof.* `Re γ = Im ρ`. For `x ≥ 2`, (X-N) at `t = x+1` and at `t = x−η` with `η ↓ 0`, together with
`M(t) = (t/2π)log(t/2πe) + 7/8`, gives:
* `#{Im ρ ∈ [x,x+1)} ≤ M(x+1) − M(x) + 2E(x+1)`, where `E(t) = 0.137 log t + 0.443 log log t + 1.588` is increasing;
* `M(x+1) − M(x) ≤ (2π)^{−1}log(x+1)`, by the mean value theorem, since `M′(t) = (2π)^{−1}log(t/2π)`.

Using `log log y ≤ log y` for `y ≥ 3`, the total is at most `1.32 log(x+1) + 3.18 ≤ ν(x)`. For `x < 2` with `x+1 ≤ 14`
there is no zero, because `γ₁ > 14`. For `x < 0`, the symmetry `γ ↦ −γ` gives
`n_Z(x) = #{Im ρ ∈ (y, y+1]}` with `y = −x−1`. If `y < 13` this is 0. Otherwise the same argument applied to the
interval `(y, y+1]`, i.e. to `N(y+1) − N(y)`, gives `≤ 1.32 log(y+2) + 3.18 = 1.32 log(|x|+1) + 3.18 ≤ ν(x)`. ∎

### 3.2 Lemma 2 (sums against a sparse multiset) — PROVED
Let `M` be a multiset with `|Im γ| ≤ ½` and `n_M(x) ≤ εν(x)` for all `x`. Then for every real `τ`,
`Σ_{γ∈M} g(τ − Re γ) ≤ ε[ν(τ)G₀ + 2G₁]`.
Also, if `M ⊂ {|Re γ| > H}` and `|τ| ≤ H/2`, then `Σ_{γ∈M} g(τ − Re γ) ≤ ε R(H)`, where
`R(H) := Σ_{|i| ≥ H/2−2} s_i(ν(H/2) + 2log(|i|+5))`. `R(H) → 0` as `H → ∞`.

*Proof.* Let `M_j` be the part of `M` with `Re γ − τ ∈ [j, j+1)`, for `j ∈ ℤ`. Then:
* `#M_j ≤ εν(τ+j)`;
* `g(τ − Re γ) ≤ s_{−j−1}` on `M_j`;
* `ν(τ+j) ≤ 2log((|τ|+4)(|j|+4)) + 4 = ν(τ) + 2log(|j|+4)`.

Put `i = −j−1`. Then `log(|j|+4) ≤ log(|i|+5)`, and summing gives the first bound. For the second, only the `j` with
`[τ+j, τ+j+1)` meeting `{|x| > H}` contribute. For those, `|i| ≥ H/2 − 2`, and `ν(τ) ≤ ν(H/2)`. `R(H) → 0` because
`s_i ≪ |i|^{−2}` (`k ≥ 0`), so `R(H) ≪ log H / H`. ∎

### 3.3 Lemma 3 (growth of a flat-top smoothing) — PROVED
For `w ∈ 𝒲_T ∩ C_c^k` and `τ ≥ 1`, `f_w(τ) ≥ log τ − B_w`.

*Proof.* By `eq:fw` and `eq:m`,
`f_w(τ) = m(τ) + ∫_{2T}^∞(1−w)cos(τu)K du − Σ_{n≥2} v_n w(log n)cos(τ log n) + 2∫_ℝ w(u)cosh(u/2)cos(τu)du`,
because `C₀ + ∫₀^∞(1 − w cos τu)K = m(τ) + ∫₀^∞(1−w)cos(τu)K` and `1 − w = 0` on `[0,2T]`. The three extra terms are
bounded in absolute value by `κ_w`, `A_w` and `P_w`, and `m(τ) ≥ log τ − β_m` for `τ ≥ 1`. ∎

### 3.4 Theorem H (the horizon of a certificate) — PROVED, new

**Statement.** Let `T > 0`, and let `w ∈ 𝒲_T ∩ C_c^k` (`k ≥ 0`) satisfy `f_w ≥ c > 0` on `ℝ`. Let `0 < ε ≤ ε₀ := 1/(4G₀)`
and let `H ≥ 8` satisfy:
* **(H1)** `R(H) ≤ c/2`;
* **(H2)** `log(H/2) ≥ 2B_w + 2.7 + G₁/G₀ + c`.

Then every `(H, ε)`-modification `Z'` of `Z` satisfies `f_{w,Z'} ≥ c/2` on `ℝ`. In words: a certificate at window `T` is
blind to any `ε₀`-sparse deletion, insertion or off-line displacement of zeros above its horizon
`H_*(w) := min{H : (H1), (H2)}`. The horizon satisfies `H_*(w) ≥ 2exp(2B_w) ≥ 2exp(2A_T)`.

*Proof.* Absolute convergence of `f_{w,Z'}`: `n_{Z'} ≤ n_Z + n_δ ≤ 2ν` by Lemma 1, and Lemma 2 applies with `ε = 2`.
Next, `f_{w,Z'} − f_w = Σ_{Z'∖Z} ŵ(τ−γ) − Σ_{Z∖Z'} ŵ(τ−γ)`, a real number, so `|f_{w,Z'} − f_w| ≤ Σ_{γ∈δ} g(τ − Re γ)`.
Since `δ ⊂ {|Re γ| > H}`, the second part of Lemma 2 (with `ε ≤ 1`) gives `|f_{w,Z'} − f_w| ≤ R(H) ≤ c/2` for
`|τ| ≤ H/2`, hence `f_{w,Z'} ≥ c/2` there.

`f_{w,Z'}` is even, so take `τ ≥ H/2 ≥ 4`. By Lemmas 2 and 3 and `ε ≤ 1/(4G₀)`,
`f_{w,Z'}(τ) ≥ log τ − B_w − ¼ν(τ) − G₁/(2G₀)`.
For `τ ≥ 4`, `ν(τ) ≤ 2log(2τ) + 4`, so `¼ν(τ) ≤ ½log τ + 1.35`. Hence
`f_{w,Z'}(τ) ≥ ½log τ − B_w − 1.35 − G₁/(2G₀)`, which is `≥ c/2` by (H2). The lower bound on `H_*` is (H2). ∎

**Scope.**
* The theorem is conditional on a certificate. It gives no sign at any window; it transports one.
* Certificates with `c > 0` exist for every `T ≤ 0.8`. Zhu 2026 (CITED, not re-verified) gives `λ^W_{0.8} > 0`, and
  `λ^W` is nonincreasing in `T`. Then `thm:Zexh(i)` supplies `w` at every `T ∈ [½log2, 0.8]`, and the certificate at
  `½log2` serves every smaller `T`, because `𝒲_{T′} ⊂ 𝒲_T` for `T′ > T`. Yoshida–Bombieri give `λ^W ≥ 0` for
  `T ≤ ½log2`. Whether that is strict was not re-checked here, so strictness below `½log2` also rests on Zhu.
* The theorem is stated for `w ∈ C_c^k`; `thm:Zexh` produces `w ∈ 𝒲^reg ⊂ C_c^∞`.

### 3.5 Corollary H1 (random thinning above the horizon) — PROVED, new
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem H, and put `q := 1 − p ≤ q₀ := (ε₀/6e)·e^{−6/ε₀}`. Condition on `ξ_O = 1` for every
orbit with `|Re γ| ≤ H`, for an `H` satisfying (H1) and (H2). Then with conditional probability `≥ 1 − 1/(H+2)`, the
thinning `S_p` satisfies `f_{w,S_p} ≥ c/2` on `ℝ`. Unconditionally, `P(f_{w,S_p} ≥ c/2 on ℝ) ≥ p^{N_orb(H)}(1 − 1/(H+2)) > 0`.

*Proof.* Let `D_k` be the number of deleted zeros with `Re γ ∈ [k,k+1)`, for integer `k ≥ ⌊H⌋`. Each orbit contributes
at most 2 zeros to such an interval. Let `O_k ≤ ν(k)` be the number of orbits meeting it. Then `D_k ≤ 2·Bin(O_k, q)`.
Chernoff, `P(Bin(n,q) ≥ a) ≤ (enq/a)^a`, with `a = (ε₀/6)ν(k)` and `ν(k) ≥ 2log(k+4)`, gives
`P(D_k > (ε₀/3)ν(k)) ≤ (k+4)^{−(ε₀/3)log(ε₀/(6eq))} ≤ (k+4)^{−2}`.
The sum over `k ≥ ⌊H⌋` is `≤ 1/(H+2)`.

On the complement, `n_δ(x) ≤ D_{⌊x⌋} + D_{⌊x⌋+1} ≤ (ε₀/3)(ν(x) + 2ν(x)) = ε₀ν(x)` for `x ≥ H`, and symmetrically for
`x ≤ −H`. So `S_p` is an `(H, ε₀)`-modification, and Theorem H applies. The protection event concerns only the orbits
with `|Re γ| ≤ H`, and the density event only the others. They are independent, and the first has probability
`p^{N_orb(H)} > 0`. ∎

**Honest size of the constants.** For the probe weight, `ε₀ = 6.3·10⁻³`, `q₀ ≈ 10^{−415}` and `H_* ≳ 10^{35}`. The
diagnostic of §2 shows survival at `q = 10⁻³` with protection below 200. The proof is qualitatively right and
quantitatively crude.

### 3.6 Proposition S (a certificate is a sampling inequality) — PROVED
Let `S ⊂ ℝ` be a symmetric multiset with `n_S ≤ Cν`, and let `w ∈ 𝒲_T ∩ C_c^k` satisfy `f_{w,S} ≥ c` on `ℝ`. Then for
every `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, `Σ_{γ∈S}|F̂(γ)|² ≥ c‖F‖²₂`.
In particular:
* **(i)** if `S` is finite, then `c ≤ 0`. This is a second proof of the `c > 0` case of `thm:finZ`, by linear algebra:
  there is `F ≠ 0` in `C_c^∞(I_T)` with `F̂ = 0` on the finitely many points of `S`;
* **(ii)** more generally, `c > 0` forces `S` to be a uniqueness set for `PW_T`.

*Proof.* Tonelli, justified by Lemma 2 on `ℝ`, gives
`(2π)^{−1}∫ f_{w,S}|F̂|² = Σ_γ (2π)^{−1}∫ ŵ(τ−γ)|F̂(τ)|²dτ`.
By Parseval, with `ŵ(·−γ)` the transform of `w(u)e^{−iγu}` and `|F̂|²` that of the autocorrelation `c_F`,
`(2π)^{−1}∫ ŵ(τ−γ)|F̂(τ)|²dτ = ∫ w(u)e^{−iγu}\overline{c_F(u)}du = |F̂(γ)|²`,
because `w ≡ 1` on `supp c_F ⊂ [−2T, 2T]`. The inequality follows. For (i), finitely many linear conditions on an
infinite-dimensional space have a common nonzero solution. ∎

*Reading.* For real sets, a certificate is a quantitative sampling (frame) inequality in the Paley–Wiener space `PW_T`.
The zero side does have a genuine "for all `T`" mechanism: Beurling's sampling theorem (CITED: Beurling, *Collected
Works* II; Seip's survey) makes any real set of infinite lower Beurling density a sampling set for `PW_T` at every `T`.
The zero set has infinite density, and its gaps tend to 0. But the mechanism needs **reality**: for complex `γ`, the
zero sum is `Σ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`, not `Σ|F̂(γ)|²`. So the zero-side for-all-`T` mechanism is "density ∧ reality", and
reality is RH. This reading is on record in R153_012 §3. What is new here is only the one-line implication certificate
⟹ sampling, and the second proof of the `c > 0` part of `finZ`.

### 3.7 Proposition R (zero-side resolution; the analogue of O3) — PROVED, elementary
Let `f_w ≥ c` with `inf f_w = f_w(τ*)`, which is attained because `f_w → ∞` by Lemma 3.
* Deleting any sub-multiset `D` of orbits with `Σ_{γ∈D} ŵ(τ* − γ) > f_w(τ*)` makes the certificate negative at `τ*`.
* Moving one real zero `γ₀` by `η` changes `f_w(τ*)` by `η ŵ′(τ*−γ₀) + O(η²‖ŵ″‖_∞)`.

Since `f_w(τ*) ≤ λ^W_T ≤ 4·10⁹ T e^{−15.449T}` (`thm:margin`, `T ≥ 1`), the certificate encodes the positions of the
zeros near `τ*` to precision `≍ e^{−15.4T}/|ŵ′|`. In the probe, `τ* = 2.86` lies in the origin gap, and every zero below
100 is rigid (§2). *Proof.* `f_{w,Z∖D}(τ*) = f_w(τ*) − Σ_D ŵ(τ*−γ)`; the second claim is Taylor's formula. ∎

## §4 The candidate law, and its honest status

### 4.1 The law the probe points to
Combining §2 and §3, the structure that turns a failing finite universe into a certificate is:
* **(a) above the horizon `H_*(w)`:** only the growth of the density (Lemma 1 upper bound + Lemma 3), robust even to
  random thinning (Theorem H, Corollary H1). On the prime side this is `prop:env`: above `2πe^{A_T}`, `m(τ)` beats the
  comb. Both statements say that the high part of every window is already done.
* **(b) below the horizon:** the exact configuration of every zero, to precision `≍ λ^W_T` (Proposition R). The only
  law that produces it from the primes is the explicit formula. By `thm:Z(i)`, the `ŵ`-smoothed zero configuration at
  **every** height is the elementary function `eq:fw` of the finitely many `Λ(n)`, `n < e^U`, and the Γ-data.

The "sequence equation" sought, a finite rule that gives the smoothed configuration of all zeros
without listing them, is therefore **`eq:fw` itself**. It is classical (Riemann 1859 for the counting function, Guinand
and Weil for smooth tests), and R155_021 already observed it as spikes at `log n`.

**Candidate (L_seq).** There exist `T_k ↑ ∞` and `w_k ∈ 𝒲_{T_k}` whose elementary functions `eq:fw`, built from `Λ(n)`
for `n < e^{U_k}`, are `≥ 0` on `ℝ`.
**Status: EQUIVALENT to RH.** It is `cor:RH(c)` combined with the monotonicity of `λ^W` in `T`. It adds nothing: the
probe located the difficulty but did not create a new law.

### 4.2 The horizon picture of "for all T" (reading; the second half is HEURISTIC)
* A certificate at window `T` controls, as a zero-side statement, only the configuration below `H_*(w_T)`
  (Theorem H: above it, sparse off-line zeros are invisible).
* Conversely, every off-line zero is detected at some window. This is Weil's criterion; `cor:margin2(iii)` gives
  `λ^W_{T₀} < 0` for some `T₀` if RH fails.
* Hence uniformity in `T` is exactly the **exhaustion of all heights by horizons, with the configuration exact below
  each one**.
* `H_*(w_T) ≥ exp(2A_T) ≈ exp(8e^T)`, which is doubly exponential. That matches the prime-side band
  `[2πe^{2T}, 2πe^{A_T}]` of `prop:band` in order of magnitude (HEURISTIC identification: horizon ≈ band end).

Checking every window one at a time is therefore checking ever larger initial segments of the zeros at ever finer
precision. No finite data decide it (`thm:finZ`, `prop:uniform(b)`), and no recursion carries it from `T` to `T′`
(`cor:mono`, W15).

## §5 What is genuinely new, and the weakest step

**New, PROVED.**
1. Theorem H: every flat-top certificate has an explicit horizon `H_*(w) ≥ 2exp(2B_w) ≥ 2exp(2A_T)`, above which it is
  blind to any `ε₀`-sparse modification of the zero set, off-line moves included.
2. Corollary H1: a random thinning of the zeros keeps the certificate with positive probability, provided the orbits
  below the horizon are kept and `q ≤ q₀(w)`. This is the infinite-set complement of `thm:finZ`: finite sets never
  carry a certificate, while infinite thinned sets can, but never without the complete initial segment.
3. Proposition S: certificate ⟹ lower sampling inequality in `PW_T`, and a second proof of the `c > 0` part of `finZ`.
4. Proposition R: zero-side resolution at the minimiser (the O3 twin).

**New, DIAGNOSTIC.**
* The random-universe probe (random finite sets fail on about 40 % of the line; successive sets fail only above their
  top).
* The rigidity table (100 % of zeros rigid below height 100, 0.8 % in `[800,1950]` for the `T = 0.2` weight).
* Thinning survival at `q = 10⁻³` with protection below 200.

**Not new, and not claimed.** That the "sequence equation" is the explicit formula. That the zero-side mechanism is
density ∧ reality (R153_012). That (L_seq) is RH (`cor:RH`).

**Relation to G2_B2_R1's OPEN expectation.** B2 expects failure for every fixed `p < 1` at large windows. Corollary H1
proves survival at each fixed window for `p ≥ 1 − q₀(w_T)`, with `q₀ ≤ e^{−24G₀}` and `G₀ ≥ ‖ŵ‖₁ ≥ 2π`. The two
statements do not conflict. Whether `G₀(w_T)` must grow with `T`, which would make H1 degenerate as `T → ∞`, is not
proved. B2's statement remains OPEN.

**Weakest step.**
* (i) The horizon picture in §4.2, which identifies `H_*` with the prime-side band end `2πe^{A_T}`, is HEURISTIC.
  Theorem H gives only the lower bound `H_* ≥ 2e^{2A_T}` for the horizon *of the proof*. It gives no lower bound for
  the *true* height below which zeros are rigid, which the probe measures only at `T = 0.2`.
* (ii) The probe weight is not certified on all of `ℝ` (grid to 200 on the prime side, to 2000 on the zero side, and
  `log τ > B_w = 39.6` beyond that), so the §2 numbers are illustrations only.
* (iii) Lemma 1 rests on the import (X-N) (Rosser 1941), as already imported in P47. The half-open-endpoint bookkeeping
  for negative `x` is absorbed into the constant 4 of `ν` and should be rechecked by the panel.

Nothing here moves the sign. Row D keeps no label, and the statement of record in `ROW-D-REDUCED-STATEMENT` §1 stands.
