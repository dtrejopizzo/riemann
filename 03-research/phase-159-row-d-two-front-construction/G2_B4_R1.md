## §2 Translation-covariant SOS: the classification (Theorem S1)

### 2.1 Setting

`W(F) := W_T(F)` for any `T` with `supp F ⊂ I_T` is well defined on `C_c^∞(ℝ)`: contacts `log n ≥ 2T` do not meet
`supp c_F`. It is translation invariant, `W(F(·−a)) = W(F)`, because it depends only on `c_F`. Hence `W(F) = ⟨g, c_F⟩`
with one even distribution `g` on `ℝ` (the datum eq:gdatum without window restriction). The global primitive space
`P := {F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ): ∫F e^{t/2} = ∫F e^{−t/2} = 0}` is translation invariant too, since translation by `a` multiplies
the two moments by `e^{∓a/2}`. On `P`, `W = 𝔞` (the polar term vanishes).

**Facts used.** (W) Weil's criterion: `W ≥ 0` on `C_c^∞(ℝ)` ⟹ RH (CITED, Weil 1952; P46 §1). (EF) The explicit
formula: for real `F`, `W(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`. Under RH it gives `W(F) = Σ_γ m_γ|F̂(γ)|²` for every complex
`F ∈ C_c^∞`, with the cross terms cancelling over `±γ` (CITED, Weil 1952 / P45 Thm 9.4, as in P46 eq:W). Write
`μ_Z := Σ_γ m_γ δ_γ`, so that `W(F) = ∫|F̂|² dμ_Z` under RH.

### 2.2 Statements

**Definition S1.1.** A *covariant SOS realization* of `W` (resp. of `𝔞` on `P`) is a triple `(H, U, J)`. Here `H` is a
Hilbert space, `U` a strongly continuous unitary representation of `ℝ` on `H`, and `J` a linear map from `C_c^∞(ℝ)`
(resp. `P`) to `H`, with `J(F(·−a)) = U(a)JF` and `W(F) = ‖JF‖²`. A *convolution-square certificate* is the special
case `H = ⊕_k L²(ℝ)`, `U` = translation, `JF = (ℓ_k ⋆ F)_k`, for distributions `ℓ_k` with `ℓ_k⋆F ∈ L²` for all `F`.
Every "Dirichlet form" or "Lévy energy" built from prime translates `τ_{log n}` and Γ-kernels, such as
`½Σ_n v_n‖F−τ_{log n}F‖²` or `½∫‖F−τ_uF‖²K(u)du`, is of this kind.

**Theorem S1.** [compl] and [prim]. **PROVED.**
(a) If `W` (resp. `𝔞|_P`) has a covariant SOS realization, then RH holds. The spectral measure of `U` on the vector
`JF` is `|F̂|²μ_Z`, and on the closed span of `J(C_c^∞)` (resp. `J(P)`) the representation `U` is unitarily equivalent
to `⊕_γ (a ↦ e^{iγa})` on `ℓ²` over the distinct ordinates. So the realization *is* the zero-side SOS: `JF` corresponds
to `(√m_γ F̂(γ))_γ`.
(b) **No convolution-square certificate exists**, unconditionally. More generally, no covariant SOS realization exists
in which `U` has absolutely continuous spectrum.
(c) If a positive Borel measure `σ` satisfies `W(F) = ∫|F̂|²dσ` for all `F ∈ C_c^∞` (resp. all `F ∈ P`), then
`σ = μ_Z`.

*Proof.* (c) first. The identity gives `W ≥ 0` on `C_c^∞` (resp. on `P`). By (W), or by Bombieri/P45 Thm 10.4 for
the primitive space, RH holds. So `∫|F̂|²dσ = ∫|F̂|²dμ_Z` on the relevant space.

*Temperedness of `σ`.* Fix a real `χ ≥ 0`, `χ ≢ 0`, with `supp χ ⊂ (−1,1)`. For `|ξ| ≤ 1`,
`Re χ̂(ξ) = ∫χ(t)cos(ξt)dt ≥ cos(1)∫χ > 0`, so `|χ̂| ≥ c_χ > 0` on `[−1,1]`. Put `F_a(t) = χ(t)e^{−iat}`. Then
`F̂_a(ξ) = χ̂(ξ−a)` and `c_{F_a}(u) = e^{−iau}c_χ(u)`, supported in `[−2,2]`. The distribution `g` has finite order `N`
on `[−2,2]`, so `|W(F_a)| = |⟨g, e^{−iau}c_χ⟩| ≤ C(1+|a|)^N`. Hence `σ([a−1,a+1]) ≤ c_χ^{−2}C(1+|a|)^N`, and `σ` is
tempered. The same argument, or Riemann–von Mangoldt, applies to `μ_Z`. In the primitive case replace `χ` by a
compactly supported `χ_a ∈ P` with `|χ̂_a| ≥ c > 0` on `[a−1,a+1]` and derivatives polynomially bounded in `a`. Such
a `χ_a` exists: take `χ e^{−iat}` minus a combination of two fixed bumps supported in `(3,5)` and `(6,8)`, with
coefficients chosen to kill the two moments. The coefficients are `O(1)` in `a`, and the correction changes
`χ̂_a` on `[a−1,a+1]` by `O(|a|^{−M})` after integration by parts. For large `|a|` the lower bound survives.

*Uniqueness.* Let `ν = σ − μ_Z`, a real tempered measure. By polarization, `∫F̂\bar{Ĝ}dν = 0` for all `F, G` in the
space. Fix `G`. The tempered measure `\bar{Ĝ}ν` annihilates every `F̂`, so its Fourier transform, a tempered
distribution, vanishes on `C_c^∞` (resp. on `P`, of codimension 2, where it equals `αe^{t/2}+βe^{−t/2}`, which is
tempered only if `α = β = 0`). Hence `\bar{Ĝ}ν = 0` as a measure. For each `ξ_0 ∈ ℝ` some `G` in the space has
`Ĝ(ξ_0) ≠ 0`. In the primitive case this holds because `F ↦ F̂(ξ_0) = ∫F e^{iξ_0t}` is not in the span of the two
moment functionals. So `ν = 0` near every point, and `σ = μ_Z`.

(a) The spectral measure `ρ_F` of `JF` satisfies `∫e^{iaξ}dρ_F(ξ) = ⟨U(a)JF, JF⟩ = ⟨J(F(·−a)), JF⟩`, and this equals
the sesquilinear Weil form `W(F(·−a), F)`. By RH and (EF), that is `∫e^{iaξ}|F̂(ξ)|²dμ_Z` (sign conventions
absorbed). Fourier uniqueness for finite measures gives `ρ_F = |F̂|²μ_Z`, which is pure point. Cyclic subspaces with
pure-point spectral measures span a pure-point subspace. For each ordinate `γ`, let `E_γ` be the spectral projection.
Then `‖E_γJF‖² = m_γ|F̂(γ)|²`, so `E_γJ` vanishes on `ker(F ↦ F̂(γ))` and `E_γJF = F̂(γ)e_γ` with `‖e_γ‖² = m_γ`.

(b) Let `ℓ_k⋆F ∈ L²` for all `F ∈ C_c^∞`. By the closed-graph theorem, `ℓ_k ∈ 𝒟'_{L²}` (Schwartz), so `ℓ_k` is
tempered, and `(ℓ_k⋆F)^ = ℓ̂_kF̂ ∈ L²`. Given a compact `K`, choose `F_1 = χ` and `F_2 = χe^{−iat}`, with `a`
generic so that the zero sets of `F̂_1, F̂_2` on `K` are disjoint; they are finite, since the transforms are entire.
Dividing gives `ℓ̂_k ∈ L²_loc`. Then `W(F) = ∫|F̂|² h dξ/2π` with `h = Σ_k|ℓ̂_k|²`, and `h ∈ L¹_loc` by testing with
`|F̂_1|²+|F̂_2|² ≥ c_K > 0` on `K`. By (c), `h dξ/2π = μ_Z`. The left side is absolutely continuous; the right side is
nonzero (`γ_1 ≈ 14.13`) and purely atomic. Contradiction. For general `U` with a.c. spectrum, the spectral measures
`ρ_F` are a.c., but `ρ_F = |F̂|²μ_Z` by (a). Contradiction. ∎

### 2.3 What S1 says about the natural prime-side SOS

By eq:Aform and `‖F‖² − Re c_F(u) = ½‖F − τ_uF‖²`, every `F ∈ L²(I_T)` satisfies, exactly,

  `𝔞_T(F) = (C_0 − A_T)‖F‖² + ½∫_0^∞‖F − τ_uF‖²K(u)du + ½Σ_{n∈𝒩_T} v_n‖F − τ_{log n}F‖²` (PROVED, rewriting thm:A)

This is a sum of convolution squares (a Dirichlet form of the positive Lévy measure `K du + Σ v_nδ_{log n}`) plus
the **negative** constant `C_0 − A_T`, where `C_0 = ψ(¼) − log π ≈ −5.37` and `A_T ≍ 4e^T`. Positivity therefore
means a Poincaré inequality for this Lévy generator on `P_T`, with constant `A_T − C_0`. S1(b) says the deficit
cannot be absorbed into more convolution squares globally: the only covariant completion is the atomic `μ_Z`. Every
prime-built square has a.c. spectrum, and the target has none. This is the SOS form of `thm:finZ` / `lem:Rrig`:
positivity is not a sum of arithmetic squares. It is a spectral identity at the zeros.

**Remark S1.2 (the "100 random zeros").** S1(c) applies to every subset of zeros as well. If `Z' ⊊ Z`, the
measure `μ_{Z'}` represents a different distribution. Removing even one ordinate changes `g` by
`2m_γcos(γu)` on all of `ℝ`. So no finite or co-finite sub-universe of zeros realizes the global form. This is the
global counterpart of `thm:finZ`, and it is elementary. PROVED.

## §3 Windowed SOS families = KEP solutions (Theorem S2)

**Definition S2.1.** A *windowed convolution-square certificate of value `c` at `T`* is a family `(ℓ_k)` of
distributions with `W_T(F) − c‖F‖² = Σ_k‖ℓ_k⋆F‖²` for all `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`.

**Theorem S2.** [compl]. **PROVED** (from P46 thm:D1 (complete version, Remark after it), thm:Z, thm:KEP1–2, and the
arguments of S1).
(a) A windowed certificate of value `c` exists ⟺ there is a positive tempered a.c. measure `σ = h dξ/2π` with
`σ̌ = g − cδ` on `(−2T, 2T)`, i.e. an a.c. solution of `KEP(T)` for the datum `g − cδ`.
(b) If a certificate of value `c` exists then `λ^W_T ≥ c`. If `λ^W_T > c`, one exists: take `h = f_w − c` with `w`
flat-top and `inf f_w > c`, which is possible by strong duality and thm:Z. So windowed SOS certificates are exactly
Paper 46's flat-top certificates, and `∀T` existence with `c = 0` is RH (EQUIV, E3/KEP3).
(c) **Limit rigidity.** Let `T_j → ∞` and let `h_j` be windowed certificates of values `c_j ≥ 0`. Then `c_j → 0`, and
`(h_j + c_j) dξ/2π → μ_Z` vaguely. In particular `lim_{T→∞} λ^W_T ≤ 0` unconditionally. This is the qualitative part
of `thm:margin` for the complete form, obtained with no zero computation and no test family.

*Proof.* (a) As in S1(b), the certificate gives `h = Σ|ℓ̂_k|² ∈ L¹_loc`, and `∫h|F̂|²/2π = ⟨g − cδ, c_F⟩` for every
`F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, where `supp c_F ⋐ (−2T,2T)`. The span of such `c_F` is dense, in the `𝒮`-topology, in the test
functions supported in `(−2T,2T)` (mollification). So `σ̌ = g − cδ` there. Conversely, `ℓ̂ = √h` works. (b) One
direction is immediate. For the other, `f_w − c ≥ 0` is continuous, `O(log|τ|)` (P46 lem:mbounds, thm:Dtail), and
`(f_w)^∨ = g` on the window (thm:KEP1(i)). (c) Fix `χ` as in S1. For `2T_j > 2`, the bound
`σ_j([a−1,a+1]) ≤ c_χ^{−2}C(1+|a|)^N` holds with `σ_j = (h_j+c_j)dξ/2π`, uniformly in `j`, because `W(F_a)` does not
depend on the window once `supp c_{F_a} ⊂ I_{2T_j}`. So `(σ_j)` is vaguely precompact, and `c_j ≤ W(F_0)/‖F_0‖²`
is bounded. Along a subsequence, `σ_j → σ` and `c_j → c ≥ 0`. For fixed `φ = c_F`, `F ∈ C_c^∞`,
`∫φ̂ dσ_j = ⟨g, φ⟩` as soon as `supp φ ⊂ (−2T_j,2T_j)`, and passing to the limit (the tails are uniformly tempered)
gives `∫|F̂|²dσ = W(F)`. By S1(c), `σ = μ_Z`, and RH holds. But `σ_j ≥ c_j dξ/2π`, so `σ ≥ c dξ/2π` and `c = 0`,
because `μ_Z` has no a.c. part. For the corollary:
if `lim λ^W_T = c > 0` (the limit exists by monotonicity (M), R155_008), apply (b) with value `c/2` at every `T`. ∎

**Remark S2.2 (what (c) means for an "algebraic identity for all `T`").** Any `T`-indexed SOS family is a family of
nonnegative functions `h_T` on the spectral line. They are pinned on `(−2T,2T)` by the prime data, and they are
*forced to converge to the zero measure*. An explicit prime-side formula for `h_T` valid for all `T` would be an
explicit prime-side approximation of `Σ_γδ_γ` by nonnegative functions. That is the explicit formula smoothed by
flat-top weights (thm:Z, thm:Zexh), whose nonnegativity is cancellation across zeros (cor:nontermwise). The SOS
language adds no new degree of freedom: the square root `√h_T` is free, and all the content is `h_T ≥ 0`. HEURISTIC
reading of a PROVED statement.

**Remark S2.3 (primitive form).** For [prim] the windowed Toeplitz SOS certificates are the dual points
`(ν, β)` of P46 def:PD (`f = σ_T + ν̂ + β·ĝ ≥ c`, `f = |√f|²`). They are the same object, never attained (`thm:O`).
The limit statement (c) for [prim] is OPEN: the multipliers `β·ĝ` are supported inside the window and do not
stabilise.

**Remark S2.4 (nested / causal SOS).** Among all SOS decompositions of `W_T`, one is canonical and nested in `T`: the
causal (Cholesky, Gohberg–Kreĭn triangular) factorisation with respect to the chain `L²(−T, −T+s)`, `s ≤ 2T`. This
is the continuous analogue of Szegő/Verblunsky recursion (Kreĭn systems), and it is the only kind of SOS that is
consistent across windows by an algebraic recursion. Its existence for all `T` is equivalent to the non-breakdown of
the recursion driven by `g`, hence to `λ^W_T > 0` for all `T` (up to the boundary case). This is the canonical-system
/ screw-function frame (R157_002 rows 38–39, EQUIV). The symbol here grows like `log|τ|` and `g` has atoms, so the
classical Gohberg–Kreĭn theorem (for `I` plus a trace-class operator) does not apply as stated; existence of the
factorisation for this unbounded symbol, even at one window, is OPEN. Recorded, not pursued: EQUIV.

## §4 Positivstellensatz in the exact weights (Theorem S3)

This section addresses the angle literally: coefficients built from the exact values of Λ(n) by an algebraic
identity.

### 4.1 Definitions

Fix `T`. Let `ℚ̄ ⊂ ℂ` be the algebraic numbers, let `V = (V_n)_{n∈𝒩_T}` be indeterminates, and let
`R_T = ℚ̄[V]`. Let `v^Λ = v_T(Λ) ∈ ℝ^{𝒩_T}` be the exact weight vector and
`I_T := {Q ∈ R_T : Q(v^Λ) = 0}`, the ideal of algebraic relations satisfied by the exact weights. Let
`I_T^{lin} ⊆ I_T` be the ideal generated by the polynomials of degree `≤ 1` in `I_T`. Let
`L_T := {v ∈ ℝ^{𝒩_T} : v_{p^k} = p^{−(k−1)/2} v_p for all p^k ∈ 𝒩_T}` (the prime-power relations). Let
`D_T := P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`.

**Definition S3.1.** A *`ℚ̄`-algebraic Positivstellensatz certificate at `T`* [prim] consists of three things:
* linear functionals `ℓ_{k,α}: D_T → ℂ`, indexed by `k ∈ ℕ` and multi-indices `α`, that do **not** depend on `V`;
* for each `F`, only finitely many nonzero `ℓ_{k,α}(F)`;
* a `ℚ̄`-subideal `I ⊆ I_T`.

It must satisfy, for each `F ∈ D_T`, that the real polynomial
  `P_F(V) := 𝔞_T^{(V)}(F) − Σ_k |Σ_α V^α ℓ_{k,α}(F)|²` (real `V`)
lies in the extension ideal `I·ℂ[V]`. Here `𝔞^{(V)}_T(F) = C_0‖F‖² + Lev(c_F) − Σ_n V_n Re c_F(log n)` is affine in
`V` (P46 prop:finite(ii)). The same definition with `W_T` and `C_c^∞(I_T)` gives [compl].

This is exactly "an SOS identity whose coefficients are built from the weights by algebra over `ℚ̄`, using only
relations the true weights satisfy". If `ℓ_{k,α}` were allowed to depend on the numbers `v^Λ_n` themselves,
*every* positive form would qualify (take `ℓ = W_T^{1/2}`), and the notion would be empty. Its content is that the
exact values enter only through `V` and the ideal.

### 4.2 Statements

**Lemma S3.0 (specialisation).** PROVED. If a certificate with ideal `I` exists, then `𝔞^{(v)}_T ≥ 0` on `D_T` for
every real point `v` of the variety `V_ℝ(I) := {v ∈ ℝ^{𝒩_T}: Q(v) = 0 ∀Q ∈ I}`.
*Proof.* Write `P_F = Σ_j q_jQ_j` with `Q_j ∈ I` and `q_j ∈ ℂ[V]`. At `v ∈ V_ℝ(I)`, `P_F(v) = 0`, so
`𝔞^{(v)}_T(F) = Σ_k|Σ_α v^αℓ_{k,α}(F)|² ≥ 0`. ∎

**Lemma S3.1 (a failing point on `L_T`).** PROVED. For every `T > ½log2` there is `v ∈ L_T` with `𝔞^{(v)}_T` not
`≥ 0` on `D_T`, and hence `W^{(v)}_T` not `≥ 0` either.
*Proof.* Let `v^θ` be `v^Λ` with `v_{2^k}` replaced by `(1+θ)v^Λ_{2^k}` for all `2^k ∈ 𝒩_T`. Then `v^θ ∈ L_T`. Take
`u_0 = log2` and `J = (−T,−T+ℓ)` with `ℓ < min(log2, 2T−log2)`, and also `ℓ` smaller than the distance from `log2`
to `{log n : n ∈ 𝒩_T, n ≠ 2}` (so no other contact lies within `ℓ` of `log2`, and `ℓ < log2` rules out `|log n| < ℓ`). Build `F^+_φ = φ + φ(·−log2)` as in P46 lem:cpos. Then
`c_F` is supported in `[−ℓ,ℓ] ∪ ±[log2−ℓ, log2+ℓ]`, so `c_F(log n) = 0` for `n ≠ 2`, and `c_F(log2) = ‖φ‖²`. On the
nonzero elements of `{F^+_φ} ∩ P_T`, which exist by the codimension-2 argument of lem:cpos,
`𝔞^{(v^θ)}_T(F) = 𝔞_T(F) − θv^Λ_2‖φ‖²`, which is `< 0` for large `θ`. For `W`, the same `F ∈ P_T` gives the same
value. ∎

**Theorem S3.** [prim] and [compl].
(a) **(Linear relations; PROVED, using Baker's theorem, CITED.)** For every `T > ½log2`, `I_T^{lin}` is generated by
the prime-power relations `V_{p^k} − p^{−(k−1)/2}V_p`, and `V_ℝ(I^{lin}_T) = L_T`. Hence **no certificate with
`I ⊆ I_T^{lin}` exists at any `T > ½log2`.**
(b) **(All relations, first windows; PROVED, using Hermite–Lindemann, CITED.)** For `T ∈ (½log2, ½log3]`,
`𝒩_T = {2}` and `I_T = 0`. **No `ℚ̄`-algebraic Positivstellensatz certificate of any degree exists there**, although
`λ^A_T > 0` for these `T` (Paper 42). The proved sign at these windows is necessarily a proof by *inequality* on the
real number `√2 log2`, not by identity.
(c) **(All relations, all windows; conditional.)** If `{log p : p < e^{2T}}` is algebraically independent over `ℚ̄`
(a consequence of Schanuel's conjecture; OPEN in general, already for `{log2, log3}`), then `I_T = I_T^{lin}` and no
`ℚ̄`-algebraic certificate exists at `T`. Under Schanuel this holds for every `T > ½log2`.

*Proof.* (a) Let `Q = c_0 + Σ_n c_nV_n ∈ I_T`, `c ∈ ℚ̄`. Then
`c_0 + Σ_p β_p log p = 0` with `β_p := Σ_{k: p^k∈𝒩_T} 2c_{p^k}p^{−k/2} ∈ ℚ̄`. The numbers `log p` are linearly
independent over `ℚ`, by unique factorisation. By Baker's theorem, `1, log p_1, …, log p_r` are then linearly
independent over `ℚ̄` (A. Baker, *Linear forms in the logarithms of algebraic numbers I*, Mathematika 13 (1966);
Baker, *Transcendental Number Theory*, Thm 2.1; CITED). So `c_0 = 0` and `β_p = 0` for every `p`. The solutions of
`Σ_k c_{p^k}p^{−k/2} = 0` are spanned, prime by prime, by the coefficient vectors of `V_{p^k} − p^{−(k−1)/2}V_p`,
which satisfy `p^{−k/2} − p^{−(k−1)/2}p^{−1/2} = 0`. These relations have real coefficients, so
`V_ℝ(I^{lin}_T) = L_T`. Lemmas S3.0 and S3.1 finish the proof. (b) `𝒩_T = {n: n < e^{2T} ≤ 3} = {2}`, and
`v^Λ_2 = √2 log2`. `log2` is transcendental (Hermite–Lindemann, CITED), hence so is `√2 log2`. A nonzero
`Q ∈ ℚ̄[V_2]` therefore does not vanish at it: `I_T = 0` and `V_ℝ(I_T) = ℝ`, which contains the failing `v^θ`. (c) Suppose
`Q ∈ I_T`. Substituting the prime-power relations reduces `Q` to a polynomial in the `V_p`, i.e. in the `log p` with
`ℚ̄` coefficients, vanishing at `(log p)_p`. Algebraic independence makes it identically zero, so `Q ∈ I_T^{lin}`.
Then (a) applies. Schanuel applied to `x_i = log p_i`, which are `ℚ`-linearly independent, gives
`trdeg ℚ(log p_i, p_i) ≥ r`, i.e. the algebraic independence. ∎

**Corollary S3.3 (Positivstellensatz with inequalities: the radius).** PROVED, [prim]. Suppose a certificate uses
`I ⊆ I_T^{lin}` together with `ℚ̄`-polynomial constraints `g_i(V) ≥ 0` satisfied by `v^Λ`. That is,
`P_F ∈ Σ_i Σ_k|…|²·g_i + I·ℂ[V]`, a Schmüdgen/Putinar-type representation. Then it proves positivity on
`S = L_T ∩ {g_i ≥ 0}`. `S` contains `v^Λ` and must avoid every failing point. By P46 lem:degen, with a real unit
minimiser `F_⋆`, `V_T(v) ≤ λ^A_T − ⟨v − v^Λ, c_{F⋆}(log·)⟩`. So `S` contains no `L_T`-ball around `v^Λ` of radius
`r > λ^A_T/‖Π_{L_T}(c_{F⋆}(log n))_n‖`, the norm being Euclidean after identifying `L_T ≅ ℝ^{π(e^{2T})}` by the free
coordinates `V_p` with the pulled-back vector `(Σ_k p^{−(k−1)/2}c_{F⋆}(k log p))_p`. With `thm:margin`, for `T` past
its crossings, `r ≤ 4·10⁹Te^{−15.449T}/‖Π_{L_T}c_{F⋆}‖`. If `Π_{L_T}c_{F⋆} = 0` (the degenerate case of lem:degen
restricted to `L_T`, OPEN), no bound follows. *Proof.* The concavity inequality of lem:degen, restricted to `L_T`,
together with Lemma S3.0 applied on `S`. ∎

### 4.3 Reading of S3 (the transcendence dichotomy)

A certificate "with coefficients built from Λ(n) exactly" has only three ways to use the exact values:
1. **Algebraically over `ℚ̄`.** By S3 the exact values then carry no more information than the class `L_T`, which
  contains the failing comb `v^θ`. This is `cor:void` again, now with "exact values" and not merely class
  properties, because algebra over `ℚ̄` cannot pin the values. Excluded (PROVED for linear relations and for the
  first windows; conditional on Schanuel in general).
2. **By inequalities on the real numbers `log p`.** Admissible, but the precision required shrinks like
  `e^{−15.4T}` (Cor S3.3 + `thm:margin`, and O3 for single weights). A fixed-precision family is not uniform in `T`.
  This is the per-window decidability of `prop:decide`, not a mechanism.
3. **Transcendentally, through the link weight = position.** In the form, `log p` appears both as the weight
  `Λ(p^k) = log p` and as the frequency `k log p`, because `v_n c_F(log n) = (2/k)·[u e^{−u/2}c_F(u)]_{u=log n}` at
  `n = p^k`. An identity that uses this link is not an identity in `V` at all: it depends on the atom positions
  through `c_F`, which is analytic, not algebraic. The only known identity of this kind is `−ζ'/ζ = ΣΛ(n)n^{−s}`,
  i.e. the explicit formula, which brings back the zeros (S2, thm:Z). Whether some other identity of type 3 exists
  is **OPEN**. It is the only door the SOS angle leaves, and it lies in the territory of F4 (A3 + A7/A8/A9, and the
  additive structure of `ℤ` of P47 Rem 27.16a). HEURISTIC.

## §6 What is genuinely new

1. **Theorem S1** (PROVED): classification of translation-covariant SOS realizations of the complete and primitive
  Weil forms. Each one is the zero-side SOS, with pure-point spectrum at the ordinates. Convolution-square and
  Dirichlet-form certificates, the natural "arithmetic squares", are impossible unconditionally. The proof is
  elementary (temperedness, polarization, Fourier uniqueness). The novelty is the statement as a closure of row 51,
  not the technique.
2. **Theorem S2(c)** (PROVED): any `T`-family of windowed SOS certificates converges vaguely to `μ_Z`, and its values
  tend to `0`. This gives a soft, computation-free proof that `lim_T λ^W_T ≤ 0`, a qualitative companion to
  `thm:margin`.
3. **Theorem S3** (PROVED via Baker and Hermite–Lindemann; conditional on Schanuel in full): the first use of
  transcendence theory in row D. `ℚ̄`-algebraic Positivstellensatz certificates in the exact weights do not exist.
  In particular, the proved sign at `T ∈ (½log2, ½log3]` (Paper 42) *cannot* have an algebraic-identity proof. It
  is necessarily an inequality on `√2 log2`.
4. **Filter F1′** for group G2: an admissible mechanism must use Λ(n) through the weight–position link (analytically),
  not algebraically, and not through fixed-precision inequalities.

Nothing here gives a sign for any `T > 0.8`, in either form.

## §7 Weakest step

* **The definition behind S3 (Def. S3.1)** is the load-bearing choice. It requires the functionals `ℓ_{k,α}` to be
  independent of `V`, with all dependence on the exact values carried by the indeterminates and the relation ideal.
  A reader can call this tailored. The defence is in §4.1: without the requirement, every positive form
  qualifies trivially (`ℓ = W_T^{1/2}`), so the requirement *is* what "an algebraic identity in the Λ(n)" means. The
  theorem nevertheless excludes only this reading. Certificates that use `log p` through the positions (door 3) are
  outside it by design.
* **S3(a) rests on Baker's theorem** (CITED, not re-proved). S3(c) rests on Schanuel's conjecture, which is OPEN.
* **S1, primitive case, temperedness.** The construction of `χ_a ∈ P` with `|χ̂_a| ≥ c` on `[a−1,a+1]` and
  polynomially bounded constants is only sketched: the correction bumps are `O(|a|^{−M})` on that interval by
  integration by parts. It should be written out in full before integration.
* **S2(c)** passes to the limit using uniform local temperedness of `σ_j`. The bound is uniform because `W(F_a)` does
  not depend on the window, but the tail uniformity needed for `∫|F̂|²dσ_j → ∫|F̂|²dσ` (rather than only
  `∫φ̂ dσ_j` for compactly supported `φ̂`) uses the polynomial bound together with the rapid decay of `F̂`. That step
  is standard but written compactly.
