  with `δ_q > 0` and `γ_q > 0`, its multiplicity `μ_q`. Then

  `δ_q t`. Each on-line pair adds a positive form of rank 2.
2. **Theorem I (the exact index of the window flow). PROVED; primitive and complete.** Suppose ζ has finitely many
  zeros off the line, and let `m` be the number of *distinct* ones in the open first quadrant. Then

  and `N^A(T) = N^W(T) = 2m` for all `T ≥ T_*`. **This is Bombieri's theorem** (2000, Rend. Lincei, CITED: "the number
  of negative eigenvalues is one half of the number of zeros off the line, for large truncations"), transported to
  P47's windows. The proof here is independent. The upper bound comes from Lemma L. The lower bound comes from the
  `T`-free transport profile of my round-4 Lemma T, now matrix-valued. What is new is only the window form and the
  observation that multiplicity adds no negative squares.
3. **Theorem U₂ (upper half, sharpened). PROVED under (BD₂); complete ⟹ primitive.** Put
  `D₂ := sup_{y∈ℝ} Σ_{q : |γ_q − y| < 1} μ_q δ_q²`. Then for `T ≥ ½`:
  `−λ^W_T ≤ 64e · D₂ · T⁴ (β* + 1/(2T)) e^{2β*T}`.
  The hypothesis **(BD₂)** `D₂ < ∞` is strictly weaker than round 4's (BD). It allows unbounded local counts of
  off-line zeros, provided their squared displacements are locally summable.
4. **Theorem M (the termwise route is exactly (BD₂)). PROVED.** Let

  `𝒩_T < ∞ ⟺ D₂ < ∞`. This is the Carleson reading the panel asked for: the measure `Σ_q μ_q δ_q² δ_{γ_q}` must be a
  Paley–Wiener Carleson measure. The upper half of the growth law without (BD₂) is OPEN, and no termwise zero-side
  argument can reach it.

**Status: partial new result (Theorems U₂ and M; the window form of Bombieri's index theorem, with an independent

## 2. Lemma L: the Lorentzian zero identity

**Lemma L. PROVED (from (E)); complete form.** For real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`:
```
W_T(F) = Σ_{Re ρ = ½, Im ρ > 0} 2μ_ρ|F̂(γ_ρ)|² + Σ_q 4μ_q [ |(F·cosh δ_q t)^(γ_q)|² − |(F·sinh δ_q t)^(γ_q)|² ], (2.1)
```
where `q` runs over the distinct zeros `ρ_q = ½ + δ_q + iγ_q` with `δ_q > 0` and `γ_q > 0`. Both sums converge
absolutely. On real `F`, the `q`-th bracket has signature exactly `(2,2)`, and the on-line term has rank 2 and is
positive.

*Proof.* Take an on-line zero `ρ = ½ + iγ`. Then `Φ_F(ρ) = F̂(γ)` and `Φ_F(1−ρ) = F̂(−γ) = conj F̂(γ)`, since `F` is real.
The zeros `ρ` and `ρ̄` together contribute `2|F̂(γ)|²`.

Take an off-line quadruple with `ρ = ½ + δ + iγ`, `δ > 0`, `γ > 0`. Put `G_± := (F e^{±δt})^(γ)`. Then
`Φ_F(ρ) = G_+` and `Φ_F(1−ρ) = ∫F e^{−δt}e^{−iγt} = conj G_−`. Conjugating gives `Φ_F(ρ̄) = conj G_+` and
`Φ_F(1−ρ̄) = G_−`. The term for `1−ρ` equals the term for `ρ`, and the term for `1−ρ̄` equals the term for `ρ̄`. So the
quadruple contributes
`2(G_+ conj G_− + conj G_+ G_−) = 4Re(G_+ conj G_−) = |G_+ + G_−|² − |G_+ − G_−|²`,
and `G_+ ± G_−` are the transforms at `γ` of `2F cosh δt` and `2F sinh δt`.

*Signature.* On real `F`, the bracket is `ℓ_1(F)² + ℓ_2(F)² − ℓ_3(F)² − ℓ_4(F)²`, where `ℓ_1, ℓ_2` are
`⟨F, cosh(δt)cos γt⟩`, `⟨F, cosh(δt)sin γt⟩` and `ℓ_3, ℓ_4` are the same with sinh. The four functions are linearly
independent on `I_T`: they are combinations of the four distinct exponentials `e^{(±δ ± iγ)t}`, with an invertible
coefficient matrix. ∎

*Reading.* In the variables `(F cosh δt, F sinh δt)`, each off-line zero is a Lorentz boost of rapidity `δ_q t`. The
negativity of `W_T` is the "spatial" part `|(F sinh δ_q t)^|²`. It is small for small `δ_q T`, since
`‖F sinh δt‖ ≤ sinh(δT)‖F‖`, and it grows like `e^{2δT}` otherwise. Every result below reads it off (2.1).

## 3. Theorem I: the exact negative index of the window flow

**Theorem I. PROVED; primitive and complete.** Suppose ζ has only finitely many off-line zeros, and let `m` be the
number of distinct ones with `δ > 0`, `γ > 0`.
(a) For every `T`: `N^A(T) ≤ N^W(T) ≤ 2m`.
(b) There is `T_*` such that `N^A(T) = N^W(T) = 2m` for all `T ≥ T_*`.
(c) Hence `N(∞) := lim_T N^W(T) = 2m`, and `RH ⟺ N^W ≡ 0 ⟺ N^A ≡ 0`. **EQUIVALENT** (Weil).

*Proof of (a).* `N^A ≤ N^W`: on `P_T`, `W_T = 𝔞_T`, and restricting a form to a subspace cannot raise its negative index.
For `N^W ≤ 2m`, write (2.1) as `W_T = 𝒫 − Σ_q 4μ_q(ℓ_{q,3}² + ℓ_{q,4}²)` on real `C_c^∞(I_T)`, where
`𝒫 ≥ 0` collects all the `|·|²` terms with a plus sign. On the common kernel of the `2m` functionals `ℓ_{q,3}`,
`ℓ_{q,4}`, which has codimension at most `2m`, we have `W_T ≥ 0`. A negative-definite subspace meets that kernel only
in `0`, so its dimension is at most `2m`. Extend to the form domain by (D). ∎

*Proof of (b): the matrix transport.* This is round 4's Lemma T with matrix-valued profiles. Put `D := −∂² + ¼`.
Choose real profiles `h_1,…,h_J ∈ C_c^∞(−½,½)`, write `H_k(s) := ∫h_k(t)e^{−st}dt`, and put `s_q := ρ_q − ½ = δ_q + iγ_q`.

*Step 1 (profiles).* The `2m` real-linear functionals `h ↦ Re H(−s_q)`, `Im H(−s_q)` are linearly independent on real
`C_c^∞(−½,½)`, and so are `h ↦ Re H(s_q)`, `Im H(s_q)`. For the first family, suppose
`Σ(α_q Re H(−s_q) + β_q Im H(−s_q)) = 0` for all real `h`. For real `h`, `conj H(−s) = H(−s̄)`, so this reads
`½Σ[(α_q − iβ_q)H(−s_q) + (α_q + iβ_q)H(−s̄_q)] = 0`. By complex linearity it then holds for all complex `h`. The `2m`
points `−s_q, −s̄_q` are distinct (`γ_q > 0`), so the exponentials `e^{−s_q t}`, `e^{−s̄_q t}` are linearly independent
distributions on `(−½,½)`. Hence all coefficients vanish. The second family is the same with `s ↦ −s`. So there is a
finite `J` and profiles `h_1,…,h_J` such that the `J × 2m` real matrices
`𝐀 := [Re H_k(−s_q), Im H_k(−s_q)]_{k,q}` and `𝐁 := [Re H_k(s_q), Im H_k(s_q)]_{k,q}` both have full column rank
`2m`. Take a spanning set for each family and concatenate; the profiles `Dh_k` can be taken linearly independent.

*Step 2 (vectors and Gram matrix).* For `x ≥ 2` put `f_k := Dh_k(· − x/2)` and `g_k := Dh_k(· + x/2)`, and let
`T > x/2 + ½`. By round-4 Lemma T(a) (integration by parts, `De^{±t/2} = 0`), all `f_k, g_k` lie in `P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`,
and on their span `W_T = 𝔞_T`. The Gram matrix is
```
G(x) = [[ P , U(x) ], [ U(x)ᵀ , P ]], P_{jk} = 𝔞_1(Dh_j, Dh_k), U_{jk}(x) = W_T(f_j, g_k).
```
`P` does not depend on `x` or `T`, since `c_{f_j,f_k}` is supported in `(−1,1)` (Lemma T(a), polarised). `U(x)` does not
depend on `T` (Lemma T(b)).

*Step 3 (the spectral expansion of U).* By polarised (E),
`U_{jk}(x) = Σ_ρ μ_ρ Φ_{f_j}(ρ)Φ_{g_k}(1−ρ)`. With `s = ρ − ½`:
`Φ_{f_j}(ρ) = e^{sx/2}∫Dh_j(t)e^{st}dt = e^{sx/2}(¼ − s²)H_j(−s)` and `Φ_{g_k}(1−ρ) = e^{sx/2}(¼ − s²)H_k(s)`
(two integrations by parts). Hence
```
U_{jk}(x) = Σ_ρ μ_ρ (¼ − s_ρ²)² H_j(−s_ρ) H_k(s_ρ) e^{s_ρ x}, (3.1)
```
which converges absolutely, uniformly in `x ≥ 0` over the zeros with `Re s_ρ ≤ 0`. The reason is that `H_k(±s)` decays
faster than any power of `|Im s|` uniformly for `|Re s| ≤ ½`, and `|e^{sx}| ≤ 1` there. Split `U = U_+ + U_0`. Here
`U_0` collects the zeros with `Re s ≤ 0` and satisfies `‖U_0(x)‖ ≤ C_0` for all `x ≥ 0`. `U_+` collects the finitely
many zeros with `Re s > 0`, namely the `s_q` and their conjugates:
`U_+(x) = Σ_q 2Re(w_q(x) a_q b_qᵀ)`, with `a_q = (H_k(−s_q))_k`, `b_q = (H_k(s_q))_k`,
`w_q(x) = μ_q(¼ − s_q²)² e^{s_q x}`.

For complex vectors `a = a′ + ia″`, `b = b′ + ib″` and complex `w`,
`Re(w a bᵀ) = [a′ a″] R_w [b′ b″]ᵀ` with `R_w = [[Re w, −Im w],[−Im w, −Re w]]`. Its two singular values are both
`|w|`. Therefore `U_+(x) = 𝐀 · diag_q(2R_{w_q(x)}) · 𝐁ᵀ`, and
`s_{2m}(U_+(x)) ≥ 2 s_min(𝐀) s_min(𝐁) min_q|w_q(x)| ≥ c e^{δ_min x}` with `c > 0`. Here `(¼ − s_q²) ≠ 0` because
`|Re s_q| < ½`, and `δ_min := min_q δ_q > 0`. By Weyl's inequality for singular values,
`s_{2m}(U(x)) ≥ c e^{δ_min x} − C_0 → ∞`.

*Step 4 (negative index of the Gram matrix).* Let `U = Σ_i s_i u_i v_iᵀ` be a singular value decomposition, and suppose
`s_{2m} > ‖P‖`. For `c ∈ ℝ^{2m} \ {0}` put `F_c := Σ_{i≤2m} c_i(Σ_k u_{ik}f_k − Σ_k v_{ik}g_k)`. Then
```
W_T(F_c) = Σ_{i,l} c_ic_l(u_iᵀPu_l + v_iᵀPv_l) − 2Σ_i c_i² s_i ≤ 2(‖P‖ − s_{2m})|c|² < 0.
```
The `F_c` span a space of dimension `2m`. The `f`'s and `g`'s have disjoint supports, the `Dh_k` are independent, and
the `u_i` and `v_i` are orthonormal. So `N^A(T) ≥ 2m` as soon as `T > x/2 + ½` for one `x` with `s_{2m}(U(x)) > ‖P‖`.
Together with (a) and (Mono), this gives (b). (c) follows: `m = 0` is RH, given finiteness. Without finiteness,
`N^W ≡ 0` is Weil's criterion. ∎

**Remarks.**
- *(I-a) Relation to Bombieri 2000 (CITED).* The count matches his: the number of off-line zeros is `4m` for simple
  zeros, and half of that is `2m`. Theorem I is the window-truncation version, with a self-contained proof. The upper
  bound needs no truncation parameter. The lower bound gives `T_*` explicitly as the first `x/2 + ½` with
  `s_{2m}(U(x)) > ‖P‖`.
- *(I-b) Primitive = complete at the top.* Eventually `N^A = N^W`. So in the finite-exception scenario, the 2×2 polar
  capacity `𝔊_T` of `thm:parityschur` carries no negative square for large `T`: `In(𝔊_T)` has `n₋ = 0` for `T ≥ T_*`.
  This is PROVED, conditional only on the finite-exception hypothesis.
- *(I-c) Multiplicity adds no negative squares.* In (E), a zero of multiplicity `μ` enters as `μ·Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`, with
  no derivative terms. Its negative part is `μ` times the same rank-2 form, so its rank is still 2. The count is
  therefore of *distinct* zeros. Formally this departs from a "number of zeros" count with multiplicity. I flag it as
  a point to reconcile with Bombieri's full text, which I have not read. It is not a claim that his statement is wrong.
- *(I-d) Infinitely many off-line zeros.* Then (a) is vacuous, and the lower bound of (b) needs `U_0` to be bounded,
  which fails. That `sup_T N^W(T) = ∞` in this case is the expected Kreĭn–Langer-type statement. It is **OPEN here**.

## 4. The upper half as a Carleson condition: Theorems U₂ and M

**Theorem U₂. PROVED under (BD₂); complete form (hence primitive, `λ^A ≥ λ^W`).** Let
`D₂ := sup_{y∈ℝ} Σ_{q:|γ_q−y|<1} μ_q δ_q²`, and assume `D₂ < ∞`. Then for every `T ≥ ½`,
```
−λ^A_T ≤ −λ^W_T ≤ 64e · D₂ · T⁴ · (β* + 1/(2T)) · e^{2β*T}.
```
Consequently, under (BD₂), `κ^A = κ^W = Θ − ½`: the upper half is from here, and the lower half is round-4 G1
(= G2_B3_R4 Thm Q, G2_B1_R4 Thm Q).

*Proof.* By (D), take real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` with `‖F‖ = 1`. By (2.1),
`−W_T(F) ≤ Σ_q 4μ_q |(F sinh δ_q t)^(γ_q)|²`.
*Segment representation.* `(F sinh δt)^(γ) = ½[Φ_F(½+δ+iγ) − Φ_F(½−δ+iγ)] = ½∫_{−δ}^{δ} Φ_{tF}(½+σ+iγ)dσ`, since
`∂_σΦ_F(½+σ+iγ) = Φ_{tF}(½+σ+iγ)`. Hence `4|(F sinh δt)^(γ)|² ≤ 4δ² sup_{|σ|≤δ}|Φ_{tF}(½+σ+iγ)|²`.
*Subharmonicity.* `|Φ_{tF}|²` is subharmonic. With `r := 1/(2T) ≤ 1`, every point `w` of the segment satisfies
`|Φ_{tF}(w)|² ≤ (πr²)^{−1}∫_{D(w,r)}|Φ_{tF}|²dA`, and `D(w,r) ⊂ B_q := {|Re z − ½| ≤ δ_q + r, |Im z − γ_q| ≤ r}`. So
```
Σ_q 4μ_q|(F sinh δ_q t)^(γ_q)|² ≤ ∫ |Φ_{tF}(z)|² ω(z) dA(z), ω := (4/(πr²)) Σ_q μ_q δ_q² 1_{B_q}.
```
At any `z`, only the `q` with `|γ_q − Im z| ≤ r < 1` contribute, so `ω ≤ 4D₂/(πr²)`. `ω` is supported in the strip
`S := {|Re z − ½| ≤ β* + r}`.
*Plancherel on vertical lines.* `∫_ℝ|Φ_{tF}(½+σ+iγ)|²dγ = 2π∫t²F(t)²e^{2σt}dt ≤ 2πT²e^{2|σ|T}`. So
`∫_S|Φ_{tF}|² ≤ 4π(β*+r)T²e^{2(β*+r)T}`.
*Assembly.* `−W_T(F) ≤ (4D₂/(πr²))·4π(β*+r)T²e^{2(β*+r)T} = 64 D₂ T⁴ (β* + 1/(2T)) e^{2β*T} · e`. Take the infimum
over `F`. ∎

Compared with round-4 Theorem U, (BD) ⟹ (BD₂) with `D₂ ≤ β*²·D_off`. The converse fails: (BD₂) allows local counts
`≍ log y` with displacements `δ ≪ (log y)^{−1/2}`. The price is `T⁴` instead of `T²`, which does not affect the
exponent.

**Theorem M (sharpness of the termwise route). PROVED.** For each fixed `T > 0`, put
`𝒩_T := sup{ Σ_q 4μ_q|(F sinh δ_q t)^(γ_q)|² : F real, C_c^∞(I_T), ‖F‖ = 1 }`, the zero-side majorant of `−W_T`
given by Lemma L. Then `𝒩_T < ∞ ⟺ D₂ < ∞`.

*Proof.* (⟸) is the proof of U₂.
(⟹) Suppose `D₂ = ∞`. A bounded range of `y` meets only finitely many zeros, so there are `y_k → +∞` with
`S_k := Σ_{|γ_q−y_k|<1} μ_qδ_q² → ∞`. Cover `(y_k−1, y_k+1)` by `⌈8T⌉` intervals of length `1/(4T)`. One of them,

with `φ ≥ 0`, `φ ≠ 0`, and put `F_k(t) := φ(t)cos(y′_k t)`, so `‖F_k‖² → ½‖φ‖²`. For such `q`:
`(F_k sinh δt)^(γ) = ½∫φ sinh(δt)e^{i(γ−y′)t}dt + ½∫φ sinh(δt)e^{i(γ+y′)t}dt`.
In the first integral, `|γ−y′|t ≤ 1/8` on `supp φ`, so its real part is at least
`½cos(1/8)∫φ sinh δt ≥ 0.49 δ∫tφ(t)dt`, using `sinh x ≥ x`. The second integral is at most
`½(γ+y′)^{−2}∫|(φ sinh δt)″| ≤ C_φ δ/(y′_k)²`, integrating by parts twice with `δ < ½`. So for large `k`,
`|(F_k sinh δ_qt)^(γ_q)| ≥ 0.4δ_q∫tφ`, and the majorant at `F_k/‖F_k‖` is at least
`c_φ S_k/⌈8T⌉ → ∞`. ∎

*Reading (the salvage made exact).* Theorem M says that the measure `ν := Σ_q μ_q δ_q² δ_{γ_q}` must be a Carleson
measure for the Paley–Wiener space of type `T` sampled on the real line. For PW spaces this is equivalent to uniformly
bounded mass on unit intervals: the classical Plancherel–Pólya/Carleson criterion (CITED as background, e.g. Seip's
lectures on sampling and interpolation; the proof above is self-contained). So:
- the upper half of the growth law holds on (BD₂) (PROVED);
- *any* argument that bounds the negativity of `W_T` zero by zero, i.e. discards the positive cosh and on-line terms of

- without (BD₂) the upper half is **OPEN**. Removing (BD₂) would need the positive terms of (2.1) to absorb nearby

  known pointwise: on-line zeros are known only on average (Selberg, Conrey), and in intervals `[y, y + y^{1/4+ε}]`

- *Status of (BD₂).* It holds under RH (vacuously) and under finiteness of `Z_off`. Unconditionally, only

  Selberg's density estimate `Σ_{0<γ≤Y}(β−½)^+ ≪ Y` (CITED, Titchmarsh §9.9–9.10 and Selberg 1946). The pointwise
  bound is OPEN and not claimed.

## 5. How W15 is avoided, and why the angle still closes

**The renormalisation flow and its invariants.** The window flow `T ↦ (W_T, 𝔞_T|P_T)` has three natural monotone
invariants: the bottom `λ_T` (nonincreasing), the rate `κ` (a `T = ∞` quantity), and the index `N(T)` (a
nondecreasing integer). The angle asked for a functional equation relating the certificate at `T` and at `T+δ` through
the new contacts `½log n ∈ (T, T+δ]`, and a monotone invariant that avoids W15.

**How W15 (P46 §21.7, `cor:mono`) is avoided.** None of the three results propagates a state from `T` to `T+δ`.
- Theorem I(a) and U₂ are proved at each `T` separately, from the zero side (2.1). No induction takes place.
- Theorem I(b) and the lower half G1 use the `T`-free profile `U(x)` of Lemma T. It is read off once, at all scales
  simultaneously. The new contacts at `log n ≈ x` enter only `U(x)`, through (3.1)'s prime-side twin (round 4, (2.1)).
- `N(∞)`, `κ` are `T = ∞` invariants. So the scheme falls in case (c) of my round-3 Theorem NB (a `T`-free statement).
  That case is outside W15's scope. NB predicted exactly this landing, EQUIVALENT.

**Why nothing is gained for the ∀T arrow.** The exact invariant is `N(∞)`, and by Theorem I it equals
`2 × #{distinct off-line zeros in the first quadrant}` in the finite case. A prime-side proof of `N(∞) = 0` would be a
prime-side count of off-line zeros equal to zero, which is RH. The integer structure does not help against W15 in the
direction needed. `N` is nondecreasing, so `N(T) = 0` never propagates upward; that is the sign problem again. The new
contacts enter the window through the enlargement `I_T ⊂ I_{T+δ}`, which adds an infinite-dimensional space. So no
interlacing inequality bounds `N(T+δ) − N(T)` by a function of the finitely many new `Λ(n)`. This is **PROVED** in the
sense that Theorem I(b) realises jumps of total size `2m` with no reference to which contacts are new. A contact-counting
bound would contradict Theorem I for a hypothetical `m ≥ 1` only if it depended on `Λ` beyond the class. That is the
`cor:void` wall.

**Taken together with rounds 3–4,** the bootstrap/renormalisation angle is closed in three forms: the sign (NB), the
rate (G1 + U/U₂, EQUIVALENT), and the index (Theorem I, EQUIVALENT, Bombieri). Each exact invariant of the window flow
is a zero-side quantity: the displacement `Θ − ½` or the number of off-line zeros.

## 8. What is genuinely new, and the weakest step

**New (modest).**
1. **Theorem M and Theorem U₂.** The hypothesis for the upper half of the growth law is weakened from bounded local
  *counts* (BD) to locally summable *squared displacements* (BD₂), and M proves (BD₂) is exactly what the termwise
  zero-side route needs. This is the Carleson formulation the panel asked for, with necessity proved.
2. **Lemma L**, the Lorentzian (cosh/sinh) form of Weil's explicit formula. It is elementary. Its use is to isolate
  the negative part as `|(F sinh δt)^|²`, which drives I(a), U₂ and M.
3. **The window form of Bombieri's index theorem**, with an independent proof (Lemma L for the upper bound, the
  matrix-valued transport profile (3.1) for the lower bound). It adds that multiplicity does not count (I-c), and that
  in the finite scenario the polar capacity carries no negative square for large `T` (I-b).

**Not new.** The index count itself (Bombieri 2000); Weil's criterion; the lower half of the rate law (rounds 4 of B1,
B2, B3, B5, B9); the Plancherel–Pólya/Carleson principle behind Theorem M.

**Weakest step.**
- The Bombieri citation rests on the EuDML abstract only. The multiplicity reconciliation (I-c) is flagged, not
  settled against his text.
- In Theorem I(b), Step 3 needs polarised (E) with absolute convergence *uniform in `x ≥ 0`* over the zeros with
  `Re s ≤ 0`. This holds because `h_k ∈ C_c^∞` gives rapid decay of `H_k(±s)` uniformly for `|Re s| ≤ ½`, and
  `|e^{sx}| ≤ 1`. It is checked but is the load-bearing analytic step.
- In Theorem M, the only non-trivial estimate is the lower bound on the first integral. It uses `|γ−y′|t ≤ 1/8` on
  `supp φ ⊂ (0,T)` and `sinh x ≥ x`. The second integral is `O(δ/y′²)`.

**No sign is claimed for ζ at any `T > 0.8`, and the ∀T arrow is not advanced. The bootstrap/renormalisation angle of
track 2 is closed as a dead end for the mechanism.**
