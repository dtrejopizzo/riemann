## G2_B1_R1: structure vs randomness (zero-free completions, centre identity)

**Checked and correct.**
* **Prop 1.** I checked the P47 conventions: `F̂(τ)=∫F e^{iτt}`, `c_F(u)=∫F(t)F(t−u)`, and comb term `Σ v_n c_F(log n)`.
  With these, `C(iτ)=|F̂|²`, `C(½)=∫c_F e^{u/2}=A(F)B(F)`, and the n = 1 term is `α‖F‖²`. Moving the line from κ > ½ to
  κ = 0 crosses only `z = ½`, with residue `r_D·AB`. The identity
  `𝔞_T = (2π)^{-1}∫σ_D|F̂|² − 2r_D AB` follows. PROVED.
* **Lemma 2.** Since `ξ(½+iτ)` is real, `ξ′/ξ` is imaginary on the line, and `Re[1/s + 1/(s−1)] = 0` there. So
  `Re ζ′/ζ(½+iτ) = −½m(τ)` and `C_0 = −2ζ′/ζ(½)`. The residual is 0.0 at 20 digits (DIAGNOSTIC re-run).
* **Theorem 3.**
  * (a) and (b) follow the proof of `prop:finsupp` exactly. The symbol `σ^str_R` is continuous and polynomially
  bounded, and the domination holds.
  * (c) Fubini gives `∫e^{-sy}Σ_{d≤e^y}μ(d)d^{-½}(y−log d)dy = 1/(s²ζ(s+½))` for Re s > ½. By Lemma 2 the double and
  simple poles at 0 cancel.
  * (d) The Landau/Widder argument is correct. The real singularities of G lie at `s ≤ −5/2`, and ζ(σ) ≠ 0 on (−2, 1).
  If g were eventually of one sign, G would be analytic on Re s > −5/2, which contradicts the pole at ρ₁ − ½.
  * I re-ran the table: g(log 2) = −1.9614, g(log 3) = −0.8036, g(log 10) = −0.0860, g(log 30) = +0.0459, matching the memo.

  PROVED, unconditional. This is the memo's best item.

**Errors and overclaims.**
1. **The Heath-Brown example is false as stated.** The memo says it is "PROVED to satisfy (a)–(d)", but it violates
  Definition 1(c), which allows at most a simple pole at s = 1. `D_{k,z} = Σ_j ±\binom kj ζ′ζ^{j−1}M_z^j` has a pole of
  order up to k+1 at s = 1 (ζ′ alone has a double pole). Prop 1 as written then does not apply to it: higher-order poles
  add derivative terms `C′(½)`, `C″(½)` to the polar part. The ζM_R example is correct, and Theorem 3 uses only ζM_R, so
  the error is not load-bearing. The paragraph must be corrected before any integration. The "formulation of R157 row
  30" is proved only for ζM_R-type completions.
2. **Prop 5(ii)–(iii) are method-level arguments, not theorems.** They are labelled "arg." and are honest, but "closes
  the structure-vs-randomness angle" rests on them.
3. **Theorem 3 refutes a modified comb, not a mechanism for ζ.** Λ_R agrees with Λ only for n ≤ R. For T > ½log R the
  form is that of a different, signed, infinitely supported comb. The theorem therefore adds a new member to the family
  of `thm:finmod`, with signed weights, infinite support and exactly the pole of ζ. It says nothing about ζ's own form,
  and it leaves the regime R = R(T) ≥ e^{2T} untouched (Q2). By Prop 1 that regime is ζ's form itself, so Q2 is not a
  gap in the refutation: it is the original problem.

**Against the record.** As a mechanism the angle is excluded:
* F2: any exact split inherits the margin (Prop 4, which is `thm:margin`).
* O3 (`rem:resweights`): an estimated pseudorandom part is killed.
* F4: the split exists for every Beurling system.
* `thm:Z`/E3: treating the pseudorandom part exactly makes its spectral side the whole zero measure (Prop 5(i)).

Q1 (strong duality over zero-free completions) would at best give a zero-free reformulation of RH (EQUIV), as the memo
says.

**Verdict: EXCLUDED.** Salvageable: Theorem 3 (with the Heath-Brown paragraph corrected or removed) and Prop 1 for
completions with a simple pole, as record material. The centre identity `C_0 = −2ζ′/ζ(½)` is classical. Its reading as
