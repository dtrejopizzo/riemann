# G2_B6_R2 — G2 (the mechanism), round 2: the Nyquist horizon, and a proved doubly exponential margin

**Round-1 verdict on my memo (5/5 restatement).** The panel accepted Lemma 1, Theorem H, Corollary H1 and Propositions
S and R as correct. It rejected the candidate law L_seq, which is `cor:RH(c)`. P1, P4 and P5 each objected that the
headline "horizon `H_* ≥ 2e^{2A_T}`" is a property of the proof, not of the certificate. P4 added that "the meaningful
bound would be an intrinsic one".

**What this round does.** It replaces the proof-artefact horizon with the intrinsic one, and proves what that
horizon forces.

* The intrinsic horizon is the **Nyquist height** `2πe^{2T}`. There the zero density `(2π)^{−1}log(τ/2π)` equals the
  Landau density `T/π` of the Paley–Wiener space `PW_T`. Below it, the zeros are too sparse to constrain the test
  functions: a test can be made to vanish on all of them, whatever their positions, on or off the line. Above it,
  they are oversampled.
* **Theorem M (PROVED, unconditional, new):** for every `ε > 0` and every `T ≥ T₀(ε)`,

  `λ^W_T ≤ λ^P_T ≤ λ_min(A_T|P_T) ≤ exp(−e^{(2−ε)T})`.

  An explicit inequality holds at every `T` (§3.5). It beats `thm:margin` (`4·10⁹Te^{−15.449T}`) from about `T ≈ 6`
  on, and gives `10^{−20464}` at `T = 10` (DIAGNOSTIC evaluation of the PROVED explicit bound).
* This is the upper half of Zhu's conjectural Landau–Widom law (`−log λ ≍ e^{2T}`, arXiv:2608.24827, Conj. 12.1), up
  to `e^{εT}` in the exponent. Zhu proves only `λ ≤ exp(−Te^T)`, and only *under RH*, with a canonical product over
  zeros up to height `2πe^T`. Paper 47 `rem:margin` calls `thm:margin` "the trivial half… exponentially weaker than
  the law". Theorem M closes that gap without RH.

**What this round does *not* do.** It builds no mechanism. Theorem M is an obstruction: it quantifies F2 and O3 and
holds verbatim for every falsifier. The honest verdict on the G2 objective from this angle is **dead end**. The only
law that gives all windows from the exact `Λ(n)` is the explicit formula itself (premise of record 3). Theorem M shows
that the sign it has to deliver at window `T` sits below `exp(−e^{(2−ε)T})`. No sign is claimed at any `T > 0.8`, and
row D keeps no label.

Forms and notation are those of Paper 47 (`eq:form`, `eq:W`, `P_T`, `λ^W_T`, `λ_min(A_T|P_T)`). All F are real unless
stated otherwise.

## §1 Definitions

* `Z`: the multiset of ordinates `γ` (`ρ = ½ + iγ`), with multiplicity. It is invariant under `γ ↦ −γ` and
  `γ ↦ γ̄`, and `|Im γ| < ½`. `N(X)` counts the `ρ` with `0 < Im ρ ≤ X`.
* For `F ∈ L²(I_T)`: `F̂(z) = ∫F(t)e^{izt}dt` (entire). `Φ_F(½+iγ) = F̂(γ)` and `Φ_F(1−ρ) = F̂(−γ)`. For real F,
  `F̂(−z̄) = conj F̂(z)`, and `Φ_F(0) = F̂(i/2) = B(F)`, `Φ_F(1) = F̂(−i/2) = A(F)`.
* `ν(x) := 2log(|x|+4) + 4`. By G2_B6_R1 Lemma 1, PROVED from (X-N) and checked by all five panelists,
  `#{γ ∈ Z : Re γ ∈ [x,x+1)} ≤ ν(x)`.

  `N(X) ≤ (X/2π)log(X/2πe) + 7/8 + E(X)` for `X ≥ 2`.
* **Parameters.** `θ ∈ (0,1)`, `β ∈ (0,1]`, `α := 1/(1+β) ∈ [½,1)`, `r := (1−θ)/2`, `c_β := 1/ζ(1+β)`,
  `κ := ((1+β)/e)(r c_β)^α`.
* **Box.** `b_a := (2a)^{−1}1_{[−a,a]}`, with `b̂_a(z) = sin(az)/(az) =: sinc(az)`.

## §2 The window

### Lemma W (a Gevrey window, flat on `[−θ,θ]`) — PROVED
Put `a_j := r c_β j^{−1−β}` for `j ≥ 1`, so that `Σ_j a_j = r`. Let `ρ_β := *_{j≥1} b_{a_j}` and
`ω := b_{(1+θ)/2} * ρ_β`. Then:
1. `ρ_β` is a probability measure on `[−r,r]`, and `ω` is a probability density on `[−1,1]` with `0 ≤ ω ≤ 1/(1+θ)`;
2. `ω ≡ 1/(1+θ)` on `[−θ,θ]`;
3. for every `z = x+iy ∈ ℂ`, `ω̂(z) = sinc((1+θ)z/2)·∏_{j≥1}sinc(a_j z)`, where the product converges locally
  uniformly on `ℂ`.

*Proof.*
* **(1)** Consider the partial convolutions `ρ_m = b_{a_1}*…*b_{a_m}`, supported in `[−r,r]`. Their characteristic
  functions `∏_{j≤m} sinc(a_j x)` converge locally uniformly, because `|sinc(w) − 1| ≤ |w|²e^{|Im w|}/6` and
  `Σ a_j² < ∞`. By Lévy's continuity theorem `ρ_m ⇒ ρ_β`, and the support stays in the closed set `[−r,r]`.
* **(2)** `ω(s) = (1+θ)^{−1}ρ_β([s−(1+θ)/2, s+(1+θ)/2])`. The interval contains `[−r,r]` exactly when
  `|s| ≤ (1+θ)/2 − r = θ`. The same formula gives the bounds in (1), and `supp ω ⊂ [−(1+θ)/2 − r, (1+θ)/2 + r] = [−1,1]`.
* **(3)** `z ↦ ∫e^{izt}dρ_m` converges to `∫e^{izt}dρ_β` for every `z`, because `e^{izt}` is bounded and continuous
  on `[−r,r]` and `ρ_m ⇒ ρ_β`. The partial products converge locally uniformly on `ℂ` by the same `Σa_j²` bound. ∎

### Lemma E (decay in the strip) — PROVED
For `x ∈ ℝ` with `|x| = u`, and any `y`,
`|ω̂(x+iy)| ≤ e^{|y|}·𝖤(u)`, where `𝖤(u) := exp(−(1+β)⌊(r c_β u)^α/e⌋) ≤ e^{1+β}e^{−κu^α}`.
`𝖤` is non-increasing and `≤ 1`.

*Proof.*
* `sinc(w) = ∫₀¹cos(sw)ds`, so `|sinc(w)| ≤ cosh(Im w) ≤ e^{|Im w|}`. Also
  `|sinc(w)| ≤ e^{|Im w|}/|w| ≤ e^{|Im w|}/|Re w|`.
* Let `J := ⌊(r c_β u)^α/e⌋`. For `j ≤ J`, `j^{1+β} ≤ r c_β u/e^{1+β}`, so `a_j u ≥ e^{1+β}` and
  `|sinc(a_j z)| ≤ e^{a_j|y|}e^{−(1+β)}`.
* Every other factor, the box factor included, is `≤ e^{a|y|}` for its own `a`. The `a`'s sum to `(1+θ)/2 + r = 1`.
* Finally, `⌊v⌋ ≥ v − 1`. ∎

### Lemma I (tail integral) — PROVED
If `κU^α ≥ 1`, then `∫_U^∞ e^{−κu^α}du ≤ (2(1+β)/κ)U^{1−α}e^{−κU^α}`.

*Proof.*
* Substitute `v = κu^α`. The integral becomes `(1+β)κ^{−(1+β)}Γ(1+β, V)` with `V = κU^α`.
* For `0 < s ≤ 1` and `V ≥ 1`, `Γ(1+s,V) = V^se^{−V} + sΓ(s,V) ≤ (V^s + sV^{s−1})e^{−V} ≤ 2V^se^{−V}`, using
  `v^{s−1} ≤ V^{s−1}` on `[V,∞)`.
* The powers of `κ` combine to `κ^{−1}`, and `αβ = 1−α`. ∎

## §3 Theorem M

### 3.1 The test
Put `χ(t) := ω(t/T)`, `g := π/(θT)`,
`X := 2πe^{2θT−1}`, `J := N(X) + 1`, `X′ := Jg`, `D := X − X′`.

For a real vector `c = (c_j)_{|j|≤J}` with `c_{−j} = conj c_j`, put `P_c(t) := Σ_{|j|≤J}c_je^{−ijgt}`, which is
real, and `F_c := χ·P_c`. Then:
* `F_c` is real and supported in `[−T,T]`;
* `F̂_c(z) = Σ_j c_j χ̂(z − jg)`;
* `χ̂(z) = Tω̂(Tz)`.

### 3.2 Lemma K (annihilating every zero below `X`, and the Tate moments) — PROVED
There is `c ≠ 0` such that `F := F_c` satisfies:
* `F ∈ P_T`, i.e. `A(F) = B(F) = 0`;
* `Φ_F(ρ) = 0` for every non-trivial zero `ρ` with `|Im ρ| ≤ X`.

*Proof.* The admissible `c` form a real vector space of dimension `2J+1`: `c₀ ∈ ℝ` and `c_j ∈ ℂ` for `1 ≤ j ≤ J`.
* Let `Z_X^+` be the set of *distinct* values `γ ∈ Z` with `0 < Re γ ≤ X`. Its size is at most `N(X)`, because
  `Re γ = Im ρ` and distinct values come from distinct zeros.
* Each condition `F̂(γ) = 0`, `γ ∈ Z_X^+`, is one complex, i.e. two real, linear condition on `c`.
* `A(F) = F̂(−i/2)` and `B(F) = F̂(i/2)` are real for real F. That is two more real conditions.

In total there are at most `2N(X) + 2 < 2J + 1` real conditions, so a nonzero `c` exists.

Now take a zero `ρ = ½+iγ` with `|Re γ| ≤ X`. There are no real zeros in `(0,1)`, so `Re γ ≠ 0`.
* If `Re γ > 0`, then `F̂(γ) = 0` directly.
* If `Re γ < 0`, then `−γ̄ ∈ Z_X^+`, and `F̂(γ) = conj F̂(−γ̄) = 0`. ∎

*Remark.* Lemma K uses **nothing** about where the zeros below `X` are: on the line or off it, simple or multiple.
It uses only how many there are.

### 3.3 Lemma N (norm) — PROVED
`‖F_c‖² ≥ (1+θ)^{−2}·2θT·Σ_j|c_j|² ≥ (θT/2)‖c‖²`.

*Proof.* `χ ≡ 1/(1+θ)` on `[−θT, θT]` (Lemma W(2)). The exponentials `e^{−ijgt}` are exactly orthogonal on an
interval of length `2θT = 2π/g`, so `∫_{−θT}^{θT}|P_c|² = 2θT‖c‖²`. ∎

*This is where the Nyquist density enters.* The lattice `gℤ` has density `θT/π`, which is the Landau density of
`PW_{θT}`. Exact orthogonality stands in for the prolate or Ingham estimates of R155_047, so there is no
constant-chasing.

### 3.4 Lemma T (the zeros above `X`) — PROVED
Let `S(δ) := Σ_{k≥0}𝖤(T(δ+kg))`. For every zero ordinate `γ` with `|Re γ| > X`,
`|F̂_c(γ)F̂_c(−γ)| ≤ ‖c‖²T²e^T S(|Re γ| − X′)`.
If `κ(Tδ)^α ≥ 1`, then `S(δ) ≤ e^{1+β}e^{−κ(Tδ)^α}[1 + (θ/π)(2(1+β)/κ)(Tδ)^{1−α}]`.

*Proof.*
* **Cauchy–Schwarz.** `|F̂_c(z)|² ≤ ‖c‖²Σ_{|j|≤J}|χ̂(z−jg)|²`.
* **Strip bound.** For `|Im z| < ½`, Lemma E gives `|χ̂(z−jg)| ≤ Te^{T/2}𝖤(T|Re z − jg|)`.
* **Ordering the distances.** Set `δ := |Re z| − X′ ≥ D > 0`. The points `jg` lie in `[−X′, X′]` and are spaced by
  `g`, so the sorted distances `|Re z − jg|` are `≥ δ, δ+g, δ+2g, …`. Since `𝖤² ≤ 𝖤` and `𝖤` is non-increasing,
  `Σ_j 𝖤(T|Re z − jg|)² ≤ S(δ)`.
* **Both factors.** The same bound holds at `−γ`, since `|Re(−γ)| = |Re γ|` and the lattice is symmetric. This gives
  the first claim.
* **Bound on S.** By the sum-integral comparison with step `Tg = π/θ`,
  `S(δ) ≤ 𝖤(Tδ) + (θ/π)∫_{Tδ}^∞𝖤`. Then apply Lemma E and Lemma I. ∎

### 3.5 Theorem M (explicit form) — PROVED, unconditional
Let `T > 0`, `θ ∈ (0,1)` and `β ∈ (0,1]`, with `X = 2πe^{2θT−1} ≥ 21` and `D > 0`. Then

`λ^W_T ≤ λ^P_T ≤ λ_min(A_T|P_T) ≤ (4Te^T/θ)·Σ_{k≥0} ν(X+k)·S(D+k)`,

and `D ≥ (X − π(15/8 + E(X)))/(θT)`.

*Proof.* Take `F` from Lemma K.
* **Hypotheses of `lem:roughEF`.** F is real and in `L²(I_T)`. Hypothesis (a) holds because `|F̂(τ)|` decays like
  `e^{−κ(T|τ|)^α}` on `ℝ` (the Cauchy–Schwarz step of Lemma T). Hypothesis (b) holds by Lemma T together with the
  counting bound below.
* **The zero sum.** So `𝔞_T(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`, since `F ∈ P_T`. The zeros with `|Im ρ| ≤ X` contribute `0`
  (Lemma K).
* **Counting the rest.** A zero with `Re γ ∈ [X+k, X+k+1)` has `|Re γ| − X′ ≥ D+k`. There are at most `ν(X+k)` of
  them (G2_B6_R1 Lemma 1), and as many on the negative side. Hence
  `𝔞_T(F) ≤ 2‖c‖²T²e^T Σ_k ν(X+k)S(D+k)`.
* **Normalise.** Divide by `‖F‖² ≥ (θT/2)‖c‖²` (Lemma N). The chain `λ^W ≤ λ^P ≤ λ^A` is P47 (`eq:W`,
  `rem:Kprim`).
* **Lower bound on D.** From (X-N) with `log(X/2πe) = 2θT − 2`:
  `πJ/(θT) ≤ X(2θT−2)/(2θT) + π(15/8 + E(X))/(θT)`. ∎

### 3.6 Corollary M1 (the doubly exponential margin) — PROVED, unconditional
For every `ε ∈ (0,1]` there is `T₀(ε)` such that, for all `T ≥ T₀(ε)`,

`λ^W_T ≤ λ^P_T ≤ λ_min(A_T|P_T) ≤ exp(−e^{(2−ε)T})`.

*Proof.* Take `θ = 1 − ε/8` and `β = ε/8`, so that `2θα = 2(1−ε/8)/(1+ε/8) ≥ 2 − ε/2`.
1. **Size of `TD`.** Put `U := TD`. By Theorem M, `U ≥ (X − π(15/8 + E(X)))/θ ≥ X/2 = πe^{2θT−1}` for large `T`,
  because `E(X) = O(T)`.
2. **Bounding the sum.** Use `ν(X+k) ≤ ν(X)(1+k)`, the bound on S from Lemma T, and
  `(U+Tk)^α ≥ ½(U^α + (Tk)^α)`. For `U ≥ U₀(β,θ)`, the polynomial factor is absorbed by half of the exponent. This
  gives `Σ_k ν(X+k)S(D+k) ≤ C(ε)ν(X)e^{−κU^α/4}`.
3. **Conclusion.** `−log λ^A ≥ (κ/4)(π/e)^α e^{(2−ε/2)T} − O(T) ≥ e^{(2−ε)T}` for `T ≥ T₀(ε)`. (If `λ^A ≤ 0`, the
  statement is trivial.) ∎

### 3.7 Corollary M2 (universality) — PROVED
Theorem M and Corollary M1 hold verbatim for the zero-side form
`Q_M(F) := Σ_{γ∈M}F̂(γ)F̂(−γ)` of any multiset `M` that satisfies all of the following:
* `M` is invariant under `γ ↦ −γ` and `γ ↦ γ̄`;
* `M ⊂ {|Im γ| < ½}`;
* `M` has no point with `Re γ = 0`;
* `M` has the same local and global counting bounds.

Examples: every sub-multiset of `Z` (finite universes, random thinnings), and every modification of `Z` below `X`.
For a strip of width `½ + a` (the falsifier `Ξ_a`), the factor `e^T` becomes `e^{(1+2a)T}`.

*Proof.* Only those properties were used. ∎

*Reading.* At window `T`, the configuration of the zeros below the Nyquist height cannot raise the margin above
`exp(−e^{(2−ε)T})`, whatever it is. The doubly exponential smallness is a **density** phenomenon, not an arithmetic
one.

### 3.9 Comparison with Zhu's Theorem 1.3
Zhu proves `λ ≤ exp(−Le^L)` **under RH**. The test is a canonical product `∏(1 − τ/γ_j)` over the zeros up to
`2πe^L`, times a power-of-sinc window; RH is used for the localisation and for the potential theory of the product.

Theorem M differs in four ways:
* **Linear algebra instead of a product.** The zeros below `X` are annihilated by a dimension count on an exactly
  orthogonal Nyquist lattice. No product, no potential theory, and no information on their location is needed.
* **Height.** It reaches `2πe^{2θT−1}` rather than `2πe^{T}`.
* **Exponent.** It gives `e^{(2−ε)T}` rather than `Te^T`.
* **Unconditional.**

Its limitation is the constant in front of `e^{(2−ε)T}`, which falls short of Zhu's measured `2π²N(T*)/log N(T*)`
by more than a factor `e^{εT}`. Novelty: I did not find it in Zhu (the abstract and HTML were read for this point),
in P47, or in R153–R155. I have not checked the prolate/Landau–Widom literature exhaustively (CITED-NOT-VERIFIED
list in `00-references/PROLATE-REFERENCES.md`).

## §4 The random-zero probe, answered intrinsically

### 4.1 The Nyquist horizon (reading PROVED by Lemma K; the "above" half is HEURISTIC)
* **Below `2πe^{2θT−1}`.** There are fewer zeros than degrees of freedom of `PW_{θT}` (Lemma K's count). So there is
  always a test function that vanishes at every one of them and still carries unit mass. **No configuration below
  the horizon, however exact, can make the window form large** (Corollary M2). This is what the round-1 rigidity
  table measured at `T = 0.2`: the low zeros are rigid for a *given* certificate because that certificate has
  doubly exponentially little room.
* **Above `2πe^{2T}`.** The zeros oversample `PW_T`. There, positivity is a sampling phenomenon: density plus reality
  (Beurling), HEURISTIC as a *local* statement.
* **The round-1 proof horizon.** `2e^{2A_T} ≈ e^{8e^T}` is replaced by the intrinsic `2πe^{2T}`. The latter is the
  resolution scale of P47 §`subsec:where` and the lower edge of `prop:band`.

### 4.2 Proposition T (random infinite thinnings keep the form positive at every T) — PROVED conditional on RH and simple zeros, from CITED results
Assume RH and that all zeros are simple. Let `p ∈ (0,1]`, and let `S_p` keep each orbit `{γ,−γ}` independently with
probability `p`. Then, almost surely, for every `T > 0` there is `c_T > 0` with
`Σ_{γ∈S_p}|F̂(γ)|² ≥ c_T‖F‖²` for all `F ∈ L²(I_T)`.

*Proof.* **(i)** CITED: Beurling's sampling theorem (Beurling, *Collected Works* II, "Balayage of Fourier–Stieltjes
transforms"; Seip, *Interpolation and sampling in spaces of analytic functions*, AMS 2004, Ch. 2). A uniformly
discrete `Λ ⊂ ℝ` with lower density `D⁻(Λ) = lim_{r→∞}inf_x #(Λ∩[x,x+r])/r > T/π` is a sampling set for `PW_T`. It
therefore suffices to find, almost surely and for every integer `M`, an `M^{−1}`-separated `Λ_M ⊂ S_p` with
`D⁻(Λ_M) ≥ M/4`.

**(ii)** CITED: under RH, `S(t) = O(log t/log log t)` (Littlewood; Titchmarsh, §14.13). Hence, for fixed `h > 0` and
`t ≥ t₀(h)`, `N(t+h) − N(t) ≥ (h/4π)log t`, and these are distinct zeros by simplicity.

**(iii)** Fix `h = 1/M`. For even `k ≥ k₀`, let `I_k = [kh, (k+1)h)`. Then `P(I_k ∩ S_p = ∅) ≤ (kh)^{−ah}` with
`a = log(1/(1−p))/4π` (if `p = 1` there is nothing to prove). The events are independent, since distinct `k` involve
distinct orbits. Take a block of `L` consecutive even-indexed intervals starting at `k`. The probability that at
least `L/2` of them are empty is `≤ 2^L(kh)^{−ahL/2}`. This is summable in `k` once `ahL ≥ 4`. By Borel–Cantelli,
almost surely every such block beyond some `k₁` has at least `L/2` nonempty intervals.

**(iv)** Pick one point of `S_p` from each nonempty even-indexed `I_k` with `k ≥ k₁`, and add the mirror images.
* The resulting set is `h`-separated.
* A window of length `r` meets the compact `[−k₁h, k₁h]` in length at most `2k₁h`. Outside it, the window contains
  at least `(r − 2k₁h)/(4h) − 2` of the chosen points.
* Hence `D⁻ ≥ 1/(4h) = M/4`. Intersecting over `M ∈ ℕ` gives an almost-sure event that works for every `T`. ∎

*Reading.*
* **What the positivity needs.** Under RH, the zero-side positivity at every `T` does **not** need the complete list
  of zeros. An infinite random thinning keeps it, whereas a finite universe never does (`thm:finZ`: density `0`).
  What it needs is **reality plus infinite density**, and reality is RH.
* **B2's OPEN expectation.** This refutes G2_B2_R1's expectation (failure for fixed `p < 1` at large `T`) *for the

**What this says about the "for all T" arrow.** The map box equates "for all T" with `ψ(x) − x ≪ x^{½+ε}` and

the estimate is a global asymptotic. Any mechanism must therefore be an *exact identity or structure* valid at all
`T` at once, which is F3/O3 again, now with its true size. Every such structure found so far is the explicit formula
itself (`thm:Z`) or an equivalent of RH.

**New, PROVED, unconditional.**

**Not claimed.** A mechanism; any sign at `T > 0.8`; strong duality for thinned sets; the lower half of Zhu's law,
