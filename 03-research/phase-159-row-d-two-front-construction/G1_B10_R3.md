## §1 Definitions

* **Prime datum.** `ν := Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n) δ_{log n}`, a positive measure on `(0, ∞)`. Its Laplace transform
  `L_ν(z) = ∫ e^{−zt} dν(t) = Σ Λ(n) n^{−z} = −ζ′/ζ(z)` converges absolutely for `Re z > 1` and diverges at every
  real `x ≤ 1`. Abscissa `a(ν) = 1`.
* **Beurling datum.** A discrete Beurling system `𝒫` with von Mangoldt function `Λ_𝒫 ≥ 0` and
  `ν_𝒫 := Σ Λ_𝒫(n) δ_{log n}`; only `Λ_𝒫 ≥ 0` and a finite abscissa `a(ν_𝒫)` are used.
* **Horizontal kernel.** For a positive measure `μ` on `ℝ` with bilateral Laplace transform `L_μ` converging on a
  real interval `(b, ∞)`, `𝒦_μ(s, w) := L_μ(s + w̄)`, `Re s, Re w > b/2`.
* **Exponentially convex (Widder).** A continuous `k : (b, ∞) → ℝ` is exponentially convex if
  `Σ_{i,j} c_i c̄_j k(x_i + x_j) ≥ 0` for all finite families `x_i ∈ (b/2, ∞)`, `c_i ∈ ℂ`. (Widder's definition asks
  positivity for all configurations whose pairwise sums lie in the interval; for the interval `(b, ∞)` this is the
  same as `x_i > b/2` for all `i`, since `2x_i` must lie in it.)
* **Widder's representation theorem** (`CITED` (std.): Widder, *The Laplace Transform*, 1941, Ch. VI §21, Thm 21;
  Bernstein 1929): `k` is exponentially convex on `(b, ∞)` **iff** `k(x) = ∫_{−∞}^{∞} e^{−xt} dα(t)` with `α`
  nondecreasing and the integral convergent for every `x ∈ (b, ∞)`.
* **Uniqueness of bilateral Laplace transforms** (`PROVED`, one line): if two locally finite measures `α, β` have
  absolutely convergent Laplace transforms on `Re z > c` that agree there, then `α = β`. *Proof.* On the line
  `Re z = x_0 > c` both transforms are the Fourier transforms of the finite measures `e^{−x_0 t}dα`, `e^{−x_0 t}dβ`;
  Fourier uniqueness for finite measures gives `e^{−x_0 t}dα = e^{−x_0 t}dβ`, hence `α = β`. ∎
* **Vertical covariance.** A stationary covariance on the flow variable: `C(F, G) = ⟨D, F ⋆ G̃⟩` for a distribution
  `D` on ℝ; it is a covariance iff `D` is positive-definite (Bochner), iff `D̂` is a positive measure.
* **Weil datum** `g` (P46 `eq:gdatum`): `⟨g,h⟩ = C_0h(0) + ∫_0^∞(h(0) − h_e(u))K(u)du − Σ_n v_n h_e(log n) + 2∫h(u)cosh(u/2)du`,
  `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`, and `⟨g, φ⟩ = Σ_ρ φ̂(γ_ρ)` (P46 `thm:Z`). Complete form: `W_T(F) = ⟨g, F⋆F̃⟩`, `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`.

## §2 The two-direction theorem (horizontal part)

### Theorem 1 (Widder–Landau cap for positive prime-side data) — PROVED

Let `μ` be a positive locally finite measure on ℝ, with Laplace abscissa `a(μ) := inf{x : ∫e^{−xt}dμ < ∞} ∈ ℝ`.

(a) `𝒦_μ(s, w) = L_μ(s + w̄)` is a positive-definite kernel on `{Re s > a(μ)/2}`: it is the reproducing kernel of
the Hilbert space `H_μ := closure of span{e_t : t ∈ supp μ}` with `⟨e_t, e_{t′}⟩ = δ_{tt′}·μ({t})` in the atomic case,
realised as functions `f(s) = ∫ c(t) e^{−st} dμ(t)`, `c ∈ L²(μ)`; the vertical flow `(U_τ f)(s) = f(s + iτ)` is a
strongly continuous unitary group on `H_μ`, with generator of spectrum `supp μ`.

(b) **Cap.** Suppose `k : (b, ∞) → ℝ` with `b < a(μ)` is continuous, exponentially convex on `(b, ∞)`, and
`k = L_μ` on `(a(μ), ∞)`. Then no such `k` exists. Equivalently: the positive kernel `𝒦_μ` admits **no**
positive-definite extension of Toeplitz–Hankel shape `k(s + w̄)` to any larger half-plane, not even along the real
axis.

(c) For `μ = ν` (the prime datum): `H_ν = 𝓗²_Λ = {Σ a_n n^{−s} : Σ |a_n|²/Λ(n) < ∞}` (sum over `Λ(n) > 0`), the
kernel is `−ζ′/ζ(s + w̄)` on `Re s > ½`, the flow generator has spectrum `{log p^k}`, and the cap is at `Re z = 1`:
the pole. The zeros of ζ are the poles of the meromorphic continuation of the kernel **outside** the domain on which
it is a kernel; they are not a property of `H_ν`.

*Proof.* (a) For `c_1, …, c_N ∈ ℂ`, `s_1, …, s_N` with `Re s_i > a/2`:
`Σ c_i c̄_j L_μ(s_i + s̄_j) = ∫ |Σ_i c_i e^{−s_i t}|² dμ(t) ≥ 0`, absolutely convergent since `Re(s_i + s̄_j) > a`.
The reproducing-kernel space is the image of `L²(μ)` under `c ↦ ∫c(t)e^{−st}dμ(t)`, the flow is
`c(t) ↦ e^{−iτt}c(t)`, unitary on `L²(μ)`, with generator multiplication by `t`.
(b) Restrict to the real axis. By Widder's theorem, `k(x) = ∫e^{−xt}dα(t)` with `α` nondecreasing, converging for
`x ∈ (b, ∞)`, hence absolutely on `Re z > b` (positive measure). On `Re z > a(μ)` both `L_α` and `L_μ` are
holomorphic and agree on the real segment `(a(μ), ∞)`, hence on the half-plane. By uniqueness, `α = μ`. But then
`∫e^{−xt}dμ < ∞` for `x ∈ (b, a(μ))`, contradicting the definition of `a(μ)`. ∎
(c) is (a)–(b) with `μ = ν`: `L_ν = −ζ′/ζ`, `a(ν) = 1`, and `Σ_n |a_n|²/Λ(n)` is the norm dual to the weights. ∎

*Reading.* Theorem 1 is Landau's theorem (P47 (X20)) in covariance language: a positive one-parameter kernel can be
continued as a positive kernel exactly as far as its measure's Laplace transform converges, and for a positive
measure that is up to the first real singularity. The prime side has its first real singularity at the pole, so the
positive kernel it generates by construction stops at `Re z = 1`, whatever the zeros do. This is **OT-5′ satisfied
and OT-2 failed** in one stroke: the form is positive before and independently of the spectrum, and the "Lefschetz
formula" of `H_ν` is `Tr U(h) = Σ_n Λ(n)·ĥ(log n)`-type — the prime side alone, with no zero and no archimedean term.

### Theorem 2 (pole subtraction kills horizontal positivity on every half-plane) — PROVED

Let `k_0(z) := −ζ′/ζ(z) − 1/(z − 1)`, holomorphic on `Re z > ½` iff RH, and in any case on `Re z > 1`. Then
`k_0(s + w̄)` is a positive-definite kernel on **no** half-plane `{Re s > c}`, for any real `c` (including `c ≥ ½`,
where the domain is inside the region of absolute convergence).

*Proof.* For `Re z > 1`, `1/(z − 1) = ∫_0^∞ e^{−zt}e^{t}dt`, so `k_0 = L_m` with the signed measure
`m := ν − e^{t}1_{t>0}dt` (in `x = e^t`: `m = dψ(x) − dx` on `[1, ∞)`), whose total variation `|m| = ν + e^t dt` has
Laplace transform converging absolutely on `Re z > 1`. Suppose `k_0(s + w̄)` were positive-definite on
`Re s > c`. Then `k_0` is exponentially convex on `(2c, ∞)`, and by Widder `k_0 = L_α` on `(2c, ∞)` with `α ≥ 0`. On
`Re z > max(1, 2c)` both `L_α` and `L_m` converge absolutely and agree; by uniqueness `α = m`. But `m` is not a
positive measure: `m((0, log 2)) = −(2 − 1) = −1 < 0`. Contradiction. ∎

*Reading.* In the horizontal picture the prime measure and the pole have the **same** sign (`dψ` and `dx` are
both positive), so the "`H¹ = primes minus pole`" residual is negative between consecutive prime powers, on every
interval. Compare the vertical picture (Weil's `g`): there the polar term `+2cosh(u/2)` and the prime atoms
`−v_nδ_{log n}` have **opposite** signs, and the whole balance, including Γ, is positive iff RH. The sign of the pole
relative to the primes flips between the two directions; this is the concrete reason no single "positivity by
construction" can serve both.

### Theorem 3 (no correction of critical-strip size repairs the pole subtraction) — PROVED

Let `A` be any real (signed) locally finite measure on `[1, ∞)` (an "archimedean or auxiliary correction") such that
its Mellin–Laplace transform `∫_1^∞ x^{−z} dA(x)` converges absolutely for `Re z > b` with some `b < 1`. Then the
measure `m_A := dψ − dx + dA` is **not** positive. Consequently `k_A(z) := −ζ′/ζ(z) − 1/(z−1) + L_{dA}(z)`, which
equals `L_{m_A}` on `Re z > 1`, is a positive-definite Toeplitz–Hankel kernel `k_A(s + w̄)` on **no** half-plane
`{Re s > c}`, for any real `c`.

*Proof.* Let `E := [1, ∞) \ {p^k}`, a set of full Lebesgue measure. `dψ(B) = 0` for every Borel `B ⊂ E`. If
`m_A ≥ 0`, then for `B ⊂ E`: `(dA − dx)(B) = m_A(B) ≥ 0`, so `dA ≥ dx` on `E`, hence `dA(B) ≥ dA(B ∩ E) ≥ dx(B ∩ E)
= dx(B)` for every Borel `B` (Lebesgue measure gives no mass to the countable set of prime powers), i.e. `dA ≥ dx`
as measures. Then for real `x_0 ≤ 1`: `∫_1^∞ x^{−x_0} dA(x) ≥ ∫_1^∞ x^{−x_0} dx = ∞`. So the transform of `dA` does
not converge (a fortiori not absolutely) at any real point `≤ 1`, contradicting convergence on `Re z > b`, `b < 1`.
Hence `m_A` is not positive. The kernel statement follows as in Theorem 2: positivity of `k_A(s + w̄)` on a half-plane
inside the region of absolute convergence forces `k_A = L_α`, `α ≥ 0`, and uniqueness gives `α = m_A`. ∎

*Reading.* Whatever one adds to the prime side — the Γ-factor `½ψ(z/2) − ½log π` (whose transform is that of a
signed measure with abscissa `0`), the `1/z` term, smooth densities, further atoms — if its Laplace abscissa is
below `1`, positivity of the total forces the density `≥ 1` between prime powers, which puts the pole back. **The
horizontal picture cannot host any positivity that sees below `Re z = 1`.** This is the "too-good error" law of
B9_R2 Thm 5 (an exact identity with a finite auxiliary cannot have an error below the fluctuation scale) in
covariance form: a positive horizontal covariance has a *step* structure `dψ`, and the compensating `−dx` can only be
hidden by putting `dx` back.

### Corollary 3.1 (T3-uniformity of the cap) — PROVED

For every discrete Beurling system `𝒫` with `Λ_𝒫 ≥ 0` and finite abscissa `a_𝒫 := a(ν_𝒫)`: `𝒦_{ν_𝒫}` is
positive-definite on `Re s > a_𝒫/2` and admits no positive Toeplitz–Hankel extension past `Re z = a_𝒫`; if `ζ_𝒫`
has a simple pole at `a_𝒫 = 1` with residue `ρ_𝒫 > 0`, Theorems 2 and 3 hold verbatim with `dψ_𝒫 − ρ_𝒫 dx`.
In particular the cap holds for the DMV system (P47 (X31), `CITED (secondary)`), whose zeros approach `Re s = 1`,
and for ζ, with the **same proof**. Nothing in Theorems 1–3 distinguishes ζ from any Beurling system: the
horizontal positivity is exactly the class statement excluded by T3 (P47 Rem 27.16) and T1 (P47 Thm 18.1(ii)).
*Proof.* Only `Λ_𝒫 ≥ 0`, the abscissa and the pole were used. ∎

### Theorem 4 (vertical part; recalled) — PROVED (G1_B10_R1 Prop C) + Lemma T

A stationary covariance in the flow variable with the Weil datum, `C(F, G) = ⟨g, F ⋆ G̃⟩` on `C_c^∞(I_T)`, exists iff
`λ^W_T ≥ 0` (complete form), and exists for every `T` iff RH (Weil). With the **prime-only** datum
`D_σ := Σ Λ(n)n^{−σ}δ_{±log n}` (even), the vertical covariance exists for every `σ` trivially (positive measure),
and it carries no archimedean term and no pole: its "Lefschetz formula" is the prime side alone. With the prime
datum **plus the pole** `D_σ − e^{(1−σ)|t|}dt` (the boundary distribution of `−ζ′/ζ(σ + i·)` for `σ < 1`, by Perron,
ignoring the zero terms), the vertical covariance fails to exist for every `σ < 1`: the transform has negative
density between the atoms. Adding the zero terms is adding the spectrum: Lemma T. ∎ (The last two sentences are
`PROVED` for the stated distributions; the identification with boundary values of `−ζ′/ζ` is `HEURISTIC` and not
used.)

*Remark 4.1 (Herglotz is pointwise, not kernel).* Hinkkanen–Lagarias' `Re ξ′/ξ(z) > 0` on `Re z > ½` (R157_002
row 39, EQUIV) is a **pointwise** positivity of a real part, i.e. positivity of the boundary measure of a Herglotz
function on the vertical line — the zero measure. It is **not** Toeplitz–Hankel positivity of `ξ′/ξ(s + w̄)`: that
would make `ξ′/ξ` exponentially convex, hence log-convex on `(½, ∞)`, while `ξ′/ξ(x) ~ ½ log x` is log-concave at
infinity. So even under RH the completed kernel `ξ′/ξ(s + w̄)` is not positive-definite on any half-plane. There is
no horizontal form of the Herglotz criterion; the two directions do not meet.

*Remark 4.2 (why these are the only two shapes; `PROVED` as a classification, `HEURISTIC` as a claim of exhaustion
for "the angle").* A kernel `K(s, w)` on a vertical strip that is invariant under the multiplicative flow
`s ↦ s + iτ` (simultaneously in both variables) depends on `(σ, σ′, t − t′)`. If it is moreover a function of
`s + w̄` — the shape produced by every `Σ_n a_n n^{−s} n^{−w̄}`, i.e. by every construction that reads the primes
through Dirichlet coefficients — then along the horizontal direction it is a Hankel kernel `k(σ + σ′)` (Widder) and
along the vertical direction a Toeplitz kernel `k(2σ + i(t − t′))` (Bochner). Theorems 1–3 govern the first,
Theorem 4 / Lemma T the second. Kernels that are not functions of `s + w̄` (e.g. `K(s, w) = Σ a_{m,n} m^{−s} n^{−w̄}`
with non-diagonal `a`) leave the class "built from the primes through Λ" and enter the correspondence-valued
Frobenius class analysed by B4_R3 and B7_R3; I make no claim there.

## §3 The canonical probabilistic object of the angle, and its blindness

### Proposition 3.1 (the `Λ`-Gaussian Dirichlet series) — PROVED

Let `(ξ_n)_{n≥2}` be i.i.d. standard complex Gaussians. Then
`Y(s) := Σ_{n≥2} √Λ(n)·ξ_n·n^{−s}` converges almost surely, locally uniformly on `Ω := {Re s > ½}`, to a random
holomorphic function; it diverges a.s. at every point with `Re s ≤ ½` (in `L²`, and a.s. by Kolmogorov's three-series
theorem). Its covariance is `E Y(s)\overline{Y(w)} = −ζ′/ζ(s + w̄)`, `s, w ∈ Ω`. The law of `Y` is invariant under the
flow `Y(·) ↦ Y(· + iτ)`. The Gaussian Hilbert space of `Y` is `H_ν = 𝓗²_Λ` of Theorem 1(c).

*Proof.* `Σ_n Λ(n) n^{−2σ} < ∞` iff `2σ > 1`. For `σ > ½` and a compact `K ⊂ Ω`, `Σ_n Λ(n) sup_{s∈K} |n^{−s}|² < ∞`,
and a Gaussian series with summable variances converges a.s. and in `L²`; local uniformity by the standard
Itô–Nisio / Cauchy-estimate argument for random holomorphic series (`CITED` (std.): Kahane, *Some Random Series of
Functions*, Ch. 5). Covariance: `E ξ_m \bar ξ_n = δ_{mn}`. Flow invariance: `(ξ_n n^{−iτ})_n` has the same law. ∎

### Proposition 3.2 (zero statistics of `Y` are blind to the strip) — PROVED, with CITED inputs

(a) The first intensity of the zero set of `Y` is
`ρ_Y(σ + it) = (1/4π)·Δ log(−ζ′/ζ(2σ)) = (1/π)·(d²/dx²) log(−ζ′/ζ(x))|_{x=2σ}`,
independent of `t` (`CITED`: Edelman–Kostlan 1995; Hough–Krishnapur–Peres–Virág, *Zeros of Gaussian Analytic
Functions*, 2009, §2.4, Thm 2.4.4: for a GAF with covariance `K`, `ρ = (1/π)∂_z∂_{z̄} log K(z, z)`).

(b) Thermodynamic form: with the **`Λ`-Gibbs state** `μ_x(n) := Λ(n)n^{−x}/(−ζ′/ζ(x))` at inverse temperature `x`,
`ρ_Y(σ + it) = (1/π)·Var_{μ_{2σ}}(log n)`. In particular `ρ_Y > 0` everywhere on `Ω`.

(c) Boundary behaviour: `ρ_Y(σ + it) = (1/π)(2σ − 1)^{−2}·(1 + O((2σ − 1)²))` as `σ ↓ ½`: the zero density of the
Szegő (Hardy-kernel) GAF of the half-plane `Ω`, whose zeros are the determinantal process with the Bergman kernel of
`Ω` (`CITED`: Peres–Virág 2005, for the disc; transported by the Cayley map).

(d) **Blindness.** `ρ_Y` depends on ζ only through `−ζ′/ζ` on `(1, ∞)`. It is identical, with the same formula, for
every Beurling system with `Λ_𝒫 ≥ 0` and a simple pole at `1`, up to the `O((2σ−1)²)` term, and it is unchanged
under every falsifier of record that keeps `Λ ≥ 0` (amplification `Λ + c·1_{n_0}`, truncation, DMV). The nontrivial
zeros of ζ enter neither the law of `Y` nor any functional of it.

*Proof.* (a) is the cited formula with `K(s, s) = −ζ′/ζ(2σ)`, which depends only on `σ`, so `Δ = ∂²_σ` and
`∂²_σ f(2σ) = 4 f″(2σ)`. (b) `log L_ν(x)` is the cumulant generating function of `t = log n` under `μ_x` up to sign:
`(d/dx) log L_ν = −E_{μ_x}[t]`, `(d²/dx²) log L_ν = Var_{μ_x}(t) > 0` (`μ_x` is not a point mass). (c) Near `x = 1`,
`−ζ′/ζ(x) = 1/(x−1) + γ_E + O(x−1)`, so `log(−ζ′/ζ(x)) = −log(x−1) + O(x−1)`, and its second derivative is
`(x−1)^{−2} + O(1)`; put `x = 2σ`. The Szegő GAF of `Ω` has `K(s,s) = 1/(2σ−1)` and density `(1/π)(2σ−1)^{−2}` by the
same formula. (d) is read off from (a)–(b). ∎

*Reading (all `PROVED` above; this paragraph is the reading).* `Y` is the honest realisation of the brief's "random
operator built from the primes, positivity by construction": it is positive (a covariance), it lives **exactly on the
critical half-plane**, it is flow-invariant, and it reads the exact `Λ(n)`. Its zeros accumulate on `Re s = ½` with

formula" that the brief asks for would require the covariance to be continued to `Re(s + w̄) ≤ 1`, and Theorem 1(b)
says this is impossible as a covariance. Props R/R′ of round 1 apply to the zero process of `Y` as well (absolutely
continuous intensity ⇒ it hits no fixed point with positive probability). **The angle's canonical object is
constructed, positive, unconditional, and blind.**

## §5 What is genuinely new, and what is not

**New at record level (literature novelty `HEURISTIC`):**
1. **Theorem 1(b)–(c) + Theorem 2 + Theorem 3**: a positive covariance built from `Λ ≥ 0` in the horizontal
  (Laplace/Widder) shape is Landau-capped at `Re z = 1`; the pole cannot be subtracted, and no correction of Laplace
  abscissa `< 1` — the Γ-factor, `1/z`, any signed auxiliary — restores positivity: the positive-measure structure
  forces density `≥ 1` between prime powers, which is the pole. Together with Theorem 4 (vertical shape = Weil, Lemma
  T) this is a **two-direction closure of "positivity by construction from the primes"**: blind or equivalent.
2. **Prop 3.1–3.2**: the `Λ`-Gaussian Dirichlet series `Y`, the canonical random object of the angle, exists exactly
  on `Re s > ½`, is flow-invariant with covariance `−ζ′/ζ(s + w̄)`, has zero intensity `π^{−1}Var_{μ_{2σ}}(log n)`
  under the `Λ`-Gibbs state, is asymptotically the Bergman-determinantal process near `Re s = ½`, and is blind to the
  nontrivial zeros. An explicit, constructed, positive, unconditional object that passes OT-3/OT-5′/OT-6/OT-7/OT-8
  and fails OT-2/OT-4/OT-12 — the cleanest illustration in the corpus of "OT-5′ without OT-2 is worthless".
3. **Remark 4.1**: the Herglotz criterion has no horizontal (kernel) form even under RH, since `ξ′/ξ` is log-concave
  at infinity; the two positivity directions never meet.

**Not new / reformulation:**
* Theorem 1(a) is the standard reproducing-kernel structure of a weighted Dirichlet–Hardy space.
* The engine of Theorems 1(b), 2, 3 is Landau's theorem (P47 (X20)); the *inequality* form of the same engine is
  B9_R2 Thm 1 (RH ⟺ one-sided von Koch, EQUIV). The covariance form proved here is a **no-go**, not an equivalence.
* Theorem 4 is round-1 Prop C recalled.
* Props R/R′ (round 1) already showed randomisation is inert; §3 does not change that, it only exhibits the object.
