## §1 Setting (recalled from R4 §1, unchanged)

`(S, 𝓕, g, φ, μ) ∈ DK_α` means the following. `S` is a second-countable locally compact foliated space with oriented
leaves of dimension `d ≥ 2`. `g` is a leafwise metric. `φ^t` maps leaves to leaves and is conformal,
`φ^{t*}g = e^{αt}g` on `T𝓕`. `μ` is a holonomy-invariant transverse Radon measure with
`μ(φ^tτ) = e^{−α_d t}μ(τ)`, where `α_d = dα/2` (R4 Lemma 1.2(b)). Also `m = vol_g ⊗ μ` is `φ`-invariant.

`W_μ := W(S, 𝓕; [m])` is the von Neumann algebra of the holonomy groupoid restricted to `supp μ`, with the measure
class of `m` (Connes, *Sur la théorie non commutative de l'intégration*, LNM 725 (1979); CITED (std.)). `τ_μ` is the
normal semifinite faithful trace defined by the invariant `μ` (ibid.). `θ_t(A) := U_t A U_t^*` is the automorphism
induced by `φ^t`, where `U_t` is the canonical unitary between leafwise `L²` half-density spaces. This is the
construction in Leichtnam, Prop 2(4).

**Lemma 1.1 (trace scaling; `PROVED`).** `τ_μ∘θ_t = e^{−α_d t}τ_μ`.
*Proof.* For `A ≥ 0` in `W_μ` with leafwise kernel `k_L(x,y)`, a half-density on `L × L`, the trace is
`τ_μ(A) = ∫ (∫_{L} k_L(x,x)) dμ(L)`. Here the inner integral is the density `k_L(x,x)` integrated over plaques, and
`μ` integrates transversally in any flow box (Connes 1979, §§I–II). Conjugation by `U_t` carries `k_L` to
`(φ^t × φ^t)_*k_L` on the leaf `φ^tL`. The diagonal density is transported with no Jacobian, since half-densities
are natural. So the inner integrals agree, and the transverse integration is against `φ^t_*μ = e^{−α_d t}μ` up to
the sign convention. Leichtnam proves the same identity for his laminations (`τ_Λ∘(φ^t)_* = e^{−t}τ_Λ`). ∎

## §2 Theorem R: the III₁ law

**Lemma 2.1 (`dim H̄^d = 1` ⟹ ergodic; `PROVED`).** Let `S` be compact, or more generally assume the trace is
`C_μ` on `H̄^d_c`, the reduced leafwise cohomology of compactly supported forms. Suppose
`dim H̄^d_c = 1`, spanned by the class of `vol_g`, or by the class of a form `f·vol_g` with `f ∈ C_c` positive on a
complete transversal's saturation. Then `μ` is ergodic: every saturated Borel set `A` has `μ(A) = 0` or
`μ(A^c) = 0`.
*Proof.* Let `A` be saturated and Borel, and put `μ_A := μ|_A`. It is holonomy-invariant, because `A` is saturated,
and Radon, because `μ_A ≤ μ`. As in R4 Lemma 1.2(a), its current `C_{μ_A}` vanishes on leafwise-exact compactly
supported forms (Stokes on plaques plus invariance) and is continuous, so it factors through `H̄^d_c`. Since
`dim H̄^d_c = 1`, we get `C_{μ_A} = c·C_μ` for some `c ∈ R`. Evaluate on `f·vol_g` for `f ∈ C_c(S)`, `f ≥ 0`:
`∫_A f dm = c∫ f dm` for all such `f`. So `m|_A = c·m`, hence `c ∈ {0,1}`, and either `m(A) = 0` or `m(A^c) = 0`.
Since `m = vol⊗μ` and `A` is saturated, the same holds for `μ`. ∎

**Theorem R (`PROVED`; the operator-algebra inputs are CITED).** Let `(S,𝓕,g,φ,μ) ∈ DK_α` with `α ≠ 0` and `μ`
ergodic, which holds for instance under Lemma 2.1. Then:
(i) `W_μ` is a factor of type II_∞.
(ii) `θ` is a trace-scaling flow on it: `τ_μ∘θ_t = e^{−α_d t}τ_μ`.
(iii) `N := W_μ ⋊_θ R` is a factor of type **III₁**. Its Connes invariant is `S(N) = [0,∞)`, and its flow of weights
is trivial.
(iv) (i)–(iii) do not depend on the closed orbits, on their lengths, or on the local germs of R4 Theorem M.
Removing all closed orbits and fixed points leaves `N` unchanged up to isomorphism.

*Proof.* (i) The centre of `W_μ` is `L^∞` of the `μ`-measurable saturated sets (Connes 1979, CITED (std.)), which
is `C` by ergodicity. `τ_μ` is a faithful normal semifinite trace, so `W_μ` is semifinite.

`W_μ` is not of type I. A type-I factor of this kind forces the measure class to be carried by one leaf `L`, i.e.
`μ` is atomic on `L ∩ τ` for transversals `τ`. Every holonomy-invariant atom on `L` has equal mass. Then
`μ(φ^t{x}) = e^{−α_d t}μ({x}) > 0` forces `φ^tL = L` for all `t`. But `hol(φ^tx) ∈ L ∩ τ` carries both the mass
`μ({x})` (invariance along `L`) and the mass `e^{−α_d t}μ({x})`. With `α_d ≠ 0` this is a contradiction.

`W_μ` is not of type II₁. If `τ_μ(1) < ∞`, Lemma 1.1 gives `τ_μ(1) = τ_μ(θ_t1) = e^{−α_d t}τ_μ(1)`, a
contradiction. So `W_μ` is II_∞.
(ii) This is Lemma 1.1.
(iii) Take `M` a II_∞ factor and `θ` an action of `R` that scales the trace, `τ∘θ_t = e^{−t}τ` (rescale `t` by
`α_d`). Then `M ⋊_θ R` is a factor of type III, and its flow of weights is the restriction of `θ` to `Z(M)`. This is
Connes–Takesaki, *The flow of weights on factors of type III*, Tôhoku Math. J. 29 (1977), together with Takesaki
duality (Takesaki, *Theory of Operator Algebras II*, Ch. XII, CITED (std.)). The same computation is the one
Leichtnam cites from Connes (1994), pp. 473, 494–495, in his Prop 2. Here `Z(M) = C`, so the flow of weights is the
trivial flow on a point, i.e. type III₁. Equivalently,
`S(N) ∩ R_{>0} = {λ : θ_{log λ} acts trivially on Z(M)} = R_{>0}`.
(iv) R4 Theorem M(i) and the argument in (i) show that the saturation of the closed orbits and of the leaves through
fixed points is `μ`-null. `W_μ` depends only on the measure class. ∎

**Remark R2 (answer to the panel's question; `PROVED`).** The panel expected (HEURISTIC, G1_P4_R4:89) that germs of
modulus `p^{−1}` at every `p` would force the ratio set up to `closure⟨p⟩ = [0,∞)`. Theorem R shows III₁ comes from
the **single pole**, that is from `dim H̄^d = 1` via ergodicity, and not from the germs, which are `μ`-null. Hence:
(a) no type obstruction to (E-orb) exists;
(b) III₁ is compatible with any orbit lengths, including none at all, two primes only, or Beurling lengths;
(c) consistency: the Bost–Connes system at `β = 1` is III₁ (Bost–Connes, Selecta Math. 1 (1995), the theorem on
uniqueness of KMS_β for `β ≤ 1` and its type; CITED (std.), confirmed by G1_P3_R4:261).

**Corollary R1 (circle-fibred models; `PROVED`).** Let `π: S → R/cZ` (`c > 0`) be Borel, constant on leaves, with
`π(φ^tx) = π(x) + t`. Then:
(i) `μ` is not ergodic;
(ii) the functionals `C_{μ|π^{−1}(I)}`, for disjoint arcs `I`, are linearly independent on `H̄^d_c`, so
`dim H̄^d_c = ∞`;
(iii) if the ergodic components are given by the fibres (as in Leichtnam), `W_μ ⋊ R` is III_λ with
`λ = e^{−α_d c}`.
*Proof.* (i) `π` is constant on leaves, so each `π^{−1}(I)` is saturated. Suppose `μ` gave full measure to one
fibre `π^{−1}(c₀)`. Take a transversal `τ` with `μ(τ) > 0` inside that fibre. Then `φ^tτ ⊂ π^{−1}(c₀+t)` is `μ`-null
for `t ∉ cZ`, yet `μ(φ^tτ) = e^{−α_d t}μ(τ) > 0`. A standard Borel argument then produces an arc `I` whose preimage
has measure strictly between 0 and full. (If every arc had preimage of measure 0 or full, the image measure on
`R/cZ` would be 0–1 valued on arcs, hence a Dirac mass, which was just excluded.)
(ii) Test on `f·vol_g` with `f` supported in `π^{−1}(I)`. This separates the currents, and each factors through
`H̄^d_c` as in Lemma 2.1.
(iii) The centre is `L^∞(R/cZ)` and `θ` rotates it with period `c`. A periodic flow of weights of period
`−log λ` means type III_λ (Connes–Takesaki 1977, CITED (std.)). ∎

## §3 Theorem U: measured universality

**Hypothesis (Am).** The measured holonomy groupoid of `(S∖Fix, 𝓕)` with the class `[m]` is amenable. Equivalently
`W_μ` is injective (Connes 1979; Connes–Feldman–Weiss 1981; CITED (std.)). (Am) holds, for example, when `μ`-a.e.
leaf has subexponential growth (Anantharaman-Delaroche–Renault, *Amenable groupoids*, CITED (std.)). This covers the
flat leaves `C` that R4 Theorem H forces through the closed orbits.

**Theorem U (`PROVED` from CITED).** Let `(S_i, …, μ_i)`, `i = 1, 2`, be ergodic DK systems with `α_{d,i} ≠ 0` that
satisfy (Am). Normalise time by `t ↦ α_{d,i}t`. Then:
(i) `W_{μ_i} ≅ R_{0,1}`, the injective II_∞ factor (Connes, Ann. Math. 104 (1976), CITED (std.)), and
`N_i ≅ R_∞`, the injective III₁ factor (Haagerup, Acta Math. 158 (1987), CITED (std.)).
(ii) The pairs `(W_{μ_i} ⊗ B(L²R), θ^i ⊗ Ad λ)` are conjugate to each other, and to the canonical core flow
`(R_∞ ⋊_σ R, σ̂)`.
(iii) The same holds for the Bost–Connes system at `β = 1`, after the identification of its core as
`L^∞(A_f × R_{>0}) ⋊ Q^×_{>0}` with scaling flow. This is the Maharam extension of `(A_f, Q^×_{>0}, Haar)`,
computed in §4.1.

*Proof.* (i) `W_{μ_i}` is an injective II_∞ factor by Theorem R(i) and (Am), which gives `R_{0,1}` (Connes).
`N_i = W_{μ_i} ⋊ R` is injective, since a crossed product of an injective algebra by an amenable group is injective
(std.), and it is III₁ by Theorem R. Haagerup's theorem then gives `R_∞`.
(ii) Takesaki duality gives `N_i ⋊_{θ̂} R ≅ W_{μ_i} ⊗ B(L²R)` with bidual action `≅ θ^i ⊗ Ad λ`. The dual weight
`τ̃` of `τ_{μ_i}` on `N_i` has modular group `σ^{τ̃}_s = θ̂_s`: it fixes `W_{μ_i}` pointwise and multiplies `λ(t)`
by `e^{−ist}`, because of the trace scaling. So `N_i ⋊_{θ̂} R` is the core of `N_i` with its dual flow. The core
with its dual flow is a conjugacy invariant of `N_i` (Connes cocycle theorem; Connes–Takesaki; CITED (std.)).
Since `N_1 ≅ N_2`, the two cores with flows are conjugate.
(iii) The BC factor is injective (an abelian crossed product) and III₁ (Remark R2(c)). Then apply (ii). ∎

**Corollary U1 (filter F-meas; `PROVED`).** Let `Φ` be any quantity attached to a DK system that is invariant
under stable conjugacy of `(W_μ, τ_μ, θ)`. Examples are the spectrum of `θ` on `L²(W_μ, τ_μ)`, any trace-scaling
invariant, and the Connes–Takesaki modules. Then `Φ` takes **the same value** on every ergodic injective DK system.
This includes a hypothetical ζ-object and every Beurling-type or formal `Ξ_a`-type DK system that is ergodic and
injective. So `Φ` cannot carry the sign. This is consistent with `cor:void` and with R158_001 OT-5.
In particular, the prime lengths and the moduli `p^{−1}` of R4 Theorem M are invisible to `(W_μ, τ_μ, θ)`: they live
on the `μ`-null orbit set (R4 Theorem M(i)).

**Remark U2 (where the arithmetic must live; `HEURISTIC`).** Deninger's positive form lives on `Ĥ¹_μ`: the
`L²(m)`-closure of leafwise harmonic 1-forms, with `Θ` generating `e^{−αt/2}φ^{t*}`. By U1, the spectrum of `Θ` on
`Ĥ¹_μ` must be decided by data outside `(W_μ, τ_μ, θ)`. Two candidates remain:
* the leafwise Hodge projection `P_harm` as an element of `W_μ ⊗ M_2` together with its position relative to `θ`;
* the topology or smooth structure of `S` along the `μ`-null set of closed orbits.
Any claim that measure-theoretic transverse data (ratio sets, modular data, or types) encode `Λ` is **excluded by
Theorem U** in the injective case.

## §4 The natural gluing candidate and its failure

### 4.1 The Maharam model (`PROVED`, elementary)

Take the nonsingular system `(A_f, Q^×_{>0}, Haar)`. Multiplication by `q` scales Haar by `|q|_f = q^{−1}`, so the
Radon–Nikodym cocycle is `q^{−1}`. The Maharam extension is `A_f × R_{>0}` with `q·(a,x) = (qa, qx)` and the
invariant measure `Haar ⊗ dx`. Invariance is the product formula `|q|_f·|q|_∞ = 1`. The scaling flow `x ↦ e^{−t}x`
multiplies the measure by `e^{−t}`. Dividing by the compact group `Ẑ^×` gives
`T := (A_f/Ẑ^×) × R_{>0}`, with `ν := ` pushforward of Haar. Up to the closure at `x = 0`, this is the point set of
the Connes–Consani scaling site, `Q^×_{>0} \ (A_f/Ẑ^× × R_{>0})` (CITED, 1507.05818, abstract level).
* `Q^×_{>0}` acts **freely** on `T`, because the coordinate `x` forces `q = 1`.
* `q·[a] = [a]` holds ⟺ `a_p = 0` for every prime `p | q`. So the stabiliser of `[a]` in the `A_f/Ẑ^×`-coordinate
  is the group generated by the primes `p` with `a_p = 0`.

### 4.2 The suspension class `Σ`

**Definition.** An **abelian scaling-site suspension** consists of:
* a connected surface `L` with a `Q^×_{>0}`-action `q·z`;
* a continuous family of metrics `g_{[a]}` on `L`.
From these, set `S := (L × T)/Q^×_{>0}` with the diagonal action, leaves the images of `L × {t}`, flow
`φ^t[z,[a],x] = [z,[a],e^{−t}x]`, leafwise metric `g = x^{−α}g_{[a]}`, and transverse measure `ν ⊗ dx`.

**Lemma 4.1 (`PROVED`).** The data define a DK_α datum, in the sense that every axiom except the local-compactness /
Hausdorff condition holds, if and only if `q^*g_{q[a]} = q^{α}g_{[a]}`. In that case:
(i) the flow is conformal with factor `e^{αt}`, and `μ = ν⊗dx` is invariant and scaled by `e^{−t}`, so `α_d = 1`;
(ii) closed orbits are exactly the points `[z₀,[a],x]` with `z₀` fixed by some `q ≠ 1` in `Stab[a]`, and they have
length `log q`;
(iii) at a closed orbit with `q = p^k`, the slice modulus is `p^{−k}`, as in R4 Theorem M;
(iv) every leaf is `L`, because the action on `T` is free.
*Proof.* Conformality: `φ^{t*}(x^{−α}g) = e^{αt}x^{−α}g`. `Q`-invariance of `g`:
`q^*((qx)^{−α}g_{q[a]}) = x^{−α}g_{[a]}` ⟺ `q^*g_{q[a]} = q^{α}g_{[a]}`. The measure statements are §4.1.
Closed orbits: `φ^t P = q·P` means `(z,[a],e^{−t}x) = (q·z, q[a], qx)`, so `q = e^{−t}`, `q[a] = [a]` and `q·z = z`.
The modulus is Haar's `|p^k|_p`. ∎

So `Σ` realises R4's normal form at every prime at once: orbits of length `k log p` at `{a_p = 0}`, modulus `p^{−k}`,
and one transverse measure scaled by `e^{−t}`. Adding `x = 0` supplies the flow's fixed locus (OT-12). This is the
first candidate for the R4 §5 gluing.

### 4.3 Proposition NH

**Proposition NH (`PROVED`).** Let `S ∈ Σ`, and suppose `(L, g_{[a]})` is complete for some `[a]` with at least two
zero coordinates `a_ℓ = a_{ℓ′} = 0` (`ℓ ≠ ℓ′` primes). Such `[a]` are dense in `A_f/Ẑ^×`. Then the quotient `S` is
**not T₁**, hence not Hausdorff, and not a foliated space in the sense of Moore–Schochet. The failure happens at
every point `[z,[a],x]` over such `[a]`, including the closed-orbit locus.

*Proof.* `H := ⟨ℓ, ℓ′⟩ ≅ Z²` fixes `[a]`. By Lemma 4.1 its elements act on `(L, g_{[a]})` as commuting homotheties
with ratio `q^{α/2} ≠ 1`. By R4 Lemma H0 and Theorem H(iii), `L ≅ C` isometrically, and `H` acts by
`z ↦ λ_q z` about a common fixed point 0, with `|λ_q| = q^{α/2}`.

The map `q ↦ λ_q` is an injective homomorphism `H → C^×`. Its image is not discrete. A discrete subgroup of `C^×`
lifts under `exp` to a discrete subgroup of `C` containing `2πiZ`. Here the lift contains `2πi` and lifts of
`λ_ℓ, λ_{ℓ′}`, whose real parts `(α/2)log ℓ` and `(α/2)log ℓ′` are rationally independent. So the lift is free of
rank 3 in `C ≅ R²`, and a rank-3 subgroup of `R²` is never discrete. Hence there are `q_n ∈ H∖{1}` with
`λ_{q_n} → 1`, and therefore `q_n → 1` in `R_{>0}`.

For `P = (z,[a],x)` this gives `q_n·P = (λ_{q_n}z, [a], q_nx) → P`. Let `H̄` be the closure of the image of `H` in
`C^× × R_{>0}`, embedded via `q ↦ (λ_q, q)`. It is a non-discrete closed subgroup, hence uncountable. The orbit
`Q^×_{>0}·P` is countable. So there is `P′ = (wz,[a],sx)` with `(w,s) ∈ H̄` and `P′ ∉ Q^×_{>0}·P`. Every
neighbourhood of `P′` contains some `q·P`, so in the quotient `[P′] ∈ closure{[P]}` with `[P′] ≠ [P]`. (For `z = 0`
take `P′ = (0,[a],sx)` with `s ∉ Q_{>0}x/x`; the closure is dense because `H` is dense in `R_{>0}`.)

Density of such `[a]`: a basic open set of `A_f/Ẑ^×` constrains only finitely many places and allows `a_ℓ = 0` at the
others. ∎

**Remark NH1 (removing the bad set fails; `PROVED`).** The set `B` of `[a]` with ≥ 2 zero coordinates is a countable
union of closed sets and is dense. The complement `T∖B` is dense but not open, so it is not locally compact (a
locally compact dense subspace of a locally compact Hausdorff space is open). A lamination with transversal `T∖B`
therefore fails local compactness. Every point of `B` lies on closed orbits of **two** lengths `log ℓ` and `log ℓ′`,
since `z = 0` is fixed by both. So in `Σ` the locus where two primes meet, which is forced by density, is exactly
where the space breaks.

**Remark NH2 (what remains of `Σ`; `HEURISTIC` plus A17).** Treated as a non-commutative space, `S` is the groupoid
`(C × T̄) ⋊ Q^×_{>0}`. Its space of leaves is `Q^×_{>0} \ T̄ = A_Q/(Q^×·Ẑ^×)` modulo sign. This is the adèle class
space modulo `Ẑ^×`, where R158_002 Lemma A excludes leafwise `H¹` and the global trace formula is RH-equivalent
(A17). Whether a non-commutative leafwise Hodge theory on this groupoid gives anything beyond Connes' framework is
**OPEN**. I expect it does not (`HEURISTIC`).

**Scope of NH (honest limits).** NH covers product actions on `L × T` with the flow acting on `x` only, and with
complete `(L, g_{[a]})` over a dense set. It does **not** cover:
* skew suspensions with a leafwise renormalisation flow `ψ_x^t` (Leichtnam's (12));
* incomplete leaves;
* transversals other than the scaling-site point set.
The residual (E-orb′) below is OPEN.

## §5 The residual, sharpened

**(E-orb′) (`OPEN`, precise).** Find a second-countable, **locally compact Hausdorff** DK_1 lamination `S` with
`dim H̄⁰ = dim H̄² = 1`. By Theorem R it is III₁ automatically. It must have closed orbits of length `k log p` for
every `p`, with the R4 normal form, and a Lefschetz formula whose orbit side is ζ's explicit formula. The constraints
of record are:
* R4 Theorem M: orbits are `μ`-null, slice modulus `p^{−1}`;
* R4 Cor M1: not of codimension one;
* R4 Theorem H: flat complete leaves through orbits, one periodic point per leaf;
* Theorem U: the measured data are universal, so the arithmetic lives in topology and Hodge data;
* Prop NH: not an abelian product suspension over the scaling site with complete leaves.

The sharp sub-question left by NH: **can closed orbits of two rationally independent lengths accumulate on each other
inside a locally compact Hausdorff lamination without forcing a non-discrete homothety group on one complete leaf?**
In `Σ` the answer is no. In general it is OPEN. A positive answer needs either incomplete leaves at such loci, which
means leaving R4 Theorem H and approaching Leichtnam's §4.4 singular structure, or non-abelian holonomy mixing the
primes.

## §7 What is new, and the weakest step

**New (to the corpus; literature status noted).**
1. **Theorem R with Lemma 2.1**: Deninger's `dim H̄^d = 1` gives ergodicity, which gives III₁, independently of the
  orbits. The operator-algebra steps are classical (Connes–Takesaki). The implication from Deninger's cohomological
  axiom is not in Deninger, Leichtnam or Kim as far as I found. Leichtnam proves the III_{1/q} case directly.
2. **Corollary R1** and the pole/type dictionary (Remark R3). This turns Connes' char-0 versus char-`p` type heuristic
  into a statement about Deninger systems.
3. **Theorem U** and filter **F-meas** (U1): measured universality in the injective case. The ingredients are all
  classical. Its use as a filter against arithmetic content in transverse measured data is new.
4. **Class `Σ`** (the scaling-site suspension, which realises the full R4 §5 normal form) and **Proposition NH** (it is
  not T₁), with Remark NH1.
5. The panel's ratio-set question is answered: there is no obstruction, and there is no information.

**Not claimed.** No object, no trace formula, no sign at any window, nothing for `T > 0.8`, nothing for the "for all
`T`" arrow. Not an RH-equivalence.

**Weakest steps.**
* **Theorem U(ii)** leans on several CITED (std.) results that I did not re-read this phase: Connes 1976, Haagerup
  1987, Connes–Takesaki 1977, and the identification of the modular group of the dual weight with the dual action. It
  also needs hypothesis (Am), which is proved for no ζ-candidate.
* **Lemma 2.1** needs the trace to be `C_μ` on `H̄^d_c` and needs test forms `f·vol` to be admissible. This is true in
  Deninger's compact setting, but in a non-compact or singular setting (Leichtnam §4.4) the right cohomology may
  differ.
* **Theorem R(i)**, the type-I exclusion, uses the standard fact that a type-I foliation factor is carried by one leaf
  (Connes 1979), not re-proved here.
* **Proposition NH** is limited to product suspensions with complete leaves. Skew suspensions, which carry
  Leichtnam's renormalisation flow, are not covered.

**Suggested next step.** The Deninger angle now reduces to the topological question in §5: can
closed orbits of two independent lengths accumulate inside a locally compact Hausdorff conformal lamination? The
first test is Leichtnam's skew suspensions with ψ-flows over two primes `{ℓ, ℓ′}`, where the homothety group on the
leaf need not be abelian. If that is also non-Hausdorff, (E-orb) should be recorded as
**closed for complete leaves**. The only residue would then be the singular (incomplete-leaf) archimedean structure.
