## §1 Setting (recalled from R3, with what is used)

* **Translation invariance (PROVED, R3 §1).** `W(G) := W_T(G)` for any window with `supp G ⊂ I_T` is well
  defined. Under `(τ_xG)(t) = G(t+x)` it is invariant: `Φ_{τ_xG}(s) = e^{−(s−½)x}Φ_G(s)`, so each term
  `Φ_G(ρ)Φ_G(1−ρ)` is invariant. The same holds termwise in `eq:W`: `c_{τ_xG} = c_G`, and
  `A(τ_xG)B(τ_xG) = e^{−x/2}A·e^{x/2}B`. Also `A(τ_xG) = e^{−x/2}A(G)` and `B(τ_xG) = e^{x/2}B(G)`, so
  translation preserves `P := {A = B = 0}`.
* **Tests give upper bounds on bottoms.** A real `G ∈ C_c^∞` whose support is a compact subset of a window of half
  length `T` (after centring) lies in the form domain. So `λ^W_T ≤ W(G)/‖G‖²`, and if `G ∈ P` also
  `λ^A_T ≤ 𝔞_T(G)/‖G‖² = W(G)/‖G‖²` (on `P_T`, `𝔞_T = W_T` by `eq:W`). **No form-core statement is needed this
  round.** This removes R3's weakest step "§1 form core" from everything below.
* **Shift function (R3 Lemma B0, PROVED).** For real `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)` put `c_ρ := c_ρ(F) := Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`,
  `w_ρ := ρ − ½ = a_ρ + iγ_ρ`. Then
  ```
  β_F(u) := W_bil(F, τ_uF) = Σ_ρ c_ρ cosh(w_ρu), W(F + bτ_uF) = (1+b²)W(F) + 2bβ_F(u) (b ∈ ℝ). (1.1)
  ```
  The sum runs over zeros with multiplicity and converges absolutely and locally uniformly. Also `c_{1−ρ} = c_ρ`,
  `c_{ρ̄} = conj c_ρ`, `β_F` is real, even and continuous, and `S_F := Σ_ρ|c_ρ| < ∞`. Since `|Φ_F(½+a+iγ)| ≤ C_j(1+|γ|)^{−j}`
  uniformly for `|a| ≤ ½`, even `Σ_ρ|c_ρ|(1+|γ_ρ|) < ∞`.
* **Two elementary bounds (PROVED).** `|cosh(x+iy)|² = sinh²x + cos²y ≤ cosh²x` and `|sinh(x+iy)| ≤ cosh x`. Hence
  ```
  |β_F(u)| ≤ S_F cosh(Θ₀u) ≤ S_F e^{Θ₀|u|}, |β′_F(u)| ≤ S′_F e^{|u|/2}, Θ₀ := sup_ρ|a_ρ| ≤ ½, S′_F := Σ|c_ρ||w_ρ|. (1.2)
  ```
  Termwise differentiation is justified by locally uniform convergence of the differentiated series.
* **Prime-side form of `β_F` (PROVED).** For real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` and `u ≥ 0`,
  ```
  β_F(u) = 2A(F)B(F)cosh(u/2) − Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n)n^{−½}[c_F(log n − u) + c_F(log n + u)] + ε_F(u),
  ε_F(u) := (1/2π)∫ m(τ)|F̂(τ)|²cos(τu)dτ → 0 (u → ∞). (1.3)
  ```
  *Proof.* Apply `eq:form`/`eq:W` to `G = F + τ_uF` on a window containing its support. Then
  `(τ_uF)^(τ) = e^{−iτu}F̂(τ)`, so the cross term of `|Ĝ|²` is `2|F̂|²cos(τu)`. Using
  `(1/2π)∫cos(τx)|F̂|²dτ = c_F(x)`, the comb gives `−Σ_n v_n·½[c_F(log n−u) + c_F(log n+u)]`. The polar part is
  `2A(G)B(G) = 2AB(1+e^{−u/2})(1+e^{u/2})`, whose cross part is `2AB·2cosh(u/2)`. Halving the cross part gives
  (1.3). The contacts needed are `log n < u + 2T₀`, which the window `T₀ + u/2` contains. For
  `ε_F → 0`: `m(τ) = O(log(2+|τ|))` and `F̂` is Schwartz, so `m|F̂|² ∈ L¹`; apply Riemann–Lebesgue. For
  `u > 2T₀` the terms `c_F(log n + u)` vanish. ∎

## §2 Lemma Λ (Landau lemma) and Lemma E (good tests)

**Lemma Λ (PROVED; uses CITED Landau's theorem).** Let `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)` be real, `κ ∈ [0,1]`, `C ≥ 0`, and suppose
`β_F(u) ≥ −Ce^{κu/2}` for all `u ≥ 0`, or `β_F(u) ≤ Ce^{κu/2}` for all `u ≥ 0`. Then `c_ρ(F) = 0` for every zero
`ρ` with `|Re ρ − ½| > κ/2`.

*Proof.* Take the lower bound; for the upper bound replace `β_F` by `−β_F` and `G` by `−G` below.
* *Step 1 (the meromorphic function).* Put `G(z) := Σ_ρ c_ρ z/(z² − w_ρ²)`. Let `K ⊂ ℂ` be compact. For
  `|γ_ρ| ≥ R_K` we have `|w_ρ²| ≥ γ_ρ² − ¼`, so `|z² − w_ρ²| ≥ 1` on `K`, and the tail is dominated by `S_F`.
  The finitely many remaining terms are rational. So `G` is meromorphic on `ℂ`, and its poles lie in `{±w_ρ}`.
  Take a zero with `a₀ := a_{ρ₀} ≠ 0` and multiplicity `m`. The zeros with `w_ρ² = w₀²` are exactly `ρ₀` and
  `1−ρ₀`: `w_ρ = ±w₀`, and `ρ̄₀` has `w² = conj(w₀²) ≠ w₀²` because `a₀γ₀ ≠ 0`. Each of the two terms has residue
  `½` at `z = w₀`, and `c_{1−ρ₀} = c_{ρ₀}`. So `Res_{z=w₀}G = m·c_{ρ₀}`. **`G` has a genuine pole at `w₀` iff
  `c_{ρ₀} ≠ 0`.** All poles are non-real, since `γ_ρ ≠ 0` (indeed `|γ_ρ| > 14`). `G` is holomorphic at every real
  point.
* *Step 2 (Laplace transform).* For `Re z > ½`, `∫_0^∞ e^{−zu}cosh(w_ρu)du = z/(z² − w_ρ²)`, and Fubini applies
  because `Σ_ρ|c_ρ|∫_0^∞ e^{−(Re z−½)u}du < ∞` by (1.2). So `∫_0^∞ e^{−zu}β_F(u)du = G(z)` on `Re z > ½`.
* *Step 3 (Landau).* Put `f(u) := β_F(u) + Ce^{κu/2} ≥ 0`, continuous. Its Laplace integral `L(z)` converges for
  `Re z > ½`, where it equals `H(z) := G(z) + C/(z − κ/2)`. Let `σ_c ≤ ½` be its abscissa of convergence and
  suppose `σ_c > κ/2`. `L` is holomorphic on `{Re z > σ_c}`, `H` is meromorphic, and they agree on `Re z > ½`, so
  they agree on `{Re z > σ_c}` minus the discrete pole set of `H`. At such a point `L` is holomorphic, so there is
  no pole there, and `L = H` on the whole half-plane. `σ_c` is real and `≠ κ/2`, and `G` is holomorphic at real
  points, so `H` is holomorphic on a disc `D ∋ σ_c`. Then `L` and `H|_D` glue to a holomorphic continuation of `L`
  to a neighbourhood of `σ_c`. Apply Landau's theorem to `α(t) = ∫_0^t f`, which is nondecreasing: `σ_c` is
  singular for `L`, a contradiction. So `σ_c ≤ κ/2`, `L` is holomorphic on `Re z > κ/2`, and `L = H` there. Hence
  `G` has no poles in `Re z > κ/2`. By Step 1, `c_ρ = 0` whenever `Re w_ρ > κ/2`. A zero with `a_ρ < −κ/2` has
  `1−ρ` with `a = −a_ρ > κ/2`, and `c_{1−ρ} = c_ρ`. ∎

**Lemma E (good tests; PROVED).** Let `ρ₀` be a zero with `a₀ ≠ 0` and `T₀ > 0`. There is a real
`F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` with `c_{ρ₀}(F) ≠ 0` in each of the following classes: (i) arbitrary; (ii) `F ∈ P_{T₀}`, i.e.
`A(F) = B(F) = 0`; (iii) `F ≥ 0`.

*Proof.* The functionals are `ℓ_±(F) := ∫F(t)e^{±w₀t}dt` (complex-valued on real `F`), with `c_{ρ₀} = ℓ_+ℓ_−`.
The four exponentials `e^{±t/2}, e^{±w₀t}` are distinct (`w₀ ∉ {0, ±½}`), so they are linearly independent as
distributions on `I_{T₀}`.
* (ii) Suppose `ℓ_+ = 0` on real `F` with `A = B = 0`. `A` and `B` are real functionals, so the complexified
  kernel of `(A,B)` is spanned by real kernel elements, and `ℓ_+` vanishes on it. Linear algebra (a functional
  vanishing on `ker A ∩ ker B` lies in `span{A,B}`) then gives `e^{w₀t} ∈ span{e^{t/2}, e^{−t/2}}` on `I_{T₀}`,
  which is false. So `ker ℓ_+` and `ker ℓ_−` are proper real subspaces of the real space `P_{T₀} ∩ C_c^∞`. A real
  vector space is not a union of two proper subspaces, so pick `F` outside both.
* (i) Same argument, without `A, B`.
* (iii) Nonnegative `C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` functions span `C_c^∞(I_{T₀})`: write `G = (G + Kχ) − Kχ` with `χ ≥ 0`,
  `χ = 1` on `supp G`, and `K ≥ max|G|`. So there are `F₁, F₂ ≥ 0` with `ℓ_+(F₁) ≠ 0` and `ℓ_−(F₂) ≠ 0`. For
  `t > 0`, `ℓ_±(F₁ + tF₂)` are affine in `t`. The first is nonzero at `t = 0` and the second has nonzero slope, so
  each vanishes for at most one `t`. Pick `t > 0` avoiding both. ∎

## §3 Theorem Γ — the growth-rate law of the window bottoms (both forms)

**Theorem Γ (PROVED).** Let `Θ := sup_ρ Re ρ ∈ [½, 1]` and `κ ∈ [0,1]`.

(i) *(primitive form)* If `λ^A_T ≥ −Ce^{κT}` for all `T ≥ T₁`, then every zero satisfies `|Re ρ − ½| ≤ κ/2`, i.e.
`Θ ≤ ½ + κ/2`.

(ii) *(complete form)* The same with `λ^W_T` in place of `λ^A_T`. Since `λ^W_T ≤ λ^A_T` (P46 l.143), (i) implies
(ii).

(iii) *(trivial bound)* `λ^W_T ≥ C_0 − Σ_{n<e^{2T}}v_n − 4sinh T ≥ −C e^{T}` for `T ≥ 1`.

(iv) With `log⁺x := log max(x,1)` and `κ^• := limsup_{T→∞}T^{−1}log⁺(−λ^•_T)`:
```
2Θ − 1 ≤ κ^A ≤ κ^W ≤ 1, and RH ⟺ κ^A = 0 ⟺ κ^W = 0 ⟺ λ^A_T ≥ −e^{o(T)} ⟺ λ^W_T ≥ −e^{o(T)} .
```

*Proof.* (i) Suppose `ρ₀` has `|a₀| > κ/2`. Fix `T₀ > 0`. By Lemma E(ii) choose real `F ∈ P_{T₀} ∩ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})`
with `c_{ρ₀}(F) ≠ 0` and `‖F‖ = 1`. For `u ≥ 0`, the test `G_u := F + τ_uF` has support in `(−T₀−u, T₀)`. After
centring (translation by `u/2`) it lies in `P_{T₀+u/2} ∩ C_c^∞`, by §1. By §1 and (1.1),
```
λ^A_{T₀+u/2}·‖G_u‖² ≤ W(G_u) = 2W(F) + 2β_F(u).
```
`‖G_u‖² ≤ 4`. If `λ^A_{T₀+u/2} ≥ 0` the left side is `≥ 0`; otherwise it is `≥ 4λ^A_{T₀+u/2}`. For
`u ≥ u₁ := 2max(T₁ − T₀, 0)` this gives `β_F(u) ≥ −W(F) − 2Ce^{κT₀}e^{κu/2}`. On `[0,u₁]`, `β_F` is bounded. So
`β_F(u) ≥ −C′e^{κu/2}` on `u ≥ 0`, and Lemma Λ gives `c_{ρ₀}(F) = 0`, a contradiction.
(ii) is the same with Lemma E(i), `F` arbitrary real.
(iii) By `eq:Aform`, `𝔞_T(F) ≥ C_0‖F‖² + 0 − Σ_{n∈𝒩_T}v_n‖F‖²` (`Lev ≥ 0`, `|c_F| ≤ ‖F‖²`). By Cauchy–Schwarz,
`|A|,|B| ≤ ‖F‖(2sinh T)^{½}`, so `|2AB| ≤ 4sinh T‖F‖²`. Chebyshev gives `Σ_{n<x}Λ(n)n^{−½} ≤ C√x`.
(iv) The middle inequality holds because `λ^W ≤ λ^A`. The right one is (iii). For the left one: if
`κ^A < κ ≤ 1`, then `−λ^A_T ≤ e^{κT}` for large `T`, and (i) gives `Θ − ½ ≤ κ/2`. Let `κ ↓ κ^A`. RH ⟹ `λ ≥ 0` ⟹
`κ = 0`. `κ^A = 0` ⟹ `Θ = ½`. ∎

**Remark Γ1 (sharpness of the constant, calibration).** Put `κ = 1` in (i) and use (iii): this gives
`Θ ≤ 1`, which is exactly what the Euler product gives trivially. The exponent `2ε` is therefore calibrated. There
is no slack in the factor 2 at the trivial end. PROVED (it is (i) + (iii)).

**Remark Γ2 (quasi-RH is a necessary sub-target of any G2 mechanism).** Any argument that gives
`λ^A_T ≥ −Ce^{(1−η)T}` for one `η > 0` and all large `T` proves `Θ ≤ 1 − η/2`, a zero-free strip. That is quasi-RH,
open since 1859. So a G2 mechanism, weakened to its growth content, must beat de la Vallée Poussin by a power. This
is consistent with R157_002 row 21 and gives that row a quantitative form. PROVED as an implication.

**Remark Γ3 (A7/A8 of P47 §18 in this language).** A8 (nonnegative Euler atoms with `Σc(n) ≤ O(x)`) gives
`κ^W ≤ 1` via (iii). Theorem Γ(ii) then gives `Θ ≤ 1`, which is A7 in non-strict form. For Epstein `Ξ_Q` with
`h(−D) > 1`, Davenport–Heilbronn zeros with `Re s > 1` (P47 X21) force `κ > 1` for its window form. This is
possible only because A8 fails for `Ξ_Q`, consistent with P47 Thm 18.14. PROVED for `ξ`. For `Ξ_Q` it holds
provided Lemma Λ transfers, which needs A5 and translation invariance, both true for `Ξ_Q` by P47 Thm 18.8. I have
not re-checked the latter line by line, so for `Ξ_Q` it is HEURISTIC.

**OPEN Γ-rev (the direct graded arrow).** Is `κ^A ≤ 2Θ − 1`, i.e. does `Θ ≤ ½ + ε` imply
`λ^A_T ≥ −C_δe^{(2ε+δ)T}`? Under RH it is true (`λ^A_T ≥ 0`). In general the zero side does not obviously close:

## §4 Theorem Q — Q-M closed: one window, all (two-term) mollifiers, is RH

Recall (R3 Def. B1): `B_N(T₀)` means `W(F_M) ≥ 0` for all real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` and all real `M` of length
`≤ N`, where `F_M := Σ_n a_nn^{−½}τ_{log n}F`. `B_∞(T₀) := ∀N B_N(T₀)`. With `M = 1 + a n^{−s}`,
`F_M = F + an^{−½}τ_{log n}F`.

**Theorem Q (PROVED; complete form; EQUIVALENT).** Fix any `T₀ > 0`. The following are equivalent.
(a) RH.
(b) `B_∞(T₀)`.
(c) `W(F + τ_{log n}F) ≥ 0` for all real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` and all `n ≥ 1`, i.e. `M = 1 + n^{½−s}`.
(d) For every real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})`: `inf_{n≥1} W(F + τ_{log n}F) > −∞`.
(d⁺) For every real `F ≥ 0` in `C_c^∞(I_{T₀})`:
  `sup_{u≥0}[Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−½}c_F(log n − u) − 2A(F)B(F)cosh(u/2)] < ∞` (smoothed prime sum with a nonnegative kernel,
  one-sided).
(d⁻) The same with `inf > −∞` in place of `sup < ∞`.

*Proof.* (a)⟹(b): Weil (under RH every `W(G) ≥ 0`), or R3 Theorem B(i) (Schur). (b)⟹(c): `c` is the special
case `a_1 = 1`, `a_n = √n`. (c)⟹(d): trivial.
(d)⟹(a): by (1.1) with `b = 1` and `u = log n`, `β_F(log n) ≥ −W(F) − C_F/2`. For `u ∈ [log n, log(n+1)]`, by
(1.2), `|β_F(u) − β_F(log n)| ≤ S′_F√(n+1)·log(1+1/n) ≤ 2S′_F`. So `β_F(u) ≥ −C′_F` on `u ≥ 0`. Lemma Λ with
`κ = 0` gives `c_ρ(F) = 0` for every off-line `ρ`, for every real `F`. Lemma E(i) makes this impossible if an
off-line zero exists.
(a)⟹(d^±): under RH, `|β_F| ≤ S_F` by (1.2) with `Θ₀ = 0`. In (1.3), `c_F(log n + u)` vanishes for `u > 2T₀` and
`ε_F` is bounded, so the bracket in (d^±) is bounded.
(d⁺)⟹(a): by (1.3), `β_F(u) ≥ −C` on `u ≥ 0`. Lemma Λ with Lemma E(iii), which supplies `F ≥ 0` with
`c_{ρ₀}(F) ≠ 0`, gives the conclusion. (d⁻)⟹(a) is the same with the upper bound. ∎

**Consequences for R3.**
* **Q-M is PROVED (yes).** `B_∞(T₀) ⟹ RH` holds unconditionally at every window `T₀ > 0`, including `T₀ ≤ 0.8`
  where `λ^W_{T₀} ≥ 0` is known. The dominance hypothesis of R3 Prop B3 is superfluous. R3's localiser proof is
  subsumed. The R3 obstacle ("no fixed `F` beats `e^{(a_ρ²−a₀²)σ²}`") was an artefact of trying to *exhibit* a
  negative value. Landau's theorem obtains the contradiction from analyticity, without exhibiting anything.
* **Classification.** `B_∞(T₀)` is an EQUIVALENT form of RH, not an intermediate statement. It is recorded as
  EQUIVALENT (Lens-1 class (ii)).
* (d⁺) is von Koch–Landau (Ingham Ch. V, CITED) in window dress. What is new is only the *route*: window sign
  ⟶ two-term mollifier ⟶ shift function ⟶ smoothed prime sum. This is an identity chain, (1.1) and (1.3).

## §5 Theorem L(θ) — the coefficient-size law (Levinson's normalisation is blind)

**Theorem L(θ) (PROVED; complete form).** Fix `T₀ > 0` and `θ ∈ [0,½]`. The following are equivalent.
(a) Every zero has `|Re ρ − ½| ≤ ½ − θ` (i.e. `Θ ≤ 1 − θ`).
(b) For every real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})` there is `C_F` with `W(F_M) ≥ −C_F` for all two-term mollifiers
`M = 1 + a n^{−s}`, `n ≥ 1`, `|a| ≤ n^θ`.

Moreover `‖F_M‖² ≤ 4‖F‖²` in (b), so (b) is a normalised lower bound.

*Proof.* Put `b := an^{−½}`, so `|b| ≤ n^{θ−½} ≤ 1`, and `W(F_M) = (1+b²)W(F) + 2bβ_F(log n)` by (1.1).
(a)⟹(b): by (1.2), `|β_F(log n)| ≤ S_F n^{½−θ}`, so `W(F_M) ≥ −2|W(F)| − 2S_F`.
(b)⟹(a): take `a = ±n^θ`. Then `2n^{θ−½}|β_F(log n)| ≤ C_F + 2|W(F)|`, i.e. `|β_F(log n)| ≤ C″n^{½−θ}`. By the
interpolation step of Theorem Q, `|β_F(u)| ≤ C‴e^{(½−θ)u}` on `u ≥ 0`. Lemma Λ with `κ = 1 − 2θ` and Lemma E(i)
give (a). ∎

**Reading (the angle's answer; PROVED statements, HEURISTIC comparison).**
* `θ = 0` is Levinson–Conrey's normalisation: `a_n = μ(n)P(log(N/n)/log N)`, `|a_n| ≤ max|P|`. Condition (a) then
  reads `Θ ≤ 1`, which is **true unconditionally**. So bounded-coefficient two-term mollified Weil forms are
  uniformly bounded below, a theorem that carries no information. Such mollifiers cannot see an off-line zero
  through the window bottom.
* `θ = ½` (coefficient `√n`, i.e. the pair test `F + τ_{log n}F`: an *amplifier*, not a mollifier) is RH
  (Theorem Q).
* Intermediate `θ` detects exactly the strip `Θ ≤ 1 − θ`.
* So there are three candidate parameters. The length `N` of the mollifier is the widened window (R3 Theorem M1).
  The proportion of zeros on the line is a density statement, EXCLUDED by `cor:void`. The coefficient growth
  `n^θ` is the one that matters. **"100 %" is forced exactly at `θ = ½`.** At that exponent the mollified form is
  the pair form of `lem:cpos`/`thm:finmod(ii)`, and its uniform lower bound is von Koch's criterion.
* Caveat, HEURISTIC: long mollifiers (length `N`, `|a_n| ≤ 1`) are not covered by L(θ). By R3 Theorem M1 their
  span is all of `C_c^∞(I_{T₀+½log N})`, so their joint bottom is `λ^W_{T₀+½log N}`, which falls under Theorem Γ
  and not under L(θ).

## §6 Theorem R_s — rigidity in the shift variable (single window, every test)

**Theorem R_s (PROVED; complete form).** Let `b : {n ≥ 2} → ℝ`, `b ≠ 0`, with `Σ|b_n|n^{−½} < ∞`, and `a := Λ + b`.
Write `W^{(a)}` for the complete window form with comb `v_n(a) = 2a(n)/√n`, `β^{(a)}_F` for its shift function,
and `B^{(a)}_∞(T₀)` as in §4. Fix `T₀ > 0`.
(i) Under RH, for **every** nonzero real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_{T₀})`, `β^{(a)}_F` is not positive definite. Hence some
mollified test `F_M` has `W^{(a)}(F_M) < 0`.
(ii) Unconditionally, `B^{(a)}_∞(T₀)` fails.
(iii) So `{a ∈ Λ + ℓ¹_w : B^{(a)}_∞(T₀)}` is `{Λ}` if RH holds and `∅` otherwise, at every single window `T₀ > 0`.

*Proof.* `W^{(a)}` is translation invariant: the comb enters through `c_G`, and the polar and archimedean parts are
unchanged. R3 Theorem B(iii)`⟹` uses only translation invariance and continuity of the shift function, so it applies
to `W^{(a)}`: `B^{(a)}_∞(T₀)` implies `β^{(a)}_F` is PD for every `F`. From `eq:Aform`,
```
β^{(a)}_F = β_F − h_b, h_b(u) := Σ_n b_n n^{−½}[c_F(u − log n) + c_F(u + log n)], ĥ_b(ξ) = 2|F̂(ξ)|² r_b(ξ),
r_b(ξ) := Σ_n b_n n^{−½}cos(ξ log n) .
```
`h_b` is continuous and bounded, `|h_b| ≤ 2‖F‖²Σ|b_n|n^{−½}`, and `h_b ∈ L¹`. The function `r_b` is a uniformly,
absolutely convergent Bohr almost periodic function. Its mean is `0` (no frequency `0`, since `n ≥ 2`), and its
Bohr coefficient at `±log n` is `b_nn^{−½}/2`.
* (i) Under RH, `β_F(u) = Σ_γ|Φ_F(½+iγ)|²cos(γu)`. Its Fourier transform is the finite positive discrete measure
  `μ_d` (finite because `S_F < ∞`). The Fourier transform of `β^{(a)}_F` is `μ_d − 2|F̂|²r_b dξ`, a finite signed
  measure whose Lebesgue decomposition has absolutely continuous part `−2|F̂|²r_b dξ`. If `β^{(a)}_F` were PD
  (Bochner), this measure would be positive, so its a.c. part would be `≥ 0`. Then `r_b ≤ 0` wherever `F̂ ≠ 0`. `F̂`
  is entire and `≢ 0`, so this holds a.e., and by continuity everywhere. A continuous almost periodic function that
  is `≤ 0` with mean `0` vanishes identically. (Otherwise `r_b < −δ` on an interval, hence on a relatively dense set
  of translates, and the mean would be `< 0`.) So all its Bohr coefficients vanish and `b = 0`, a contradiction.
  Non-PD of `β^{(a)}_F` gives finitely many points `x_j` and weights `c_j` with `Σc_ic_jβ^{(a)}_F(x_j−x_i) < 0`. By
  R3 Theorem B(iii) (approximating `x_j` by `log n_j(L)`) this gives an `F_M` with `W^{(a)}(F_M) < 0`.
* (ii) If RH fails, take an off-line `ρ₀` and, by Lemma E(i), an `F` with `c_{ρ₀}(F) ≠ 0`. By Lemma Λ (`κ = 0`),
  `β_F` is unbounded below on `u ≥ 0`, so `β^{(a)}_F = β_F − h_b` is unbounded below too. A PD function satisfies
  `|β| ≤ β(0)`, so `β^{(a)}_F` is not PD, and `B^{(a)}_∞(T₀)` fails. If RH holds, use (i).
* (iii) combines (i), (ii), and Theorem Q for `a = Λ`. ∎

**Remark R_s1 (asymmetry made exact).** In the shift variable, *boundedness* of `β^{(a)}_F` is invariant under
`a ↦ a + b` with `b ∈ ℓ¹_w`, because `h_b` is bounded. *Positive definiteness* is destroyed by every such
`b ≠ 0`, through every test. For `a = Λ` the two coincide (Theorem Q: bounded ⟺ PD ⟺ RH). The reason is exactly
the zero expansion (1.1), which makes the spectral measure of `β_F` purely atomic. Any `b ≠ 0` adds a nonzero
absolutely continuous part. This is the panel's asymmetry lesson (G2_B9_R3 salvage (c)) as a theorem: *the
spectral measure is atomic because the explicit formula says so. The difficulty is not atomicity but the reality of
the atoms, and reality is equivalent to boundedness.* R_s covers only `ℓ¹_w`. B9's Remark 3.4 question (tempered,
non-summable `b` whose transform is carried by the ordinates, a crystalline-measure question) is untouched and stays
OPEN.

## §7 Proposition S — the ∀T arrow in two steps (what O3 does and does not forbid)

**Proposition S (PROVED as a logical factorisation; the target is EQUIVALENT).** Let `E_A := [∀T: λ^A_T ≥ 0]`
(primitive) and `E_W := [∀T: λ^W_T ≥ 0]` (complete). Let `E_sub := [λ^A_T ≥ −e^{o(T)}]`.
(i) `E_W ⟹ E_A ⟹ E_sub`. By Theorem Γ(iv), `E_sub ⟹ RH`, and by Weil, `RH ⟹ E_W`. So all three are equivalent,
and the only nontrivial implication `E_sub ⟹ E_W` is **proved** (Theorem Γ followed by Weil).
(ii) **Robustness.** For `a = Λ + b` with `Σ|b_n|n^{−½} < ∞`:
`|λ^A_T(a) − λ^A_T(Λ)| ≤ 2Σ|b_n|n^{−½}` for all `T`. Proof from `eq:Aform`: the comb term changes by
`−Σ2b_nn^{−½}c_F(log n)`, `|c_F| ≤ ‖F‖²`, and the class `P_T` does not depend on the comb. So `E_sub(a) ⟺ E_sub(Λ)`
for every such `a`.
(iii) **Hence**, for a proof of `E_sub`, O3 (`rem:resweights`: one weight changed by `e^{−14.4T}` flips the sign)
and `thm:finmod`/`cor:void` (finite deletion or amplification) are **void**. These operations preserve `E_sub` by
(ii). Every such `a ≠ Λ` violates `E_A` (B9 R′; R_s), yet satisfies `E_sub` if RH. So the "exactness" these
obstructions demand is supplied entirely by the step `E_sub ⟹ E_A`. That step uses the explicit formula of `ζ`
(zero expansion (1.1) in Lemma Λ), which is a theorem. For `a ≠ Λ` the zero expansion fails, and the step fails as
it must. This is the method-constraint reading in the sense of P47 Thm 18.1 / Rem 18.1a.
(iv) **What still binds `E_sub`.** T2 (Epstein, P47 Thm 18.16): `Ξ_Q` satisfies A1–A6 and has `κ > 1` (Remark Γ3,
HEURISTIC transfer), so a proof of `E_sub` must invoke A7, A8 or A9. `Ξ_a`: it satisfies all axioms but A3 and has
`Θ = ½ + a`, so a proof of `E_sub` must invoke A3. T1 (field uniformity) and T3 (Beurling, F4) apply verbatim, since
they are statements about derivations of RH-strength conclusions. And Remark Γ2: any proof of `E_sub` proves
quasi-RH along the way.

**Reading for G2 (HEURISTIC, methodological).** The standing instruction "survive O3, so the argument must be an
exact identity, not an estimate" is correct for any per-window sign argument. It is **not** a necessary property of
the ∀T arrow. The ∀T arrow is equivalent to a robust, margin-free **estimate** (`E_sub`), and the exact part sits in
a proved lemma. This does not make the problem easier: `E_sub` is RH, and its weakest nontrivial form (`κ^A < 1`) is
quasi-RH. It does move the G2 search. The candidates to examine are no longer "exact identities that produce a
sign". They are "prime-side arguments that beat the Chebyshev exponent `κ = 1` for the bottom of the primitive form".
Row 21 of R157_002 (dlVP/Rankin, capped by the archimedean sea) is the record's evidence that the classical tools
stop at `κ = 1`.
