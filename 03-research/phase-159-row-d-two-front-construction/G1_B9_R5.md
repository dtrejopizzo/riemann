## §1 Setting and classical inputs

- `C/F_q` is smooth, projective and geometrically irreducible, of genus `g ≥ 1`. `J` is its Jacobian, with
  principal polarisation `Θ`, and `† ` is the Rosati involution.
- `φ ∈ End(J)` is the `q`-Frobenius endomorphism, and `V := φ† = qφ^{−1}`.
- `S̄ := C̄×C̄`. On the fibres, `F_1 := {P_0}×C` and `F_2 := C×{P_0}`.
- `ι : C → J` is an Abel–Jacobi map, defined over `F_q` through an `F_q`-rational divisor `D_0` of degree 1, which
  exists by F. K. Schmidt (`CITED`).
- `Tr` denotes the trace on `V_ℓJ` (dimension `2g`).

**Facts (`CITED`).**
- **(F1)** `φ` is semisimple on `V_ℓJ`, with eigenvalues `α_1,…,α_{2g}` and `|α_k|² = q` (Weil 1948; Tate 1966). The
  algebra `R[φ] := Q[φ]⊗R` is commutative and semisimple, and `λ_k : R[φ] → C` is the character `φ ↦ α_k`. Since
  `λ_k(φ†) = q/α_k = ᾱ_k`, the involution `†` acts on `R[φ]` as complex conjugation of every character:
  `λ_k(x†) = \overline{λ_k(x)}`. Also `Tr(x) = Σ_{k=1}^{2g} λ_k(x)` and `Tr(x†) = Tr(x)`.
- **(F2)** The map `NS(S̄) → Z ⊕ Z ⊕ End(J̄)`, `D ↦ (A_D, B_D, f_D) := (D·F_1, D·F_2, D_*)`, is additive and
  injective. For the graphs, `Γ_{φ^n} ↦ (1, q^n, φ^n)` and `Γ^t_{φ^n} ↦ (q^n, 1, V^n)`. References: Weil 1948;
  Mumford, *Abelian Varieties* §§19–21; Birkenhake–Lange, *Complex Abelian Varieties* §11.5 (characteristic-free by
  the same proof). This is P47 (X27) for `g = 1`.
- **(F3)** Galois descent. The `q`-Frobenius of `F̄_q/F_q` acts on `End(J̄)` by `f ↦ φfφ^{−1}` (Tate). Hence the
  class of an `F_q`-rational divisor has `f_D` in the commutant of `φ`. When `P_C` is squarefree, that commutant in
  `End(J̄)⊗R` is `R[φ]`: `φ` is then regular semisimple on `V_ℓ`, so its commutant is the algebra of polynomials in `φ`.
- **(F4)** Abelian varieties. Riemann–Roch gives `χ(L) = L^g/g!` and `χ(L)² = deg φ_L`. The degree of an isogeny is
  `det` on `V_ℓ`. Poincaré's formula gives `[ι(C)] ≡ Θ^{g−1}/(g−1)!`. References: Mumford §§16, 19; Milne,
  *Jacobian Varieties* §5.
- **(F5)** Surfaces. The pull-back of an ample line bundle along a morphism is nef. A nef class pairs nonnegatively
  with every pseudo-effective class. `Nef ⊂ Eff‾` (Kleiman; Lazarsfeld I §1.4).

**Definition 1.1.**
- `NS_φ := {D ∈ NS(S̄)⊗R : f_D ∈ R[φ]}`.
- For `D ∈ NS_φ`, the datum is `Φ_D(ρ_k) := λ_k(f_D)` and the degree is `deg D := A_D + B_D`.
- For `g = 1` this is P47 (21.8): `Φ_D(ρ_1) = x_D + iy_D`. For `g ≥ 2` it is the only extension compatible with
  (21.6): `D·D' = A_D B_{D'} + A_{D'} B_D − Σ_k Φ_D(ρ_k)\overline{Φ_{D'}(ρ_k)}` on `NS_φ`. See the weakest step.
- `σ_{θ,k}(𝒞) := sup{|Re(e^{iθ}Φ_D(ρ_k))|/deg D : D ∈ 𝒞, deg D > 0}`.

**Lemma 1.2 (degree of a theta pull-back; `PROVED` from F4).** For `c ∈ End(J)`,
`deg (c∘ι)^*Θ = ½Tr(c†c)`.

*Proof.* The left side is `(c^*Θ·Θ^{g−1})/(g−1)!` by the projection formula and Poincaré's formula. Let `L := c^*Θ`.
Then `φ_Θ^{−1}φ_L = c†c =: s`, a symmetric element.

For `t ∈ Q`, Riemann–Roch gives `((Θ+tL)^g/g!)² = χ(Θ+tL)² = deg(φ_Θ(1+ts)) = det_{V_ℓ}(1+ts) = 1 + t·Tr(s) + O(t²)`.

Comparing the coefficients of `t` in `(Θ+tL)^g/g! = 1 + t(Θ^{g−1}·L)/(g−1)! + O(t²)`, we get
`(Θ^{g−1}·L)/(g−1)! = ½Tr(s)`. Both sides are polynomials in `t`, and the square root is taken on the branch that
equals `1` at `t = 0`. ∎

*Check:* `c = n` gives `n²g`, and `Θ·n_*[C] = n²(Θ·C) = n²g`.

**Lemma 1.3 (`PROVED`).**
- `NS_φ = RF_1 ⊕ RF_2 ⊕ span{Γ_{φ^n}}` (via F2).
- `V_C ⊆ NS_φ`, with equality when the `α_k` are pairwise distinct.

*Proof.* Under F2, `NS_φ ≅ R² ⊕ R[φ]`, and the classes `Γ_{φ^n}` (`n ≥ 0`) have Hom parts `φ^n`, which span `R[φ]`.
Every `γ_n` lies in `NS_φ`. If the `α_k` are distinct, `dim R[φ] = 2g`, so `dim NS_φ = 2+2g = dim V_C` by P47
Thm 21.8. ∎

## §2 Theorem G: the effective cone of `C×C` has support `½` in every genus

For `a, b ∈ End(J)`, put `u_{a,b} : S̄ → J`, `(x,y) ↦ aι(x) − bι(y)`, and `𝔑_{a,b} := [u_{a,b}^*𝒪(Θ)] ∈ NS(S̄)`.

**Lemma 2.1 (the pairing; `PROVED`).** Let `a, b ∈ Z[φ]` and `D ∈ NS_φ`. Then

  D·𝔑_{a,b} = A_D·½Tr(a†a) + B_D·½Tr(b†b) − Tr(f_D a†b). (2.1)

*Proof.* Both sides are linear in `D`: the left side trivially, the right side by F2. By Lemma 1.3 it suffices to
check `D ∈ {F_1, F_2, Γ_{φ^n}, Γ^t_{φ^n}}`. In each case, `u_{a,b}` restricted to the curve is `c∘ι` plus a constant,
and Lemma 1.2 gives `½Tr(c†c)`. Everything below lies in the commutative algebra `Q[φ]`, which is stable under `†`,
and `Tr(x†) = Tr(x)`.
- `F_1`: here `c = −b`, giving `½Tr(b†b)`. This matches (2.1) with `(A,B,f) = (0,1,0)`.
- `F_2`: here `c = a`, giving `½Tr(a†a)`. This matches `(1,0,0)`.
- `Γ_{φ^n} = {(x, F^n x)}`: here `c = a − bφ^n`. Then
  `½Tr(c†c) = ½Tr(a†a) + q^n·½Tr(b†b) − Tr(a†bφ^n)`, using `φ^{n†}φ^n = q^n`. This matches `(1, q^n, φ^n)`.
- `Γ^t_{φ^n} = {(F^n y, y)}`: here `c = aφ^n − b`. Then
  `½Tr(c†c) = q^n·½Tr(a†a) + ½Tr(b†b) − Tr(V^n a†b)`, using `Tr(b†aφ^n) = Tr((b†aφ^n)†) = Tr(V^n a†b)`. This matches
  `(q^n, 1, V^n)`. ∎

**Definition 2.2.** For `a, b ∈ R[φ]`, `N(a,b) ∈ NS_φ` is the class with `(A, B, f) = (½Tr(b†b), ½Tr(a†a), b†a)`.
It exists by Lemma 1.3.

**Theorem G.**
- **(i) (Upper bound, every `C`, every `g`; `PROVED`.)** For every `D ∈ Eff‾(S̄) ∩ NS_φ` and every `k`,
  `|Φ_D(ρ_k)| ≤ √(A_D B_D) ≤ ½deg D`. Hence `σ_{θ,k}(Eff‾ ∩ NS_φ) ≤ ½` for every `θ` and `k`.
- **(ii) (Attainment; `PROVED` when `P_C` is squarefree.)** For every `k` with `α_k ∉ R` and every `θ`, the class
  `N_k(θ) := N(e_k, e^{iθ}e_k)` is nef and has `N_k(θ)² = 0`, `deg = 2` and `|Re(e^{iθ}Φ(ρ_k))|/deg = ½`. Here `e_k`
  is the primitive idempotent of `R[φ]` for the pair `{α_k, ᾱ_k}`, and `e^{iθ}e_k` uses the identification
  `R[φ]e_k ≅ C` via `λ_k`. Hence
  `σ_{θ,k}(Eff‾ ∩ V_C) = σ_{θ,k}(Nef ∩ V_C) = σ_{θ,k}(Pos) = ½`.
- **(iii) (Integral witnesses.)** The nef integral classes `𝔑_{a,b}`, `a, b ∈ Z[φ]`, have ratios whose supremum is
  `½` in every direction.
- **(iv) (`g = 1`.)** `N(F^l, V^k) = B_{k,l}`, the bigraphs of R4 Lemma 3.1.

*Proof.*

(i) Fix `k`. Suppose first `α_k ∉ R`, so that `R[φ]e_k ≅ C` via `λ_k`. Let `m_k` be the multiplicity of `α_k`. For
`z, w ∈ C`, put `a = ze_k` and `b = we_k`. Then:
- `λ_j(x) = λ_k(x)`, `\overline{λ_k(x)}` or `0` for `x ∈ R[φ]e_k`, according as `α_j = α_k`, `α_j = ᾱ_k`, or
  neither. Hence `Tr(x) = 2m_k Re λ_k(x)`.
- By F1, `½Tr(a†a) = m_k|z|²` and `½Tr(b†b) = m_k|w|²`.
- `Tr(f_D a†b) = 2m_k Re(λ_k(f_D) z̄ w)`.

Equation (2.1) holds for `a, b ∈ Z[φ]`. Both sides are quadratic in `(a,b)`, so it extends to `Q[φ]` by homogeneity
and to `R[φ]` by continuity.

`𝔑_{a,b}` is nef (F5), and `D` is pseudo-effective. So for `a, b ∈ Z[φ]` we have `D·𝔑_{a,b} ≥ 0`. By homogeneity
this holds for `a, b ∈ Q[φ]`, and since `Q[φ]` is dense in `R[φ]`, by continuity for all `a, b ∈ R[φ]`. Hence, for
all `z, w`:

  A_D|z|² + B_D|w|² ≥ 2Re(λ_k(f_D) z̄ w).

Also `A_D, B_D ≥ 0`, since `F_1, F_2` are nef. Maximising the phase and putting `s = |z|`, `t = |w|`, we get
`A_D s² + B_D t² − 2|λ_k(f_D)|st ≥ 0` for all `s, t ≥ 0`. This is equivalent to `|λ_k(f_D)|² ≤ A_D B_D`.
AM–GM then gives `√(A_D B_D) ≤ ½(A_D+B_D)`. If `α_k = ±√q`, the same computation with `z, w ∈ R` applies.

(ii) *`N(a,b)` is nef.* Let `a, b ∈ Z[φ]`. With `D_0` rational, the line bundle `u_{a,b}^*𝒪(Θ)` is defined over
`F_q`, where `Θ` is the rational translate of `W_{g−1}` (Riemann). So F3 puts `f_{𝔑_{a,b}}` in the commutant, which
is `R[φ]`. Thus `𝔑_{a,b} ∈ NS_φ`. By Lemma 2.1 it pairs with every `D ∈ NS_φ` as `N(a,b)` does, since the pairing on
`NS_φ` is given by the same formula. The pairing (21.6) is nondegenerate on `NS_φ = V_C` (P47 Thm 21.8), so
`𝔑_{a,b} = N(a,b)`.

Hence `N(a,b)` is nef for integral `a, b`. By homogeneity, and because the nef cone is closed, it is nef for all
`a, b ∈ R[φ]`.

*The value.* For `N_k(θ)` with `m_k = 1`: `A = B = 1` and `f = e^{−iθ}·e_k`, so `Φ(ρ_k) = e^{−iθ}` and `deg = 2`.
Then `|Re(e^{iθ}e^{−iθ})|/2 = ½`. Also `N_k(θ)² = 2AB − Tr(ff†) = 2 − Tr(e_k) = 2 − 2 = 0`. Finally `Nef ⊂ Eff‾`
(F5), `Nef ⊂ Pos`, and the upper bound is (i).

(iii) `Q[φ]` is dense in `R[φ]`, the ratio is continuous and homogeneous of degree 0, and `N(ta, tb) = t²N(a,b)`.

(iv) With `a = F^l` and `b = V^k`: `½Tr(V^{k†}V^k) = ½Tr(F^kV^k) = q^k`, `½Tr(F^{l†}F^l) = q^l`, and
`b†a = F^kF^l = F^{k+l}`. This is R4 Lemma 3.1. The curve `{F^l x = V^k y}` is `u_{F^l,V^k}^{−1}(pt)` on `E×E`. ∎

**Remarks.**

1. **No use of `D² ≥ 0`.** The negative curves are consistent with the bound:
  - `Δ` has `|λ| = 1 = √(1·1)`, with equality.
  - `Γ_{φ^n}` has `|α|^n = √(1·q^n)`, with equality. So every graph is **Castelnuovo-extremal**.
  - The graphs lose only through `√(AB) < ½(A+B)` for `A ≠ B`. That is exactly R4's "polar lock", now proved in
  every genus.
2. **Circularity inside the model.** The step `λ_k(x†) = \overline{λ_k(x)}` is Rosati positivity, i.e. Weil's RH
  for `C`. Theorem G is a statement **about** the model, where RH holds, and not a new proof of it. The positivity
  used is the ampleness of `Θ` (F5 applied to `u^*Θ`). §3 transports exactly that input.
3. **Multiplicity.** If `m_k ≥ 2` (for example `J ∼ E^m`), then `N(e_k, we_k)` has ratio `1/(2m_k)`. Whether
  `Eff‾ ∩ NS_φ` still reaches `½` then needs classes outside `NS_φ` and is `OPEN`. It lies outside P47's standing
  hypothesis.

  `V_C` (G1_B9_R5 Thm G(i)), by pairing with the nef pull-backs of `Θ`. Equality holds when the `α_i` are distinct

## §3 Theorem H: what the transport keeps, and why it is row D

**Frame form on the model.** For `E ∈ D^W_C` (finite support), put `b_E := f_{D_E} = Σ_n E(n) f_{γ_n}`. Here
`f_{γ_n} = q^{−n/2}φ^n` for `n ≥ 0` and `q^{−|n|/2}V^{|n|}` for `n < 0`. Then `λ_k(b_E) = Σ_n E(n)u_k^n`, with
`u_k = α_k/√q` and `|u_k| = 1`. By P47 (21.3)/(21.6) this is `Φ_E(ρ_k)`.

**Theorem H.**
- **(i) (Model; `PROVED`.)** For real `E ∈ D^W_C`,

  ½Tr(b_E†b_E) = ½Σ_{k=1}^{2g}|Φ_E(ρ_k)|² = Φ_E(0)Φ_E(1) − ½I^C_∞(E,E) =: 𝔗_C(E).

  The family `N(a,b)` (Def. 2.2), written in frame terms, is the class with polar weights `(𝔗_C(E_b), 𝔗_C(E_a))`
  and datum `Φ_{E_a}(ρ)Φ_{E_b}(1−ρ) = Φ_{E_a⋆E_b^∨}(ρ)`. Its nefness for all `a, b` implies `𝔗_C ≥ 0` on `D^W_C`,
  which is Weil's positivity for `C`, in the complete form. The Castelnuovo bound of Theorem G(i) is the
  Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the semi-inner product `𝔗_C`.
- **(ii) (`Spec Z`; `EQUIVALENT`.)** Define, **zero-free**, `𝔗_ζ(E) := Φ_E(0)Φ_E(1) − ½I_∞(E,E)` for real
  `E ∈ D^W`. Here `I_∞` is P47's form, given on the prime side by the explicit formula, so no zero is used. Then:
  - `𝔗_ζ(E) = ½Σ_ρ Φ_E(ρ)Φ_E(1−ρ)`, by (11.6);
  - `𝔗_ζ ≥ 0` on real `D^W` ⟺ RH;
  - on primitive `F` (`Φ_F(0) = Φ_F(1) = 0`), `𝔗_ζ(F) = −½I_∞(F,F)`. So "`𝔗_ζ ≥ 0` on primitives" is the row-D
  target `I_∞(F,F) ≤ 0`, verbatim.
- **(iii) (Relation to R4.)** R4's cone `𝓦` is the rebalanced family with polar weights `(e^{−t/2}, e^{t/2})`
  **decoupled** from `𝔗`. Its admissibility is RH (R4 Thm 5(iv)). The geometrically correct transport ties the polar
  weights to `𝔗`, and its admissibility is row D itself. Neither provides a sign.

*Proof.*

(i) By F1, `½Tr(b†b) = ½Σ_k|λ_k(b)|²`. For real `E` and `|u_k| = 1`, `Φ_E(1−ρ_k) = \overline{Φ_E(ρ_k)}`. Then (21.6)
gives `I^C_∞(E,E) = 2Φ_E(0)Φ_E(1) − Σ_k|Φ_E(ρ_k)|²`. The frame form of `N(a,b)` is Definition 2.2 read through the
dictionary (21.7). Cauchy–Schwarz: `|Σ_kλ_k(a)\overline{λ_k(b)}|`, restricted to one eigenline, is at most
`√(𝔗(a)𝔗(b))`. This is Theorem G(i) for `D = N(a,b)`.

(ii) The first identity is (11.6) with `F = G = E`.
- ⟸: under RH, `1−ρ = ρ̄` and `Φ_E(ρ̄) = \overline{Φ_E(ρ)}`, so each term is `|Φ_E(ρ)|²`. The series converges
  absolutely by (11.17).
- ⟹: this is Weil's criterion in the complete form. The functional `E ↦ Σ_ρ Φ_{E⋆E^∨}(ρ)` is nonnegative for all
  real `E` if and only if RH holds (Weil 1952; Bombieri, *Remarks on Weil's quadratic functional*, Rend. Mat. Acc.
  Lincei 2000, Thm 1; P47 D.1 / X2; `CITED`).
- The primitive statement follows by substitution. ∎

**Answer to the angle's question.** Of the Hodge-index geometry of `C×C`, the transport keeps:
- (a) the Frobenius-cone constant `s(γ,θ)` (R4 Thm 4);
- (b) the extremal family `N(a,b)` and the constant `½`, **in every genus** (Theorem G);
- (c) the positivity input that makes (b) nef: the ampleness of `Θ`. In frame terms this is `𝔗 ≥ 0`, which over
  `Spec Z` is Weil's criterion and on primitives is row D (Theorem H).

The Hodge-index proof on `C×C` therefore **reduces**, in the frame, to the ampleness of one polarisation on one
"Jacobian". The transport keeps its statement and loses its proof. That proof is `Θ = W_{g−1}`, an effective divisor
on a genuine abelian variety, followed by F5.

**Proposition J (`CITED` fact + `HEURISTIC` reading).**
- **Fact.** The Arakelov Picard group of `Spec Z̄` is `Pic(Spec Z̄) ≅ R` via the degree, and
  `Pic⁰(Spec Z̄) = 0`. The reason is class number 1 and unit rank 0: `r_1 + r_2 − 1 = 0`. References:
  R. Schoof, *Computing Arakelov class groups*, MSRI Publ. 44 (2008), §§2–4; van der Geer–Schoof, Selecta Math. 6
  (2000). In the van der Geer–Schoof theory, "`h⁰`" on `Pic(Spec Z̄)` is
  `h⁰(d) = log Σ_{n∈Z} e^{−πn²e^{−2d}}`, a Jacobi theta function on the **degree line**.
- **Reading (`HEURISTIC`).** In the model, `Θ` lives on `Pic⁰ = J`, whose `H¹` carries all `2g` zeros:
  `H¹(C) = H¹(J)`. Over `Q` the Arakelov `Pic⁰` is a point, and its `H¹` is `0` against infinitely many zeros. The
  only theta object is on the degree line, i.e. on the **polar** plane of the frame. The difference map
  `u : X×X → Pic⁰` is constant, and `u^*Θ` carries no datum. So the mechanism of Theorem G has no arithmetic carrier
  in Arakelov theory: OT-1 fails at dimension.
- An infinite-dimensional "Jacobian" whose `H¹` is the zeros exists on record only as `C_Q = A^×_Q/Q^×` with Connes'
  cokernel. Its global trace formula is RH-equivalent (R158_001 A17), and a "`Θ` ample" statement there would be
  `𝔗_ζ ≥ 0`, which is Theorem H(ii) again.
