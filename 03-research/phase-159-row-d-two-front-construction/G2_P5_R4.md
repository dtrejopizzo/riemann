## §1 The shared result: the graded converse (B1, B2, B3, B5, B9)

The core is the same in all five memos:
1. **Translation invariance.** `P_T` is preserved by translations inside the window: `A(ψ_x) = e^{x/2}A(ψ)`, and the form
  `𝔞_T` depends only on `c_F`.
2. **One Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.** It is applied to the shifted positive form `𝔞_T + M(T)⟨·,·⟩`, with two translated
  bumps at distance `x`. This bounds the `T`-free smoothed prime profile `g(x) = ⟨u_Λ, c_ψ(·−x)⟩` by `e^{κ x}`-type growth.
3. **Landau's Laplace step.** On `Re z > ½` the Laplace transform of `g` equals `Φ(z)·(ζ′/ζ)(½+z)` plus terms holomorphic on
  `Re z > −½`. With `Φ(z) = Ψ(z)Ψ(−z)` and a per-zero choice of `ψ` making `Φ(z_0) ≠ 0`, a zero at `½ + z_0` would be a pole
  inside the half-plane of holomorphy.

I checked each variant separately.

- **B1 Theorem Q.** VERIFIED.
  - `g_ψ(x)` does not depend on `T` once `2T > x + 2ε`, because contacts with `log n ≥ 2T` miss `supp c_ψ(x ± ·)`.
  - `C_0 c_ψ(x) = 0` and `⟨F,G⟩ = c_ψ(x) = 0` for `x ≥ 2ε`.
  - The window `T = x/2 + 2ε` admits both bumps.
  - `log 2 > 5ε` puts every `c_ψ(·−log n)` inside `[3ε, ∞)`, so the Laplace factorisation is exact.
  - Choice of `ψ`: `Re/Im e^{−z_0 t}` do not lie in `span_ℝ{e^{±t/2}}` for `z_0 ≠ ±½`. So `{Ψ(z_0) = 0}` and `{Ψ(−z_0) = 0}`
  are proper subspaces of `P_ε ∩ C_c^∞`, and one `ψ` avoids both.
  - Q′ for general combs is VERIFIED; the pole order at `z = ½` is at most 2 because `Φ` has a double zero there.
  - Prop. Q2 is VERIFIED: `|Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)| ≤ (∫|F|e^{|δ||t|})² ≤ 2T e^{2δT}‖F‖²`, and on-line terms equal `|F̂(γ)|² ≥ 0`.
  - Cor. Q1(iii) robustness is VERIFIED: `|c_F| ≤ ‖F‖²`.
- **B2 Lemma T and Theorem G1.** VERIFIED.
  - `A(Dh(·−c)) = ∫h(·−c)·De^{t/2} = 0`.
  - `U(x)` does not depend on `T`, and satisfies `|U| ≤ a_0 + ‖Dh‖²K⁻(x/2+1)`.
  - The Laplace transform converges absolutely on `Re s > α`, so Landau's theorem is not needed.
  - The `b`-series is holomorphic on `Re s > α` under `Σ|b|n^{−½−α} < ∞`.
- **B3 Theorem Q.** VERIFIED. It uses the one-sided two-bump identity `W(F_T) = 2W(f) + 2g_f(2T)` (Lemma 2), so it needs
  Landau's theorem (Widder II.5b).
  - That use is legitimate. `g_f = O(e^{x/2})` gives `σ_c ≤ ½`. The right-hand side has no real singularity on `(ε, ∞)`:
  the pole of `ζ′/ζ` at `s = ½` cancels against `1/(s−½)`, `ζ` has no zeros in `(0,1)`, and the trivial zeros lie at
  `Re s < 0`.
  - Lemma 3 is VERIFIED: `f = h″ − h/4` gives `Φ_f(z) = z(z−1)Φ_h(z)`.
  - Lemma 1's formula `k_∞(y) = −e^{−|y|/2}/(1−e^{−2|y|})` agrees with `−½K(y)` (identity checked). It is used only through
  its decay.
- **B5 Lemma 2.1 and Theorem Q.** VERIFIED.
  - Primitive form via `G ↦ PG`, with `‖PG‖² = ⟨P²δ, c_G⟩`, so the δ-shift is positive definite.
  - `(PG)⋆(PH)~` equals `P²k(·−y)`, and it vanishes off `(−¼, ¼)`.

  - The bump is chosen by scaling `c ↦ G_1(cs_0)`, which is legitimate.

  - `W(F_±) = 2S(0) ± 2S(x)` and `‖F_±‖² ≤ 4‖φ‖²`.

  - Patching the local continuations `g_ψ` by the identity theorem is legitimate.

**Consistency across the five.** In the `2T`-normalisation, B2 U1 and B3 Q1 state `κ ≥ Θ − ½`. In the `T`-normalisation,
