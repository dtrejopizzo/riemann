## §0 Verdict

**No object passing OT-5′ ∧ OT-2 was constructed.** What this round produces, in order of weight:

1. **Theorem C (conservation law for "free" positivity; `PROVED`).** Let `H` be an invariant subquotient of a
  Hausdorff locally convex space on which the normalized flow `U_t = e^{−t/2}φ^t` is *tempered*: for each continuous
  seminorm there is a polynomial bound in `|t|`. Take this bound as a norm defined **before and without** the
  spectrum. Then:
  * OT-5′ holds automatically, in the relaxed "slope-½" form of Lemma S.
  * Every eigenvalue of `Θ` on `H` lies on `Re s = ½`, with Jordan chains of length `≤ N+1`.
  * Hence **OT-2 for such an `H` implies RH, together with a multiplicity cap.**

  In this class, "prior positivity" costs nothing, and the whole content of RH moves into the Lefschetz formula: into
  *completeness*, meaning that no zero is lost to a closure. This covers every FF-type, Banach, condensed-normed or
  Hilbert (Connes `L²_δ`) carrier built as a subquotient of a function space with a scaling action. It corrects the
  reading "the whole content of MW-5 is OT-5′" (R158_005 §1). The content is **OT-5′ ∧ OT-2 jointly**, and it can be
  pushed into either one.
2. **Lemma S (slope-½ Serre lemma; `PROVED`, elementary).** Weil/Serre/Deninger unitarity (R158_001 Lemma T, (D3)) can
  be weakened to a **polynomial growth bound in any norm**. This weakening is the archimedean analogue of
  "semistable of slope ½" on the Fargues–Fontaine curve. It allows Jordan blocks, so it is compatible with multiple
  zeros. It therefore removes the obstruction behind N-b (R158_002 §4.7, the `S₃` falsifier): **N-b is an artifact
  of demanding isometry.**
3. **Lemma N (no point spectrum on isometric function lattices; `PROVED`).** On an order-continuous Banach function
  lattice on `R_+^×`, a group of isometric normalized dilations has **no eigenvectors at all**. Zeros can become
  eigenvalues only through a subquotient, or through polynomial (not isometric) growth, which is Connes' `δ`. So the
  subquotient class of Theorem C is **forced** for lattice carriers.
4. **The local FF/Tate carriers (`PROVED`, elementary).** These are the complex Tate curves `E_p = C^×/p^Z` that
  Connes–Consani 2606.06604 attach to `Spec Z` via the `F_1`-curve, together with the archimedean analogue of
  `B^{φ=p^d}`.
  * Their smooth `φ`-eigenspaces realise exactly the **Euler factor** `1 − p^{d−s}` (the prime side).
  * The holomorphic `B_∞^{φ=p^d}` is one-dimensional.
  * The Riemann form on `H¹(E_p)` is a genuine polarization defined before any spectrum, but the scaling flow acts on
  `H¹(E_p)` as the identity, so its Lefschetz number is `0`.

  So these carriers supply **prior positivity with no spectral content**, and **spectral content with no global
  zeros**. This answers, in the elementary archimedean model, the question left open in the Outlook of 2606.06604,
  and it is the archimedean counterpart of phase 15 M6 (prismatic = local).
5. **Proposition M (the global Mazur inequality is RH; `PROVED`).** Proposition W (weak admissibility; `CITED` +
  `HEURISTIC`) shows why FF theory cannot supply the archimedean sign:
  * the only *derived* positivity in p-adic Hodge theory (Colmez–Fontaine; FF modification) is fed by a
  **compact-group** (Galois) realization;
  * such a realization controls p-adic or ℓ-adic valuations of eigenvalues, never complex absolute values.

  In Weil's and Deligne's proofs, the archimedean input is a positivity over `R`. In Deligne's proof it is Rankin
  squaring plus nonnegative coefficients. The record has shown this to be the de la Vallée-Poussin mechanism, capped
  (R151_005 §2.2).

## §2 The slope-½ relaxation of OT-5′

### 2.1 Setting

A **flow carrier** is a Hausdorff locally convex space `V` over `C`, with a strongly continuous group `φ^t` (`t ∈ R`),
generator `Θ`, and normalized group `U_t := e^{−t/2}φ^t`. Its generator is `A = Θ − ½`. An **eigenvector** is
`v ≠ 0` in the domain of `Θ` with `Θv = ρv`. Equivalently, `φ^t v = e^{ρt}v` for all `t`. A **Jordan chain of length
k** at `ρ` is `v_1, …, v_k` with `(Θ − ρ)v_1 = 0`, `(Θ−ρ)v_j = v_{j−1}`, and `v_1 ≠ 0`. Then

  `U_t v_k = e^{(ρ−½)t} Σ_{j=0}^{k−1} (t^j/j!) v_{k−j}`. (2.1)

(2.1) holds because `t ↦ U_t v_k` and the right side both solve `ẋ = Ax` with the same initial value. On the
finite-dimensional invariant span of the chain, `A` is nilpotent plus scalar, so the solution is unique.

**Definition 2.1 (tempered of order N; the "slope-½" condition).** `U` is *tempered of order `N`* if for every
continuous seminorm `q` there are a continuous seminorm `q′` and `C_q ≥ 1` with

  `q(U_t v) ≤ C_q (1+|t|)^N q′(v)` for all `t ∈ R`, `v ∈ V`. (2.2)

It is *isometric* if `V` is normed and `‖U_t v‖ = ‖v‖`.

*Reading* (`HEURISTIC`). On the Fargues–Fontaine curve, a φ-module that is isoclinic of slope `λ` is one where
`p^{−λ}φ` preserves a lattice up to bounded distortion (Dieudonné–Manin; Kedlaya's "pure" φ-modules; CITED as
standard). (2.2) is the archimedean, one-parameter version: `e^{−t/2}φ^t` is bounded up to polynomial distortion,
i.e. "pure of slope ½". The Deninger/Serre condition (D3) of Lemma T is the special case `N = 0` in a Hilbert norm.

### 2.2 Lemma S

**Lemma S (slope-½ Serre lemma; `PROVED`, elementary).** If `U` is tempered of order `N` on `V`, then:
* (i) every eigenvalue `ρ` of `Θ` has `Re ρ = ½`;
* (ii) every Jordan chain has length `k ≤ N + 1`.

*Proof.*
* (i) Take `q` with `q(v) ≠ 0`, which exists because `V` is Hausdorff. By (2.2) with `U_t v = e^{(ρ−½)t}v`,
  `e^{(Re ρ−½)t} q(v) ≤ C_q(1+|t|)^N q′(v)` for all `t ∈ R`. Letting `t → +∞` gives `Re ρ ≤ ½`, and `t → −∞` gives
  `Re ρ ≥ ½`.
* (ii) By (i), `|e^{(ρ−½)t}| = 1`. Take `q` with `q(v_1) ≠ 0`. From (2.1),
  `q(U_t v_k) ≥ (|t|^{k−1}/(k−1)!) q(v_1) − Σ_{j<k−1}(|t|^j/j!) q(v_{k−j})`,
  which grows like `|t|^{k−1}`. (2.2) bounds it by `C(1+|t|)^N`. Hence `k − 1 ≤ N`. ∎

**Converse (`PROVED`).** Let `H` be finite-dimensional, with all eigenvalues of `Θ` on `Re = ½` and Jordan blocks of
size `≤ N+1`. Then `U` is tempered of order `N` in every norm, by (2.1). It is isometric in some norm iff `Θ` is
semisimple, since isometric forces `N = 0` by (ii).

### 2.3 Consequences for the record

**Corollary S1 (N-b is an artifact of isometry; `PROVED`).**
* The `S₃` falsifier of R158_002 §4.7 shows that zeros of `ζ_K` of order `≥ 2` would carry Jordan blocks on the cyclic
  adelic `H¹_K`, given the `K`-version of Meyer's theorem (`ANALYSIS` in the record). It then concludes that a unitary
  (isometric Hilbert) structure cannot exist on `X_K` "whatever RH says". Lemma S(ii) with `N ≥ 1` shows the same
  Jordan blocks are **compatible** with a tempered slope-½ norm.
* Finite-dimensional witness: a 2×2 Jordan block at `ρ = ½ + iγ` has `‖U_t‖ ≍ 1+|t|`. It is tempered of order 1 and
  not isometrizable.
* So the demand N-b ("a non-cyclic `H¹`", R158_005 §2) follows only from choosing isometry. It is not forced on the
  slope-½ route.
* **Price** (`PROVED`): any fixed order `N` caps the multiplicities at `N+1`. A field-uniform object (OT-10) must
  therefore either allow `N` to grow with the multiplicities, or pay the implied bound on multiplicities (see §8).

**Remark 2.2 (consistency with Connes; `CITED`).** Connes' `L²_δ` realisation (Selecta 1999, Thm 1) has
`‖U_t‖ ≤ C(1+|t|)^{δ/2}` from the Sobolev weight `(1+log²|x|)^{δ/2}`. It has spectrum equal to the critical zeros,
with multiplicities capped in terms of `δ`. Lemma S(ii) is the abstract form of that cap: order `N = δ/2`, chains
`≤ N+1`, up to Connes' exact normalisation of the cap, which I have not re-verified.

**Remark 2.3 (condensed / solid topologies; `PROVED` for the stated class, `HEURISTIC` beyond).** Lemma S uses only
(a) Hausdorffness via seminorms and (b) the bound (2.2). A condensed `R`-vector space that comes from a Hausdorff
locally convex space satisfies both. Clausen–Scholze liquid / solid vector spaces that are not of this form (e.g.
non-quasi-separated) have no seminorm to test with. For them, "tempered" has to be *defined*, as polynomially bounded
in some bornology, and Lemma S carries over verbatim for any Hausdorff bornology that separates points. **Changing the
category does not change the conclusion: in the condensed world, positivity is a growth bound, and a growth bound
places the spectrum on the line.**

## §3 Theorem C: the conservation law for subquotient carriers

### 3.1 Statement

**Theorem C (`PROVED`).** Let `(V, φ^t)` be a flow carrier with `U` tempered of order `N`. Let `W_2 ⊂ W_1 ⊂ V` be
closed `φ`-invariant subspaces, and let `H := W_1/W_2` carry the quotient topology.
* (i) The induced group on `H` is tempered of order `N`. The tempered norm on `H` is defined from `V` alone.
* (ii) Every eigenvalue and Jordan chain of `Θ_H` satisfies Lemma S: `Re ρ = ½`, length `≤ N+1`.
* (iii) Suppose `H` satisfies the *complete Lefschetz clause*: for every non-trivial zero `ρ` of `ζ` of multiplicity
  `m_ρ`, `Θ_H` has generalized eigenvalue `ρ` with algebraic multiplicity `m_ρ`. (This is the spectral content of
  OT-2 and of Leichtnam's axiom 6], in the reading R158_001 (D2).) Then RH holds, and `m_ρ ≤ N+1` whenever the
  `ρ`-part is a single chain.
* (iv) Hence, in the class of Theorem C, OT-5′ (relaxed to slope ½, which is what Lemma T(⇒) needs) is free. **OT-2 is
  at least RH-strength.**

*Proof.*
* (i) Restriction to `W_1` preserves (2.2). For the quotient seminorms, `q̄([v]) = inf_{w∈W_2} q(v+w)`. Since
  `U_t W_2 = W_2`,
  `q̄(U_t[v]) = inf_w q(U_t v + U_t w) = inf_{w′} q(U_t(v + w′)) ≤ C_q(1+|t|)^N inf_{w′} q′(v+w′) = C_q(1+|t|)^N q̄′([v])`.
  `H` is Hausdorff because `W_2` is closed.
* (ii) Lemma S applied to `H`.
* (iii) By (ii), each such `ρ` has `Re ρ = ½`. The chain bound is (ii).
* (iv) (i) and (iii). ∎

**Scope of Theorem C.** It covers the carriers the FF/condensed school would naturally write down:
* Banach or Fréchet completions of Schwartz–Bruhat functions on `A_Q`, `C_Q` or `R_+^×`, with the scaling action
  normalized by `|λ|^{1/2}` and weighted by polynomial Sobolev weights in `log|x|`;
* their cokernels and kernels (Connes' `E`, Meyer's `Z`-quotients, provided the topology is tempered);
* Nyman–Beurling-type `L^p` quotients (§3.3).

It does **not** cover Meyer's nuclear `𝓗_−`. Meyer's representation has exponential growth, and R158_002 records that
it passes OT-2 and fails OT-5′. That is consistent: exponential growth is exactly what lets it see every zero. Theorem C
does not cover `H¹_ét` of a curve over `F_q` either: there the form is not inherited from an ambient tempered carrier,
and it is proved from Castelnuovo on `C×C` (W-5).

### 3.2 Where the eigenvalues can come from: Lemma N

**Lemma N (`PROVED`).** Let `E` be a Banach function lattice on `(R_+^×, d^×x)`. That is: `f ∈ E`,
`|g| ≤ |f|` a.e. implies `g ∈ E` and `‖g‖ ≤ ‖f‖`. Assume `E` is order continuous: `0 ≤ f_n ↓ 0` a.e. implies
`‖f_n‖ → 0`. Let `(U_λ f)(x) = λ^{c} f(x/λ)` act isometrically on `E` for some real `c`. Then no `f ≠ 0` satisfies
`U_λ f = χ(λ) f` for all `λ`, for any function `χ`.

*Proof.*
* Isometry gives `|χ(λ)| = 1`.
* For `h` supported in `[a,∞)`, `U_λ h` is supported in `[λa,∞)`. Hence
  `U_{2^n}(1_{[1,∞)}f) = 1_{[2^n,∞)} U_{2^n}f = χ(2^n) 1_{[2^n,∞)} f`.
  Taking norms, `‖1_{[2^n,∞)} f‖ = ‖1_{[1,∞)} f‖` for all `n ≥ 0`.
* By the lattice property, `‖h‖ = ‖|h|‖`. Order continuity applied to `1_{[2^n,∞)}|f| ↓ 0` gives `1_{[1,∞)}f = 0`.
* Applying the same to `U_μ f = χ(μ)f` for every `μ > 0` gives `1_{[μ,∞)}f = 0` for every `μ`, so `f = 0`. ∎

**Consequences (`PROVED`).**
* On `L^p(R_+^×)` with `1 ≤ p < ∞` (order continuous) and isometric normalized dilations, the point spectrum is empty.
* On `L^∞` (not order continuous), every `x^{−iγ}` is an eigenvector, so the point spectrum is the *whole* line.
* Polynomial weights (Connes' `δ`) make `x^{−½+iγ}` square-integrable against `dx/(1+log²x)^{δ/2}` for `δ > 1`.

In each case the discrete set `{γ_ρ}` can only be carved out by a subquotient, or by the dual of a subspace (Connes: the
cokernel of `E`). So for lattice carriers the subquotient structure of Theorem C is **forced**, and with it (iv): the
RH content sits in whether the carving loses zeros.

### 3.3 The two mirror instances (`CITED`)

* **Connes (critical zeros as point spectrum).** On the cokernel of `E` in `L²_δ`, the spectrum is the critical zeros
  only. "All zeros appear" (the global trace formula) is equivalent to RH (Selecta 1999 Thm 1, §VIII Thm 5; R158_001
  A17). Theorem C(iii) is the abstract form of this: an off-line zero cannot be an eigenvalue, so it is lost.
* **Nyman–Beurling (off-line zeros as annihilators).** In `L^p(0,1)`, `1 < p < ∞`, the dilation semigroup `f ↦ f(x/λ)`
  (`λ ≤ 1`) is a contraction semigroup up to the factor `λ^{1/p}`. The closed span of `{θ/x} − θ{1/x}` is dense iff
  `ζ` has no zeros in `Re s > 1/p` (Beurling 1955, CITED). Here the *off-line* zeros show up in the annihilator, the
  critical ones do not, and RH is the vanishing of the quotient.

Both are the same law seen from two sides: **in a slope-½ carrier, the critical line is the boundary of the spectrum,
so a subquotient sees one side of RH and loses the other.** Label: the two facts are `CITED`; the reading is
`HEURISTIC`.

## §4 The local FF/Tate carriers of Connes–Consani 2606.06604

**Setting (`CITED`, FF.tex l.1550–1640).** The complex points of the absolute curve at `p`, modulo the discrete
Frobenius `z ↦ pz`, form the complex Tate curve `E_p = C^×/p^Z`. Its real structure gives
`E_p ≅ C_p × X̃_∞`, with `C_p = R_+^×/p^Z` (the periodic orbit, length `log p`) and `X̃_∞ = C^×/R_+^× = S¹` (the
"real analogue of the FF curve"). The scaling flow `z ↦ e^t z` preserves `E_p`.

**Proposition L1 (archimedean `B^{φ=p^d}`, holomorphic; `PROVED`).** Let `d ∈ Z` and
`B_∞^{φ=p^d} := {f holomorphic on C^× : f(pz) = p^d f(z)}`. Then `B_∞^{φ=p^d} = C·z^d`.

*Proof.* `g = f/z^d` is holomorphic on `C^×` and `p`-invariant, so it descends to the compact Riemann surface `E_p`.
By Liouville it is constant. ∎

The contrast is the point. In the p-adic FF theory, `B^{φ=p}` is an infinite-dimensional Banach–Colmez space. Its
archimedean analogue is a line. The `p`-adic "`H⁰(O(1))`" has no archimedean analogue of the same size. (`CITED`:
Fargues–Fontaine, Astérisque 406, 2018, for `B^{φ=p}`.)

**Proposition L2 (smooth eigenspaces = the Euler factor; `PROVED` for the spectrum, `CITED` for the determinant).**
* Let `S_d := {f ∈ C^∞(C^×) : f(pz) = p^d f(z)}`, with the flow `(φ^t f)(z) = f(e^{−t}z)`. Restrict to
  `S¹`-invariant `f` (functions of `|z|`, i.e. trivial on the `X̃_∞` factor). Then `f(r) = r^d g(log r)` with `g`
  `log p`-periodic. In the Fourier basis `e_k(r) = r^{d}e^{2πik log r/log p}`, the generator of `φ^t` is diagonal, with
  eigenvalues `−(d + 2πik/log p)`, each of multiplicity one.
* Up to the sign convention of the flow, the spectrum is `{s : p^{d−s} = 1}`, the pole set of the Euler factor
  `(1 − p^{d−s})^{−1}`. The zeta-regularized determinant `∏_k (s − d − 2πik/log p)` equals `1 − p^{d−s}` up to a
  constant.
* Without the `S¹`-restriction, each eigenvalue carries the characters `e^{imθ}` of `X̃_∞`. The multiplicity is then
  infinite, and the `X̃_∞` factor contributes no spectral parameter.

The determinant is `CITED`: Deninger, *Local L-factors of motives and regularized determinants*, Invent. Math. 107
(1992). *Proof of the spectrum:* `φ^t e_k = e^{−t(d + 2πik/log p)} e_k` by direct substitution. ∎

**Proposition L3 (prior polarization, trivial Frobenius; `PROVED`).**
* `H¹(E_p, R)` carries its Riemann form, the intersection pairing with the complex structure of Thm 4.6 of
  2606.06604. This is a positive polarization (Hodge–Riemann on an elliptic curve) defined with no reference to any
  spectrum or zero.
* The scaling flow acts on `E_p` by translations (it is multiplication in the group `C^×/p^Z`), so each `φ^t` is
  homotopic to the identity. Hence `φ^t` acts on `H^*(E_p)` as the identity.
* The flow's Lefschetz number is `Σ(−1)^i Tr(φ^t | H^i) = χ(E_p) = 0` for every `t`. The scaling orbits are all
  closed (period `log p`) and come in a degenerate one-parameter family indexed by `X̃_∞`.

*Proof.* Translations of a connected Lie group are homotopic to the identity, and the Lefschetz number is a homotopy
invariant. ∎

**Reading (`HEURISTIC`, with the facts `PROVED`).**
* L3: a genuine OT-5′-type form exists, and the flow is spectrally dead on the cohomology that carries it.
* L2: spectral content exists on sections, and it is the prime side (the Euler factor at `p`). The global union
  `∐_p E_p` realises `∏_p(1−p^{−s})^{−1}` in `Re s > 1` and no zero.
* Gluing the `E_p` into one carrier whose cohomology has the zeros as spectrum is exactly the missing object. It is
  the archimedean counterpart of M6 ("prismatic = local").

So the FF/Tate data split the Weil package into two halves that never meet: polarization on `H¹(E_p)`, and Euler
factors on sections. This is the answer, in the elementary model, to the Outlook question of 2606.06604. The p-adic
Banach–Colmez version of that question stays `OPEN`.

## §5 Why the FF / p-adic Hodge machinery cannot supply the archimedean sign

**Proposition M (the global Mazur inequality is RH; `PROVED`).** Let `(H, Θ)` realise the zeros with multiplicity, as
in the complete Lefschetz clause, with the functional-equation involution pairing the `ρ`-part with the `(1−ρ̄)`-part.
Define on each `Θ`-stable finite-dimensional `H′`:
* the *Newton number* `t_N(H′) := Σ Re(eigenvalues)`, with multiplicity;
* the *Hodge number* `t_H(H′) := ½ dim H′`, i.e. a single Hodge jump at weight 1, which is the archimedean
  Γ-datum `w = 1`.

Then "`t_N(H′) ≥ t_H(H′)` for every `H′`" (weak admissibility in the Colmez–Fontaine shape) is equivalent to RH.

*Proof.*
* (⇐) Under RH, `t_N = t_H` on every `H′`.
* (⇒) Take `H′` the eigenline of `ρ`: `Re ρ ≥ ½`. Take the eigenline of `1 − ρ̄`, also a zero: `1 − Re ρ ≥ ½`. ∎

The single Hodge jump is what makes weak admissibility collapse to isoclinicity. The same happens p-adically: a
filtered φ-module with one Hodge weight is weakly admissible iff it is isoclinic of that slope (`CITED`, standard).

**Proposition W (where derived positivity comes from in FF theory; `CITED` + `HEURISTIC`).**
* *Colmez–Fontaine* (Invent. Math. 140, 2000): weakly admissible implies admissible.
* *Fargues–Fontaine* (Astérisque 406, 2018): `D` is weakly admissible iff the modification `E(D, φ, Fil)` of the
  φ-bundle at `∞` is semistable of slope 0.

These are the only places where FF theory derives a slope condition from Hodge data. In the geometric case, the input
"weakly admissible" is obtained from the existence of a crystalline Galois representation `V`, with
`E(D_cris(V)) ≅ V ⊗ O_X` trivial, hence semistable of slope 0. The origin of that is the **compactness** of
`Gal(Q̄_p/Q_p)` acting continuously on a `Q_p`-space, which is bounded, so a lattice exists. Compactness controls the
**p-adic (or ℓ-adic) valuations** of Frobenius eigenvalues. It never controls their complex absolute values.

For varieties over `F_q`, the complex absolute values of crystalline Frobenius eigenvalues come from Deligne (Weil I,
via ℓ-adic cohomology), transported by Katz–Messing (1974), and not from crystalline theory (`CITED`). In Weil I the
archimedean input is Rankin squaring together with **nonnegativity of coefficients** and a pole bound. For ζ over `Q`,
the record identifies this mechanism with de la Vallée-Poussin's, which is capped at depth `1/log γ` by the
archimedean density (R151_005 §2.2; phase 151 README l.10). **So the FF angle offers no new source of an archimedean
sign.** Label: the statements about the literature are `CITED`; "no new source" is `HEURISTIC`. It is a reading of the
known proofs, not an impossibility theorem.

## §7 What is genuinely new, and what it tells the other fronts

1. **Theorem C + Lemma N (`PROVED`)**: for every subquotient carrier with a prior tempered norm, OT-5′ is free and OT-2
  is RH-strength. The function-lattice construction forces the subquotient shape. So a G1 object in the FF/condensed
  style has to prove a **completeness theorem**: the carved `H¹` loses no zero. It must do so from exact `Λ(n)`, since
  the ambient carrier is shared by all modified combs (OT-4). **That completeness statement is precisely the G2
  mechanism problem** in spectral dress. The two fronts meet here. `HEURISTIC` as a claim about the fronts; `PROVED`
  as the implication OT-2 ⇒ RH in the class.
2. **Lemma S / Cor S1 (`PROVED`)**: isometry can be replaced by slope-½ temperedness. This removes N-b and reconciles
  Deninger-type positivity with multiple zeros (the `S₃` fields). It lowers the bar for OT-5′ from "Hilbert definite
  form" to "norm with polynomial growth".
3. **§4 (`PROVED`, elementary)**: the archimedean `B^{φ=p^d}` is a line. Smooth Frobenius eigenspaces on the Tate curve
  realise the Euler factor. The prior polarization on `H¹(E_p)` is spectrally dead. This is the elementary model of the
  Outlook question of 2606.06604.
4. **§5**: the FF source of derived positivity is compact-group boundedness, which is non-archimedean. Reading
  `HEURISTIC`, facts `CITED`.

**A precise OPEN problem that this angle leaves (RH-strength, stated for the record).**
*Completeness problem (C_δ).* For Connes' cokernel `H_δ` (`δ > 1`), prove that every zero `ρ` of `ζ` with
`Re ρ ∈ (0,1)` is detected by a nonzero continuous functional on `L²_δ(C_Q)` vanishing on the range of `E`, i.e. an
element of the annihilator transforming by `ρ`.
* By Theorem C this is equivalent to RH together with the multiplicity cap: an off-line `ρ` cannot be detected.
* Its "free" half (critical zeros are detected) is Connes' Thm 1.
* The missing half is a statement that the range of `E` is not too large. Via Mellin, that is a lower bound for `|ζ|`,
  or an exact-`Λ` identity, not an estimate (O3).

Label: `OPEN`, RH-strength, equivalent to Connes Thm 5. Stated so that it is not resubmitted as new.

## §8 Weakest step

* **The weakest step is the scope of Theorem C.** It is PROVED for subquotients of Hausdorff locally convex (or
  bornological, Rem 2.3) carriers with a tempered group.
  * A condensed carrier that is not of this form, e.g. a non-quasi-separated solid module where eigenvectors cannot
  be separated by bounded seminorms, is outside the theorem. The FF-school claim "changing the category does not
  help" is then only `HEURISTIC`.
  * An object whose `H¹` is not a subquotient of an ambient carrier with a prior norm (a genuinely cohomological `H¹`,
  as `H¹_ét` is over `F_q`) escapes Theorem C entirely. That is where a real G1 object would have to live, and none
  is constructed here.
* Second-weakest: Remark 2.2's match of Lemma S(ii) with Connes' multiplicity cap is up to his normalisation, which I
  have not re-verified.
* In Cor S1, the multiplicity price for field uniformity is real. `ζ_K` for large Galois groups has zeros of large
  order, so any fixed-`N` construction fails OT-10, and `N` must grow with `K`.
* §4 is elementary and correct as stated. Its reading as "the answer" to CC's Outlook concerns only the archimedean,
  holomorphic/smooth model, not their p-adic Banach–Colmez question.
