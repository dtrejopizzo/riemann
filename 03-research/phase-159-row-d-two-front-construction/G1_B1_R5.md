1. **Lemma 2.1 (norm law; `PROVED`).** If `h` is a Θ-eigenform with finite positive norm, then `Re ρ = α/2` and `|h|_g`
  is a φ-invariant function on `X`.
2. **Theorem O1 (local Weil-number law; `PROVED`).**
  * At a periodic point `x` of period `l`, with a smooth leaf germ: if `h(x) ≠ 0`, then `e^{ρl} ∈ {λ_x, λ̄_x}`. Here

  * At an orbifold fixed point (`Z/k`, `k ≥ 2`): `h` vanishes near `x`.

  This is local. It uses no completeness, so it removes the weak step of G1_B2_R4 Theorem H that the panel flagged.
3. **Theorem O2 (orbit-closure law; `PROVED`).** `|h|` is constant on the closure of every φ-orbit. So O1 applies at

4. **Theorem O3 (separation; `PROVED`).** Let `ρ′ ∉ {ρ, ρ̄}` and `h(y)h′(y) ≠ 0`. Then:
  * `cl(φ^R y)` contains **no fixed point**;
  * the periods of its periodic points lie in `(2π/|γ−γ′|)Z ∪ (2π/|γ+γ′|)Z`.

  Three pairwise incommensurable periods, e.g. `log 2, log 3, log 5`, are impossible.
5. **Corollary O4 (ergodic exclusion; `PROVED`).** Suppose `m = vol_g ⊗ μ` is φ-ergodic.
  * (i) If `m(X) = ∞`: `Θ` has **no eigenvector** in `Ĥ¹_μ`. This closes B3's escape (X1) in the ergodic case.
  * (ii) If a smooth fixed point lies in `supp m`: `σ_p(Θ|H̄¹) ⊆ {α/2 ± iθ_x}`. If an orbifold fixed point does:
  `σ_p = ∅`.
  * (iii) If `supp m` contains three periodic points of pairwise incommensurable periods: `σ_p` lies in **one conjugate
  pair**.

  For ζ (lengths `log p`, the archimedean fixed point in `supp m`, which OT-12 requires), such a carrier cannot have ζ's

6. **Lemma O6 (flatness on eigenform supports at infinite trace; `PROVED`).** B3's curvature-scaling mechanism works on

## §1 Setting

**Definition 1.1 (class DK_α^c).** `(X,𝓕,g,φ,μ)` is as in G1_B2_R4 Def 1.1 (re-checked):
* `X` is a second-countable, locally compact foliated space;
* the leaves are oriented, of dimension `d = 2`;
* `g` is a leafwise metric;
* `φ^t` maps leaves to leaves and satisfies `(φ^t)^*g = e^{αt}g` on `T𝓕`, with `α ≠ 0`;
* `μ` is a holonomy-invariant transverse Radon measure with (D2).

Put `m := vol_g ⊗ μ`. It is φ-invariant (G1_B2_R4 Lemma 1.2(b), `PROVED` there). Hypotheses:

* **(C) Continuous Hodge realisation.** Every class in `H̄¹` (complexified) has a unique representative in
  `Harm¹ := {h : leafwise smooth, continuous on X, Δ_𝓕 h = 0}`, and `φ^{t*}` acts on `Harm¹`. This is DLS (13)
  (Álvarez López–Kordyukov) for Riemannian foliations on compact manifolds, `CITED`, read. For laminations it is the
  standing assumption of Deninger's formalism. Invariance of `Harm¹` under `φ^{t*}` is automatic: in dimension 2,
  `ker Δ` on 1-forms depends only on the conformal class (G1_B3_R5 Thm F(iv), re-checked).
* **(Def) Definiteness.** `(h,h) = ∫|h|²_g dm > 0` for `0 ≠ h ∈ Harm¹` (DLS (15): a scalar product on `H̄¹`).
* **(Sm_x) Smooth leaf germ at `x`.** The leaf `L_x` is a smooth surface near `x`, and `g|_{L_x}` is `C²` there.
  **(Orb_x)** is the variant in which the germ is `(D,g̃)/G`, with `G ⊂ SO(2)` finite and nontrivial, fixing `0`, and
  the flow lifting to `D` near `0`.

An **eigenform** is `0 ≠ h ∈ Harm¹ ⊗ C` with `φ^{t*}h = e^{ρt}h` for all `t`. By uniqueness in (C), this is the same
as an eigenclass of `Θ` on `H̄¹`. Write `ρ = α/2 + iγ` once Lemma 2.1 has fixed the real part.

For a periodic point `x` of period `l > 0` (`φ^l x = x`, `x ∉ Fix`), `φ^l(L_x) = L_x`. Conformality and orientation
give `T_xφ^l|_{T_x𝓕} = ` multiplication by a complex number `λ_x = e^{αl/2}u_x` with `|u_x| = 1` (in an oriented
isometric identification `T_x𝓕 ≅ C`). For a fixed point `x`, similarly `T_xφ^t|_{T_x𝓕} = e^{(α/2 + iθ_x)t}`, because
it is a continuous one-parameter group in `CO⁺(2) ≅ C^×`. This is Deninger's "Fact" (DLS §7 Rem. 5), and it is
consistent with G1_B2_R4 Theorem H(iv).

## §2 The local law

**Lemma 2.1 (norm law; `PROVED`).** Let `h` be an eigenform with `(h,h) < ∞`. Then `Re ρ = α/2`, and
`|h|_g(φ^t x) = |h|_g(x)` for all `x ∈ X` and `t ∈ R`.

*Proof.* For a covector `ξ ∈ T*_{φ^t x}𝓕`, `|ξ∘Tφ^t|_g = e^{αt/2}|ξ|_g` by conformality. So pointwise
`|φ^{t*}h|_g(x) = e^{αt/2}|h|_g(φ^t x)`. With `φ^{t*}h = e^{ρt}h` this gives
`|h|_g(φ^t x) = e^{(Re ρ − α/2)t}|h|_g(x)`. Integrate the square against the φ-invariant `m`:
`(h,h) = e^{2(Re ρ − α/2)t}(h,h)`. Now `0 < (h,h) < ∞` by (Def), so `Re ρ = α/2`. Then the pointwise identity gives
invariance. ∎

(This is Deninger's Thm 4.1 in pointwise form. The pointwise invariance of `|h|` is what gets used below.)

**Lemma 2.2 (local flat normal form, no completeness; `PROVED`).** Let `x` be periodic of period `l`, with (Sm_x).
Assume `α > 0`; for `α < 0`, exchange `φ^l` and `φ^{−l}`. Then there is `r > 0` such that the leafwise geodesic disc
`U = B(x,r) ⊂ L_x` has these properties:
* (a) `g|_U` is flat;
* (b) in the Euclidean coordinate `z` of `U` centred at `x`, `φ^{−l}(z) = λ_x^{−1}z`;
* (c) `φ^{−nl}(U) ⊂ B(x, e^{−nαl/2}r) → {x}`.

The same holds at a fixed point with (Sm_x), using `φ^{−t}` for `t > 0`, which acts as `z ↦ e^{−(α/2+iθ_x)t}z`.

*Proof.*
* `f := φ^{−l}|_{L_x}` is a diffeomorphism of `L_x` fixing `x`, with `f^*g = c²g` and `c = e^{−αl/2} < 1`. Lengths of
  curves scale exactly by `c`.
* Take `r` below the injectivity and convexity radius at `x`. Then `f(B(x,r)) = B(x,cr)`, which is (c).
* Curvature: `(L_x, f^*g) → (L_x, g)` via `f` is an isometry, and `K_{c²g} = c^{−2}K_g`, so `K(f(y)) = c^{−2}K(y)`.
  Iterating, `K(y) = c^{2n}K(fⁿ(y))`. Since `fⁿ(y) → x` and `K` is continuous at `x`, `K(y) = 0` on `U`, which is (a).
  This is the local form of G1_B2_R4 Lemma H0 and of G1_B3_R5 Lemma 2.1.
* On the flat disc, `exp_x` is an isometry from the Euclidean disc. A map between flat convex discs that scales all
  distances by `c` is `c` times an isometry, hence affine. It fixes `0`. Orientation-preserving means `z ↦ μz`, and
  `μ = T_xf = λ_x^{−1}`. This is (b).
* Fixed point: apply this to each `φ^{−t}`, `t > 0`. The linear maps `z ↦ c(t)z` form a continuous one-parameter
  semigroup, so `c(t) = e^{−(α/2+iθ_x)t}`. ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (harmonic forms of constant norm; `PROVED`).** On a connected open set `U ⊂ C` with the flat metric, let
`h = f dx + g dy` (complex `f, g`) be closed and coclosed, with `|h|² = |f|² + |g|²` constant. Then `h = a dz + b dz̄`
with `a, b ∈ C` constant.

*Proof.*
* `dh = 0` gives `g_x = f_y`. `d⋆h = 0` gives `f_x + g_y = 0`, where `⋆h = f dy − g dx`.
* Hence `F := f − ig` satisfies `∂_{z̄}F = ½(f_x + g_y) + (i/2)(f_y − g_x) = 0`, and `H := \overline{f + ig}` satisfies
  `∂_{z̄}H = \overline{∂_z(f+ig)} = \overline{½(f_x + g_y) + (i/2)(g_x − f_y)} = 0`. Both are holomorphic.
* `|F|² + |H|² = 2(|f|² + |g|²)` is constant. Apply `Δ = 4∂∂̄`: `Δ|F|² = 4|F′|²`, so `4(|F′|² + |H′|²) = 0`, and `F, H`
  are constant.
* Then `f = (F + H̄)/2` and `g = i(F − H̄)/2` are constant, and `f dx + g dy = a dz + b dz̄` with `a = (f − ig)/2`,
  `b = (f + ig)/2`. ∎

**Theorem O1 (local Weil-number law; `PROVED` under (C), (Def), (Sm_x)/(Orb_x)).** Let `h` be an eigenform with
`(h,h) < ∞`, and let `x ∈ X`.
(i) *Periodic point, (Sm_x).* `|h|` is constant on the disc `U` of Lemma 2.2, and `h|_U = a dz + b dz̄`. If `h(x) ≠ 0`,
then `e^{ρl} = λ_x` (if `a ≠ 0`) or `e^{ρl} = λ̄_x` (if `b ≠ 0`). Equivalently, `e^{iγl} ∈ {u_x, ū_x}`.
(ii) *Fixed point, (Sm_x).* If `h(x) ≠ 0`, then `ρ ∈ {α/2 + iθ_x, α/2 − iθ_x}`.
(iii) *Orbifold fixed or periodic point, (Orb_x).* `h ≡ 0` near `x`.

*Proof.*
(i)
* By Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2(c), for `z ∈ U`: `|h|(z) = |h|(φ^{−nl}z) → |h|(x)`. So `|h|` is constant on `U`.
* `h|_U` is harmonic for the flat metric (Lemma 2.2(a)), so Lemma 2.3 gives `h|_U = a dz + b dz̄`.
* On `B(x, r/|λ_x|)`, `φ^l` is `z ↦ λ_x z` (Lemma 2.2(b)). So `φ^{l*}h = aλ_x dz + bλ̄_x dz̄`, and this equals
  `e^{ρl}(a dz + b dz̄)`.
* `h(x) ≠ 0` means `(a,b) ≠ (0,0)`. Compare coefficients.

(ii) The same argument with `φ^t`, `t ∈ R`: `a e^{(α/2+iθ)t} = a e^{ρt}` and `b e^{(α/2−iθ)t} = b e^{ρt}` for all `t`.

(iii)
* Pull `h` back to `h̃` on `D`. It is `G`-invariant and harmonic.
* The lifted contraction gives `|h̃|` constant near `0`, so by Lemma 2.3 `h̃ = a dw + b dw̄`.
* For the generator `w ↦ ζw` of `G` (`ζ ≠ 1`), invariance gives `aζ = a` and `bζ̄ = b`. So `a = b = 0`. ∎

**Remark 2.4 (the weight law without positivity; `PROVED`).** Drop (Def) and Lemma 2.1, and keep (Sm_x) at a periodic
`x`. Expand the holomorphic parts in power series. From `φ^{l*}h = e^{ρl}h` one gets `h|_U = a z^n dz + b z̄^k dz̄` with
`e^{ρl} = λ_x^{n+1}` or `λ̄_x^{k+1}`. So `Re ρ ∈ (α/2)·{1,2,3,…}`: each closed orbit alone forces "weights" in
`(α/2)N_{≥1}`. Positivity (Lemma 2.1) selects weight 1 exactly when `h(x) ≠ 0`. This is the dynamical shadow of Weil's
weight argument; it is not used below.

## §3 The global laws

**Theorem O2 (orbit-closure law; `PROVED` under (C), (Def)).** Let `h` be an eigenform with `(h,h) < ∞`, and `y ∈ X`.
Then `|h|` is constant on `Ω(y) := cl(φ^R y)`. Hence, if `h(y) ≠ 0`:
* at every periodic point `x ∈ Ω(y)` with (Sm_x): `e^{iγ l(x)} ∈ {u_x, ū_x}`;
* at every fixed point `x ∈ Ω(y)` with (Sm_x): `ρ ∈ {α/2 ± iθ_x}`;
* and `Ω(y)` contains no point satisfying (Orb_x).

*Proof.* `|h|` is continuous (by (C)) and φ-invariant (Lemma 2.1), so it is constant on the orbit, hence on its closure.
So `h(x) ≠ 0` at every `x ∈ Ω(y)`. Apply Theorem O1. ∎

**Theorem O3 (separation; `PROVED` under (C), (Def)).** Let `h, h′` be eigenforms with eigenvalues
`ρ = α/2 + iγ`, `ρ′ = α/2 + iγ′`, `γ′ ∉ {γ, −γ}`, and let `y` satisfy `h(y)h′(y) ≠ 0`. Put `δ_± := γ′ ± γ`; both are
nonzero. Then:
(a) `Ω(y)` contains no fixed point satisfying (Sm_x) or (Orb_x).
(b) For every periodic `x ∈ Ω(y)` with (Sm_x): `l(x) ∈ (2π/|δ_−|)Z ∪ (2π/|δ_+|)Z`.
(c) `Ω(y)` does not contain three periodic points with (Sm) whose periods are pairwise incommensurable.
(d) If `Ω(y)` contains periodic points `x₁, x₂` with (Sm) and incommensurable periods, the set
`Γ_y := {γ : some eigenform with ordinate γ is nonzero at y}` has at most 4 elements, i.e. at most two conjugate pairs.
If `Ω(y)` contains a fixed point with (Sm), then `Γ_y ⊆ {θ_x, −θ_x}`.

*Proof.*
(a) By O2, both `ρ` and `ρ′` lie in `{α/2 ± iθ_x}` (smooth case), which forces `γ′ = ±γ`, a contradiction. In the
orbifold case, O2 already gives `h(y) = 0`.
(b) By O2, `e^{iγl}` and `e^{iγ′l}` both lie in `{u_x, ū_x}`. If they are equal, `e^{iδ_−l} = 1`. If they are
conjugate, `e^{iδ_+l} = |u_x|² = 1`. So `δ_−l ∈ 2πZ` or `δ_+l ∈ 2πZ`.
(c) By pigeonhole, two of the three periods fall into the same cyclic group `(2π/|δ|)Z`. Both are nonzero multiples of
`2π/|δ|`, so their ratio is rational. Contradiction.
(d) `Γ_y ⊆ ⋂_{j=1,2}{γ : e^{iγl_j} ∈ {u_j, ū_j}} = ⋃_{s,s′=±}(sθ₁′ + (2π/l₁)Z) ∩ (s′θ₂′ + (2π/l₂)Z)`, where
`u_j = e^{iθ_j′}`. Two arithmetic progressions with incommensurable steps meet in at most one point, and there are 4
sign choices. The fixed-point case is O2(ii). ∎

**Corollary O4 (the ergodic class; `PROVED` under (C), (Def)).** Suppose `m` is ergodic for the flow `φ` (a φ-invariant
measurable set is null or conull). Let `h` be an eigenform with `(h,h) < ∞`.
(i) If `m(X) = ∞`, there is no such `h`. The same holds for measurable `L²(m)` eigen-sections of `Λ¹T*𝓕 ⊗ C`, with
no continuity used. So `Θ` has **empty point spectrum on `Ĥ¹_μ`**.
(ii) If `m(X) < ∞`, then `|h| ≡ c_h > 0` on `supp m`, so `h(x) ≠ 0` at every `x ∈ supp m`. Consequently:
  * a fixed point in `supp m` with (Sm) gives `σ_p(Θ|H̄¹) ⊆ {α/2 ± iθ_x}`;
  * a point of `supp m` with (Orb) gives `σ_p(Θ|H̄¹) = ∅`;
  * three periodic points in `supp m` with (Sm) and pairwise incommensurable periods make `σ_p(Θ|H̄¹)` a single
  conjugate pair (or a real point, or empty);
  * two such points give at most 4 eigenvalues.

*Proof.* `|h|` is measurable and φ-invariant (Lemma 2.1; the pointwise identity holds a.e. for measurable sections), so
`|h| = c` `m`-a.e. by ergodicity.
(i) `(h,h) = c²m(X)` is finite only if `c = 0`, i.e. `h = 0` in `Ĥ¹_μ`.
(ii) `c > 0` by (Def). `{|h| ≠ c}` is open (continuity) and `m`-null, so it misses `supp m`. Now apply O1 at each named
point, with O3(c),(d) for two distinct non-conjugate eigenvalues. ∎

**Corollary O5 (ζ, number fields, Beurling systems; `PROVED` as an exclusion under the stated hypotheses).** Suppose a
DK_1^c carrier obeys Deninger's spectral axiom (R158_001 Lemma T (D1)–(D2); Leichtnam axiom 6]): the eigenvalues of `Θ`
on `H̄¹` are the nontrivial zeros of ζ, which are infinitely many (`N(T) ~ (T/2π)log T`, `CITED` std.). Suppose also
that its prime orbits have lengths `log p` and satisfy (Sm). Then:
* `m` is **not** φ-ergodic if some fixed point with (Sm)/(Orb) lies in `supp m`, or if three prime orbits do. For ζ,
  `log 2, log 3, log 5` are pairwise incommensurable, since `2^a3^b5^c = 1` forces `a = b = c = 0`.
* **If `m(X) = ∞`, `m` is never φ-ergodic.**
* On the set `X_rich := {y : Ω(y) contains a fixed point with (Sm)/(Orb), or three prime orbits}`, at most one
  conjugate pair of zeros has an eigenform that is nonzero at `y`, for each `y` separately (O3).

Two remarks on scope:
* For the archimedean fixed point of ζ with `θ_∞ = 0` (the Γ_R normal form of G1_B2_R4 Prop F), O2(ii) gives
  `ρ = ½`. Since `ζ(½) ≠ 0` (`CITED` std.), **every zero eigenform vanishes on every orbit closure through `x_∞`**.
* The same statements hold verbatim for Dedekind ζ_K (lengths `log N𝔭`; distinct rational primes give incommensurable
  lengths) and for Beurling systems with three multiplicatively independent generalised primes (OT-4/OT-10 uniformity:
  the exclusion is data-blind, as an exclusion may be).
∎

**Lemma O6 (flatness on eigenform supports, any trace; `PROVED` under (C), (Def), leafwise curvature continuous).**
This is G1_B3_R5 Thm F(i) localised. Let `h` be an eigenform with `(h,h) < ∞`. Then `Rm = 0` on
`supp(m|_{\{h≠0\}})`, **without (FT)**.

*Proof.*
* For `s > 0`, `E_s := {|h| > s}` is open and φ-invariant (Lemma 2.1), and `m(E_s) ≤ (h,h)/s² < ∞` (Chebyshev).
* Put `F(s′) := m(E_s ∩ {|Rm| > s′})`. By `|Rm|(φ^t x) = e^{−αt}|Rm|(x)` (G1_B3_R5 Lemma 2.1, re-checked) and
  invariance of `E_s` and `m`, `F(s′) = F(e^{αt}s′)`. So `F` is constant, bounded by `m(E_s)`, and tends to `0` as
  `s′ → ∞`. Hence `F ≡ 0`.
* `Rm` is continuous and vanishes `m`-a.e. on the open set `E_s`, so it vanishes on `supp(m|_{E_s})`. Take the union
  over `s`. ∎

So in B3's escape (X1), the positive Hilbert space still lives on flat leaves wherever it is nonzero. Bochner
parallelism (B3 Thm F(ii)) would need (IBP) on the non-compact sets `E_s`; I do not claim it (§7).

## §4 Inverse checks (the laws must not exclude what exists)

* **Deninger's CM-elliptic system** (DLS §7 Example, read): `X ≅ (C ×_Γ T_πΓ) ×_{p^Z} R*_+`, `α = 1`.
  * Every closed orbit has length `k log p`, and the return map at a point fixed by `π^k` is `z ↦ π^k z`, so
  `λ = π^k` with `|π^k| = p^{k/2} = e^{αkl/2}` ✓.
  * Theorem O1(i) says an eigenform nonzero there has `p^{kρ} ∈ {π^k, π̄^k}`. The eigenforms `f_j(t)dz`, with
  `f_j = e^{(2πij/log p)t}` a leafwise-constant function (`t` the `R*_+`-coordinate mod `log p`), have
  `p^ρ = π·e^{2πij} = π` ✓.
  * So O1 returns **exactly** the `H¹` zeros of `ζ_E`, namely `(1 − πp^{−s})(1 − π̄p^{−s}) = 0`, and nothing more.
  * The flow is ergodic (the return map `×π^{−1}` on the compact solenoid is an ergodic group automorphism, since
  `|π| ≠ 1`; std.). All periods lie in `(log p)Z`, so O3(b) is satisfied with `δ_− ∈ (2π/log p)Z`. **Not excluded** ✓.
  * B3's Thm F does not exclude it either, because (D) fails: the leaves `C × {t}` are not dense in `X`, and `H̄⁰` is
  infinite-dimensional (the functions of `t` mod `log p`).
* **Function fields (Weil's case).** All lengths lie in `(log q)Z`. O3(c) never triggers, and O4(ii) allows a whole
  progression per eigenform family. Not excluded ✓. The theorem separates `Spec Z` from curves over `F_q` precisely by
  the incommensurability of the `log p`. HEURISTIC reading: this is the dynamical form of "`Spec Z` has no single
  Frobenius length".
* **Deninger Thm 4.1 manifolds** (compact 3-manifold, codimension one, dense leaf): here G1_B2_R4 Cor M1 already puts
  every closed orbit outside `supp m`. O1–O4 are consistent, being vacuous at such orbits.
* **The adèle class space / Connes–Consani scaling-site sector** (`d = 0`: the periodic orbits `C_p ≅ R*_+/p^Z`;
  Connes–Consani, *Geometry of the scaling site*, arXiv:1603.03191, `CITED` std.). This carrier has no leaves, and
  Theorem O's degree-0 version is the classical fact (`CITED` std.): a continuous eigenfunction of a flow nonvanishing
  on an orbit closure through a periodic orbit of period `l` has `e^{iγl} = 1`. So a continuous eigen-section seeing
  `C_p` and `C_q` (`p ≠ q`) is φ-invariant. This agrees with the record that the zeros appear there only through
  non-continuous data: Connes' absorption / cokernel, A17 `EQUIV`, and B10_R5's "no normal invariant state ⇒ no point
  spectrum". HEURISTIC reading: it explains why CC's Riemann–Roch on `C_p` sees one prime at a time.
* **Falsifiers of record.**
  * `Ξ_a` needs two conformal factors (R158_001 OT-5 row), so it lies outside DK_α. Theorem O does not apply and claims
  nothing.
  * DMV/Beurling data with incommensurable lengths are excluded in the ergodic class, which is correct behaviour for an
  exclusion.
  * No positivity is produced for any falsifier, because nothing here is a positivity.

## §5 What remains: the non-ergodic residual

### 5.1 Precise statement (`OPEN`)

After Corollaries O4–O5, an (E-orb) DK_1^c carrier for ζ must have `m` **non-ergodic** under `φ`. Take its spectral
cover: for each zero pair `ρ`, the open φ-invariant set `U_ρ := {h_ρ ≠ 0}`, where `h_ρ` ranges over the eigenspace. The
constraints are:
* **(R1)** `m(U_ρ) > 0` (definiteness);
* **(R2)** `U_ρ ∩ X_rich` meets `U_{ρ′} ∩ X_rich` only if `ρ′ ∈ {ρ, ρ̄}` (O3);
* **(R3)** every `y ∈ U_ρ` has `Ω(y)` free of `x_∞` when `θ_∞ = 0` (O5);
* **(R4)** if `Ω(y)` contains exactly one prime orbit `x_p`, then `p^{iγ} ∈ {u_p, ū_p}` (O2).

Also, by B2_R4 Thm M(iv), the positive form sees `p` only if `x_p ∈ supp m`. None of (R1)–(R4) contradicts this: the
zero eigenforms may be nonzero near `x_p` on orbits whose **closures** avoid `x_p`.

The construction problem is therefore:

> find a non-ergodic conformal lamination whose zero eigenforms live on orbit closures that avoid the archimedean point
> and contain at most two prime orbits, while the Lefschetz formula still produces every `log p·δ_{k log p}` and the
> Γ_R term.

I have no candidate.

### 5.2 Why I do not label it an installation (`HEURISTIC`)

The index set of the pieces `{U_ρ}` is in bijection with the zero pairs on `X_rich`, which is the pattern of the tail
wall (B4_R4 W♯: one summand per zero, Lemma T). But the pieces need not be defined from the zeros. A prime-free
component (`Ω(y)` containing no closed orbit and no fixed point) is unconstrained by Theorem O.

A conditional closure would follow from a component-wise localised trace formula (L_ω), stronger than B2_R4's (L):
* On a prime-free closure the trace would be supported at `{0}`, forcing an empty spectrum there (a positive discrete
  spectral measure cannot be a polynomial).
* On a one-prime closure the spectrum would be a union of full progressions `γ + (2π/log p)Z`. These contain
  non-zeros of ζ by Li–Radziwiłł (arXiv 1208.2684, Thm 4, `CITED` via G1_B10_R4), contradicting the spectral axiom.

But (L_ω) is not a theorem. The Lefschetz trace of the record is built on the holonomy-invariant `μ`, and φ-ergodic
components of `m` are not of the form `vol ⊗ μ_ω`. So this closure is `HEURISTIC` only.

## §7 What is new, what is not, and the weakest step

**New (to the corpus; literature status as far as I checked).**
1. **Theorem O1**, the local Weil-number law at periodic points, smooth fixed points and orbifold points, with **no
  completeness**. Lemma 2.2 is the local version of B2_R4 Lemma H0 and removes the completeness caveat the panel
  raised against Theorem H / Cor H1.
2. **Theorems O2–O3**, the orbit-closure law and the arithmetic separation of eigenforms by incommensurable periods.
3. **Corollary O4**: ergodic DK_α^c carriers carry at most one pair (finite trace, with `x_∞` or three primes in the
  support) or nothing (infinite trace). This closes (X1) of G1_B3_R5 in the ergodic case.
4. **Lemma O6**: B3's curvature mechanism localised to eigenform supports, at infinite trace.
5. The sharp inverse check: O1 recovers exactly the `H¹` spectrum of `E/F_p` in Deninger's CM example.
6. The precise non-ergodic residual (R1)–(R4).

**Not new.**
* The degree-0 statement (continuous flow eigenvalues satisfy `e^{iγl} = 1` at periodic orbits in the orbit closure) is
  classical (std.).
* Lemma 2.1 is Deninger's Thm 4.1 read pointwise.
* The curvature scaling is B3_R5 Lemma 2.1.
* Lemma 2.3 is elementary complex analysis.
* The combination with conformal normal forms at closed orbits and fixed points, and its use as an exclusion for
  (E-orb), I have not found in Deninger, Leichtnam, Kopei or Kim (DLS read in full at §4–§7; the others at the level of
  G1_B2_R4's reading).

**Not claimed.** No object, no trace formula for ζ, no sign at any window, nothing for `T > 0.8`, no step toward the
"for all T" arrow. All results are exclusions or necessary conditions.

**Weakest steps.**
* **(C) for laminations.** The continuous Hodge realisation is a theorem only for Riemannian foliations on compact
  manifolds (Álvarez López–Kordyukov, via DLS (13)), and there Cor M1 of B2_R4 already removes the closed orbits from
  `supp m`. For the non-manifold (E-orb) laminations, (C) is Deninger's standing assumption, not a theorem. Without
  continuity only O4(i) survives (it is measurable). O1–O3 then apply only to eigenforms that happen to be continuous
  at the relevant orbit closures.
* **(Sm_x)/(Orb_x).** A leaf germ that is singular in some other way at `x_∞` (not smooth, not a finite rotation
  quotient) is not covered. G1_B2_R4 Prop F puts Leichtnam's §4.4 singularity in the **transversal**
  (`μ ∝ |y|^{−1/2}dy`), which leaves the leaf germ smooth; that identification is `HEURISTIC` there.
* **Ergodicity is a hypothesis.** Deninger's `H̄⁰ = R` comes from a dense leaf of `𝓕`, not from φ-ergodicity of `m`.
  The non-ergodic residual of §5 is genuinely open, and its closure through (L_ω) is `HEURISTIC`.
* **Lemma O6** stops at flatness. Parallelism at infinite trace would need (IBP) on the non-compact invariant sets
  `E_s`, which I do not prove.
