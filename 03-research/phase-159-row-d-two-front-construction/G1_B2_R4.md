## §0 Verdict

**No object is built.** The round-3 panel (0/5, "excluded": Cor H is a correct unconditional exclusion of its own
class) named one escape as "the honest open problem": **(E-orb)**. In (E-orb) Frobenius is not an operator family.
Primes appear only as closed orbits of the flow, as in Deninger's actual axioms (Leichtnam 1307.3851 §4.1; R158_001
row "Deninger's formalism"). Round 3 had no theorem about this class. This round supplies three.

The class is **DK_α**, Deninger's own positivity mechanism (Deninger, *Arithmetic geometry and analysis on foliated
spaces*, Thm 4.1 and Cor 5.5, re-read this phase from the source PDF). It consists of a leafwise metric that the flow
scales by `e^{αt}`, and a trace `H̄^d → R` given by a holonomy-invariant transverse measure `μ`. Number theory needs
`α = 1` (Deninger §6, (31)). For DK_α with `α ≠ 0`:

1. **Theorem M (orbit–measure law; `PROVED`).** Let `γ` be any closed orbit. Every flow segment of `γ` is `μ`-null, and
  so is its saturation. Pick a flow box on a transversal at `x ∈ γ`. Then either `x ∉ supp μ`, or the slice `V`
  transverse to the flow inside the transversal is non-discrete at `x` and carries a non-atomic measure `ν`. In the
  second case the first-return map `P_γ` on `V` has **measure modulus exactly `e^{−αl(γ)}` at the orbit**. For ζ
  (`α = 1`, `l = log p`) this modulus is `p^{−1}`: the Haar modulus of multiplication by `p` on `Q_p` is **forced by
  global positivity**. Nothing local is assumed.
2. **Corollary M1 (codimension-one wall; `PROVED`).** Take a codimension-one foliated manifold, compact or not, with
  **fixed points allowed**. In class DK_α with `α ≠ 0`, every closed orbit lies in the open set `X ∖ supp μ`, where
  the positive form vanishes identically. The positive carrier is the Hilbert space of the lamination `Y = supp μ`,
  and `Y` has **no closed orbit**. This extends C5 (Deninger Rem. 2–3), which assumed compactness and no fixed points,
  to the case Deninger himself says number fields require ("the flow must have fixed points").
3. **Theorem H (homothety law; `PROVED`, for complete leaves).** Every leaf through a closed orbit or a fixed point is
  isometric to Euclidean `R^d`. The return map is a linear similarity `z ↦ λz` with `|λ| = e^{αl/2}`. Each leaf
  carries at most one periodic or fixed point. For `d = 2` with oriented leaves this forces `ε_γ(k) = +1` for all `k`,
  and for ζ `λ_p = √p·u_p` with `|u_p| = 1`. Deninger postulated the sign condition and its consequence (his "Fact",
  §7 Rem. 5). Here both are **derived**. A compact invariant leaf forces `α = 0` (Prop K), which excludes the
  compact-leaf model of the archimedean place (Kim, 1906.02424, Def. 1.5) under conformality.
4. **Proposition F (archimedean transverse law; `PROVED` given `C¹` linearisation, CITED).** Take a fixed point in
  `supp μ` with a one-dimensional transversal and normal rate `κ`. In the linearising coordinate, `μ = c_±|y|^β dy`
  with `κ(β+1) = −α`. For `α = 1`: a smooth transverse density gives `κ = −1`, the complex-place factor
  `(1−e^{−t})^{−1}`. The real-place factor `κ = −2` holds **iff** `β = −½`, i.e. iff `μ` becomes smooth on the ramified
  double cover `y = w²`. This is the singular structure of Leichtnam's §4.4 resolution (C6, A6), obtained here as a
  consequence and not as a choice.
5. **Corollary H2 (`PROVED` conditional on Candel's hypothesis, CITED).** Take `K ≠ Q`, where Deninger expects
  `χ_Co < 0` for every measure (his §7 Rem. 7). A compact regular DK_1 lamination with continuous leafwise metric that
  satisfies Candel's hypothesis (every harmonic measure has negative Euler characteristic) has **no closed orbits and
  no fixed points**. So Deninger's list of expectations is inconsistent for `K ≠ Q` unless the lamination is singular
  or the leaves through the primes are incomplete.

**What this does for G1.** (E-orb) was an unstructured escape. It is now a problem with a forced local normal form: at
each `p`, the Deninger CM-elliptic local model (flat leaf, similarity of ratio `√p`, transverse slice of modulus `p^{−1}`).
At `∞`, a flat leaf with `φ^t z = e^{t/2+iθt}z` and transverse density `|y|^{−1/2}`. Codimension-one manifolds are
excluded outright. What remains **`OPEN`** is the global gluing of these local models into one lamination with
`dim H̄⁰ = dim H̄² = 1` and a Lefschetz formula equal to ζ's explicit formula. I have no candidate for the gluing.

## §1 The class DK_α

**Definition 1.1 (Deninger–Kähler flow datum).** `(X, 𝓕, g, φ, μ)` consists of:
* `X`: a second-countable, locally compact foliated space in the sense of Moore–Schochet. Locally it is
  `R^d × T` with `T` locally compact, and the leaves are oriented, of dimension `d ≥ 2` (d = 2 for Deninger's
  Riemann-surface leaves).
* `g`: a leafwise Riemannian metric, leafwise `C^∞` and transversally continuous.
* `φ^t`: a continuous flow mapping leaves to leaves, leafwise smooth, and **conformal**:
  `g(Tφ^t v, Tφ^t w) = e^{αt} g(v,w)` for `v, w ∈ T_x𝓕` (Deninger (20)). The flow is transverse to `𝓕` off its
  fixed-point set `Fix`. A point of `X ∖ Fix` is **periodic** if `φ^l x = x` for some `l > 0`, and its minimal such `l`
  is `l(γ)`.
* `μ`: a holonomy-invariant transverse Radon measure (not assumed of full support), with Ruelle–Sullivan current `C_μ`.
  It satisfies **(D2)** `C_μ(φ^{t*}β) = e^{dαt/2} C_μ(β)` for every compactly supported, leafwise-smooth leafwise
  `d`-form `β`.

The positive form is `(h,h′) := C_μ(h ∧ ⋆_g h̄′)` on leafwise 1-forms (for `d = 2`).

**Lemma 1.2 (DK_α is Deninger's mechanism; `PROVED`, Ruelle–Sullivan facts CITED (std.), Candel–Conlon I §10).**
(a) Assume Deninger's setting of Thm 4.1: `H̄^d` is one-dimensional, spanned by `[vol_g]`, and the trace is
`tr = C_μ`. Then (D2) holds.
(b) (D2) holds ⟺ the measure `m := vol_g ⊗ μ` on `X` is `φ^t`-invariant ⟺ `μ(φ^t τ) = e^{−dαt/2} μ(τ)` for every
Borel transversal `τ`.
(c) For `d = 2`, `(φ^{t*}h, φ^{t*}h′) = e^{αt}(h,h′)`. So on the Hilbert completion `Ĥ¹_μ`, `e^{−αt/2}φ^{t*}` is
unitary, and eigenvalues of the generator `Θ` there have `Re = α/2` (Deninger Thm 4.1/Cor 5.5).

*Proof.* (a) `C_μ` vanishes on leafwise-exact forms (Stokes on plaques plus holonomy invariance). It is continuous in
the `C⁰` topology on compactly supported forms, so it factors through `H̄^d`. Because `φ^t` maps leaves to leaves, the
functional `β ↦ C_μ(φ^{t*}β)` is the current of the holonomy-invariant measure `φ^{−t}_*μ`, and it also factors through
`H̄^d = R[vol_g]`. So `C_μ∘φ^{t*} = c(t)·C_μ`. Conformality gives `φ^{t*}vol_g = e^{dαt/2}vol_g` pointwise, hence
`c(t) = e^{dαt/2}`.
(b) Write `β = f·vol_g`. Then `φ^{t*}β = (f∘φ^t)e^{dαt/2}vol_g`, and (D2) says `∫ f∘φ^t dm = ∫ f dm` for all `f ∈ C_c`,
which is invariance of `m`. In a flow-compatible chart `m = vol_g ⊗ μ`, and leaf areas scale by `e^{dαt/2}`, so the
transverse part scales inversely. The disintegration is unique (std.).
(c) In dimension 2, `⋆` on 1-forms depends only on the conformal class, so `φ^{t*}` commutes with `⋆`. Then
`(φ^*h, φ^*h′) = C_μ(φ^*(h∧⋆h̄′)) = e^{αt}(h,h′)` by (D2). (This is G1_B2_R1 Prop 1, restated.) ∎

Consistency checks against the literature: in Leichtnam's laminations, `μ = λ_g μ_L dx` is `φ`-invariant and
`τ_Λ∘φ^t_* = e^{−t}τ_Λ` (math/0603576, Prop 2.3–2.4, read). In Deninger's CM-elliptic example, `g = e^t Re(ξη̄)` with
`α = 1` (DLS §7). Both are DK_1.

## §2 Theorem M: the orbit–measure law

Put `α_d := dα/2 ≠ 0`, so that `μ(φ^tτ) = e^{−α_d t}μ(τ)` (Lemma 1.2(b)).

**Theorem M (`PROVED`; the local-section input is CITED).** Let `(X,𝓕,g,φ,μ) ∈ DK_α` with `α ≠ 0`, and let `γ` be a
closed orbit of length `l`, with `x ∈ γ`.
(i) Every compact flow segment `σ ⊂ γ` is a Borel transversal with `μ(σ) = 0`. The saturation `Sat(γ)` is `μ`-null.
(ii) If some transversal at `x` is, near `x`, contained in the orbit (a one-dimensional transversal, e.g. a
codimension-one foliated manifold), then `x ∉ supp μ`.
(iii) In general, choose a local transversal `T ∋ x` and a flow box `h: (−ε,ε) × V → T`, `h(s,v) = ψ^s(v)`, where
`ψ` is the local flow induced on `T` and `V ∋ x` is a local cross-section. Then:
  * `h^*μ = e^{−α_d s} ds ⊗ ν` for a Radon measure `ν` on `V`.
  * The return map has the form `Φ(s,v) = (s + σ(v), P(v))`, with `P` a local homeomorphism of `(V, x)` fixing `x` and
  `σ` continuous with `σ(x) = 0`.
  * For every Borel `B ∋ x` in a small enough neighbourhood, with `δ_B := sup_B|σ|`:
  `e^{−|α_d|δ_B} ≤ ν(P(B)) / (e^{−α_d l}ν(B)) ≤ e^{|α_d|δ_B}` whenever `ν(B) > 0`.
  Hence `ν(P(B))/ν(B) → e^{−α_d l}` as `B ↓ {x}` (**modulus law**).
(iv) (Dichotomy.) Either `ν = 0` near `x`, i.e. `x ∉ supp μ`, or `x` is a non-isolated point of `V`, `ν({x}) = 0`,
`ν(U) > 0` for every neighbourhood `U` of `x` in `V`, and `P` has `ν`-modulus `e^{−α_d l} ≠ 1` at `x`.

*Proof.* (i) `σ` meets each plaque of a small chart at most once (transversality off `Fix`), so it is a Borel
transversal. It is compact, so `μ(σ) < ∞` (Radon). `φ^l` fixes `γ` pointwise, so `φ^l(σ) = σ` and
`μ(σ) = μ(φ^lσ) = e^{−α_d l}μ(σ)`. Since `α_d l ≠ 0`, `μ(σ) = 0`. `Sat(γ) ∩ T` is the union of the images of pieces of
`γ` under the holonomy pseudogroup, which is countably generated because `X` is second countable. So it is a countable
union of null sets.
(ii) `T` is locally an orbit segment, so `μ` vanishes on a transversal through `x`, and by holonomy invariance on every
small transversal through `x`.
(iii) The flow is transverse near `x`, so `ψ^u := hol∘φ^u` is a continuous local flow on `T` without fixed points near
`x`. Local cross-sections and the tube `(−ε,ε) × V ≅` neighbourhood exist for such flows on locally compact metrizable
spaces (Whitney 1933; Bebutov; Nemytskii–Stepanov Ch. V; CITED).
  * By holonomy invariance, `μ(ψ^uA) = μ(φ^uA) = e^{−α_d u}μ(A)`. So `m′ := e^{α_d s}h^*μ` is invariant under local
  `s`-translations. For each Borel `B ⊂ V`, the measure `E ↦ m′(E × B)` on `(−ε,ε)` is a translation-invariant Radon
  measure, hence `ν(B)·Leb`. This defines `ν`.
  * `Φ := hol∘φ^l` fixes `x`, commutes with `ψ^u` as a germ (both are induced by `φ`), and is a local homeomorphism.
  So `Φ(s,v) = ψ^sΦ(0,v) = (s + σ(v), P(v))`, and `Φ(x) = x` gives `σ(x) = 0`, `P(x) = x`.
  * For `E = (a,b) × B`: `m(Φ E) = (∫_a^b e^{−α_d s}ds)·∫_{P(B)} e^{−α_dσ(P^{−1}w)}dν(w)`. Setting this equal to
  `e^{−α_d l}m(E) = e^{−α_d l}(∫_a^b e^{−α_d s}ds)ν(B)` gives
  `∫_{P(B)} e^{−α_dσ∘P^{−1}}dν = e^{−α_d l}ν(B)`. The integrand lies in `[e^{−|α_d|δ_B}, e^{|α_d|δ_B}]`, and `σ` is
  continuous at `x` with value 0.
(iv) If `x` is isolated in `V`, then `T` is locally `(−ε,ε) × {x}` and (ii) applies. Otherwise (i) gives
`ν({x}) = μ(σ)/∫e^{−α_d s}ds = 0`. If `ν(U) > 0` for all `U`, apply (iii) to the neighbourhoods themselves. ∎

**Corollary M1 (codimension-one wall; `PROVED`).** Let `X` be a (possibly non-compact) manifold with a codimension-one
foliation. The flow may have fixed points and need not have a dense leaf. Then in DK_α with `α ≠ 0`:
(a) All closed orbits lie in the open saturated `φ`-invariant set `U := X ∖ supp μ`. The measure `m = vol_g ⊗ μ` is
zero on `U`.
(b) The positive form satisfies `(h,h) = ∫_X |h|²_g dm`. So `Ĥ¹_μ` depends only on the restriction to `Y := supp μ`,
a closed, saturated, `φ`-invariant set **containing no closed orbit**. Every leafwise form supported in `U`, in
particular near any closed orbit, has norm 0.
(c) (E-orb) requires Frobenius at `p` to be a closed orbit of the flow **on the carrier of the positive form**. That
requirement fails, unconditionally.
(d) (`PROVED` conditional on (L).) Let (L) be the localisation property that every proved foliated Lefschetz formula
has: the distributional trace on a carrier depending only on `(Y, 𝓕|Y, g, φ|Y, μ)` is supported on `{0}`, the periods
of closed orbits in `Y`, and fixed-point terms that are smooth on `R ∖ 0`. (Examples: Álvarez López–Kordyukov; Deninger
Thm 7.8; Leichtnam Thm 2; ALKL 2402.06671; all CITED.) Under (L), the trace formula of `Ĥ^•_μ` has no atom at any
`t = k log p`, so it is not ζ's explicit formula, whose prime atoms `log p·δ_{k log p}` are nonzero.

*Proof.* (a) and (c) follow from Theorem M(ii). `U` is saturated and `φ`-invariant because `supp μ` is: `φ` scales `μ`,
and holonomy preserves it. For (b), `|h|²_g vol_g = h ∧ ⋆h̄` and `C_μ(f vol_g) = ∫ f dm`. For (d), the fixed-point terms
`W_x|_{R∖0} = ε_x|1−e^{κ_x t}|^{−1}` (and its `t<0` analogue) are smooth on `R ∖ 0` (DLS §7 Rem. 2). ∎

**Remark M2 (relation to C5; `PROVED` comparison).** C5 (Deninger Cor 5.5, Rem. 2–3) assumes a compact 3-manifold, a
dense leaf, and a flow that is everywhere transverse, so no fixed points. It concludes `α = 0` and that the length group
is finitely generated. Corollary M1 needs **none** of compactness, a dense leaf, absence of fixed points, or `d = 2`.
It shows that, even in the setting Deninger says number fields require (fixed points, an infinitely generated length
group), a manifold of codimension one separates the primes from the positive form. The escape from C5 must therefore
be **transversally non-manifold at the orbits** (Theorem M(iv)), not merely non-compact.

**Remark M3 (sanity checks against the two realised DK_1 systems; `PROVED` by direct computation).**
* Deninger's CM-elliptic system `X = (C ×_Γ T_πΓ) ×_{p^Z} R_{>0}`: `T = (flow) × T_πΓ`, `T_πΓ ≅ Z_p`, `ν` = Haar, and
  the return map on the slice is multiplication by `π` (π-adic valuation 1). Haar modulus: `ν(πB) = p^{−1}ν(B)`,
  which is `e^{−α_d l}` with `α_d = 1`, `l = log p`. Theorem M(iv), second branch. ✓
* Leichtnam's laminations: `V = Z_p^m`, `μ_L(q·T) = q^{−1}μ_L(T)` (his Lemma 6.1) is the modulus law with
  `l = log q`. ✓
Neither system is excluded, as it must not be.

## §3 Theorem H: the homothety law on leaves through periodic and fixed points

**Lemma H0 (`PROVED`; the classical statement is Kobayashi–Nomizu I, Ch. VI Thm 3.6, CITED (std.)).** Let `(L, g)` be
a complete connected Riemannian manifold with a diffeomorphism `f` such that `f^*g = c²g`, `c ≠ 1`. Then `f` has a
unique fixed point, `(L, g)` is isometric to Euclidean `R^n`, and in Euclidean coordinates centred at the fixed point
`f(z) = cAz` with `A ∈ O(n)`.
*Proof.* Replacing `f` by `f^{−1}`, assume `c < 1`. Since `f` is bijective and scales lengths of curves by `c`, it
scales distance exactly by `c`. It is a contraction of a complete metric space, so it has a unique fixed point `x₀`.
Sectional curvature transforms as `K(f(p), df σ) = c^{−2}K(p, σ)`. Iterating, `|K(p,σ)| = c^{2n}|K(fⁿp, dfⁿσ)|`, and
`fⁿp → x₀` where `K` is bounded, so `K ≡ 0`. For a loop `ℓ` at `x₀`, `fⁿ∘ℓ` has length `cⁿ|ℓ|` and eventually lies in
a convex normal ball, so it is null-homotopic. Since `f_*` is an automorphism of `π₁(L,x₀)`, `[ℓ] = 1`. A flat,
complete, simply connected manifold is Euclidean (Killing–Hopf, CITED (std.)). A distance-scaling bijection of `R^n` is
`c` times an isometry, hence affine, and it fixes 0. ∎

**Theorem H (`PROVED`).** Let `(X,𝓕,g,φ,μ) ∈ DK_α`, `α ≠ 0`, and let `L` be a leaf that is complete for `g`.
(i) If `L` contains a periodic point `x` of period `l`, then `φ^l(L) = L`, `(L,g) ≅ R^d`, and `φ^l|_L(z) = e^{αl/2}A z`
in coordinates centred at `x`.
(ii) If `L` contains a fixed point `y`, then `(L,g) ≅ R^d` and `φ^t|_L(z) = e^{αt/2}A_t z` with `A_t` a one-parameter
subgroup of `SO(d)`.
(iii) (**One periodic point per leaf.**) `L` contains at most one point that is periodic or fixed. The orbit `γ` of `x`
meets `L` only in `x`.
(iv) For `d = 2` with `T𝓕` oriented: `φ^{kl}|_L(z) = λ^k z` with `λ = e^{αl/2}e^{iθ_γ} ∈ C`, and
`ε_γ(k) := sgn det(1 − Tφ^{kl}|T_x𝓕) = sgn|1−λ^k|² = +1` for all `k ∈ Z ∖ 0`.
Also `|det Tφ^{kl}|T_x𝓕| = e^{kαl}`.

*Proof.* (i) `φ^l` maps `L` onto the leaf through `φ^lx = x`, bijectively and leafwise smoothly, with
`(φ^l)^*g = e^{αl}g` on `T𝓕`. Apply Lemma H0 with `c = e^{αl/2} ≠ 1`. The fixed point of `φ^l|_L` is `x`.
(ii) Each `φ^t|_L` for `t ≠ 0` is a homothety fixing `y`. Lemma H0 gives flatness, and in Euclidean coordinates
`t ↦ e^{−αt/2}φ^t|_L` is a continuous one-parameter group of linear isometries fixing 0, hence lies in `SO(d)`.
(iii) Suppose `x, x′ ∈ L` are periodic or fixed. Take `t, t′ ≠ 0` with `φ^t x = x` and `φ^{t′}x′ = x′`. Both
`φ^t|_L` and `φ^{t′}|_L` are homotheties of `R^d` with ratios `≠ 1`, and they commute. So each maps the other's
unique fixed point to itself: `φ^t x′` is fixed by `φ^{t′}`, hence equals `x′`. The unique fixed point of `φ^t|_L` is
`x`, so `x′ = x`. If `φ^s x ∈ L` for some `s`, the same argument with `φ^s x` in place of `x′` gives `φ^s x = x`, so
`s ∈ lZ`.
(iv) `s ↦ φ^s` is continuous, starts at the identity, and maps leaves to leaves, so it preserves the continuous
orientation of `T𝓕`. Hence `A ∈ SO(2)`, which acts as multiplication by `e^{iθ_γ}`. For `λ ∈ C` acting on `R²`,
`det(1 − λ^k) = |1 − λ^k|²`, which is positive because `|λ^k| = e^{kαl/2} ≠ 1`. Also `det λ^k = |λ|^{2k}`. ∎

**Corollary H1 (ζ's local model at `p`; `PROVED` for complete leaves, α = 1, d = 2).** At the orbit `γ_p`
(`l = log p`), the leaf is `C`. The return map is `z ↦ λ_p z` with `λ_p = √p·u_p`, `|u_p| = 1`. The signs are
`ε_{γ_p}(k) = +1` for all `k`. The coefficient of `δ_{k log p}` for `k ≤ −1` in Deninger's (32)–(35) is
`ε·|det| = p^k`, exactly as in ζ's explicit formula. Deninger (§7 Rem. 5, "Fact") showed the converse direction:
`ε = +1` ⟺ `Tφ^{kl} = e^{kl/2}O_k` on `T_x𝓕`, **at the point**. Theorem H derives linearity **on the whole leaf**, and
derives `ε = +1` rather than assuming it. Deninger asks whether `λ_p` is a Weil number, which would bring in an elliptic
curve over `F_p` (Honda–Tate); that remains **`OPEN`**, and the realised CM example has `λ_p = π`.

**Proposition K (compact invariant leaves; `PROVED`).** If a compact leaf `L` satisfies `φ^t(L) = L` for some
`t ≠ 0`, then `α = 0`. *Proof.* `vol_g(L) = vol_g(φ^tL) = e^{dαt/2}vol_g(L)`. ∎
So the archimedean model of Kim (1906.02424, Def. 1.5(i): finitely many compact `φ`-invariant leaves `L_i^∞`) is
incompatible with conformality `α ≠ 0` on those leaves. In DK_1 the archimedean place must be a fixed point (Theorem
H(ii)) on a non-compact flat leaf.

**Corollary H2 (`K ≠ Q`; `PROVED` conditional; Candel's theorem CITED: A. Candel, *Uniformization of surface
laminations*, Ann. Sci. ÉNS 26 (1993) 489–516).** Let `X` be a compact regular lamination by surfaces (no singular
points) in DK_α, `α ≠ 0`, with `g` continuous on `X`. Then all leaves are `g`-complete (uniform plaque size; CITED (std.),
Candel–Conlon I). Suppose Candel's hypothesis holds: `χ(𝓕, m) < 0` for every harmonic measure `m`. Then no leaf is
conformally `C`, so by Theorem H **there is no closed orbit and no fixed point**.
Reading: Deninger expects `χ_Co(𝓕, ν) = −log|d_K| < 0` for all transverse measures when `K ≠ Q` (DLS §7 Rem. 7, which
invokes Candel). Harmonic measures include the measures `vol⊗ν` but are more general, so Deninger's hypothesis is weaker
than Candel's as stated. The corollary is therefore conditional. With that caveat it shows that Deninger's expectations
for `K ≠ Q` (compact, regular, conformal `α = 1`, negative Euler characteristic, primes as closed orbits) are
**jointly inconsistent** unless the lamination is singular (for instance at the archimedean fixed points, as in
Leichtnam §4.4) or the leaves through the primes are incomplete. For `K = Q`, `χ_Co = −log|d_Q| = 0` and there is no
conflict: flat leaves are allowed.

## §4 Proposition F: the archimedean transverse law (OT-12)

**Proposition F (`PROVED`, given a `C¹` linearisation of the normal flow; Sternberg's 1-dimensional linearisation for
hyperbolic `C²` germs is CITED (std.)).** Let `y₀ ∈ Fix ∩ supp μ` have a one-dimensional transversal `T`, on which
`φ^t` induces (with holonomy) a local flow `Φ_t` fixing `y₀` with `Φ_t′(y₀) = e^{κt}`, `κ ≠ 0`. In a coordinate where
`Φ_t(y) = e^{κt}y`, set `F_±(r) := μ(±(0,r])`. Then `F_±(r) = c_± r^{−α_d/κ}`, i.e.
`μ = c_±′|y|^{β}dy` on each side with **`κ(β+1) = −α_d`**. Local finiteness forces `sgn κ = −sgn α`.
*Proof.* Lemma 1.2(b) gives `F_±(e^{κt}r) = μ(Φ_t(±(0,r])) = e^{−α_d t}F_±(r)` for all small `r` and all `t` (the
direction of `t` is chosen so that it stays in the chart). Put `r = e^{κt}r₀`: `F_±(r) = F_±(r₀)(r/r₀)^{−α_d/κ}`. The
limit `F_±(r) → 0` as `r → 0` requires `−α_d/κ > 0`. ∎

**Consequences for ζ (`α = 1`, `d = 2`, so `α_d = 1`; `PROVED` from Prop F, matching `HEURISTIC`).**
* Smooth positive transverse density (`β = 0`): `κ = −1`. The Guillemin–Sternberg index for `t > 0` is
  `(1 − e^{−t})^{−1}`, the **complex**-place factor (Deninger (6)).
* Real place, `κ = −2`: this holds iff `β = −½`, i.e. `μ = c|y|^{−1/2}dy`. Under `y = w²`,
  `|y|^{−1/2}dy = 2 sgn(w)dw`. The density is smooth upstairs, where the rate is `κ/2 = −1`. So **the real place is
  forced to be the `Z/2`-quotient of a complex-place fixed point with smooth measure.** This is exactly the singular
  structure of Leichtnam §4.4 (`S_{Q[i]} → S_Q`, a mild singularity at `x_∞`; C6/A6). Here it is **derived** from
  conformal positivity; the record had it as a formal resolution. (`HEURISTIC`: identifying this double cover with
  Leichtnam's.)
* The `t < 0` riddle of C6 is not solved here: Prop F concerns only the transverse measure.

## §5 Assembly: the forced local normal form of an (E-orb) object for ζ

Combining Lemma 1.2, Theorems M and H, and Props F and K: any DK_1 lamination with 2-dimensional leaves whose closed
orbits have lengths `k log p`, complete at the orbits, satisfies the following (`PROVED` as necessary conditions):

| place | leaf | leafwise return | transverse slice | signs / weights |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `p` (orbit `γ_p`) | flat `C`, carrying no other periodic or fixed point | `z ↦ √p·u_p z` | non-atomic `ν_p`, return `P_p` of modulus `p^{−1}` at the orbit; **not** a manifold point of a codimension-one transversal | `ε = +1` ∀k; `|det| = p^k` |
| `∞` (fixed point) | flat `C`, not compact (Prop K) | `φ^t z = e^{t/2}e^{iθt}z` | `μ ∝ |y|^{−1/2}dy` if the transversal is 1-dim and `κ = −2`; smooth on the double cover | `ε_x = +1` |

This is Deninger's CM-elliptic local model at each `p` (where `ν_p` = Haar on `T_πΓ ≅ Z_p` and `P_p = π`), together
with the ramified complex model at `∞`. **`HEURISTIC`:** the slice `(V_p, ν_p, P_p)` is a self-similar non-atomic
germ with ratio `p^{−1}`, as for `(Z_p, Haar, ×p)`. Theorem M does not force it to be `Z_p`: `(R^n, Leb, ×p^{−1/n})` has
the same modulus. The modulus alone cannot separate these; the Lefschetz weights would have to.

**The remaining problem (`OPEN`, precise).** Glue these local models into one lamination `S` so that:
(1) `H̄⁰ = R` and `H̄² = R[vol_g]`, with `supp μ = S`;
(2) the local models above hold at every prime, and at `∞`;
(3) a Lefschetz formula on `Ĥ^•_μ` holds with orbit side equal to ζ's explicit formula.
By Theorem M(i), each prime's orbit passes through `μ`-null slices, so the prime atoms must come from the non-atomic
germ `(V_p, ν_p)`. In Deninger 7.8 and Leichtnam Thm 2 they do, through a single `p` with `V = Z_p^m`. **No known
lamination carries such germs for infinitely many distinct `p` at once, with a single transverse measure scaled by
`e^{−t}`.** The adelic solenoid `A_Q/Q` of G1_B2_R1 has the right transversals (`A_f`), but it has no fixed point and
`H¹ = 0` (R1 Cor D1). The adèle class space `A_Q/Q^×` puts periodic orbits at the Haar-null sets `{a_p = 0}`, which
agrees with Theorem M(i), but it is not a lamination (C5 and R158_002 Lemma A), and its global trace formula is
RH-equivalent (A17). I have no candidate for the gluing.

## §7 What is new, and the weakest step

**New (to the corpus; literature status noted).**
1. **Theorem M** (orbit–measure law and modulus law). The ingredients are elementary: scaling of the transverse
  measure, Whitney–Bebutov local sections, and a translation-invariant disintegration. I found no statement of it in
  Deninger, Leichtnam, Kopei or Kim. Deninger's Rem. 2 ("the conditions force `α = 0`") is the compact,
  fixed-point-free shadow of it.
2. **Corollary M1**: codimension-one foliated manifolds are excluded from (E-orb) unconditionally, with fixed points
  and non-compactness allowed. This goes beyond C5/N11.
3. **Theorem H** (homothety law; one periodic point per leaf; `ε = +1` derived) and **Prop K**. The Riemannian input is
  classical (Kobayashi–Nomizu). Its use to derive Deninger's sign and linearity conditions, and to exclude compact
  archimedean leaves, is new to the corpus.
4. **Prop F**: the real-place exponent `β = −½`, derived, which reproduces Leichtnam's §4.4 double-cover singularity.
5. **Cor H2** (conditional): Deninger's expectations for `K ≠ Q` are inconsistent on compact regular laminations under
  Candel's hypothesis.
6. §5: the forced local normal form of an (E-orb) object, which turns the escape into a gluing problem.

**Not claimed.** No object, no trace formula for ζ, no sign at any window, nothing for `T > 0.8`, no progress on the
"for all `T`" arrow. All results are necessary conditions or exclusions.

**Weakest steps.**
* **Completeness in Theorem H / Cor H1–H2.** Leaves through primes must be `g`-complete. If the lamination is singular
  at the archimedean fixed points (Leichtnam §4.4 requires this for `Q`), a leaf through `γ_p` could run into the
  singularity and be incomplete. Lemma H0 then fails; flat cones are an example of incomplete homothetic surfaces. So
  Cor H1 is proved only for complete leaves, and Cor H2 only for regular laminations.
* **Cor H2's hypothesis.** Candel's theorem is stated for harmonic measures. Deninger's text states the negativity for
  transverse measures. The corollary is conditional on the stronger form.
* **Cor M1(d)** needs the localisation property (L). It is true in every proved foliated Lefschetz formula, but it is
  not a theorem for arbitrary carriers. Parts (a)–(c) are unconditional.
* **Class definition.** DK_α requires the trace on `H̄^d` to be a holonomy-invariant transverse measure (Deninger Thm
  4.1, Leichtnam axiom 5]). A positive form built from a non-invariant (harmonic, Garnett) measure is not covered. But
  such a form does not descend to reduced cohomology, since `C_m(d_𝓕β) ≠ 0` in general.
* **CITED inputs, not re-proved:** Whitney–Bebutov local sections; Ruelle–Sullivan correspondence; Killing–Hopf;
  Sternberg linearisation (Prop F only); Candel 1993 (Cor H2 only); completeness of leaves of compact laminations.

**Suggested next step.** The (E-orb) residual is now the gluing problem of §5. The sharpest sub-question
is whether a single transverse measure scaled by `e^{−t}` can have modulus-`p^{−1}` germs at orbits of every length
`log p` inside one lamination with a dense leaf and `H̄² = R`. A negative answer (for instance a Connes-type
ratio-set or type-III obstruction for the crossed product `W(S,𝓕) ⋊ R`; Leichtnam Prop 2.4 computes `S = q^Z` in the
one-prime case) would close the Deninger angle completely. A positive one would be the first genuine carrier
candidate.
