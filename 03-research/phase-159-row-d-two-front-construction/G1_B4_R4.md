### What this round does

This round settles (E2) completely inside every class that a Frobenius *compatible with a degree theory* can belong to. It
also shows that the remaining part of (E2) is exactly the super-degree regime.

The key observation is that the R3 worry was misplaced. The infinite product in Lemma W4 is a product of **orthogonal**
projections onto the eigenspaces. Those are orthogonal whatever `T` is, so W4 never needed normality or contractivity.
Only Lemma W3, the orthogonality of two eigenspaces, used (C) or (N). Replacing orthogonality by a quantitative
**transversality** of the two eigenspaces gives the following.

1. **Proposition S (the separation principle; `PROVED`).** Take arbitrary bounded tail operators, with no contractivity,
  normality, commutation or power bound. Suppose two distinct real `γ ≠ γ′` both lie in the tail criterion set `A(ξ)`.
  Then the eigenspaces become asymptotically non-transversal, with square-summable rate:
  `Σ_j κ_j(γ,γ′)² ≤ 8 Σ_j (2 − a_j(γ) − a_j(γ′)) < ∞`,
  where `κ_j` is the minimal distance between the unit spheres of `E_j(γ)` and `E_j(γ′)`.
2. **Lemma K (three lower bounds for `κ_j`; `PROVED`).**
  * (K1, norm) `κ_j ≥ |p^{iγ} − p^{iγ′}| / (1 + ‖T_{j,p}‖)` for every `p ∈ B_j`.
  * (K2, Cesàro) `κ_j ≥ 1/M` whenever `T_{j,p}` is Cesàro-bounded by `M` and `p^{iγ} ≠ p^{iγ′}`.
  * (K3) `κ_j = √2` under (C) or (N), which recovers Theorem W.
3. **Theorem W♯ (the sharp tail wall; `PROVED`, with PNT `CITED`).** A tail-factorizable summand carries **at most one**
  critical point in each of the following cases:
  * (a) uniformly Cesàro-bounded tails, in particular uniformly power-bounded tails (Foguel-type operators that are not
  similar to contractions included), with arbitrary blocks;
  * (b) uniformly bounded tails, `sup‖T_{j,p}‖ < ∞`, with arbitrary blocks and a cofinite tail;
  * (c) blocks of bounded size, with `‖T_{j,p}‖ ≤ g(p)` for a nondecreasing envelope `g` such that
  `Σ_p g(p)^{−2} = ∞` and `Σ_{p∈P_0} g(p)^{−2} < ∞`. **This includes the trivial degree bound `‖S_p‖ ≤ C p^{1/2}`**,
  i.e. unnormalised `‖F_p‖ ≤ Cp`, and C₀-groups of exponential type `≤ ½`.
4. **Proposition Σ (sharpness; `PROVED`).** Case (c) is **exactly sharp**:
  * If `Σ_p g(p)^{−2} < ∞`, then for any two real `γ ≠ γ′` there is an Euler-factorizable carrier with `H_p = C²`,
  `P_0 = ∅` and `‖S_p‖ ≤ (1+π)g(p)` that carries both `γ` and `γ′`.
  * Bounded block size cannot be dropped: dyadic blocks with `‖T_{j,p}‖ ≤ 1 + π√p` carry two points.
  * Both examples are installations (Lemma T), and they serve only to show that the hypotheses are sharp.
5. **Corollary F and Proposition D (the FH/Arakelov reading; `PROVED`, with Schur test `CITED` (std.)).**
  * A correspondence of bidegree `(d₁,d₂)`, acting through a kernel with the Schur property, has `‖c‖ ≤ √(d₁d₂)`.
  * So every Frobenius or Hecke correspondence of bidegree `≤ (p+1, p+1)` at `p` lies in class (c).
  * The Hecke bidegree `(p+1,p+1)` sits **exactly at the critical exponent** `½`, and the wall holds there because
  `Σ_p 1/p = ∞` (Euler).
  * The degree theory that the Arakelov side supplies (the FH mechanism and the Schur bound) therefore puts every
  prime-local Frobenius it can produce inside the wall.

## §2 Setting (Theorem W's, with arbitrary bounded tails)

The setting is that of G1_B4_R3 §2.1, with the tail hypotheses (C)/(N) **dropped**.

* The primes are partitioned as `P = P_0 ⊔ ⨆_{j≥1}B_j`, with each `B_j` finite and nonempty and infinitely many `j`.
* The tail primes are `𝒯 := ⨆B_j`.
* The carrier is `X = H_0 ⊗ ⊗′_{j≥1}(H_j, ξ_j)`, von Neumann's incomplete tensor product, with `‖ξ_j‖ = 1`.
* For `p ∈ B_j`, `S_p = T_{j,p}` acts on the `j`-th factor, where `T_{j,p} ∈ B(H_j)` is **arbitrary bounded**.
* For `p ∈ P_0`, `S_p = A_p ⊗ 1`, with `A_p` arbitrary bounded.

For `γ ∈ R`:
* `E_j(γ) := ⋂_{p∈B_j} ker(T_{j,p} − p^{iγ})` is closed; `P_j(γ)` is the **orthogonal** projection onto it;
* `a_j(γ) := ‖P_j(γ)ξ_j‖²`;
* `A(ξ) := {γ : ∃ j_0, a_j(γ) > 0 for j ≥ j_0, Σ_{j≥j_0}(1 − a_j(γ)) < ∞}`;
* `Γ(X) := {γ : ∃ w ≠ 0, S_p w = p^{iγ}w for all p}`;
* `Γ_𝒯(X)` is defined in the same way, with `p ∈ 𝒯` only.

**Lemma W♭ (Theorem W(i) needs no tail hypothesis; `PROVED`).** For arbitrary bounded `T_{j,p}`, `Γ(X) ⊆ Γ_𝒯(X) ⊆ A(ξ)`.

*Proof.* This is the proof of G1_B4_R3 Thm W(i), checked for where (C)/(N) enter. They do not.
* Lemma W1 (associativity, `CITED` (std.)) and Lemma W2 (`ker(B ⊗ 1) = ker B ⊗ K`, `PROVED` there) hold for any bounded
  `B`. Applying W2 to `B = T_{j,p} − p^{iγ}` for each `p ∈ B_j` gives `w ∈ ker(T_{j,p} − p^{iγ}) ⊗ X^{(j)}`.
* Intersecting over the finitely many `p ∈ B_j`: `(K_1⊗X′) ∩ (K_2⊗X′) = (K_1∩K_2)⊗X′`. To see this, expand
  `w = Σ_k w_k ⊗ e_k` in an orthonormal basis of `X′`. Then `w ∈ K_i ⊗ X′` iff every `w_k ∈ K_i`. So `R_j w = w`, where
  `R_j := P_j(γ)` acts on the `j`-th factor.
* The `R_j` are **orthogonal** projections acting on distinct factors, so they commute. Lemma W4 (`PROVED` there) uses
  only that. So `0 ≠ w ∈ ⋂_j ran R_j` forces `Π_{j≥j_0} a_j(γ) > 0` for some `j_0`, i.e. `γ ∈ A(ξ)`. ∎

The **only** place where G1_B4_R3 used (C)/(N) was Lemma W3 (`E_j(γ) ⊥ E_j(γ′)`) inside Thm W(ii). Everything below
replaces W3.

## §3 The separation principle

**Definition.** For closed subspaces `E, E′` of a Hilbert space, both nonzero, let
`κ(E, E′) := inf{‖x − y‖ : x ∈ E, y ∈ E′, ‖x‖ = ‖y‖ = 1} ∈ [0, 2]`.
Write `κ_j(γ,γ′) := κ(E_j(γ), E_j(γ′))` when both spaces are nonzero.

**Proposition S (separation principle; `PROVED`).** Let the `T_{j,p}` be arbitrary bounded, and let `γ ≠ γ′` both lie in
`A(ξ)`. Then there is `j_0` such that for `j ≥ j_0` both `E_j(γ), E_j(γ′)` are nonzero and
`Σ_{j≥j_0} κ_j(γ,γ′)² ≤ 8 Σ_{j≥j_0} (1 − a_j(γ) + 1 − a_j(γ′)) < ∞.`

*Proof.*
* Take `j_0` so large that `a_j(γ), a_j(γ′) > 0` for `j ≥ j_0`.
* Put `x̃ := P_j(γ)ξ_j`, so `‖x̃‖² = a := a_j(γ)`, and `‖ξ_j − x̃‖² = 1 − a` by orthogonality of the projection.
* Put `x := x̃/√a`. Then `‖x − x̃‖ = 1 − √a ≤ 1 − a ≤ √(1−a)`, so `‖ξ_j − x‖ ≤ 2√(1 − a)`.
* Likewise a unit `y ∈ E_j(γ′)` has `‖ξ_j − y‖ ≤ 2√(1 − a′)`.
* So `κ_j ≤ ‖x − y‖ ≤ 2√(1−a) + 2√(1−a′)`, and `κ_j² ≤ 8((1−a) + (1−a′))` by `(u+v)² ≤ 2u² + 2v²`.
* Sum over `j`. ∎

So **two points in one tail-factorizable summand require the two joint eigenspaces to become non-transversal along the
tail, at a square-summable rate.** Contractive and normal tails forbid this outright (κ = √2). Any quantitative
transversality does too.

**Lemma K (lower bounds for `κ`; `PROVED`).** Let `T ∈ B(H)`, let `μ ≠ ν` with `|μ| = |ν| = 1`, and let `x, y` be unit
vectors with `Tx = μx` and `Ty = νy`.
* **(K1, norm bound)** `‖x − y‖ ≥ |μ − ν| / (1 + ‖T‖)`.
* **(K2, Cesàro bound)** If `M := sup_{N≥1} ‖N^{−1}Σ_{n=0}^{N−1}(μ̄T)^n‖ < ∞` (for instance `M ≤ sup_n‖T^n‖` when `T` is
  power-bounded), then `‖x − y‖ ≥ 1/M`.
* **(K3)** If `T` is a contraction or normal, then `x ⊥ y` and `‖x − y‖ = √2` (G1_B4_R3 Lemma W3).

*Proof.*
* (K1) `(T − ν)(x − y) = (μ − ν)x`. Hence `|μ − ν| = ‖(T − ν)(x−y)‖ ≤ (‖T‖ + 1)‖x − y‖`.
* (K2) Put `C_N := N^{−1}Σ_{n<N}(μ̄T)^n`. Then `C_N x = x`, and
  `C_N y = N^{−1}Σ_{n<N}(μ̄ν)^n y`, whose norm is `≤ 2/(N|1 − μ̄ν|) → 0`, since `μ̄ν ≠ 1` is unimodular. So
  `1 = ‖x‖ = ‖C_N x‖ ≤ ‖C_N(x − y)‖ + ‖C_N y‖ ≤ M‖x − y‖ + o(1)`.
* (K3) Record, Lemma W3. ∎

*Remark K4.* (K2) needs no similarity to a contraction. There are power-bounded Hilbert-space operators that are not
similar to contractions (Foguel 1964; Lebow 1968; `CITED` (std.)). So (K2) is not reducible to (K3) through Sz.-Nagy's
similarity theorem. This is exactly the gap B2_R3 l.458–460 pointed to.

## §4 The sharp tail wall

### 4.1 Statement

For real `δ ≠ 0` write `e_δ(p) := |1 − p^{iδ}|² = 2 − 2cos(δ log p)`. Note that `|p^{iγ} − p^{iγ′}|² = e_{γ′−γ}(p)`.

**Theorem W♯ (`PROVED`; the prime number theorem is `CITED` (std.) in (b) and (c)).** Let `X` be as in §2.
* **(a) Uniformly Cesàro-bounded tails, arbitrary blocks.** Suppose
  `M := sup_j sup_{p∈B_j} sup_{|λ|=1} sup_N ‖N^{−1}Σ_{n<N}(λT_{j,p})^n‖ < ∞`.
  Then `|A(ξ)| ≤ 1`, hence `|Γ(X)| ≤ 1`. This covers uniformly power-bounded tails, and contractions (`M = 1`).
* **(b) Uniformly bounded tails, arbitrary blocks, cofinite tail.** Suppose `C := sup_j sup_{p∈B_j}‖T_{j,p}‖ < ∞` and
  `P_0` is finite. Then `|A(ξ)| ≤ 1`.
* **(c) Degree-class tails, bounded blocks.** Suppose:
  * `|B_j| ≤ K` for all `j`;
  * `‖T_{j,p}‖ ≤ g(p)` for `p ∈ 𝒯`, where `g : P → [1, ∞)` is nondecreasing and `Σ_{p∈P} g(p)^{−2} = ∞`;
  * `Σ_{p∈P_0} g(p)^{−2} < ∞`.

  Then `|A(ξ)| ≤ 1`. In particular this holds when `‖S_p‖ ≤ C p^{1/2}` on `𝒯`, i.e. when the **unnormalised** Frobenius
  `F_p := p^{1/2}S_p` satisfies the trivial bound `‖F_p‖ ≤ Cp`, and `P_0` is finite.
* **(d) Flows and direct sums.** Under (a), (b) or (c), Theorem W(iii) (any interpolating family `V_t` with
  `V_{log p} = S_p` on `𝒯`) and W(iv) (countable orthogonal sums of reducing tail-factorizable summands: at most one
  point per summand) hold verbatim.

*Proof.* Suppose `γ ≠ γ′` both lie in `A(ξ)`, and put `δ := γ′ − γ`. By Prop S, for `j ≥ j_0`,
`Σ_j κ_j² < ∞`. (∗)
* **(a)**
  * `p^{iγ} = p^{iγ′}` for all `p ∈ B_j` can hold for at most one `j`. Two such `j` would give `δ log p, δ log q ∈ 2πZ∖0`
  with `p ≠ q`, hence `log p/log q ∈ Q`, which is impossible (G1_B4_R3 Thm W(ii)).
  * For every other `j`, pick `p ∈ B_j` with `p^{iγ} ≠ p^{iγ′}`. For unit `x ∈ E_j(γ)` and `y ∈ E_j(γ′)`, (K2) with
  `λ = p^{−iγ}` gives `‖x − y‖ ≥ 1/M`. So `κ_j ≥ 1/M` for all but one `j`, which contradicts (∗).
* **(b)**
  * By (K1), `κ_j ≥ max_{p∈B_j} e_δ(p)^{1/2}/(1 + C)`. So (∗) forces `max_{p∈B_j} e_δ(p) → 0` as `j → ∞`.
  * Each `B_j` is finite, so all but finitely many tail primes then satisfy `e_δ(p) < 2`.
  * Since `P_0` is finite, all but finitely many primes satisfy `e_δ(p) < 2`, i.e. `cos(δ log p) > 0`.
  * This contradicts Lemma D(i).
* **(c)**
  * By (K1) and `1 + ‖T‖ ≤ 2g(p)`, `κ_j² ≥ max_{p∈B_j} e_δ(p)/(4g(p)²) ≥ (1/(4K))Σ_{p∈B_j} e_δ(p) g(p)^{−2}`.
  * Summing and using (∗): `Σ_{p∈𝒯} e_δ(p) g(p)^{−2} < ∞`. (The finitely many `j < j_0` contribute finitely.)
  * But `Σ_{p∈P_0} e_δ(p) g(p)^{−2} ≤ 4Σ_{P_0} g^{−2} < ∞`. So `Σ_{p∈P} e_δ(p) g(p)^{−2} < ∞`, which contradicts
  Lemma D(ii).
  * For `g(p) = C p^{1/2}` (and `C ≥ 1`), `Σ_p g^{−2} = C^{−2}Σ_p 1/p = ∞` (Euler; `CITED` (std.)).
* **(d)** The proofs of W(iii) and W(iv) in G1_B4_R3 use only W(i) and W(ii), which are now Lemma W♭ and parts (a)–(c).
  ∎

### 4.2 The arithmetic input

**Lemma D (`PROVED` from the prime number theorem, `CITED` (std.)).** Fix real `δ ≠ 0`. Put `L := 2π/|δ|`, and let
`G_k := {p : kL + L/4 < log p ≤ kL + 3L/4}` (the "good arcs", on which `cos(δ log p) ≤ 0`, i.e. `e_δ(p) ≥ 2`).
* (i) `G_k ≠ ∅` for all large `k`.
* (ii) If `g : P → [1,∞)` is nondecreasing and `Σ_p g(p)^{−2} = ∞`, then `Σ_p e_δ(p)g(p)^{−2} = ∞`.

*Proof.*
* By the PNT, for fixed `c > 1`, `π(cX) − π(X) ∼ (c − 1)X/log X`.
* **(i)** Take `c = e^{L/2}` and `X = e^{kL + L/4}`.
* **(ii)**
  * Let `R_k := {p : kL + 3L/4 < log p ≤ (k+1)L + L/4}`. For large `k`, the sets `G_k ∪ R_k` partition all large primes.
  * Both `G_k` and `R_k` are prime sets in intervals `(X, cX]`, with `c = e^{L/2}` and `X = e^{kL+L/4}`, resp.
  `X = e^{kL+3L/4}`. By the PNT, `#R_k/#G_k → e^{L/2} = c`, so `#R_k ≤ 2c·#G_k` for `k ≥ k_0`.
  * Let `b_k := e^{kL+3L/4}`. Every prime of `G_k` is `≤ b_k` and every prime of `R_k` is `> b_k`. Since `g` is
  nondecreasing:
  `Σ_{p∈R_k} g(p)^{−2} ≤ #R_k·g(b_k)^{−2} ≤ 2c·#G_k·g(b_k)^{−2} ≤ 2c Σ_{p∈G_k} g(p)^{−2}`.
  (Here `g(b_k)` means `g` at the largest prime `≤ b_k`, and the bound only uses monotonicity.)
  * So `∞ = Σ_{p large} g^{−2} ≤ (1 + 2c)Σ_{k≥k_0}Σ_{p∈G_k} g^{−2}`.
  * Hence `Σ_p e_δ(p)g^{−2} ≥ 2Σ_kΣ_{G_k} g^{−2} = ∞`. ∎

*Remark D1 (why the real primes matter; `PROVED` by example).* For a lattice Beurling system (`log p_j ∈ Z·log q` for all
`j`), every `δ ∈ (2π/log q)Z` has `e_δ ≡ 0`, and Lemma D fails. This is right: an `F_q`-curve carries `2g` Frobenius
eigenvalues on one `H¹`. The wall is a property of the **independence of the prime logarithms** combined with a degree
bound. It is not a property of Hilbert spaces alone (E-P5.9 of the R3 panel).

### 4.3 Sharpness

**Proposition Σ (`PROVED`).**
* **(i) The exponent/summability threshold in (c) is exact.** Let `g : P → [1,∞)` be any function with
  `Σ_p g(p)^{−2} < ∞`, and let `γ ≠ γ′` be real. There is an Euler-factorizable carrier (`P_0 = ∅`, `B_j = {p_j}`,
  `H_p = C²`) with `‖S_p‖ ≤ (1 + π)g(p)` and `{γ, γ′} ⊆ Γ(X)`.
* **(ii) Bounded blocks are necessary in (c).** With dyadic blocks `B_j = P ∩ (2^j, 2^{j+1}]` and `H_j = C²`, there is a
  carrier with `‖T_{j,p}‖ ≤ 1 + π√p ≤ 2π√p`, commuting diagonalizable tail operators, and `{γ, γ′} ⊆ Γ(X)`.

*Proof.*
* **(i)** Put `θ_p := 1/g(p) ∈ (0, 1]`, `u_p := (1, 0)` and `v_p := (cos θ_p, sin θ_p)`. Let `ξ_p` be the unit bisector
  `(cos(θ_p/2), sin(θ_p/2))`, and `μ := p^{iγ}`, `ν := p^{iγ′}`.
  * **The operators.** Define `T_p u_p = μu_p` and `T_p v_p = νv_p`, which is well defined since `u_p, v_p` is a basis.
  Then `T_p = νI + (μ − ν)Π_p`, where `Π_p` is the idempotent onto `Cu_p` along `Cv_p`, with
  `‖Π_p‖ = 1/sin θ_p ≤ π/(2θ_p)` (`CITED` (std.): the norm of the oblique projection between two lines at angle
  `θ` is `1/sin θ`). So `‖T_p‖ ≤ 1 + 2‖Π_p‖ ≤ 1 + πg(p) ≤ (1+π)g(p)`.
  * **The eigenvectors.** Let `w_N := u_{p_1} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ u_{p_N} ⊗ ξ_{p_{N+1}} ⊗ ⋯ ∈ X`. Then, for `N < N′`,
  `‖w_N − w_{N′}‖² = 2 − 2Π_{N<n≤N′}cos(θ_{p_n}/2)`.
  * Since `1 − cos(θ/2) ≤ θ²/8` and `Σθ_p² < ∞`, the product `Π_n cos(θ_{p_n}/2)` converges to a positive limit. So
  `(w_N)` is Cauchy, and its limit `w` has `‖w‖ = 1`.
  * For each `p`, `S_p w_N = μ w_N` once `N ≥` the index of `p`, and `S_p` is bounded. So `S_p w = p^{iγ} w`. The same
  construction with `v_p` gives `γ′`.
* **(ii)** The same construction with one pair `(u_j, v_j)` per block.
  * The angle is `θ_j := 2^{−j/2}`, and `T_{j,p}u_j = p^{iγ}u_j`, `T_{j,p}v_j = p^{iγ′}v_j` for every `p ∈ B_j`. These
  operators commute, being simultaneously diagonalizable.
  * `‖T_{j,p}‖ ≤ 1 + π2^{j/2} ≤ 1 + π√p` for `p > 2^j`, and `Σ_jθ_j² < ∞`. ∎

*Reading.* Both examples are **installations**: the two points are put in by hand (Lemma T(⇐), C2). They carry no trace
formula and fail OT-2 and OT-3. They are recorded only to show that Theorem W♯(c) cannot be improved in its growth
condition or in its block condition. The dividing line is:

**`Σ_p ‖S_p‖^{−2} = ∞`: at most one critical point per Euler-factorizable summand; `< ∞`: installation of any
two points is possible.** For power envelopes `g = p^ω`, this is the line `ω ≤ ½` versus `ω > ½`. The critical exponent
`½` of the normalised Frobenius is the half-width of the critical strip. (The last sentence is `HEURISTIC` as an
interpretation; the dichotomy itself is `PROVED`.)

## §5 The Arakelov / Faltings–Hriljac reading, and the test against P47 Thm 22.17

### 5.1 Flows

**Corollary F (`PROVED`).** Let `V_t` (`t ∈ R`) be any `C_0`-group on `X` with `‖V_t‖ ≤ M e^{ω|t|}` and `V_{log p} = S_p`
for `p ∈ 𝒯`. Assume the tail has bounded blocks and `P_0` is finite.
* If `ω ≤ ½`, then each tail-factorizable summand carries at most one `γ` with `V_t w = e^{iγt}w`.
* If `ω = 0` (a bounded group), the same holds with arbitrary blocks.

*Proof.*
* The case `ω ≤ ½`: `‖S_p‖ ≤ M p^{ω} ≤ M p^{1/2}`. Apply W♯(c), then (d).
* The case `ω = 0`: `sup_n‖S_p^n‖ = sup_n‖V_{n log p}‖ ≤ M`. Apply W♯(a). ∎

For Deninger's normalisation `φ^t = e^{t/2}V_t` (eigenvalues `ρ` on `H¹`), `ω ≤ ½` says `‖φ^t‖ ≤ M e^{t}` for `t ≥ 0`.
That is the growth permitted by the weight of `H²` (`Θ = 1`), i.e. the trivial bound. **A Deninger-type flow obeying only
the trivial weight bound cannot put two zeros on one tail-factorizable summand.**

### 5.2 Correspondences with a degree

**Proposition D (Schur degree bound; `PROVED`, Schur test `CITED` (std.)).** Let `(Y, m)` be σ-finite, and let `c` act on
`L²(Y, m)` by `(cf)(x) = ∫k(x,y)f(y)dm(y)` with `k ≥ 0`, `∫k(x,y)dm(y) ≤ d₁` for all `x`, and `∫k(x,y)dm(x) ≤ d₂` for
all `y`. Then `‖c‖ ≤ √(d₁d₂)`.

*Proof.* Cauchy–Schwarz: `|cf(x)|² ≤ (∫k(x,y)dm(y))(∫k(x,y)|f(y)|²dm(y)) ≤ d₁∫k(x,y)|f(y)|²dm(y)`. Integrate in `x` and
use Tonelli with the column bound: `‖cf‖² ≤ d₁d₂‖f‖²`. ∎

**Consequences (`PROVED` for the bounds; the identification of "the Frobenius that geometry supplies" is `HEURISTIC`).**
* A Frobenius correspondence of bidegree `(1, p)`, e.g. `x ↦ x^p`, or `V_p` on ghost Witt coordinates (G1_B4_R3 Prop
  BW), gives `‖F_p‖ ≤ √p`, i.e. `‖S_p‖ ≤ 1`: class (C).
* A Hecke correspondence of bidegree `(p+1, p+1)` (the `p+1` sublattices of index `p`; on `L²(Γ\𝔥)` or on a finite
  quotient graph, each branch measure-preserving) gives `‖T_p‖ ≤ p + 1`. Normalised, `‖S_p‖ = ‖T_p‖/√p ≤ 2√p`: **class
  (c), exactly at the critical exponent `½`.** The wall still holds there because `Σ_p 1/p = ∞`.
* Any correspondence whose degree at `p` is `≤ Cp²` (`d₁d₂ ≤ C p²`), commuting or not and normal or not, gives
  `‖S_p‖ ≤ √C·p^{1/2}`: class (c).
* So **no Euler-factorizable realisation of Frobenius by correspondences with Schur degree `O(p²)` at `p` escapes the
  wall.** Escape (E2♯) needs correspondences of super-quadratic degree, or kernels without the Schur property (signed or
  complex kernels whose absolute value has larger degree), with norms beyond the Schur bound infinitely often in the
  quantitative sense `Σ_p ‖S_p‖^{−2} < ∞`.

### 5.3 The FH mechanism and Thm 22.17

* **What the FH mechanism supplies.** It supplies a positive form (Néron–Tate), identified with minus the arithmetic
  intersection pairing (Faltings 1984, Hriljac 1985; `CITED` (std.)).
* **What Rosati compatibility gives.** On the FH carrier, compatibility of that form with correspondences is Rosati's:
  `⟨cx, y⟩ = ⟨x, c′y⟩`, with `c′` the transpose. In Weil's `C×C`, the degree identity `F′F = q·Δ` then makes `q^{−1/2}F`
  an isometry of the positive form. That is class (C), with **one** prime and no tail, so the wall is vacuous and `2g`
  eigenvalues fit on one `H¹` (G1_B4_R3 Rem W6).
* **Over `Spec Z`.** P47 Thm 22.17 (`CLASSICAL`/`PROVED` there) says there is no ring of self-maps of the base in which
  `m + n·Frob_p` lives. So the degree identity cannot be imposed on one-variable objects.
* **What this memo adds.** Whatever correspondences are used instead (Galois: weight 0, G1_B4_R3 Prop G; Witt/BC:
  isometries, Prop BW; Hecke: bidegree `p+1`), **any degree control of the Schur or Rosati kind puts them into class (a)
  or (c)**. If they act prime by prime on a tail-factorizable carrier, Theorem W♯ allows one critical point per summand,
  i.e. installation (Lemma T, C16).
* **The consequence (`PROVED` as a filter).** The degree theory, the ingredient that Weil's proof uses and that FH
  supplies on arithmetic surfaces, is by itself **incompatible with prime-local zero-carrying carriers**. It can coexist
  with zero-carrying only through entanglement (E1).
* **The reading (`HEURISTIC`).** This is Thm 22.17 seen from the analytic side. The `+` that Weil needs would have to
  produce a degree theory that is not prime-local. The only global mechanism on record, the product formula in `C_Q`, has
  RH-strength OT-2 (R158_003 A17).

### 5.4 P-HI carriers (`HEURISTIC`, not load-bearing)

Theorem W♯ works in the Hilbert topology of `X` and uses only operator norms. If a primitive-form (P-HI) carrier is a
Pontryagin space `Π_κ` whose Hilbert majorant topology is the incomplete tensor topology, then the Frobenius is
`J`-unitary.
* With finitely many eigenvalues off the unimodular circle (at most `κ` pairs), one expects
  `‖S_p‖ ≪ (log p)^{2κ} p^{a}`, where `a < ½` is the largest `|Re ρ − ½|` among the off-line eigenvalues. That would put
  the carrier in class (c).
* The growth bound for `J`-unitary groups on `Π_κ` is not proved here. It would follow from a Pontryagin invariant
  nonpositive subspace theorem for the group (Iohvidov–Kreĭn–Langer; not re-read). Hence this is `HEURISTIC`.
* It replaces R3 Rem W6's clause ("indefiniteness in finitely many factors"), which the panel downgraded.

## §7 What is genuinely new

1. **Lemma W♭.** The observation that Theorem W(i) (the infinite-product criterion) holds for arbitrary bounded tail
  operators. The only (C)/(N)-dependent step of the whole tail wall was eigenspace orthogonality.
2. **Proposition S and Lemma K.** The separation principle: two critical points in one summand force square-summable
  non-transversality of the joint eigenspaces. It comes with three explicit transversality bounds (norm, Cesàro, normal).
3. **Theorem W♯.** Escape (E2), which the round-3 panel called the precise open question, is **closed** for:
  * uniformly power-bounded or Cesàro-bounded tails with arbitrary blocks (including tails not similar to contractions);
  * uniformly bounded tails with a cofinite tail;
  * every Euler-factorizable tail with monotone norm envelope `g`, `Σ 1/g² = ∞`. This includes the trivial degree bound
  `‖F_p‖ ≤ Cp` and flows of type `≤ ½`.
4. **Proposition Σ.** The threshold is exact, in the growth condition and in the block condition. The wall is precisely
  `Σ_p‖S_p‖^{−2} = ∞`, at the exponent `½`.
5. **Proposition D and §5.3.** Schur/Hecke degree theory sits at or below the critical exponent. The degree theory that
  Arakelov geometry supplies is therefore incompatible with prime-local zero-carrying carriers. This is the analytic
  companion of P47 Thm 22.17.

None of these is a sign, none is an object, and none reopens row D.

## §8 Weakest step

* **The scope of "tail-factorizable" remains the load-bearing hypothesis.** Theorem W♯ excludes nothing entangled. (E1)
  is where any genuine object must live, and there the only candidate of record (`C_Q`) has RH-strength OT-2. The G1
  objective is **not** met.
* **(E2♯) is not empty.** The following remain `OPEN` with no candidate:
  * super-degree Euler-factorizable tails (`Σ_p‖S_p‖^{−2} < ∞`), and non-monotone norm profiles resonant for every
  difference of zero ordinates;
  * large blocks with block-norm growth beyond `(block scale)^{1/2}`.

  Prop Σ shows that these regimes admit installations. Whether a non-installed object can live there is `OPEN`. That
  such an object would violate any degree compatibility of the form with Frobenius is `PROVED` only for Schur-type
  kernels (Prop D). For general Rosati-type compatibility it is `HEURISTIC`.
* **Lemma D uses the PNT** (`CITED` (std.)) in the form `π(cX) − π(X) ∼ (c−1)X/log X`. This is standard, but it is the
  one analytic input and is not re-proved.
* **§5.4 (P-HI)** is `HEURISTIC`. The growth bound for `J`-unitary groups on Pontryagin spaces is not proved.
* **The oblique projection norm `1/sin θ`** in Prop Σ is `CITED` (std.). Only an upper bound is needed. It can be checked
  directly: for unit `u, v` at angle `θ`, `Π(αu + βv) = αu`, and `‖αu + βv‖ ≥ |α| sin θ`.
