## §2 Setting

**Definition 2.1 (Frobenius family).** A *Frobenius family* on a separable Hilbert space `H` is a map `n ↦ T_n`,
`N^× → B(H)`, with `T_1 = 1`, `T_mT_n = T_{mn}` and `‖T_n‖ ≤ 1`. Equivalently, it is a family `(T_p)_p` of commuting
contractions indexed by the primes. Such a family is *unitary* if every `T_p` is unitary; it then extends uniquely to a
unitary representation of `Q^×_{>0}`, which is `G1_B2_R2`'s setting.

For a character `χ ∈ T^P = ∏_p T` put
`E(χ) := {η ∈ H : T_pη = χ(p)η for all p}`, the joint eigenspace. The **Kronecker characters** are
`ι(γ) = (p^{iγ})_p`, `γ ∈ R`; `ι` is injective (`G1_B2_R2` §2). Put
`Γ(T) := {γ ∈ R : E(ι(γ)) ≠ 0}`, the Kronecker point spectrum.

*Relation to flows (`PROVED`).* Let `S(t)`, `t ≥ 0`, be a `C_0` contraction semigroup on a closed subspace `H′ ⊂ H` with
`S(log n) = T_n|H′` (a *suspension* of the family on `H′`). If `S(t)η = e^{iγt}η` for all `t ≥ 0`, then
`T_pη = S(log p)η = p^{iγ}η`, so `γ ∈ Γ(T)`. Any on-line zero realised as an eigenvalue `iγ` of the generator of a
suspended flow therefore lies in `Γ(T)`, and `Γ(T)` is the only thing a zero-carrying `H¹` has to populate.

**Lemma 2.2 (normalised correspondences are Markov contractions; `PROVED`, standard).** Let `(X, μ)` be a probability
space and let `Γ` be a correspondence, i.e. a probability space `(Γ, ν)` with measurable maps `a, b : Γ → X` such that
`a_*ν = b_*ν = μ`. Define `A f := E_ν[f∘b | a]`, viewed as a function on `X`, i.e. `(Af)∘a = E_ν[f∘b | σ(a)]`. Then:
* `A` is positive;
* `A1 = 1`;
* `∫Af dμ = ∫f dμ`;
* `‖A‖_{L²→L²} ≤ 1`.

For a finite correspondence of bidegree `(d, d)` with counting measures on the fibres, `A` is `(1/d)·a_*b^*`.

*Proof.* Positivity, `A1 = 1` and invariance of the integral are properties of conditional expectation, because
`b_*ν = μ`. Conditional Jensen gives `|Af|²∘a ≤ E_ν[|f∘b|² | a]`. Integrating against `ν` and using `a_*ν = μ` gives
`‖Af‖² ≤ ∫|f∘b|²dν = ‖f‖²`. ∎

So every Frobenius or Hecke *correspondence* over a measured base, once normalised, is a contraction. This is why the
contraction class is the right class for "correspondence-valued Frobenius". Unnormalised Hecke operators satisfy
`T_pT_p = T_{p²} + p`, which is not multiplicative. The normalised Frobenius *elements* `a_p` (as in `G1_B2_R2` §4) and
the CC correspondences restricted to `Q_{>0}` (§4.3) are multiplicative.

**Definition 2.3 (tail-factorizable family).** Let the primes be split as `P = P_0 ⊔ ⨆_{j≥1} B_j`, where each `B_j` is
finite and nonempty and there are infinitely many `j`. Suppose
`H = H_0 ⊗ ⊗'_{j≥1}(H_j, ξ_j)`
is von Neumann's incomplete tensor product with unit reference vectors `ξ_j ∈ H_j` (`CITED` std: von Neumann,
*Compositio Math.* 6 (1939)). Suppose also that `T_p = A_{j,p}` acts on the `j`-th factor for `p ∈ B_j`, and on `H_0`
for `p ∈ P_0`, where the `A_{j,p}` (`p ∈ B_j`) are commuting contractions on `H_j`. The **Euler-factorizable** case is
`P_0 = ∅` with `|B_j| = 1`. For `χ_j ∈ T^{B_j}` let `Q_j(χ_j)` be the orthogonal projection onto
`E_j(χ_j) := ⋂_{p∈B_j} ker(A_{j,p} − χ_j(p)) ⊂ H_j`. Put
`Γ_tail(ξ) := {γ ∈ R : ∃ j_0, ∏_{j≥j_0} ‖Q_j(ι_{B_j}(γ))ξ_j‖² > 0}`.

ITP facts used (`CITED` std, von Neumann 1939):
* (a) elementary tensors `w_0 ⊗ w_1 ⊗ w_2 ⊗ …` with `w_j = ξ_j` for all but finitely many `j` are total;
* (b) `⟨⊗w_j, ⊗w′_j⟩ = ∏⟨w_j, w′_j⟩`;
* (c) `H ≅ H_j ⊗ K_j` canonically, with `K_j` the ITP of the other factors, and an operator on the `j`-th factor is
  `X ⊗ 1` in this splitting.

## §3 Lemmas 1–2 and Theorem C

**Lemma 1 (unimodular eigenvectors of contractions; `PROVED`).** Let `‖A‖ ≤ 1`, `Aη = λη` and `|λ| = 1`. Then
`A*η = λ̄η`. Consequently:
* (a) eigenvectors of `A` for distinct unimodular eigenvalues are orthogonal;
* (b) for a Frobenius family, `E(χ) ⊥ E(χ′)` whenever `χ ≠ χ′` are unimodular characters;
* (c) `H_u := ⊕_{χ} E(χ)` reduces every `T_n`, and `T|H_u` is a unitary representation of `Q^×_{>0}` with pure point
  spectrum.

*Proof.* `⟨A*η, η⟩ = ⟨η, Aη⟩ = λ̄‖η‖²`. So
`‖A*η − λ̄η‖² = ‖A*η‖² − 2Re(λ⟨A*η, η⟩) + ‖η‖² = ‖A*η‖² − ‖η‖² ≤ 0`.
(a): if `Aη = λη` and `Aη′ = λ′η′` with `λ ≠ λ′` unimodular, then `λ⟨η, η′⟩ = ⟨Aη, η′⟩ = ⟨η, A*η′⟩ = ⟨η, λ̄′η′⟩ = λ′⟨η, η′⟩`,
so `⟨η, η′⟩ = 0`.
(b): `χ ≠ χ′` differ at some `p`; apply (a) to `T_p`.
(c): each `E(χ)` is invariant under `T_p` and, by the first claim, under `T_p*`, and `T_p|E(χ) = χ(p)` is unitary. ∎

**Lemma 2 (dilations inherit unimodular eigenvectors; `PROVED`).** Let `H ⊂ K`, let `P` be the orthogonal projection onto
`H`, let `‖U‖ ≤ 1` on `K`, and let `A = PU|_H`. If `Aη = λη` with `|λ| = 1`, then `Uη = λη`. In particular, if a
Frobenius family `(T_n)` has a contractive power dilation `(U_n)` (so `T_n = PU_n|_H`), then `E^T(χ) ⊂ E^U(χ)` for every
unimodular `χ`, and `Γ(T) ⊂ Γ(U)`.

*Proof.* `‖η‖ = ‖Aη‖ = ‖PUη‖ ≤ ‖Uη‖ ≤ ‖η‖`, so `‖PUη‖ = ‖Uη‖`. Hence `Uη ∈ H`, and `Uη = PUη = λη`. ∎

*Reading.* This answers the round-2 question on dilations for the point spectrum. If the dilation's suspended `V_c` has
no eigenvalue, the compression has none either. What is **not** inherited is the continuity set `V_c` itself. I leave that
aside: a zero must be realised as an eigenvector (§2, relation to flows), and eigenvectors are inherited.

**Theorem C (the contraction Kronecker wall; `PROVED`).** Let `(T_n)` be a tail-factorizable Frobenius family (Def 2.3).
Then:
* (i) `|Γ_tail(ξ)| ≤ 1`;
* (ii) `Γ(T) ⊂ Γ_tail(ξ)`.

So `Γ(T)` has at most one element, determined by the tail `(ξ_j)_{j ≥ j_0}` for any `j_0`.

For a countable orthogonal sum of invariant tail-factorizable summands, `Γ` is the union of the summands' `Γ`: at most
one point per summand.

*Proof of (ii).* Let `η ≠ 0` with `T_pη = p^{iγ}η` for all `p`. Fix `j` and put `χ_j := ι_{B_j}(γ)`. In the splitting (c),
`T_p = A_{j,p} ⊗ 1` for `p ∈ B_j`. For any bounded `X` on `H_j` and any orthonormal basis `(f_k)` of `K_j`, write
`v = Σ_k v_k ⊗ f_k`. Then `(X ⊗ 1)v = Σ_k Xv_k ⊗ f_k`, which vanishes iff `Xv_k = 0` for every `k`. So
`ker(X ⊗ 1) = ker X ⊗ K_j`. Applying this to `X = A_{j,p} − p^{iγ}` for each `p ∈ B_j` and intersecting gives
`η ∈ E_j(χ_j) ⊗ K_j = ran R_j`, where `R_j := Q_j(χ_j) ⊗ 1`.

The `R_j` are commuting orthogonal projections acting on different factors. `R^{(J)} := R_1R_2⋯R_J` decreases strongly
to the projection `R` onto `⋂_j ran R_j`, and `Rη = η`.

Take an elementary tensor `w = w_0 ⊗ w_1 ⊗ … ⊗ w_{J′} ⊗ ξ_{J′+1} ⊗ ξ_{J′+2} ⊗ …` with unit factors. By (b), for `J > J′`,
`‖R^{(J)}w‖² = ⟨R^{(J)}w, w⟩ = ∏_{j≤J′}‖Q_j(χ_j)w_j‖² · ∏_{J′<j≤J}‖Q_j(χ_j)ξ_j‖²`.

Suppose `γ ∉ Γ_tail(ξ)`. Then `∏_{j>J′}‖Q_j(χ_j)ξ_j‖² = 0` for every `J′`, so `‖Rw‖² = lim_J ‖R^{(J)}w‖² = 0`. `R` then
vanishes on the linear span of such `w`, which is dense by (a). So `R = 0` and `η = Rη = 0`, a contradiction. Hence
`γ ∈ Γ_tail(ξ)`.

*Proof of (i).* Let `γ ≠ γ′` both lie in `Γ_tail(ξ)`. For a single large `j_0`, both tail products are positive. A
convergent infinite product of numbers in `[0, 1]` with positive value has factors tending to `1`. So there is `j_1` such
that `‖Q_j(ι_{B_j}(γ))ξ_j‖² > ½` and `‖Q_j(ι_{B_j}(γ′))ξ_j‖² > ½` for all `j ≥ j_1`.

Suppose `ι_{B_j}(γ) = ι_{B_j}(γ′)` for two indices `j ≠ j′`. Pick `p ∈ B_j` and `q ∈ B_{j′}`; they are distinct primes.
Then `(γ − γ′)log p = 2πa` and `(γ − γ′)log q = 2πb` with `a, b ∈ Z∖{0}`, so `p^b = q^a`, which contradicts unique
factorisation. Hence `ι_{B_j}(γ) ≠ ι_{B_j}(γ′)` for all but at most one `j`.

Pick such a `j ≥ j_1`. By Lemma 1(b) applied to the commuting contractions `(A_{j,p})_{p∈B_j}` on `H_j`, the ranges of
`Q_j(ι_{B_j}(γ))` and `Q_j(ι_{B_j}(γ′))` are orthogonal. So the two squared norms sum to at most `‖ξ_j‖² = 1`, which
contradicts both exceeding `½`.

The direct-sum clause follows because `E(χ)` of an orthogonal sum of invariant summands is the sum of the summands'
`E(χ)`: each `T_p` is diagonal in the sum. ∎

**Corollary C1 (one prime without unimodular eigenvalues suffices; `PROVED`).** If `H = H_p ⊗ H^{(p)}` with `T_p = A ⊗ 1`
and `A` has no unimodular eigenvalue, then `Γ(T) = ∅`. This covers `A` a pure isometry (unilateral shift), `‖A‖ < 1`,
or `A` unitary with continuous spectrum. No tail hypothesis is needed.
*Proof.* `ker(A ⊗ 1 − p^{iγ}) = ker(A − p^{iγ}) ⊗ H^{(p)} = 0`, as in the proof of (ii). ∎

**Corollary C2 (repair of `G1_B2_R2` Thm E for the point spectrum; `PROVED`).** In the unitary tail-factorizable
setting of `G1_B2_R2`, the suspended flow's eigenvalues `iγ` on `V_c` satisfy `γ ∈ Γ_tail(ξ)`, and `|Γ_tail(ξ)| ≤ 1`.
Their counterexample to Thm E(i) as stated is consistent with this: `u(2) = diag(1, −1)`, `ξ_1 = e_2`, trivial tails
give `Γ(ξ) = ∅` but `Γ_tail(ξ) = {0}`. No Fubini is used, so the `P_0 = ∅` gap (E6) does not arise for eigenvalues.

**Corollary C3 (Markov tails see only the pole; `PROVED`).** Let `(X_j, μ_j)` be probability spaces and let
`H_0 ⊗ L²(∏_j X_j, ⊗_j μ_j)` be the carrier. Recall `L²(∏_j X_j) = ⊗'_j(L²(X_j), 1)`, the ITP with reference vectors the
constants (`CITED` std; Guichardet, *Symmetric Hilbert spaces*, LNM 261, 1972). Let `T_p` (`p ∈ B_j`) act on `L²(X_j)`
by commuting Markov contractions (`A1 = 1`), for instance normalised measure-preserving correspondences (Lemma 2.2).
Then `Γ(T) ⊂ {0}`, and `E(ι(0))` is the joint fixed space.
*Proof.* `ξ_j = 1` satisfies `A_{j,p}1 = 1 = p^{i·0}`, so `Q_j(ι_{B_j}(0))ξ_j = ξ_j` and the tail product is `1`. Hence
`0 ∈ Γ_tail`, and by Theorem C(i) `Γ_tail = {0}`. Theorem C(ii) finishes the proof. ∎

*Reading (`HEURISTIC`; the statement above is proved).* A "motive of Spec Z" assembled as a restricted product of local
objects has natural reference vectors: the local unit classes, i.e. the constants. Normalised local Frobenius
correspondences make it a Markov carrier. Cor C3 says that such a carrier sees **exactly the pole** `γ = 0`, the
class of `H⁰` (and dually `H²`), on the Kronecker line, and nothing else. To see a zero `γ_ρ`, the reference vectors
must be tilted so that `‖Q_j(ι(γ_ρ))ξ_j‖ → 1`, i.e. installed. This is Prop R of `G1_B2_R2`, now for correspondences.

**Proposition F (contractivity with functional-equation symmetry is RH; `PROVED`; classification `EQUIVALENT`, the
contraction form of Lemma T).** Let `(T_n)` be a Frobenius family. Suppose that for each non-trivial zero `ρ` there is
`η_ρ ≠ 0` with `T_nη_ρ = n^{½−ρ}η_ρ` for all `n`, and that the realised set is stable under `ρ ↦ 1 − ρ`. Then every
`ρ` has `Re ρ = ½`. Conversely, under RH the diagonal family on `⊕_ρ Cη_ρ` is unitary (installation, Prop R of B2).
*Proof.* `‖T_n‖ ≤ 1` forces `n^{½−Re ρ} ≤ 1`, i.e. `Re ρ ≥ ½`, and `ρ ↦ 1 − ρ` gives `Re ρ ≤ ½`. ∎

This is only the classification, not a route: a contraction proof made *before* the spectrum is OT-5′ verbatim. Theorem C
and Cor C3 locate where such a proof cannot come from. For correspondences, contractivity is proved by Lemma 2.2, i.e.
prime by prime from the Markov property. That locality is exactly what Theorem C turns into "at most one point per
summand" and what Cor C3 turns into "only the pole".

**Remark C4 (non-unimodular eigenvalues).** Theorem C concerns only unimodular eigencharacters. A contraction may have
eigenvalues `p^{−δ}` with `δ > 0`, and Lemma 1 fails for them. By Prop F, however, a contractive carrier that realises
the zero set symmetrically has only unimodular zero-eigencharacters. So for such carriers Theorem C covers every
realised zero.

## §4 The tests: Bost–Connes, Borger's cyclotomic λ-ring, Connes–Consani `Ψ(λ)`

### 4.1 Prop BC (Bost–Connes Frobenius isometries; `PROVED`, definitions `CITED` std: Bost–Connes, *Selecta* 1 (1995))

On `ℓ²(N^×)`, `μ_n e_m = e_{nm}`. The unique factorisation `m = ∏p^{v_p(m)}` gives
`ℓ²(N^×) = ⊗'_p(ℓ²(N_0), e_0)`, and `μ_p = S ⊗ 1` on the `p`-th factor, with `S` the unilateral shift. This family is
Euler-factorizable and multiplicative. `S` has no eigenvalues at all: `Sv = λv` forces `v_0 = 0`, then all `v_k = 0` if
`λ ≠ 0`, and `λ = 0` is impossible since `S` is isometric. By Cor C1, `Γ(μ) = ∅`.

For the adjoints `μ_n*` (co-isometries): a unimodular eigenvector of `μ_p*` is, by Lemma 1 applied to `A = μ_p*`, a
unimodular eigenvector of `μ_p`, so none exists. The same holds for the modified family `n^{−σ}μ_n` with `σ > 0`,
whose operators have norm `< 1`.

This is consistent with the record's classification of Bost–Connes as zero-blind on the β-axis (R157_002 row 33, R158_004
row 17(ii)). What is added is that its *Frobenius* carries no Kronecker point either, by a one-prime argument.

### 4.2 Prop BL (Borger's cyclotomic λ-ring; `PROVED`; the λ-structure `CITED`: Borger, arXiv 0906.3146, the Adams operations on `Z[Q/Z]` are `ψ_n(e(r)) = e(nr)`)

Realise `Z[Q/Z] ⊂ ℓ²(Q/Z)`, with `e(r)` the basis vector at `r`, and set `T_n := n^{−1/2}ψ_n`, extended linearly.
Then `(ψ_n f)(s) = Σ_{nr=s} f(r)`, and multiplication by `n` on `Q/Z` is surjective with fibres the cosets of
`(1/n)Z/Z`, each of size exactly `n`.
* **Contraction.** By Cauchy–Schwarz on each fibre, `|Σ_{nr=s}f(r)|² ≤ n Σ_{nr=s}|f(r)|²`, so `‖ψ_nf‖² ≤ n‖f‖²` and
  `‖T_n‖ ≤ 1`.
* **Multiplicativity.** `ψ_mψ_n = ψ_{mn}` gives `T_mT_n = T_{mn}`.
* **Adjoint.** `(T_n*g)(r) = n^{−1/2}g(nr)`.

**Claim: `T_2` has no unimodular eigenvalue; hence `Γ(T) = ∅`, and every `T_n*` likewise.** Suppose `T_2f = λf` with
`|λ| = 1`. Then `‖ψ_2f‖² = 2‖f‖²`, which is equality in every fibre's Cauchy–Schwarz. So `f` is constant on each coset
of `(1/2)Z/Z`. Applying the same to `T_2^k f = λ^k f`, which is also unimodular, `f` is constant on each coset of
`(1/2^k)Z/Z` for every `k`, hence on each coset of `Z[1/2]/Z`. That group is infinite, so `f ∈ ℓ²` forces `f = 0`. For
the adjoints use Lemma 1. ∎

**Entanglement.** `Q/Z = ⊕_p Q_p/Z_p`, so `ℓ²(Q/Z) = ⊗'_p(ℓ²(Q_p/Z_p), e_0)`. In this natural splitting, `ψ_q` acts on
the `q`-th factor by the `q`-to-1 map and on **every** other factor `p ≠ q` by the automorphism "multiply by the unit
`q ∈ Z_p^×`". So the family is not tail-factorizable in the natural splitting. That it is entangled in every splitting
I state as `HEURISTIC`, since it is not needed.

*Consequence for N-e1 (`PROVED` by example).* Infinite entanglement of Frobenius across primes, which `G1_B2_R2` proved
necessary, is **not sufficient** for Kronecker points. The cyclotomic Galois action entangles, and it comes from class
field theory, not from the product formula. Yet its unimodular point spectrum is empty. The obstruction here is
"one-sided" (an `n`-to-1 map), not a factorisation. This answers `G1_B2_R2` §5's question: an entangling mechanism
other than the product formula does exist, namely the cyclotomic λ-structure, but it carries no zero.

The trivial λ-structure on `Z` (`ψ_p = id`) gives `T_n = 1` and `Γ = {0}`, only the pole.

### 4.3 Prop Ψ (Connes–Consani Frobenius correspondences; `PROVED` modulo the `CITED` composition law, CC arXiv 1405.4527 Thm 4.3 as quoted in R158_001 l.368)

The composition law is `Ψ(λ)∘Ψ(λ′) = Ψ(λλ′)` except when `λ, λ′` are irrational with rational product, where the defect
`Id_ε` appears. So `{Ψ(n)}_{n∈N^×}` is a genuine multiplicative semigroup.

*(a)* For any realisation `n ↦ T_n` of `{Ψ(n)}` by contractions on a Hilbert space, Theorem C applies: if the
realisation is tail-factorizable, `|Γ(T)| ≤ 1` per summand.

*(b)* Suppose the realisation extends to a strongly continuous contraction representation `λ ↦ S(λ)` of `R^×_{>0}`
(the scaling action; for example `ϑ(λ)` on `L²(C_Q)` or on Connes' cokernel). Then `T_n = S(n)` is the flow at time
`log n`, and `n ↦ T_nw` is log-continuous for every `w`, so `V_c = H` in `G1_B2_R2`'s notation.
* This realisation escapes Theorem C only through the identity **Frobenius = flow**, the product formula of B2 §5(3).
  The Frobenius family then contains no information beyond the one-parameter flow.
* Its point spectrum on the Kronecker line is the flow's point spectrum.
* Any positivity is a positivity of the flow's space, i.e. the Weil/trace-pairing positivity, which is `EQUIV`
  (R158_002; R158_004 A17).

*Proof.* (a) is Theorem C, applied because the restricted law is multiplicative. (b): `T_n = S(n)`, and
`‖S(λ)w − w‖ → 0` as `λ → 1` restricts to `n → 1` in the log topology. ∎

*Reading.* The CC square does not escape the wall through its correspondence structure. It escapes, where it does, by
being the flow, and the flow's positivity is the one thing OT-5′ asks to be proved.

## §6 What is new, what is not, the residual, the weakest step

**Not new.** Lemma 1 is the classical fact behind the Sz.-Nagy–Foiaş canonical decomposition (unimodular eigenvectors
lie in the unitary part; Sz.-Nagy–Foiaş, *Harmonic Analysis of Operators on Hilbert Space*, Ch. I). Lemma 2 is folklore.
Lemma 2.2 is conditional Jensen. Prop F is the Hilbert–Pólya/Lemma T triviality. The ITP and product-measure facts are
von Neumann's and Guichardet's.

**New to the record (`PROVED`, elementary).**
1. **Theorem C:** the Kronecker-line wall for **contraction-valued (correspondence-valued) Frobenius families** with
  finite blocks of any size, with the tail set `Γ_tail` and an intrinsic proof. It needs no dilation (commuting triples
  need not dilate), no Fubini and no unitarity. It repairs E5 and E6 of `G1_B2_R2` Thm E for the point spectrum
  (Cor C2).
2. **Lemma 2 as an answer to the panel:** compressions do inherit the Kronecker point spectrum of any contractive
  dilation.
3. **Cor C3:** a restricted product of local motives with Markov Frobenius correspondences sees only the pole on the
  Kronecker line.
4. **Props BC, BL, Ψ:** the three F₁/correspondence frameworks on record carry, respectively, no Kronecker point, no
  Kronecker point, and only the flow's own spectrum.
  Prop BL gives the first entangled, non-product-formula Frobenius family in the record, and shows that N-e1 is not
  sufficient.
5. **N-f (necessary condition, proved inside the contraction class):** the contractivity (prior positivity) of the
  Frobenius family on the zero-carrying part must come from a source that is neither local (tail-factorizable or
  Markov, Thm C/Cor C3) nor the flow itself (Prop Ψ(b), `EQUIV`).

**The residual, stated so that it can be tested.** A G1 object in correspondence form needs all of the following:
* a multiplicative contraction family `(T_n)` on a Hilbert space;
* (1) **globally entangled**, i.e. not tail-factorizable;
* (2) with `T_n ≠ S(log n)` for any flow `S`, or else the Frobenius structure is idle;
* (3) whose contractivity is proved by an argument that fails for `Ξ_a` (OT-5 derivation);
* (4) with a Kronecker point spectrum that is infinite and not installed.

Prop BL shows that (1) alone gives nothing. I know of no family satisfying (1)–(3) (`OPEN`).

**Weakest step.**
* *Scope: point spectrum only.* Theorem C says nothing about continuous spectrum on the Kronecker line, and nothing about
  non-unimodular eigenvalues. Remark C4 covers the latter only for symmetric contractive realisations, which already
  presupposes RH by Prop F. A carrier in which the zeros appear as **resonances** or through a **cokernel**, not as
  eigenvectors of the Frobenius family (Connes' absorption spectrum, Meyer's nuclear Fréchet spaces), lies outside the
  theorem. In those carriers the Frobenius is the flow (Prop Ψ(b)), so they fall under the `EQUIV` classification
  instead, but that step is a reading of the known constructions, not a theorem about all non-Hilbert carriers.
* *Tail-factorizability is a hypothesis.* Theorem C is sharp inside its class, but whether a given correspondence family
  admits *some* infinite tensor splitting is not decided here. The entanglement of `Z[Q/Z]` in every splitting is
  `HEURISTIC`.
* *Cor C3 depends on the reference vectors being fixed vectors,* which is natural for `L²` of a product probability
  space. A tilted reference is installation.
* *`CITED` inputs:* von Neumann's ITP facts (a)–(c), `L²` of product spaces as an ITP (Guichardet), the CC composition law
  (as quoted in the record, not re-read), and Borger's Adams operations on `Z[Q/Z]` (standard λ-ring structure on a
  group ring).
