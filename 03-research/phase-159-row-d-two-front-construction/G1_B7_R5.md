## §2 Theorem A″: local summability forbids finite genus

**Theorem A″ (`PROVED`; the prime number theorem in Mertens' form `Σ_{p≤y}log p/p = log y + B + o(1)` is `CITED`
(std.)).**
* *Hypotheses.* Let `φ` be a positive trace datum with `φ(p) = −w(p)` for every prime `p` (only primes are needed),
  and genus `c = φ(1) < ∞`.
* *Conclusion.* For every `L > 0`, `S_L := Σ_{x∈Q_{>0}, x≠1, e^{−L}≤x≤e^{L}}|φ(x)| = ∞`.

*Proof.* Suppose `S_L < ∞` for some `L > 0`.

*Step 1 (the Gram inequality).* Positivity forces `c > 0`. Indeed, `c ≥ 0`, and `c = 0` would force `φ ≡ 0` by the
2×2 minors, contradicting `φ(2) ≠ 0`.

For `X > 8e^L`, let `P_X` be the set of primes in `(Xe^{−L}, X]`, and put `v_p := w(p) = log p/√p`. Let `E` be the
Hermitian matrix `(φ(p/q))_{p≠q∈P_X}`, with zero diagonal. Test positivity on `u = δ_1 + sΣ_{p∈P_X}v_pδ_p`, `s ∈ R`:
* the `(1,1)` term is `c`;
* the cross terms are `s·v_p(φ(p) + φ(1/p)) = −2s v_p²`;
* the `(p,p)` terms are `s²v_p²c`;
* the `(p,q)` terms are `s²v_pv_qφ(p/q)`.

So `0 ≤ c − 2s‖v‖² + s²(c‖v‖² + ⟨Ev,v⟩)` for all real `s`. The discriminant gives
`‖v‖⁴ ≤ c(c‖v‖² + ⟨Ev,v⟩)`, that is, `⟨Ev,v⟩ ≥ ‖v‖²(‖v‖²/c − c)`. (2.1)

*Step 2 (upper bound through unique factorization).* For distinct primes, the ordered pair `(p,q)` is determined by
the reduced fraction `p/q`. Every such ratio with `p, q ∈ P_X` lies in `[e^{−L}, e^{L}] ∖ {1}`. Also, `t ↦ (log t)²/t`
decreases for `t ≥ e²`, so `v_p² ≤ (log X)²e^L/X` on `P_X`. Hence
`|⟨Ev,v⟩| ≤ Σ_{p≠q∈P_X}|φ(p/q)|v_pv_q ≤ (max_{P_X}v_p²)·S_L ≤ (log X)²e^LS_L/X → 0` as `X → ∞`. (2.2)

*Step 3 (lower bound through PNT).*
`‖v‖² = Σ_{p∈P_X}(log p)²/p ≥ (log X − L)·Σ_{Xe^{−L}<p≤X}log p/p = (log X − L)(L + o(1))`, which tends to `∞`.
So the right side of (2.1) tends to `+∞`, while by (2.2) the left side tends to `0`. Contradiction. ∎

*Remarks.*
* **(A″.1)** Theorem B's Künneth trace is positive with finite genus. By A″ it has `S_L = ∞` for every `L`. This is a
  consistency check, and it can be confirmed directly: the pairs `p, q ∈ (X/2, X]` alone contribute
  `≍ Σ(log X)²/X ≍ X`.
* **(A″.2)** The proof gives more than `S_L = ∞`. By (2.1), the **weighted** mixed mass
  `Σ_{p≠q∈P_X}|φ(p/q)|v_pv_q` over one short window is `≥ (1+o(1))L²(log X)²/c`, which is unbounded in `X`.
* **(A″.3)** A″ uses only the primes, and only the multiplicative window `(Xe^{−L}, X]`. It is therefore insensitive to
  the values at prime powers `k ≥ 2`, and to any change of `φ` on finitely many primes. The same holds for any Beurling
  system obeying Mertens in the `o(1)` form (OT-4 reading, §5).

## §3 Theorem Γ₀: no Radon polarization

**Setting.**
* *Distributions.* A real even distribution `D` on `R` is **positive-definite** if `D(f∗f̃) ≥ 0` for all
  `f ∈ C_c^∞(R)`, where `f̃(t) := conj f(−t)`.
* *The comb.* `comb := Σ_{r prime power} w(r)(δ_{log r} + δ_{−log r})`. It is locally finite. In Weil's explicit
  formula the prime side enters as `−comb` (see (4.4) below).
* *The probe.* Fix a real `ψ ∈ C_c^∞([−½,½])` with `‖ψ‖₂ = 1`, and put `ψ_ε(t) := ε^{−1/2}ψ(t/ε)` and
  `g_ε := ψ_ε∗ψ̃_ε`. Then `g_ε` is real, even, supported in `[−ε, ε]`, with `g_ε(0) = 1`, `|g_ε| ≤ 1` and
  `∫|g_ε| ≤ 2ε`.
* *Smeared Frobenius vectors.* For points `s_1, …, s_k` and `a ∈ C^k`, put `f = Σ_j a_jψ_ε(· − s_j)`. Then
  `D(f∗f̃) = Σ_{j,l}a_jā_l D(g_ε(· − (s_j − s_l)))`. (3.1)

**Theorem Γ₀ (`PROVED`).**
* *Hypotheses.* Let `D` be a real even positive-definite distribution which is a Radon measure on `R`. Suppose it
  carries the prime atoms: `D({±log p}) = −w(p)` for every prime `p`.
* *Conclusion.* No such `D` exists.

*Proof.*

*Step 1 (the limit is a trace datum).* For fixed `s`, dominated convergence (with respect to `|D|` on a compact
neighbourhood of `s`) gives `D(g_ε(· − s)) → D({s})` as `ε → 0`, because `g_ε(t − s) → 1_{t=s}` pointwise, with
`|g_ε| ≤ 1`. Apply (3.1) to `s_j = log x_j` for finitely many `x_j ∈ Q_{>0}`, and let `ε → 0`. The matrix
`[D({log(x_j/x_l)})]_{j,l}` is then a limit of positive semidefinite matrices, hence positive semidefinite.

So `φ(x) := D({log x})` is a positive trace datum on `Q^×_{>0}`, with
* `φ(p) = −w(p)` for every prime `p`,
* genus `φ(1) = D({0}) < ∞`.

*Step 2 (local summability).* Distinct `x` give distinct points `log x`. Hence
`Σ_{x≠1, |log x|≤L}|φ(x)| ≤ |D|([−L, L]) < ∞` for every `L`.

*Step 3.* Step 2 contradicts Theorem A″. ∎

*Reading.* The hypothesis on atoms is the minimal form of "the Lefschetz numbers of the prime Frobenii are the
explicit formula's". Everything else is allowed:
* atoms at every other point (every invented mixed Lefschetz number, Künneth-style);
* any continuous or singular-continuous part;
* a finite atom at `0` (a finite genus).

A polarization of the continuous completion that is a measure does not exist. The divergence must sit **at the
identity**, and it cannot be a measure there.

## §4 Theorem Γ (the genus law) and ζ's saturation

**Hypothesis (H_M).** `D = −comb + D₀`, where `D₀` is a real even distribution such that:
* on `R ∖ {0}`, `D₀` is a locally integrable function;
* it satisfies `|D₀(t)| ≤ M(1 + |t|^{−1} + e^{|t|/2})` for `t ≠ 0`.

Near `0`, `D₀` is an arbitrary distribution. Its **diagonal at scale `ε`** is `d(ε) := D₀(g_ε)`.

**Lemma 4.1 (decoupling at scale `ε < 1/(2N)`; `PROVED`).** Let `m ≠ n ≤ N`, and let `r ≥ 2` be an integer with
`m/n ≠ r^{±1}`. Then:
* (i) `|log(m/n)| ≥ 1/N`;
* (ii) `|log(m/n) ∓ log r| ≥ 1/(2N)`.

Hence for `ε < 1/(2N)` and `f = Σ_{n≤N}a_nψ_ε(· − log n)`:
`D(f∗f̃) = d(ε)‖a‖² − ⟨A′_Na, a⟩ + ⟨Ea, a⟩`, where `E_{mn} := D₀(g_ε(· − log(m/n)))` for `m ≠ n` and `E_{nn} := 0`.

*Proof.*

(i) `|log(m/n)| ≥ log(1 + 1/(N−1)) ≥ 1/N`.

(ii) Put `a = m/n` and `b = r`, and treat `+log r`; the other sign follows by exchanging `m` and `n`.
* If `b > 2a`, then `|log b − log a| > log 2 ≥ 1/(2N)`.
* Otherwise `a ≠ b` and `n(a − b) = m − bn` is a nonzero integer, so `|a − b| ≥ 1/n`. Then
  `|log a − log b| ≥ |a − b|/max(a,b) ≥ (1/n)/(2m/n) = 1/(2m) ≥ 1/(2N)`.

*The comb.* Since `supp g_ε ⊂ [−ε, ε]`, the pair `(m, n)` sees the comb atom at `±log r` only when
`|log(m/n) ∓ log r| ≤ ε`. By (ii) that happens only if `m/n = r^{±1}` exactly, and then the contribution is
`−w(r)g_ε(0) = −w(r)`. The diagonal pairs see no comb atom, since `log r ≥ log 2 > ε`. So the comb block is exactly
`−A′_N`.

*The rest.* The `D₀` diagonal gives `d(ε)`, and the `D₀` off-diagonal gives `E`. ∎

**Lemma 4.2 (the off-diagonal is small; `PROVED`).** Under (H_M) and `ε ≤ 1/(N(1+log N))` with `N ≥ 3`, one has
`‖E‖_{op} ≤ 18M`.

*Proof.* Note first that `ε ≤ 1/(N(1+log N)) ≤ 1/(2N)`.

*Entry bound.* Take `m ≠ n`, `t₀ = log(m/n)`, and `|t − t₀| ≤ ε`. Since `|t₀| ≥ 1/N ≥ 2ε`, we have `|t| ≥ |t₀|/2`. So
`|D₀(t)| ≤ M(1 + 2/|t₀| + 2e^{|t₀|/2})`. Also `1/|t₀| ≤ max(m,n)/|m−n| ≤ N/|m−n|` and
`e^{|t₀|/2} ≤ √(m/n) + √(n/m)`. Since `∫|g_ε| ≤ 2ε`, we get
`|E_{mn}| ≤ 2εM[1 + 2N/|m−n|] + 4εM[√(m/n) + √(n/m)]`.

*Norm bound.* An entrywise-dominated Hermitian matrix has `‖E‖ ≤ ‖|E|‖`. Bound each piece of `|E|`:
* The first bracket: by the Schur row-sum test, `2εM(N + 4N(1+log N)) ≤ 2εMN(5 + 4log N) ≤ 10M`.
* The second: put `a_n = √n` and `b_n = 1/√n`. This piece is dominated by `4εM(ab^⊤ + ba^⊤)`, of norm
  `≤ 8εM‖a‖‖b‖ ≤ 8εMN√(1+log N) ≤ 8M`. ∎

**Theorem Γ (the genus law; `PROVED`, with Mertens' `Σ_{r≤y}Λ(r)/r = log y + O(1)` `CITED` (std.) through A(iii)).**
If `D` satisfies (H_M) and is positive-definite, then for all sufficiently small `ε > 0`:
`d(ε) ≥ log(1/ε) − log(1 + log(1/ε)) − C₁ − 18M − log 2`. (4.1)
In particular `liminf_{ε→0} d(ε)/log(1/ε) ≥ 1`, and `D₀` is not a Radon measure near `0`.

*Proof.* Given small `ε`, put `N := ⌊1/(ε(1+log(1/ε)))⌋ ≥ 3`.
* Since `log N ≤ log(1/ε)`, we have `ε ≤ 1/(N(1+log N))`.
* By Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, positivity on the span of `{ψ_ε(· − log n)}_{n≤N}` gives
  `d(ε) ≥ λ_max(A′_N) − 18M`.
* Round-4 A(iii) gives `λ_max(A′_N) ≥ log N − C₁`.
* Finally, `log N ≥ log(1/ε) − log(1+log(1/ε)) − log 2`.

The non-Radon clause: a finite measure near `0` has `D₀(g_ε) → D₀({0})`, which is finite. ∎

**Lemma 4.3 (upper bound; `PROVED`; the idea is `G2_B1_R5` Lemma S, credited).** Let
`E₀ := sup_{y≥1}(Σ_{j≤y}Λ(j)/j − log y) < ∞` (Mertens). Then `λ_max(A′_N) ≤ log N + E₀`.

*Proof.* `A′_N` is symmetric, with nonnegative entries and zero diagonal. Apply the Schur test with weights
`w_k = k^{−1/2}`. Row `m` splits in two:
* the entries with `k | m`, `k < m`, contribute `m^{1/2}Σ_{k|m,k<m}Λ(m/k)(m/k)^{−1/2}k^{−1/2} = Σ_{d|m}Λ(d) = log m`;
* the entries with `k = mj ≤ N` contribute `Σ_{2≤j≤N/m}Λ(j)/j ≤ log(N/m) + E₀`.

The total is `≤ log N + E₀`. ∎

So **`λ_max(A′_N) = log N + O(1)`, two-sided** (`PROVED`).

**Proposition Σ_ζ (ζ saturates the genus law; `PROVED`, given the `CITED` inputs below).**

*Inputs (`CITED`).*
* The explicit formula in Weil's form, for even `g = f∗f̃`, `f ∈ C_c^∞(R)`, with `ĝ(τ) = ∫g(t)e^{iτt}dt`:
  `W(g) := ∫g(t)(e^{t/2}+e^{−t/2})dt − comb(g) + (2π)^{−1}∫ĝ(τ)m(τ)dτ`,
  `m(τ) = Re ψ(¼+iτ/2) − log π`, (4.4)
  and `W(g) = Σ_ρĝ((ρ−½)/i)`. This is P46 `eq:W`/`eq:form` in the notation of l.114–146, and Weil 1952 / Bombieri
  2000. Weil's criterion: `W` is positive-definite iff RH.
* The archimedean term `(2π)^{−1}∫ĝm` equals, on `R ∖ {0}`, an even smooth kernel bounded by `M_∞(1 + |t|^{−1})`
  (P46's `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`, up to the normalisation `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n ↔ 2w`).
* Stirling: `Re ψ(¼+iτ/2) = log(|τ|/2) + O(τ^{−2})` for `|τ| ≥ 1`.

*Statement.*
* (i) `W = −comb + D₀^W`, where `D₀^W` is the archimedean term plus the polar term `2cosh(t/2)`. It satisfies (H_M)
  with `M = M_∞ + 2`.
* (ii) `d_W(ε) = log(1/ε) + C_ψ + O(ε)`, where
  `C_ψ := (2π)^{−1}∫|ψ̂(η)|²log(|η|/2)dη − log π`.
* (iii) Hence (4.1) is sharp to within `log log(1/ε) + O(1)`. **The archimedean place of ζ supplies exactly the leading
  rate that positivity forces on any polarization carrying ζ's prime side.**
* (iv) At the decoupling scale `ε = 1/(N(1+log N))`, the smeared Gram of `W` is
  `(log N + log(1+log N) + C_ψ + o(1))I − A′_N + E_W`, with `‖E_W‖ ≤ 18M`. Its diagonal exceeds `λ_max(A′_N)` by
  `log log N + O(1)`, and by nothing more at leading order.

*Proof.*

(i) The polar term is the function `2cosh(t/2) ≤ 2e^{|t|/2}`, and the archimedean kernel is as cited.

(ii) The polar part of the diagonal is `∫g_ε·2cosh(t/2) ≤ 4ε`. For the archimedean part, `ĝ_ε(τ) = ε|ψ̂(ετ)|²`. Split
`m(τ) = log(|τ|/2) − log π + h(τ)`.
* The term `h` is in `L¹`: it is `O(τ^{−2})` for `|τ| ≥ 1`, and near `0` it is `m(τ) − log(|τ|/2) + log π`, with an
  integrable logarithmic singularity. So `(2π)^{−1}∫ε|ψ̂(ετ)|²h ≤ ε‖ψ̂‖_∞²‖h‖₁/2π = O(ε)`.
* Substitute `η = ετ` in the main term:
  `(2π)^{−1}∫|ψ̂(η)|²(log|η| − log ε − log 2)dη = log(1/ε)‖ψ‖₂² + (2π)^{−1}∫|ψ̂|²log(|η|/2)`,
  by Plancherel.
* The `−log π` term contributes `−log π·g_ε(0) = −log π`.

(iii) Compare (ii) with (4.1).

(iv) Combine Lemma 4.1, (ii), Lemma 4.2 and Lemma 4.3. ∎

*What Σ_ζ does not say.* It gives **no sign** of `W` on any subspace beyond the smeared family at the decoupling
scale. On that family the sign is unconditional (it is `G2_B1_R5` Prop P) and void (`cor:void`: the same proof works
for the falsifier combs of `thm:finmod`). It says nothing about windows `T > 0.8`, and nothing about `P_T`.

*Relation to the record.*
* **P47 Thm 5.8 / Rem 5.9** is the ζ-specific fixed-`N`, `ε → 0` asymptotic. Γ is its **converse and universal
  version**: any positive completion must have that divergence, with leading coefficient `≥ 1`.
* **`G2_B1_R5` Thm D** is the dual reading from the comb side: fix the archimedean term `m`; then the comb's density is
  at most Λ's. Γ fixes the comb and bounds every possible archimedean term from below. The two together say that Λ
  and `m` are *mutually extremal* at leading order.

## §6 What is new, what is not, the weakest step

**Not new.**
* Weil's criterion; P47 Thm 5.8 (ζ's own `log(1/ε)` diagonal); `G2_B1_R5` Lemma S (the Schur bound, reused in
  Lemma 4.3 with credit) and Thm D (the dual, comb-side ceiling).
* Mertens, PNT, Stirling, Plancherel, and the Schur test are standard.
* That the zero density forces a `log`-divergent archimedean term is folklore in the explicit-formula literature. What
  is new here is the direction and the class: *positivity plus the prime side alone* force it, for *every* completion
  in (H_M), with constant 1.

**New to the record (`PROVED`).**
1. **Theorem A″.** A finite-genus positive trace carrying the prime atoms is never locally summable near the identity.
  The proof uses a short-window prime star and unique factorization.
2. **Theorem Γ₀.** No Radon polarization of the continuous Frobenius algebra carries the prime atoms. This is
  "infinite genus" in the continuous completion, proved for all completions, not just ζ's.
3. **Theorem Γ with Proposition Σ_ζ.**
  * The genus law: `d(ε) ≥ log(1/ε) − log log(1/ε) − O(1)`.
  * ζ's archimedean place attains the rate: `d_W(ε) = log(1/ε) + O(1)`.
  * `λ_max(A′_N) = log N + O(1)`, two-sided.
4. **Corollary M′ and N-h.** The continuous motivic trichotomy, and a proved archimedean necessary condition for OT-12.
  Together with round-4 Corollary M, this closes the motivic angle on both completions, including the failure of
  André's positivity-import device.

**Weakest step.**
* **Hypothesis (H_M).** Branch (c) of M′ covers completions that are *not functions* away from the identity, e.g.
  measures with atoms accumulating away from `0`, or distributions of higher order. Theorem Γ does not reach them. Γ₀
  covers the Radon ones only if they are Radon everywhere, including at `0`.
  * Case (c) is where the adelic crossed products sit, and there the record already has `EQUIV` (A17).
  * I have no candidate in (c) outside A17.
  * The mixed case "Radon off `0`, singular at `0`, atoms away from `0`" is the one real gap. The A″ argument needs the
  near-identity ratios `p/q`, which fall into the singular region, and Γ's decoupling needs a pointwise bound. I
  label this case `OPEN`.
* **`OPEN` (from round 4, restated sharply).** Does there exist a finite positive measure `m` on `R` with
  `m̂(log r) = −Λ(r)r^{−1/2}` for every prime power `r`? This is a pure-line finite-genus completion.
  * By Prop G, a positive answer is not an object: it carries no zero atoms with multiplicity, and any zero content
  would be weighted, i.e. installation.
  * A″ does not apply, since `m̂∘log` is continuous, not summable. The trivial majorant construction fails because
  `Σ_rw(r) = ∞`.
* **`CITED` inputs.** PNT in Mertens' `o(1)` form (A″ only). Mertens' `O(1)` form (A(iii), Lemma 4.3). Stirling and
  P46's kernel `K` (Σ_ζ only). Γ, Γ₀ and A″ themselves use no zero information.
* **The G1 objective is not met. No sign is proved or claimed, in the primitive or the complete form, for any `T`.**
