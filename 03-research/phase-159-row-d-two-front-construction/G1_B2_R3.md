3. **Theorem I (§3, `PROVED`), which answers the panel's dilation question by avoiding dilations.** For a
  tail-factorizable commuting family of **contractions**, every eigenvector with a unimodular eigencharacter lies in

  So at most one on-line eigenvalue survives. Compressions do *not* inherit the wall off the axis, and a compressed

## §2 Theorem E\*: the abstract point-spectrum wall (normal families, any eventually separating shape)

**Definition 2.1 (local shape).** A *local shape* is a family of functions `φ_p: Ω → C` (`p ∈ P`) on a parameter set
`Ω ⊂ R`. It is **eventually separating** if, for all `γ ≠ γ′` in `Ω`, `φ_p(γ) ≠ φ_p(γ′)` for all but finitely many `p`.
Write `Φ_A(γ) := (φ_p(γ))_{p∈A}`.

**Lemma P\* (the two shapes of the record; `PROVED`).**
(a) *Unitary shape* `φ_p(γ) = p^{iγ}` on `Ω = R`. For `γ ≠ γ′`, `#{p : p^{iγ} = p^{iγ′}} ≤ 1`.
(b) *Hecke shape* `φ_p(γ) = p^{iγ} + p^{−iγ} = 2cos(γ log p)` on `Ω = [0, ∞)`. For `γ ≠ γ′` in `Ω`,
`#{p : cos(γ log p) = cos(γ′ log p)} ≤ 2`.
(c) Composing with injective maps `f_p: C → C` (for example the renormalisations `c ↦ a_p c + b_p` with `a_p ≠ 0`)
preserves eventual separation.

*Proof.* (a) If `p ≠ q` both satisfy it, then `(γ − γ′)log p = 2πa` and `(γ − γ′)log q = 2πb` with `a, b ∈ Z ∖ {0}`,
so `p^b = q^a`, which contradicts unique factorisation.
(b) `cos x = cos y` iff `x ≡ ±y (mod 2π)`. For `γ ≠ γ′`, both `≥ 0`, both `γ − γ′` and `γ + γ′` are nonzero. By (a)'s
argument applied to each sign, each sign occurs for at most one prime.
(c) is immediate. ∎

**Setting 2.2 (tail-factorizable normal family).** Let `(C_p)_{p∈P}` be bounded commuting **normal** operators on
`H = H_0 ⊗ ⊗'_j(H_j, ξ_j)`. For `p ∈ B_j`, `C_p = c_p ⊗ 1` acts on the `j`-th factor, and `(c_p)_{p∈B_j}` is a commuting
normal family on `H_j`; likewise for `p ∈ P_0`. By Fuglede's theorem the `C_p, C_q^*` all commute. Let `E` be the joint
spectral measure on the compact metrisable space `X := ∏_p σ(C_p)`, with `C_p = ∫ z_p dE(z)` (`CITED`: the spectral
theorem for the commutative C\*-algebra generated by `{C_p, C_p^*}`, whose Gelfand spectrum embeds in `X`; standard).

**Lemma 2.3 (product measure; `PROVED`).** For an elementary unit tensor `w` with `w_j = ξ_j` for `j > J`,
`μ_w = μ^{(0)}_{w_0} ⊗ ⊗_j μ^{(j)}_{w_j}`.
*Proof.* Both sides are Borel probability measures on the compact space `X`. The functions `z^α z̄^β` (finitely
supported multi-indices) span a `*`-algebra that separates points and contains `1`, so by Stone–Weierstrass it is dense
in `C(X)`. The left side gives `∫ z^α z̄^β dμ_w = ⟨C^α C^{*β} w, w⟩`. This factorises over the finitely many blocks that
meet `supp α ∪ supp β`, with every other block contributing `⟨w_j, w_j⟩ = 1`. The right side gives the same product.
Measures that agree on a dense subspace of `C(X)` are equal (Riesz). ∎

**Theorem E\* (`PROVED`).** In Setting 2.2, let `φ` be an eventually separating local shape on `Ω`, and put
`Γ_pp := {γ ∈ Ω : E({Φ_P(γ)}) ≠ 0}` (the parameters whose Kronecker point is an atom, i.e. a **joint eigencharacter**).
Then `Γ_pp ⊂ Γ^φ_tail(ξ) := {γ ∈ Ω : ∃ j_0, ∏_{j≥j_0} μ^{(j)}_{ξ_j}({Φ_{B_j}(γ)}) > 0}`, and `|Γ^φ_tail(ξ)| ≤ 1`.
*Proof.* Suppose `E({Φ_P(γ)}) ≠ 0`. It cannot annihilate every elementary tensor, since their span is dense, so some
elementary `w` (tail `ξ` beyond `J`) has `μ_w({Φ_P(γ)}) > 0`. By Lemma 2.3 and the product formula for product sets,
this mass is `μ_{w_0}({Φ_{P_0}(γ)}) ∏_{j≤J} μ_{w_j}({Φ_{B_j}(γ)}) ∏_{j>J} μ_{ξ_j}({Φ_{B_j}(γ)})`. So the tail product
from `J+1` on is positive, and `γ ∈ Γ^φ_tail`.
Now let `γ ≠ γ′` both lie in `Γ^φ_tail`, and put `a_j := μ_{ξ_j}({Φ_{B_j}(γ)})`, `a′_j` likewise. For `j` large,
`a_j, a′_j ∈ (0,1]` and the tail products are positive. Hence `a_j → 1` and `a′_j → 1`, since `a_j ≤ 1 − δ` infinitely
often would force the product to `0`. By eventual separation, only finitely many primes have
`φ_p(γ) = φ_p(γ′)`. The `B_j` are disjoint and nonempty, so for all large `j` the points `Φ_{B_j}(γ) ≠ Φ_{B_j}(γ′)` are
distinct singletons. Their masses `a_j + a′_j → 2 > 1` contradict `μ_{ξ_j}` being a probability measure. ∎

For the unitary shape, `Γ_pp` is the point spectrum of the suspended flow on `V_c` (Theorem S). The theorem covers it
and also the Hecke shape, where there is no flow at all.

**Prop X (intersection law; `PROVED`).** Let `P = A ⊔ B` with `A, B` infinite, `H = H_A ⊗ H_B`, and `C_p` acting on the
factor containing `p`, with `(C_p)` normal and `φ` a shape such that `Φ_A` and `Φ_B` are injective on `Ω`. Then
`Γ_pp(H) = Γ^A_pp(H_A) ∩ Γ^B_pp(H_B)`, where `Γ^A_pp := {γ : E_A({Φ_A(γ)}) ≠ 0}`.
*Proof.* `E = E_A ⊗ E_B` on `X_A × X_B`: check on `z^α z̄^β` as in Lemma 2.3, where product rectangles generate. So
`E({Φ_A(γ)} × {Φ_B(γ)}) = E_A({Φ_A(γ)}) ⊗ E_B({Φ_B(γ)})`, and a tensor product of projections is nonzero iff both
factors are. ∎

*Reading (`HEURISTIC`; the statements are proved).* A Künneth-type splitting of the carrier over two halves of the
primes forces **each half, by itself**, to carry every zero's local character `Φ_A(γ_ρ)`. Iterating over infinitely
many finite blocks leaves at most one point (Theorem E\*). This is the precise sense of N-e1: the zeros cannot be the
intersection of infinitely many independent prime-local conditions.

## §3 Theorem I: contractive (correspondence-valued) Frobenius, with no dilation needed

The panel's question was whether a correspondence-valued Frobenius dilates to a unitary `Q^×_{>0}` representation.
In general **it does not**. Andô's theorem gives a commuting unitary dilation for **two** commuting contractions, and
Parrott's example gives **three** commuting contractions with none (`CITED`: Andô, Acta Sci. Math. 24 (1963); Parrott,
J. Funct. Anal. 5 (1970); Sz.-Nagy–Foiaş, *Harmonic Analysis of Operators on Hilbert Space*, Ch. I.6). So a
dilation route cannot be the general answer. P2's hazard also applies: a dilation changes the space. Theorem I needs
neither.

**Lemma U0 (`PROVED`, classical).** If `C` is a contraction, `Cx = λx`, and `|λ| = 1`, then `C^*x = λ̄x`.
*Proof.* `‖C^*x − λ̄x‖² = ‖C^*x‖² − 2Re(λ⟨C^*x, x⟩) + ‖x‖² = ‖C^*x‖² − 2Re(λ⟨x, Cx⟩) + ‖x‖²`
`= ‖C^*x‖² − 2‖x‖² + ‖x‖² ≤ 0`. ∎

**Lemma U1 (joint unitary part; `PROVED`).** Let `(C_p)_{p∈A}` be a countable commuting family of contractions on `K`.
Put `K_u := {x : ‖C^α x‖ = ‖x‖ = ‖C^{*α}x‖ for all finitely supported α ∈ N^{(A)}}`. Then:
(i) `K_u = ⋂_α ker(1 − C^{*α}C^α) ∩ ker(1 − C^αC^{*α})`, a closed subspace;
(ii) `K_u` reduces every `C_p`, and `C_p|K_u` is unitary;
(iii) every subspace that reduces all `C_p` and on which all `C_p` are unitary lies in `K_u`.
*Proof.* (i) `X := C^{*α}C^α` satisfies `0 ≤ X ≤ 1`, and `⟨(1 − X)x, x⟩ = ‖x‖² − ‖C^αx‖²`. For `1 − X ≥ 0` the
quadratic form vanishes iff `(1 − X)^{1/2}x = 0` iff `(1 − X)x = 0`. The same holds for `C^αC^{*α}`.
(ii) Let `x ∈ K_u` and `y := C_p x`. Then `‖y‖ = ‖x‖` and `‖C^α y‖ = ‖C^{α+e_p}x‖ = ‖x‖ = ‖y‖`. By (i), `C_p^*y = x`.
Hence `‖y‖ ≥ ‖C^{*α}y‖ ≥ ‖C_p^*C^{*α}y‖ = ‖C^{*α}C_p^*y‖ = ‖C^{*α}x‖ = ‖x‖ = ‖y‖`, using that the `C^*`s commute. So
`y ∈ K_u`. The definition of `K_u` is symmetric under `C ↔ C^*` (the family `(C_p^*)` is again commuting and
contractive), so `C_p^* K_u ⊂ K_u` too. On `K_u`, `C_p^*C_p = 1 = C_pC_p^*` by (i).
(iii) On such a subspace every `C^α` is unitary, so the defining norms are preserved. ∎

**Setting 3.1 (tail-factorizable contractive family).** As in Setting 2.2, with "normal" replaced by "contraction":
`C_p = c_p ⊗ 1` on the block factor, and each block family `(c_p)_{p∈B_j}` (resp. `P_0`) is commuting and contractive.
Write `(H_j)_u` for the joint unitary part of block `j` and `Q_j` for its projection.

**Lemma U2 (the unitary part of a product is the product of unitary parts; `PROVED`).** In Setting 3.1:
(i) `H_u = ⋂_{j≥0} ran(Q̃_j)`, where `Q̃_j := Q_j ⊗ 1` acts on the `j`-th factor.
(ii) Either `H_u = 0`, or the following holds. After changing finitely many `ξ_j`, which changes neither `H` nor the
family, put `η_j := Q_jξ_j/‖Q_jξ_j‖`. Then `Σ_j(1 − ‖Q_jξ_j‖) < ∞`, and `H_u = (H_0)_u ⊗ ⊗'_j((H_j)_u, η_j)` with
`C_p|H_u = (c_p|(H_j)_u) ⊗ 1`. So `H_u` is a tail-factorizable **unitary** family.
*Proof.* (i) "⊂": let `x ∈ H_u` and `α` be supported in `B_j`. Under the canonical identification
`H = H_j ⊗ H^{(j)}` (von Neumann, loc. cit.), `C^{*α}C^α = (c^{*α}c^α) ⊗ 1`. For any bounded `Y`,
`ker(Y ⊗ 1) = ker Y ⊗ H^{(j)}`: expand `z = Σ_k z_k ⊗ e_k` in an orthonormal basis of `H^{(j)}`, and note that
`(Y⊗1)z = Σ Yz_k ⊗ e_k`. So `x` lies in `⋂_α (ker(1 − c^{*α}c^α) ∩ ker(1 − c^αc^{*α})) ⊗ H^{(j)} = ran Q̃_j`. The
intersection of `ran(P_α ⊗ 1)` is `ran((⋀P_α) ⊗ 1)`.
"⊃": the `Q̃_j` are commuting projections. For `p ∈ B_j`, `C_p` commutes with `Q̃_i` for `i ≠ j` (different factors),
and with `Q̃_j` because `(H_j)_u` reduces `c_p`. So `M := ⋂ ran Q̃_j` reduces every `C_p`. On `M`,
`C_p^*C_p = (c_p^*c_p ⊗ 1)Q̃_j = Q̃_j = 1`, and similarly for `C_pC_p^*`. By Lemma U1(iii), `M ⊂ H_u`.
(ii) `Q := s-lim_J Q̃_0 ⋯ Q̃_J` is the projection onto `M`, as a decreasing limit of commuting projections. For an
elementary `w` with tail `ξ` beyond `J`, `‖Q̃_0⋯Q̃_{J′}w‖ = ‖Q_0w_0‖ ∏_{1≤j≤J} ‖Q_jw_j‖ ∏_{J<j≤J′} ‖Q_jξ_j‖`.
If `∏_{j>J}‖Q_jξ_j‖ = 0` for every `J`, then `Qw = 0` on a spanning set, so `H_u = 0`. If some `(H_j)_u = 0`, also
`H_u = 0`. Otherwise there is `J` with `∏_{j>J}‖Q_jξ_j‖ > 0`. Then `Σ_{j>J}(1 − ‖Q_jξ_j‖) ≤ −Σ log‖Q_jξ_j‖ < ∞`.
For `j ≤ J`, replace `ξ_j` by a unit vector of `(H_j)_u`; finitely many changes do not change the ITPS. Now
`⟨ξ_j, η_j⟩ = ‖Q_jξ_j‖` and `Σ|1 − ⟨ξ_j, η_j⟩| < ∞`, so `(ξ_j)` and `(η_j)` are equivalent C₀-sequences and
`⊗'(H_j, ξ_j) = ⊗'(H_j, η_j)` canonically (`CITED`: von Neumann 1939, the equivalence criterion for C₀-sequences;
Guichardet, *Tensor products of C\*-algebras* II, 1969). `Q` fixes every elementary tensor with factors in `(H_j)_u` and
tail `η`. It maps every elementary tensor with tail `η` to such a tensor, because `Q_jη_j = η_j`. Density and continuity
give `ran Q = ⊗'((H_j)_u, η_j)` (with the `P_0` factor). ∎

**Theorem I (`PROVED`).** In Setting 3.1, let `Γ_u := {γ ∈ R : ∃ x ≠ 0 with C_n x = n^{iγ}x for all n ∈ N}`. Then
`|Γ_u| ≤ 1`. In particular, let `T_t` (`t ≥ 0`) be any C₀ contraction semigroup with `T_{log p} = C_p` for all `p` (a
contractive, not necessarily unitary, "flow" whose value at `log p` is the prime-local Frobenius). Then the generator
of `T_t` has at most one eigenvalue on `iR`. More precisely, all eigenvectors with eigenvalues on `iR` lie in one
eigenspace `ker(A − iγ_0) ⊂ H_u`.
*Proof.* Let `C_n x = n^{iγ}x` for all `n`, with `x ≠ 0`. By Lemma U0, `C_n^*x = n^{−iγ}x`, so
`C^{*α}C^αx = x = C^αC^{*α}x` and `x ∈ H_u` by Lemma U1(i). Then `H_u ≠ 0`, and by Lemma U2(ii) it carries a
tail-factorizable **unitary**, hence normal, family, and `x` is a joint eigenvector with eigencharacter `ι(γ)`. The
joint eigenspace of a commuting unitary family for a character `χ` is `ran E({χ})`, so `γ ∈ Γ_pp(H_u)`. Theorem E\*
with the unitary shape (Lemma P\*(a)) gives `|Γ_u| ≤ 1`. For the semigroup: if `Ax = iγx`, then
`T_t x = e^{iγt}x` (standard, via the resolvent / `d/dt T_t x = T_tAx`), so `C_n x = T_{log n}x = n^{iγ}x`. ∎

**Remark I.1 (what compressions do and do not inherit; `PROVED` for the "do", `CITED` for the "do not").**
* *Do not, off the axis.* Take the translation semigroup on `H²` of a half-plane, compressed to a model space
  `K_Θ = H² ⊖ ΘH²` with `Θ` a Blaschke product. The compressed semigroup is completely non-unitary, and its generator's
  point spectrum is the zero set of `Θ` (`CITED`: Lax–Phillips, *Scattering Theory*, Ch. III; Nikolski, *Operators,
  Functions, and Systems* I, Lecture I). Any Blaschke set, for instance the reflected zeros of ζ or of `Ξ_a`, can be
  **installed** off the axis. P2's hazard is real: a compression does not inherit the dilation's constraint there.
* *Do, on the axis.* On-axis eigenvectors of contractions are reducing (Lemma U0), so they sit in the unitary part,
  where the tensor structure survives (Lemma U2). Theorem I needs no dilation for this reason.

**Remark I.2 (the semigroup's own dilation; `PROVED` + `CITED`).** When `C_p = T_{log p}` for a C₀ contraction
semigroup, the minimal unitary dilation `Ũ_t` on `K ⊃ H` exists (`CITED`: Sz.-Nagy–Foiaş Ch. I.8), and
`n ↦ Ũ_{log n}` is a unitary `Q^×_{>0}` representation on `K`. But tail-factorizability of `(C_p)` on `H` does not give
tail-factorizability of `(Ũ_{log p})` on `K`, since the dilation is built from the flow, not from the blocks. Theorem I
bypasses this. Its hypothesis is on the carrier's own Frobenius operators.

## §4 Corollary H: the unconditional exclusion

**Definition 4.1 (prime-local operator carrier).** A triple `𝒪 = (H, (C_p)_p, (x_ρ))` where:
* `H` is a Hilbert space: the positive form, P-HS shape;
* `(C_p)` is a tail-factorizable commuting family that is **either** (a) contractive, **or** (b) normal;
* `(x_ρ)` assigns to each nontrivial zero `ρ = ½ + iγ` of ζ **on the critical line** a vector `x_ρ ≠ 0` with the matching
  local shape: `C_n x_ρ = n^{iγ}x_ρ` for all `n` (unitary shape, cases (a) and (b)), or
  `C_p x_ρ = f_p(2cos(γ log p))x_ρ` for all `p` with fixed injective `f_p` (Hecke shape, case (b)).

This is the minimal content of OT-1 + OT-2 in Deninger's normalisation. With `⟨φ^{t*}·, φ^{t*}·⟩ = e^t⟨·,·⟩`
(P-HS), `T_t := e^{−t/2}φ^{t*}` is isometric/contractive, and a zero `ρ` with Θ-eigenvector `h` satisfies
`T_t h = e^{(ρ−½)t}h = e^{iγt}h` on the line. Frobenius at `p` is `T_{log p}`, the Lefschetz-trace reading of the
closed orbit of length `log p`.

**Corollary H (`PROVED` modulo Hardy's theorem, `CITED`).** No prime-local operator carrier exists. The same holds for
countable direct sums of such carriers, unless there is at least one summand per critical zero, i.e. installation
(Prop R of round 2).
*Proof.* ζ has infinitely many zeros on the critical line (`CITED`: Hardy, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 158 (1914)). Two of
them, `γ_1 ≠ γ_2 > 0`, suffice, and for these the rigorous classical verification (Gram 1903, Backlund 1914) also
suffices. In case (a), Theorem I gives `γ_1, γ_2 ∈ Γ_u`, so `|Γ_u| ≥ 2`, a contradiction. In case (b), Theorem E\* with
Lemma P\*(a) or (b)+(c) gives `γ_1, γ_2 ∈ Γ_pp`, a contradiction. For direct sums, each summand carries at most one
`γ` (Theorem I / E\* per summand; the unitary part and the spectral measure respect the orthogonal decomposition), so
infinitely many zeros need infinitely many summands, one each. ∎

**Remarks.**
1. *Unconditional.* The exclusion uses no hypothesis on the off-line zeros. It is not RH-strength and not an
  equivalence. Round 2's "zeros can only be installed" is thus sharpened to "a single prime-local carrier cannot even
  hold two zeros".
2. *Falsifiers.* The corollary equally excludes carriers for `Ξ_Q`, for modified combs, and for Beurling systems with
  two or more critical zeros. It proves no false positivity for `Ξ_a`, since it proves no positivity. Consistent with
  OT-4 read negatively.
3. *Consistency with `thm:finZ`.* `thm:finZ` says no finite zero set carries a certificate. Corollary H says a
  prime-local carrier cannot carry more than one zero. The two are independent, and the second concerns objects,
  not certificates.

## §5 Tests on the objects the panel named (N-e1 outside the round-2 class)

### 5.1 Bost–Connes (`PROVED`; structure `CITED`: Bost–Connes, Selecta Math. 1 (1995))
`H = ℓ²(N^×)`, `μ_nε_m = ε_{nm}`. The map `ε_m ↦ ⊗_p δ_{v_p(m)}` is a unitary isomorphism
`ℓ²(N^×) ≅ ⊗'_p(ℓ²(N), δ_0)`, under which `μ_p` is the unilateral shift `S` on the `p`-th factor. So the family is
**Euler-factorizable isometric**, inside Setting 3.1. `(ℓ²(N))_u = 0` for `S`, since `‖S^{*k}y‖ → 0` (Wold). Hence
`H_u = 0` and `Γ_u = ∅`.

Moreover **no C₀ semigroup extends it**: there is no C₀ contraction semigroup `T` with `T_{log n} = μ_n`. Suppose there
were. For `n = m+1`, `‖μ_nε_1 − μ_mε_1‖ = ‖T_{log m}(T_{log(n/m)}ε_1 − ε_1)‖ ≤ ‖T_{log(1+1/m)}ε_1 − ε_1‖ → 0`, yet the
left side is `‖ε_n − ε_m‖ = √2`. So BC fails N-d. Its time evolution `e^{itH}`, with `Hε_n = log n·ε_n`, is a different
flow, covariant rather than suspending (`σ_t(μ_n) = n^{it}μ_n`), with point spectrum `log N^×`, i.e. the primes, and it
is zero-blind (R157_002 [A23]).

### 5.2 Borger's ghost Frobenius on a weighted ℓ² (`PROVED`)
On `ℓ²_σ(N^×)`, with `‖a‖² = Σ|a_m|²m^{−2σ}` (`σ ∈ R`), put `(ψ_p a)_m := a_{pm}`. These are the Frobenius lifts in ghost
coordinates of the big Witt vectors (Borger 0906.3146; see G1_B5_R1/R2). One computes
`‖ψ_pa‖² = p^{2σ}Σ_{p|k}|a_k|²k^{−2σ}`, so `F_p := p^{−σ}ψ_p` is a contraction. The weight is multiplicative, so
`ℓ²_σ ≅ ⊗'_p(ℓ²(N; p^{−2σk}), δ_0)` with `F_p` acting on factor `p` alone: **Euler-factorizable contractive**.
* Unitary part: on factor `p`, `‖f^k y‖² = Σ_{m≥k}|y_m|²p^{−2σm} → 0`, so `(H_p)_u = 0` and `H_u = 0`.
* Point spectrum: `a^{(s)}_m := m^{−s}` satisfies `F_n a^{(s)} = n^{−s−σ}a^{(s)}` and lies in `ℓ²_σ` iff
  `Re s > ½ − σ`. So **every** `s` in an open half-plane is a joint eigencharacter, and each eigenvector is an Euler
  product `⊗_p(p^{−ks})_k`. The moduli are `n^{−Re s−σ} < n^{−1/2}`, never unimodular, as Theorem I requires.

Reading (`HEURISTIC`): the ghost Frobenius knows every `s` equally. Selecting the zeros would need a global condition,
and the prime-local structure supplies none. This is Prop R in the contractive class.

### 5.3 Hecke operators, finite and adelic level (`PROVED` from Theorem E\*; automorphic inputs `CITED`)
Normalised Hecke operators `T_p/√p` on a space of weight-2 forms are commuting self-adjoint operators (Petersson) and
are factorised by Flath on each `π_f`. For the cos shape with `f_p = id`, Theorem E\* gives: each tail-factorizable
summand has at most one `γ ≥ 0` with `a_p(π)/√p = 2cos(γ log p)` for all `p`. For cuspidal `π` there is **none**. Equality
at almost all `p` would give `π` the Satake parameters of the isobaric `|·|^{iγ} ⊞ |·|^{−iγ}`, and strong multiplicity
one for isobaric representations (`CITED`: Jacquet–Shalika, Amer. J. Math. 103 (1981)) would make `π` Eisenstein. So
Hecke positivity controls Satake parameters (Deligne), never ζ-zeros. This matches round 2 Remark (c), now as a
theorem.

### 5.4 Connes' `L²(C_Q)` (`PROVED` given the `CITED` structure `C_Q ≅ R_+^* × Ẑ^*`, Tate/Artin–Tate)
The Frobenius at `p` is the class of the uniformiser idèle `π_p`. Modulo the principal idèle `p`, it is
`(p^{−1} at ∞; p^{−1} at ℓ ≠ p; 1 at p) ∈ R_+^* × Ẑ^*`. Decompose `L²(Ẑ^*) = ⊕_χ Cχ` over the (countably many)
Dirichlet characters. Then `L²(C_Q) = ⊕_χ L²(R_+^*) ⊗ χ`. On the `χ`-summand, `u(n) = ψ_χ(n)·D_{log n}`, where `D` is the
dilation group (Lebesgue spectrum, by Mellin transform) and `ψ_χ(p) := χ(e_p)` is a character of `Q^×_{>0}` with values
in the roots of unity of order dividing `ord χ`.
* If `ψ_χ · ι(γ) = ι(γ′)` for some `γ, γ′`, then `n^{ik(γ′−γ)} = 1` for all `n`, where `k = ord χ`. So `γ = γ′`
  (Lemma P\*(a)) and `ψ_χ = 1`. For `χ ≠ 1` of conductor `f`, pick a prime `p ≡ a (mod f)` with `χ(a) ≠ 1` (Dirichlet);
  then `ψ_χ(p) = χ(p)^{−1} ≠ 1`. Hence **`V_c` is exactly the trivial-character summand** (Theorem S). Its spectrum is
  Lebesgue, and `Γ_pp(L²(C_Q)) = ∅` unconditionally.
* So on Connes' carrier the suspended subspace holds no zero. The zeros appear only in the **cokernel** of
  `E: L²_δ(A)_0 → L²_δ(C_Q)`, the absorption spectrum (`CITED`: Connes, Selecta Math. 5 (1999), §§I–II). That cokernel is
  a quotient carrier. There the filter is N-e2 (G1_B6_R2 Theorem A/B, panel-confirmed inside Def B.1), not N-e1.
  Positivity on the cokernel is the Weil distribution itself (R158_002/004, EQUIV).

### 5.5 Connes–Consani `Ψ(λ)` (`HEURISTIC` for the transfer; the statements it transfers are `PROVED`)
On the arithmetic site, `Ψ(λ)` for `λ ∈ Q_{>0}` composes exactly (CC 1405.4527, Thm 4.3; R158_001 table). Any Hilbert
realisation of the rational `Ψ(n)` by contractions, or by normal operators, that is prime-local in the sense of Setting
3.1/2.2 falls under Theorem I/E\*, hence under Corollary H. The `Id_ε` defects occur only for irrational pairs with
rational product, i.e. in the flow direction. So the only freedom they add is that the flow is not the suspension of
the rational family. That is exactly escape (E-orb) of §6. No Hilbert realisation of the square exists on record (OT-1,
R158_001).

## §6 The Deninger shape: what remains, stated precisely

Rounds 1–3 prove the following trichotomy for a Deninger-type carrier whose Frobenius is realised by operators on the
positive space (each clause `PROVED` in its stated class):

| where the zeros sit | proved filter | status of the escape |
|---|---|---|
| eigenvectors in the positive space, Frobenius prime-local (restricted tensor over finite blocks), contractive or normal | **excluded unconditionally** (Cor H) | closed |
| same, Frobenius **not** prime-local (N-e1) | Prop X: every Künneth half must carry all zeros; Theorem E\*: no splitting into infinitely many finite blocks | **OPEN**. No construction on record is non-prime-local except through global quotients (`C_Q`), where Prop 5.4 shows the subspace holds no point spectrum |
| a quotient / cokernel (`H/V̄`) | N-e2 (B6 R2): super-critical resolution; P-HS false for Def B.1 carriers | OPEN outside Def B.1; Connes' cokernel = Weil positivity (EQUIV) |
| Frobenius is **not an operator** at all: primes only as closed orbits of the flow (Deninger's actual axioms) | none of Theorems S/E/E\*/I applies. C5 (non-manifold, dense length group), Lemma C (leaves transverse to the flow), Prop G (for `C_Q`, no transverse room) | **OPEN** = escape (E-orb) |

**N-e1 (final form; `PROVED` as the contrapositive of Theorem I/E\*, in the operator class).** In any carrier in which
Frobenius acts on the positive space by contractions or normal operators and two or more critical zeros are joint
eigenvectors with their local shape, the positive space admits **no** decomposition
`H_0 ⊗ ⊗'_j(H_j, ξ_j)` over infinitely many finite blocks of primes that the Frobenius family respects. Equivalently:
**no Künneth formula over primes.**

**Caveat (`PROVED`, and important for the reading).** N-e1 constrains the **construction**, not the abstract
representation. Every unitary representation of `Q^×_{>0}` is a direct integral of characters. The representation
`⊕_ρ C_{γ_ρ}` has all the zeros and is not tail-factorizable (Theorem E\*), so it satisfies N-e1 trivially, as an
installed direct sum (Prop R). N-e1 excludes carriers **built** as products of prime-local pieces (functions or forms on
restricted products `∏′_p X_p` with product measure, Shimura laminations, BC, Witt/ghost spaces). It does not select the
zeros, and it cannot. The selection must come from a global structure (a quotient by a global group, or the topology of
a connected foliated space). Among the constructions on record, only Connes–Tate have such a structure, and there the
selection is the Weil distribution (EQUIV).

**(E-orb), stated as a problem (`OPEN`).** Construct a foliated space `S` with a flow `φ^t`, leaves of dimension
`≥ 2` transverse to the flow (Lemma C), and a leafwise-`L²` positive form (N-a) satisfying `‖φ^{t*}h‖² = e^t‖h‖²` on
reduced `H¹`. Its closed orbits must have lengths `k log p`, and its fixed-point structure at `∞` must meet OT-12
(Leichtnam §4.4). And it must be such that **no family of operators on `H¹` with `C_p = e^{−(log p)/2}φ^{(log p)*}` is
prime-local**. By Corollary H, any construction of `S` as a suspension over, or a product of, prime-local transversals
fails. By Prop G, `S` cannot be built from `C_Q` for `K = Q`. I have no candidate. Honest status: **dead end for the
constructive part of this angle, with the wall now unconditional.**

## §8 What is new, and the weakest step

**New (to the corpus; literature status noted).**
1. **Theorem E\*** (abstract point-spectrum wall for normal prime-local families with any eventually separating
  shape), with Lemma P\*(b) (the Hecke shape separates up to two primes) and **Prop X** (the intersection law). This is
  elementary, Kakutani-type. I claim no novelty in the literature for the technique.
2. **Lemmas U1–U2 + Theorem I**: the joint unitary part of a prime-local contractive family is prime-local, and on-axis
  eigenvectors live there. This answers the panel's dilation question negatively for dilations (Parrott) and
  positively for the wall, which needs no dilation. Each ingredient is classical (Sz.-Nagy–Foiaş unitary part, von
  Neumann ITPS); the assembly and its use as a filter are new to the corpus.
3. **Corollary H**: the Deninger-suspension / prime-local wall becomes an **unconditional exclusion** via Hardy's
  theorem (two critical zeros suffice).
4. §5: BC (Euler-factorizable, `H_u = 0`, no extending flow); ghost Frobenius (a continuum of off-axis Euler-product
  eigencharacters, no selection); Hecke (no cuspidal `π` carries a ζ-zero shape, via strong multiplicity one);
  `L²(C_Q)` (`V_c` = trivial-character summand, Lebesgue spectrum).
5. The repairs E5/E6 and the renaming N-e → N-e1.

**Not claimed.** No object for ζ, no trace formula with zeros, no sign at any window, no progress on the "for all `T`"
arrow. Nothing here approaches RH. Every result is an exclusion. The Deninger-angle constructive programme is reported
as a **dead end** on everything the operator class can instantiate. The one open escape (E-orb) has no candidate.

**Weakest step.**
* **Scope of Definition 4.1.** Cor H needs Frobenius to act by **operators** on the positive space that are
  **contractive or normal**. It does not cover merely bounded (non-contractive, non-normal) Frobenius, for instance a
  P-HI / Pontryagin-space form, or a uniformly bounded but non-contractive semigroup, where Lemma U0 fails; Sz.-Nagy's
  similarity theorem for bounded groups may destroy the tensor structure. It does not cover zeros realised as
  resonances or quotient spectrum (the domain of N-e2), or Frobenius realised only as closed orbits (E-orb).
* **Normalisation.** Cor H identifies Frobenius at `p` on `H¹` with the normalised flow at time `log p` (case (a)), or
  fixes an injective Hecke-type normalisation (case (b)). A carrier whose Frobenius–flow relation is some other function
  of `p^{iγ}` is covered only if that function is injective per prime (Lemma P\*(c)).
* **`CITED` inputs** not re-proved: von Neumann's ITPS equivalence criterion; the joint spectral theorem; Hardy 1914;
  Jacquet–Shalika (only §5.3); the structure of `C_Q` (only §5.4); Lax–Phillips model spaces (only Remark I.1).

**Suggested next step.** Close (E-orb) or state it as the residual of the Deninger angle. The concrete
test is whether Deninger's `W_rat` system (1807.06400), after passing to any `L²` model of its leafwise cohomology,
admits operators `C_p = T_{log p}` that are prime-local. If they are, Cor H excludes it. If they are not, it is the only
object on record that meets N-e1, and it would be the one to push toward OT-5′.
