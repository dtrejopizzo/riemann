## Verdict, stated first

**As a G2 mechanism: dead end, reported honestly for the fourth time.** Nothing in this memo gives a lower bound for
`λ^A_T(Λ)` or `λ^W_T(Λ)` at any `T > log 2`.

The round-4 panel ruled G2_B7_R4 (Theorem P, one-generator rigidity) correct mathematics but EXCLUDED as a mechanism.
P2's reason was that the decisive input, Li–Radziwiłł, is zero-side. P3 and P4 said it is a rigidity statement on the
fibre over Λ. R4 left one question OPEN (§5 there): perturbations with two or more generators. The reason given was
that Kronecker's approximate periods sit at heights where the zeros are dense.

This round has **one new idea**. **Do not move the positivity region along a period. Average the spectral inequality
along exact periods, for every integer `m` at once.** The Cesàro mean along `τ ↦ τ + j·2π/log m` does two things:
* it kills every frequency `log n` of the comb except the powers of `m`. The reason is exact arithmetic:
  `log n / log m ∈ ℤ ⟺ n = m^k`;
* its cost is the **mean density of the zeros that the perturbation actually charges**, not the density
  `(2π)^{−1}log t` of all zeros.

So the obstruction of R4 Remark 5.1 disappears, and no zero-side theorem is used.

* **Lemma C (Cesàro progression projection; PROVED).** If `|β_b|` is tempered, then for every `m ≥ 2` the Cesàro mean
  of `D_b(· + jω_m)` over `|j| ≤ J` converges in `𝒮′` to `D_{b·1_{M_m}}`, where `ω_m = 2π/log m` and
  `M_m = {m^k : k ≥ 1}`.
* **Theorem M (density rigidity; PROVED, unconditional, both forms).** Let `|β_b|` be tempered and `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W` (or
  `𝒮^A`). Then the positive part `D_b^+` of the measure `D_b` is atomic and carried by `Γ_ζ`. Its upper mean density
  `d⁺(b) := limsup_X D_b^+([−X,X])/2X` satisfies
  ```
  |b_n| ≤ 2 d⁺(b) √n for every n ≥ 2.
  ```
  In particular, if `d⁺(b) = 0` then `b = 0`.
* **Corollary M0 (a class defined on the prime side; PROVED, unconditional, both forms).** If
  `Σ_{n≤x}|b_n| n^{−1/2} = o(log x)` and `b ≠ 0`, then `Λ + b ∉ 𝒮^W ∪ 𝒮^A`. This covers perturbations for which
  `D_b` is not a measure a priori, for example lacunary ones, where Theorem R′ of G2_B9_R3 does not apply.
* **Corollary M1 (the fibre over Λ in the round-4 open class; PROVED).** Every ∀T-positive `a ∈ Λ + 𝒯_abs` other than
  `Λ` has a spectral perturbation `D_{a−Λ}` with positive atoms on a set of ordinates of positive upper mean density.
  Together with Theorem P (R4), the R4 multi-generator question is reduced to the positive-density case. That case
  stays **OPEN**.
* **Proposition S′ (sharpness; PROVED under RH).** The Prop S construction of R4 shows that `d⁺ > 0` is exactly the
  boundary. A hypothetical progression of zeros would give a nonzero admissible `b` with `d⁺ = 2c` and
  `max|b_{m^k}|m^{−k/2} = 2c`, within a factor 2 of the Theorem M bound.

**Status: partial new result** (Lemma C, Theorem M, Corollaries M0 and M1). It is rigidity on the fibre over Λ, and it is
**not** a mechanism. Unlike Theorem P, its positivity transfer uses only prime-side arithmetic and no zero-side theorem.

## 1. Setting (record conventions, as in G2_B7_R4 §1 and G2_B9_R2 §1–§2)

* **Combs and the Weil distribution.** A comb is a real function `a` on `ℕ_{≥2}`, with

* **Fourier convention.** `φ̂(τ) = ∫φ(u)e^{iτu}du`, and for distributions `⟨T̂, ψ⟩ = ⟨T, ψ̂⟩`.
* **Perturbations.** For a perturbation `b`: `β_b := Σ_n b_n n^{−1/2}(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n})`, `D_b := β̂_b`, and

* **Zeros.** `Γ_ζ` is the set of ordinates of zeros on the critical line (closed, discrete, symmetric, `0 ∉ Γ_ζ`), and
  `μ_Λ := Σ_γ m_γ δ_γ`.
* **Classes.**

  * `𝒯_abs := {b : |β_b| ∈ 𝒮′}`, which by Lemma 2.1 of R4 applied to `|β_b|` is the same as `B_b(X) := |β_b|([−X,X])`

**Lemma 1.1 (spectral inequality; PROVED, both forms; this is R4 Theorem P steps 1–2 and (ii) steps 1–4).** Let
`b ∈ 𝒯` and `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W`, or `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^A`. Then:
* RH holds;
* `D_b = 2π(μ_Λ − ν)` for a tempered positive measure `ν` (for `𝒮^A`, take `ν := ν^P`);
* hence `D_b` is a real Radon measure.

*Proof.* Complete form: (N), (B), (Z). Primitive form:
* by RH, `u_Λ` is tempered (Lemma L, converse);
* since `β_b ∈ 𝒮′`, `u_{Λ+b}` is tempered, so `(τ²+¼)² û_{Λ+b} = 2π(τ²+¼)²ν^P`;
* `(τ²+¼)^{−2} ∈ 𝒪_M` then gives `û_{Λ+b} = 2πν^P`.

In both cases `D_b = û_Λ − û_{Λ+b}`. ∎

**Lemma 1.2 (the positive part lives on the zeros; PROVED).** In the situation of Lemma 1.1, let
`D_b = D_b^+ − D_b^−` be the Jordan decomposition. Then `D_b^+ = Σ_{γ∈Γ_ζ} a_γ δ_γ` with
`0 ≤ a_γ = max(D_b({γ}), 0) ≤ 2π m_γ`.

*Proof.* `μ_Λ` is carried by the countable set `Γ_ζ`. So for every Borel `E ⊂ ℝ∖Γ_ζ`, `D_b(E) = −2πν(E) ≤ 0`. By the
Hahn decomposition, `D_b^+(E) = sup{D_b(E′) : E′ ⊂ E} ≤ 0`, so `D_b^+(ℝ∖Γ_ζ) = 0`. A positive measure carried by a
countable set is atomic. At `γ`, `D_b({γ}) = 2π(m_γ − ν({γ})) ≤ 2πm_γ`, and `D_b^+({γ}) = max(D_b({γ}), 0)`. ∎

## 2. Lemma C: the Cesàro progression projection

**Lemma C (PROVED; no hypothesis on ζ).** Let `b ∈ 𝒯_abs`, `m ≥ 2` and `ω = ω_m`. Put `b^{(m)} := b·1_{M_m}`. Then for
every `ψ ∈ 𝒮(ℝ)`:
```
lim_{J→∞} (2J+1)^{−1} Σ_{|j|≤J} ⟨D_b, ψ(· − jω)⟩ = ⟨D_{b^{(m)}}, ψ⟩ .
```
`D_{b^{(m)}}` is `ω`-periodic, has zero mean, and has Fourier series
`Σ_{k≥1} b_{m^k} m^{−k/2}(e^{ikτ log m} + e^{−ikτ log m})`.

*Proof.*
1. The Fourier transform of a translate is `(ψ(·−jω))^(u) = e^{ijωu} ψ̂(u)`. Since `β_b` is a measure with `|β_b|`
  tempered and `ψ̂ ∈ 𝒮`, the sum
  ```
  ⟨D_b, ψ(·−jω)⟩ = Σ_{n≥2} b_n n^{−1/2}[ e^{ijω log n} ψ̂(log n) + e^{−ijω log n} ψ̂(−log n) ]
  ```
  converges absolutely, uniformly in `j`. It is dominated by `Σ|b_n|n^{−1/2}(|ψ̂(log n)| + |ψ̂(−log n)|) < ∞`.
2. Put `θ_n := ω log n = 2π log n / log m`. The Cesàro means `(2J+1)^{−1} Σ_{|j|≤J} e^{±ijθ_n}` are bounded by 1. They
  tend to 1 if `θ_n ∈ 2πℤ` and to 0 otherwise. Moreover `θ_n ∈ 2πℤ ⟺ log n / log m = k ∈ ℤ ⟺ n = m^k`, with `k ≥ 1`
  because `n ≥ 2`. This is the only arithmetic used: exact integrality, with no Diophantine approximation.
3. By dominated convergence, the limit is `Σ_{k≥1} b_{m^k} m^{−k/2}[ψ̂(k log m) + ψ̂(−k log m)] = ⟨β_{b^{(m)}}, ψ̂⟩`.
4. `β_{b^{(m)}}` is carried by `(log m)(ℤ∖{0})`. So `e^{iωu}β_{b^{(m)}} = β_{b^{(m)}}`, which says `D_{b^{(m)}}` is
  `ω`-periodic. It has no atom at `u = 0`, which says the mean is zero.
5. The Fourier series is Poisson/Fourier bookkeeping, with `2π/ω = log m`. ∎

*Remark 2.1 (why this is not the R4 Remark 5.1 failure).*
* R4 tried to **transport** the region where `−D_b ≥ 0` along approximate periods. That needs whole windows free of
  zeros at large height, and Littlewood's gap bound rules them out.
* Lemma C instead **averages** the inequality `D_b ≤ D_b^+` along exact periods of each single integer `m`. The mixing
  between generators (the Kronecker part) is done by the Cesàro mean, exactly, through step 2.
* The error term is the average of `D_b^+`. That is a quantity attached to the perturbation, bounded by its mean
  density (§3). The density of `Γ_ζ` never enters.

## 3. Theorem M: density rigidity

**Theorem M (PROVED; unconditional; complete and primitive forms).** Let `b ∈ 𝒯_abs` and suppose `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W` or
`Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^A`. Let `D_b^+` be as in Lemma 1.2 and put
```
d⁺(b) := limsup_{X→∞} D_b^+([−X, X]) / (2X) ∈ [0, ∞] .
```
Then for every `m ≥ 2`:
* the periodic measure `D_{b^{(m)}}` has total variation at most `2ω_m d⁺(b)` per period;
* consequently `|b_{m^k}| ≤ 2 d⁺(b) m^{k/2}` for all `k ≥ 1`;
* in particular `|b_n| ≤ 2 d⁺(b) √n` for every `n ≥ 2`, and `d⁺(b) = 0 ⟹ b = 0`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1.1, RH holds, `D_b` is a Radon measure, and Lemma 1.2 applies. Assume `d⁺ := d⁺(b) < ∞`, since
otherwise there is nothing to prove. Fix `m`, write `ω = ω_m`, and fix `a ∈ ℝ`.

1. *Bounding the positive part per period.* Let `h ∈ C_c^∞((a, a+ω))` with `0 ≤ h ≤ 1`.
  * The translates `h(·−jω)` have disjoint supports, so `H_J := Σ_{|j|≤J} h(·−jω)` satisfies `0 ≤ H_J ≤ 1` and
  `supp H_J ⊂ [−X_J, X_J]`, with `X_J := (J+1)ω + |a|`.
  * Since `D_b ≤ D_b^+` as measures and `h ≥ 0`:
  ```
  Σ_{|j|≤J} ⟨D_b, h(·−jω)⟩ ≤ ∫ H_J dD_b^+ ≤ D_b^+([−X_J, X_J]) .
  ```
  * Divide by `2J+1` and let `J → ∞`. Lemma C (with `h ∈ 𝒮`) gives the left side. On the right,
  `2X_J/(2J+1) → ω` and `D_b^+([−X_J,X_J])/(2X_J) ≤ d⁺ + o(1)`. Therefore `⟨D_{b^{(m)}}, h⟩ ≤ ω d⁺`.
  * Once `D_{b^{(m)}}` is known to be a measure (step 2), taking the supremum over such `h` gives
  `D_{b^{(m)}}^+((a, a+ω)) ≤ ωd⁺`.
2. *Why `D_{b^{(m)}}` is a measure.* Fix a compact interval `K`, and cover it by finitely many (`N_K`) open intervals
  of length `< ω`, with a subordinate smooth partition of unity. Step 1 applies to each piece: every
  `h ∈ C_c^∞(int K)` with `0 ≤ h ≤ 1` satisfies `⟨D_{b^{(m)}}, h⟩ ≤ N_K ωd⁺`.
  * Pick `φ_K ∈ C_c^∞` with `0 ≤ φ_K ≤ 1` and `φ_K = 1` on `K`. Let `A_J := (2J+1)^{−1}Σ_{|j|≤J} D_b(·+jω)`. These are
  Radon measures, and by Lemma C `A_J → D_{b^{(m)}}` in `𝒮′`.
  * Each `A_J` splits as `A_J^+ − A_J^−`, where `A_J^± := (2J+1)^{−1}Σ_j D_b^±(·+jω)` are positive measures. The
  step-1 computation, with `supp φ_K` in place of one period, gives `⟨A_J^+, φ_K⟩ ≤ (N′_K+1)ωd⁺ + o(1)`.
  * Test `A_J^− = A_J^+ − A_J` against `φ_K ≥ 0`:
  `A_J^−(K) ≤ ⟨A_J^−, φ_K⟩ = ⟨A_J^+, φ_K⟩ − ⟨A_J, φ_K⟩`, which is bounded uniformly in `J`, because `⟨A_J, φ_K⟩`
  converges.
  * So `(A_J)` is locally uniformly bounded in total variation. Its `𝒮′`-limit `D_{b^{(m)}}` is therefore a Radon
  measure (weak-∗ compactness, Banach–Alaoglu on `C_c(int K)′`). Moreover `D_{b^{(m)}} ≤ lim inf A_J^+` on
  `h ≥ 0`, so its positive part obeys the bound of step 1.
3. *Mean zero forces the negative part down.* Choose `a` with no atom of `D_{b^{(m)}}` at `a`; the atoms are countable.
  By Lemma C the mean is zero, so `D_{b^{(m)}}([a, a+ω)) = 0`, and `D_{b^{(m)}}^−([a,a+ω)) = D_{b^{(m)}}^+([a,a+ω)) ≤ ωd⁺`.
  Hence `|D_{b^{(m)}}|([a,a+ω)) ≤ 2ωd⁺`.
  * The zero-mean identity for the measure is the pairing with a periodic partition `χ`, `Σ_jχ(·+jω) = 1`, as in R4
  Lemma 2.2(2): `⟨D_{b^{(m)}}, χ⟩ = ω·(0th coefficient) = 0`. For a measure, `⟨μ, χ⟩ = μ(one period)` by periodicity.
4. *Coefficient bound.* For an `ω`-periodic measure `μ`, the Fourier coefficient is
  `c_k = ω^{−1}∫_{[a,a+ω)} e^{−ikτ log m} dμ(τ)`, so `|c_k| ≤ ω^{−1}|μ|([a,a+ω)) ≤ 2d⁺`. By Lemma C,
  `c_{±k} = b_{m^k}m^{−k/2}`. So `|b_{m^k}| ≤ 2d⁺m^{k/2}`, and `m = n`, `k = 1` gives `|b_n| ≤ 2d⁺√n`. ∎

*Remark 3.1 (a cleaner route for step 2).* Step 2 can be avoided. With `d⁺ = 0`, step 1 gives `⟨D_{b^{(m)}}, h⟩ ≤ 0`
for every `h ≥ 0` in `C_c^∞` (partition of unity), and the periodic mean-zero lemma (R4 Lemma 2.2(2)) gives
`D_{b^{(m)}} = 0` at once. The quantitative bound for `d⁺ > 0` is the only place step 2 is needed. It is standard: a
distribution bounded above by a constant multiple of Lebesgue measure on test functions `0 ≤ h ≤ 1` of small support is
a measure.

**Corollary M0 (PROVED; unconditional; both forms).** Let `b ≠ 0` with `B_b(X) = |β_b|([−X,X]) = o(X)`, i.e.
`Σ_{n≤x}|b_n|n^{−1/2} = o(log x)`. Then `Λ + b ∉ 𝒮^W` and `Λ + b ∉ 𝒮^A`.

*Proof.* `B_b(X) = o(X)` implies `b ∈ 𝒯_abs`. Suppose `Λ + b` is ∀T-positive. By Lemma 1.1, `D_b` is a Radon measure.
Fix `τ_0` and `φ ∈ C_c^∞(−1,1)` with `0 ≤ φ ≤ 1` and `φ(0) = 1`, and put `ψ_R := φ(R(· − τ_0))`.
1. Dominated convergence, `|D_b|` being locally finite, gives `⟨D_b, ψ_R⟩ → D_b({τ_0})`.
2. `ψ̂_R(u) = R^{−1} e^{iuτ_0} φ̂(u/R)`, with `|φ̂(v)| ≤ C(1+|v|)^{−3}`. So
  `|⟨D_b, ψ_R⟩| ≤ R^{−1}∫|φ̂(u/R)| d|β_b| ≤ C R^{−1}Σ_{ℓ≥0}(1+ℓ)^{−3} B_b((ℓ+1)R)`.
3. Given `ε > 0`, write `B_b(Y) ≤ εY + C_ε`. The bound becomes `≤ C Σ_ℓ(1+ℓ)^{−3}(ε(ℓ+1) + C_ε/R) → Cε′`.

Hence `D_b` has no atoms. By Lemma 1.2, `D_b^+ = 0`, so `d⁺(b) = 0`, and Theorem M gives `b = 0`. ∎

*Example (outside Theorem R′).* Take `b_n := √n` for `n ∈ {2^{2^k} : k ≥ 1}` and `b_n = 0` otherwise. Then
`Σ_{n≤x}|b_n|n^{−1/2} ≍ log log x = o(log x)`, so M0 applies.
* `D_b = Σ_k 2cos(τ 2^k log 2)` is a lacunary series with coefficients 1. By Zygmund's theorem on lacunary
  Fourier–Stieltjes series, it is not a locally finite measure (CITED, Zygmund, *Trigonometric Series*, Vol. I, Ch. V,
  in the periodic form; here it is applied along the Bohr-almost-periodic extension, and only as illustration).
* So `b ∉ 𝒯_meas`, and Theorem R′ of G2_B9_R3 is silent.
* The example is illustrative. M0 itself does not depend on it.

**Corollary M1 (the fibre over Λ; PROVED; both forms).** Let `a ∈ Λ + 𝒯_abs` be ∀T-positive in either form. Then RH
holds. Also, either `a = Λ`, or the ordinates charged by `D_{a−Λ}`, i.e.
`Γ_b := {γ ∈ Γ_ζ : D_b({γ}) > 0}` with `b = a − Λ`, satisfy the following. Counted with multiplicity, they number at
least `(d⁺(b)/π − o(1))X` in `[−X, X]` for infinitely many `X`, with `d⁺(b) ≥ sup_n|b_n|/(2√n) > 0`.

*Proof.* Theorem M gives `d⁺ ≥ sup|b_n|/(2√n)`. Lemma 1.2 gives `a_γ ≤ 2πm_γ`, so
`D_b^+([−X,X]) ≤ 2π Σ_{γ∈Γ_b∩[−X,X]} m_γ`. ∎

*Reading (HEURISTIC).* The number of zeros up to `X` is `≍ X log X`, so Corollary M1 asks only for a thin set of
charged ordinates, a proportion `≫ 1/log X`. It is still a positive-density *crystalline coupling*: the Bohr spectrum of
`b`, which is computable from prime data, must sit on `≫ X` ordinates. Along one generator, R4 Theorem P with (LR) kills
this coupling as well. With two or more generators, the positive-density coupling is the entire remaining case.

**Proposition S′ (sharpness; PROVED under RH).** Suppose `τ_0 + ω_mℤ ⊂ Γ_ζ`, which is false by (LR); this is used only
to calibrate. Then the R4 Prop S perturbation `b_{m^k} = 2c cos(kφ) m^{k/2}` with `2cω ≤ 2π` lies in `𝒮^W`. It has
`D_b^+ = cω·(atoms on ±τ_0 + ω_mℤ)`, so `d⁺ = 2c` (it is `c` if the two progressions coincide) and
`sup_k|b_{m^k}|m^{−k/2} = 2c`. So `d⁺ > 0` is the exact threshold of Theorem M, and the constant 2 there is at most a
factor 2 from optimal.

*Proof.* The Prop S computation of R4 (panel-verified). In `[−X,X]` there are `≈ 2X/ω` atoms on each progression, each
of mass `cω`, so `D_b^+([−X,X]) ≈ 4cX`. ∎

The residual OPEN case asks this. Under RH, is there a positive atomic measure `α` on `Γ_ζ`, with `α({γ}) ≤ 2πm_γ` and
positive upper density, and a positive measure `σ`, such that `α − σ` has Fourier support in

## 5. Why the same averaging gives nothing for Λ itself (the arrow)

**Proposition 5.1 (PROVED under RH; unconditional part: Landau).** Apply Lemma C's averaging to `û_Λ = 2πμ_Λ` in
place of `D_b`. For `h ≥ 0` in `C_c^∞` with `∫h > 0`,
`(2J+1)^{−1}Σ_{|j|≤J}⟨μ_Λ, h(·−jω)⟩ = (2π)^{−1}(log J)∫h + O_h(1) → +∞`. The divergent part is the archimedean density
`(2π)^{−1}log t`. The `O(1)` part is the progression distribution of Ford–Zaharescu, which is governed by `Λ` at
`e^{2π/ω}` (CITED; see §0).

*Proof sketch of the divergence (PROVED from Riemann–von Mangoldt).* `N(t+1) − N(t) = (2π)^{−1}log t + O(log t)` on
average, and more precisely `N(t) = (t/2π)log(t/2πe) + O(log t)`. Summing `∫h(τ − jω)dμ_Λ` over `|j| ≤ J` samples
`μ_Λ` on `[−Jω, Jω]` with weight `≈ ∫h/ω` per unit length. The count is `≈ (2Jω)(2π)^{−1}log(Jω)·∫h/ω`. Divide by
`2J+1`. ∎

*Reading.*
* On a perturbation, the averaged inequality `D_b ≤ D_b^+` has a finite right side, and the mean-zero structure of
  `D_{b^{(m)}}` gives the sign.
* On Λ itself there is no perturbation to average. The quantity to be controlled is `λ_T(Λ)`, and the averages of the
  spectral side diverge like `log J`. That divergence is the archimedean density, the same one that capped dlVP (phase
  151) and that R2 Theorem J flattened on the hull.
* So the progression projection is an exact mean-value identity with a sign **only relative to Λ**: `𝒮^W` is convex,
  and Lemma C measures departures from Λ. This is the fifth time my angle lands on the fibre over Λ.
* The ∀T arrow asks for Λ's own membership. By (N), membership of **any** `a ∈ Λ + 𝒯` already implies RH. So a
  comparison mechanism ("prove positivity for a nearby comb, then compare") could not be easier than RH. By Corollary
  M1, under RH the nearby comb must couple crystalline-ly to a positive density of ordinates, which is zero-side
  knowledge. **HEURISTIC** as a statement about mechanisms; the two ingredients, (N) and Corollary M1, are PROVED.

## 7. What is genuinely new, and what is not

**New to the corpus (§0 check).**
1. **Lemma C (PROVED).** The Cesàro progression projection. Averaging along the exact period of `m` isolates the powers
  of `m` in any `𝒯_abs` comb, by integrality alone.
2. **Theorem M (PROVED, unconditional, both forms).** `|b_n| ≤ 2d⁺(b)√n` for every ∀T-positive `Λ + b`, `b ∈ 𝒯_abs`.
  Rigidity for every perturbation whose charged ordinates have mean density zero. This is new in three ways: it has no
  support restriction, it does not use (LR), and it does not require `D_b` to be a measure a priori.
3. **Corollary M0 (PROVED).** A prime-side class, `Σ_{n≤x}|b_n|n^{−½} = o(log x)`, strictly beyond `𝒯_meas` (the
  lacunary example).
4. **Corollary M1 and Proposition S′ (PROVED).** The only possible escapes couple crystalline-ly to a positive density
  of ordinates, and that threshold is sharp.
5. **§4 table.** The R4 open question is reduced to the multi-generator positive-density case (OPEN). The sub-question
  with the opposite-cone spectral constraint is isolated (OPEN).

**Not new.** Jordan and Hahn decompositions, Riesz–Schwartz, dominated convergence, Fourier series of periodic measures,
Riemann–von Mangoldt, and the CITED items: Zygmund, Ford–Zaharescu, Kurasov–Sarnak and Olevskii–Ulanovskii, all
illustrative only. Also not new: the record inputs (N), (B), (Z), R4 Lemma 2.2 and Prop S.

**Not a mechanism.** No lower bound on `λ^A_T(Λ)` or `λ^W_T(Λ)` at any `T > log 2`. The G2 arrow is untouched, and §5
