## 2. Theorem NB: the no-bootstrap trichotomy

### 2.1 What a bootstrap is

**Definition 2.1 (bootstrap scheme).** A *bootstrap scheme* `𝔅 = (𝒟, J, δ, T₀)` consists of: a set `𝒟` of real
arithmetic functions `a` with `a(1) = 0` and `Λ ∈ 𝒟`; a step `0 < δ ≤ ½log 2`; a starting point `T₀ ∈ (0, ½log 2)`;
and a functional `J(T, a|_{𝒩_T}) ∈ ℝ` defined for `T ≥ T₀`, `a ∈ 𝒟`, depending on `a` only through its restriction to
`𝒩_T` (locality), together with **proofs** of

- (i) `J(T₀, a|_{𝒩_{T₀}}) ≥ 0` for `a ∈ 𝒟` (the empty window; `𝒩_{T₀} = ∅`);
- (ii) for all `T ≥ T₀`, all `T′ ∈ (T, T+δ]`, all `a ∈ 𝒟`: `J(T, a|_{𝒩_T}) ≥ 0 ⟹ J(T′, a|_{𝒩_{T′}}) ≥ 0`;
- (iii) for all `T ≥ T₀`, all `a ∈ 𝒟`: `J(T, a|_{𝒩_T}) ≥ 0 ⟹ λ^{W,(a)}_T ≥ 0` (complete form) or `⟹ RD_T(a)` (primitive
  form).

This covers: round 1 (`J` = "a certificate of a given shape exists at `T`"), round 2 (M-Ψ) (`J` a functional of the
data propagated by `Ψ`), G2_B4's edge-profile families (`J` = "the profile `φ` certifies the window"), and any
Lyapunov-type scheme (`J` monotone along `T`). It does **not** cover statements without a `T` in them; that is the
point of (c) below. The word "proof" in (ii) matters: Theorem NB is a statement about derivations, in the sense of
P46 Thm 18.1(ii), Rem 18.1a and P47 `cor:void`, exactly as those record statements are. Its mathematical content is
small; its function is to close an angle.

### 2.2 The theorem

**Theorem NB (no-bootstrap trichotomy).** *PROVED as a statement about derivations; form-independent.* Let `𝔅` be a
bootstrap scheme. Then exactly one of the following holds.

**(a) `𝒟 ⊋ {Λ}` — the scheme is class-level, and it is false or vacuous.** For every `a ∈ 𝒟`, (i)–(iii) chain to
`λ^{W,(a)}_T ≥ 0` (resp. `RD_T(a)`) for **all** `T ≥ T₀`. Consequently:
  - (a1) `𝒟` contains no deletion `Λ𝟏_{n≥N}` (`N ≥ 5`) and no amplification `Λ + c𝟏_{n=n₀}` with `c > c_*(T, n₀)` for
  some `T ≥ T₀` with `2T > log n₀`; in particular `𝒟` is not any of the classes `𝒞_PNT(ε)`, `𝒞_zeros`, `𝒞_BT`
  of `cor:void`, nor the class `𝒞_supp` of R157_002 row 27, nor any class closed under either operation.
  *Unconditional*, by (FinMod).
  - (a2) Under RH, `𝒟 ∩ (Λ + ℓ¹_w) = {Λ}` (by (Rig)); unconditionally, `𝒟 ∩ (Λ + ℓ¹_w)` contains no `Λ + b` with
  `limsup_T λ^A_T(Λ+b) < 0`, and by G2_B9_R2 Cor R1 a derivation valid on a class containing `Λ` and one such `Λ+b`
  proves a false statement. So `𝒟` is, near `Λ` in the natural weighted `ℓ¹` topology, the singleton — the scheme
  is "class-level" only nominally, and the proof of (ii) must use the exact values of `a = Λ` on `𝒩_{T′}`.

**(b) `𝒟 = {Λ}` — the scheme is an induction in `T`, and its step is the `∀T` arrow.** Put `j(T) := J(T, Λ|_{𝒩_T})`.
Then (ii) reads `j(T) ≥ 0 ⟹ j(T′) ≥ 0` for `T′ ∈ (T, T+δ]`, and (i)–(ii) give `j ≥ 0` on `[T₀, ∞)` by finite chaining
(any `T` is reached in `⌈(T−T₀)/δ⌉` steps). Moreover:
  - (b1) *No reduction.* For each fixed `T`, (ii) at `T` together with (iii) at `T′` gives the implication
  `j(T) ≥ 0 ⟹ λ^W_{T′} ≥ 0` for all `T′ ∈ (T, T+δ]`. So the proof of the step at `T` is already a proof of positivity
  of the next window from a hypothesis at `T`; ranging over all `T ≥ T₀`, the union of the step proofs *is* a proof
  of `∀T λ^W_T ≥ 0`, i.e. of RH by (Weil). The scheme relocates the `∀T` quantifier from the conclusion into the
  hypothesis "(ii) holds for all `T`"; it does not remove it. This is `cor:mono`/W15 verbatim (P46 §21.7).
  - (b2) *The data flow adds nothing.* On `𝒟 = {Λ}` the propagated data `Ψ_{T→T′}(v|_{𝒩_T}) = v|_{𝒩_{T′}}` (Theorem Ψ1
  of round 2) is a fixed sequence; the "propagation" is the identity on `Λ`, and `J(T′, Ψv) = j(T′)`. So the round-2
  distinction between propagating certificates and propagating data is empty on the only orbit. The panel's
  observation, verbatim.
  - (b3) *Where the step's content lives.* By Prop Ψ4 (round 2, PROVED, Mellin inversion), the only `ℕ`-specific
  input of the step at `T` — the exact new contacts `Λ(n)`, `log n ∈ [2T, 2T′)`, expressed through the old ones —
  is the identity `K(s)ζ(½+s) = −ζ′(½+s)` restricted to an annulus, whose residue expansion is `thm:Z`. A step proof
  that uses the exact values is therefore a statement about `ζ` on the critical line at the window `T′`; and by
  (Weil) plus (Mono) the family of such statements over all `T′` is RH.

**(c) The escape: a `T`-free statement.** The only way out of (a) and (b) is a scheme whose "step" is not a step: a
single statement `S` with no window parameter, together with a proof `S ⟹ ∀T λ^W_T ≥ 0`. But `∀T λ^W_T ≥ 0` is RH
(Weil), and every `S` implying it is at least as strong as RH; the record's list of such `S` on the prime side is
R157_002 (rows 17, 31, 32 and the `EQUIV` column), and the one such `S` that is *known* to be equivalent and to carry
all windows is the positivity of the zero measure `Σ_ρ δ_{γ_ρ}` (E3, `thm:Z`, Bochner). **EQUIVALENT**, not
excluded — but not a mechanism: there is no positivity to extract from a `T`-free prime-side identity without first
knowing the sign (F3, `thm:O`-type: an identity gives no sign).

*Proof.* (a) Chaining (i)–(ii) along `T₀ < T₀+δ < …` reaches every `T ≥ T₀` in finitely many steps; (iii) converts
`J ≥ 0` into the window sign. (a1): if `a = Λ𝟏_{n≥N} ∈ 𝒟` with `N ≥ 5`, then `RD_T(a)` (or, since
`λ^{W,(a)}_T ≤ λ^{A,(a)}_T` by P47 Rem after `cor:mono`, `λ^{W,(a)}_T ≥ 0`) would hold at `T = 0.7 ≤ ½log 5`,
contradicting (FinMod)(i); amplification likewise with (FinMod)(ii). The named classes are closed under the operations
(P47 `thm:finmod`(iii), row 27 for `𝒞_supp`). (a2): (Rig) under RH; the unconditional clause is G2_B9_R2 Cor R1
applied to `𝒟`. (b) is bookkeeping as written; (b2) is Theorem Ψ1–Ψ2 of round 2; (b3) is Prop Ψ4 of round 2. (c) is
(Weil). The cases are exhaustive (`𝒟 = {Λ}` or not; a scheme with (ii) for all `T` versus a `T`-free statement) and
exclusive. ∎

**Remark 2.2 (Lyapunov schemes and the `T = ∞` mirror).** A monotone invariant is the special case of (b) in which
(ii) is proved by showing `j` nonincreasing (then `j(T) ≥ lim_{T→∞} j(T) =: j(∞)`, and `j(∞) ≥ 0` suffices) or
nondecreasing. With `j = λ^W_T` (nonincreasing by (Mono)), `j(∞) = inf_T λ^W_T`, which is `0` under RH by (M) and
`−∞` under ¬RH by (Weil); so "`j(∞) ≥ 0`" is Weil's criterion — the `T = ∞` endpoint of G2_B9_R2, EQUIVALENT (row 31).
For a normalised `j = λ^W_T · g(T)` with `g > 0`, monotonicity of `j` is a statement about the decay rate of `λ^W_T`
in `T`, i.e. about `−log λ^W_T ≍ e^{2T}` (Zhu's law, upper half proved in (M)); its lower half at every `T` is
`λ^W_T > 0`, the sign itself. HEURISTIC as a reading, PROVED as the tautology it is.

**Remark 2.3 (fixed points and self-similarity).** A renormalisation fixed point — a certificate profile invariant
under `T ↦ T+δ` in rescaled variables — is a fixed or convergent edge profile in the sense of G2_B4_R2, and Theorem R
there (PROVED, panel-verified) shows that every such profile fails for arbitrarily large `T`: `T ↦ f_{w_T}(τ)` is a
mean-zero Bohr almost-periodic function with spectrum `2(τ − Ord)`. So the fixed-point form of the bootstrap is
EXCLUDED, not merely equivalent. Its `T`-dependence must carry `e^{2iTγ}` at every ordinate — zero input (OT-5′
shadow, G2_B4_R2 Rem R3).

### 2.4 The quantitative tolerance of a step

**Proposition Q (tolerance).** *PROVED, conditional on (ND) at `T′`; primitive form.* Let `𝔅` be a scheme and suppose
the proof of (ii)–(iii) at `(T, T′)` is *tolerant at one new contact*: there are `n₀` with `2T ≤ log n₀ < 2T′` and
`ε₀ > 0` such that (ii) and (iii) at `T′` remain valid for every `a_ε := Λ + ε𝟏_{n=n₀}`, `|ε| ≤ ε₀`, `a_ε ≥ 0`. Then
`ε₀ ≤ ε_*(T′, n₀) = √n₀ λ^A_{T′}/(2|c_{F_⋆}(log n₀)|)`, and for `T′ ≥ T₀(ε)`,
`ε₀ ≤ e^{T′} exp(−e^{(2−ε)T′})/(2|c_{F_⋆}(log n₀)|)`.

*Proof.* Tolerance plus `J(T, a_ε|_{𝒩_T}) = J(T, Λ|_{𝒩_T}) ≥ 0` (locality: `a_ε = Λ` on `𝒩_T` since `log n₀ ≥ 2T`)
gives `RD_{T′}(a_ε)` for `|ε| ≤ ε₀`. (Res) forbids this for `ε > ε_*(T′, n₀)` with `sgn ε = sgn c_{F_⋆}(log n₀)`, so
`ε₀ ≤ ε_*`. The last inequality is (M) with `√n₀ < e^{T′}`. ∎

*Reading.* A step proof that survives a change of one new contact by more than `exp(−e^{(2−ε)T′})` (times a
non-degeneracy factor) proves a false statement. This is O3 (`rem:resweights`) transported to the step, and (M) makes
it doubly exponential. It refutes any hope of a "coarse" bootstrap in which only approximate new contacts enter. The
hypothesis (ND) — `c_{F_⋆}(log n₀) ≠ 0` — is OPEN (G2_B6_R2 Cor M3(ii)); without it the statement is `ε₀ ≤ ∞`.

## 3. Lemma H: the horizontal polynomial bound for `1/ζ`, and the repair of Theorem Ψ5(2)

The round-2 proof of Ψ5(2) moved the contour of `∫ Φ_F(s) n₀^{−½−s}/ζ(½+s) ds` across the critical strip along
horizontal segments `Im s = ±T_j`, and it cited "Titchmarsh Thm 9.7" for `|1/ζ(σ+iT_j)| = O(T_j^A)`. The panel asked
for the primary statement. Here is the situation.

**What Theorem 9.7 gives.** Titchmarsh, *The Theory of the Riemann Zeta-Function*, 2nd ed. (1986), §9.7: for large
`T` there is `T₁ ∈ [T, T+1]` with `ζ′/ζ(σ+iT₁) = O(log² T)` uniformly for `−1 ≤ σ ≤ 2` (CITED; this is the statement
used in the classical proof of the explicit formula, Davenport *Multiplicative Number Theory* Ch. 17). Integrating in
`σ` from `2` gives only `log|1/ζ(σ+iT₁)| = O(log² T)`, i.e. `|1/ζ| ≤ exp(A log² T)`. A general `F ∈ C_c^∞` has
`|Φ_F(σ+it)| = |ĉ_F|`-type decay faster than any power of `|t|` but **not** faster than `exp(−A log² t)` in general
(Paley–Wiener: `C_c^∞` allows arbitrarily slow super-polynomial decay). So with 9.7 alone, the horizontal integrals
in Ψ5(2) are not shown to vanish for general `C_c^∞` tests; they are for Gevrey tests (`|ĉ_F(t)| ≤ e^{−c|t|^{1−ε}}`).
Lemma H removes the restriction.

**Lemma H (polynomial lower bound for `|ζ|` on most horizontal lines).** *PROVED, modulo one CITED classical formula.*
There is an absolute constant `A` such that for every `T ≥ 3` the set
`G_T := {t ∈ [T, T+1] : |ζ(σ+it)| ≥ t^{−A} for all σ ∈ [−1, 2]}` has Lebesgue measure `≥ ½`. In particular there is a
sequence `T_j → ∞`, `T_j ∈ [j, j+1]`, with `|1/ζ(σ ± iT_j)| ≤ T_j^{A}` uniformly on `−1 ≤ σ ≤ 2`.

*Proof.* **Cited input (H0):** for `−1 ≤ σ ≤ 2` and `t ≥ 2`,
`ζ′/ζ(σ+it) = Σ_{ρ : |t−γ| ≤ 1} 1/(σ+it−ρ) + O(log t)`, the sum over nontrivial zeros `ρ = β+iγ` (with multiplicity)
and the `O` uniform in `σ`. This is Titchmarsh (9.6.1)/Theorem 9.6(A), also Davenport Ch. 15 eq. (after Lemma, "for
`−1 ≤ σ ≤ 2`"); it follows from the Hadamard product and Stirling. CITED; the theorem number is the one thing in this
memo I have not re-checked against the printed text, and the formula itself is standard.

Fix `t ≥ 2` and `σ ∈ [−1, 2]`, and integrate (H0) in the real direction from `σ` to `2` at fixed `t`; the zero set
`{ρ : |t−γ| ≤ 1}` does not depend on `σ`, so
`log|ζ(σ+it)| = log|ζ(2+it)| + Σ_{|t−γ|≤1} ( log|σ+it−ρ| − log|2+it−ρ| ) + O(log t)`,
the `O(log t)` being `≤ 3·` the constant of (H0). Now:
- `|ζ(2+it)| = Π_p |1−p^{−2−it}|^{−1} ≥ Π_p (1+p^{−2})^{−1} = ζ(4)/ζ(2)`, so `log|ζ(2+it)| ≥ −log(ζ(2)/ζ(4))`;
- `1 ≤ |2+it−ρ| ≤ 3` since `0 < β < 1`, `|t−γ| ≤ 1`, so `0 ≤ log|2+it−ρ| ≤ log 3`;
- `|σ+it−ρ| ≥ |t−γ|`, so `log|σ+it−ρ| ≥ −log⁺(1/|t−γ|)`, **independently of `σ`**;
- the number of zeros with `|t−γ| ≤ 1` is `N(t+1) − N(t−1) ≤ A₁ log t` (Riemann–von Mangoldt, CITED).

Hence, uniformly in `σ ∈ [−1, 2]`,
`log|1/ζ(σ+it)| ≤ A₂ log t + S(t)`, `S(t) := Σ_{|t−γ|≤1} log⁺(1/|t−γ|)`.
Average `S` over `t ∈ [T, T+1]`: every zero contributing to some `S(t)` has `γ ∈ [T−1, T+2]`, and
`∫_ℝ log⁺(1/|t−γ|) dt = ∫_{−1}^{1} log(1/|x|) dx = 2`, so
`∫_T^{T+1} S(t) dt ≤ 2 (N(T+2) − N(T−1)) ≤ A₃ log T`.
By Chebyshev's inequality the set `{t ∈ [T, T+1] : S(t) ≤ 2A₃ log T}` has measure `≥ ½`, and on it
`log|1/ζ(σ+it)| ≤ (A₂ + 2A₃ + o(1)) log t` for all `σ ∈ [−1, 2]`. Take `A := A₂ + 2A₃ + 1`. Choosing `T_j ∈ G_j` for
each `j` gives the sequence; the same argument with `t ↦ −t` (or conjugation symmetry `ζ(s̄) = ζ(s)̄`) covers
`−T_j`. ∎

**Corollary H1 (Theorem Ψ5(2) holds for all `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`).** *PROVED (given Lemma H and the round-2 argument).*
In the contour proof of Ψ5(2) replace the cited bound by Lemma H on `Im s = ±T_j`, `T_j ∈ G_j`. Inside
`−1 ≤ Re(½+s) ≤ 2` the integrand is `≤ T_j^{A} n₀^{1/2} · sup_{|Im s| = T_j}|Φ_F(s)|`, and `|Φ_F(σ+iT_j)| ≤
‖c_F^{(k)}‖₁ e^{2T|σ|}/T_j^{k}` for every `k` (integration by parts `k` times, `c_F ∈ C_c^∞` with support in
`[−2T, 2T]`); take `k = A+2`. The segments left of the strip are handled by the functional equation exactly as in
round 2 (polynomial growth of the `Γ`-factor and `|ζ(1−s′)|^{−1} ≤ ζ(2)`), and the vertical line at
`Re(½+s) = −2K−1` as in round 2. So all horizontal contributions vanish along `T_j → ∞`, and the residue expansion

## 7. What is genuinely new, and the weakest step

**New.**
1. Theorem NB: the closure of the bootstrap angle in all its forms, with the record theorem that kills each branch.
  It is a statement about derivations, of the same kind as `cor:void` and P46 Thm 18.1(ii); its value is that the
  angle need not be reopened under another name (certificate flow, data flow, Lyapunov functional, renormalisation
  fixed point, "monotone invariant").
2. Lemma H and Cor H1: the polynomial horizontal bound for `1/ζ` on a half-measure set, which makes Theorem Ψ5(2)
  unconditional for all `C_c^∞` tests. The round-2 citation was insufficient for non-Gevrey tests; this is a real
  repair, not a renumbering.
3. Cor Ψ3″: the F4 placement with the record's own falsifiers (`ζ_odd`, `ζE`), which separates "lattice" from "Euler
  product" and from "zero set" as inputs.
4. Prop Q: O3 at the level of a bootstrap step, doubly exponential under (ND).

**Not new.** Everything else; in particular the panel's observation that (M-Ψ) is W15 on a singleton orbit, which I
merely formalise.

**Weakest step.** In Theorem NB, the exhaustiveness of the trichotomy rests on Definition 2.1: a scheme is either
indexed by the window parameter with a step (cases (a)/(b)) or not (case (c)). A construction that is indexed by
some *other* parameter (a place, a modulus, a spectral cutoff) with its own step is not a bootstrap in `T` and is not
covered — it is covered by other record obstructions (`thm:SC`/`prop:finsupp` for places, `thm:finZ` for spectral
cutoffs), but not by this memo. Of the proved statements, the weakest is the citation (H0) in Lemma H (formula
standard, theorem number unconfirmed in the printed text); Lemma H's proof is otherwise self-contained.
