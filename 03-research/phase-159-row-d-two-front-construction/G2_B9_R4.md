## §2 Lemma J: the doubling (jump–bottom) inequality

**Lemma J (PROVED; both forms; unconditional; any real comb `a`).** Let `φ ∈ C_c^∞(−r, r)` be real, and put
`S_φ(x) := ⟨u_a, c_φ(· − x)⟩ = (u_a ⋆ c_φ)(x)`, a smooth even function of `x`.
(i) *(Complete form.)* If `|x|/2 + r ≤ T`, then
```
|S_φ(x)| ≤ S_φ(0) + 2 λ^W_T(a)^− ‖φ‖².
```
(ii) *(Primitive form.)* Let `φ = Pψ` with `ψ ∈ C_c^∞(−r,r)` real. Then (i) holds with `λ^A_T(a)^−` in place of
`λ^W_T(a)^−`. In this case `S_φ = (P²u_a) ⋆ c_ψ`, and `P²` annihilates the polar term: `P cosh(u/2) = 0`.

*Proof.* (i) Put `φ_h := φ(· − h)` and `F_± := φ_{x/2} ± φ_{−x/2}`. Then `supp F_± ⊂ [−|x|/2 − r, |x|/2 + r] ⊂ Ī_T`.
(If equality holds at the endpoints, shrink `r` slightly; `S_φ` is continuous in `φ`.) Translation gives
`φ_h ⋆ φ̃_k = c_φ(· − (h − k))`, so
```
c_{F_±} = 2c_φ ± (c_φ(· − x) + c_φ(· + x)), ‖F_±‖² = 2‖φ‖² ± 2c_φ(x).
```
Since `u_a` is even, `⟨u_a, c_φ(·+x)⟩ = ⟨u_a, c_φ(·−x)⟩`, and so `W^{(a)}_T(F_±) = 2S_φ(0) ± 2S_φ(x)`. By the definition of
the bottom, `W^{(a)}_T(F_±) ≥ λ^W_T‖F_±‖²`. Since `|c_φ(x)| ≤ ‖φ‖²`, we have `0 ≤ ‖F_±‖² ≤ 4‖φ‖²`, hence
`λ^W_T‖F_±‖² ≥ −4λ^{W,−}_T‖φ‖²`. Dividing by 2 gives `±S_φ(x) ≤ S_φ(0) + 2λ^{W,−}_T‖φ‖²`.
(ii) `F_± = Pψ_{x/2} ± Pψ_{−x/2}` lies in `C_c^∞(I_T)`, and its Tate moments vanish:
`∫(−G″ + ¼G)e^{±t/2}dt = ∫G(−¼ + ¼)e^{±t/2}dt = 0` for `G ∈ C_c^∞`. So `F_± ∈ P_T`, and there
`𝔞^{(a)}_T(F_±) = ⟨u_a, c_{F_±}⟩`. The computation of (i) goes through with `λ^A_T`. Finally `c_{Pψ} = P²c_ψ`, because
`P` has constant coefficients and is even, so `⟨u_a, c_{Pψ}(·−x)⟩ = ⟨P²u_a, c_ψ(·−x)⟩`. ∎

**Remark 2.1 (the jump-calculus content; PROVED).** For `x ≥ 0.5 + 2r`,
```
S_φ(x) = ∫[−½K(|y|) + 2cosh(y/2)] c_φ(y − x) dy − Σ_{n≥2} (a(n)/√n) c_φ(x − log n),
```
because `c_φ(· − x)` is supported in `[x − 2r, x + 2r]`, where `u_a` is this measure (the `δ_{−log n}` terms and the atom
of `k_∞` at `0` do not meet it). So `S_φ` is the **smoothed jump train**: at each contact `x = log n` a bump of height
`a(n)/√n`, minus the smoothed drift `2cosh`. This is R1 Thm 3 (`E(u)`) seen at resolution `r`. In the primitive case
`φ = Pψ` the drift is exactly annihilated. What remains is the archimedean tail plus the **4th-order jump train**
`−Σ (a(n)/√n)(P²c_ψ)(x − log n)`. Lemma J is the exact jump inequality asked for in the angle: *the window of size
`T = |x|/2 + r` bounds the jump train at distance `x`*. Its direction is the converse direction: the bottom bounds the
jumps, and the jumps do not bound the bottom.

## §3 Theorem G: the growth-graded converse

**Theorem G (PROVED; both forms; unconditional).** Let `a = Λ + b` with `β(b) < ½`. Then
```
Θ* ≤ ½ + max( κ_W(a)/2 , β(b) ) and Θ* ≤ ½ + max( κ_A(a)/2 , β(b) ).
```
The primitive statement is the stronger one, since `κ_A ≤ κ_W`.

*Proof.* We give the primitive form; the complete form is the same with `φ = ψ` (no factor `P`). Fix
`σ_0 > max(κ_A(a)/2, β(b))` with `σ_0 < ½`, and put `η := 2σ_0 − κ_A(a) > 0`.

1. *Growth of the jump train.* For every real `ψ ∈ C_c^∞(−r,r)` and `φ = Pψ`, Lemma J(ii) with `T = x/2 + r` and
  `λ^A_T(a)^− ≤ C_η e^{(κ_A + η)T}` (definition of limsup) gives
  `|S_φ(x)| ≤ C′ e^{(κ_A+η)x/2} = C′ e^{σ_0 x}` for `x ≥ 0`.
2. *Laplace transform of the jump train.* Put `S^θ(x) := ⟨θu_a, c_φ(· − x)⟩`. Then:
  * `S^θ(x) = S_φ(x)` for `x ≥ 0.5 + 2r`, because `(1 − θ)u_a` is supported in `(−∞, 0.5]`;
  * `S^θ(x) = 0` for `x ≤ 0.1 − 2r`;
  * `S^θ` is smooth.

  Hence `𝓢(s) := ∫_ℝ S^θ(x)e^{−sx}dx` is holomorphic on `Re s > σ_0`: the part over `[0, 0.5 + 2r]` is entire, and the
  rest converges absolutely by step 1.
3. *Factorisation for `Re s > ½`.* On `(0.1, ∞)`, `θu_a` is the measure
  `θ(u)[−½K(u) + 2cosh(u/2)]du − Σ_{n≥2}(a(n)/√n)δ_{log n}`. Since `K = O(e^{−u/2})`, `Σ Λ(n)n^{−½−σ} < ∞` and
  `Σ|b(n)|n^{−½−σ} = σ∫e^{−σX}B_b(X)dX < ∞` for `σ > max(½, β(b))`, it satisfies `∫e^{−σu}d|θu_a|(u) < ∞` for
  `σ > ½`. Fubini applies, since `∫∫|c_φ(u−x)|e^{−σx}dx d|θu_a|(u) ≤ 4r‖c_φ‖_∞e^{2rσ}∫e^{−σu}d|θu_a| < ∞`.
  Substituting `x = u − y` gives
  ```
  𝓢(s) = ℒ(θu_a)(s) · Φ(s)Φ(−s), ℒ(θu_a)(s) := ∫e^{−su}dθu_a(u), Φ(s) := ∫φ(t)e^{st}dt = (¼ − s²)Ψ(s),
  ```
  where `Ψ(s) := ∫ψ e^{st}dt` and `∫c_φ(y)e^{sy}dy = Φ(s)Φ(−s)`.
4. *Continuation.* So `ℒ(θu_a)` coincides on `Re s > ½` with `g_ψ := 𝓢_ψ / [(¼ − s²)²Ψ(s)Ψ(−s)]`, which is meromorphic
  on `Re s > σ_0`. All the `g_ψ` agree, by uniqueness of meromorphic continuation on the connected half-plane. Let
  `s_0` satisfy `Re s_0 > σ_0` and `s_0 ≠ ½`. The two sets `{ψ : Ψ(s_0) = 0}` and `{ψ : Ψ(−s_0) = 0}` are kernels of
  the nonzero linear functionals `ψ ↦ ∫ψe^{±s_0t}`, so they are proper hyperplanes of `C_c^∞(−r,r)` and their union is
  not the whole space. Choosing `ψ` outside it shows that `ℒ(θu_a)` is holomorphic at `s_0`. Hence `ℒ(θu_a)` is
  holomorphic on `{Re s > σ_0} ∖ {½}`.
5. *Identify.* For `Re s > ½`,
  `ℒ(θu_a)(s) = A(s) + C(s) + (ζ′/ζ)(½ + s) − Σ_n b(n)n^{−½−s}`. Here:
  * `A(s) = −½∫θKe^{−su}` is holomorphic on `Re s > −½`;
  * `C(s) = ∫θ(u)2cosh(u/2)e^{−su}du` is meromorphic on `ℂ`, with simple poles at `±½` only (R3 Lemma L step 3);
  * the `b`-series is holomorphic on `Re s > β(b)`;
  * `−Σ Λ(n)n^{−½−s} = (ζ′/ζ)(½ + s)`.

  So `(ζ′/ζ)(½ + s)` continues meromorphically to `Re s > σ_0`, with its only possible pole at `s = ½`. A zero `ρ` of
  `ζ` with `Re ρ > ½ + σ_0` would give a pole at `s = ρ − ½`, which is `≠ ½` since `ρ ≠ 1`, a contradiction. Letting
  `σ_0 ↓ max(κ_A/2, β(b))` gives `Θ* ≤ ½ + max(κ_A/2, β(b))`. If that maximum is `≥ ½`, the claim is trivial. ∎

**Remark 3.1 (what it uses).** Theorem G uses neither Bochner–Schwartz, nor Lemma L's distributional transposition, nor
any positivity of `Λ`. Its inputs are:
* the doubling identity (Lemma J);
* absolute convergence of the Dirichlet series on `Re s > ½`;
* uniqueness of analytic continuation.

It is Landau's method, applied to the smoothed jump train instead of to `ψ(x) − x`. **CITED for its content:** Landau's
theorem on Dirichlet integrals, and von Koch's `RH ⟺ ψ(x) − x ≪ x^{½+ε}` (Ingham, *The Distribution of Prime Numbers*,
Ch. IV and V). **Sibling in the corpus:** G1_B8_R2 Theorem G proves the *exact* law
`limsup (2t)^{−1}log⁺N_t = Θ* − ½` for Suzuki's Hankel window norms. The present theorem is the one-sided analogue for
P46's Weil bottoms `λ^A_T, λ^W_T`, uniform over the comb class `Λ + ℬ`.

**Remark 3.2 (a second proof of R3 Theorem N(i) on `Λ + ℬ`).** If `inf_T λ^A_T(a) > −∞` and `b ∈ ℬ`, then
`κ_A(a) = 0` and `β(b) = 0`, so `Θ* = ½`, which is RH. This covers every `b` the panel used (`ℓ¹_w`, finite
modifications, `ζ_odd`) without the step-2 issue. R3 N(i) remains the more general statement for `b ∈ 𝒯`, whose
perturbations need not be measures; there the §0 patch is used.

## §4 Corollary G1: the robust equivalence and the growth dichotomy

**Corollary G1 (PROVED).** For every `a ∈ Λ + ℬ`:
```
RH ⟺ κ_A(a) = 0 ⟺ κ_W(a) = 0 ⟺ −λ^W_T(a) = e^{o(T)} . (EQUIVALENT)
```
Unconditionally, exactly one of the following holds:
* `κ_W ≡ κ_A ≡ 0` on all of `Λ + ℬ` (this is RH);
* `κ_A(a) ≥ 2(Θ* − ½) > 0` for **every** `a ∈ Λ + ℬ` (this is ¬RH).

*Proof.* (⟸) is Theorem G with `β(b) = 0`. (⟹) Under RH, `λ^W_T(Λ) ≥ 0` for all `T` (Weil; P46 l.140–146, CITED).
For `F ∈ L²(I_T)`, `W^{(Λ+b)}_T(F) = W_T(F) − Σ_{log n<2T}2b(n)n^{−½}c_F(log n) ≥ −2B_b(2T)‖F‖²`. So
`λ^A_T(a) ≥ λ^W_T(a) ≥ −2B_b(2T) = −e^{o(T)}`. The dichotomy follows: under ¬RH, Theorem G gives
`κ_A(a) ≥ 2(Θ* − ½)` for each `a ∈ Λ + ℬ`. ∎

**Contrast with R3 (PROVED, both statements on record).**

| target | set of `a ∈ Λ + ℬ` satisfying it | tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| `∀T λ_T(a) ≥ 0` (sign) | on `Λ + (ℬ ∩ 𝒯_meas)` (which contains `Λ + ℓ¹_w`): `{Λ}` if RH, `∅` if not (R3 Thm N/R′). For `b ∈ ℬ∖𝒯_meas`, which need not even be tempered, nothing is claimed | zero |
| `κ(a) = 0` (growth) | all of `Λ + ℬ` if RH, `∅` if not (Cor G1) | all of `ℬ` |

Both targets are equivalent to RH at `a = Λ`.

**Open upper half (OPEN; HEURISTIC support).** Is `κ_W(Λ) = 2Θ* − 1`, i.e. is Theorem G sharp for ζ itself? The inequality
`κ_W(Λ) ≤ 2Θ* − 1` would need a lower bound `W_T(F) ≥ −C_ε e^{(2Θ*−1+ε)T}‖F‖²`. The shifted-contour representation
`W_T(F) = π^{−1}Re∫(ξ′/ξ)(½+δ+iτ)(Fe^{δ·})^(τ)(Fe^{−δ·})^(−τ)dτ` with `δ > Θ* − ½`, together with
`ξ′/ξ ≪_δ log|τ|` there (CITED, Titchmarsh §9.6), bounds `|W_T|` only by log-Sobolev norms of `Fe^{±δ·}`, not by
`‖F‖²`. I have no proof. It holds in G1_B8_R2's Hankel-norm model, which is the HEURISTIC support. Bombieri (Remarks on
Weil's quadratic functional I, 2001, CITED from the abstract) counts the negative eigenvalues but does not give their
size.

## §5 What this changes in the reading of G2 (filters and obstructions)

**The point.** R3 Remark 3.1 read the ∀T arrow as intrinsically zero-tolerant ("atoms absorb no perturbation"), and so
required "an exact identity, not an estimate" (O3). Corollary G1 shows that this reading belongs to the **sign
normalisation**. The chain
```
(prime data) ⟹ κ(a) = 0 for one a ∈ Λ + ℬ ⟹ RH ⟹ ∀T λ^A_T(Λ) ≥ 0, λ^W_T(Λ) ≥ 0
```
is valid (PROVED arrows 2 and 3: Theorem G, then Weil). Its first arrow is an **estimate** that tolerates every
`ℬ`-perturbation. The exactness demanded by O3 is supplied entirely by the classical third arrow, which is the explicit
formula with an atomic spectral measure *after* RH. This is consistent with R′: `κ(Λ + b) = 0` does not give
`λ(Λ + b) ≥ 0`, because arrow 3 is used only at `Λ`. **PROVED (as a statement about implications); the strategic reading
is HEURISTIC.**

**The price.** Arrow 1 for `a = Λ` is `−λ_T = e^{o(T)}`. By Theorem G and von Koch it is equivalent to
`ψ(x) = x + O(x^{½+ε})`. So the growth route makes G2 the classical problem. It removes one family of obstructions and
brings in the classical family.

| obstruction / filter | applies to the sign target? | applies to the growth target `κ = 0`? |
|---|---|---|

## §6 R3 Remark 3.4, restated (OPEN)

The R3 "PROVED part" is withdrawn (§0). What stands:

**Question Q (OPEN; under RH).** Is there a real `b ≠ 0` with `β_b ∈ 𝒮′` and `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W`? Equivalently: is there a comb
`a ≠ Λ`, supported on `log ℕ_{≥2}`, with `u_a − u_Λ` tempered and `u_a` positive definite? Equivalently:
`D_b = β̂_b ≤ 2πμ_Λ`.

What is PROVED about Q:
* **(a)** Every solution has `b ∉ 𝒯_meas` (R3 Thm R′), so `D_b` must charge `Γ_ζ`.
* **(b)** Bandlimited means vanish. If `ω ∈ 𝒮` has `ω̌` supported in `(−log2, log2)`, then
  `∫ω(τ − τ_0)dD_b(τ) = 0` for all `τ_0`, because `β_b` has no mass in `(−log2, log2)`. So the positive part of `D_b`
  (atomic, on `Γ_ζ`) and the negative part have equal local averages at scale `≳ 1/log 2`. This is not a contradiction.
* **(c)** By scale invariance (`b ↦ εb`), Q only requires `D_b^+` atomic on `Γ_ζ` with `D_b^+({γ}) ≤ C m_γ`.

Q is a one-sided Fourier-pair problem between the comb `±log ℕ` and the ordinates. It is the kind of question studied for
crystalline measures (Meyer 2016; Kurasov–Sarnak arXiv:2004.05678; Olevskii–Ulanovskii), CITED as context only and not
verified to decide Q. Broucke–Debruyne–Vindas arXiv:2004.11501 (CITED via the panel) give Weil-positive Beurling systems,
but those are not supported on `log ℕ`, so they do not answer Q.
