## 1. The construction: the Bohr–Jessen torus of twisted Weil forms

**Notation** (P46 §1, `eq:symbol`, `eq:form`, `eq:W`, `eq:Aform`; `subsec:finite`):
* `I_T = (−T,T)` and `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`. `N_T = {n ≥ 2 : log n < 2T}`. `c_F` is the autocorrelation.
* `C₀ = −5.3721834`, `K(u) = ½/sinh(u/2) + ½/cosh(u/2)`, and `Lev(Φ) = ∫₀^∞ (Φ(0) − Φ(u))K(u)du`.
* `P_T = {A(F) = B(F) = 0}`.
* For a real (signed) vector `v ∈ ℝ^{N_T}`, `𝔞^{(v)}_T(F) = C₀‖F‖² + Lev(c_F) − Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n c_F(log n)` for real `F`
  (`thm:A`). Its bottom on `P_T` is `V_T(v)` (`lem:degen`).
* The complete comb-level form is `W_T(v) = 𝔞^{(v)}_T + 2Re(A B̄)` on `L²(I_T)`, with bottom `V^W_T(v)`
  (`subsec:finite`).

**Definition 1.1 (the torus and the twists).** Let `𝒫` be the set of primes and `Ω := 𝕋^𝒫 = (ℝ/2πℤ)^𝒫`, with product
Haar probability `μ`. For `θ ∈ Ω`, let `χ_θ` be the completely multiplicative unimodular function with `χ_θ(p) = e^{iθ_p}`.
The **twisted weights** are
```
v^θ_n := v_n · Re χ_θ(n) = (2 log p / p^{k/2}) · cos(kθ_p) (n = p^k),
```
and the **twisted forms** are `𝔞^θ_T := 𝔞^{(v^θ_T)}_T` and `W^θ_T := W_T(v^θ_T)`. The twisted bottoms are
`λ^θ_T := V_T(v^θ_T)` (primitive, on `P_T`) and `λ^{W,θ}_T := V^W_T(v^θ_T)` (complete, on `L²(I_T)`). So `θ = 0` gives
`λ^0_T = λ^A_T` and `λ^{W,0}_T = λ^W_T`. At window `T` only the coordinates `θ_p` with `p < e^{2T}` enter.

*Why this is the right object for the angle.* By the Kronecker–Weyl theorem (CITED; unique ergodicity of a linear flow
with Q-independent frequencies), the line `τ ↦ τω`, `ω = (log p)_p`, is dense and equidistributed in every finite
subtorus. The comb `Σ_{N_T} v_n cos(τ log n)` is the restriction to this line of the torus function
`Φ_T(θ) = Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n cos(k(n) θ_{p(n)})`. Every mean-value, Bohr–Jessen or ergodic statement about the comb is a
statement about `Φ_T` under `μ`. The twisted forms are what those statements also describe.

**Lemma 1.2 (the Kronecker orbit consists of Γ-mismatched L-functions; PROVED identity, CITED explicit formula).**
Take `θ = tω` with `t ∈ ℝ`. Then `v^{tω}_n = v_n cos(t log n)`, and these are exactly half the weights `2Λ(n)cos(t log n)/√n`
of `L_t(s) := ζ(s+it)ζ(s−it)`. The function `L_t` has real coefficients and satisfies the functional equation
`Λ_t(s) = Λ_t(1−s)`, where `Λ_t = ξ-factors`, with Γ-factor `Γ_ℝ(s+it)Γ_ℝ(s−it)` and poles at `1 ± it`.

Put `Δ_t(τ) := m(τ+t) + m(τ−t) − 2m(τ)`. For real `F ∈ L²(I_T)` in the form domain, the Weil functional of `L_t` is
```
W_{L_t}(F) = 2·𝔞^{tω}_T(F) + (1/2π)∫ Δ_t(τ)|F̂(τ)|² dτ + (polar terms at 1 ± it).
```
*Proof.* The Dirichlet coefficients of `−L_t'/L_t` are `Λ(n)(n^{−it} + n^{it}) = 2Λ(n)cos(t log n)`. The archimedean
symbol of `Γ_ℝ(s±it)` at `s = ½ + iτ` is `m(τ ± t)`. Then apply the explicit formula for `L_t` (Weil 1952;
Iwaniec–Kowalski Thm 5.12, CITED), as in P46 `eq:W`. ∎

So the orbit points are genuine self-dual L-functions whose zeros are `ρ ± it`. Under RH they satisfy RH, so
`W_{L_t} ≥ 0` (Weil's criterion for `L_t`, CONDITIONAL on RH). But their Γ-factor is not that of `ζ²`. On the torus, ζ is
singled out *only* by the matching of the Γ-factor `m` to the point `θ = 0`. The sign of `Δ_t` is mixed:
`Δ_t(0) = 2(m(t) − C₀) > 0`, while `Δ_t(τ) ≈ −t²/τ² < 0` for `|τ| ≫ t`. So nothing about `𝔞^{tω}` follows. This is
**OPEN** and not pursued.

## 2. Theorem A: the average of the twists is the archimedean form (a single window)

**Theorem A (PROVED; both forms).** Fix `T > 0`.
1. For every real `F` in the form domain, `∫_Ω 𝔞^θ_T(F) dμ(θ) = C₀‖F‖² + Lev(c_F) =: 𝔄_∞(F)`. This is the archimedean
  form.
2. `∫_Ω λ^θ_T dμ ≤ λ^∞_{P}(T)`, the bottom of `𝔄_∞` on `P_T`. Likewise `∫_Ω λ^{W,θ}_T dμ ≤ λ^∞(T)`, the bottom of the
  archimedean complete form on `L²(I_T)`.
3. The sets `N_T^A := {θ : λ^θ_T < 0}` and `N_T^W := {θ : λ^{W,θ}_T < 0}` are open. `N_T^A` is nonempty, hence of
  positive Haar measure, for every `T ≥ 0.60`. `N_T^W` is nonempty for every `T` for which the archimedean complete
  form is negative, in particular for `T ≥ 0.4608`. As `T → ∞`, `∫λ^θ_T dμ ≤ λ^∞_P(T) ↓ C₀`.

*Proof.*
1. `𝔞^θ_T(F) = 𝔄_∞(F) − Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n cos(k(n)θ_{p(n)}) c_F(log n)`. This is a finite sum, and
  `∫_𝕋 cos(kθ) dθ/2π = 0` for `k ≥ 1`.
2. By `lem:degen`, `V_T` is finite and concave on `ℝ^{N_T}` (an infimum of affine functions of `v`), hence continuous.
  The map `θ ↦ v^θ_T` is continuous and bounded, with `∫ v^θ_T dμ = 0` by part 1. Jensen's inequality for concave
  functions of an integrable `ℝ^d`-valued random vector gives `∫V_T(v^θ_T) dμ ≤ V_T(0) = λ^∞_P(T)`. The complete form
  works the same way: `V^W_T` is again an infimum of affine functions of `v`, since the polar term does not depend on
  `v`.
3. Openness follows from the continuity of `θ ↦ V_T(v^θ_T)`. If `N_T^A` were empty, then `λ^θ_T ≥ 0` for all `θ`, so the
  integral would be `≥ 0`. But `λ^∞_P(T) < 0` for `T ≥ 0.60` (`cor:Tarch`). The complete case uses `prop:archneg` (the
  archimedean complete form is negative from `T = 0.4608`). The limit is `prop:arch`(ii). ∎

**Corollary A′ (mean-value blindness at a single window; PROVED, in the derivation sense of `cor:void` / P47 Thm 18.1(ii)).**
Fix `T`. Call a *Kronecker–Weyl input* at `T` any statement of the form "`lim_{X→∞} X^{−1}∫_0^X G(τω + θ₀) dτ = c`". Here
`G` is continuous on the finite torus `𝕋^{π_T}` (where `π_T = {p < e^{2T}}`), or is a monotone limit of such functions.
It is built from the comb phases and weights. This covers moments, Bohr–Jessen value distributions of `Φ_T` or of any
functional of it, frequencies of alignment arcs, and ergodic averages along the flow. By unique ergodicity (CITED,
Kronecker–Weyl), the value `c = ∫G dμ` does not depend on `θ₀`. So every such input holds verbatim for the twisted comb
at every `θ`.

A derivation of `RD_T` (primitive) or of `λ^W_T ≥ 0` (complete) whose only prime input consists of such statements
would therefore also derive `λ^θ_T ≥ 0` (resp. `λ^{W,θ}_T ≥ 0`) for all `θ`. By Theorem A.3 this is false for
`T ≥ 0.60` (resp. `T ≥ 0.4608`). **EXCLUDED**, at every window beyond these thresholds.

*What is different from the record.*
* `thm:SC` concerns nonnegative sub-combs of mass `< 5.37`. The twisted weights are signed and carry the *full* mass
  `Σ|v^θ_n|`. They are not covered.
* `cor:void` concerns classes closed under deletion or amplification. The torus family is closed under neither. It is a
  third closure operation, "unimodular twist", and Theorem A is the corresponding void.
* R157_002 row 27 excluded *position-only* inputs. Corollary A′ excludes mean values that do involve the exact weights
  `Λ(p^k)`.

## 3. Theorem B: "for all `T`" positivity is a Haar-null property of the twist

A ∀`T` mechanism could use properties of `θ = 0` that are not θ-invariant but hold almost surely: laws of the iterated
logarithm, Wintner-type bounds, a.s. value distributions. Theorem B excludes these as well.

**Theorem B (PROVED; primitive form, and a fortiori complete form).** For μ-almost every `θ ∈ Ω`, there are arbitrarily
large `T` with `λ^θ_T < 0`. More precisely, there is an absolute `c > 0` with `liminf_{T→∞} λ^θ_T / T ≤ −c` μ-a.s. In
particular, for every `T₀`, the closed set `G_{T₀} := {θ : λ^θ_T ≥ 0 for all T ≥ T₀}` has `μ(G_{T₀}) = 0`. The same holds
for `λ^{W,θ}_T ≤ λ^θ_T`, and for the bottom of `𝔞^θ_T` on all of `L²(I_T)` without the polar term (which is `≤ λ^θ_T`).
Under RH, `0 ∈ G_{T₀}` for every `T₀`: **ζ sits in a Haar-null closed subset of the Bohr–Jessen torus.**

*Proof.*

**Step 0 (the test family; `prop:finsupp`).** Fix a real, nonzero `g ∈ C_c^∞(−1,1)`. Put `G_T(t) = g(t/T)` and
`F_T = (−∂² + ¼)G_T`. Then `F_T ∈ P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)` (two integrations by parts), and
`F̂_T(τ) = (τ²+¼)·T·ĝ(Tτ)`. Hence `c_{F_T}(u)/‖F_T‖² = r_T(u/T)`, where
```
r_T(x) = ∫ cos(xs) w_T(s) ds , w_T(s) = (s²/T² + ¼)² |ĝ(s)|² / ∫(s'²/T² + ¼)² |ĝ(s')|² ds' ,
```
and `w_T` is a probability density. For every θ,
```
λ^θ_T ≤ 𝔞^θ_T(F_T)/‖F_T‖² = a_T − X_T(θ),
a_T := C₀ + ∫₀^∞ (1 − r_T(u/T)) K(u) du,
X_T(θ) := Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n cos(k(n)θ_{p(n)}) r_T(log n / T).
```

**Step 1 (`a_T` is bounded).** For `T ≥ 1`, `(s²/T²+¼)² ≤ (s²+¼)²` and the denominator is `≥ (1/16)∫|ĝ|²`. So
`M := sup_{T≥1} ∫ s² w_T(s) ds < ∞`. Also `1 − r_T(x) ≤ min(2, x²M/2)`, and `K(u) ≤ 1/u + 1` on `(0,1]`. Hence
`a* := sup_{T≥1} a_T ≤ C₀ + M/(2T²)·∫₀¹ u(1+u) du + 2∫₁^∞ K < ∞`. (By dominated convergence, `a_T → C₀`, but this is
not needed.)

**Step 2 (the limit profile).** `w_T → w_∞ := |ĝ|²/∫|ĝ|²` in `L¹`, by dominated convergence, so `r_T → r_∞` uniformly
on `ℝ`. Here `r_∞(0) = 1` and `r_∞` is continuous. Fix `x₀ ∈ (0,2)` with `r_∞ ≥ 3/4` on `[0,x₀]`. For `T ≥ T*`,
`r_T ≥ ½` on `[0,x₀]` and `|r_T| ≤ 1` everywhere.

**Step 3 (the blocks).** Choose `T_j := T*·2^{j²}`, and let `B_j` be the set of primes in `[e^{2T_{j−1}}, e^{2T_j})`, with
`B_1` all primes `< e^{2T_1}`. Split `X_{T_j} = Y_j + R_j`, where
```
Y_j := Σ_{p∈B_j} v_p cos θ_p · r_{T_j}(log p / T_j),
R_j := Σ_{p<e^{2T_{j−1}}} v_p cos θ_p · r_{T_j}(log p/T_j) + Σ_{p^k∈N_{T_j}, k≥2} v_{p^k} cos(kθ_p) r_{T_j}(log p^k/T_j).
```
The `Y_j` are functions of disjoint sets of coordinates, so they are **independent** under μ. Each `Y_j` is a sum of
independent, mean-zero summands `ξ_p` with `|ξ_p| ≤ max_p 2log p/√p < 1.48`.

**Step 4 (variances).** By Mertens, `Σ_{p≤x} log p / p = log x + O(1)` (CITED).
* *Lower bound for `Y_j`.* `s_j² := Var Y_j = Σ_{p∈B_j} (2 log² p / p) r_{T_j}(log p/T_j)²`. With `y = e^{x₀T_j/2}`, we
  have `y ≥ e^{2T_{j−1}}` once `T_j ≥ 4T_{j−1}/x₀`, which holds for `j ≥ j₀`. Keeping only `y < p ≤ y²` (where
  `r ≥ ½`):
  `s_j² ≥ ½ log y · Σ_{y<p≤y²} log p/p = ½ log y (log y + O(1)) ≥ c₁² T_j²`.
* *Upper bound for `R_j`.* By `(a+b)² ≤ 2a² + 2b²` and orthogonality of the `cos(kθ_p)` (distinct `(p,k)` are
  orthogonal in `L²(μ)`):
  `E R_j² ≤ 2Σ_{p<e^{2T_{j−1}}} 2log²p/p + 2Σ_p Σ_{k≥2} 2log²p/p^k ≤ C T_{j−1}² + C'`.

**Step 5 (the product estimate).** Let `Φ` be the standard normal distribution function. By Berry–Esseen (CITED),
`sup_x |P(Y_j ≤ x s_j) − Φ(x)| ≤ C_BE · 1.48/s_j`. Let `E_j := {λ^θ_{T_j} ≥ 0}`. By Step 0,
`E_j ⊆ {X_{T_j} ≤ a*} ⊆ A_j ∪ D_j`, with `A_j := {Y_j ≤ a* + s_j}` and `D_j := {|R_j| > s_j}`. Then for `J ≤ J'`,
```
μ(⋂_{j=J}^{J'} E_j) ≤ μ(⋂_{j=J}^{J'} A_j) + Σ_{j≥J} μ(D_j) = Π_{j=J}^{J'} μ(A_j) + Σ_{j≥J} μ(D_j).
```
* `μ(A_j) ≤ Φ(1 + a*/s_j) + C/s_j → Φ(1) < 0.85`, so the product tends to `0` as `J' → ∞`.
* By Chebyshev, `μ(D_j) ≤ (C T_{j−1}² + C')/(c₁² T_j²) ≤ C'' 4^{−(2j−1)}`, which is summable.

Hence `μ(G_{T₀}) ≤ μ(⋂_{j≥J} E_j) ≤ Σ_{j≥J} μ(D_j) → 0` (take `J` with `T_J ≥ T₀`).

**Step 6 (the rate).** Choose `c := c₁/4`. Then `μ(Y_j > 2cT_j) ≥ 1 − Φ(½) − C/s_j ≥ 0.3` for large `j`. By independence
and the second Borel–Cantelli lemma, `Y_j > 2cT_j` for infinitely many `j`, a.s. By Chebyshev,
`μ(|R_j| > cT_j) ≤ (CT_{j−1}² + C')/(c² T_j²)` is summable, so by the first Borel–Cantelli lemma `|R_j| ≤ cT_j`
eventually, a.s. Hence `X_{T_j} > cT_j` infinitely often, and `λ^θ_{T_j} ≤ a* − cT_j` infinitely often, a.s.

The other forms have smaller bottoms: `λ^{W,θ}_T ≤ λ^θ_T` (they agree on `P_T`), and the bottom on `L²(I_T)` is at most
the bottom on `P_T`. ∎

**Corollary B′ (almost-sure inputs are excluded; PROVED, in the derivation sense).** A derivation of `∀T λ^A_T ≥ 0` (or
`∀T ≥ T₀`) whose only prime input is a property `𝒬` of the twist θ, valid for all θ that satisfy `𝒬`, where `𝒬` holds on
a set of positive μ-measure, proves a statement that is false on a set of positive measure. It is therefore unsound.
**EXCLUDED.** This covers every Bohr–Jessen or probabilistic property of the Euler product that holds almost surely
(laws of the iterated logarithm for prime sums, Wintner/Harper-type bounds, a.s. limit distributions). In particular it
covers the property in Theorem C.

**Remark B″ (the quantitative contrast).**
* Under RH, `λ^A_T ∈ [0, 4·10⁹·T·e^{−15.449T}]` (`thm:margin`).
* For the typical twist, `λ^θ_T ≤ a* − cT` infinitely often.
* O3 (`rem:resweights`) says one weight move of size `e^{−14.4T}` flips the sign of ζ.

So ζ is exponentially close to the boundary of positivity, while almost every one of its Bohr–Jessen companions is
linearly far inside the negative region. Positivity is not a typical property that a "pattern" on the torus could
explain. It is an exceptional property of one point. (The "100 random zeros" probe asks the dual question on the
zero side. `thm:finZ` answers it there; this answers the prime-side randomisation.)

## 4. Theorem C: square-root cancellation does not give Weil positivity off θ = 0

**Theorem C (PROVED; the probabilistic part is elementary).** For μ-a.e. θ and every `ε > 0`,
`ψ_θ(x) := Σ_{n≤x} Λ(n) Re χ_θ(n) ≪_{θ,ε} x^{½+ε}`. The same bound holds for `Σ_{n≤x} Λ(n)χ_θ(n)`. Consequently
`−ζ_θ'/ζ_θ = Σ Λ(n)χ_θ(n) n^{−s}` and `log ζ_θ` converge in `Re s > ½`, where `ζ_θ(s) := Π_p (1 − e^{iθ_p}p^{−s})^{−1}`. So
**ζ_θ is analytic and zero-free in `Re s > ½` for μ-a.e. θ**, which is the zero-free form of "RH for ζ_θ". Yet by
Theorem B, the twisted Weil forms built with ζ's Γ-factor are negative at arbitrarily large `T` for the same μ-a.e. θ.

*Proof.*
* *The prime part.* `S(x) := Σ_{p≤x} log p · cos θ_p` is a sum of independent, mean-zero terms, with
  `Var S(2^j) = ½Σ_{p≤2^j} log²p ≤ j·2^{j+1}` by Chebyshev's bound `θ(x) ≤ 2x log 2`. By Kolmogorov's maximal inequality,
  `μ(max_{x≤2^j}|S(x)| ≥ 2^{j(½+ε)}) ≤ j·2^{j+1}/2^{j(1+2ε)}`, which is summable. By Borel–Cantelli, a.s.
  `|S(x)| ≤ 2^{j(½+ε)} ≤ (2x)^{½+ε}` on `2^{j−1} < x ≤ 2^j` for all large `j`. The imaginary part is handled the same
  way.
* *The prime powers.* `|Σ_{p^k≤x, k≥2} Λ(p^k)Re χ_θ(p^k)| ≤ ψ(x) − θ(x) ≪ √x log² x` deterministically.
* *The Dirichlet series.* Partial summation gives convergence for `Re s > ½`. The series for `log ζ_θ` is dominated by
  the same bound, since `Λ(n)/log n ≤ Λ(n)/log 2`. ∎

**Reading.** The box states "`∀T` ⟺ `ψ(x) − x ≪ x^{½+ε}` ⟺ RH". The second equivalence goes through the
analytic continuation of ζ and its functional equation (A1, A3 of P47 §18.14). Only these give an explicit formula
tying the comb to the Γ-symbol `m`. On the torus the partial-sum property holds almost surely (Theorem C), while Weil
positivity with the Γ-factor `m` fails almost surely (Theorem B).

So **a G2 mechanism cannot be a square-root-cancellation argument about `Λ(n)` alone.** At a load-bearing step it must
use the coupling between `m` and the specific point `θ = 0`. That coupling is the functional equation: the matching of
Γ-factor and Euler product that Lemma 1.2 shows is broken along the Kronecker orbit. This is F4 (A3 together with one of
A7/A8/A9), now derived independently from the Kronecker side. It gives F4 a concrete test: **a candidate mechanism must
fail for μ-a.e. twist θ.**

## 5. What the exact values `Λ(p^k)` buy on the torus, and why arc control is closed

### 5.1 The Poisson completion (PROVED identity; no sign)

Take `r_p := p^{−1/2}` and `P_r(x) := (1−r²)/(1−2r cos x + r²) = 1 + 2Σ_{k≥1} r^k cos kx`. Because `Λ(p^k) = log p` for
every `k`,
```
Σ_{k≥1} v_{p^k} cos(kx) = log p · (P_{r_p}(x) − 1) (absolutely and uniformly convergent).
```
Set `σ̃_T(τ) := m(τ) − Σ_{p<e^{2T}} log p · (P_{r_p}(τ log p) − 1)`. Then for every `F ∈ L²(I_T)` in the form domain,
```
𝔞_T(F) = (1/2π) ∫ σ̃_T(τ) |F̂(τ)|² dτ.
```
*Proof.* `σ̃_T − σ_T = −Σ_{p<e^{2T}} Σ_{k: k log p ≥ 2T} v_{p^k} cos(τ log p^k)`. This series converges absolutely and
uniformly, with total mass `Σ_{p<e^{2T}} Σ_{k≥K_p} 2log p·p^{−k/2} < ∞`. The terms can therefore be integrated
separately against `|F̂|² ∈ L¹`, and each gives `2π c_F(k log p) = 0`, because `supp c_F ⊆ [−2T, 2T]` and
`c_F(±2T) = 0`. ∎

So the torus lift of the symbol is *separable by prime*: `Φ̃_T(θ) = Σ_p log p(P_{r_p}(θ_p) − 1)`, a sum of independent
Poisson kernels. This is where the constancy of `Λ(p^k)` along powers enters. It also matches P122_017 on the carrier
side, where the cocycle splits by prime.

In the language of `thm:D1`, the completion is a feasible dual variable of finite total variation, supported on
`|u| ≥ 2T`: it is the high-power part of the flat-top certificate of `thm:Z`. It gives **no sign**:
`σ̃_T(0) = C₀ − Σ_p 2r_p log p/(1−r_p) < σ_T(0)`, and pointwise it is no better than `σ_T` on the alignment set. Its value
as a certificate is not computed (OPEN, low priority). Separability is exactly what makes Theorems A and B work: a
separable symbol has independent coordinates under μ.

### 5.2 Arc control is closed on both sides

The angle's premise was to control the alignment arcs of `prop:align` uniformly in `T`. There are two ways to do this,
and both are closed.
1. *Asymptotic control (equidistribution, Bohr–Jessen densities of `{Φ_T > m}`).* It concerns `τ → ∞`, while the band
  `[0, τ^lo(A_T)]` is compact (`prop:band`). What it does say is θ-invariant. **EXCLUDED** by Corollary A′.
2. *Finite-height control (discrepancy, Baker's lower bounds for `|Σ k_p log p|`, which is exactly the Diophantine
  content of `prop:align`).* It is a position-only input, so `cor:void` on `C_supp` applies (R157_002 row 27, F1).
  **EXCLUDED.**

Even complete control of the arcs would not decide the sign: `prop:negtrace`(v) shows that two symbols agreeing on the
band can give forms of opposite signs.
