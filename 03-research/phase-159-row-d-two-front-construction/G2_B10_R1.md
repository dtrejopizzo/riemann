# G2_B10_R1 — G2 (the mechanism), round 1: mollifiers, Levinson–Conrey, and the exact Jensen identity

*Phase 159, group G2. Angle: turn the positive-proportion method (Levinson 1974,
Conrey 1989) into an exact identity for the sign, uniform in `T`, and find the step where "100 %" would be forced.
Pure mathematics, no numerics. Normalisations are those of Paper 47 (`eq:symbol`, `eq:form`, `eq:W`). Labels:
PROVED / CITED / HEURISTIC / OPEN, plus EQUIVALENT (to RH) and EXCLUDED (by a proved obstruction of the record). No
sign is claimed at any window.*

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The angle gives **no mechanism**.
* The Levinson–Conrey method in its *density* form ("100 % of the zeros on the line") is **EXCLUDED** by O2 of R157_002.
* In its *exact* form, meaning Littlewood/Jensen at `σ = ½` with the Poisson weight at `s = 1`, it is the
  Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte criterion, which is **EQUIVALENT** to RH (R157_002 rows 43 and 57). I write it as an exact
  identity with four nonnegative terms (Theorem M1). This makes the "100 % step" precise: it is the cyclicity of
  `ζ·𝒟₀` in `H²(μ)`.
* What is **new and PROVED** is Theorem N. The mollifier closure `[ζ_a𝒟₀]` is **the same subspace** for every comb in
  the class `𝒞_E ⊃` the finite modifications of `thm:finmod`. So every statement that comes from a mollifier is shared
  by combs whose window forms have opposite signs, for both forms, at every `T ∈ (½log2, log2]` and, citing Zhu, on
  `(½log2, 0.8]`. By `cor:void`, a mollifier argument therefore decides **no window**. It can enter a proof of the
  `∀T` arrow only through the route "mollifier ⟹ RH, then Weil ⟹ `∀T`", and in that route the functional equation
  does the work. That is RH first, not a per-window mechanism.
* A second PROVED item (Prop. W-M) gives the mollified explicit formula for the Weil form on windows. It is exact,
  but it is the window form `W_{T+½log N}` restricted to a subspace of tests. Mollification can only hide off-line
  zeros, never certify positivity.

## §1 Setting

*Hardy space at the point `s = 1` (standard; CITED: Duren, *Theory of H^p spaces*, ch. 2 and 11; Garnett, *Bounded
analytic functions*, ch. II).* Put `Ω = {Re s > ½}` and `φ(s) = (s−1)/s`, a conformal map `Ω → D` with `φ(1) = 0` and
`|φ| = 1` on `Re s = ½`. The pull-back of normalised arc length is the Poisson measure of `Ω` at `1`:
```
dμ(t) = dt / (2π(¼ + t²)) = dt / (2π|s|²), s = ½ + it, μ(ℝ) = 1.
```
`H²(μ) := {h∘φ : h ∈ H²(D)}`, with boundary values in `L²(μ)`. Two facts are used:
* **Unitary transfer.** `k ∈ H²(Ω)` (unweighted, `sup_σ∫|k(σ+it)|²dt < ∞`) if and only if `s·k(s) ∈ H²(μ)`. This is
  the standard unitary map `H²(D) ↔ H²(ℂ_+)` with `w = s − ½` and base point `a = ½`.
* **Mean value.** For `f ∈ H²(μ)`, `f(1) = ∫f dμ = ⟨f, 1⟩`, so `‖f‖² = |f(1)|² + ‖f − f(1)‖²`.

*Dirichlet polynomials.* `𝒟` is the set of `M(s) = Σ_{n≤N} a_n n^{−s}`, and `𝒟₀ = {M ∈ 𝒟 : M(1) = 0}`. Every `M ∈ 𝒟`
is bounded and analytic on the closed half-plane `Re s ≥ ½`, with `|M| ≤ Σ|a_n|n^{−½}`.

**Lemma 0 (`ζ𝒟₀ ⊂ H²(μ)`; PROVED, classical).** For `M ∈ 𝒟₀`, `f_M := ζM ∈ H²(μ)` and `f_M(1) = M′(1) = −Σ a_n log n / n`.

*Proof.* Let `h̃(s) = ∫₀¹{1/x}x^{s−1}dx`. With `x = e^{−y}` this is the Laplace transform in `w = s − ½` of
`y ↦ {e^y}e^{−y/2} ∈ L²(0,∞)`, so `h̃ ∈ H²(Ω)` by Paley–Wiener. For `½ < σ < 1`, `−ζ(s)/s = ∫₀^∞{1/x}x^{s−1}dx`
(Titchmarsh 2.1.5), and `∫₁^∞x^{−1}x^{s−1}dx = 1/(1−s)`, so `ζ(s)/s = −h̃(s) + 1/(s−1)`. Put
`M̃(s) = M(s)·s/(s−1)`. It is analytic on `Re s ≥ ½` (the pole cancels because `M(1) = 0`), continuous, and tends to `M`
as `|s| → ∞`, so it is bounded. Then
```
f_M(s)/s = M̃(s)·((s−1)/s)·ζ(s)/s = M̃(s)·[−((s−1)/s)h̃(s) + 1/s].
```
Both `h̃` and `1/s` lie in `H²(Ω)`, and `(s−1)/s` and `M̃` are bounded analytic on `Ω`. So `f_M/s ∈ H²(Ω)`, and by the
unitary transfer `f_M ∈ H²(μ)`. The value at `1` is the residue computation `ζ(s)M(s) = (s−1)⁻¹(M′(1)(s−1) + …)`. ∎

## §2 The exact mollified Jensen identity (restatement of NB/BSY, derived from the mollifier side)

For a zero `ρ` with `Re ρ > ½`, put `ℓ(ρ) := log|ρ/(ρ−1)| > 0`. It is positive because `|ρ| > |ρ−1| ⟺ Re ρ > ½`, and
`ℓ(ρ) = −log|φ(ρ)|`. Define
```
S_ζ := Σ_{ζ(ρ)=0, Re ρ>½} ℓ(ρ) (with multiplicity; S_ζ = 0 ⟺ RH, by the functional equation),
S*_M := Σ_{M̃(ρ)=0, Re ρ>½} ℓ(ρ) (zeros of M in Ω other than one copy of s = 1),
Q(M) := ‖ζM‖²_{L²(μ)} / |M′(1)|², J(M) := ½log ∫|f_M|²dμ − ∫log|f_M|dμ (Jensen gap),
σ_s(M) := −log|S_M(1)| ≥ 0 (the singular inner factor S_M of f_M, evaluated at 1).
```

**Theorem M1 (exact identity with four nonnegative terms; PROVED).** For every `M ∈ 𝒟₀` with `M′(1) ≠ 0`,
```
½ log Q(M) = S_ζ + S*_M + σ_s(M) + J(M), all four terms ≥ 0,
```
and `Q(M) = 1/(1 − d(M)²)`, where `d(M) := dist_{L²(μ)}(1, ℂ·ζM)`.

*Proof.* Write `f = f_M ∈ H²(μ)` (Lemma 0) with canonical factorisation `f = B·S_M·O`. Its zeros in `Ω` are those of `ζ`
(none at `s = 1`, where `f(1) = M′(1) ≠ 0`) together with those of `M̃`. So
`−log|B(1)| = Σ −log|φ(ρ)| = S_ζ + S*_M`. The outer factor satisfies `log|O(1)| = ∫log|f|dμ`. Hence
`log|f(1)| = −S_ζ − S*_M − σ_s(M) + ∫log|f|dμ`. Adding `J(M)` to both sides and using `f(1) = M′(1)` gives the identity.
The gap `J ≥ 0` is Jensen's inequality for the concave function `log`. For the last claim, `f(1) = ⟨f, 1⟩` gives
`d(M)² = 1 − |⟨1,f⟩|²/‖f‖² = 1 − 1/Q(M)`. ∎

**Corollary M2 (the mollifier criterion).** Put `d_N := inf{d(M) : M ∈ 𝒟₀, length ≤ N}`.
* (i) `S_ζ ≤ −½ log(1 − d_N²)` for every `N`, equivalently `d_N² ≥ 1 − Π_{Re ρ>½}|(ρ−1)/ρ|²`. **PROVED**, from M1. The
  inequality is classical in the NB literature (Nikolski 1995 and Burnol), so this is **not new**.
* (ii) `d_N → 0 ⟹ RH`. **PROVED**, from (i) and the functional equation.
* (iii) `RH ⟹ d_N → 0`. **CITED**: Báez-Duarte, *Atti Accad. Naz. Lincei* 14 (2003), in the equivalent form
  `1/s ∈ closure{ζ(s)M(s)/s : M ∈ 𝒟₀}` in `H²(Ω)`. The unitary transfer turns this into `1 ∈ [ζ𝒟₀]` in `H²(μ)`.
* Hence **RH ⟺ inf_{M∈𝒟₀} Q(M) = 1 ⟺ `d_N → 0`**, and the mollifier route in exact form is **EQUIVALENT** (R157_002
  rows 43 and 57).

**Proposition M3 (no finite mollifier certifies; PROVED).** `d_N > 0` for every `N`. Equivalently,
`inf_{len ≤ N} Q > 1`, so the Jensen side is never closed at finite length.

*Proof.* The set `{ζM : M ∈ 𝒟₀, len ≤ N}` is a finite-dimensional, hence closed, subspace of `L²(μ)`, so `d_N` is
attained. If `d_N = 0`, then `ζM = 1` for some `M`, and this contradicts `ζ(½ + iγ) = 0` at Hardy's zeros. ∎

Quantitatively, Burnol (*Adv. Math.* 170 (2002)) gives `liminf d_N² log N ≥ Σ_{Re ρ=½} m_ρ²/|ρ|² > 0`, unconditionally.
This is **CITED** (as recalled; not re-verified) and is the "second no-margin law" of R157_002 row 43. M3 is the
mollifier analogue of `thm:finZ` and `thm:arena`: no truncation decides. Here the truncation is the length of the
mollifier, not a set of zeros or modes.

**Where "100 %" is forced (the step asked for; PROVED as a location, EQUIVALENT as content).** By M1, the statement
`inf Q = 1` holds exactly when one sequence `M_N` drives three terms to 0 at once:
* `S*_{M_N} → 0`: the mollifier's own zeros are pushed onto the line;
* `σ_s → 0`: no singular inner mass appears;
* `J(M_N) → 0`: `log|ζM_N|` becomes asymptotically constant in `L¹(μ)`.

Together these say `ζM_N → 1` in `H²(μ)`, i.e. `ζ𝒟₀` is cyclic. That single step is the whole of RH (M2(iii)). There
is no slack elsewhere, because M1 is an identity. Levinson's method loses at two further places:
* Jensen is applied on `σ₀ = ½ − R/L`, not at `σ = ½` with the Poisson weight;
* the mollified moment is an *asymptotic* evaluation, and that loss is Paper 33's `c(θ) > 1`.

**Proposition M4 (the density version is excluded; PROVED from the record).** Any hypothesis of the form
"`N₀(T)/N(T) → 1`", i.e. Levinson–Conrey at 100 %, including Farmer's `θ = ∞` consequence, is consistent with the
existence of one off-line zero. By O2 (R157_002 §1.2, via `cor:margin2`(iii) and P45 Thm 10.4) it therefore does not
imply `∀T: λ^A_T ≥ 0`, and a fortiori not `∀T: λ^W_T ≥ 0`. **EXCLUDED.** *Proof:* one off-line quadruple changes
`N₀(T)` by `O(1)`. ∎

## §3 Theorem N — the mollifier closure does not see the comb (NEW)

Let `A_½` be the Banach algebra of Dirichlet series `D(s) = Σ d_n n^{−s}` with `‖D‖ := Σ|d_n|n^{−½} < ∞`, under the
sup norm on `Re s ≥ ½` (which is at most `‖D‖`). Define the class
```
𝒞_E := { a : n ↦ a(n) ≥ 0 on n ≥ 2, ζ_a := exp(Σ a(n)n^{−s}/log n) = ζ·exp(D_a) with D_a ∈ A_½ }.
```
It contains `Λ` (with `D = 0`) and both modifications of `thm:finmod`:
* deletion `Λ·1_{n≥N}`, with `D = −Σ_{n<N}Λ(n)n^{−s}/log n`;
* amplification `Λ + c·1_{n=n₀}`, with `D = c·n₀^{−s}/log n₀`.

Hence it also contains the members of `𝒞_PNT` and `𝒞_zeros` produced in the proof of `cor:void`.

**Theorem N (PROVED).** For every `a ∈ 𝒞_E`, `ζ_a𝒟₀ ⊂ H²(μ)`, and the closed subspaces coincide:
```
[ζ_a𝒟₀] = [ζ𝒟₀] in H²(μ).
```
Consequently `d_N(a) → d_∞`, with `d_∞ := dist(1, [ζ𝒟₀])` the same number for every `a ∈ 𝒞_E`. So does every functional
of the closure: `inf Q_a`, the limit Jensen data, and `S_ζ` itself, which is the same because `ζ_a` has the zeros of `ζ`.

*Proof.* `E := exp(D_a)` and `E⁻¹ = exp(−D_a)` lie in `A_½`, because `A_½` is a Banach algebra and `exp` converges in it.
So both are bounded analytic on `Ω` and continuous up to the line. For `M ∈ 𝒟₀`, `ζ_aM = (ζM)·E ∈ H²(μ)` by Lemma 0.

For the inclusion `⊂`: `EM ∈ A_½` vanishes at `1`. Let `P_K` be its partial sums, so `P_K → EM` uniformly on `Re s ≥ ½`
and `P_K(1) → 0`. Then `P_K − P_K(1) ∈ 𝒟₀`, it converges uniformly to `EM`, and
```
‖ζ(P_K − P_K(1)) − ζ_aM‖_{L²(μ)} ≤ ‖ζ‖_{L²(μ)} · sup_{Re s=½}|P_K − P_K(1) − EM| → 0.
```
Here `‖ζ‖_{L²(μ)} < ∞` because `∫_0^X|ζ(½+it)|²dt ≪ X log X` (Hardy–Littlewood). So `ζ_aM ∈ [ζ𝒟₀]`.

For the reverse inclusion, apply the same argument with `E⁻¹` and the roles exchanged. ∎

**Corollary N1 (the mollifier is window-blind; PROVED, with the citations shown).** Let `𝔐` be any statement, number or
functional that depends on the comb `a` only through the closed subspace `[ζ_a𝒟₀]`. This covers the whole exact
mollifier mechanism of §2, including every limit of Levinson–Conrey or NB type. Then `𝔐(a) = 𝔐(Λ)` on `𝒞_E`, and
`𝔐` determines the sign of no window form:
* (i) *primitive form*, `T ∈ (½log2, log2]`: `λ^A_T(Λ) ≥ 0` (Paper 42, of record), while
  `a = Λ + c·1_{n=2} ∈ 𝒞_E` has `λ^A_T(a) < 0` for `c > c_*(T,2)` (`thm:finmod`(ii));
* (ii) *complete form*, `T ∈ (½log2, 0.8]`: `λ^W_T(Λ) ≥ 0` (Zhu 2026, **CITED**, not re-verified), while the same `a` has
  `λ^W_T(a) ≤ λ^A_T(a) < 0`;
* (iii) at any `T > 0.8`, the same pair shows that `𝔐` cannot decide the sign unless the sign for `Λ` is already
  known, and it is not.

*Proof.* Theorem N plus the cited signs. ∎

**Corollary N2 (placement in a proof of the `∀T` arrow; PROVED as a statement about derivations, in the sense of P46
Thm 18.1 / Rem 18.1a and `cor:void`).** A derivation of `λ^W_T ≥ 0` or of `λ^A_T ≥ 0` must use, at a load-bearing step,
something that separates `Λ` from `Λ + c·1_{n=2}`. Mollifier data cannot be that step (N1). The available separator is
the functional equation, which `ζ_a` lacks (`thm:finmod`(iii)); this is item A3 of F4. The only way a mollifier enters
is therefore
```
[ζ𝒟₀] cyclic ⟹(M2) S_ζ = 0 (uses the FE) ⟹ RH ⟹(Weil) ∀T: λ^W_T ≥ 0 ⟹ ∀T: λ^A_T ≥ 0.
```
That is RH first. It is not a mechanism for the arrow, and at finite length it delivers nothing at any window (M3,
N1). **Status of the angle: EQUIVALENT (exact form), EXCLUDED (density form); no per-window content.**

*Why Theorem N is the right lens.* O3 (`rem:resweights`) says that at a fixed window a change of one weight by
`≈ e^{−14.4T}` flips the sign. Theorem N says the mollifier closure is invariant under changes of **any** size within
`𝒞_E`. The two statements are compatible, and together they are decisive: the object of the mollifier method lives
entirely on the zero side of the explicit formula, modulo outer functions. The window sign lives in the interaction
of the comb with the Γ-factor and the pole, which is where the functional equation enters. This is the mollifier
instance of the rule in `rem:twohalves`, clause (a).

## §4 The mollified explicit formula for the Weil form on windows (the literal reading of the angle)

For real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` and real `M = Σ_{n≤N}a_n n^{−s}`, put `F_M(t) := Σ_n a_n n^{−½}F(t + log n)`.

**Proposition W-M (PROVED).**
* (i) `Φ_{F_M}(s) = M(s)Φ_F(s)`.
* (ii) `W` is translation invariant, `W(F(·−c)) = W(F)`, so `F_M` centred by `½log N` is a test on `I_{T+½log N}`, and
  ```
  W_{T+½log N}(F_M) = Σ_ρ M(ρ)M(1−ρ)Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ) (absolutely convergent).
  ```
* (iii) Prime side: `c_{F_M}(u) = Σ_{m,n} a_m a_n (mn)^{−½} c_F(u + log(n/m))`, so the comb acts at the contacts
  `log(kn/m)`. This is the Hecke-type action of the correspondences of row A.
* (iv) Under RH, the zero side equals `Σ_γ |M(½+iγ)|²|Φ_F(½+iγ)|² ≥ 0`.

*Proof.* (i): `Φ_{F(·+log n)}(s) = e^{−(s−½)log n}Φ_F(s) = n^{½−s}Φ_F(s)`. (ii): `Φ_{F(·−c)}(s) = e^{(s−½)c}Φ_F(s)`, and the
factors `e^{(ρ−½)c}` and `e^{(½−ρ)c}` cancel. Then apply `eq:W` and `thm:Z`'s explicit formula to the smooth test.
(iii): expand the autocorrelation. (iv): for real data, `M(½−iγ)` is the conjugate of `M(½+iγ)`, and so is
`Φ_F(1−ρ)` of `Φ_F(ρ)`. ∎

**Remark W-M′ (what it gives: nothing beyond E1; PROVED).**
* The mollified form is the complete window form restricted to the subspace `S_{T,N} = {F_M}`. The span of
  `∪_N S_{T,N}` contains all finite sums of `log n`-translates of `I_T`-tests, so positivity on it for all `N` is
  E1 = RH.
* Mollification can only **hide** zeros. If `ρ₀` were an off-line zero, any `M` with `M(ρ₀) = 0` makes the whole
  quadruple `{ρ₀, 1−ρ₀, ρ̄₀, 1−ρ̄₀}` drop out of (ii); for real `a`, one complex condition suffices. So positivity on
  mollified tests is *weaker* than positivity, and never a certificate for it.
* Levinson's design, `M ≈ 1/ζ`, points the wrong way here: it enlarges the weights `|M(ρ)|²` near zeros instead of
  controlling their sign.
* Proposition W-M does **not** escape `thm:finZ`, `lem:Rrig` or `thm:arena`, because it is literally `W_{T′}` on a
  subspace.

**Remark (the "100 random zeros", from the mollifier side; HEURISTIC).** The Jensen detector of M1 is
*termwise* signed: `ℓ(ρ) ≥ 0`, additive over zeros, and 0 exactly on the line. In the Weil case every flat-top kernel
is never termwise (`lem:Rrig`) and no finite zero set certifies (`thm:finZ`). So on the zero side the Jensen frame
does let a finite, even random, subset of zeros "certify itself". The price is paid on the other side: `∫log|ζ|dμ` has
no convergent prime-side expansion (`log ζ(½+it)` is not given by its Euler series), and the only exact arithmetic
access is the mean square `‖ζM‖²`. That mean square is an explicit finite Gram form in the `a_n` (Vasyunin 1995, with
cotangent sums; **CITED**, as recalled). Its optimisers are `a_n ≈ μ(n)(1 − log n/log N)` (Bettin–Conrey–Farmer 2013,
**CITED**, conditional on RH and more). Termwise positivity on the zero side and a prime-side evaluation appear to
exclude each other. This rhymes with `lem:Rrig` + `thm:Z` and is **not proved** as a theorem here: it is **OPEN**
(O-B10.1 below).

## §7 Open items raised

* **O-B10.1 (OPEN).** Make the heuristic of §4 a theorem: no linear functional of the zero measure is at once
  termwise nonnegative, 0 exactly on the line, and given by a Weil-admissible test, i.e. with a prime side that
  converges on the window. A proof would put BSY/NB and `lem:Rrig` under one statement.
* **O-B10.2 (OPEN, optional).** Is `inf_{len≤N} Q_a` monotone or continuous in `a ∈ 𝒞_E` at fixed `N`? Theorem N
  controls only the limit. A finite-`N` sensitivity to the comb would be the only place where the mollifier could
  carry window information, and by N1 it would have to be sign-free.

## Weakest step

The novelty and the scope of **Theorem N** are the weakest points.
* The proof is short and standard: a Banach-algebra `exp` plus uniform approximation in `L²(μ)`. A panel should
  check two things:
  * that `‖ζ‖_{L²(μ)} < ∞` is used correctly;
  * that "depends only on `[ζ_a𝒟₀]`" really covers every exact-limit mollifier statement. It does **not** cover
  finite-`N` mollified quantities (O-B10.2), which by M3 and Burnol decide nothing anyway.
* The results cited from memory and not re-verified here:
  * Báez-Duarte's direction M2(iii);
  * Burnol's constant;
  * Vasyunin's Gram formula;
  * Bettin–Conrey–Farmer's optimiser;
  * Zhu's `T ≤ 0.8`.

  None of them is needed for Theorem N or for N1(i).
