## 1. Closure of the angle (restatement; recorded once)

**Fact 1.1 (CITED: de Branges, *Hilbert spaces of entire functions*, Thms 35–40; Winkler, Romanov's survey on the
inverse theorem).** Every trace-normed Hamiltonian `H ≥ 0`, locally integrable on `[0, ∞)`, is the Hamiltonian of a
canonical system whose spectral measure is unique up to the standard normalisation. Conversely, every positive
spectral datum of the relevant class has exactly one such `H`.

**Consequence (PROVED from Fact 1.1 and G2_B5_R1 Thm B).** "`H(t) ≥ 0` for all time" is **not a condition**. It holds
for every canonical system. The question of the angle is whether the chain attached to the exact datum `u_Λ` exists
up to time `σ(T)`. For each `T` that is `λ^W_T > 0` (up to the boundary case), and for all `T` it is `thm:KEP3`(b),
which is EQUIVALENT to RH. G2_B5_R2 Theorem L (localisation) and G2_B5_R3 Lemma G (inverse rigidity) add the
following. A finite segment carries no sign forward. The segment determines `{Λ(n) : n < e^{2T}}` exactly. So a
"Hamiltonian built from the exact `Λ(n)`" is the datum itself, rewritten.

**What is not a restatement.** Only quantitative statements about the **defect** `M(T)`, the minimal shift
`u_Λ ↦ u_Λ + M δ` for which the chain exists on `[0, σ(T)]`. `thm:KEP3` is the case `M ≡ 0`. The graded converse
(round 4, all memos) gives `limsup`-growth of `M` against zero-free half-planes. Theorem Q♯ below gives the
every-`T` lower bound. Neither proves a sign. **The angle is closed as a mechanism.**

## 2. Theorem Q♯: no recovery windows

### 2.1 The two-bump identity and the zero-side shift function

Fix a real, even `g ∈ C_c^∞(−1/8, 1/8)`, and put `k = g ∗ g` (even, `supp k ⊂ (−¼, ¼)`) and
`G(s) = ∫ g(w)e^{sw}dw` (entire and even, real on `ℝ`, with `G(s̄) = conj G(s)`). Define
`U(y) := ⟨u_Λ, k(· − y)⟩` and `V(y) := ⟨u_Λ, (P²k)(· − y)⟩`. Both are even in `y`, because `u_Λ` and `k` are even.

**Lemma 2.1 (two-bump identity; PROVED).** Let `y ≥ ¼`, `T > y/2 + 1/8`, `φ_± := g(· ∓ y/2)` and `Γ := φ_+ + φ_−`.
* (complete) `Γ ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, `‖Γ‖² = 2‖g‖²`, and `W_T(Γ) = 2U(0) + 2U(y)`.
* (primitive) `PΓ ∈ P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`, `‖PΓ‖² = 2(P²k)(0)`, and `𝔞_T(PΓ) = 2V(0) + 2V(y)`.

Hence `M_W(T) ≥ −(U(0) + U(y))/‖g‖²` and `M_A(T) ≥ −(V(0) + V(y))/(P²k)(0)`.

*Proof.* The supports are `(±y/2 − 1/8, ±y/2 + 1/8) ⊂ I_T`. We have `c_Γ = 2k + k(· − y) + k(· + y)`. The cross terms
of `‖Γ‖² = c_Γ(0)` are `2k(y) = 0`, since `y ≥ ¼`. By (1.1) and evenness of `u_Λ`, `W_T(Γ) = 2U(0) + 2U(y)`. For the
primitive form, `A(PΓ) = B(PΓ) = 0` by two integrations by parts (`P e^{±t/2} = 0`; P46 `prop:finsupp`). Also
`c_{PΓ} = P²c_Γ`, since `P` is even with constant coefficients. So `‖PΓ‖² = 2(P²k)(0) + 2(P²k)(y) = 2(P²k)(0)`, because
`supp P²k ⊂ (−¼, ¼)`, and `𝔞_T(PΓ) = ⟨u_Λ, P²c_Γ⟩ = 2V(0) + 2V(y)`. Finally, `(P²k)(0) = ‖Pg‖² > 0`. The bounds on
`M` follow from the definition of the bottoms. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (zero-side expansion; PROVED, with the Weil explicit formula CITED).** For every real `y`,

`U(y) = Σ_ρ G(ρ−½)² e^{(ρ−½)y}` and `V(y) = Σ_ρ a_ρ e^{(ρ−½)y}`, where `a_ρ := ρ²(1−ρ)²G(ρ−½)²`.

The sums run over the nontrivial zeros with multiplicity and converge absolutely, locally uniformly in `y`.

*Proof.* The function `h_y := ½[(P²k)(· − y) + (P²k)(· + y)]` is even and lies in `C_c^∞`, and by evenness of `u_Λ`,
`V(y) = ⟨u_Λ, h_y⟩`. The Weil explicit formula (CITED: Weil 1952; Bombieri 2000 §2; Iwaniec–Kowalski Thm 5.12; it is
the unconditional identity behind P46 `eq:W`/`thm:Z`) gives `⟨u_Λ, h⟩ = Σ_ρ H(ρ − ½)` for even `h ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)`, with
`H(s) = ∫h(u)e^{su}du`. Here `H(s) = cosh(sy)·∫(P²k)e^{su}du = cosh(sy)(¼ − s²)²G(s)²`, and at `s = ρ − ½` we have
`¼ − s² = ρ(1 − ρ)`. The zero set is invariant under `ρ ↦ 1 − ρ`, which sends `s ↦ −s`, and `a_ρ` is invariant under
it. So `Σ a_ρ cosh(s_ρ y) = Σ a_ρ e^{s_ρ y}`. Absolute convergence: `|G(β + iγ)| ≤ C_N e^{|β|/8}(1 + |γ|)^{−N}` for
every `N`, and `N(t) ≪ t log t`. The same argument with `k` in place of `P²k` gives `U`. ∎

### 2.2 The theorem

**Theorem Q♯ (PROVED; primitive and complete forms).** Suppose `Θ := sup{Re ρ} > ½` and some zero `ρ₀` has
`Re ρ₀ = Θ`. Put `β* := Θ − ½ > 0`. Then there are `c > 0` and `T₁` such that, for **every** `T ≥ T₁`,

`−λ^A_T ≥ c·e^{2β*T}` (primitive) and hence `−λ^W_T ≥ c·e^{2β*T}` (complete).

*Proof.* **Step 0 (bump).** As in G2_B5_R4 Step 0, rescale a fixed bump so that `G(ρ₀ − ½) ≠ 0`. This is possible
because `c ↦ G₁(c(ρ₀ − ½))` is entire and equals `1` at `c = 0`. Then `a_{ρ₀} ≠ 0`, because `ρ₀ ∉ {0, 1}`.

**Step 1 (split).** Let `Z_Θ := {ρ : Re ρ = Θ}`. It is nonempty and closed under conjugation. For `y > 0` write
`V(y) = e^{β*y}P(y) + R(y)`, with `P(y) := Σ_{ρ∈Z_Θ} a_ρ e^{iγ_ρ y}` and `R(y) := Σ_{ρ∉Z_Θ} a_ρ e^{(ρ−½)y}`.

**Step 2 (`P` is a nonzero, real, mean-zero Bohr almost periodic function).**
* `Σ|a_ρ| < ∞` (Lemma 2.2), so `P` is a uniform limit of trigonometric polynomials and is Bohr almost periodic.
* It is real, because `a_{ρ̄} = conj a_ρ` (`G(s̄) = conj G(s)`, and `ρ̄(1 − ρ̄) = conj(ρ(1 − ρ))`).
* **Every frequency `γ_ρ`, `ρ ∈ Z_Θ`, is nonzero.** A zero with `γ = 0` would be a real zero in `(½, 1)`. But ζ has no
  real zeros in `(0, 1)`: `(1 − 2^{1−σ})ζ(σ) = Σ(−1)^{n−1}n^{−σ} > 0` there (CITED, classical). Hence the Bohr mean is
  `M(P) = 0`.
* The Bohr coefficient of `P` at the frequency `γ₀ = Im ρ₀` is `a_{ρ₀}`, counted with multiplicity. Only one point of
  the line `Re = Θ` has ordinate `γ₀`. So this coefficient is nonzero, and `P ≢ 0`.

A real, continuous, almost periodic `P ≢ 0` with `M(P) = 0` takes a negative value. If `P ≥ 0`, then `P > η > 0` on
some interval, hence on a relatively dense set of translates of it, and `M(P) > 0`. Fix `y₁` with `P(y₁) = −2δ < 0`.
By Bohr's theorem (CITED: Besicovitch, *Almost periodic functions*, Ch. I), the set `E_δ` of `δ`-almost periods is
relatively dense, with some inclusion length `L`. So for every `X` there is `τ ∈ E_δ ∩ [X − L − y₁, X − y₁]`, and
`y := y₁ + τ ∈ [X − L, X]` satisfies `P(y) ≤ P(y₁) + δ = −δ`.

**Step 3 (`R` is lower order).** `e^{−β*y}|R(y)| ≤ Σ_{ρ∉Z_Θ} |a_ρ| e^{(Re ρ − Θ)y}`. Each term tends to `0` as
`y → ∞`, because `Re ρ < Θ` off `Z_Θ`, and each is at most `|a_ρ|` for `y ≥ 0`. By dominated convergence,
`R(y) = o(e^{β*y})`. Choose `Y₀` with `|R(y)| ≤ (δ/2)e^{β*y}` for `y ≥ Y₀`.

**Step 4 (every window).** Let `T ≥ T₁ := (Y₀ + L + 1)/2 + 1`, and set `X := 2T − ½`. Step 2 gives
`y ∈ [X − L, X]` with `y ≥ Y₀ ≥ ¼` and `V(y) ≤ −δe^{β*y} + (δ/2)e^{β*y} = −(δ/2)e^{β*y}`. Also
`y/2 + 1/8 ≤ T − 1/8 < T`, so Lemma 2.1 applies at window `T`:
`M_A(T) ≥ [(δ/2)e^{β*(2T − ½ − L)} − V(0)]/(P²k)(0)`.
This is `≥ c·e^{2β*T}` with `c := δe^{−β*(½+L)}/(4(P²k)(0))` once `T` is large, since `V(0)` is a fixed number.
Enlarge `T₁` if needed. The complete form follows from `M_W ≥ M_A`. It can also be proved directly, with `U`,
`G(ρ − ½)²` and `‖g‖²` in place of `V`, `a_ρ` and `(P²k)(0)`. ∎

### 2.3 Corollaries

**Corollary Q♯1 (the sequential converse, attained case; PROVED; both forms).** Suppose that for some `η ≥ 0` and some
sequence `T_j → ∞`, `M_A(T_j) = O(e^{2ηT_j})` (or the same for `M_W`). Then no zero lies on the line
`Re ρ = Θ` unless `Θ ≤ ½ + η`. Equivalently: either `Θ ≤ ½ + η`, or the supremum `Θ` is not attained.
*Proof.* This is the contrapositive of Q♯ with `β* > η`. ∎
This answers, in the attained case, the question G2_B5_R4 §3 left OPEN ("a bound along a sequence `T_j → ∞`").
The **non-attained case is OPEN**. There, zeros accumulate at `Re = Θ` from the left, the dominant part is no longer
`e^{β*y}` times an almost periodic function, and a one-sided oscillation theorem in every interval of bounded length
would be needed. That is a Kaczorowski–Pintz / Turán power-sum type statement (HEURISTIC pointer, not verified here).

**Corollary Q♯2 (two-sided law, conditional; PROVED from Q♯ and B2_R4 Theorem U).** Under B2's hypothesis (BD) and
attainment of `Θ`, `log M_•(T) = 2(Θ − ½)T + O(log T)` for every large `T`, in both forms. The limit
`lim (2T)^{−1}log M_•(T) = Θ − ½` exists. (BD) is OPEN. Attainment is OPEN.

**Reading in window time.** `M(T)` is nondecreasing (`lem:monoT`), so a negative window never recovers. That much is
trivial. Q♯ adds that, in the attained case, the defect grows at its full exponential rate at **every** window, with
no stretches of slow growth. In the canonical-system language, the regularising shift needed for the chain to reach
time `σ(T)` is `≍ e^{2(Θ−½)T}` from below, uniformly in `T`, and not merely along a sequence.

### 2.4 Where ζ-specific information enters, and the falsifier battery

Step 2 uses one arithmetic fact about ζ: **no real zeros in `(½, 1)`**. It is not a consequence of the functional
equation, reality, the pole or nonnegative coefficients. Epstein zeta functions of class number `> 1` share all of
these (P47 T2 = Thm 18.16), and some of them have real zeros in `(½, 1)`. For a datum with a real off-line zero
`β₀ = Θ`, the term `a_{β₀}e^{(β₀−½)y}` has `a_{β₀} = β₀²(1 − β₀)²G(β₀ − ½)² > 0` (`G` is real on `ℝ`). The dominant
part is then a positive constant plus an almost periodic function, and Step 2 fails. **The proof of Q♯ fails there, as
it should.** Such data are negative on some window by other arguments (Theorem Q, and T2), but Q♯'s "every window" rate
is not obtained this way.

For the other falsifiers of the record (`Ξ_a`, DMV/Zhang Beurling systems, `ζ·E`, `Ξ_Q`), Q♯ applies verbatim whenever
the datum has an explicit formula with absolutely summable zero side, an attained supremum, and no real zero on it. It
asserts **negativity**, never positivity, so no falsifier is given a false sign. For `ζ·E` (`thm:finmod`(iii)), the
zeros are those of ζ, together with those of `E`, which lie left of `Re s = ½`. The conclusion is the same as for ζ,
consistent with its negative windows.

This is an **F4-type ζ-specific ingredient in a converse**. It does not help the direct arrow. It is recorded because it
is the first place in the G2 round-4/5 material where an input beyond "Dirichlet series plus functional equation"
changes a conclusion.

## 3. Consolidated record lemma (the panel's merge request) and corrections

**Record Lemma GC (graded Weil converse; PROVED; primitive and complete forms).** Normalise
`κ^•_+ := limsup_{T→∞}(2T)^{−1}log⁺ M_•(T)` and `κ^•_− := liminf_{T→∞}(2T)^{−1}log⁺ M_•(T)`. Then:

(i) `Θ − ½ ≤ κ^A_+ ≤ κ^W_+ ≤ ½`.
(ii) If `Θ > ½` is attained, then `Θ − ½ ≤ κ^A_− ≤ κ^W_−` (Q♯).
(iii) `RH ⟺ κ^A_+ = 0 ⟺ κ^W_+ = 0` (**EQUIVALENT**; Weil plus von Koch–Landau).
(iv) `κ^•_±` is invariant under comb perturbations `b ∈ ℓ¹_w` (G2_B5_R4 Prop Q2).
(v) Any proved bound `κ^A_+ ≤ ½ − δ/2` is the zero-free strip `Re s > 1 − δ/2` (quasi-RH; the output dual of O2).

Proofs and credits. For (i), the cleanest proof is B2_R4 Thm G1: two-sided transport, no Landau step, class
`Σ|b|n^{−1/2−α} < ∞`. The same bound was proved concurrently by B1_R4 Thm Q (Cauchy–Schwarz and Landau), B3_R4
(two-bump and Landau), G2_B5_R4 Thm Q (Gram inequality and Landau), B9_R4 Thm G and B10_R4 Thm Γ. The upper bound
`½` is the trivial bound, G2_B5_R4 Lemma 1.2. B10 and B9 normalise by `T^{−1}`, which doubles every exponent: `2Θ − 1 ≤ κ ≤ 1`.
(ii) is §2 of this memo.

**Corrections to G2_B5_R4 (withdrawn or amended).**
* The claim that `e^{2ηT}` is "the sharper rate" than P5's `e^{4εT}` is **withdrawn**. P5's statement,
  "`M ≤ Ce^{4εT}` ⟹ `Θ ≤ ½ + ε`", has a weaker hypothesis and would be the stronger theorem. It is **not provable**:
  by B1_R4 Prop Q2, a zero at `½ + β` with `ε < β < 2ε` gives `M ≍ e^{2βT} ≤ e^{4εT}`. The correct and sharp statement
  is (i).
* The de Branges reading of `M(T)` (the "minimal shift for the chain") stays **interpretive**. No proof above uses it.
* The Beurling-genericity remark of R4 §3 should read "rightmost singularity of `−D_a`", not "rightmost zero" (P5_R4).

**Weakest step.** The explicit formula of Lemma 2.2 for the translated test `h_y`. It is CITED in the standard

`W(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`. A normalisation slip would change `a_ρ` by a nonvanishing factor and would not affect

**State of the angle.** Closed as a mechanism after five rounds. The canonical system in window time is a faithful
recoding of the datum (R1–R3). Its existence for all time is RH (`thm:KEP3`(b)). Its failure, if RH is false, is
quantitatively rigid (Lemma GC, Theorem Q♯). **No sign is claimed at any window `T > 0.8`.**
