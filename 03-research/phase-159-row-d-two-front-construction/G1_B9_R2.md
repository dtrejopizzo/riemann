## §1 The closing step: Landau, not rationality (Theorem 1)

**Theorem 1 (`PROVED` from `CLASSICAL`; Landau 1905 / Ingham Ch. V).** Let `½ ≤ σ < 1`. The following are equivalent:
(i) `ζ(s) ≠ 0` for `Re s > σ`;
(ii) for every `ε > 0`, `ψ(x) ≤ x + O_ε(x^{σ+ε})` (upper bound only);
(iii) for every `ε > 0`, `ψ(x) ≥ x − O_ε(x^{σ+ε})` (lower bound only).
In particular, **RH ⟺ one-sided von Koch**. `EQUIVALENT`.

*Proof.* (i) ⟹ (ii), (iii): the classical two-sided bound (truncated explicit formula). For (ii) ⟹ (i), fix
`ε > 0`, write `σ′ = σ + ε`, and pick `C`, `x_0` with `F(x) := x + Cx^{σ′} − ψ(x) ≥ 0` for `x ≥ x_0`. For `Re s > 1`,
`∫_1^∞ ψ(x)x^{−s−1}dx = −ζ′(s)/(sζ(s))`, `∫_1^∞ x^{−s}dx = 1/(s−1)`, and `∫_1^∞ Cx^{σ′−s−1}dx = C/(s−σ′)`. So
`H(s) := ∫_{x_0}^∞ F(x)x^{−s−1}dx = 1/(s−1) + C/(s−σ′) + ζ′(s)/(sζ(s)) + E(s)`, with `E` entire, since it is the
integral over `[1, x_0]`.
- At `s = 1`: `ζ′/ζ = −1/(s−1) + O(1)`, so the pole cancels.
- On the real segment `(σ′, 1]`: `ζ(s) < 0` there (from `(1 − 2^{1−s})ζ(s) = η(s) > 0`, an alternating series), and
  `s ≠ 0`. So `H` continues analytically to a neighbourhood of `(σ′, ∞)`.
- Landau's theorem: a Mellin integral with a nonnegative integrand is singular at the real point of its abscissa of
  convergence. So the abscissa is `≤ σ′`, and `H` is holomorphic on `Re s > σ′`.

Hence `ζ′/ζ` is holomorphic there, i.e. there is no zero with `Re ρ > σ + ε`. Let `ε → 0`.
(iii) ⟹ (i) is the same argument with `F(x) := ψ(x) − x + Cx^{σ′}`, whose transform has the opposite polar sign. ∎

**Remark 1.1 (the closing uses only three analytic facts about ζ, all `PROVED`).** They are: meromorphy of `ζ′/ζ`
on `Re s > σ`; the pole at `1` with residue matching the main term; no real zero in `(σ, 1)`. There is no finite zero
set, no rationality and no window. The function-field counterpart is Pringsheim for the rational generating function
`Σ s_m z^m` together with Bombieri's twist step, which excludes a real Frobenius eigenvalue `> √q`. The ζ counterpart
of the twist step is `ζ(σ) < 0` on `(0,1)`.

**Prop 1.2 (`thm:finZ` does not apply; `PROVED`).** The input of Theorem 1(ii) is an inequality for all large `x`,
not a zero set. `thm:finZ` concerns finite symmetric zero multisets carrying a flat-top certificate. The Landau
closing produces no certificate at any window. It yields the zero statement, and `∀T λ^W_T ≥ 0` then follows by
Weil's criterion (E1). ∎

**Prop 1.3 (`cor:void` does not apply; `PROVED`).** Let `a` be a deletion or amplification falsifier of
`thm:finmod`: `a = Λ` except at finitely many `n`. Then `ψ_a − ψ` is eventually constant, so `a` satisfies 1(ii)
iff `Λ` does. Moreover `−ζ_a′/ζ_a = −ζ′/ζ + D`, where `D` is a finite Dirichlet polynomial, which is entire. Theorem 1
applied to `a` therefore concludes a statement about the **zeros of ζ**. If RH holds, that statement is true for `a`.
So the route proves no false statement for any member of `C_BT` or `C_PNT`, and P46 T1 (Thm 18.1(ii)) is not
triggered. What fails for `a` is `RD_T(a)`, which the route never asserts: the Weil form of `a` is ζ's form plus a
finite, non-PD atomic perturbation. ∎

*Reading.* This is the precise sense in which the Stepanov route bypasses layer 5 of the map. It never
proves windows. It proves one large-`x` inequality and gets "∀T" from Weil's criterion. But that inequality is RH
(Theorem 1). **Nothing is gained in strength. What is gained is the location:** the RH content of this route is a
one-sided asymptotic count, and neither `cor:void` nor `thm:finZ` blocks it.

## §2 Anatomy of Bombieri–Stepanov (Theorem 2)

**Theorem 2 (`CITED`: Bombieri 1974; the decomposition is `PROVED` by inspection of that proof).** Let `C/F_q` be a
curve of genus `g`, with `q = p^{2a}` and `q > (g+1)^4`. Then `N_1 = #C(F_q) ≤ q + 1 + (2g+1)√q`. The proof uses
exactly:
- **(RR)** `ℓ(D) ≥ deg D + 1 − g`, and `= deg D + 1 − g` for `deg D > 2g − 2`. It is used to show that a space of
  auxiliary functions `Σ_i f_i^{p^a} g_i` (`f_i, g_i` in Riemann–Roch spaces of a point) is large enough that a nonzero
  element vanishes at every rational point `P ≠ P_0`;
- **(Fr)** for `f ∈ F_q(C)`, `f^q = f∘Fr` on `C(\bar F_q)`, and `d(f^{p^a}) = 0`. So an element of the form
  `Σ f_i^{p^a}g_i`, once it vanishes at `P`, vanishes there to order `≥ p^a = √q`. This is the amplification;
- **(deg)** the degree count: `(N_1 − 1)·p^a ≤ deg(poles) ≈ (m p^a + q)`-terms, optimised in `m`.

RH for `C` then follows by applying the bound to every `F_{q^m}` (all large `m`), from a lower bound for `N_m`
(Galois-cover step), and by the closing step (L).

**What is load-bearing.** (Fr) is what makes the vanishing order large at no cost in conditions. Without it, a
function vanishing at `N` points to order 1 gives only `N ≤ deg`, which is Riemann–Roch-trivial. The **size** of the
error, `(2g+1)√q`, comes from (RR) through `g`. The **validity range** `q > (g+1)^4` comes from balancing (RR) against
(Fr).

**Lemma 3 (no characteristic-0 amplification; `PROVED`).** Let `R` be a domain of characteristic 0, `D: R → R` a
derivation, `f ∈ R`, and `n ≥ 1`. If `D(f^n) = 0`, then `D(f) = 0`. Hence no derivation on `Z[x]`, `Q(x)` or any
characteristic-0 function field makes all `p`-th powers constants unless it is zero on the `p`-th roots involved.

*Proof.* `0 = D(f^n) = n f^{n−1}D(f)`, and `n ≠ 0` in `R`. If `f ≠ 0`, cancel `f^{n−1}` (domain). If `f = 0`,
`D(0) = 0`. ∎

**Remark 3.1 (`CITED` + `HEURISTIC`).** Buium's `p`-derivations `δ_p(x) = (φ_p(x) − x^p)/p` are the arithmetic
substitutes for (Fr). They exist one prime at a time and are not additive. A single auxiliary object on `Spec Z`
would need amplification at **every** prime `≤ x` at once, because `ψ(x)` counts all of them. That is a collective
requirement (compare `thm:SC`: positivity is collective). P46 Thm 22.17 (`Spec Z` is terminal, so there is no
Frobenius endomorphism) excludes the geometric form of (Fr). That no per-prime family `{δ_p}` can be assembled into a
global amplification is `HEURISTIC`. I have not proved it.

## §4 Decoupling of RR-genus and spectral genus (Theorem 4)

Over `F_q`, one integer `g` plays two roles:
- the Riemann–Roch defect, `h¹(𝒪_C) = g`;
- half the number of zeros, `dim H¹ = 2g`.

In the Stepanov error `(2g+1)√q`, `g` enters **through (RR)**, i.e. in the first role. It is correct **because** it
equals the second, since the true maximal fluctuation is `2g√q` (attained by maximal curves, e.g. Hermitian curves
over `F_{q}` with `q` a square).

**Theorem 4 (`PROVED` given Littlewood, `CITED`).**
(i) Let `G: [x_0,∞) → [0,∞)` satisfy `ψ(x) ≤ x + G(x)√x` for all `x ≥ x_0`. Then
`limsup G(x)/log log log x > 0`. In particular `G` is unbounded. The same holds for lower bounds.
(ii) (`CITED`: van der Geer–Schoof; Tate) Arakelov Riemann–Roch for `\overline{Spec Z}` reads
`h⁰(D) − h⁰(κ − D) = deg D − ½ log|Δ_Q| = deg D`. So the RR defect ("`(g − 1)`-term") of `\overline{Spec Z}` is
`0`. This matches R1's continuum limit of `(g − 1)η`, the conductor atom, which is `0` for ζ in `eq:gdatum`.
(iii) Consequently, any transport of Bombieri's inequality in which the error constant is an affine function of the
RR defect is **false** for ζ. With defect 0 it would give `ψ(x) ≤ x + c√x`, contradicting (i). The effective genus
must be an **unbounded** function of `x`. Under RH, `G(x) = (8π)^{−1}log² x` suffices (Schoenfeld). This is the ζ
analogue of `2g + 1`.

*Proof.* (i) Littlewood: `ψ(x) − x > c√x log log log x` for arbitrarily large `x`, and likewise with `<` and `−c`.
(ii) is the cited theorem, with `Δ_Q = 1`. (iii) follows from (i) and (ii). ∎

**Reading (sharpening of P46 Rem 21.23, "the arithmetic curve has infinite genus").** The statement splits in two:
- `\overline{Spec Z}` has RR defect `0`: its Riemann–Roch is exact (Poisson / theta);
- it has infinite **spectral** genus.

Stepanov needs the spectral genus to enter through RR. Over `Spec Z` the two are decoupled. This decoupling is the
precise obstruction to the literal transport. Rows 29 and 47 of R157_002 do not state it.

## §5 Finite auxiliaries are strictly lossy (Theorem 5)

This is the Z-side analogue of Stepanov's auxiliary functions **without** amplification: integer data, the product
formula ("a nonzero integer has `|·| ≥ 1`"), and the exact von Mangoldt identity.

**Setting.** Let `S ⊂ N` be finite, `c_k ∈ Z` (`k ∈ S`), `Σ_k c_k/k = 0`, and `u(t) := Σ_k c_k⌊t/k⌋`, so `u` is
bounded and periodic. The exact identity (Chebyshev) is `Σ_{n≥1} Λ(n)u(x/n) = Σ_k c_k log⌊x/k⌋!`, valid for all
`x > 0` with no truncation. It holds because `log⌊y⌋! = Σ_n Λ(n)⌊y/n⌋`, and then by linearity. By Stirling
(`log⌊y⌋! = y log y − y + O(log(y+2))`) and `Σ c_k/k = 0`, the right side is `A x + O(log x)` with
`A := −Σ_k c_k (log k)/k`.

**Theorem 5 (`PROVED` given Littlewood, `CITED`).**
(i) *(upper)* Suppose `u ≥ 0` on `(0,∞)` and `u ≥ 1` on `[1, a)` for some `a > 1`. Then
`ψ(x) ≤ B_+ x + O(log² x)` with `B_+ := A a/(a−1)`, and **`B_+ > 1` strictly**.
(ii) *(lower)* Suppose `u ≤ 1` on `(0,∞)`. Then `ψ(x) ≥ A x − O(log x)`, and **`A < 1` strictly**.
(iii) *(Gelfond–Schnirelman, `CITED`: Gorshkov; Pritsker Prop 1.3)* For every fixed weight `w` of Pritsker's class
(1.12), the multivariate integer-polynomial bound gives `∫_1^x ψ ≥ B(w)x²/2 + O(x log² x)` with `B(w) < 1`. The
one-variable method is capped at `ψ(x) ≥ (log(1/t_Z)) x`, where `log(1/t_Z([0,1])) < 0.865`.
(iv) *(`CITED`: Diamond–Erdős)* `inf B_+ = 1` and `sup A = 1` over all admissible finite `(S, c)`. So the loss tends
to 0 along the family, but it is nonzero at every member. Their proof that the constants tend to 1 uses the PNT.

*Proof.*
(i) `ψ(x) − ψ(x/a) = Σ_{x/a<n≤x}Λ(n) ≤ Σ_n Λ(n)u(x/n) = A x + O(log x)`, because `u(x/n) ≥ 1` for `x/a < n ≤ x`
and `u ≥ 0` elsewhere. Telescoping over `x/a^j`, `0 ≤ j ≤ log x/log a`, gives `ψ(x) ≤ Ax a/(a−1) + O(log² x)`. If
`B_+ ≤ 1`, then `ψ(x) − x ≤ O(log² x)`, which contradicts Littlewood's `Ω_+(√x log log log x)`.
(ii) `Σ_n Λ(n)u(x/n) ≤ Σ_{n≤x}Λ(n) = ψ(x)`, since `u(x/n) = 0` for `n > x` and `u ≤ 1`. So `ψ(x) ≥ Ax − O(log x)`.
If `A ≥ 1`, this contradicts `Ω_−`.
(iii) Cited, with Pritsker's (1.5)–(1.6) and Prop 1.3 read this phase.
(iv) Cited. ∎

**Reading (the "too-good error" law; `PROVED` as stated, the interpretation `HEURISTIC`).** Chebyshev's identity is a
mechanism of exactly the kind G2 asks for: exact `Λ(n)`, every `x`, no margin, no truncation. It fails at every
finite stage for one reason: its error term `O(log² x)` lies **below the fluctuation scale** `√x log log log x`. A
correct one-sided bound with main term exactly `x` must have an error at least that large (Theorem 4(i)). So any
successful auxiliary construction must **grow with `x` at the rate of the zeros' amplitude**. It must know the size
of `Σ_{|γ|≤T} x^{ρ}/ρ` (not the locations) from somewhere other than the zeros. In Stepanov's proof, that "somewhere"
is `g` via Riemann–Roch. This refines `cor:void`/`thm:margin` in one respect. The obstruction is not only that
estimates are closed under modification. It is also that an **exact** identity with a fixed finite auxiliary has an
error of the wrong size.

## §6 The open problem this route leaves (stated, not solved)

**Problem S (`OPEN`; RH-strength).** Construct, for each large `x`, an auxiliary datum `𝒜_x` over `Spec Z` with:
- **(RR_x)** a dimension/degree count whose defect is an effective genus `g_ζ(x)`, defined **before the spectrum**
  from the Γ-factor. A natural candidate is the Γ-side (Riemann–von Mangoldt main-term) value of `Σ_{0<γ≤T} 1/|ρ|`, namely
  `g_ζ(x) := π^{−1}∫_1^{T} θ′(t) t^{−1} dt ~ (4π)^{−1} log² T`, with `θ` the Riemann–Siegel theta function and `T = T(x)`;
- **(Amp_x)** a multiplicity amplification at every prime `≤ x` simultaneously, which Lemma 3 forbids in any
  derivation-based form;
- **(deg_x)** a product-formula count giving `ψ(x) ≤ x + O(g_ζ(x)√x)`.

By Theorem 1, a solution proves RH, so Problem S has full RH strength.
- By Theorem 4, `g_ζ` must be unbounded.
- By Theorem 5, `𝒜_x` cannot be a fixed finite Chebyshev auxiliary.
- By Lemma 3 and P46 Thm 22.17, (Amp_x) cannot come from a derivation or a Frobenius endomorphism.

What would be genuinely new is (Amp_x). I have **no candidate** for it. **The Γ-side definition of `g_ζ` does pass
OT-3/OT-5′-type independence** (it reads no zero). But the `S(T)` part of `N(T)`, which is `O(log T)`, does depend on
the zeros, and the Γ-side value is only the main term. Whether the main term suffices is part of Problem S.
