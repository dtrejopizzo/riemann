## §2 The rationality wall

### 2.1 Three lemmas

**Lemma A (eigenvalues on a `K`-structure are algebraic over `K`; `PROVED`, elementary).** Let `K ⊂ C` be a subfield,
`V` a `K`-vector space of arbitrary dimension, and `F ∈ End_K(V)`. If `0 ≠ w ∈ V ⊗_K C` and `(F⊗1)w = λw`, then `λ` is
algebraic over `K`.

*Proof.*
1. Suppose `λ` is transcendental over `K`, and put `L := K(λ)`. Write `w = Σ_{j=1}^m v_j ⊗ z_j` with `v_j ∈ V` linearly
  independent over `K` and `z_j ∈ C`, not all zero.
2. `C` is an `L`-vector space. By Zorn, extend a nonzero `z_{j₀}` to an `L`-basis and let `ℓ : C → L` be the
  `z_{j₀}`-coordinate. `ℓ` is `L`-linear, hence `K`-linear.
3. So `1⊗ℓ : V⊗_K C → V⊗_K L` is well defined. It commutes with `F⊗1` and is `L`-linear. Hence `w' := (1⊗ℓ)w` satisfies
  `(F⊗1)w' = λw'`.
4. `w' ≠ 0`: the `v_j⊗1` are `L`-linearly independent in `V⊗_K L`, and `ℓ(z_{j₀}) = 1`.
5. Since `λ` is transcendental, `L ≅ K(X)` with `λ ↦ X`. Multiplying `w'` by a common denominator `q(X) ∈ K[X]∖0`, which
  commutes with `F⊗1`, gives `0 ≠ w'' ∈ V⊗_K K[X] = V[X]` with `(F⊗1)w'' = Xw''`.
6. Write `w'' = Σ_{k=0}^d X^k u_k` with `u_d ≠ 0`. `F⊗1` acts coefficientwise, so comparing the coefficients of
  `X^{d+1}` gives `0 = u_d`. This is a contradiction. ∎

*Remark.* Generalized eigenvectors reduce to eigenvectors. If `(F−λ)^k w = 0` with `k` minimal, then
`(F−λ)^{k−1}w ∈ V⊗_K C` is an eigenvector.

**Lemma B (a non-rational exponent is algebraic at no more than two primes; `PROVED`, with the six exponentials theorem
`CITED`).** Let `ρ ∈ C∖Q` and put `p^ρ := e^{ρ log p}` with real `log p`. Then `#{p prime : p^ρ ∈ Q̄} ≤ 2`.

*Six exponentials theorem* (Lang, *Introduction to Transcendental Numbers*, 1966, Ch. II; Ramachandra, Acta Arith. 14
(1968); Waldschmidt, *Diophantine Approximation on Linear Algebraic Groups*, 2000, Thm 1.12; `CITED` (std.)). If
`x₁, x₂ ∈ C` are linearly independent over `Q` and `y₁, y₂, y₃ ∈ C` are linearly independent over `Q`, then at least one
of the six numbers `e^{x_i y_j}` is transcendental.

*Proof.*
1. Suppose `p₁, p₂, p₃` are distinct primes with `p_j^ρ ∈ Q̄`.
2. Put `x₁ = 1` and `x₂ = ρ`. They are `Q`-independent because `ρ ∉ Q`.
3. Put `y_j = log p_j`. They are `Q`-independent: `Σ a_j log p_j = 0` with `a_j ∈ Z` means `Π p_j^{a_j} = 1`, and unique
  factorization forces `a_j = 0`.
4. The six numbers are `p_j` and `p_j^ρ`. All are algebraic, which contradicts the theorem. ∎

**Lemma C (no nontrivial zero of ζ is rational; `PROVED`, with `0 < Re ρ < 1` `CITED` (std.)).**
1. A rational zero is real, and nontrivial zeros lie in `0 < Re s < 1`.
2. For `0 < σ < 1`, `ζ(σ) = η(σ)/(1 − 2^{1−σ})`, with `η(σ) = Σ(−1)^{n−1}n^{−σ}`.
3. `η(σ) > 0`: the series is alternating with decreasing terms and first term `1`.
4. `1 − 2^{1−σ} < 0` for `σ < 1`.
5. Hence `ζ(σ) < 0` on `(0,1)`, and `ζ` has no zero there. ∎

### 2.2 The theorem

**Theorem R (rationality wall; `PROVED`, with the six exponentials theorem `CITED`).** Let `K ⊂ C` be algebraic over `Q`,
and let `V` be a `K`-vector space of arbitrary dimension. Let `S` be a set of primes with `|S| ≥ 3`, and let
`F_p ∈ End_K(V)` for `p ∈ S` be arbitrary. Suppose `0 ≠ w ∈ V⊗_K C` and, for every `p ∈ S`,
`(F_p ⊗ 1) w = a_p · p^{ρ} · w`, with `a_p ∈ Q̄^×` and `ρ ∈ C`. Then `ρ ∈ Q`.

*Proof.*
1. By Lemma A, `a_p p^ρ` is algebraic over `K`, hence lies in `Q̄`.
2. So `p^ρ ∈ Q̄` for three primes.
3. Lemma B then forces `ρ ∈ Q`. ∎

**Corollary R1 (`PROVED`).**
* In the setting of Theorem R, no nontrivial zero `ρ` of ζ can occur (Lemma C). This holds for either normalisation: the
  unnormalised eigenvalue `p^{ρ}`, the normalised `p^{iγ}` (`a_p = p^{−1/2}`), and any twist by algebraic numbers or roots
  of unity.
* More generally, if `ρ` is any complex number with `ρ ∉ Q`, then `p^ρ` is an eigenvalue of a `Q̄`-rational Frobenius at
  most at two primes.

**Theorem R′ (algebra form; `PROVED`).**
* **Statement.** Let `A` be a `K`-algebra (K as above) and `F_p ∈ A` for `p ∈ S`, `|S| ≥ 3`, each **algebraic over
  `K`**, i.e. `m_p(F_p) = 0` for some `0 ≠ m_p ∈ K[x]`. Let `π : A → End_C(H)` be any representation on any complex
  vector space. Suppose `0 ≠ w ∈ H` with `π(F_p)w = a_p p^ρ w` and `a_p ∈ Q̄^×`. Then `ρ ∈ Q`.
* **Proof.** `m_p(a_p p^ρ)w = π(m_p(F_p))w = 0`, so `a_p p^ρ` is a root of `m_p`, hence in `Q̄`. Conclude with Lemma B.
  ∎
* **Where it applies.** Every element of a ring that is finitely generated as a `Z`-module is integral over `Z`
  (Cayley–Hamilton on the lattice). So R′ applies to every **order**: `End(J)`, Weil's `Z[π]`, the Hecke algebras `T_N`,
  `Z[Gal(K/Q)]`, and to any finite-rank ring whatever its origin.

### 2.3 Reading against P47 Thm 22.17 / Rem 22.18 and Weil

* **Thm 22.17(iii)** says the "missing `+`" (a ring of Frobenius correspondences in which `m + n·Frob` is an element with a
  kernel to count) is not realised by one-variable objects. Its "what the theorem does not say" paragraph leaves
  two-variable objects and derived correspondences open. Theorem R′ closes the **finite-rank** part of that opening
  (`PROVED`): if a ring supplies the `+` and has finite rank over `Z`, as Weil's `Z[π] ⊂ End(E)` does, then it carries no
  zero of ζ at three primes, whatever geometry produced it.
* **Weil's degree identity reading (`PROVED`).** Let `W` be finite-dimensional and `F_p`-stable, with
  `det(m + nF_p | W) ∈ Q̄` for all `m, n ∈ Z`, which is what a count `#ker(m + nF_p)` delivers.
  1. `det(1 + nF_p|W) = Σ_k e_k(λ) n^k` is a polynomial in `n` of degree `≤ dim W` taking algebraic values at
  `n = 0, …, dim W`.
  2. Lagrange interpolation gives `e_k ∈ Q̄`, so the eigenvalues of `F_p|W` are algebraic.
  3. By Lemma B, no zero-eigenvector lies in such a `W` at three primes.

  The counts that give Weil his `+` therefore force eigenvalues to be algebraic, and algebraic eigenvalues are what ζ's
  zeros cannot be at three primes.
* **Rem 22.18** (`Z[Gal(K/Q)]`): R3 Thm H showed weight 0. R′ gives more: no representation of the group ring or of any
  order containing it carries a zero.
* **Weil's inverse check (`PROVED`).** Over `F_q` there is one "prime", and `q^ρ ∈ Q̄` for the zeros (they are
  `log α/log q`), so Lemma B does not apply. The wall uses the **`Q`-independence of `log 2, log 3, log 5`**, which is
  unique factorization in `Z`: exactly what separates `Spec Z` from a curve over `F_q`.

### 2.4 Sharpness and scope

* **Three primes, not two (`OPEN`).** With two primes the conclusion is the **four exponentials conjecture**
  (Schneider–Lang–Ramachandra; `OPEN`, `CITED` Waldschmidt 2000 §1.3). With one prime no transcendence statement can help:
  for any algebraic `α ≠ 0`, `ρ = (log α + 2πik)/log p` has `p^ρ = α`.
* **Lattice Beurling systems (`PROVED`).** If all `log p_j ∈ Z·log q`, then `p_j^ρ = (q^ρ)^{k_j}` is algebraic as soon as
  `q^ρ` is, so Thm R does not apply. This is correct, since those systems are function-field-like (R4 Rem D1).
* **General Beurling systems, `Ξ_a`, `ζ·E`, DMV (`PROVED` as a check).** Thm R constrains the *arithmetic* of the Frobenius
  exponents, never their *position*. It excludes algebraic carriers for true and false data alike, so it produces no
  positivity for any falsifier (OT-4 is not triggered). Its only arithmetic input is the `Q`-independence of the logarithms
  of the generators.
* **What is not reached (`OPEN`).**
  * Eigenvectors in a completion `\bar{V⊗C}∖V⊗C`.
  * Fields `K` containing transcendental numbers. If `K ∋ p^{iγ}` the statement is vacuous; that is installation
  (Lemma T).
  * `ℓ`-adic structures over `Q_ℓ`, where an abstract `Q̄_ℓ ≅ C` destroys the meaning of "algebraic". Over Spec Z,
  however, for `H^i_et` of a variety over `Q` the characteristic polynomial of `Frob_p` at good `p` has integer
  coefficients (Deligne, Weil I; `CITED` (std.)). So its eigenvalues are algebraic and Lemma B applies directly, with no
  `Q̄`-form needed. Frobenius itself does not act on Betti cohomology.

## §3 The Hodge–Rosati degree bound

**Proposition HR (`PROVED`; the standard facts used are marked).** Let `X, Y` be compact Riemann surfaces and
`D = Σ m_k D_k` an effective correspondence on `X × Y` with no fibre components. Let `d₁ = deg(D → X)` and
`d₂ = deg(D → Y)`. Define `D_* : H⁰(Y,Ω¹) → H⁰(X,Ω¹)` by `D_* = Σ m_k f_{k*} g_k^*`, where
`D̃_k → D_k` is the normalisation, `f_k : D̃_k → X` has degree `d₁^{(k)}`, and `g_k : D̃_k → Y` has degree `d₂^{(k)}`. Use
the Hodge norm `‖ω‖² = (i/2)∫ω∧ω̄`.
* (i) `‖D_*ω‖ ≤ √(d₁d₂)‖ω‖`.
* (ii) The Hodge adjoint of `D_*` is `(D^t)_*`.
* (iii) The same bound holds on `H¹(·,C) = H^{1,0} ⊕ \bar{H^{1,0}}` with its positive harmonic-`L²` form.
* (iv) For `X = Y = C` over a number field, with `α := D_* ∈ End(J)`: `ĥ(αx) ≤ d₁d₂ ĥ(x)` for the Néron–Tate height on
  `J(Q̄)`, hence `‖α‖ ≤ √(d₁d₂)` on the Néron–Tate completion of `J(Q̄)⊗R`.
* (v) For `p ∤ N`, `‖T_p‖_{Pet} ≤ p+1` on `S₂(Γ₀(N))`.

*Proof.*
1. **Pullback.** Let `g : Z → Y` be finite of degree `e`. Then `∫_Z g^*(ω∧ω̄) = e∫_Y ω∧ω̄`: change of variables off the
  finite branch locus, `CITED` (std.). So `‖g^*ω‖² = e‖ω‖²`.
2. **Pushforward.** Let `f : Z → X` be finite of degree `d`, and `B` its branch locus. Over a disc `U ⊂ X∖B`,
  `f^{−1}U = ⊔_{i≤d}U_i` and `f_*η = Σ_i (f|_{U_i}^{−1})^*η =: Σ_i η_i`. With `η_i = a_i dz`, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
  `|Σa_i|² ≤ d Σ|a_i|²`. Hence `(i/2)f_*η∧\bar{f_*η} ≤ d Σ_i (i/2)η_i∧η̄_i` as densities.
3. Integrating over `X∖B` gives `‖f_*η‖²_{X∖B} ≤ d‖η‖²`. So `f_*η` is holomorphic on `X∖B` and `L²` near each point of
  `B`. An `L²` holomorphic 1-form on a punctured disc extends holomorphically (Laurent coefficients `a_n` with `n ≤ −1`
  are not `L²`). Hence `f_*η ∈ H⁰(X,Ω¹)` and `‖f_*‖ ≤ √d`.
4. **(i).** For each `k`, `‖f_{k*}g_k^*‖ ≤ √(d₁^{(k)}d₂^{(k)})`. By the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz,
  `Σ_k m_k √(d₁^{(k)}d₂^{(k)}) ≤ √(Σm_kd₁^{(k)})(Σm_kd₂^{(k)}) = √(d₁d₂)`.
5. **(ii).** The same local computation gives `⟨f_*η, ω⟩_X = ⟨η, f^*ω⟩_Z`, so `f_*^† = f^*` and
  `(f_*g^*)^† = g_*f^*`, which is the transpose correspondence. That the Rosati involution of `End(J)` is `D ↦ D^t` is
  `CITED` (std.; Birkenhake–Lange, *Complex Abelian Varieties*, §5.1, §11.5).
6. **(iii).** `D_*` is algebraic, so it preserves the Hodge decomposition, and on `\bar{H^{1,0}}` it acts as the
  conjugate of its action on `H^{1,0}`. Harmonic representatives realise the positive form.
7. **(iv).** Choose a number field `L` over which `D` and `C` are defined. Then `α` maps `J(L)` to `J(L)`, and
  `J(L)⊗R` is finite-dimensional (Mordell–Weil, `CITED`).
  * The Néron–Tate pairing satisfies `⟨αx,y⟩ = ⟨x,α′y⟩`, with `α′` the Rosati involution (`CITED` (std.);
  Hindry–Silverman, *Diophantine Geometry*, B.5).
  * So `β := α′α` is Néron–Tate self-adjoint and positive on `J(L)⊗R`, and `ĥ(αx) = ⟨βx,x⟩ ≤ λ_max(β)ĥ(x)`.
  * The eigenvalues of `β` on `J(L)⊗C` are roots of its minimal polynomial in the `Q`-algebra `End⁰(J)`. That algebra
  acts faithfully on `H¹(C(C),Q)`, so these roots are eigenvalues of `β` on `H¹`.
  * On `H¹`, by (ii)–(iii), `β = α^†α` has norm `‖α‖²_{Hodge} ≤ d₁d₂`.
  * So `λ_max(β) ≤ d₁d₂`. Take the union over `L`, then the completion.
8. **(v).** `S₂(Γ₀(N)) ≅ H⁰(X₀(N),Ω¹)` with Petersson equal to Hodge up to a constant. For `p ∤ N`, `T_p` is the Hecke
  correspondence of bidegree `(p+1,p+1)` (`CITED` (std.); Shimura, *Arithmetic Theory of Automorphic Functions*, §7.2).
  Apply (i). ∎

**Corollary HR-W♯ (`PROVED`, given R4 Thm W♯(c), which the round-4 panel checked 5/5).**
* **Setting.** The R4 §2 setting with bounded block size, and tail factors that are Hodge spaces `H¹(X_j,C)` or
  Néron–Tate completions of `J_j(Q̄)⊗R`. For `p ∈ B_j`, unnormalised Frobenius acts through effective correspondences of
  bidegree `(d₁(p),d₂(p))`, and `S_p = F_p/√p`.
* **Statement.** Let `g(p) := sup_{q≤p} √(d₁(q)d₂(q)/q)` (nondecreasing). Suppose `Σ_p g(p)^{−2} = ∞` and
  `Σ_{p∈P₀} g(p)^{−2} < ∞`. Then each summand carries at most one critical point.
* **Special cases.**
  * The trivial bound `d₁d₂ ≤ Cp²` gives `Σ1/p = ∞` (Euler).
  * Frobenius graphs have bidegree `(1,p)`, so `‖S_p‖ ≤ 1`, which is R3 Thm W.
  * Hecke `(p+1,p+1)` gives `g ≍ √p`, exactly at threshold.
* **Proof.** By Prop HR, `‖S_p‖ ≤ g(p)`. Apply W♯(c). ∎

This replaces the Schur-kernel hypothesis of R4 Prop D by the Kähler/Rosati structure that the FH angle actually has, and
so repairs the over-label P2/P3/P5 flagged. **Beyond Kähler/Rosati realisations it stays `HEURISTIC`.**

### 4.2 N-a / N-b / N-c (R158_005 §2)

* **N-a** (a counting reason for definiteness). In Weil's form, counts `#ker(m + nF_p)` on a finite piece force algebraic
  eigenvalues (§2.3), so they cannot occur on a zero-carrying piece at three primes. An N-a count over `Spec Z` must be a
  regularised (infinite-dimensional) count. **PROVED as a filter.**
* **N-b** (a correspondence algebra with `+`). Allowed only in infinite rank, and with its zero-carrying vectors outside
  every `Q̄`-form (Thm R′). **PROVED as a filter.**
* **N-c** (a cone strictly larger than `𝓔`). Not addressed.

### 4.3 Obstructions of record

* **`thm:finZ`.** Different content. finZ says finitely many zeros never carry a certificate. Thm R says that not even
  **one** zero sits on an algebraic carrier at three primes. They are compatible, and neither implies the other.
* **`cor:void`.** Not triggered. Thm R is not a derivation from prime-side inputs.
* **A17 (`C_Q`, OT-2 RH-strength).** `C_Q` is not a `Q̄`-structure. Its zero-carrying vectors are Connes' cokernel,
  analytic, consistent with Thm R. Thm R explains why every "algebraic" shortcut to `C_Q` fails.
* **P47 Thm 22.17 / Rem 22.18.** See §2.3. Their finite-rank opening is closed.
* **R1 Lemma 4.4, R2 Thm A and Ex. E, R3 Props G and BW.**
  * R1 Lemma 4.4 (conformal endomorphism families miss primes) is subsumed in its eigenvalue form by Thm R′ for any
  `S` with `|S| ≥ 3`.
  * R2 Ex. E (`K_∞ = Q(√−p)`, conformal at every `p`): eigenvalues `±i√p`, i.e. `ρ = ½` with `a_p = ±i`. This is the
  rational exponent Thm R allows, so the check is consistent.
  * R3 Prop G (only Galois, weight 0) is extended to **arbitrary** `Q`-linear Frobenius on `J(Q̄)⊗Q`.
  * R3 Prop BW shows transcendental joint spectrum occurs **only** in the completion (`w_s ∉ Q̄^{(N)}`), which illustrates
  the scope limit.

### 4.4 `(BC-mod)` (B6_R4 §5.2)

* **Status.** Not settled (`OPEN`). The BC modular flow has spectrum `β log Q^×_{>0}`, and `(BC-mod)` asks whether all
  ordinates satisfy `γ/β ∈ log Q^×_{>0}`.
* **Why Thm R does not apply.** Thm R constrains eigenvalues `p^ρ` of operators rational at fixed primes. `(BC-mod)`
  constrains the ordinates themselves, a statement about the individual `γ` of which nothing is known.
* **What remains.** The modular group is a flow, and no `Q̄`-structure is preserved by a nontrivial flow (R2 Thm A for
  countable sets). So Thm R and R2 Thm A together exclude only the "algebraic" versions of (BC-mod).

### 4.5 A consistency check with the Tate/BSD philosophy (`HEURISTIC`, no weight)

Algebraic cycles, as in Tate's conjecture (poles at `s = i+1`) and BSD (zeros at the centre), see zeros and poles only at
**rational** `s`. Theorem R is the unconditional statement behind this: an algebraic structure with rational
Frobenius at three primes sees only rational exponents. The nontrivial zeros of ζ are not rational (Lemma C). So,
unconditionally, no algebraic-cycle theory can carry them as eigenvalues.

## §5 What remains, and the `OPEN` question for the angle

**Q-R5 (`OPEN`; RH-strength when combined with OT-2).** Take a countable-dimensional `Q̄`-space `V₀` with `Q̄`-rational
Frobenius `F_p` for all `p` and a positive form `⟨,⟩` on `V₀`, defined before the spectrum (for instance Néron–Tate on
`⊕_N J₀(N)(Q̄)⊗Q`, or Hodge on towers). The question has two parts:
* (a) Can the Hilbert completion carry the zeros of ζ as discrete joint point spectrum `{p^{ρ}}`, necessarily on
  transcendental vectors (Thm R)?
* (b) If it can, is the resulting trace on the completion ζ's explicit formula?

What is known:
* **Positivity.** It transfers to the completion by continuity (`PROVED`, trivial). This is exactly why the question is
  not settled by positivity: the form is positive on the completion whatever the spectrum is.
* **What must be proved.** That the completion's joint spectrum is the zeros, with the right multiplicities (OT-1/OT-2)
  and without choosing vectors from the zeros (OT-3).
* **The one example of record.** BC (R3 Prop BW) gives an open half-plane, not a discrete set. The poles and zeros of ζ
  enter only through cyclicity (Noor/Báez-Duarte, EQUIV, R157_002 row 43).

**`HEURISTIC` reading.** Completions of countable `Q̄`-structures under bounded rational operators tend to give either
algebraic point spectrum or open sets, as the BC weighted shifts do. I have no theorem excluding a discrete transcendental
point spectrum. A rank-one operator `u⊗v^*` with `u ∈ V₀` has eigenvalue `v^*(u) ∈ Q̄`. In general it is unclear, and I do
not claim anything.

## §6 What is genuinely new

1. **Theorem R / R′ (`PROVED` via six exponentials).** Frobenius eigen-exponents on any `Q̄`-structure, or in any
  representation of an order, are rational as soon as three primes are involved. Hence no nontrivial ζ-zero can be carried
  by any algebraic (cycle-theoretic, Mordell–Weil, Hodge-Betti, Hecke, group-ring) structure.
  * It uses no tensor factorization, so it reaches **entangled** carriers (E1) in their algebraic part.
  * It is the unconditional form of phase-15-arithmetic-hodge-index M10 l.35 ("not Weil numbers"), which the record stated without proof.
  * I found no such statement in the record. The ingredients are classical, and the observation may be folklore.
2. **Weil's degree-identity corollary (§2.3).** Counts `#ker(m + nF_p)` on finite pieces are impossible on zero-carrying
  pieces at three primes. This precisely closes the finite-rank opening of P47 Thm 22.17's "what the theorem does not
  say" and Rem 22.18.
3. **Proposition HR and Cor HR-W♯.** The Hodge, Rosati/Néron–Tate and Petersson degree bound turns R4's `HEURISTIC`
  "degree-type compatibility ⟹ inside the wall" into `PROVED` for every Kähler/Arakelov carrier.

Not new: Lemma A (standard linear algebra), Lemma C (classical), the pullback and pushforward estimates (standard; the
trivial Hecke bound).

## §7 Weakest step

* **Scope, not correctness.** Thm R reaches only zero-eigenvectors in the algebraic span `V⊗_K C` (or in representations
  of algebras in which `F_p` is algebraic). Eigenvectors in analytic completions are untouched, and Bost–Connes shows that
  completions do carry transcendental joint spectrum. Q-R5 is therefore the honest residual, and it is OT-5′ in
  analytic form.
* **The one cited input** is the six exponentials theorem, which is established (Lang, Ramachandra, 1966–68). Lowering
  "three primes" to two is the four exponentials conjecture (`OPEN`).
* **Cor HR-W♯** inherits R4 Thm W♯(c)'s hypotheses: tail factorization, bounded blocks and a monotone envelope.
