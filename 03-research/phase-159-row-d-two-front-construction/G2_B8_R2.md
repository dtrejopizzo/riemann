## §1 The ferromagnetic reading and the coupling ray

### 1.1 The ferromagnetic form (CITED plus a two-line identity)

By P46 thm:A, for real `F ∈ L²(I_T)` in the form domain,
`𝔞_T(F) = C_0‖F‖² + Lév(c_F) − Σ_{n∈𝒩_T} v_n c_F(log n)`, with `Lév(c_F) = ∫_0^∞ (c_F(0) − c_F(u))K(u)du ≥ 0`.
Since `c_F(u) = ‖F‖² − ½‖F − F(·−u)‖²` for real `F`:

  **(1.1)** `𝔞_T(F) = ½∫_0^∞ ‖F − F_u‖² K(u) du + ½ Σ_{n∈𝒩_T} v_n ‖F − F_{log n}‖² − (A_T − C_0)‖F‖²`,

where `F_u = F(·−u)` and `A_T = Σ_{𝒩_T} v_n`. (PROVED: the identity. The rest is CITED.)

(1.1) is a Dirichlet form of a jump process on ℝ, killed off `I_T`, with **nonnegative ("ferromagnetic") couplings**:
the archimedean Lévy density `½K` and the arithmetic jumps `½v_n` at `±log n`. Nonnegativity of the arithmetic
couplings is `Λ ≥ 0`. The form is a chemical potential `A_T − C_0 ~ 4e^T` subtracted from that energy.
`RD_T` ⟺ the killed process restricted to `P_T` has spectral gap `≥ A_T − C_0`. **Label: EQUIV** (a restatement of
`RD_T`). It passes the a.e.-twist test only because twisting makes some couplings negative. It fails `cor:void`:
deletion and amplification stay ferromagnetic.

The Lee–Yang / GKS toolkit proves statements that hold **for all ferromagnetic couplings**, or monotonically in them.
The next theorem shows that `RD_T` has neither property along the most natural one-parameter family.

### 1.2 Theorem R (the coupling ray). PROVED, primitive and complete forms.

For `β ≥ 0` let `a_β = βΛ`. Its comb is `βv`, and `ζ_{a_β} = ζ^β` on `Re s > 1` (`exp Σ βΛ(n)n^{-s}/log n`). Put
`g_T(β) := V_T(βv) = λ_min(A^{(βv)}_T | P_T)` and `g^W_T(β) := V^W_T(βv)`. The comb is scaled; the archimedean symbol
`m`, the constant `C_0` and the polar term are those of ζ. Let `T ≥ 0.60`. Then:

1. `g_T` and `g^W_T` are finite and concave on `[0,∞)`, with `g^W_T ≤ g_T`.
2. `g_T(0) = λ^∞_P(T) < 0`, `g^W_T(0) < 0`, and `g_T(β) → −∞` as `β → ∞`. Precisely: fix `0 < ℓ < min(log(3/2), 2T − log 2)`
  and a real `F_+ = φ + φ(·−log 2) ∈ P_T` as in `lem:cpos` with `u_0 = log 2` and `φ ∈ C_c^∞(−T, −T+ℓ)`. Then
  `g_T(β) ≤ [𝔞^0_T(F_+) − β v_2‖φ‖²] / (2‖φ‖²)`, where `𝔞^0_T` is the empty-comb form. Hence
  `I_T := {β ≥ 0 : g_T(β) ≥ 0}` is a compact interval `[β_−, β_+] ⊂ (0,∞)` or empty, with
  `β_− ≥ |λ^∞_P(T)| / A_T` and `β_+ ≤ 𝔞^0_T(F_+) / (v_2‖φ‖²)`.
  The same holds for `I^W_T := {g^W_T ≥ 0} ⊂ I_T`.
3. (Criticality of `β = 1`.) Suppose `λ^A_T > 0`, let `F_⋆` be a real unit minimiser (it exists: `rem:resweights`), and
  put `D_T := Σ_{𝒩_T} v_n c_{F_⋆}(log n) = C_0 + Lév(c_{F_⋆}) − λ^A_T`. If `D_T > 0`, then `β_+ ≤ 1 + λ^A_T/D_T`.
  If `D_T < 0`, then `β_− ≥ 1 − λ^A_T/|D_T|`. The complete form satisfies the same with its own minimiser
  `F^W` and `λ^W_T`. By `thm:margin`, for `T ≥ 1`: `dist(1, ℝ∖I_T) ≤ 4·10⁹ T e^{−15.449T} / |D_T|`.
4. RH ⟺ `1 ∈ I_T` for every `T` ⟺ `1 ∈ I^W_T` for every `T` (Weil; P45 Thm 10.4).

*Proof.*
1. `β ↦ βv` is affine and `V_T`, `V^W_T` are concave and finite (lem:degen; round-1 Theorem B for `V^W`). `V^W ≤ V`
  because the forms agree on `P_T`.
2. `g_T(0) = V_T(0) = λ^∞_P(T)`, which is `< 0` for `T ≥ 0.60` (cor:Tarch), and `g^W_T(0) ≤ g_T(0)`. For the bound, the
  construction of `lem:cpos` with `ℓ < log(3/2) < log 3 − log 2` puts the support of `c_{F_+}` inside
  `(−ℓ,ℓ) ∪ ±(log 2 − ℓ, log 2 + ℓ)`. The only contact `log n` (`n ≥ 2`) in that set is `log 2`, because
  `log 2 > ℓ`, `log 3 > log 2 + ℓ` and `log n ≥ log 2` for all `n ≥ 2`. Also `c_{F_+}(log 2) = ‖φ‖²` and
  `‖F_+‖² = 2‖φ‖²` (disjoint supports). The condition `ℓ < 2T − log 2` gives `J ∪ (J + log 2) ⊂ I_T`. By `eq:Aform`,
  which is affine in the weights,
  `𝔞^{βv}_T(F_+) = 𝔞^0_T(F_+) − β v_2 ‖φ‖²`. Divide by `‖F_+‖²`.
  * Concavity plus `g(0) < 0` plus `g → −∞` make `{g ≥ 0}` a closed, bounded interval that misses 0.
  * For the lower bound: for any real unit `F ∈ P_T`, `𝔞^{βv}(F) = 𝔞^0(F) − β Σ v_n c_F(log n) ≤ 𝔞^0(F) + βA_T`, since
  `|c_F| ≤ 1`. Taking the infimum over `F` gives `g_T(β) ≤ λ^∞_P(T) + βA_T`, which is `< 0` for
  `β < |λ^∞_P|/A_T`.
  * The upper bound on `β_+` is read off the display.
3. By lem:degen applied to `v = βv^0`: `g_T(β) ≤ λ^A_T − (β − 1)D_T`. If `D_T > 0`, the right side is `< 0` for
  `β > 1 + λ^A_T/D_T`. If `D_T < 0`, it is `< 0` for `β < 1 − λ^A_T/|D_T|`. The identity for `D_T` is `eq:Aform` at
  `F_⋆`. For the complete form, the same inequality holds with `F^W`, as in the last paragraph of `rem:resweights`.
  The numerical bound is `thm:margin` / cor:margin1 with `λ^W ≤ λ^A`.
4. This is the definition of `RD_T` at `β = 1` together with the premises of record. ∎

**What is new.**
* `cor:void` perturbs one weight (amplification) or finitely many (deletion). Theorem R moves **all weights
  proportionally**, and so keeps every *ratio* `Λ(n)/Λ(m)` exact. Positivity is still confined to a compact window of
  couplings away from 0.
* Lee–Yang, Asano and GKS arguments are uniform over the coupling cone, or monotone in it. Theorem R(2) proves that no
  such argument decides `RD_T` for `T ≥ 0.60`: along the ray the positive set is bounded and misses `β = 0`, so

* R(3): `β = 1` is critical to within the margin, divided by the prime energy `|D_T|` of the minimiser.
* **Physical reading (HEURISTIC).** ζ is a ferromagnet that is exactly at a critical temperature, not in a phase that
  is stable under changes of temperature.

*Proof.* Theorem R(2). The FE and the linearity statements are immediate from `ξ(s)^k = ξ(1−s)^k`. ∎

## §2 Proposition K: FE-extendable points are dense, and the FE pins a conductor, not a phase

Let `𝕋_T` be the Satake torus of round 1 (D2): `v_n(θ) = v_n cos(kθ_p)` for `n = p^k ∈ 𝒩_T`, `p ∈ 𝒫_T = {p < e^{2T}}`.
For a Dirichlet character `χ` put `θ_χ := (arg χ(p))_{p∈𝒫_T}`, defined when `χ(p) ≠ 0` for every `p ∈ 𝒫_T`.

**Lemma K1 (equidistribution). PROVED.** Let `X_q` be the set of nontrivial even characters mod a prime `q > e^{2T}`.
As `q → ∞` through primes, the uniform measure on `{θ_χ : χ ∈ X_q}` converges weakly to Haar measure on `𝕋_T`.

*Proof.* Take a nonzero `k ∈ ℤ^{𝒫_T}`. Write `a = ∏_{k_p>0} p^{k_p}` and `b = ∏_{k_p<0} p^{−k_p}`. Then `a ≠ b` are
coprime positive integers, and `e^{i⟨k,θ_χ⟩} = χ(a)\overline{χ(b)}`. Orthogonality over the even characters mod `q`
gives
`(2/(q−1)) Σ_{χ even} χ(a)\overline{χ(b)} = 𝟏[a ≡ b] + 𝟏[a ≡ −b] (mod q)`.
This vanishes once `q > a + b`. Removing the trivial character changes the average by `O(1/q)`. Weyl's criterion
finishes the proof. For prime `q` every nontrivial character is primitive, and `q > e^{2T}` makes `χ(p) ≠ 0` for
`p ∈ 𝒫_T`. ∎

**Proposition K (conductor transfer).** CITED inputs: the Weil explicit formula for `L(s,χ)` with
`Λ(s,χ) = (q/π)^{s/2}Γ(s/2)L(s,χ)` for even primitive `χ` (Weil 1952; Iwaniec–Kowalski Thm 5.12), and Weil's criterion
GRH(χ) ⟺ `W^χ_T ≥ 0` for all `T`. For even primitive `χ` mod `q` with `q > e^{2T}` and real `F ∈ L²(I_T)`:

  **(K)** `W^χ_T(F) = log q·‖F‖² + 𝔞^{v(θ_χ)}_T(F)`, where `𝔞^{v(θ)}_T` has ζ's `m` and `C_0`, the rotated comb, and **no
  polar term** (`L(s,χ)` is entire).

Consequently:
* (i) on `P_T`, `Φ_T(θ_χ) ≥ λ_min(W^χ_T|P_T) − log q`, so GRH(χ) ⟹ `Φ_T(θ_χ) ≥ −log q` (primitive). PROVED given
  CITED; the implication is conditional on GRH(χ).
* (ii) (PROVED, unconditional) For `T ≥ 0.60` and all large primes `q`, a positive proportion of `χ ∈ X_q` have
  `θ_χ ∈ U_T`. There the conductor-dropped form `W^χ_T − log q‖·‖²` fails `RD_T`. Proof: Lemma K1 and round-1
  Theorem S(1) (`U_T` is open with positive Haar measure).

*Proof of (K).* The archimedean term for `Λ(s,χ)` is ζ's with `log(q/π)` in place of `log(1/π)`, i.e. symbol
`m(τ) + log q`. The prime term is `Σ Λ(n)n^{−1/2}[χ(n)c_F(log n) + \overline{χ(n)}c_F(−log n)] = Σ v_n Re χ(n) c_F(log n)`
for real `F` (`c_F` is real and even), and `Re χ(p^k) = cos(kθ_p)`. There is no pole. ∎

**Reading (PROVED content, HEURISTIC gloss).**
* Round 1 read the FE as isolating the single point `θ = 0` on `𝕋_T`. Lemma K1 corrects this: the window data of
  L-functions **with** an FE and **with ζ's Γ-factor Γ_ℝ(s)** are dense in `𝕋_T`.
* What distinguishes ζ among them is a **scalar**, the conductor mass `log q`. Every such `q` has `log q > 2T`,
  because `q` can have no prime factor below `e^{2T}`: that is what keeps all window Satake parameters unimodular.
  The one exception is `q = 1`, which forces `θ = 0` and adds ζ's polar term.
* So the FE's load-bearing content in the window is **"conductor 1 (and the pole)"**. A mechanism must detect the
  absence of a mass term `log q‖F‖²`. Mass shifts move `λ` one-for-one, so the detection must be exact to the margin
  (`thm:margin`). This is the conductor analogue of O3.

## §3 The two remaining stability constructions I tried, and why they fail

**3.1 Lee–Yang = one-sided stability + reflection. EQUIV.** The Lee–Yang circle theorem is
(zero-free in `|z| < 1`, via Asano contractions) + (the symmetry `z ↦ 1/z̄`). Over Spec Z, with Bohr variables
`x_p = p^{−s}`, the FE is the involution `x_p ↦ 1/(p x_p)` (coupled with Γ_ℝ). Its fixed torus is `|x_p| = p^{−1/2}`,
i.e. `Re s = ½`. The FE supplies the reflection half. The one-sided half is zero-freeness on `Re s > ½`, which is RH
(R157_002 rows 39, 42). The Euler product supplies it only on `Re s > 1`, where the product converges.
Asano-type contractions are equivariant under `x_p ↦ e^{iθ_p}x_p`. That gives two outcomes.
* On the zero side they do **not** contradict anything, because a.e. rotated `L_θ` *is* zero-free on `Re s > ½`
  (Wintner 1944, CITED; B7 Thm C).
* On the Weil-form side, with ζ's `m`, they prove false statements (Theorem S; B7 Thm B).

So an equivariant stability argument, run on zeros, can at best reach "a.e. θ", and `θ = 0` is Haar-null. Bagchi's
strong recurrence (RH ⟺ recurrence at `θ = 0`) is the known equivalent that uses the specific point (P4's audit of B7;
phase 55/58). **EQUIV.**

**3.2 Barycentres of FE-matched data. EXCLUDED.** `𝒱_T` (the positive set of combs) is convex, since `V_T` is concave
(round-1 Theorem B). So a sufficient mechanism would be: write ζ's comb as a barycentre of combs that are
**provably** in `𝒱_T`. The comb `v(0)` is coordinatewise maximal on `𝕋_T` (`v_n(θ) ≤ v_n(0)`, with equality for all
`n` only at `θ = 0`). So it is an extreme point of the torus hull, and no nontrivial barycentre of torus points
reaches it. By Theorem R, the coupling ray contains no provably positive point at `β ≠ 1` either: `I_T` is not known to
be nonempty, and `0 ∉ I_T`. Off the torus, FE-matched combs of other L-functions carry their own `m`, conductor and
polar data. By (K) and Cor. R′, re-normalising them to ζ's symbol costs a scalar mass. **EXCLUDED** on the torus and
on the ray (PROVED as stated). Off both it is OPEN, and no candidate has been found.
