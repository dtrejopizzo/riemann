* **Lemma 2.2 (periodic transfer; PROVED).** Let `f` be a real, `ω`-periodic tempered distribution with Fourier
  coefficient `0` at frequency `0`. Suppose `f ≥ 0` on `ℝ∖Γ` for a closed discrete set `Γ`. Then `f ≥ 0` on the
  complement of `B_ω(Γ) := {τ : τ + ωℤ ⊂ Γ}`. If `B_ω(Γ) = ∅`, then `f = 0`.
* **Theorem P (one-generator rigidity; PROVED, unconditional, both forms).** Fix an integer `m ≥ 2`. Let `b` be a
  real perturbation supported on `M_m := {m^k : k ≥ 1}` with `β_b` tempered, which is the same as
  `|b_{m^k}| ≤ C m^{k/2}(1+k)^N`. If `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W` or `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^A`, then `b = 0`. No hypothesis on ζ is used. The
  inputs are Theorem N(i) of G2_B9_R3 (panel-verified, to get RH), Lemma 2.2, and the Li–Radziwiłł theorem that no
  infinite vertical arithmetic progression consists of critical ordinates (CITED).
* **Proposition S (the input is on the zero side, and it is sharp; PROVED).** Assume RH and fix `m`. There is a nonzero
  `b` on `M_m` with `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W` if and only if `Γ_ζ` contains a two-sided progression `τ_0 + (2π/log m)ℤ`. So
  rigidity along one Euler factor is *equivalent* to a statement about progressions of zeros, not about primes.
* **Corollary P1 (PROVED, unconditional).** Modify ζ at a single prime `p`, or along the powers of a single integer, by
  any factor whose log-derivative comb is tempered, keeping conductor 1 and the gamma factor `Γ_ℝ`. The resulting
  comb is never ∀T-positive in either form. This includes perturbations outside `ℓ¹_w` and outside `𝒯_meas`, such as
  the `ζ·(1 − 2^{½−s})^{κ}` test of G2_P5_R3, for which Theorem R′ of G2_B9_R3 needs `|D_b|(Γ_ζ) = 0`, and that is
  not known.
* **Remark 5.1 (why Kronecker does not extend Theorem P to two or more generators; HEURISTIC with CITED inputs).**
  For `S = {p_1, …, p_r}` with `r ≥ 2`, Kronecker's theorem replaces the exact period by approximate periods `t_j`.
  These lie at heights where the zeros become dense: the gaps are `≪ 1/log log t` under RH (Littlewood). So every
  fixed window near height `t_j` contains ordinates, and positivity cannot be carried back. The multi-generator case
  stays **OPEN**. It would need a theorem that critical ordinates avoid Bohr sets, i.e. generalized arithmetic
  progressions.

**Status: partial new result.** Theorem P, Proposition S and Corollary P1 are unconditional obstruction and rigidity
statements, new to the corpus. They sit next to Theorem R′ of G2_B9_R3 and extend it beyond `𝒯_meas` along one
generator. They are **not** a mechanism for the "for all `T`" arrow. They show that Λ is the only candidate within
`Λ + (one-generator tempered perturbations)`. Whether Λ itself is ∀T-positive is RH.

## 1. Setting (record conventions, G2_B9_R2 §1–§2)

* A **comb** is a real function `a` on `ℕ_{≥2}`. The **Weil distribution** of `a` is
  `u_a := k_∞ + 2cosh(u/2) − Σ_{n≥2}(a(n)/√n)(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n})`, with the Fourier convention
  `φ̂(τ) = ∫φ(u)e^{iτu}du`.
* The forms are `W^{(a)}_T(F) = ⟨u_a, c_F⟩` for real `F` in the form domain of `L²(I_T)`, and `𝔞^{(a)}_T(F) = ⟨u_a, c_F⟩`
  on `P_T`. Here `c_F = F ⋆ F̃`. (PROVED in G2_B9_R3 §1, from P46 `thm:S`/`eq:A`.)
* `𝒮^W := {a : λ^W_T(a) ≥ 0 ∀T}` and `𝒮^A := {a : λ^A_T(a) ≥ 0 ∀T}`.
* For a perturbation `b`, put `β_b := Σ_n b_n n^{−1/2}(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n})` and `D_b := β̂_b`. Then
  `u_{Λ+b} = u_Λ − β_b`.
* `Γ_ζ := {γ ∈ ℝ : ζ(½+iγ) = 0}`. It is closed, discrete and symmetric, and `0 ∉ Γ_ζ` because `ζ(½) ≠ 0`.
* `μ_Λ := Σ_{γ∈Γ_ζ} m_γ δ_γ`.

**Record inputs used (all PROVED and panel-verified).**
* **(N) G2_B9_R3 Theorem N(i).** Let `β_b ∈ 𝒮′`. If `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W` or `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^A`, then RH. This rests on Lemma L,
  with the step-2 truncation patch written out by P2 and P3 in round 3: truncate the test by `χ_1(·/R)` and use
  dominated convergence for `Re s > ½`. Five of five panelists re-derived it.
* **(B) G2_B9_R2 Lemma B(i)–(ii).**
  * If `a ∈ 𝒮^W`, then `u_a` is positive definite and tempered, and `û_a = 2πμ_a` with `μ_a ≥ 0` a tempered measure.
  * If `a ∈ 𝒮^A`, then `(−∂²+¼)²u_a` is positive definite, with Fourier transform `2π(τ²+¼)²μ^P_a`, `μ^P_a ≥ 0`.
  * Both rest on Bochner–Schwartz (CITED: Schwartz, *Théorie des distributions*, Ch. VII §9) and the real-to-complex
  reduction (G2_B9_R3 Lemma 1.2).
* **(Z) G2_B9_R2 Lemma 2.1.** Under RH, `μ_Λ = μ^P_Λ = Σ_γ m_γδ_γ`, i.e. `û_Λ = 2πμ_Λ` as tempered distributions.
* **(LR) CITED: X. Li and M. Radziwiłł, "The Riemann zeta function on vertical arithmetic progressions", arXiv:1208.2684
  (IMRN 2015; journal data from memory).** Abstract, checked on the arXiv page: *for `a > 0` and `b` real, at least
  one third of the elements of the arithmetic progression `an + b` are not ordinates of zeros of ζ on the critical
  line.* The only consequence used here: **no set `{b + na : n ≥ 1}` with `a > 0` is contained in `Γ_ζ`.**
  * History: Putnam (Amer. J. Math. 76 (1954) 97–99) proved the case of progressions through the origin. Van
  Frankenhuijsen (J. Number Theory 115 (2005) 360–370) proved a length bound for progressions `D + inp` centred on
  the real axis. Neither covers an arbitrary offset `b`, which is what Theorem P needs. Li–Radziwiłł does.

## 2. Two lemmas: tempered combs on a lattice, and the periodic transfer

**Lemma 2.1 (temperedness on a lattice; PROVED).** Let `ℓ > 0` and `c: ℤ∖{0} → ℝ` be even. The measure
`β := Σ_{k≠0} c_k δ_{kℓ}` is a tempered distribution if and only if `|c_k| ≤ C(1+|k|)^N` for some `C` and `N`.
For `ℓ = log m` and `c_k = b_{m^k}m^{−k/2}`, this says `β_b` is tempered iff `|b_{m^k}| ≤ Cm^{k/2}(1+k)^N`.

*Proof.* (⟸) A measure with polynomially bounded mass on `[−R, R]` is tempered. (⟹) Fix `η ∈ C_c^∞(−ℓ/2, ℓ/2)` with
`η(0) = 1`. Then `c_k = ⟨β, η(· − kℓ)⟩`, and temperedness gives `|⟨β, ψ⟩| ≤ C Σ_{α,j≤N} sup|x^α ψ^{(j)}|`. For
`ψ = η(· − kℓ)` the right side is `≤ C′(1+|k|ℓ)^N`. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (periodic transfer; PROVED).** Let `ω > 0` and let `f ∈ 𝒮′(ℝ)` be real and `ω`-periodic, i.e.
`f(· + ω) = f`. Write its Fourier series `f = Σ_{k∈ℤ} f_k e^{2πikτ/ω}` (convergent in `𝒮′`) and assume `f_0 = 0`. Let
`Γ ⊂ ℝ` be closed and discrete, and suppose `f|_{ℝ∖Γ} ≥ 0`, i.e. `⟨f, h⟩ ≥ 0` for every `h ≥ 0` in `C_c^∞(ℝ∖Γ)`. Put
`B_ω(Γ) := {τ ∈ ℝ : τ + jω ∈ Γ for all j ∈ ℤ}`. Then:
1. `B_ω(Γ)` is closed, discrete and `ω`-periodic, and `f|_{ℝ∖B_ω(Γ)} ≥ 0`;
2. if `B_ω(Γ) = ∅`, then `f = 0`.

*Proof.*
1. `B_ω(Γ) = ⋂_j (Γ − jω)` is closed, lies in `Γ` (so it is discrete), and is `ω`-periodic. Let `τ ∉ B_ω(Γ)` and pick
  `j` with `τ + jω ∉ Γ`. Since `Γ` is closed there is an open interval `I ∋ τ + jω` with `I ∩ Γ = ∅`. For `h ≥ 0` in
  `C_c^∞(I − jω)`, periodicity gives `⟨f, h⟩ = ⟨f(·+jω), h⟩ = ⟨f, h(· − jω)⟩ ≥ 0`, because `h(·−jω) ∈ C_c^∞(I)` and
  `I ⊂ ℝ∖Γ`. The sets `I − jω` form an open cover of `ℝ∖B_ω(Γ)`. A partition of unity subordinate to it, applied to
  any `h ≥ 0` in `C_c^∞(ℝ∖B_ω(Γ))`, gives `⟨f, h⟩ ≥ 0`.
2. Now `f ≥ 0` on ℝ, so by the Riesz–Schwartz theorem `f` is a positive Radon measure. Choose `χ ∈ C_c^∞`, `χ ≥ 0`,
  with `Σ_{j∈ℤ} χ(τ + jω) = 1` for all `τ`. For each `k`,
  `⟨e^{2πik·/ω}, χ⟩ = ∫_0^ω e^{2πikτ/ω}Σ_jχ(τ+jω)dτ = ωδ_{k0}`. The Fourier series converges in `𝒮′` and `χ ∈ 𝒮`, so
  `⟨f, χ⟩ = ωf_0 = 0`. A positive measure that integrates the nonnegative continuous `χ` to `0` vanishes on
  `{χ > 0}`. By periodicity it vanishes on `⋃_j({χ > 0} − jω)`, which is all of ℝ because `Σ_jχ(·+jω) = 1 > 0`.
  So `f = 0`. ∎

*Remark 2.3.* Part 2 uses **exact** periodicity twice: once to move positivity from `τ + jω` back to `τ`, and once to
pass from `⟨f, χ⟩ = 0` to `f = 0`. Neither step survives replacing the period by an approximate (Bohr) period when
`Γ` has unbounded local density. This is the content of Remark 5.1.

## 3. Theorem P: rigidity along one generator

**Theorem P (PROVED; unconditional; complete and primitive forms).** Fix an integer `m ≥ 2` and put
`M_m := {m^k : k ≥ 1}`. Let `b` be a real perturbation supported on `M_m` with `β_b` tempered (by Lemma 2.1:
`|b_{m^k}| ≤ Cm^{k/2}(1+k)^N`).
(i) If `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W`, then `b = 0`.
(ii) If `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^A`, then `b = 0`.
No hypothesis on ζ is assumed.

*Proof of (i).*
1. *RH.* `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W` and `β_b ∈ 𝒮′`, so input (N) gives RH.
2. *The spectral inequality.* By (B), `u_{Λ+b}` is tempered and positive definite, with `û_{Λ+b} = 2πν` for some
  `ν ≥ 0` tempered. Since `u_{Λ+b} = u_Λ − β_b` and, under RH, `û_Λ = 2πμ_Λ` (input (Z)), we get
  `D_b = β̂_b = 2π(μ_Λ − ν)` as tempered distributions. So `D_b` is a real, locally finite measure, and on the open set
  `ℝ∖Γ_ζ` we have `D_b = −2πν ≤ 0`. At each `γ ∈ Γ_ζ`, `D_b({γ}) ≤ 2πm_γ`.
3. *Periodicity.* `β_b = Σ_{k≠0} c_kδ_{k log m}` with `c_{±k} = b_{m^k}m^{−k/2}`. Put `ω := 2π/log m`. Translation by
  `ω` in `τ` corresponds to multiplying `β_b` by `e^{iωu}`: `D_b(· + ω) = (e^{iωu}β_b)^`. Since `e^{iωk log m} = 1` and
  `β_b` is a measure carried by `(log m)ℤ`, we have `e^{iωu}β_b = β_b`. So `D_b` is `ω`-periodic. Its Fourier series
  is `D_b = Σ_{k≠0} c_k e^{ikτ log m}`, with `2π/ω = log m`. The coefficient at `k = 0` is `0` because `β_b` has no
  atom at `u = 0`: the comb starts at `n = 2`, and `M_m` does not contain `1`.
4. *Transfer.* Apply Lemma 2.2 to `f := −D_b`, which is real, `ω`-periodic, has `f_0 = 0`, and satisfies
  `f|_{ℝ∖Γ_ζ} ≥ 0`, with `Γ = Γ_ζ`.
5. *No progression of zeros.* Suppose `τ_0 ∈ B_ω(Γ_ζ)`. Then `{τ_0 + nω : n ≥ 1} ⊂ Γ_ζ`, and under RH (step 1) every
  element of `Γ_ζ` is the ordinate of a zero on the critical line. This contradicts (LR) with `a = ω`, `b = τ_0`. So
  `B_ω(Γ_ζ) = ∅`.
6. *Conclusion.* By Lemma 2.2(2), `D_b = 0`. By Fourier uniqueness `β_b = 0`, and since the atoms `±k log m` are
  distinct, `b = 0`. ∎

*Proof of (ii).*
1. `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^A` and `β_b ∈ 𝒮′`, so input (N) (the primitive, `k = 2` case) gives RH.
2. By (B), `v := (−∂²+¼)²u_{Λ+b}` is positive definite, with `v̂ = 2π(τ²+¼)²μ^P ≥ 0`.
3. Under RH, `u_Λ` is tempered (G2_B9_R3 Lemma L, converse), and `β_b` is tempered by hypothesis. So `u_{Λ+b}` is
  tempered and `v̂ = (τ²+¼)² û_{Λ+b}`.
4. `(τ²+¼)^{−2}` is a smooth multiplier with polynomially bounded derivatives, hence a multiplier of `𝒮′`. Therefore
  `û_{Λ+b} = 2πμ^P ≥ 0` as a measure on ℝ; the only zeros of `τ²+¼` are at `±i/2`, which are not on ℝ.
5. From here the proof of (i), steps 2–6, applies verbatim with `ν := μ^P`. ∎

**Remark 3.1 (what Theorem P adds to the record).**
* **Theorem R′ of G2_B9_R3** needs `D_b` to be a locally finite measure with `|D_b|(Γ_ζ) = 0`, i.e. `b ∈ 𝒯_meas`.
  Theorem P needs no a-priori information on `D_b`: step 2 derives that it is a measure, and it may charge ordinates.
  In exchange it restricts the *support* of `b` to one multiplicative generator.
* **Theorem R(iv) of G2_B9_R2 (OPEN)** asks for a nonzero `b` with `D_b ≤ 2πμ_Λ`. Theorem P says there is none among
  perturbations supported on `M_m`, for every `m`. This is the first proved case of R(iv) outside `𝒯_meas`.
* **The OPEN question of G2_B9_R3 Remark 3.4** ("a nonzero Fourier pair between `±log ℕ` and `Γ_ζ`") is answered
  negatively for pairs supported on `±(log m)ℤ` of polynomial growth. This includes pure crystalline pairs, where
  `D_b` is atomic on `Γ_ζ`.

**Corollary P1 (single Euler factor; PROVED, unconditional, both forms).** Let `p` be a prime, or more generally let
`m ≥ 2` be an integer. Let `R(z) = Σ_{k≥0} r_k z^k` with `r_0 = 1` be holomorphic and zero-free on `|z| < m^{−1/2}`,
with log-derivative coefficients `−z R′(z)/R(z) = Σ_{k≥1} ℓ_k z^k` satisfying `|ℓ_k| ≤ Cm^{k/2}(1+k)^N`. Put
`ζ_R(s) := ζ(s)R(m^{−s})`. Its comb is `Λ + b` with `b_{m^k} = −ℓ_k·(log m)`, supported on `M_m`, and `β_b` is
tempered. Keep the Weil distribution with the same archimedean kernel `k_∞`, the same polar term, and no `δ_0` term
(conductor 1). If `R ≢ 1`, then `Λ + b ∉ 𝒮^W` and `Λ + b ∉ 𝒮^A`.

*Proof.* The log-derivative computation `−ζ_R′/ζ_R(s) = Σ Λ(n)n^{−s} + (log m)·m^{−s}R′(m^{−s})/R(m^{−s}) = Σ Λ(n)n^{−s} − (log m)Σ_k ℓ_k m^{−ks}` identifies `b`.
Temperedness follows from Lemma 2.1. Then apply Theorem P. `R ≢ 1` ⟺ some `ℓ_k ≠ 0` ⟺ `b ≠ 0`. ∎

*Examples (all PROVED instances of Corollary P1).*
* `R(z) = (1 − m^{1/2}z)^{κ}`, i.e. `ζ(s)(1 − m^{1/2−s})^{κ}`, for real `κ ≠ 0`. Since `−zR′/R = κm^{1/2}z/(1 − m^{1/2}z)`, its log-derivative coefficients are
  `ℓ_k = κ m^{k/2}`, so `c_k = b_{m^k}m^{−k/2} = −κ log m`: bounded, not in `ℓ¹_w`. By Poisson summation
  `D_b = −κ log m·(ωΣ_jδ_{jω} − 1)`, a measure charging `(2π/log m)ℤ`. Whether that set meets `Γ_ζ` is not known, so Theorem R′ does not apply. For
  `m = 2`, `κ = 1` this is P5's extra test `ζ·(1−2^{1/2−s})`.
  * P5 recorded that this function has its extra zeros on the line. Theorem P says its comb is still not ∀T-positive
  *at conductor 1*.
  * The function's own functional equation carries the factor `m^{s−½}`, i.e. a conductor term `∝ δ_0` in its own
  Weil distribution. With that term included, the Weil distribution is positive definite under RH; that is the
  `δ_0` escape noted in §4.
* `ζ_odd = (1 − 2^{−s})ζ(s)`. Here `c_k = log 2·2^{−k/2}` is in `ℓ¹_w`. This was already covered by Theorem R′; it is
  recovered here.
* Any finite modification `b = εδ_{m}` (`thm:finmod` at `n_0 = m`, `T = ∞`). Already covered by Theorem R′; recovered.

## 4. Proposition S: the rigidity along one generator is a zero-side statement, and it is sharp

**Proposition S (PROVED; complete form; under RH).** Assume RH and fix `m ≥ 2`, `ω = 2π/log m`. The following are
equivalent:
(a) there is a nonzero real `b` supported on `M_m`, with `β_b` tempered, such that `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W`;
(b) `B_ω(Γ_ζ) ≠ ∅`, i.e. `τ_0 + ωℤ ⊂ Γ_ζ` for some `τ_0 ∈ ℝ`.
Unconditionally, (b) is false by (LR), so (a) is false. This is Theorem P(i).

*Proof.* (a) ⟹ (b): steps 2–4 of the proof of Theorem P, then Lemma 2.2(2) in contrapositive form.

(b) ⟹ (a): let `τ_0 ∈ B_ω(Γ_ζ)` and put `φ := τ_0 log m`. By symmetry of `Γ_ζ`, `−τ_0 + ωℤ ⊂ Γ_ζ` as well. For
`c > 0` define
```
b_{m^k} := 2c·cos(kφ)·m^{k/2} (k ≥ 1), i.e. β_b = Σ_{k≥1} 2c·cos(kφ)(δ_{k log m} + δ_{−k log m}).
```
* `β_b` is tempered by Lemma 2.1, and `b ≠ 0` because `cos(kφ) = 0` for all `k ≥ 1` is impossible.
* Poisson summation, `Σ_jδ_{τ_0+jω} = ω^{−1}Σ_k e^{ik(τ−τ_0)log m}`, gives
  ```
  D_b = c·[ ωΣ_{j∈ℤ}(δ_{τ_0+jω} + δ_{−τ_0+jω}) − 2 ].
  ```
  This is a measure: positive atoms on `±τ_0 + ωℤ ⊂ Γ_ζ` of mass `cω` (mass `2cω` where the two progressions
  coincide), and the constant density `−2c`.
* Hence `2πμ_Λ − D_b = 2πμ_Λ − cω·(atoms on Γ_ζ) + 2c ≥ 0` as soon as `2cω ≤ 2π`, since `m_γ ≥ 1` on `Γ_ζ`.
* By G2_B9_R2 Theorem R(iv) (Lemma B(iii)(⟸) with Lemma 2.1), `Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W`. ∎

**Reading (PROVED content; the G2 phrasing is HEURISTIC).**
* Inside the one-generator class, "Λ is the only ∀T-positive comb" is *equivalent*, under RH, to the zero-side
  statement "no two-sided progression of step `2π/log m` lies in `Γ_ζ`". The prime side contributes only the exact
  period.
* This is the third time my angle lands on the zero set: round 2 (hull blindness), round 3 (growth exponent `Θ`), and
  now (progressions of ordinates). In all three, the almost-periodic structure of the comb, which comes from ℚ-linear
  independence of `{log p}` or from exact periods, is used exactly. What decides the question is a property of `Γ_ζ`.
* Proposition S also explains why the `δ_0` term (conductor) matters. With a free `δ_0` coefficient, `b_{m^k} =
  c·m^{k/2}` plus `cδ_0` gives the positive Poisson comb `c(log m)^{−1}·2πΣδ_{jω}`: positive with no condition on zeros.
  Fixing conductor 1 is what forces the progression condition.

## 5. Why Kronecker's theorem cannot extend Theorem P, and the OPEN multi-generator case

Let `S = {p_1, …, p_r}` with `r ≥ 2`, `⟨S⟩` the `S`-smooth integers `≥ 2`, and `b` supported on `⟨S⟩` with `|β_b|`
tempered. Then `D_b(τ) = Σ_k c_k 2cos(τ ι(k))` with `ι(k) = Σ k_j log p_j` injective on `ℕ^r` by unique
factorization. `D_b` is the restriction to the Kronecker line `τ ↦ (τ log p_j)_j` of a distribution on `𝕋^r`. The
line is dense because `{log p_j}` is ℚ-independent: PROVED, Kronecker–Weyl, CITED.

**What still works (PROVED).**
* If `b ∈ ℓ¹_w`, then `D_b` is continuous and `≤ 0` off the discrete set `Γ_ζ`, hence everywhere. By density and
  continuity it is `≤ 0` on `𝕋^r`, and it has mean `0`, so `b = 0`. This is Theorem R′ restricted to `⟨S⟩`; nothing new.
* Approximate periods exist: for every `ε > 0` the Bohr set `E_ε := {t : ‖t log p_j/2π‖ < ε ∀j}` is relatively
  dense. If `|β_b|` is tempered, then `D_b(· + t_i) → D_b` in `𝒮′` along any `t_i ∈ E_{ε_i}` with `ε_i → 0`, by
  dominated convergence on `Σ_k|c_k||ψ̂(ι(k))|`.

**What fails (HEURISTIC, with a CITED input).** To use step 1 of Lemma 2.2 with approximate periods, we would need, for
a fixed test `h ≥ 0` supported in a fixed interval `J`, a sequence `t_i ∈ E_{ε_i}`, `ε_i → 0`, with
`(J + t_i) ∩ Γ_ζ = ∅`.
* Since `ε_i → 0`, necessarily `t_i → ∞`.
* Under RH, `γ_{n+1} − γ_n ≪ 1/log log γ_n` (Littlewood 1924; CITED from memory, e.g. Titchmarsh §14.13). So for
  large `t` every interval of fixed length contains an ordinate, and such `t_i` do not exist.
* The approximate-period transfer therefore carries no positivity back from large heights. The density of zeros
  there is `(2π)^{−1}log t → ∞`, which is the same archimedean density that capped dlVP in phase 151.
* Exact periods (`r = 1`) avoid this because they allow the translate `τ + jω` to be chosen at bounded height, as long
  as it avoids `Γ_ζ`, and (LR) guarantees that one does.

**OPEN (multi-generator Theorem P).** For `|S| ≥ 2` and `b` supported on `⟨S⟩` with `|β_b|` tempered: does
`Λ + b ∈ 𝒮^W` force `b = 0`? The natural zero-side input would be:
* (i) a Li–Radziwiłł-type theorem for Bohr sets, i.e. that critical ordinates cannot fill `τ_0 + E_ε` (generalized
  arithmetic progressions; cf. Özbek–Steuding, *Arch. Math.* 112 (2019) 53–59, on values on generalized progressions,
  CITED from a reference list, not read); together with
* (ii) a control of the singular part of the torus distribution on the codimension-one set that the Kronecker line
  meets.

Neither is available, so the multi-generator case is OPEN. The mixed case (`b` periodic plus `ℓ¹_w`) is OPEN for the
same reason. It would need `{j ∈ ℤ : τ + jω ∉ Γ_ζ}` to meet every Bohr set in ℤ, and (LR) gives density `≥ 1/3`,
which is not enough for small Bohr sets.

## 7. What is genuinely new, and what is not

**New to the corpus (§0 check).**
1. **Lemma 2.2 (periodic transfer), PROVED.** The mechanism that turns "positive off a discrete set" into "positive off
  the set of full progressions" for exactly periodic distributions of mean zero.
2. **Theorem P, PROVED, unconditional, both forms.** No nonzero tempered perturbation supported on the powers of one
  integer keeps Λ ∀T-positive. This is the first proved case of the OPEN Theorem R(iv) of G2_B9_R2 outside `𝒯_meas`.
3. **Proposition S, PROVED (under RH).** Along one generator, uniqueness of Λ in `𝒮^W` is equivalent to the absence of
  progressions of ordinates with step `2π/log m`. This identifies the decisive input as zero-side, and it shows that
  the (LR) input cannot be dropped.
4. **Corollary P1, PROVED.** Single-Euler-factor modifications at conductor 1 are never Weil-positive; this covers
  `ζ·(1−2^{½−s})^{κ}`, which Theorem R′ could not reach.
5. **Remark 5.1, HEURISTIC with CITED inputs.** The exact point where Kronecker's theorem fails: approximate periods
  live at heights where the zero density `(2π)^{−1}log t` fills every fixed window.

**Not new.** Bochner–Schwartz, Poisson summation, Fourier series of periodic distributions, Kronecker–Weyl, the
Li–Radziwiłł theorem (CITED), Putnam and van Frankenhuijsen (history only), Theorem N(i), Lemma B and Lemma 2.1 of
G2_B9 (record inputs).

**Not a mechanism.** Nothing here bounds `λ^A_T(Λ)` or `λ^W_T(Λ)` from below at any `T > log 2`. The G2 arrow
("prime data ⟹ ∀T positivity of Λ") is untouched. What is proved is again the rigid, fibre-over-Λ direction: Λ is the
only candidate in a structured neighbourhood.
