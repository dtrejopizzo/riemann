## §0 Verdict

The round-1 panel (1/5, negative structural result only) killed my carrier `Y_K = A_K/K` by its own Cor D1: the
suspension has `H¹ = 0`. The panel asked for two things in round 2: (1) test the Hecke/Shimura solenoid, which Theorem D
did not cover; (2) prove the general dichotomy, "an R-continuous Frobenius family arising from p-adic geometry is trivial
on the mixing part". This memo does both and goes somewhat further. **No object is built.** The result is a sharp wall
for the whole Deninger-suspension class.

1. **Theorem S (`PROVED`).** For any unitary representation `u` of `Q^×_{>0}`, the part that survives suspension along
  `log` is exactly the spectral subspace `E(L)H` of the **Kronecker line** `L = {(p^{iγ})_p : γ ∈ R}` inside the dual
  torus `T^∞ = (Q^×_{>0})^∧`. The suspended flow is `∫ e^{itγ} dE(ι(γ))`.
2. **Theorem E (`PROVED`).** Suppose `u` is **factorizable along an infinite tail**: primes split as `P_0 ⊔ B_1 ⊔ B_2 ⊔ …`
  with the `B_j` finite and infinitely many of them, and `H = H_0 ⊗ ⊗'_j (H_j, ξ_j)` with `u(p)` acting on the factor
  containing `p`. Then the suspended flow on `E(L)H` has **at most one eigenvalue** `iγ_0`, and `γ_0` is fixed by the
  unramified tail `(ξ_j)`. A countable direct sum of such representations therefore gives at most one point per summand.
3. **Cor D2 (`PROVED` modulo `CITED` classical inputs).** This answers the round-2 test. On the adelic modular (Shimura)
  solenoid, suspending the leafwise `L²` harmonic 1-forms (cusp forms of weight 2, Petersson norm) along the Hecke
  elements `a_p = diag(p,1)_p` leaves **nothing**: `V_c ∩ H¹_{(2)} = 0`. One prime suffices, via Howe–Moore at `p`.
4. **Prop R (`PROVED`).** Every countable `Γ ⊂ R` is realised as the suspended spectrum of a direct sum of factorizable
  one-dimensional representations, namely the Größencharacters `|·|^{iγ}`. So in this class the zeros can enter only by
  **installing** them one summand at a time: OT-3 fails, and so does OT-4, since `Ξ_Q` and every modified comb are
  installed the same way.
5. **N-e (new necessary condition, `PROVED` inside the suspension class).** On the part of the carrier where the zeros
  live, Frobenius must be **entangled across all but finitely many primes**: no tensor splitting into infinitely many
  independent finite blocks. With Cartan's formula this gives a three-way conflict, `HEURISTIC` as a general claim and
  proved for `C_Q` and `Y_K`. Leafwise positivity needs leaves transverse to the flow. Entanglement is realised on
  record only by the product formula, which turns Frobenius into archimedean scaling. For `K = Q` the connected directions of the
  idèle class group are exactly the flow direction (dimension count, Prop G).

Status: **partial new result, negative/structural**. It extends round 1's Theorem D from mixing Koopman solenoids to all
prime-by-prime (factorizable) Frobenius families, including automorphic ones. The d = 1 error the panel flagged is corrected in §1.

## §2 Theorem S: what survives suspension is the Kronecker line

**Notation.** `G := Q^×_{>0} = ⊕_p p^Z`, a free abelian group of countable rank. Its Pontryagin dual is
`Ĝ = ∏_p T` (compact, metrisable), where `χ ∈ Ĝ` is determined by `(χ(p))_p`. The **Kronecker map** is
`ι: R → Ĝ`, `ι(γ)(n) := n^{iγ}`. It is continuous. It is injective: `ι(γ) = 1` forces `γ log 2, γ log 3 ∈ 2πZ`, and since
`log 3/log 2 ∉ Q`, `γ = 0`. Set `L := ι(R)`. By the Lusin–Souslin theorem (a continuous injective image of a Polish space
into a Polish space is Borel, and the map is a Borel isomorphism onto its image; `CITED`, Kechris, *Classical Descriptive
Set Theory*, Thm 15.1), `L` is Borel and `ι: R → L` is a Borel isomorphism.

Let `u` be a unitary representation of `G` on a separable Hilbert space `H`. By the SNAG/Stone theorem for the discrete
abelian group `G` (`CITED`, classical) there is a unique projection-valued measure `E` on `Ĝ` with
`u(n) = ∫_Ĝ χ(n) dE(χ)`. For `w ∈ H`, `μ_w(B) := ⟨E(B)w, w⟩`.
As in G1_B2_R1 Theorem D (with `ker ℓ = 1`), set
`V_c := {w ∈ H : n ↦ u(n)w is continuous at n = 1 for the topology induced on G by log: G → R}`.

**Theorem S (`PROVED`).** (i) `V_c = E(L)H`.
(ii) On `V_c`, the unique strongly continuous unitary extension `ũ` of `u` to `R` (Theorem D(ii)) is
`ũ(t) = ∫_R e^{itγ} dE(ι(γ))`. So the generator `Θ` of the suspended flow on `V_c` is `i·(multiplication by γ)` for the
spectral measure `ι^{−1}_* (E|_L)`. In particular every eigenvector of `Θ|V_c` with eigenvalue `iγ` is a joint eigenvector
of all `u(p)`, with eigenvalue `p^{iγ}`.

*Proof.* (i) "⊂": let `w ∈ V_c`, and put `φ(log n) := ⟨u(n)w, w⟩` for `n ∈ G`. Because
`|φ(s) − φ(s′)| ≤ ‖w‖·‖u(n)w − u(n′)w‖ = ‖w‖·‖u(n/n′)w − w‖`, continuity at 1 makes `φ` uniformly continuous on the
dense subgroup `log G ⊂ R`. So it extends to a continuous `φ̃` on `R`. `φ̃` is positive definite, since the defining
inequalities `Σ c_j c̄_k φ(s_j − s_k) ≥ 0` pass to limits. By Bochner, `φ̃(s) = ∫ e^{isγ} dν(γ)` for a finite positive
Borel measure `ν` on `R`. Now `μ_w` and `ι_*ν` are finite Borel measures on the compact group `Ĝ`. Their Fourier
coefficients at `n ∈ G = (Ĝ)^∧` are `μ̂_w(n) = ⟨u(n)w, w⟩ = φ(log n)` and `(ι_*ν)^∧(n) = ∫ n^{iγ}dν = φ̃(log n)`, which
agree. By uniqueness of Fourier–Stieltjes coefficients on a compact abelian group, `μ_w = ι_*ν`. Hence
`μ_w(Ĝ∖L) = 0`, i.e. `‖E(Ĝ∖L)w‖² = 0`, i.e. `w = E(L)w`.
"⊃": if `w = E(L)w`, then `μ_w = ι_*ν` with `ν := (ι^{−1})_*(μ_w|_L)`, which is Borel because `ι` is a Borel isomorphism
onto `L`. Then `‖u(n)w − w‖² = ∫ |n^{iγ} − 1|² dν(γ) → 0` as `log n → 0`, by dominated convergence.
(ii) For `w ∈ E(L)H`, `t ↦ ∫ e^{itγ} dE(ι(γ))w` is a strongly continuous unitary group on `E(L)H` that agrees with `u(n)`
at `t = log n`. Uniqueness of the extension is Theorem D(ii). ∎

**Remark S1 (`PROVED`).** Theorem D(iii) of round 1 (mixing kills) follows. Suppose `⟨u(g_k)x, y⟩ → 0` along `g_k` with
`log g_k → 0`. For `w ∈ V_c` one has `u(g_k)w → w`, and the orthogonality argument of Theorem D(iii) applies. The
advantage of Theorem S is that it turns "what survives" into a **measure question on `T^∞`**: does the spectral
measure charge the null set `L`? (`L` is Haar-null: its image in `T_2 × T_3` is a line.)

## §3 Theorem E: locality leaves at most one archimedean point

**Setting (tail-factorizable representations).** Partition the primes as `P = P_0 ⊔ ⨆_{j≥1} B_j`, where each `B_j` is
finite and nonempty and there are infinitely many `j`. `P_0` may be empty, finite or infinite. Put `G_A := ⊕_{p∈A} p^Z`,
with dual `T^A`. Let
* `(H_0, u_0)` be a unitary representation of `G_{P_0}`;
* for each `j`, `(H_j, u_j)` a unitary representation of `G_{B_j}` on a separable space, with a unit vector `ξ_j ∈ H_j`;
* `H := H_0 ⊗ ⊗'_{j}(H_j, ξ_j)`, von Neumann's incomplete tensor product through `⊗_j ξ_j`, with
  `u(p) := u_j(p)` acting on the `j`-th factor if `p ∈ B_j`, and `u(p) := u_0(p)` on `H_0` if `p ∈ P_0`.

The case `P_0 = ∅`, `|B_j| = 1` is the **Euler-factorizable** case: `H = ⊗'_p(H_p, ξ_p)` with `u(p) = U_p` on the
`p`-th factor. This is the representation-theoretic form of "Frobenius defined prime by prime". Automorphic
`π_f = ⊗'π_p` with `U_p = π_p(a_p)` is of this form.

Write `ι_A: R → T^A` for the restricted Kronecker map, and `μ^{(j)}_w` for the spectral measure of `w ∈ H_j` on `T^{B_j}`.

**Lemma P (the point masses of a tail; `PROVED`).** Let `ν_j` be Borel probability measures on `T^{B_j}` and
`ν := ⊗_j ν_j` on `T' := ∏_j T^{B_j}`. Put `ι' := ι_{P∖P_0}` and `c(γ) := ν({ι'(γ)}) = ∏_j ν_j({ι_{B_j}(γ)})`.
Then `A := {γ ∈ R : c(γ) > 0}` has **at most one element**. Moreover, if `ν_j = μ^{(j)}_{ξ_j}` for all `j ≥ j_0`,
then `A` depends only on the tail `(ξ_j)_{j ≥ j_0}`.
*Proof.* The product formula for singletons follows from continuity from above on the cylinders. A product
`∏_j a_j` with `a_j ∈ [0,1]` is `> 0` iff all `a_j > 0` and `Σ_j (1 − a_j) < ∞` (`CITED`, classical). Suppose
`γ ≠ γ′` both lie in `A`. Then `ν_j({ι_{B_j}(γ)}) → 1` and `ν_j({ι_{B_j}(γ′)}) → 1`. The points `ι_{B_j}(γ)` and
`ι_{B_j}(γ′)` differ for all but at most one `j`. Indeed, equality means `(γ − γ′) log p ∈ 2πZ` for every `p ∈ B_j`, and
two distinct primes `p, q` cannot both satisfy this, since then `log p / log q ∈ Q`. So for large `j` two distinct
points each carry `ν_j`-mass `> ½`, which is impossible. For the tail statement: changing finitely many factors changes
finitely many `a_j`, and `c(γ) > 0` stays equivalent to "all changed factors are positive and the tail sum is finite". ∎

**Theorem E (`PROVED`).** In the tail-factorizable setting, let `Γ(ξ) := {γ : ∏_{j≥1} μ^{(j)}_{ξ_j}({ι_{B_j}(γ)}) > 0}`.
By Lemma P it has at most one element. Then:
(i) If `Γ(ξ) = ∅`, then `V_c = 0`.
(ii) If `Γ(ξ) = {γ_0}`, then `V_c ⊂ ker(Θ − iγ_0)`. So the suspended flow on `V_c` is the scalar `e^{itγ_0}`.
In both cases the suspended flow has **at most one eigenvalue and no continuous spectrum**.
*Proof.* By Theorem S, `V_c = E(L)H`. Elementary tensors `w = w_0 ⊗ (⊗_j w_j)` with unit `w_j` and `w_j = ξ_j` for
`j > J` span a dense subspace, and `E(L)` is bounded. So it suffices to show that `E(L)w ∈ E({ι(γ_0)})H` for such `w`,
and `E(L)w = 0` when `Γ(ξ) = ∅`. For such `w`, `⟨u(n)w, w⟩ = μ̂^{(0)}_{w_0}(n_0)·∏_j μ̂^{(j)}_{w_j}(n_j)`, where `n_A` is
the `G_A`-part of `n`. So by Fourier uniqueness `μ_w = μ^{(0)}_{w_0} ⊗ ν` with `ν = ⊗_j μ^{(j)}_{w_j}`. Write
`Ĝ = T^{P_0} × T'` and apply Fubini:
`μ_w(B ∩ L) = ∫_{T^{P_0}} ν({χ′ : (χ_0, χ′) ∈ B ∩ L}) dμ^{(0)}_{w_0}(χ_0)`.
For fixed `χ_0`, the section `{χ′ : (χ_0, χ′) ∈ L} = {ι'(γ) : ι_{P_0}(γ) = χ_0}` is countable: `ι_{P_0}(γ) = χ_0`
determines `γ` modulo `⋂_{p∈P_0}(2π/log p)Z`, which is `{0}` if `|P_0| ≥ 2`, a lattice if `|P_0| = 1`, and all of `R`
only if `P_0 = ∅`. When `P_0 = ∅`, take `ν` directly on `Ĝ = T'`. So the `ν`-measure of the section is a countable sum
of point masses `ν({ι'(γ)})`. By Lemma P these vanish unless `γ ∈ A_w`, and `A_w = Γ(ξ)` because `w_j = ξ_j` for `j > J`.
Hence `μ_w|_L` is `0` if `Γ(ξ) = ∅`, and supported on `{ι(γ_0)}` otherwise, i.e. `E(L)w = E({ι(γ_0)})E(L)w`. ∎

**Corollary E1 (one prime without eigenvalues suffices; `PROVED`).** If `H = H_p ⊗ H^{(p)}` with `u(p)` acting on `H_p`
alone and `U_p = u(p)|H_p` has **no eigenvalues** (purely continuous spectrum), then `V_c = 0`. This needs no tail
hypothesis.
*Proof.* Apply the Fubini of Theorem E with the roles reversed:
`μ_w(L) = ∫ μ_{w_p}({x : (x, χ′) ∈ L}) dμ_{w^{(p)}}(χ′)`. The section is at most one point, because `ι_{P∖{p}}` is
injective. Since `μ_{w_p}` has no atoms, the integrand is `0`. Density of elementary tensors finishes the proof. ∎

**Corollary E2 (direct sums; `PROVED`).** If `H = ⊕_k H^{(k)}` is a countable orthogonal sum of invariant
tail-factorizable summands, then `V_c = ⊕_k V_c^{(k)}` (`E(L)` commutes with the decomposition), and `Θ|V_c` has pure
point spectrum `{iγ_0^{(k)}}`, **at most one point per summand**, each fixed by that summand's unramified tail.

**Prop R (realisability, i.e. installation; `PROVED`).** For every countable multiset `Γ ⊂ R`, the representation
`⊕_{γ∈Γ} C_γ` with `u(n) = n^{iγ}` on `C_γ` is Euler-factorizable in each summand (`H_p = C`, `U_p = p^{iγ}`,
`ξ_p = 1`). Its suspended spectrum is exactly `iΓ`, and it is unitary, i.e. "positive" in the sense of Prop 1(d). Each
summand is the Größencharacter `|·|^{iγ}` restricted to `Q^×_{>0}`. The local condition at `p` ("`U_p` has an eigenvalue
`p^{iγ}`") is met for every `γ ∈ R` by the one-dimensional representation. **So no local datum separates a zero ordinate
from any other real number.**

**Reading (`HEURISTIC`, but every step above is proved).** This asks for a "pattern" that knows every result
without the whole series. In the suspension class, a factorizable summand does know its `γ` from its unramified tail:
two primes already determine it (Lemma P). But the only tails that produce a point are Größencharacter tails, and every
real `γ` has one. The zero set is therefore a **selection** among a continuum of locally admissible characters.
Theorem E proves that the selection is not made prime by prime. In Tate's and Connes' `L²(C_Q)` the selection is
made by the zeta distribution (the cokernel), not by the representation. That is Weil positivity, EQUIV (R158_002).

## §4 The round-2 test: the Hecke (Shimura) solenoid

**Construction M (`CITED`, classical).** Let `G = GL_2`, `Z` its centre, and
`M := G(Q)\(H^± × G(A_f))`, the adelic modular lamination. Its leaves are the images of `H^± × {g_f}`: Riemann
surfaces, each with the conformal structure of `H`. Transversals are totally disconnected. `G(A_f)` acts by right
translation, mapping leaves to leaves conformally. By Prop 1 of round 1, the leafwise `L²` pairing of 1-forms is
conformally invariant, and here it is the Petersson inner product. **It is defined before any spectrum and without
the zeros of ζ (OT-3, OT-5′ as a form).** The reduced leafwise `L²` cohomology in degree 1, for a fixed unitary central
character `ω`, is
`H¹_{(2)} ≅ ⊕_{π ⊂ L²_cusp(G(Q)\G(A), ω), π_∞ ≅ D_2} π_f ⊗ C²` (holomorphic and antiholomorphic weight-2 vectors),
with `π_f = ⊗'_p(π_p, ξ_p)` (multiplicity one; Flath's factorization; `ξ_p` spherical for `p ∤ cond(π)`). Harmonic
1-forms are the lowest-weight vectors of the discrete series `D_2`. The Eisenstein continuous spectrum contributes no
`L²` harmonic 1-form (its Casimir parameter meets 0 on a null set), and the one-dimensional residual spectrum `χ∘det`
has only weight-0 vectors. `CITED`: Gelbart, *Automorphic forms on adele groups*, §§3–5; Jacquet–Langlands; Flath 1979.
This is the round-1 panel's "leafwise `H̄¹` infinite-dimensional (cusp forms)". **OT-1's infinite-dimensionality holds
here**, unlike on `Y_K`.

**Frobenius family.** `a_p := diag(p, 1) ∈ GL_2(Q_p) ⊂ G(A_f)`. For distinct primes these commute, so `u(p) := R(a_p)`
defines a unitary representation of `Q^×_{>0}` on `H¹_{(2)}`, factorizable on each summand with `U_p = π_p(a_p)`. The
suspension along `log` is the Hilbert-space suspension of Theorem D.

**Corollary D2 (`PROVED` from Theorem E1 plus the `CITED` inputs).** `V_c ∩ H¹_{(2)} = 0`. The Hecke suspension of the
modular solenoid carries no eigenvalue at all in degree 1.
*Proof.* By Cor E2 it suffices to treat one summand `π_f`. Fix any prime `p`. `π_p` is an infinite-dimensional
irreducible unitary representation of `GL_2(Q_p)`: local components of cuspidal `π` are generic (global Whittaker model),
hence infinite-dimensional (`CITED`, Jacquet–Langlands). Restricted to `SL_2(Q_p)` it has no nonzero invariant vector,
since otherwise it would factor through `det` and be one-dimensional. Now
`a_p^{2k} = diag(p^k, p^k)·diag(p^k, p^{−k})`, so `π_p(a_p^{2k}) = ω_p(p)^k π_p(diag(p^k, p^{−k}))`. By Howe–Moore for
the simple group `SL_2(Q_p)` (`CITED`: Howe–Moore 1979; Zimmer, *Ergodic theory and semisimple groups*, Thm 2.2.20),
`⟨π_p(a_p^{2k})x, y⟩ → 0` as `|k| → ∞`. Odd powers follow from `⟨π_p(a_p^{2k})x, π_p(a_p)^* y⟩`. So the spectral measure
of `U_p` is Rajchman, hence atomless by Wiener's lemma
(`lim (2N+1)^{−1}Σ_{|k|≤N}|μ̂(k)|² = Σ_x μ({x})²`; `CITED`, Katznelson, *Harmonic Analysis* I.7.13). Cor E1 then gives
`V_c ∩ π_f = 0`. ∎

**Remarks.** (a) One prime suffices. The tail and Lemma P are not needed here, because local Frobenius at a single
prime is already mixing. This is the non-abelian analogue of Cor D1, and it answers the round-1 question negatively
and unconditionally (modulo the classical inputs).
(b) The same argument applies to any `G(A_f)` with `G` semisimple or reductive and any
`a_p` generating an unbounded subgroup modulo the centre at infinitely many (indeed at one) primes, on cuspidal
summands with infinite-dimensional local components. So it covers Shimura laminations generally (`PROVED` under the
same `CITED` inputs: Howe–Moore for the simple factors of `G(Q_p)`).
(c) **Finite level, Hecke correspondences.** On `K_f`-invariants (a finite-level modular curve) Frobenius is no longer a
group element but the Hecke operator `T_p` (a correspondence), which is self-adjoint for the Petersson norm. There is
no unitary `Q^×_{>0}` representation to suspend. The prior positivity (Petersson plus self-adjointness) constrains the
**Satake parameters** `α_p β_p = p`, `|α_p| = |β_p| = √p` (Ramanujan: Deligne via Eichler–Shimura, i.e. Weil's RH for
the curves `X_0(N)/F_p`; `CITED`). These are the Frobenius eigenvalues on `H¹` of the reduction mod `p`, not zeros of ζ.
The round-1 expectation ("positivity controls Satake parameters, not zeros") is thus **correct** at finite level
(`CITED`) and **sharpened** at adelic level (Cor D2: after suspension nothing survives).

## §5 The necessary condition N-e and the three-way conflict

**N-e (`PROVED` inside the suspension class, as the contrapositive of Theorem E and Cor E2).** Consider any carrier in
which (a) the Frobenius family acts on the positive space as a unitary `Q^×_{>0}` representation, and (b) the flow is
its archimedean-continuous extension (suspension). If the positive space's `H¹` has infinitely many eigenvalues coming
from a *single* summand, that summand admits **no** splitting `H_0 ⊗ ⊗'_j(H_j, ξ_j)` along infinitely many finite blocks
of primes. Within a countable sum of tail-factorizable summands, each eigenvalue costs one summand, installed as a
Größencharacter tail (Prop R).
Frobenius on the zero-carrying part must be **infinitely entangled**: the action at almost all primes cannot be
independent. N-e refines round 1's N-d (R-continuity), which Theorem S makes exact: "N-d ⟺ the spectral measure lives
on the Kronecker line".

**Lemma C (Cartan; `PROVED`, and on record as R158_004 row 1′).** If the generator `V` of the flow is tangent to the
leaves, then `Θ = L_V = d_𝓕ι_V + ι_V d_𝓕` is zero on reduced leafwise cohomology in every degree: for closed `ω`,
`L_Vω = d_𝓕(ι_Vω)` is exact.

**Prop G (dimension count; `PROVED`, structure of `C_K` `CITED`).** For a number field `K` of degree `d`, the path
components of `C_K = A_K^×/K^×` are the orbits of the connected Lie group `K_∞^{×,0}` (real dimension `d`), because the
finite idèles are totally disconnected (`CITED`, Artin–Tate Ch. IX). The norm flow is the `R_{>0}` factor of
`K_∞^{×,0}`. So a foliation with connected leaves transverse to the flow has leaf dimension `≤ d − 1`: leaves lie in
path components, and there they are transverse to `R_{>0}`. **For `K = Q` it is `0`**: the path components of `C_Q` are
the flow lines. This is R158_002 Lemma A(i) in a general form.

**The conflict (`HEURISTIC` as a general statement; each clause proved in the cases named).**
1. Leafwise positivity of Prop 1 type (N-a) needs leaves of real dimension 2 carrying a conformal structure. By Lemma C
  the leaves must be **transverse** to the flow.
2. The zeros in the positive `H¹` need **infinite entanglement** of Frobenius across primes (N-e, Theorem E).
3. The one entangling mechanism on record is the product formula (Connes, Tate). It turns `Frob_p` into archimedean
  scaling by `p^{−1}` times a compact rotation. The entanglement therefore lives in the archimedean directions, where
  for `K = Q` there is no room transverse to the flow (Prop G). Geometric p-adic constructions (the solenoids `Y_K`
  and `M`, Leichtnam's disk × `Z_p` laminations) factorize prime by prime and are killed by Theorem E/E1.

An object for ζ in the Deninger shape thus needs an **entangling mechanism for Frobenius that is not the product formula,
or a flow that is not a suspension**. Neither exists in the record or in the literature surveyed (R158_001 table,
18 frameworks). Whether one can exist: `OPEN`. I do not claim an impossibility theorem outside the suspension class.

## §7 What is new, and the weakest step

**New (to the corpus; literature status noted).**
1. **Theorem S**: the exact identification of what survives suspension along `log` with the spectral subspace of the
  Kronecker line `L ⊂ T^∞`. Elementary (Bochner, Fourier uniqueness, Lusin–Souslin). Not found in the corpus. I do not
  claim novelty in the literature. It is the statement "archimedean-continuous characters of `Q^×_{>0}` are the
  `n^{iγ}`" at the level of spectral measures.
2. **Theorem E, Lemma P, Cor E1–E2**: tail-factorizable representations give at most one archimedean eigenvalue, fixed
  by the unramified tail. One prime with continuous local spectrum gives none. This is a Kakutani-type argument on
  infinite product measures (Kakutani 1948, the product-measure dichotomy, related but not used).
3. **Cor D2**: the Hecke suspension of the adelic modular/Shimura lamination kills all leafwise `L²` harmonic 1-forms.
  This answers the round-1 panel's round-2 test, via Howe–Moore at one prime.
4. **Prop R** (installation) and **N-e** (infinite entanglement), with Prop G (no transverse room at `∞` for `Q`). This
  gives the three-way conflict of §5 as the precise form of the Deninger-suspension wall.
5. Correction §1 of round 1's d = 1 statement (the panel's E1).

**Not claimed.** No object for ζ, no trace formula with zeros, no sign at any window, no progress on the "for all `T`"
arrow. Nothing here approaches RH. The memo proves that a whole class cannot carry it.

**Weakest step.** The **scope** of N-e. Theorem E is proved only for carriers in which (a) Frobenius acts on the positive
space by a *unitary group representation* of `Q^×_{>0}`, and (b) the flow is its suspension. Two escapes remain outside:
* Frobenius acting by **correspondences** that do not assemble into a group (finite-level Hecke algebras, the
  Connes–Consani `Ψ(λ)` with `Id_ε` defects);
* flows that are **not suspensions** (Deninger's `W_rat` systems in 1807.06400, where the relation between the
  `Q_{>0}`-action and the flow is not a Hilbert suspension of a unitary representation; `OPEN` whether Theorem S applies
  after passing to any `L²` model).
Cor D2 also rests on `CITED` classical inputs (Flath factorization, genericity of local components, Howe–Moore), which I
did not re-prove. Prop G's structure of `C_K^0` is `CITED` (Artin–Tate). The "three-way conflict" is `HEURISTIC` as a
general statement.

**Suggested next step.** Test whether a correspondence-valued Frobenius (a representation of the
Connes–Consani semigroup of `Ψ(λ)` on a Hilbert space) can evade Theorem E. The first question is whether its
spectral theory reduces to a unitary `Q^×_{>0}` representation on some dilation (Sz.-Nagy/Stinespring). If it does,
Theorem E applies to the dilation.
