## §0 Verdict

1. **Theorem L (local-gauge no-go; PROVED, unconditional).** Fix `η > 0` and let `Θ_η = exp(−2ηψ)` be Suzuki's Schur
  candidate. Take any rule that assigns to each prime `p` and each local datum `b` a **contraction**
  `f_{p,b} ∈ H^∞(𝔻)`, `‖f_{p,b}‖ ≤ 1`, evaluated at the Frobenius variable `w = p^{iz}`, together with **any**
  archimedean factor `Θ♮_∞` (no condition at all on it). No such rule can satisfy
  `Θ_η[a] = Θ♮_∞ · Π_p f_{p,a_p}(p^{iz})` on `Im z > ½` for both `a = Λ` and the single-prime substitutions
  `a = Λ + εδ_p` (fixed `ε > 0`, all `p`).
  The reason is a quantitative Poisson–Jensen deficit: each prime forces `log|f_{p,Λ}(p^{iz})| ≤ −(2/π)ηεβ(y)p^{−½}`,
  and `Σ_p p^{−½} = ∞`. With single-prime removals in place of the ε-substitutions, the deficit is
  `≥ 0.033·ηβ(y)·log p/√p` per prime.
2. **Dichotomy (Prop L.2; PROVED given R1 (A2)).** Allow the local factors to depend on the global datum (a
  "non-functorial" rule). Then a Frobenius-factorised Schur representation of `Θ_η` on all of `C₊` exists ⟺ RH, and it
  is trivial under RH (`f_p ≡ 1`). **So the brief has exactly two readings.** Read locally (a rule), it is impossible
  unconditionally. Read globally, it is Prop 5.1, EQUIV.
3. **Translation to systems (Cor L.3; CITED for the dictionary).** Suppose the Hamiltonian is assembled as a **cascade
  of passive (Schur) scattering systems**, one per prime, each defined from its local Euler datum by a fixed rule, plus
  an arbitrary archimedean system. Its scattering function would then be a product as in Theorem L (Sz.-Nagy–Foiaș
  cascade ⟺ product of characteristic functions). Such an assembly does not exist. The structural positivity of a
  local cascade is therefore incompatible with ζ's explicit formula, unconditionally.
4. **What survives.** The only construction that the record does not exclude is a **non-multiplicative (Redheffer /
  Potapov–Ginzburg) coupling** of the prime blocks through global ports. This is phase 114 D.252–D.254 and phase 120
  P3. By the record, its remaining step (the "positive bulk identity" / global square) is OPEN and RH-strength
  (P3_GLOBAL_FOUR_PORT_REDUCTION §1).
5. **Status of the angle.** It is a **dead end for the object**. What it adds is a new proved filter, Theorem L, which
  is the de Branges/Schur counterpart of `thm:SC` and of the divergent capacity constant `A_P ~ Σ log p/√p` of
  R157_005 §4.2. **No object is built.**

## §2 Setting and definitions

Notation (R1/R2): `s = ½ − iz`, `ψ(z) = (ξ′/ξ)(s)`, `Θ_η(z) = exp(−2ηψ(z))`. Put `z = x + iy`. By the Euler product,
for `y > ½` (that is, `Re s > 1`),
```
ψ(z) = ψ_∞(z) − Σ_n Λ(n) n^{−½} n^{iz}, ψ_∞ := 1/s + 1/(s−1) − ½log π + ½(Γ′/Γ)(s/2).
```
Hence, for `y > ½`,
```
Θ_η(z) = Θ_∞(z) · Π_p Θ_{η,p}(p^{iz}), Θ_∞ := e^{−2ηψ_∞}, Θ_{η,p}(w) := exp(2η Σ_{k≥1} Λ(p^k) p^{−k/2} w^k).
```
The product converges absolutely, because `Σ_{p,k} log p · p^{−k(½+y)} < ∞`.

**Def 2.1 (local data, comb data).** A *local datum at `p`* is a real sequence `b = (b_k)_{k≥1}` with
`Σ_k |b_k| p^{−k/2} R^k < ∞` for some `R > 1`. Put
```
g_{p,b}(w) := Σ_k b_k p^{−k/2} w^k,
```
which is analytic on a neighbourhood of the closed disc `𝔻̄`, with `g_{p,b}(0) = 0`. Write `Λ_p := (log p)_{k≥1}`. A
*comb datum* is `a = (a_p)_p` with `a_p = Λ_p` for all but finitely many `p`. Its Schur candidate on `y > ½` is
```
Θ_η[a](z) := Θ_∞(z) · Π_p exp(2η g_{p,a_p}(p^{iz})),
```
so that `Θ_η[Λ] = Θ_η`. This is exactly Suzuki's `Θ_η` computed for the comb `a`, as in R1 (A4). The single-point
substitution `Λ + εδ_{p}` of OT-4 (R158_001) is the datum `a_p = Λ_p + ε e_1`, with `a_q = Λ_q` for `q ≠ p`.

**Why the variable `w = p^{iz}` is the Frobenius at `p`.** On the canonical-system line `x ↦ e^{ixz}`, translation by
`log p` (the prime's "Frobenius shift" in the explicit formula, `u ↦ u + log p`) is multiplication by `w = p^{iz}`.
A factor that depends on `z` only through `p^{iz}` is periodic of period `2π/log p`. It is therefore a function of the
local Frobenius alone. This is the de Branges form of "compatible with Frobenius at `p`".

**Def 2.2 (Frobenius-local Schur rule, FLS rule).** Let `𝔄` be a set of comb data. An *FLS rule on `𝔄`* consists of:
* (R1) for each prime `p` and each local datum `b` that occurs as `a_p` for some `a ∈ 𝔄`, a function
  `f_{p,b} ∈ H^∞(𝔻)` with `‖f_{p,b}‖_∞ ≤ 1` (a **local passive cell**). It depends only on `(p, b)`.
* (R2) one function `Θ♮_∞` on `{y > ½}`, finite-valued. **No analyticity or boundedness is required.** It depends on
  nothing but the fixed archimedean datum.
* (R3) for every `a ∈ 𝔄` and every `z` with `y > ½`, the identity
  `Θ_η[a](z) = Θ♮_∞(z) · lim_{P→∞} Π_{p≤P} f_{p,a_p}(p^{iz})`, where the limit exists.

Remarks on the definition.
* (R1) is the whole of the "structural positivity" demand. A contraction is the scattering function of a passive
  system, and conversely every contraction arises this way (Sz.-Nagy–Foiaș functional model, CITED: *Harmonic Analysis
  of Operators on Hilbert Space*, Ch. VI).
* (R2) is as weak as possible. The archimedean place may compensate anything that does not depend on the prime data.
* (R3) is required only in the half-plane of absolute convergence. That makes the hypothesis weaker and Theorem L
  stronger.

## §3 The Poisson–Jensen deficit lemma

**Lemma P (PROVED).** Let `f ∈ H^∞(𝔻)` with `|f| ≤ 1`, `f ≢ 0`. Let `h` be analytic on a neighbourhood of `𝔻̄` with
`|e^{h} f| ≤ 1` on `𝔻`. Then for every `w ∈ 𝔻`,
```
log|f(w)| ≤ − ((1−|w|)/(1+|w|)) · I(h), I(h) := ∫_0^{2π} (Re h(e^{iθ}))^+ dθ/2π .
```

*Proof.*
1. By the two hypotheses, `log|f| ≤ min(0, −Re h) = −(Re h)^+` pointwise on `𝔻`.
2. `log|f|` is subharmonic on `𝔻`. For `|w| < ρ < 1` the sub-mean-value property on the disc of radius `ρ` gives
  `log|f(w)| ≤ ∫ P_ρ(w,θ) log|f(ρe^{iθ})| dθ/2π`, where `P_ρ(w,θ) = (ρ² − |w|²)/|ρe^{iθ} − w|² > 0`.
3. Combining 1 and 2: `log|f(w)| ≤ −∫ P_ρ(w,θ)(Re h(ρe^{iθ}))^+ dθ/2π`.
4. Let `ρ ↑ 1`. For fixed `w`, `P_ρ(w,·) → P(w,·)` uniformly, and `(Re h(ρe^{iθ}))^+ → (Re h(e^{iθ}))^+` uniformly,
  because `h` is continuous on `𝔻̄`.
5. Finally `P(w,θ) = (1 − |w|²)/|e^{iθ} − w|² ≥ (1 − |w|²)/(1 + |w|)² = (1 − |w|)/(1 + |w|)`. ∎

**Two evaluations (PROVED, elementary).**
* **(i)** `h(w) = c·w` with `c > 0` gives `I = c ∫(cos θ)^+ dθ/2π = c/π`.
* **(ii)** `h(w) = c · qw/(1 − qw)` with `c > 0` and `0 < q ≤ 2^{−½}` gives
  `I ≥ 0.01657 · c · q`. This case covers single-prime removal, with `c = 2η log p` and `q = p^{−½}`.

  Proof of (ii):
  * `Re(qe^{iθ}/(1 − qe^{iθ})) = (q cos θ − q²)/(1 − 2q cos θ + q²)`, and the denominator is `≤ (1 + q)²`.
  * `∫_{cos θ > q}(q cos θ − q²) dθ/2π = (q/π) η₀(q)` with `η₀(q) := √(1 − q²) − q·arccos q`.
  * `η₀′(q) = −arccos q < 0`, so `η₀(q) ≥ η₀(2^{−½}) = 2^{−½}(1 − π/4) = 0.15175…`.
  * Hence `I ≥ c·q·0.15175/(π(1 + 2^{−½})²) = 0.016575… · c · q`.

## §4 Theorem L and its proof

**Theorem L (PROVED, unconditional; for every `η > 0`).**
* **(a) OT-4 substitutions.** Fix `ε > 0`. Let `𝔄 ⊇ {Λ} ∪ {Λ + εδ_p : p ∈ 𝒫′}`, where `𝒫′` is any set of primes with
  `Σ_{p∈𝒫′} p^{−½} = ∞` (for example all primes, or all primes in an arithmetic progression). Then no FLS rule on `𝔄`
  exists.
* **(b) Single-prime removals.** Let `𝔄 ⊇ {Λ} ∪ {Λ^{(p)} : p ∈ 𝒫′}`, where `Λ^{(p)}` has `a_p = 0` (the Euler factor at
  `p` removed) and `Σ_{p∈𝒫′} log p/√p = ∞`. Then no FLS rule on `𝔄` exists.
* **(c) General form.** Suppose that for each `p` in a set `𝒫′` the class `𝔄` contains `Λ` and some datum `a^{[p]}`
  that differs from `Λ` only at `p`, with `a^{[p]}_p = Λ_p + d_p`. If `Σ_{p∈𝒫′} I(2η g_{p,d_p}) = ∞`, then no FLS rule on
  `𝔄` exists.

*Proof of (c).* (a) and (b) are special cases, by evaluations (i) and (ii) of §3.
* In (a), `d_p = εe_1` and `g_{p,d_p}(w) = εp^{−½}w`, so `I = 2ηεp^{−½}/π`.
* In (b), `d_p = −Λ_p` and `h = 2η g_{p,d_p} = −2η log p · g_p` with `g_p(w) = qw/(1 − qw)`, `q = p^{−½}`. Since
  `g_p(0) = 0`, the mean of `Re g_p` on the unit circle is `0`, so `∫(Re g_p)^− = ∫(Re g_p)^+`. Hence
  `I(h) = 2η log p ∫(Re g_p)^+ dθ/2π ≥ 0.0331·η·log p/√p` by evaluation (ii), and
  `Σ_{p∈𝒫′} I_p = ∞`.

**Step 1 (the local ratio is forced).** Fix `p ∈ 𝒫′`, and fix `z` with `y > ½`.
1. `Θ_η[·]` never vanishes, since it is an exponential times `Θ_∞ = e^{−2ηψ_∞}`. So (R3) for `Λ` shows that
  `Θ♮_∞(z) ≠ 0` and that `L(z) := lim_P Π_{q≤P} f_{q,Λ_q}(q^{iz})` exists and is nonzero. In particular
  `f_{p,Λ_p}(p^{iz}) ≠ 0`. The same holds for `a^{[p]}`, with limit `L^{[p]}(z) ≠ 0`, so `f_{p,a^{[p]}_p}(p^{iz}) ≠ 0`.
2. For `P ≥ p` the two partial products differ only in their `p`-th factor. Hence
  `L^{[p]}(z)/L(z) = f_{p,a^{[p]}_p}(p^{iz}) / f_{p,Λ_p}(p^{iz})`.
3. By Def 2.1, `Θ_η[a^{[p]}]/Θ_η[Λ] = exp(2η g_{p,d_p}(p^{iz}))`. Dividing the two identities (R3), the factor
  `Θ♮_∞(z)` cancels whatever it is, and
  ```
  f_{p,a^{[p]}_p}(w) = exp(2η g_{p,d_p}(w)) · f_{p,Λ_p}(w) for all w = p^{iz}, y > ½,
  ```
  that is, on the punctured disc `0 < |w| < p^{−½}`.
4. Both sides are analytic on `𝔻`. By the identity theorem the equality holds on all of `𝔻`.

**Step 2 (each ζ-cell is forced to be strictly contracting).** Put `f := f_{p,Λ_p}`, which is nonzero by Step 1, and
`h := 2η g_{p,d_p}`, which is analytic near `𝔻̄`. Then `|f| ≤ 1` by (R1), and `|e^{h} f| = |f_{p,a^{[p]}_p}| ≤ 1` by
(R1) applied to the other datum. Lemma P gives
```
log|f_{p,Λ_p}(w)| ≤ − ((1−|w|)/(1+|w|)) · I(2η g_{p,d_p}) for all w ∈ 𝔻.
```

**Step 3 (the product dies).** Fix `z` with `y > ½`, and put `β(y) := (1 − 2^{−y})/(1 + 2^{−y}) > 0`. Since
`|p^{iz}| = p^{−y} ≤ 2^{−y}`, the factor `(1 − |w|)/(1 + |w|)` is at least `β(y)` at `w = p^{iz}`.
* For `p ∈ 𝒫′`, Step 2 gives `log|f_{p,Λ_p}(p^{iz})| ≤ −β(y) · I_p`.
* For `p ∉ 𝒫′`, `|f_{p,Λ_p}| ≤ 1`.

Therefore `|Π_{p≤P} f_{p,Λ_p}(p^{iz})| ≤ exp(−β(y) Σ_{p≤P, p∈𝒫′} I_p) → 0`, so `L(z) = 0`. This contradicts
Step 1.1. ∎

**Remark L.1 (sizes; PROVED).**
* In (a), each ζ-cell satisfies `|f_{p,Λ}(p^{iz})| ≤ exp(−(2/π)ηεβ(y)p^{−½})`.
* In (b), each ζ-cell satisfies `|f_{p,Λ}(p^{iz})| ≤ exp(−0.0331·ηβ(y)·log p/√p)`. The window form follows: the
  partial product over `p < e^{2t}` is at most `exp(−0.0331ηβ Σ_{p<e^{2t}} log p/√p) = exp(−(0.066 + o(1))ηβe^t)`,
  using `Σ_{p≤x} log p/√p ∼ 2√x` (PNT, CITED).
* Up to constants this is the growth `∼ 4e^T` of the comb mass after `cor:SC1`, and the capacity constant
  `A_P ~ Σ log p/√p` of R157_005 §4.2. The coincidence of the rates is PROVED. Reading it as "the same obstruction" is
  HEURISTIC.

**Prop L.2 (dichotomy; PROVED given R1 (A2)).** Drop the rule condition: let the cells `f_p ∈ ball H^∞(𝔻)` be chosen
for the single datum `Λ`, possibly depending on all of it. Require (R3) for `Λ` alone, with the product converging
locally uniformly on all of `C₊` and `Θ♮_∞` analytic on `C₊` with `|Θ♮_∞| ≤ 1`.
* Such a representation exists ⟺ RH.
* (⇐) Under RH, `Θ_η` is Schur on `C₊` (R1 (A1)). Take `f_p ≡ 1` and `Θ♮_∞ = Θ_η`.
* (⇒) The continuation of `Θ_η` equals `Θ♮_∞ · Π f_p(p^{iz})`, which is bounded by 1 on `C₊`. R1 (A2) then gives RH. ∎

This is Prop 5.1 of R2 in multiplicative dress, **EQUIV**. It shows that Theorem L's hypothesis "rule" (R1) cannot be
weakened to "choice" without collapsing to Lemma T.

**Cor L.3 (no local passive cascade; PROVED modulo the CITED dictionary).** Let `S_p` be passive linear systems
(contractive colligations) with scattering function `f_{p,b}(p^{iz})`, defined by a fixed rule from the local datum.
Let `S_∞` be any system built from Γ alone. The cascade connection `S_∞ · Π_p S_p` has scattering function equal to the
product of the factors. This is the multiplicativity of characteristic functions under cascade coupling (CITED:
Sz.-Nagy–Foiaș Ch. VII §1–2; Brodskiĭ, *Triangular and Jordan representations*, Ch. I).

So no such cascade reproduces Suzuki's `Θ_η[a]` for `Λ` together with the OT-4 substitutions, and none reproduces it for
`Λ` together with the single-prime removals. In particular the canonical-system Hamiltonian of Suzuki's chain (R2
Prop 5.1) cannot be assembled as a cascade of local positive blocks, one per prime, each defined from its Euler factor.

Scope: "cascade" here means **scalar multiplicative** coupling. Concatenating Hamiltonians on consecutive intervals
multiplies the `J`-inner transfer matrices. The induced operation on scattering functions is a Redheffer star product,
not the scalar product, so it is **not** covered (§5, escape E1).

  persists for cells with total negative index `< ∞`", is **OPEN** but looks routine: apply Lemma P to `f₀` on the
  primes that carry no negative square, and absorb the finite Blaschke products into `Θ♮_∞`, which (R2) permits.
* **(E3) Non-Frobenius-local cells.** The cells need not be functions of `p^{iz}` alone; for example, they could be
  mixed with the archimedean variable. Then "local rule" loses its meaning, and the construction falls under Prop L.2
  (EQUIV).

## §8 Weakest step

The mathematics of Theorem L is elementary and, I believe, airtight: an identity-theorem division in Step 1,
subharmonicity in Lemma P, and a divergent series. The **weakest point is interpretive**, namely the claim that
Def 2.2 captures "a Hamiltonian built directly from Γ and the Euler product with structural positivity".
* (R1) insists on (i) **scalar** cells, (ii) that are functions of `p^{iz}`, and (iii) **multiplicative** coupling.
* A memo who couples the cells by Redheffer products (E1), or allows finitely many negative squares (E2), is not
  covered.
* Cor L.3's translation to systems is CITED (Sz.-Nagy–Foiaș cascade multiplicativity) and applies only to cascades,
  not to concatenation of Hamiltonian intervals.
* The constant `0.01657` in evaluation (ii) is proved by hand (monotonicity of `η₀`); it is not interval-certified, and
  nothing depends on its value (any positive constant suffices, as does (a) with `1/π`).

**Recommendation.** Record Theorem L as the proved filter "no Frobenius-local passive cascade" (a companion to
`thm:SC`, B2's Thm E and D.252). Keep the canonical-system/de Branges angle **closed**: R2 Theorem G and Prop 5.1, plus
Theorem L and Prop L.2 here. Reopen it only with a Redheffer-coupled (E1) construction that carries its own proof of the
global-square step.
