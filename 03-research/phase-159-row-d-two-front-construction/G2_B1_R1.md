# G2_B1_R1 — G2 (the mechanism), round 1: structure vs randomness for the window comb

Angle (Tao school): split the prime comb exactly, `Λ = Λ^str + Λ^rnd`, and try to show that the structured part carries
the positivity uniformly in `T`. Normalisations are those of Paper 47 (`eq:symbol`, `eq:form`, `eq:W`):
`m(τ) = Re ψ(¼+iτ/2) − log π`, `v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n`, `𝔞_T(F) = (2π)^{-1}∫σ_T|F̂|²`, `P_T = {A(F)=B(F)=0}`,
`C_0 = ψ(¼) − log π = −5.3721834…`. For weights `a` on `n ≥ 2` write `𝔞^{(a)}_T` for the form with `v_n(a) = 2a(n)/√n`.

**Verdict, stated first.** The angle gives no mechanism. Three statements are new and proved. (i) Every "zero-free
completion" of the window comb gives an exact representation of the window form with no zeros in it (Prop. 1). (ii) The
full comb is exactly critical at the centre: `C_0 = −2ζ′/ζ(½)` (Lemma 2, classical). (iii) The canonical structured
part, the Selberg / Goldston–Yıldırım truncated divisor sum `Λ_R`, does **not** carry positivity: at infinitely many `R`
its primitive form is negative on `P_T` for all large `T` (Theorem 3, unconditional). The angle then closes by a
dichotomy (Prop. 5). Either the pseudorandom part is estimated, and `cor:void`/O3 exclude it, or it is treated exactly,
and then its spectral side is the whole zero measure, which is the restatement E3. No sign is claimed at any window.

## §1 Zero-free completions and the exact symbol identity

**Definition 1 (zero-free completion).** Fix `T > 0`. A *zero-free completion* of the window comb is a Dirichlet series
`D(s) = Σ_{n≥1} a_n n^{-s}` such that:
(a) `D` converges absolutely for `Re s > 1`;
(b) `a_n = Λ(n)` for `2 ≤ n < e^{2T}`, and `a_1 =: α ∈ ℝ` is free;
(c) `D` continues meromorphically to `Re s > ½ − η`, for some `η > 0`, with at most a simple pole at `s = 1` (residue `r_D`);
(d) `D` has polynomial growth in `½ ≤ Re s ≤ 2`, `|t| ≥ 1`.
Put `σ_D(τ) := m(τ) + 2α − 2 Re D(½+iτ)`. The coefficients are real, so `D(½−iτ) = \overline{D(½+iτ)}`.

Examples (all PROVED to satisfy (a)–(d); the growth comes from `ζ, ζ′ ≪ |t|^{1/4+ε}` on `Re s ≥ ½`, and the rest are
Dirichlet polynomials):
* **Selberg / Goldston–Yıldırım.** `Λ_R(n) := Σ_{d|n, d≤R} μ(d) log(R/d)` has `Σ Λ_R(n)n^{-s} = ζ(s)M_R(s)`, with
  `M_R(s) = Σ_{d≤R} μ(d) log(R/d) d^{-s}`. For `n ≤ R`, `Λ_R(n) = Λ(n)` (for `n ≥ 2`) and `Λ_R(1) = log R`. So `ζM_R` is
  a zero-free completion as soon as `R ≥ e^{2T}`, with `α = log R` and `r_D = M_R(1)`.
* **Heath-Brown.** With `M_z = Σ_{d≤z} μ(d)d^{-s}` and `k ≥ 1`,
  `−ζ′/ζ = −(ζ′/ζ)(1−ζM_z)^k + Σ_{j=1}^{k} (−1)^{j}\binom kj ζ′ζ^{j−1}M_z^j` (Heath-Brown 1982). The first term has
  coefficients supported on `n > z^k`. So for `z^k ≥ e^{2T}` the sum `D_{k,z}` is a zero-free completion.

**Proposition 1 (exact zero-free representation; PROVED; both forms).** Let `D` be a zero-free completion and
`F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` real. Then
```
𝔞_T(F) = (2π)^{-1} ∫_ℝ σ_D(τ) |F̂(τ)|² dτ − 2 r_D A(F)B(F),
W_T(F) = (2π)^{-1} ∫_ℝ σ_D(τ) |F̂(τ)|² dτ + 2(1 − r_D) A(F)B(F).
```
In particular, on `P_T ∩ C_c^∞`: `𝔞_T(F) = W_T(F) = (2π)^{-1}∫σ_D|F̂|²`.

*Proof.* Put `c = c_F ∈ C_c^∞(−2T,2T)`, which is even because `F` is real, and `C(z) := ∫c(u)e^{zu}du`. `C` is entire,
with `|C(x+iy)| ≪_N e^{2T|x|}(1+|y|)^{-N}`, `C(iτ) = |F̂(τ)|²` and `C(½) = A(F)B(F)`. By Mellin inversion,
`c(u) = (2πi)^{-1}∫_{(κ)} C(z)e^{-zu}dz` for every `κ`. For `κ > ½`, absolute convergence gives
`Σ_{n≥1} a_n n^{-½} c(log n) = (2πi)^{-1}∫_{(κ)} C(z) D(z+½) dz`.
Now move the line to `κ = 0`. This is legitimate by (c) and (d) together with the decay of `C` in vertical strips, and
it crosses only the pole at `z = ½`, whose residue is `r_D C(½)`:
`Σ_{n≥1} a_n n^{-½} c(log n) = (2π)^{-1}∫ |F̂(τ)|² D(½+iτ) dτ + r_D A(F)B(F)`.
Since `|F̂|²` is even, only `Re D` survives. By (b) and `supp c ⊂ (−2T,2T)`, the left side equals
`α‖F‖² + ½Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n c(log n)`. Hence the comb term of `𝔞_T` is
`Σ_{N_T} v_n c(log n) = (2π)^{-1}∫ 2Re D |F̂|² − 2α‖F‖² + 2r_D AB`.
Substitute this into `𝔞_T = (2π)^{-1}∫m|F̂|² − Σ v_n c(log n)`, and use `W_T = 𝔞_T + 2AB` for real `F`. ∎

**Corollary 1 (a new class of dual points; PROVED).**
(i) Weak duality: `λ^A_T ≥ inf_ℝ σ_D` for every zero-free completion `D`, and `λ^W_T ≥ inf σ_D` whenever `r_D = 1`
(because then `W_T` equals the integral on all of `C_c^∞`). Here `λ^A_T` is computed on `P_T ∩ C_c^∞`, which gives the
same bottom by density in the form norm (P47 §2).
(ii) No margin: `inf σ_D ≤ λ^A_T ≤ 4·10⁹·T·e^{−15.449T}` for `T ≥ 1` (`thm:margin`, `cor:margin1`).
(iii) Reading in the language of P47 (D): `σ_D − σ_T = −Σ_{n≥e^{2T}} 2a_n n^{-½}cos(τ log n)` (distributionally). This
difference has Fourier support in `{|u| ≥ 2T}`, so `σ_D` is a dual point with atoms on `log ℕ`. In general it is **not a
finite measure** (`Σ |Λ_R(n)|/√n = ∞`), so it lies outside Definition `def:PD`. Weak duality holds directly by
Prop. 1, not by `prop:weak`.

Contrast with `thm:Z`. A flat-top certificate damps `Λ` outside the window, and its value is a smoothing
`Σ_ρ ŵ(τ−γ)` of the zero measure. A zero-free completion replaces `Λ` outside the window by coefficients whose
Dirichlet series has **no** poles at the zeros, and its value `m + 2α − 2Re D(½+iτ)` contains no zero. The positivity
`σ_D ≥ 0` is a one-sided pointwise bound on a ζ-built Dirichlet series on the critical line:
`Re D(½+iτ) ≤ ½m(τ) + α` for all `τ`.

## §2 The centre identity

**Lemma 2 (classical; PROVED here).** Off the ordinates, `Re(ζ′/ζ)(½+iτ) = −½ m(τ)`. In particular
`C_0 = m(0) = −2ζ′(½)/ζ(½)`. Numerically, `C_0 + 2ζ′/ζ(½) = 0` to 30 digits (mpmath check, not load-bearing).

*Proof.* Put `ξ(s) = ½s(s−1)π^{-s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ(s)`. The function `ξ(½+iτ)` is real, so `ξ′/ξ(½+iτ) ∈ iℝ` off zeros. Also
`ξ′/ξ = 1/s + 1/(s−1) − ½log π + ½ψ(s/2) + ζ′/ζ`, and at `s = ½+iτ` one has `Re[1/s + 1/(s−1)] = 0`. Take real parts. ∎

**Reading.** As a distribution, the symbol of the *infinite* comb is `m − 2Re(−ζ′/ζ)(½+0+iτ) = 2π Σ_γ δ_γ` under RH
(the explicit formula). Pointwise off the ordinates it is identically `0`. At `τ = 0` the regularised comb mass
`"Σ_{n≥2} 2Λ(n)/√n" = −2ζ′/ζ(½)` equals the archimedean deficit `−C_0` **exactly**. So the complete comb sits exactly on
the critical level at the centre. This explains why the probes `F_T` of `prop:finsupp` (which concentrate at `τ ≈ 0`)
see `C_0 − A_S < 0` for any finite sub-comb, and a limit `0` (under RH) for the complete one.

## §3 The structured part does not carry positivity (the main theorem of this memo)

**Theorem 3 (the Selberg/GY structured comb; PROVED, unconditional; primitive form).** Fix `R ≥ 1`, and put
`σ^str_R(τ) := m(τ) + 2log R − 2Re(ζM_R)(½+iτ)` and `g(y) := σ^str_{e^y}(0) = C_0 + 2y − 2ζ(½) M_{e^y}(½)`.
(a) For every `T > 0` and real `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, `𝔞^{(Λ_R)}_T(F) = (2π)^{-1}∫σ^str_R|F̂|² − 2M_R(1)A(F)B(F)`, where
`Λ_R` is taken on `n ≥ 2`. **The explicit formula of the structured comb has an empty zero side.**
(b) `limsup_{T→∞} λ_min(A_T(Λ_R)|P_T) ≤ σ^str_R(0) = g(log R)`.
(c) The Laplace transform of `g` is `G(s) = C_0/s + 2/s² − 2ζ(½)/(s²ζ(s+½))` for `Re s > ½`. It is regular at
`s = 0`, and it has a pole at `s = ρ − ½` for every non-trivial zero `ρ`.
(d) `g` takes negative values and positive values on unbounded sets of `y`.
Consequently there are arbitrarily large `R` such that the primitive form of the structured comb `Λ_R` is negative on
`P_T` for all `T > T_R`. The structured part does not carry positivity uniformly in `T`, and its centre value is a
Mertens-type oscillation around the critical level `0`.

*Proof.*
(a) Apply the computation of Prop. 1 to `D = ζM_R` with no window condition. Nothing in that proof used (b) of Def. 1
except the identification of the comb, and here the comb is `Λ_R` itself.
(b) Use the argument of `prop:finsupp` with `σ` replaced by the `T`-independent symbol `σ^str_R`. Take
`F_T = (−∂² + ¼)g_0(·/T)` with `g_0 ∈ C_c^∞(−1,1)` real and nonzero. Then `F_T ∈ P_T ∩ C_c^∞`,
`F̂_T(τ) = (τ²+¼)T ĝ_0(Tτ)`, and the Rayleigh quotient is
`∫σ^str_R(s/T)(s²/T²+¼)²|ĝ_0(s)|²ds / ∫(s²/T²+¼)²|ĝ_0(s)|²ds`. The domination is
`|σ^str_R(s/T)| ≪_R (1+|s|)^{1/4+ε}` for `T ≥ 1`, from `m ≪ log`, `|ζ(½+it)| ≪ (1+|t|)^{1/4+ε}` and
`|M_R| ≤ Σ_{d≤R}log R/√d`. Also `ĝ_0` is Schwartz and `σ^str_R` is continuous at `0`. By dominated convergence the
quotient tends to `σ^str_R(0)`, and `λ_min` is at most the quotient.
(c) `g` is continuous and piecewise linear, and `M_{e^y}(½) ≪ e^{y/2}y`. For `Re s > ½`, Fubini gives
`∫_0^∞ e^{-sy} Σ_{d≤e^y} μ(d)d^{-½}(y − log d) dy = Σ_d μ(d)d^{-½-s}/s² = 1/(s²ζ(s+½))`, and
`∫_0^∞(C_0 + 2y)e^{-sy}dy = C_0/s + 2/s²`. At `s = 0`,
`1/ζ(s+½) = 1/ζ(½) − sζ′(½)/ζ(½)² + O(s²)`, so `−2ζ(½)/(s²ζ(s+½)) = −2/s² + (2ζ′/ζ(½))/s + O(1) = −2/s² − C_0/s + O(1)`
by Lemma 2. The poles cancel. The poles at `s = ρ−½` come from `1/ζ(s+½)`, with order equal to the multiplicity of `ρ`,
and `1/s²` does not cancel them because `ρ ≠ ½`.
(d) Suppose `g ≥ 0` for `y ≥ y_0` (the case `g ≤ 0` is the same with `−g`). By Landau's theorem for Laplace transforms
of eventually one-signed functions (Widder, *The Laplace Transform*, Ch. II, Thm 5b), the real point `σ_c` of the
abscissa of convergence is a singularity of `G`, and `σ_c ≤ ½`. On the real axis, `G` is analytic on `(−5/2, ∞)`. The
point `s = 0` is removable by (c). The real zeros of `ζ(s+½)` are at `s = −2k−½ ≤ −5/2`, and `ζ(0) = −½ ≠ 0`. Hence
`σ_c ≤ −5/2`, so `G` is analytic on `Re s > −5/2`. This contradicts the pole at `s = ρ_1 − ½ = 14.1347…i`. So `g` is
not eventually one-signed. It is continuous, so it is negative on an unbounded set, and likewise positive. The
consequence follows from (b) with any `R = e^y` at which `g(y) < 0`. ∎

*Illustration (double precision, not certified, not load-bearing):*

| `R` | 2 | 3 | 5 | 10 | 30 | 100 | 300 | 1000 | 3000 | 10⁴ | 3·10⁴ | 10⁵ | 2·10⁵ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `g(log R)` | −1.961 | −0.804 | −0.206 | −0.086 | +0.046 | +0.033 | −0.026 | +0.038 | −0.052 | +0.005 | −0.022 | −0.025 | +0.026 |

The amplitude `≈ 0.05` matches the size of the first zero term,
`|4ζ(½)/(ζ′(ρ_1)γ_1²)| ≈ 0.037` (HEURISTIC: under RH and `Σ 1/|ζ′(ρ)γ²| < ∞`, `g` is almost periodic in `y` with mean
`0`).

**Remark 3a (what Theorem 3 adds to the record).** `thm:SC` and `prop:finsupp` concern sub-combs with nonnegative
weights and finite mass. `Λ_R` has infinite support, signed weights, and a Dirichlet series with **no zeros at all**,
and it still fails. What decides its sign at the centre is not a mass deficit but the Möbius oscillation
`Σ_{d≤R} μ(d)d^{-½}log(R/d) − (log R)/ζ(½) + ζ′(½)/ζ(½)²`. Through `μ`, the zeros re-enter the "structured" part.
This is the exact form of the Tao dichotomy for `Λ`:

| part | Dirichlet series | what it carries |
|---|---|---|
| structured | `ζ·(Dirichlet polynomial)` | the pole, plus a continuous symbol, with no zero side |
| pseudorandom | `−ζ′/ζ − ζM_R` | the poles at every `ρ`, with residue exactly the multiplicity |

## §4 Margin inheritance and the closing dichotomy

**Proposition 4 (any exact split inherits the margin; PROVED).** Let `𝔞_T = Q^str + Q^rnd` on `P_T` be any exact
splitting of the window form. Suppose `Q^str ≥ δ_T‖·‖²` and `|Q^rnd| ≤ η_T‖·‖²` on `P_T`. Then
`δ_T − η_T ≤ λ^A_T ≤ 4·10⁹T e^{−15.449T}` (`T ≥ 1`). If `F_⋆` is a minimiser (`rem:resweights`), then
`Q^rnd(F_⋆) = λ^A_T − Q^str(F_⋆) ≤ λ^A_T − δ_T`.
So the pseudorandom part is never negligible against the structured margin at the minimiser: the structured margin
must be cancelled to within `e^{−15.4T}`.
*Proof.* Evaluate at `F_⋆`, and use `thm:margin`. ∎

**Proposition 5 (dichotomy for the angle).**
(i) *(PROVED, for the completions that continue to ℂ with polynomial growth in vertical strips. This includes
`ζM_R`, `D_{k,z}` and every `ζ^{(i)}·`polynomial combination.)* For such a `D`, the Dirichlet series `−ζ′/ζ − D` of
the pseudorandom part has a simple pole at each non-trivial zero `ρ`, with residue equal to the multiplicity `m_ρ`. Its
only other poles are at `s = 1` and at the trivial zeros. Hence the explicit formula for the pseudorandom comb, tested against `h = c_F`, has spectral side
`Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)` minus the continuous term `(2π)^{-1}∫(σ_D − m)|F̂|²`. **Its zero side is the entire zero measure.**
(ii) *(arg., method level in the sense of P46 Thm 18.1.)* An argument that bounds the pseudorandom part by an estimate
is excluded. By Prop. 4 the estimate must be exact to within `e^{−15.4T}`. By O3 (`rem:resweights`), changing one
`Λ(n_0)` by `ε_*` changes only the pseudorandom part (`Λ_R` is built from `μ`, not from `Λ`) and flips the sign. The
estimate classes are the `C_PNT`/`C_zeros` classes of `cor:void`. An argument that treats the pseudorandom part exactly
is, by (i), a statement about the whole zero measure, which is E3/`thm:Z` (a restatement).
(iii) *(arg.; F4.)* The identity `Λ = μ*log` and the split `ζM_R + (−ζ′/ζ − ζM_R)` exist verbatim for every Beurling
system (`Λ_𝒫 = μ_𝒫 * log`). So the decomposition uses nothing that the DMV system of T3 lacks. Any sign it yields must
come from A7/A8/A9 at a load-bearing step, and the decomposition supplies none.

**Remark 5a (arithmetic regularity).** A Green–Tao regularity decomposition of `(Λ(n))_{n<e^{2T}}` at error `ε` has
structured complexity that grows at least tower-type in `1/ε`. Prop. 4 and O3 force `ε ≲ e^{−14.4T}`, and there are only
`|N_T| ≈ e^{2T}` coordinates, so the vector itself is the cheapest "structured part". The finite regularity lemma
reproduces `prop:finite` and adds nothing (arg.).

**Remark 5b (the random-zeros probe).** By Prop. 5(i), the zero side of the pseudorandom part is the complete
zero multiset. A random finite subset of 100 zeros corresponds to a pseudorandom comb whose Dirichlet series has
finitely many poles, i.e. to no comb with the structure of Def. 1. Such a subset carries no certificate
(`thm:finZ`, whose proof along a lattice in `u` is insensitive to how the subset is chosen). The probe answers the
question "which infinite structure is needed" with: all of the zeros, through `−ζ′/ζ − D`.

## §6 What is genuinely new, and what is open

New (PROVED):
1. Zero-free completions and the exact window representation `𝔞_T = (2π)^{-1}∫σ_D|F̂|²` on `P_T` (Prop. 1). This is
  the "formulation" of identity-level Vaughan / Heath-Brown use that R157_002 row 30 said was missing. It is also a
  class of dual points that are not finite measures (Cor. 1).
2. The centre identity `C_0 = −2ζ′/ζ(½)` read as exact criticality of the complete comb at `τ = 0` (Lemma 2).
3. Theorem 3: the canonical structured part fails at infinitely many `R`, unconditionally, by Landau's theorem.

OPEN:
* **(Q1)** Strong duality in the class of Def. 1: is `sup_D inf σ_D = λ^A_T`? A positive answer would give the zero-free
  reformulation "RH ⟺ `∀T ∃D`: `Re D(½+iτ) ≤ ½m(τ) + a_1` on ℝ". That is a reformulation (EQUIV), not a mechanism.
* **(Q2)** Intermediate levels `R = e^{2θT}`, `0 < θ < 1`. Theorem 3(b) needs fixed `R`, because the symbol varies on the
  scale `1/log R`.
* **(Q3)** HEURISTIC: `inf_τ σ^str_R(τ) = −∞` for every fixed `R`, from large values of `ζ(½+it)` in resonance with the
  phases `d^{-it}`, `d ≤ R`. If so, fixed-level structured completions give no certificate at any window.

**Weakest step.** Theorem 3 refutes the structured part only on the unbounded set of `R` where `g(log R) < 0`, and only
for `T → ∞` at fixed `R`. The regime `R = R(T)` (Q2) is untreated. Prop. 5(ii) is a method-level argument ("arg."),
not a labelled theorem.
