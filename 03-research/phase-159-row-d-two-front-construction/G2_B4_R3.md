## §2 Theorem R′: dilated profiles fail by resolution

### 2.1 The family

**Definition 2.1 (dilated profile).** Let `Φ: ℝ → ℝ` be even with
* `Φ ≡ 1` on `[−1,1]`;
* `Φ` absolutely continuous, `Φ′` of bounded variation on `ℝ`, `‖Φ′‖_{BV} := ∫|dΦ′| < ∞`;
* superexponential decay: for every `A > 0`, `e^{A|x|}(|Φ(x)| + |Φ′(x)|) ∈ L^∞` and `∫e^{A|x|}|dΦ′| < ∞`.

Compactly supported `Φ` (e.g. a trapezoid, or a `C²` plateau function) are the main examples; Gaussian-tailed `Φ`
also qualify. The *dilated family* is `w_T(u) := Φ(u/2T)`, `T > 0`.

**Lemma 2.2 (PROVED).** (i) `w_T ∈ 𝒲_T` for every `T > 0` (with `C = C_T`, any `δ`). (ii) `ŵ_T(x) = 2T Φ̂(2Tx)`.
(iii) `|Φ̂(ξ)| ≤ ‖Φ′‖_{BV} ξ^{−2}` for `ξ ≠ 0`. (iv) There is `x_0 ∈ ℝ` with `Φ̂(x_0) < 0`.

*Proof.* (i) `w_T` is even, `≡ 1` on `[−2T,2T]`, and `|Φ(u/2T)| ≤ C_A e^{−A|u|/2T}`; take `A = 2T(½+1)` to get
`|w_T(u)| ≤ C e^{−(3/2)|u|}`, which satisfies def:W with `δ = 1`. (ii) Change of variable. (iii) Two integrations by
parts: `Φ̂(ξ) = −ξ^{−2}∫e^{iξx}dΦ′(x)`. (iv) `Φ` is even, continuous, in `L¹`, `≡ 1` on `[−1,1]`; by `lem:Rrig`
`Φ̂ ≥ 0` is impossible. ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (zero side; PROVED).** For every `T > 0` and `τ ∈ ℝ`, `f_{w_T}(τ) = Σ_ρ ŵ_T(τ−γ_ρ) = Σ_ρ 2TΦ̂(2T(τ−γ_ρ))`,
absolutely convergent.

*Proof.* `w_T` is an edge-profile weight in the sense of G2_B4_R2 Def. 3.1 with profile
`φ_T(v) = Φ(1 + v/2T)`: `φ_T(0) = 1`, absolutely continuous, `φ_T′` of locally bounded variation, and
`N_δ(φ_T) < ∞` for every `δ` by the superexponential decay. Apply Lemma Z′ (amended form, §4). ∎

### 2.2 The theorem

**Theorem R′ (PROVED).** **[compl]** Let `Φ` be as in Def. 2.1 and `w_T = Φ(·/2T)`.

(a) *(Unconditional.)* There are arbitrarily large `T` with `inf_τ f_{w_T}(τ) < 0`; the dilated family is not a
certificate family for `W_T ≥ 0` on any half-line.

(b) *(Under RH.)* Let `x_0` be as in Lemma 2.2(iv), `γ_1` any zero ordinate with multiplicity `m_1`, and
`d := min{|γ−γ_1| : γ ∈ Ord, γ ≠ γ_1} > 0`. Then for every `T ≥ T_0 := max(|x_0|/d, 1)`,

`f_{w_T}(γ_1 + x_0/2T) ≤ 2T m_1 Φ̂(x_0) + (2‖Φ′‖_{BV}/T)·Σ_{γ≠γ_1} m_γ(γ−γ_1)^{−2}`.

In particular `inf_τ f_{w_T} ≤ −c_Φ T` for all `T ≥ T_1(Φ)`, with `c_Φ = |Φ̂(x_0)|`, and the failure is not
recurrent but **permanent**: every large window fails, at depth growing linearly in `T`.

*Proof.* **Case ¬RH.** By Weil's criterion there is `T_1` with `λ^W_{T_1} < 0`; by `lem:monoT`, `λ^W_T < 0` for all
`T ≥ T_1`; by def:W, prop:weak, prop:P (the remark after def:W: `W_T ≥ inf f_w`), `inf f_{w_T} ≤ λ^W_T < 0` for all
`T ≥ T_1`. This gives (a) under ¬RH.

**Case RH.** All `γ` are real. Put `τ_T := γ_1 + x_0/2T`. By Lemma 2.3,
`f_{w_T}(τ_T) = 2T m_1 Φ̂(x_0) + Σ_{γ≠γ_1} m_γ 2TΦ̂(2T(τ_T − γ))`.
For `γ ≠ γ_1` and `T ≥ |x_0|/d`: `|τ_T − γ| ≥ |γ−γ_1| − |x_0|/2T ≥ |γ−γ_1|/2`. By Lemma 2.2(iii),
`|2TΦ̂(2T(τ_T−γ))| ≤ 2T‖Φ′‖_{BV}(2T|τ_T−γ|)^{−2} ≤ (2‖Φ′‖_{BV}/T)(γ−γ_1)^{−2}`.
The sum `Σ_{γ≠γ_1} m_γ(γ−γ_1)^{−2}` converges because `N(t) ≪ t log t`. This is the displayed bound; since
`Φ̂(x_0) < 0` the right side is `≤ −c_Φ T + O(1/T)`, negative for `T` large. This gives (b), and (a) under RH. ∎

**Corollary R′2 (mixed rules; PROVED).** **[compl]** Let `φ` be an admissible profile (G2_B4_R2 Def. 3.1) with
compact support or superexponential decay, and `a_T ≥ 1` with `a_T → ∞`. For the rule `w_T(u) = 1` on `[−2T,2T]`,
`w_T(u) = φ((|u|−2T)/a_T)` outside, there are arbitrarily large `T` with `inf_τ f_{w_T} < 0`; under RH,
`inf_τ f_{w_T} ≤ −c a_T` for all `T ≥ T_0`.

*Proof.* Lemma 3.2 of G2_B4_R2 with the dilated profile gives `ŵ_T(x) = 2Re[e^{2iTx}Ψ(a_T x)]·(a_T)`, more precisely
`ŵ_T(x) = 2Re[e^{2iTx} iΦ_1(a_T x)]/x` with `Φ_1(z) = ∫_0^∞φ′(v)e^{izv}dv`. The function `x ↦ ŵ_T(x)` is the
transform of the even weight `W_T(u) := w_T(u)`, which equals `1` on `[−2T,2T]` and is integrable and continuous;
by `lem:Rrig` it takes a negative value, say at `x_T`. Because `Φ_1(0) = −1` and `Φ_1` is continuous with
`|Φ_1(ξ)| ≤ C/|ξ|`, the function `ŵ_T` is `≥ (2/x)·(sin 2Tx)(1 + o(1))`-sized only for `|x| ≲ 1/a_T`, and there
`|ŵ_T(x)| ≍ a_T`; choose `x_T` in the first negative lobe of `x ↦ 2Re[e^{2iTx}iΦ_1(a_T x)]/x`, which exists in
`|x| ≤ C/a_T` (the phase `2Tx` and the amplitude `Φ_1(a_T x)` are both controlled there; if `a_T ≥ T` the lobe is that
of `Φ_1` itself, if `a_T ≤ T` it is that of `sin(2Tx)/x`), with value `≤ −c a_T`. The tail over `γ ≠ γ_1` is bounded,
for `|τ−γ| ≥ d/2`, by `Σ_γ 2‖dφ′‖·(a_T)^{−1}... ≤ C/(a_T d²)·Σ(γ−γ_1)^{−2}` using the second-derivative bound of
Lemma 3.2 (`|ŵ_T(x)| ≤ C N_δ(φ_{a_T})|x|^{−2}` with `N_δ(φ(·/a_T)) ≪ a_T^{−1}` for the derivative part). Then argue
as in Theorem R′(b) at `τ = γ_1 + x_T`. ∎

*Remark on the proof of Cor. R′2.* The constants are not tracked (the statement is qualitative); the mechanism is
the same as in Theorem R′: a kernel whose negative lobe has height `≫ 1` at a distance `≪ d` from a zero cannot be
screened by the other zeros, whose total contribution at that point is `O(1)` or smaller. Only the `a_T` bounded case
escapes this argument, and that case is Theorem R / Cor. R2 (almost periodicity).

### 2.3 What the two theorems say together

Write a rule as a family `T ↦ w_T ∈ 𝒲_T`. Under RH, `f_{w_T}(τ) = Σ_γ ŵ_T(τ−γ)`. The edge of `w_T` lives at a scale
`a_T`:
* `a_T` bounded (Theorem R, Cor. R2): `f_{w_T}(τ)` is a **mean-zero Bohr almost-periodic** function of `T` at every
  generic `τ`; it changes sign with bounded gaps.
* `a_T → ∞` (Theorem R′, Cor. R′2): `ŵ_T` **resolves the zeros**; its negative lobe of height `≍ a_T` sits at distance
  `≲ 1/a_T` from a zero and is not screened; failure is permanent with depth `≍ a_T`.

Neither regime is RH-sensitive in the direction that matters: both are *proved failures*, valid under RH. The only
rules not covered are those where `w_T` is chosen **per window from a non-convergent, non-dilating set**, i.e. the
free choice of `thm:Zexh`. By `cor:RH`, under RH such choices exist for every `T`; by Theorems R and R′ they can be
neither a fixed shape nor a dilated shape. This is the precise sense in which the "for all `T`" arrow cannot be a
formula: HEURISTIC reading of two PROVED statements.

## §3 Q-R(1): compact families — precise statement and why the single-`τ` reduction fails

**Statement (OPEN).** Let `𝒦` be a compact set of admissible profiles (in the `N_δ` norm, common `δ`). Say `𝒦`
*certifies from `T_0`* if for every `T ≥ T_0` there is `φ_T ∈ 𝒦` with `inf_τ f_{w^{φ_T}_T} ≥ 0`. Question: does
every compact `𝒦` fail to certify from every `T_0`?

By `cor:RH`, under RH the answer is "no" for `𝒦` large enough (the flat-kernel form of RH says the free choice works
for every `T`); a positive answer for a `𝒦` containing the `thm:Zexh` optimisers would refute RH. So the honest
question is the threshold: **is there a finite `𝒦` that certifies?** Under RH, this asks whether the certificates of
`cor:RH` can be chosen from finitely many shapes.

**Proposition Q1 (PROVED; a negative result about the method, not about the question).** Let `φ_1, …, φ_k` be
admissible. For fixed generic `τ ∈ E`, the functions `G^j_τ(T) = f_{w^{φ_j}_T}(τ)` are mean-zero Bohr a.p. (Theorem R,
Steps 1–2). The condition "for every `T` some `j` has `G^j_τ(T) ≥ 0`" is **not** contradicted by mean zero: the mean of
`max_j G^j_τ` is `≥ 0` and can be positive. Concretely, `max(G, −G) = |G| ≥ 0` for any real a.p. `G`.

*Proof.* Immediate. ∎

*Consequence.* No extension of Step 3 of Theorem R at a single `τ` can decide Q-R(1) for `k ≥ 2`. A proof must use
several `τ` simultaneously (the choice `j = j(T)` is one for all `τ`), and the coupling between different `τ` is the
zero configuration itself. Whether two admissible profiles with `G^2_τ ≈ −G^1_τ` at all generic `τ` exist is itself
constrained: exact antipodality `Ψ_2 = −Ψ_1` forces `φ_2 = 2 − φ_1`, not admissible; approximate antipodality at the
ordinates only is the zero-tuning question of Remark R3. **Q-R(1) stays OPEN**, and I do not expect it to be resolved
by SOS-side tools alone: its content under RH is the "finitely many shapes" question about `cor:RH`, which is a
statement about the zeros.

## §4 Lemma Z′, amended (panel fix)

**Lemma Z′ (PROVED).** Let `w ∈ 𝒲_T` be even, absolutely continuous, with `w′` of locally bounded variation and
`∫e^{(½+δ)|u|}(|w| + |w′|)du + ∫e^{(½+δ)|u|}|dw′| < ∞` for some `δ > 0`. Then for every `τ ∈ ℝ`,
`f_w(τ) = Σ_ρ ŵ(τ−γ_ρ)`, absolutely convergent.

*Amendments to G2_B4_R2.* In the proof, `χ ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)` is **even** with `χ ≡ 1` on `[−1,1]`, and the mollifier
`η ∈ C_c^∞` is **even**, `η ≥ 0`, `∫η = 1`, `η_{1/k}(u) = kη(ku)`. Then `h_k = (hχ(·/k)) ⋆ η_{1/k}` is even and in
`C_c^∞`, as required by the explicit formula in the form used in `thm:Z(ii)` (CITED: Weil 1952; Iwaniec–Kowalski
Thm 5.12). The hypothesis above is exactly what makes the `e^{(½+δ/2)|u|}`-weighted total variation of `h_k″` bounded
uniformly in `k` (the kinks of `w` give atoms of `h″` of fixed mass; the cutoff contributes `O(1/k)`). Everything else
is as in R2. The dilated weights of §2 and the compactly supported weights satisfy the hypothesis with every `δ`. ∎

## §5 Remark G: the GCD/Smith sum of squares (the "algebraic identity for all `N`", checked and excluded)

The angle's brief asks for an SOS with coefficients exactly `Λ(n)` valid for all `N` by an identity. There is a
classical one.

**Identity (PROVED; classical, Smith 1875 / Chebyshev `Λ ⋆ 1 = log`).** For every `N` and every `a ∈ ℂ^N`,

`Σ_{m,n≤N} a_m \bar a_n log gcd(m,n) = Σ_{d≤N} Λ(d) |Σ_{m≤N, d|m} a_m|² ≥ 0`.

*Proof.* `log gcd(m,n) = Σ_{d | m, d | n} Λ(d)`, by `Σ_{d|k}Λ(d) = log k` at `k = gcd(m,n)`. Expand the right side. ∎

**Why it is excluded (PROVED / by record).**
1. **F4 / `cor:void`.** The identity uses only `Λ ≥ 0` and the divisor lattice `d | m`. It holds verbatim for every
  Beurling generalised prime system (with `Λ_B ≥ 0` and its own divisibility), for the DMV and Epstein combs and for
  the modified combs of `thm:finmod`. A positivity that survives all falsifiers decides no window (`cor:void`; P46
  Thm 18.1(ii)).
2. **It is not the Weil prime side.** On Dirichlet-polynomial tests the prime side of `W_T` is the matrix
  `P_{m,n} = −Λ(m/n)(m/n)^{−1/2}` for `n | m` (and symmetrically), with **zero diagonal** and **negative** entries: it
  is indefinite by itself and must be dominated by the polar and archimedean parts. The GCD matrix has diagonal
  `log m` and positive entries. There is no change of basis `D` with `D^*(log gcd)D = −P + (polar + arch)`: the
  left side is PSD for every `N`, and the right side is PSD for every `N` only under RH (Weil), so any such identity
  would be a proof of RH from `Λ ≥ 0` alone, contradicting `cor:void`.
3. The GCD matrix does appear in the Nyman–Beurling Gram matrix (Vasyunin's formula) — row 43 of R157_002, EQUIV — but
  there the positivity is the trivial Gram positivity, and RH is the *approximation* statement `d_N → 0`, not a sign.

So the one exact all-`N` SOS identity with coefficients `Λ(d)` is Beurling-generic and off-target. This closes the
"algebraic" branch S3 from the other side: R1 showed there is no `ℚ̄`-algebraic identity *for the Weil form*; Rem. G
shows the algebraic identity that *does* exist is not about the Weil form.

## §7 What is genuinely new this round

1. **Theorem R′ and Cor. R′2 (PROVED).** Dilated profiles `Φ(u/2T)`, and edge profiles with diverging edge scale,
  fail for all large `T` under RH with depth `≍ a_T`, and for arbitrarily large `T` unconditionally. The proof is a
  two-line resolution argument (negative lobe of `ŵ_T` at distance `≲ 1/a_T` from a zero, tail `O(1/a_T)`), which
  turns one half of P47 `subsec:where` ("below `2πe^{2T}` the sum is about individual zeros") from DIAGNOSTIC into a
  theorem about certificate rules. Q-R(2) is closed negative.
2. **The two-regime picture (§2.3).** Bounded edge scale ⟹ mean-zero almost periodicity in `T` (R); diverging edge
  scale ⟹ resolution failure (R′). Every rule with a scale is covered; only the scale-free per-window choice of
  `thm:Zexh` survives, and that is `cor:RH`.
3. **Prop. Q1.** The single-`τ` route to Q-R(1) is closed; the question is a "finitely many shapes" question about
  `cor:RH`, hence about the zeros.
4. **Rem. G.** The Smith/GCD SOS identity, checked against F4/`cor:void` and against the Weil prime side, and excluded.
  New to the corpus as an explicit exclusion.
5. Lemma Z′ amended as the panel asked.

Not new: `lem:Rrig`, `lem:monoT`, Weil's criterion, the explicit formula, Chebyshev's identity.

## §8 Weakest step, and the honest verdict

* **Cor. R′2** is stated qualitatively and its proof sketches the location of the negative lobe in the two sub-regimes
  `a_T ≤ T`, `a_T ≥ T` without tracking constants; Theorem R′ (pure dilation) is fully explicit and is the
  load-bearing statement. If the panel prefers, Cor. R′2 may be downgraded to HEURISTIC-with-proof-sketch; nothing
  else depends on it.
* **Theorem R′ needs `Φ′ ∈ BV`** for the `ξ^{−2}` tail bound. Profiles with a mere jump in `Φ` (sharp truncation)
  give `Φ̂ = O(1/ξ)` and the tail sum `Σ|γ−γ_1|^{−1}` diverges; that case is `thm:arena`/`prop:finite` territory and
  is not claimed here.
* **[prim] is not covered.** Theorems R and R′ are about the complete form; primitive certificates carry the two
  per-window polar multipliers `β_0, β_1` (`thm:Zexh(ii)`), which are a 2-parameter free choice per `T`, i.e. a
  compact-family situation of the Q-R(1) type. Since `λ^W_T ≤ λ^A_T`, no primitive conclusion follows.
* **Verdict for G2.** The SOS/Positivstellensatz angle does not produce a mechanism and, after three rounds, is
  closed on every branch that is not literally `cor:RH`: covariant SOS = zero side (S1); windowed SOS with free weight
  = `thm:Zexh` (S2, EQUIV); algebraic identities = none for the Weil form (S3) and the one that exists is generic
  (Rem. G); fixed/convergent shapes fail (R, R2); dilated shapes fail (R′, R′2). What remains (Q-R(1), finite shape
