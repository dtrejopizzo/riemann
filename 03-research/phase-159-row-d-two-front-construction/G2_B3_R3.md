## §1 Theorem K: every all-`T` Hermitian square is Weil's criterion

**Setting.** `P_∞ := ∪_{T>0} P_T ∩ C_c^∞(ℝ) = {F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ): A(F) = B(F) = 0}` (primitive compactly supported tests);
`𝒟 := C_c^∞(ℝ)`. For `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` the value `𝔞_T(F)` does not depend on `T` once `supp F ⊂ I_T` (P46 `eq:A`:
the archimedean part is `T`-free and the comb sum over `𝒩_T` reads only `c_F(log n)` for `log n < 2T`, where
`c_F(log n) = 0` for `log n ≥ 2T` anyway). Write `𝔞(F)` and `W(F)` for these `T`-free values on `P_∞` and `𝒟`.
By `eq:W` and the explicit formula, `W(F) = Σ_ρ Φ_F(ρ)Φ_F(1−ρ)`, absolutely convergent for `F ∈ 𝒟` (P46
`lem:roughEF`, `[Paper45, Thm 9.4]`).

**Definition.** An *all-`T` Hermitian square for the primitive form* is a family `(B_j)_{j∈J}` of linear maps
`B_j: P_∞ → H_j` into Hilbert spaces, with `Σ_j ‖B_jF‖² < ∞` and
```
𝔞(F) = Σ_j ‖B_jF‖² for every F ∈ P_∞. (K)
```
No hypothesis is placed on the `B_j`: they may be nonlinear in `T`, non-covariant, non-local, built from primes,
from theta functions, from zeros, or from anything else. Likewise for the complete form with `𝒟` and `W`.

**Theorem K. PROVED.**
(i) If an all-`T` Hermitian square (K) exists for the primitive form, then RH holds.
(ii) If RH holds, an all-`T` Hermitian square exists for the primitive form and for the complete form.
(iii) Hence "there exists an exact identity `𝔞_T = Σ_jB_j^*B_j` on `P_T` for all `T`" is EQUIVALENT to RH, and
R2 §4's door (with its constraints (a)–(e)) is the statement RH, not a mechanism toward it.

*Proof.* (i) Let `T > 0` and `F ∈ P_T ∩ C_c^∞(I_T)`. Then `F ∈ P_∞` and (K) gives `𝔞_T(F) = 𝔞(F) ≥ 0`. The
form `𝔞_T` is continuous on its form domain in `P_T` and `C_c^∞(I_T) ∩ P_T` is dense in `P_T` for the form norm
(P46, proof of `prop:finite`(ii); the two moment constraints are continuous linear functionals, so the density
statement for `C_c^∞(I_T) ⊂ L²(I_T)` restricts to the primitive subspace after a two-dimensional correction inside
`C_c^∞(I_T)`, exactly as in the proof of `thm:Zexh`(ii)). Hence `𝔞_T ≥ 0` on `P_T`, for every `T`. By
`[Paper45, Thm 10.4]` / Bombieri (P46 §1.1, CITED), `∀T: 𝔞_T ≥ 0 on P_T ⟺ RH`.
(ii) Under RH every `ρ = ½ + iγ` with `γ ∈ ℝ`, and `Φ_F(1−ρ) = \overline{Φ_F(ρ)}` for real `F` (for complex `F`,
`W(F) = Σ_ρ |F̂(γ_ρ)|²` after polarisation, since `Φ_F(½+iγ) = F̂(γ)` in P46's convention `F̂(τ) = ∫F(t)e^{iτt}dt`).
Take `J = {ρ}`, `H_ρ = ℂ`, `B_ρF = F̂(γ_ρ)`. Then `W(F) = Σ_ρ|B_ρF|²` on `𝒟`, absolutely convergent by
`lem:roughEF`. On `P_∞`, `W = 𝔞`, which gives the primitive square.
(iii) is (i) and (ii). ∎

**Remark K1 (what the theorem does and does not say).** It does not classify squares (G2_B4_R1 Thm S1 does that under
covariance; R2 Thm F under multiplier structure; G2_B4_R2 Thm R under edge-profile structure). It says that the
*existence* question has no content beyond RH, whatever the structure. Consequently R2's list of five constraints
on a hypothetical mixed square — not all multipliers, FE at a load-bearing step, linear in `log ζ`, exact, not termwise
in the zeros — is a correct description of what a *proof of RH* through Weil's criterion must look like, and nothing
less. The word `OPEN` in R2 §4 was wrong; the right label is EQUIVALENT. Honesty requires recording this.

**Remark K2 (per window is different, and is the record).** At a fixed `T`, a Hermitian square for `𝔞_T` on `P_T`
exists iff `λ^A_T ≥ 0` (trivially, via the spectral theorem on the compression), and P46 `thm:KEP3`, `thm:Zexh`(ii)
describe them: flat-top explicit-formula certificates. The per-window squares are the record's certificates
(Paper 42 at `T ≤ log 2`, Zhu at `T ≤ 0.8`, `thm:C` at `½log3`). The "for all `T`" arrow is the passage from
per-window squares to one square on `P_∞`, and Theorem K says that passage *is* RH; `cor:mono` and W15 (F2) say
that it cannot be an upward induction. Nothing here is a margin or a truncation.

## §2 Theorem S: Selberg's identity splits

**Notation.** `𝒟` also denotes, in this section only, the ring of arithmetic functions under Dirichlet convolution
(R1 Lemma 1). `L(n) := log n`. For a completely multiplicative unimodular `χ ∈ G_T` (R1 Def. 1) put
`ι_χ(f) := fχ` (pointwise), a ring automorphism (R1 Lemma 1: `(fχ)∗(gχ) = (f∗g)χ`, `1χ = χ`, `μχ` is the inverse of
`χ`). The twisted comb is `Λχ`, with `Λχ = (μχ)∗(Lχ)`.

**Higher von Mangoldt functions.** `Λ_k := μ∗L^k` (`k ≥ 1`), so `Λ_1 = Λ`, and Selberg's identity is
`Λ_2 = Λ·L + Λ∗Λ`. In Dirichlet series (`σ > 1`): `Σ Λ_k(n)n^{−s} = (−1)^kζ^{(k)}(s)/ζ(s)`. The recursion
`Λ_{k+1} = Λ_k·L + Λ_k∗Λ` holds in `𝒟` (both sides have Dirichlet series `(−1)^{k+1}ζ^{(k+1)}/ζ`, or directly:
`L·(f∗g) = (Lf)∗g + f∗(Lg)` is the Leibniz rule for the derivation `f ↦ Lf` of `𝒟`, and `μ∗(L·1) = Λ`).

**Theorem S. PROVED.**
(i) *(ring half, covariant)* For every `χ ∈ G_T` and `k ≥ 1`: `(μχ)∗(L^kχ) = Λ_kχ`, and
`Λ_{k+1}χ = (Λ_kχ)·L + (Λ_kχ)∗(Λχ)`. In particular Selberg's identity holds verbatim for the twisted comb:
`Λ_2χ = (Λχ)·L + (Λχ)∗(Λχ)`. Vaughan's identity (with parameters `U, V`), Heath-Brown's identity (with parameter
`K`) and Linnik's identity hold for `(Λχ, μχ, χ)` in place of `(Λ, μ, 1)` with the same proofs. Hence any hypothesis
`𝓗` consisting of these identities, with any moduli and truncations, satisfies `𝓗(Λ) ⟹ 𝓗(Λχ)`, and by R1 Cor D
no derivation `𝓗(a) ⟹ W^{(a)}_T ≥ 0` (or `𝔞^{(a)}_T ≥ 0` on `P_T`) is valid at any `T ≥ 0.38`.
(ii) *(positivity half, class-level)* For every arithmetic function `a ≥ 0` supported on `n ≥ 2`, the function
`a_2 := a·L + a∗a` is `≥ 0`, and `a_k := a_{k−1}·L + a_{k−1}∗a` is `≥ 0` for all `k`. So `Λ_k ≥ 0` is an instance of a
statement valid on the class `𝒞_{≥0} = {a ≥ 0}`, which contains `Λ` and is closed under deletion of finitely many
terms and amplification of one term (`thm:finmod`(iii)). By `cor:void`, a derivation whose only prime input is
`Λ_k ≥ 0` (for any set of `k`) proves a false statement for some `a ∈ 𝒞_{≥0}` at every `T > ½log2`.
(iii) *(the two halves are disjoint)* The positivity `Λ_2 ≥ 0` is not twist-covariant: for `χ_2` (R1 §5,
`χ_2(2) = −1`, `χ_2(p) = 1` otherwise), `Λ_2χ_2(2) = −log²2 < 0`. The ring identity `Λ_2 = μ∗L²` is not
class-level: it fails for `a = Λ + cδ_{n_0}` (`c ≠ 0`) in the sense that `a·L + a∗a ≠ μ_a∗L²` where `μ_a` is the
inverse of the exponential of `a/L` — more simply, the identity that *does* hold for `a`, `a_2 = μ_a∗L²` with
`Σ μ_a(n)n^{−s} = 1/ζ_a(s)`, involves `μ_a ≠ μ`, so the exact-value content of Selberg's identity is exactly the
statement `μ_a = μ`, i.e. `a = Λ`, which is the datum F1 asks for and which the identity presupposes rather than
supplies.

*Proof.* (i) `ι_χ` is a ring automorphism of `𝒟` fixing `L^k` up to the twist: `ι_χ(L^k) = L^kχ`. Apply `ι_χ` to
`μ∗L^k = Λ_k`: `(μχ)∗(L^kχ) = Λ_kχ`. Apply `ι_χ` to the recursion, using `ι_χ(f·L) = ι_χ(f)·L` (pointwise products
commute with pointwise twists): `Λ_{k+1}χ = (Λ_kχ)·L + (Λ_kχ)∗(Λχ)`. Vaughan's identity is the ring identity
`−ζ′/ζ = F − ζ′G − FGζ + (−ζ′/ζ − F)(1 − ζG)` for `F = Σ_{n≤U}Λ(n)n^{−s}`, `G = Σ_{n≤V}μ(n)n^{−s}`, i.e. an identity
among truncations of `Λ`, `μ`, `L·1` and `1` in `𝒟`; Heath-Brown's is the expansion of `ζ^{−1} = ζ^{−1}(1 − (1 − ζG))^K`
plus binomial theorem; Linnik's is `log ζ = Σ_k (−1)^{k+1}(ζ−1)^k/k`. Each is a polynomial identity in `𝒟` among
`1, μ, Λ, L·1` and their truncations, and `ι_χ` preserves truncations (`ι_χ(f𝟙_{n≤U}) = ι_χ(f)𝟙_{n≤U}`). Hence
each holds for the twisted data. The conclusion is R1 Cor D.
(ii) `a ≥ 0` and `L ≥ 0` on `n ≥ 2` give `a·L ≥ 0`; `a∗a(n) = Σ_{mk=n}a(m)a(k) ≥ 0`. Induct on `k`. The class
closure is `thm:finmod`(iii) (both modifications keep `a ≥ 0`). `cor:void` applies with amplification at `n_0 = 2`.
(iii) `Λχ_2(2) = −log 2`, `(Λχ_2∗Λχ_2)(2) = 0` (no factorisation `2 = mk` with `m,k ≥ 2`), so
`Λ_2χ_2(2) = Λχ_2(2)·log 2 = −log²2`. For the second statement: for any `a` with `Σ|a(n)|n^{−σ} < ∞` (`σ > 1`),
put `ζ_a = exp(Σ a(n)n^{−s}/log n)` (P46 `thm:finmod`), `μ_a` the coefficients of `1/ζ_a`. Then `−ζ_a′/ζ_a = Σ a(n)n^{−s}`,
`ζ_a″/ζ_a = Σ a_2(n)n^{−s}`, and `a_2 = μ_a∗(L²·1_a)` where `1_a` are the coefficients of `ζ_a` — the Selberg identity
for `a` has `μ_a` and `1_a` in place of `μ` and `1`. It reduces to `μ∗L²` iff `ζ_a = ζ`, iff `a = Λ`. ∎

**Corollary S1 (the quadratic prime side). PROVED; no sign.** Let `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` be real and put
`c(u) := c_F(u)/u` for `u ≠ 0` (`c_F` is even, `c_F(u) = O(1)`; for `u = log n ≥ log 2` this is harmless). The prime
side of `𝔞_T(F)` is `−Σ_{n∈𝒩_T} v_n c_F(log n) = −2Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−½}L(n)c(log n)`. Selberg's identity gives
```
−2Σ_n Λ(n)L(n)n^{−½}c(log n) = −2Σ_n Λ_2(n)n^{−½}c(log n) + 2Σ_{m,k≥2} Λ(m)Λ(k)(mk)^{−½} c(log m + log k),
```
all sums finite (`c_F(u) = 0` for `|u| ≥ 2T`). The quadratic term is `2 ⟨Λ_w, M Λ_w⟩` with `Λ_w(m) = Λ(m)m^{−½}` and
`M_{mk} = c(u_m + u_k)`, `u_m = log m`. Since `c_F` is positive definite (`ĉ_F = |F̂|² ≥ 0`), the matrix
`[c_F(u_m − u_k)]` is PSD, but `M` involves `c(u_m − (−u_k))`, the block coupling the points `{u_m}` with `{−u_k}`;
such a block is not PSD in general (for two points it is `[[c(2u_1), c(u_1+u_2)],[c(u_1+u_2), c(2u_2)]]`, whose
determinant has no fixed sign). So Selberg's identity does not produce a square; it produces the first term, a
`Λ_2`-comb, which is a `𝒞_{≥0}`-class quantity (Theorem S(ii)), minus a quadratic form of indefinite type. ∎

*Reading.* This is the concrete form of R157_002 row 55's exclusion, and it also shows why "Selberg symmetry" cannot
be a mechanism: its positivity is `𝒞_{≥0}`-class (F1 fails by `cor:void`) and its identity is covariant (F1 fails by
Cor D). Neither half sees a single value `Λ(n)` beyond nonnegativity.

## §5 What is genuinely new

1. **Theorem K.** The record had three structural no-gos for all-`T` squares (covariant: B4_R1 S1; multiplier: R2
  Thm F; edge-profile: B4_R2 Thm R). Theorem K needs no structure at all and shows that the *existence* of an all-`T`
  square is Weil's criterion verbatim. It corrects R2's `OPEN` to EQUIVALENT and ends the search for "the right
  operators": the operators are not the problem, the sign is.
2. **Theorem S.** Selberg's identity (and `Λ_k`, Vaughan, Heath-Brown, Linnik) is split into a covariant ring half
  (Cor D) and a `𝒞_{≥0}` class half (`cor:void`), with the explicit witness `Λ_2χ_2(2) = −log²2` showing the halves are
  disjoint, and the observation that the exact-value content of any convolution identity for a comb `a` is the
  statement `μ_a = μ`, i.e. F1 itself. Row 55 of R157_002 excluded this by F3 only; it is now excluded by F1 twice
  over.
3. **Corollary S1.** The "quadratic prime side" of Selberg symmetry is written out and shown to be an indefinite
  off-diagonal block, not a Gram matrix.

## §6 Weakest step

Theorem K is elementary to the point of being a tautology once `∪_T P_T ∩ C_c^∞ = P_∞` is noticed; its only
non-trivial inputs are the density of `C_c^∞(I_T) ∩ P_T` in `P_T` for the form norm (P46 `prop:finite`(ii),
`thm:Zexh`(ii), used as in the record) and the cited theorem that primitive tests suffice for Weil's criterion
(`[Paper45, Thm 10.4]`, Bombieri 2000). Its value is not depth but the correction of a label: it removes the last
`OPEN` of this angle. Theorem S(i)'s covariance claim for Vaughan/Heath-Brown/Linnik rests on their being polynomial
identities in the Dirichlet ring among `1, μ, Λ, L·1` and truncations; I have checked this for the three standard
forms (Iwaniec–Kowalski Prop. 13.4, 13.3, and Linnik's `log ζ` series) but not for every variant in the literature
with smoothed truncations — those are also ring identities with weights, and `ι_χ` preserves pointwise weights, so
the claim extends, but I have not written each one out. No step here produces a sign; the memo's status is dead end.
