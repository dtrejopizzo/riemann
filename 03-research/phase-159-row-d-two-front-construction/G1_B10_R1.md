## §2 The literal versions of the angle, and why each is inert or equivalent

### 2.1 Random operators: randomisation is inert (PROVED, elementary)

**Proposition R.** Let `H_ω` be a random self-adjoint operator on a separable Hilbert space. Assume that almost surely
it has a complete orthonormal eigenbasis and spectrum `{γ_ρ}` with multiplicities `m_ρ`. Then almost surely `H_ω` is
unitarily equivalent to the fixed diagonal operator `H_0 = ⊕_ρ γ_ρ·1_{m_ρ}`. Every almost-sure spectral or positivity
property of `H_ω` is therefore a property of `H_0`, and the existence of such an `H_ω` implies RH (Lemma T(⇒), R158_001
§3.1).

*Proof.* Two self-adjoint operators with complete eigenbases and the same eigenvalues with the same multiplicities are
unitarily equivalent: map an eigenbasis to an eigenbasis. Self-adjointness forces real spectrum, so every `γ_ρ` is real,
which is RH. ∎

**Proposition R′ (point processes).** Let `X` be a simple point process on `ℝ` or `ℂ` whose first intensity is
absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure. This covers every determinantal process with Lebesgue reference
measure and a locally trace-class kernel. Then `P(x ∈ X) = 0` for each fixed `x`. So `X` cannot almost surely equal the
zero set, and it cannot contain any given zero with positive probability.

*Proof.* `P(x ∈ X) ≤ E#(X ∩ {x}) = ρ_1({x}) = 0`. ∎

*Reading.* A random object whose spectrum is "forced to be the zeros" is either deterministic, and then Lemma T applies,
or its reference measure is the zero-counting measure, which violates N1 and OT-3. The randomness adds nothing. For the
 probe of 100 random zeros: every realisation is a finite set, so `thm:finZ` kills it pathwise. Under constant
Bernoulli thinning the expectation is `p·f_w`, the full certificate scaled. The fluctuations, of variance
`≍ p(1−p) Σ_ρ ŵ(τ−γ_ρ)²`, carry no information about Λ, because the explicit-formula link to primes holds only for the
full measure (`HEURISTIC`).

### 2.2 The covariance of the Weil distribution: restatement of the window form (PROVED)

**Proposition C.** Fix `T`. There is a centred complex Gaussian linear process `{X(F): F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)}` with
`E X(F)\overline{X(G)} = ⟨g, F⋆G̃⟩` if and only if `λ^W_T ≥ 0` (**complete** form). The process is stationary: the
covariance depends on `F, G` only through `F⋆G̃`, so it is invariant under simultaneous translation inside the window.
Such processes exist for every `T` if and only if RH holds (Weil).

*Proof.* A Gaussian linear process with a prescribed Hermitian covariance form on a vector space exists if and only if
the form is positive semi-definite. (⇐) is Kolmogorov extension on finite-dimensional marginals; (⇒) holds because
`E|X(F)|² ≥ 0`. The form is `W_T` on `C_c^∞(I_T)`, which is a form core (`thm:KEP2` proof). ∎

*Reading.* "Positivity by construction (covariance)" becomes the **hypothesis** of Proposition C. The only positive
covariance the explicit formula offers is `g` itself, and its positivity is E1. Extending the window process to a
stationary process on `ℝ` is KEP(T) solvability (`thm:KEP1–3`, EQUIV of record, R157_002 row 3).

### 2.3 Covariances built from independent prime variables live on the wrong Bochner side (PROVED + HEURISTIC)

Take a stationary Gaussian process built linearly from independent standard Gaussians indexed by prime powers,
`X_t = Σ_n a_n ξ_n e^{iω_n t}`. Its spectral measure is `Σ_n |a_n|² δ_{ω_n}` (`PROVED`, direct computation). There are
two possible choices of frequencies:
* `ω_n = ±γ`: this is spectral data (N1).
* `ω_n = ±log n`: the prime-built covariance is a positive measure **on the prime axis** (A8). For example, `u ↦
  −ζ'/ζ(σ + iu)` is positive definite for `σ > 1`, with spectral measure `Σ Λ(n)n^{−σ}δ_{log n}` (`PROVED`, Bochner).

Weil positivity asks for the opposite: the distribution `g` on the prime axis must be **positive definite**, meaning
that its Fourier transform, the zero measure, is a positive measure on `ℝ`. In `g` the prime atoms enter as the
**negative** measure `−½Σv_n(δ_{log n} + δ_{−log n})`. P46 `lem:polar` adds one negative square carried by the pole. A
positive measure on one Bochner side is not positive definiteness on the same side. This is the Bochner form of the
"wrong sign" wall MW-4 (NO-GO-LIST) (`HEURISTIC` as a reading; the two facts it uses are `PROVED`).

## §3 The construction: the Cauchy-damped Weil covariance

**Definition.** For `ε ≥ 0` define the distribution `G_ε ∈ 𝒟′(ℝ)` by `⟨G_ε, h⟩ := ⟨g, e^{−ε|·|}h⟩`. Written out:

`⟨G_ε,h⟩ = C_0h(0) + ∫_0^∞(h(0) − e^{−εu}h_e(u))K(u)du − Σ_n v_n n^{−ε}h_e(log n) + 2∫h(u)cosh(u/2)e^{−ε|u|}du.`

The Lévy integral converges for Lipschitz `h`, because `K(u) ~ 1/u` at `0`. So `G_ε` is well defined and has order at
most 1. It is defined from `Λ(n)`, `n^{−ε}` and the Γ-factor. **It reads no zero (OT-3).**

For `F ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)` the **damped complete window form** is `W^ε_T(F) := ⟨G_ε, F⋆F̃⟩`. Its bottom on the unit sphere is
`λ^{W,ε}_T`, and `W^0_T = W_T`.

**Probabilistic reading (PROVED).** Let `M_cF(t) = e^{ict}F(t)`. Then

`W^ε_T(F) = E_{c∼Cauchy(ε)}[ W_T(M_cF) ]`.

*Proof.* `(M_cF ⋆ \widetilde{M_cF})(u) = e^{icu}(F⋆F̃)(u)` and `E e^{icu} = e^{−ε|u|}`. `G_ε` has order at most 1 and the
integrand is continuous in `c` in the relevant `C^1` seminorm on the compact support, so the Bochner integral and the
pairing commute. ∎

So `W^ε_T` is the Weil form averaged over random Cauchy frequency shifts of the test function. The object is the
stationary Gaussian process with covariance `G_ε`, when it exists. Its time shift `U_t` is unitary and its spectral
measure is `S_ε(τ)dτ/2π` (Theorem D). That measure is absolutely continuous. **The zeros appear as resonances** of the
continued density at `τ = γ' ± i(ε ∓ δ)`, not as spectrum. This answers the angle's premise: the spectrum is never "the
zeros" at any `ε > 0`.

## §4 Theorems

### Theorem D (damping ladder) — PROVED; zero-side content CLASSICAL

For `ε > 0` the following are equivalent:
* (i) `G_ε` is a positive-definite distribution: `⟨G_ε, F⋆F̃⟩ ≥ 0` for all `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)`;
* (ii) `Θ ≤ ½ + ε`;
* (iii) `∀T: λ^{W,ε}_T ≥ 0` (damped **complete** form).

When they hold, for every `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)`:

`⟨G_ε, F⋆F̃⟩ = (1/2π)∫|F̂(τ)|² S_ε(τ)dτ`, where `S_ε(τ) = 2 Re (ξ'/ξ)(½ + ε + iτ) = Σ_{pairs}
2[P_{ε−δ} + P_{ε+δ}](τ − γ')`.

In particular **(i)–(iii) hold unconditionally for every ε ≥ ½** (Θ ≤ 1). At `ε = 0` the statement is Weil's criterion.

*Proof.* (i)⟺(iii): every `F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ)` is a translate of an element of some `C_c^∞(I_T)`, and `F⋆F̃` is translation
invariant.

(ii)⟹(i), case `Θ − ½ < ε`. Put `φ = e^{−ε|u|}h` with `h = F⋆F̃`. Then `φ` is compactly supported and Lipschitz, and
`⟨G_ε,h⟩ = ⟨g,φ⟩ = Σ_ρ φ̂(γ_ρ)` (§1).
* On `ℝ`, `φ̂ = (2π)^{−1} ĥ ∗ k_ε`, with `k_ε(z) = 2ε/(ε² + z²)`.
* Both sides are holomorphic in `|Im z| < ε`: `ĥ` is entire and of Schwartz class on horizontal lines, and `k_ε` is
  holomorphic there. So `φ̂(z) = (2π)^{−1}∫ĥ(τ)k_ε(z − τ)dτ` on that strip.
* Every `γ_ρ` lies in the strip, since `|Im γ_ρ| = |δ_ρ| ≤ Θ − ½ < ε`.
* On `|Im z| ≤ Θ − ½` one has `|k_ε(x + iy)| ≤ C/(1 + x²)`. With `Σ(1 + γ'^2)^{−1} < ∞` and `ĥ = |F̂|²` rapidly
  decreasing, Fubini applies:
  `⟨G_ε,h⟩ = (2π)^{−1}∫|F̂(τ)|² Σ_ρ k_ε(γ_ρ − τ)dτ`.
* For a pair with displacements `±δ`, `k_ε(x − iδ) + k_ε(x + iδ) = 2Re k_ε(x + iδ) = 2[P_{ε−δ}(x) + P_{ε+δ}(x)] ≥ 0`,
  with `x = γ' − τ`.
* `Σ_ρ Re 1/(½ + ε + iτ − ρ) = Re ξ'/ξ(½ + ε + iτ)`, absolutely convergent, from the Hadamard product with
  `Re B = −Σ Re 1/ρ` (Davenport ch. 12, CITED). This identifies `S_ε`, and `S_ε ≥ 0` gives (i).

(ii)⟹(i), case `Θ − ½ = ε`. For each `h`, `⟨G_{ε′},h⟩ → ⟨G_ε,h⟩` as `ε′ ↓ ε`: the integrand converges pointwise under a
Lipschitz bound, so dominated convergence applies. A pointwise limit of nonnegative numbers is nonnegative.

(i)⟹(ii).
* A positive-definite distribution on `ℝ` is tempered: it is the Fourier transform of a positive tempered measure
  (Bochner–Schwartz; Schwartz, *Théorie des distributions* ch. VII §9; Gel'fand–Vilenkin vol. 4 ch. II; CITED).
* Let `χ ∈ C^∞`, `χ = 0` on `(−∞,1]` and `χ = 1` on `[2,∞)`. Then `χG_ε ∈ 𝒮′` is supported in `[1,∞)`. Its Laplace
  transform `L(z) = ⟨χG_ε, e^{−zu}⟩` is holomorphic on `Re z > 0` (Laplace transform of `𝒮′_+`; Vladimirov, *Methods of
  the theory of generalized functions*, §9; CITED).
* For `Re z` large, put `s = ½ + ε + z` and compute term by term on `u > 0` (the `δ_0`-term and the Lévy singularity are
  cut off by `χ`):
  * prime atoms: `−Σ_n χ(log n)Λ(n)n^{−s} = ζ'/ζ(s) + E_1(s)`, where `E_1` is a finite Dirichlet polynomial (entire);
  * polar term: `∫χ(u)(e^{(1−s)u} + e^{−su})du = 1/(s − 1) + E_2(s)`, with `E_2` holomorphic on `Re s > 0`;
  * Lévy term: `−½∫χe^{−(s−½)u}K(u)du = −Σ_k∫χe^{−(s+2k)u}du`, holomorphic on `Re s > 0`.
* Hence `L(z) = ζ'/ζ(s) + 1/(s − 1) + H(s)`, with `H` holomorphic on `Re s > 0`. The identity continues analytically to
  `Re s > ½ + ε` minus the zeros.
* `L` is holomorphic on `Re s > ½ + ε`. The pole of `ζ'/ζ` at `s = 1` cancels against `1/(s−1)`. A zero of order `m`
  would give a pole of residue `m ≠ 0`. So `ζ` has no zero with `β > ½ + ε`: `Θ ≤ ½ + ε`. ∎

*Remarks.*
* The proof of (i)⟹(ii) uses only temperedness. So **positive definiteness of `G_ε` already forces growth control of
  `ψ`**: `e^{−(½+ε)u}d(ψ(e^u) − e^u)` is tempered.
* Theorem D holds for `Ξ_a` with `Θ = ½ + a`, and for `ζ_K` with the same proof (`HEURISTIC` for general L-functions;
  not written out).

### Theorem E (the window ladder) — PROVED; the `T ≤ 0.8` clause uses Zhu (CITED)

1. **Schur monotonicity.** For `0 ≤ ε ≤ ε′`, `λ^{W,ε′}_T ≥ λ^{W,ε}_T` for every `T`.
  *Proof.* With `a = ε′ − ε` and `M_c` as in §3, `W^{ε′}_T(F) = ∫W^ε_T(M_cF)dP^{(a)}(c)`, where `P^{(a)}` is the
  Cauchy(`a`) law. Also `‖M_cF‖ = ‖F‖` and `M_cF ∈ C_c^∞(I_T)`, so the integral is at least `λ^{W,ε}_T‖F‖²`. ∎
2. **Continuity at 0.** `λ^{W,ε}_T ↓ λ^W_T` as `ε ↓ 0`.
  *Proof.* By 1., `λ^{W,ε}_T ≥ λ^W_T`. For fixed `F`, `W^ε_T(F) → W_T(F)`: the Lévy integrand is dominated by
  `C|u|·(1/u)` near 0, and there are finitely many atoms in the window. So `limsup λ^{W,ε}_T ≤ λ^W_T`. ∎
3. **Monotone in `T`.** `λ^{W,ε}_T` is nonincreasing in `T` (nested windows).
4. **Threshold function.** Put `ε_*(T) := inf{ε ≥ 0: λ^{W,ε}_T ≥ 0}`. Then:
  * `λ^{W,ε}_T ≥ 0 ⟺ ε ≥ ε_*(T)`. The function `ε ↦ λ^{W,ε}_T` is an infimum of continuous functions, hence upper
  semicontinuous, and it is nondecreasing, so the set `{ε: λ ≥ 0}` is closed.
  * `ε_*(T)` is nondecreasing in `T`.
  * `ε_*(T) ≤ ½` for all `T` (unconditional, uniform in `T`).
  * `ε_*(T) = 0` for `T ≤ ½log2` (Yoshida, Bombieri) and for `T ≤ 0.8` (Zhu 2026, CITED, not re-verified).
  * `sup_T ε_*(T) = Θ − ½`. *Proof:* combine Theorem D(iii) with the closedness above.
  * Consequently **RH ⟺ ε_*(T) = 0 for all T**. Also `λ^W_T ≥ 0 ⟺ ε_*(T) = 0`.
5. **A margin at every rung above Θ − ½.** For `ε > Θ − ½`, `λ^{W,ε}_T ≥ c(ε) := π^{−1}inf_τ Re ξ'/ξ(½ + ε + iτ) > 0` for
  all `T`.
  *Proof.* Use the spectral identity of Theorem D, Plancherel `‖F̂‖² = 2π‖F‖²`, and the fact that the pair terms are
  all positive. Positivity of the infimum: take the pair nearest to `τ`, whose ordinate is at bounded distance because
  gaps between consecutive ordinates are bounded (Riemann–von Mangoldt; CITED). It contributes at least
  `P_{ε+δ}(d) ≥ (ε − (Θ−½))/((2ε)² + d²)`, and `d` is bounded. ∎
  In particular, **for ε > ½ there is an unconditional margin, uniform in `T`**, for the damped complete form.

*Reading of Theorem E against the record.*
* `thm:margin` (C18: no uniform margin, `λ^A_T ≤ 4·10⁹Te^{−15.449T}`) is a phenomenon of the rung ε = 0 only. Every rung
  above `Θ − ½` carries a uniform margin.
* The one-way Schur monotonicity (damping can be added, never removed: `e^{ε|u|}` is unbounded, hence not a
  positive-definite multiplier) is the damping analogue of `cor:mono` / `lem:monoT` (no upward induction).
* The whole "for all T" arrow of the map becomes one question: can the nondecreasing function `ε_*(T)`, known to be
  `≤ ½` everywhere and `0` on `T ≤ 0.8`, be pushed down to `0`? Its supremum **is** `Θ − ½`, so any uniform improvement
  from `½` to `½ − η` is a zero-free half-plane `Re s > 1 − η`. No such `η > 0` is known (Vinogradov–Korobov regions
  shrink to `Re s = 1`).

  certify `W_T` at a window, and why it can be termwise positive in the zeros without contradicting `cor:nontermwise`.
* **`thm:finZ`, `thm:arena`**: nothing here is finite.
* **`cor:void` / O3**: the rungs ε ≥ ½ are class statements (they hold for every datum with Θ ≤ 1), hence silent on RH,
  exactly as `cor:void` predicts. The rungs ε < ½ are not decided by any known estimate.

## §6 What is genuinely new, and what it is not

**New (not located in the corpus; the literature novelty claim is HEURISTIC):**
1. The Weil-side ladder `G_ε = e^{−ε|u|}g` with the exact equivalence `PD ⟺ Θ ≤ ½ + ε` (Theorem D). The proof of
  (i)⟹(ii) goes through temperedness alone, and the reading as a Cauchy-randomised Weil form is PROVED.
2. The per-window structure: Schur monotonicity in ε, the threshold function `ε_*(T)` with `sup_T ε_*(T) = Θ − ½`, and
  `RH ⟺ ε_* ≡ 0` (Theorem E.1–E.4).
3. A uniform-in-T margin at every rung ε > Θ − ½, unconditional for ε > ½ (Theorem E.5). This isolates `thm:margin` as
  an ε = 0 phenomenon.
4. Propositions R and R′: random operators and determinantal processes cannot carry the zeros as spectrum except
  trivially.

**Not new:** the zero-side content of Theorem D (`Re ξ'/ξ ≥ 0` on half-planes ⟺ zero-free half-planes: Lagarias 1999;
Sondow–Dumitrescu; Freitas for Li-type half-plane criteria), Weil's criterion (ε = 0), and Proposition C (KEP of record).

**What this gives G1/G2.** The map's "for all T" arrow now has an interpolating parameter. Unconditional positivity
uniform in T exists at ε = ½. Every lowering of ε is a zero-free half-plane, and ε = 0 is RH. **The ladder does not
cross the gap. It shows that the gap has no intermediate rung easier than the classical quasi-RH, and that crossing it
by any amount η > 0 already needs non-Beurling input (T3).**

## §7 Weakest step

The (i)⟹(ii) direction of Theorem D rests on two CITED classical theorems of distribution theory, which I did not
re-derive here:
* positive-definite distributions are tempered (Bochner–Schwartz);
* the Laplace transform of a tempered distribution supported in `[1,∞)` is holomorphic on `Re z > 0`.

The term-by-term Laplace computation also needs the identity `L = ζ'/ζ + 1/(s−1) + H` for large `Re s`. That is
elementary, but the panel should check it line by line, including the partial weights `χ(log n)` for `e < n < e²`.
Theorem E.5 uses bounded gaps between consecutive zero ordinates (CITED). The novelty of the Weil-side packaging is
asserted only after a non-exhaustive search. Structurally, the weakest point is that **the only rung this construction
reaches unconditionally (ε ≥ ½) is RH-silent by construction (OT-4 fails)**. The angle therefore produces no OT-5′ object
at ε = 0, and I report it as a reformulation with a partial new result, not a construction.
