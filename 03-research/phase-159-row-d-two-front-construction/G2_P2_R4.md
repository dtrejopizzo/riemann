### §2.2 Can the rate target be a mechanism under a filter reading?

The strongest new claim of the round, made in B3 §4, B5 Q2 and B9 §5, is that the record's strongest G2 obstructions
are artefacts of normalising the target as a sign. I checked this against the text of `cor:void` and `rem:twohalves`.
They are correct as stated.

The robust target `κ(Λ) = 0` is not a new route, however. Theorem Q plus Weil gives `κ(Λ) = 0 ⟺ RH`. So a prime-side
proof of it is a prime-side proof of RH in von Koch's form. Every input consistent with an off-line zero is ruled out:
- for sign derivations, by O2 (R157_002 l.67);
- for rate derivations, by Theorem Q itself (Cor U1 / Prop Q2: the exponent is the zero displacement).

The residual obstructions (F4, T1, T2) are exactly those of the classical problem. **Verdict on the rate route:
EQUIVALENT; no mechanism.**

### §2.3 Correction: the direction of the P5 exponent

B3 Remark Q3 says P5's `e^{4εT}` "was pessimistic by a factor 2". B5 §0 and Verdict 1 call `e^{2ηT}` "the sharper
rate". Both invert the comparison.

P5's heuristic reads: the hypothesis `λ ≥ −Ce^{4εT}` implies no zeros in `Re s > ½ + ε`. That is the *stronger*
theorem, because its hypothesis is weaker. It is **not provable**: in the finite-exception scenario with
`Θ = ½ + δ`, B1 Prop Q2 (and B2 Cor U1) give `λ ≥ −2NTe^{2δT}`. That satisfies P5's hypothesis for any
`ε ∈ (δ/2, δ)`, yet a zero lies at `½ + δ > ½ + ε`.

B1 §0 states this correctly: P5's form is "too strong by a factor 2". Theorem Q's exponent `½ + κ/2` is the sharp one.
None of this is load-bearing. B3 and B5 should reword the remark.
