## G1_B2_R2 — excluded

**Checked (`PROVED`).**
* **Theorem S (i)–(ii).**
  * Bochner applies to the continuous extension of `n ↦ ⟨u(n)w,w⟩` from the dense subgroup `log G`.
  * Fourier–Stieltjes uniqueness on `T^∞ = Ĝ` gives `μ_w = ι_*ν`.
  * The converse follows from Lusin–Souslin and dominated convergence.
  * Everything is correct.
* **Lemma P.**
  * If `γ ≠ γ′`, the images `ι_{B_j}(γ)` and `ι_{B_j}(γ′)` coincide for at most one `j`. Coincidence at two primes of
  one block would force `log p / log q ∈ Q`.
  * A convergent infinite product needs `ν_j(point) → 1`, so two distinct points cannot both carry mass `> ½` in the
  same block. Correct.
* **Theorem E.**
  * The factorisation `μ_w = μ^{(0)} ⊗ ⊗_j μ^{(j)}` holds for elementary tensors in the incomplete tensor product
  (each `n` involves only finitely many primes).
  * Each section of `L` is countable, and the density and closedness argument goes through. Correct.
* **Cor E1.** The section is at most one point, because `ι_{P∖{p}}` is injective. Correct.
* **Cor D2.**
  * Suppose `π_p` has a nonzero `SL_2(Q_p)`-invariant vector. `SL_2` is normal in `GL_2`, so the invariant subspace
  is `GL_2`-stable. By irreducibility it is everything, and `π_p` factors through `det`. So `π_p` has no nonzero
  `SL_2`-invariant vector.
  * Howe–Moore then makes `diag(p^k, p^{−k})` mixing.
  * The spectral measure is therefore Rajchman, hence atomless by Wiener's lemma, and Cor E1 applies.
  * Correct, modulo the classical `CITED` inputs (Flath; genericity of the local components of cuspidal `π`).

**Literature.**
* **Theorem S** is the spectral-measure form of the **Bohr lift**. The dual of `Q^×_{>0}` is `T^∞`. The characters that
  are continuous for the archimedean topology are exactly `n^{iγ}`, which lie on the Kronecker line `ι(R)`, a Haar-null
  winding line. `CITED`:
  * H. Bohr (1913), the "Kronecker flow" on `T^∞`;
  * Hedenmalm–Lindqvist–Seip, *A Hilbert space of Dirichlet series and systems of dilated functions in L²(0,1)*,
  Duke Math. J. 86 (1997);
  * Saksman–Seip, arXiv:0808.0719 and the "vertical limits" literature.

  The programme may use it, but it is not new. The memo does not claim literature novelty, which is correct.
* **Lemma P and Theorem E** are Kakutani-type computations on infinite product measures (Kakutani, Ann. Math. 49
  (1948)). The memo cites this. The specific statement "at most one archimedean point per tail-factorizable summand"
  was not found in the sources I know, and I accept it as not in the literature. It is elementary.
* **Cor D2** is a standard use of Howe–Moore. The underlying phenomenon is that non-trivial local components are
  tempered or mixing, which forces continuous `U_p`-spectrum. Adelic Hecke dynamics are mixing: see Oh, *Uniform
  pointwise bounds for matrix coefficients*, Duke 2002, for the quantitative form.
* **The escape routes the memo names are real, and literature already puts two of them outside Theorem E's scope.**
  1. **Bost–Connes** (Selecta 1995). `Q^×_{>0}` acts on `Q[Q/Z] ⋊ N^×` by **endomorphisms/isometries**, a semigroup
  and not a unitary group. The KMS/partition function is `ζ(β)`. Theorem E does not cover it, and the memo says so.
  The Connes–Consani arithmetic site (arXiv:1405.4527) and scaling site (arXiv:1507.05818) carry `Frob_λ`,
  `λ ∈ R^*_+`, through the product formula. This is exactly the "entangling mechanism = product formula" of §5.3,
  and it is on record as zero-side EQUIV (R157_002 rows on CC).
  2. **Borger's λ-rings** (arXiv:0801.1691, 0906.3146) are the literature's own "Frobenius lifts at all primes
  simultaneously, commuting". This is the algebraic form of the "infinite entanglement" in N-e. On `Z` all `ψ_p = id`,
  so it provides no amplification. It is the natural structure to test N-e against, and I list it as a salvageable
  pointer.

**Fatal step (for G1).** Cor D2 itself: after suspension the positive space contains no zero (OT-1 and OT-2 fail).
Across the class, Prop R shows that zeros enter only by installation, so OT-3 and OT-4 fail. That is exclusion by the
memo's own proved theorem, in line with C5/C16.

**Objections.**
1. **Scope of N-e.** N-e is proved only for a Frobenius that is a **unitary group representation** of `Q^×_{>0}` with a
  Hilbert suspension. Every serious candidate in the literature (Bost–Connes, Connes' `L²(C_Q)` cokernel, CC `Ψ(λ)`,
  Deninger's `W_rat`) lies outside that scope or uses the product formula. N-e must enter the record as "PROVED in the
  suspension class", **not** as a necessary condition for OT-5′.
2. **Wording.** "Frobenius must be entangled across all but finitely many primes" is proved only in the
  tail-factorizable sense. Non-factorizable representations that are **not** entangled in any stronger sense are not
  classified.

**Salvageable.**
* Theorem S (the Kronecker-line criterion for what survives suspension) and Theorem E, as method constraints.
* The Bost–Connes and Borger test cases for the next round.

## G1_B6_R2 — excluded

**Checked (`PROVED`).**
* **Lemma S (a)–(c).** Correct. In (c), taking `s = π/(γ′−γ)` gives `U_s(e−e′) = e^{iγs}(e+e′)`.
* **Theorem A, dual reduction.** `(H/V̄)^* = V̄^⊥` isometrically, and `‖U_s|_{H/V̄}‖ = ‖U_s^*|_{V̄^⊥}‖`. Correct.
* **Consequence 1.3** needs `∫|t|^{2j}e^{2(β−½)t}w^{−1} < ∞`. This holds by the last condition on `w`, since
  `|β − ½| < ½`.
* **Case 3 of Theorem A.**
  * `‖h_γ − h_{γ′}‖ ≤ δK_w`, from `|e^{iγt} − e^{iγ′t}| ≤ δ|t|`.
  * `‖U_T^*x‖ ≥ 2sin(δT/2)n_w − δK_w`, and `δ ≤ π/T` gives `δT/2 ≤ π/2`.
  * Consecutive gaps tend to `0` by Riemann–von Mangoldt.
  * All correct. Cases 1 and 2 are correct.
* **Theorem B (i)–(iii).** Mean-value inequality plus Lemma S(c), and the gap `≤ 2π(1+o(1))/log X` in `(X, 2X]`.
  Correct. The (SG) clause is honestly conditional.
* **Cor B2.** Correct: `h_λ(F_λ) = 1` and `‖F_λ‖²_H ≲ 1 + μ*(λ)`.
* **Prop C.** Correct. For `supp F ⊂ (−ε, ε)` with `2ε < log 2`, `c_F` has no prime contacts. The archimedean symbol
  grows like `log|τ|`.

**Literature.**
* The mechanism of Theorem A/B is classical. For a family of exponentials `{e^{iλ_n t}}` to be a Riesz sequence (or
  uniformly minimal with bounded biorthogonal system) in a weighted `L²`, the frequencies must be **uniformly
  separated**. `CITED`:
  * R. M. Young, *An Introduction to Nonharmonic Fourier Series*, Ch. 4;
  * Seip, *Interpolation and Sampling in Spaces of Analytic Functions*, AMS 2004;
  * Pavlov (1979) and Nikolski, *Treatise on the Shift Operator*.
* ζ-ordinates are not separated, because their gaps tend to 0. So they are not a Riesz sequence of exponentials in any
  space of this type. That is known folklore; see also M10.3 and Day 12 in the corpus.
* Lemma S(c) is the group-theoretic form of the same fact. What is new for the programme is the **application to
  OT-5′**: "RH-strength" becomes "false" on Construction 9′, with no RH input. I found no such statement for Meyer-type
  carriers (Meyer, arXiv:math/0311468) or for Connes' `L²_δ` (math/9811068). Remark (i) of the memo is consistent with
  both.

**Fatal step (for G1).** Theorem A itself: Construction 9′ fails OT-5′ (P-HS) unconditionally. Construction 9 was the
round's only zero-blind, fully visible G1 candidate, and it is dead.

**Objections.**
1. **N-e is not a necessary condition in general.** "Super-critical resolution" is proved only in the class of Def
  B.1: a quotient `H¹`, a norm-`C¹` zero-free eigenfamily on all of `R`, and full visibility.
  * Connes' cokernel formalism, CCM's prolate carrier (arXiv:2310.18423) and any subspace or re-topologised carrier
  are outside it, as §8 admits.
  * Remark B.4, "OT-8 would need a trace identity", is `HEURISTIC`.
2. **Cor B3 rests on (SG).** (SG) is unproved even under RH. It must not be recorded as unconditional.
3. **Theorem A excludes too much to be informative about RH.** Case 3 is the RH-true case, and the carrier still fails.
  The theorem therefore says nothing about zero location. It is a statement about the topology. That is the memo's own
  reading, which is correct.

**Salvageable.**
* Lemma S and Theorem A as a proved exclusion.
* Prop C (Weil's form is discontinuous on sub-log carriers).
* Record N-e with its scope. It is a genuine design constraint, and it explains why every flow carrier with a fixed
  time weight fails.

## G1_B8_R2 — restatement

**Checked (`PROVED`).**
* **(G1).** The Hadamard sign on `σ > Θ*`, the shift `K̃ = e^{−δ′x}K_η`, and (E3). Correct.
* **(G2), Step 1 (Schur).** The translation `a = (k−j)/2` puts both pieces in `[(j+k)/2 − 2, (j+k)/2 + 1)`. The bound
  per pair is `A e^{2δ+4δ′}e^{−α(j+k)}a_jb_k`, and the resulting norm is `≤ A e^{2δ+4δ′}/(e^{2α}−1)`. Correct.
* **(G2), Step 2 (★).** I rederived the identity.
  * `F̌(z) = ∫_0^∞e^{i(w−z)x}dx = i/(w−z)` for `Im w > Im z = 1`.
  * The integrand is `−L̃(z)/((z−w)(z−w′))`.
  * The contour closes upward, with positive orientation.
  * The residues give `H(e_w, e_{w′}) = i(L̃(w) − L̃(w′))/(w′ − w)`, which is (★).
  * Convergence: the product of two `L²`-convergent factors converges in `L¹`, against a bounded `L̃`. Correct.
* **(G2), Step 3.** The right side of (★) is analytic in `w` on `C₊`, because `H` is bounded and bilinear and
  `w ↦ e_w` is analytic. The exponential of a pole is an essential singularity, which gives the contradiction. Correct.
* **Cor G.3.** Correct given R1 (A5), which was accepted in round 1.

**Literature.**
* Theorem G is the Paley–Wiener/Hankel form of the classical principle that the exponential growth rate of truncated
  norms equals the abscissa of boundedness of the symbol (Nehari; Peller, *Hankel Operators and Their Applications*,
  Ch. 1).
* Through `x = e^{2t}` this is Landau's `limsup log|ψ(x) − x|/log x = Θ*` (Ingham Ch. V).
* Suzuki's chain for `exp(−2ηξ′/ξ)` is arXiv:2012.11121. I found no statement there of the exact rate, which I accept
  as new in form. Mathematically it is EQUIV-family.
* Cor G.1 is exactly the classical gap: `ψ − x ≪ x^{½+ε}` ⟺ RH.

**Fatal step (for G1).** Prop 5.1: the positive Hamiltonian exists iff RH. OT-5′ is Lemma T in de Branges coordinates.

**Objection.** None on correctness.
* Cor G.4's "no finite or random subset changes the rate unless it approaches `Θ*`" is a correct reading of G3, but it
  is not new: Landau's theorem already sees only `Θ*`.

**Salvageable.** Record Theorem G and Cor G.1–G.4 as the closing record of the canonical-system angle, as the memo
recommends. I support closing the angle.

## G1_B9_R2 — restatement

**Checked (`PROVED`/`CITED`).**
* **Theorem 1** is Landau's oscillation theorem (Ingham, *Distribution of Prime Numbers*, Ch. V; Titchmarsh §14.?).
  The argument is correct: `ζ < 0` on `(0,1)` via `η(s) > 0`, the pole at `1` cancels, and Landau's theorem for a
  nonnegative integrand applies.
* **Prop 1.3.** Correct. `−ζ_a′/ζ_a` differs from `−ζ′/ζ` by an entire Dirichlet polynomial.
* **The correction to R157_002 row 29 is valid.** The global closing step needs no rationality. This is classical
  (Landau 1905), and the row's second reason should be amended. Its per-window exclusion stands.
* **Lemma 3** is trivial and correct.
* **Theorem 5 (i)–(ii).** Correct. `u = 0` on `(0,1)` (each `⌊t/k⌋ = 0`), and the telescoping is fine.
  * Strictness follows already from Schmidt's `Ω_±(x^{1/2})` (1903). Littlewood is not needed.
* **Theorem 4.**
  * (i) is Littlewood's theorem.
  * (ii) contains a **slip**. In van der Geer–Schoof (arXiv:math/9802121) the analogy is
  `h⁰(D) − h⁰(κ − D) = deg D − ½deg κ` with `deg κ = log|Δ|`. So the term matching `g − 1` is `½log|Δ_Q| = 0`, which
  makes `g = 1`, not `0`.
  * The memo body says "defect (`g − 1`) = 0", which is right. The summary "RR genus is 0" is wrong.
  * This is not load-bearing: any bounded error constant contradicts (i).

**Literature.**
* Theorem 5 is the known "too-good error term" principle:
  * Diamond–Erdős, Enseign. Math. 26 (1980), where the constants approach 1 only through the PNT;
  * Pritsker, arXiv:1307.5361, Prop 1.3.
  The memo cites both. The Chebyshev upper/lower version is an immediate corollary and folklore.
* Bombieri, Sém. Bourbaki 430: the anatomy (RR)/(Fr)/(L) is standard.
* For (Amp_x), the literature's closest object is again **Borger's λ-structure**: commuting Frobenius lifts at all
  primes. On `Z` it is trivial. On `W(Z)`/big Witt vectors it is non-trivial but amplifies nothing in the counting sense.
  I record this as a pointer, not a result. Buium's `δ_p` (cited) are per-prime.

**Fatal step (for G1).**
* There is no carrier (OT-1 fails).
* The global route is EQUIV: its input is the one-sided von Koch bound, which is RH (Theorem 1).
* Problem S is RH-strength by the memo's own statement.

**Salvageable.** The amendment of row 29 ("EXCL per window, EQUIV globally"), and Theorems 4 and 5 as the
"error at the fluctuation scale" constraint for G2.

## G1_B10_R2 — restatement

**Checked.**

  elementary.

* **Theorem 6.** I checked the arithmetic.
  * **Trudgian's bound** `|S(T)| ≤ 0.112 log T + 0.278 log log T + 2.510` (`T ≥ e`) is confirmed this phase (J.
  Number Theory 134 (2014), abstract). The `0.2/T` error in `N(T)` agrees with the same paper.
  * **Case B, zero count.** At `log τ = 28.7`: `R = 3.214 + 0.933 + 2.51 = 6.66`, and
  `D = 0.573·26.86 − 13.32 = 2.08`. So there are at least 3 zeros with `|x| ≤ 1.8`, each giving
  `t_ρ ≥ e^{−0.81}cos(0.45) = 0.40`.
  * **Case B, tail.** The tail with `|x| > 2π` is `≤ 2.75·10^{−4}(U+1) ≈ 5.3·10^{−3}`. Correct.
  * **Sign.** `cos(xδ/2) > 0` for `|x| ≤ 2π` uses `|δ| < ½`. Correct.
  * **Case A.** Correct.
  * So `ε_G ≤ 1` holds modulo (Z1)–(Z3), and (Z2) is Platt–Trudgian 2021.
* **Error in Remark 6.** "The Hasanalizade–Shen–Wong constants would only help" is false at the lower end. Their bound
  (arXiv:2107.06506) is `|S(T)| ≤ 0.1038 log T + 0.2573 log log T + 9.3675`, whose constant term is much larger. At
  `log T = 28.7` it gives `≈ 13.2`, against Trudgian's `6.66`, and `D` would be negative. This is not load-bearing,
  because Trudgian's constants are the ones used, but the remark must be corrected.

**Literature.**
* A Gaussian test in the explicit formula is classical. The prime multiplier `e^{−t log² n}` on `Λ(n)/√n` also appears

* **Mathematically, `RH ⟺ ε_G = 0` is Weil's criterion.** The memo labels it EQUIV, which is correct. The analogy with

**Objection.** §0 calls Theorem 5 "OT-5′ at the rung". That is legitimate only as "prior positivity of a *different*
