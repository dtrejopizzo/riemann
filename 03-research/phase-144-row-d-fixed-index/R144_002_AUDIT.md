### **M1 (MAJOR) — D.37.3(iii): "`Ψ` is an entire function of `T`" is not established, and (iv)'s quantifier is an overclaim.**

`Ψ(T) = −2Σ_ρ d_ρ\cosh((½−ρ)T)`, `d_ρ = Φ_{F_+}(ρ)Φ_{g_a}(1−ρ)`.

1. The bound the round gives, `|\cosh((½−ρ)T)| ≤ e^{θ|T|}` with `θ = ½+a`, is valid **only for
   real `T`**: for real `T` and `½−ρ = x+iy`, `|\cosh((x+iy)T)|² = \cosh²(xT) − \sin²(yT) ≤
   \cosh²(xT) ≤ e^{2θ|T|}` uniformly in `ρ`. For **complex** `T` the correct bound is
   `e^{|½−ρ||T|}`, and `|½−ρ| = |γ_ρ ∓ a·i^{0}| → ∞` over the zeros. So the series is **not** shown
   to converge locally uniformly on `C`, and "entire" is unjustified.
2. Local uniform convergence on a complex neighbourhood of `R` would require
   `Σ_ρ|d_ρ|e^{δ|γ_ρ|} < ∞`. Admissibility (Def. D.34.1 Convention) gives only **rapid** (super-
   polynomial) decay of `Φ_{g_a}(1−ρ)` — which is what Paley–Wiener yields for `C_c^∞` data along
   the imaginary direction — **not** exponential decay. So real-analyticity of `Ψ` on `R` is also
   not available from the stated hypotheses.
3. Consequently the step *"an entire function `≢ 0` has a discrete zero set"* fails, and with it the
   phrase **"outside a discrete exceptional set"**.
4. **What the argument does prove, and it is enough for the headline.** The Laplace-transform
   computation on `Re z > θ` (real `T ≥ 0`; the hypotheses there are exactly the ones the round
   has) is correct: `Ψ ≡ 0` on `[0,∞)` forces the residue `½(d_ρ + d_{1−ρ}) = d_ρ = 0` at every
   zero, hence `Φ_{F_+}(ρ) ≡ 0`, hence `F_+ ∈ 𝓝^{(a)}_∞`, hence `q_a = 0` — contradiction. (The
   evenness step `d_{1−ρ} = d_ρ` is correct: `F_+` is even by Prop. 12.4B, `g_a` even by
   admissibility.) `Ψ` is **continuous** on `R` (dominated by `Σ|d_ρ|e^{θ|T|}`, locally uniform on
   `R`), so `Ψ ≢ 0` gives a **non-empty open set** of `T` on which `Ψ ≠ 0`; intersecting with the
   complement of the countable admissibility-exclusion set (which the round derives correctly) is
   still non-empty. So: **there exists an open set of `T` for which `m_*(g^{(T)}_a) = 1`.**
5. **Additional overclaim.** Even granting entirety, D.37.3(iii) quantifies over `T` inside the
   one-parameter family `g^{(T)}_a = τ_Tg_a + τ_{−T}g_a`. D.37.3(iv) and the README then assert
   *"for every admissible `g_a` outside a discrete exceptional set"* — a quantifier over the **whole
   admissible class**, in which no "discrete exceptional set" has been defined (it is not a
   one-dimensional parameter space). This does **not** follow from (iii) even with entirety.

**Required repair (M1).** Replace D.37.3(iii)–(iv), the README bullet, and D.39's first paragraph by:
*"the smallest failing index for `Ξ_a` is `m = m_*(g_a) = \min\{n : t^{(a)}_n(F_+) ≠ 0\}`, finite by
(T1′)_a; and `m_*(g_a) = 1` for a non-empty open set of admissible test classes (the cosh family
`g^{(T)}_a` at all `T` in a non-empty open set, minus a countable set)."* Delete "entire", delete
"discrete exceptional set", delete "for every admissible `g_a` outside …". Nothing downstream
depends on the stronger form: D.38 never uses `m_* = 1` for `Ξ_a`, only that *some* fixed finite
index fails, which is D.37.3(i)+(ii) and is untouched. **The headline "the smallest failing index is
`m = 1`" survives, as an existence statement over `g_a`, not a genericity statement.**

## 9. Programme-level assessment

**The question.** After phases 139–144 the trace-cone route yields `RH ⟺ I_∞(E,g_λ) ≥ 0` on `S_0`.
Is there any remaining unconditional foothold on this page — O-30, an unconditional substitute for
`W(E) ≥ 0` (equivalently for `t_1(E) ≥ 0`) on `S_0` — or does the Riemann–Roch/Hodge-index frame
produce restatements only?

**Finding M3 (MAJOR, and it decides the phase-145 recommendation): O-30 is already closed,
negatively, by this round's own witness ray. The round recommends it as the phase-145 target
without noticing that D.38.2 answers it in one line.**

O-30 asks for an unconditional `W(E) ≥ −Ψ(a,b,\deg E)` on `S_0` with `Ψ` finite. Take the round's own
`E_t = tF + K` with `F` primitive, `\deg F = 0`, `q = I_∞(F,F) > 0` (which exists iff `¬RH`, Thm
10.4). Since `Φ_K(ρ) = ξ(ρ) = 0`, the zero data of `E_t` is `tΦ_F(ρ)`, so

    W(E_t) = t²W_F = −t²q \;\longrightarrow\; −∞ ,

while **simultaneously** `a = Φ_{E_t}(1) = ½`, `b = Φ_{E_t}(0) = ½`, `\deg E_t = 1` are **constant
in `t`**, and `E_t ∈ S_0` for every `t ≠ 0` (`R(E_t) = ½t²q > 0`). So no function `Ψ` of `(a,b,\deg)`
alone can bound `W` below on `S_0`: the three arguments of `Ψ` are frozen while `W → −∞`. Hence
**O-30 ⟺ RH**, closed negatively by the same single ray that closes O-29, D.38.4(iii) and D.38.5(i).
D.39(b) should be rewritten from "recommended phase-145 target; the witness ray suggests no" to
"closed, negatively, by the witness ray; `PROVED`".

**Assessment: the honest conclusion is that the Riemann–Roch / Hodge-index frame, as instantiated in
row D, produces restatements only, and the sign must come from outside it.** Reasons, in the order
of their force:

1. **One ray kills everything on this page.** `E_t = tF + K` is a *single* one-parameter family. It
   has frozen `(a,b,\deg) = (½,½,1)`, lies in `S_0` for all `t ≠ 0`, and has unbounded zero data. It
   simultaneously refutes: `(P_m)` at every fixed `m` (D.38.3), the sparse cone (O-29), the
   stratification of the `d = 1` head (D.38.4(iii)), bounded density on `S_0` (D.38.5(i)),
   the large-constant hope of the blueprint's D.38 item 3 (D.38.5a), and O-30 (above). Every
   "weakening" proposed in phases 143–144 is refuted by the *same* object. That is the signature of
   a frame whose unconditional content is exhausted: the frame's data `(a,b,\deg,R)` is invariant
   along the ray, and the truth of the clause is not.
2. **The frame's invariants are blind to the zero data.** `R(E) > 0` is, unconditionally and
   exactly, `2ab − W(E) > ½(a+b) > 0` (D.38.1). `W(E)` is a `ρ ↔ 1−ρ` **pairing**, not a norm; it is
   a norm **iff** RH. So `R > 0` constrains `W` from **above** only, and the only thing that could
   convert an upper bound on an indefinite pairing into control of `|Φ_E(ρ)|` is precisely the
   statement `1−ρ = \barρ`. Every unconditional statement in the frame is therefore a statement
   about `(a,b,\deg)` and about upper bounds on `W` — and the ray shows that is not enough.
3. **The two RH-uses in Thm D.34.10 are exactly the two facts the frame cannot see.**
   `W(E) = Σ|Φ_E(ρ)|² ≥ 0` and `|n^{½−ρ}| = 1`. Both are *zero-location* inputs, neither is a
   Riemann–Roch or Hodge-index fact, and D.37.4 shows that the one thing the frame *does* supply
   (the AM–GM polar floor) transports to the falsifier and separates nothing. Phase 144's
   contribution is precisely to have removed the last candidate that looked like a frame-internal
   separator.
4. **The falsifier is not merely a counterexample; it is a proof of frame-uniformity at every
   statement tested.** `Ξ_a` satisfies (T1′) (D.34.5 item 2), the transfer theorem D.34.8 verbatim
   (item 4), Prop. D.34.16's eventual half (Remark D.34.16c), the polar floor (D.37.2), the
   `\cosh` normal form (D.37.1(iv)), and the degree/`R` positivity of the ray (D.37.1(iii)). The
   *only* place `ξ` and `Ξ_a` part company is the existence of a primitive `F` with `I_∞(F,F) > 0`
   — which for `Ξ_a` is a theorem (Prop. 12.4B) and for `ξ` is literally `¬RH` (Thm 10.4). D.38.3c
   says this and it is the correct diagnosis: **the separation is the statement itself, not a step
   towards it.**
5. **The localisation results are real but localise the wrong thing.** Prop. D.34.16 (tail
   automatic, head finite per class, head uniform per slice under RH) is a correct and useful
   structure theorem. D.38.4(iii) shows it localises the **positivity** and not the **difficulty**:
   the finite head is `64` copies of RH.

**Therefore: no unconditional foothold remains on this page.** There is no remaining candidate in
row D's Horn B whose failure for `Ξ_a` is not literally the failure of the `ξ`-statement. I
recommend recording the route as **closed, with `RH ⟺ I_∞(E,g_λ) ≥ 0` on `S_0`
