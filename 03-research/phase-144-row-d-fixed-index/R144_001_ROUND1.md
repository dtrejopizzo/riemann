## D.37 — The failing index for `Ξ_a`

### D.37.0 Set-up, and the trace formula in the form used throughout

For an admissible `Z ∈ {ξ, Ξ_a}` with polar set `P` (`P = {0,1}` for `ξ`, `P = {−a,a,1−a,1+a}` for
`Ξ_a`, Proposition 12.6 (S1)), `P = 1−P`, and an admissible test class `g` (Definition D.34.1's
Convention: real, even, `≥ 0`, `≢ 0`, in `C_c^∞` or in `D^W` with rapid decay of `Φ_g(1−ρ)`, and the
Baire condition `Φ_g(1−ρ) ≠ 0` at every zero), Theorem 11.5 under (S1)–(S4) and
`Φ_{τ_c X}(s) = e^{(s−½)c}Φ_X(s)` (Lemma 11.2(iv)) give, for `n ≥ 1`,

    t^{(Z)}_n(E) = Σ_{p∈P} Φ_E(p)\,Φ_g(1−p)\,n^{½−p}  −  Σ_{ρ∈Z(Z)} Φ_E(ρ)\,Φ_g(1−ρ)\,n^{½−ρ} .
                                                                                        (D.37.1)

For `Z = ξ` this is exactly (D.26.1) of `R141_001` Lemma D.26.3 — `p = 0` contributes
`Φ_E(0)Φ_g(1)n^{½} = b\,Φ_g(1)n^{½}` and `p = 1` contributes `a\,Φ_g(0)n^{−½}` — so the blueprint's
pairing is confirmed against D.26.1: **`b = Φ_E(0)` carries `n^{+½}`**. Call the first sum the
**polar part** `A_n(E)` and the second the **zero part** `Z_n(E)`, so `t_n = A_n − Z_n`.

Two facts used repeatedly. (i) `g ≥ 0`, `g ≢ 0` gives `Φ_g(σ) = ∫g(u)e^{(σ−½)u}du > 0` for **every
real** `σ`; in particular `Φ_g(1−p) > 0` for all `p ∈ P ⊂ R`. (ii) `g` even gives
`Φ_g(s) = Φ_g(1−s)`, hence `Φ_g(1−p) = Φ_g(p)` and the weights attached to `p` and to `1−p` are
**equal**.

### D.37.2 The lemma on polar exponent sets — and the refutation of the proposed A3-line

**Lemma D.37.2 (the polar floor; `PROVED`, elementary, valid for every symmetric polar set).** Let
`P ⊂ R` be finite with `P = 1−P`, let `C_p > 0` for `p ∈ P`, and put `A(x) := Σ_{p∈P}C_p x^{½−p}` for
`x > 0`. Then:

 (i) *(weighted AM–GM, any `#P`)* since `Σ_{p∈P}(½−p) = 0` (because `P = 1−P`),

         A(x) \;≥\; \#P·\Big(∏_{p∈P}C_p\Big)^{1/\#P} \qquad\text{for every } x > 0 ,

     a **strictly positive floor, uniform in `x`**;
 (ii) *(pairwise)* pairing `p` with `1−p`, `A(x) ≥ Σ_{\{p,1−p\},\,p≠½} 2\sqrt{C_pC_{1−p}} \;(+\;C_{½}
      \text{ if } ½ ∈ P)`, again uniform in `x`, and this bound dominates (i). **[AMENDED, round 2,
      finding m3: the involution `p ↦ 1−p` has the fixed point `½`, whose term `C_½x^0 = C_½` is a
      constant and must be added as a singleton; vacuous for `ξ` and for `Ξ_a`, but the lemma is stated
      for every symmetric `P`.]**
 (iii) *(the equimodular two-point case)* if `P = {½−θ,½+θ}` then `\inf_x A = 2\sqrt{C_{½−θ}C_{½+θ}}`
       **exactly**, attained at `x = (C_{½−θ}/C_{½+θ})^{1/2θ}`;
 (iv) *(pair-symmetric coefficients)* if `C_{1−p} = C_p` for all `p` — which is the case for every
      **even** admissible `g` and every `E` with `Φ_E(1−p) = Φ_E(p)` (in particular `E = K_Z`) —
      then `A(x) = Σ_{\{p,1−p\}}2C_p\cosh((½−p)\log x)` is a sum of even convex functions of
      `\log x`, so `\inf_{x>0}A = A(1) = Σ_pC_p`, **attained at `x = 1`**.

*Proof.* (i) Weighted AM–GM with equal weights `1/\#P` applied to the positive numbers `C_px^{½−p}`:
`\frac1{\#P}Σ C_px^{½−p} ≥ (∏C_px^{½−p})^{1/\#P} = (∏C_p)^{1/\#P}x^{(Σ(½−p))/\#P} = (∏C_p)^{1/\#P}`.
`Σ_{p∈P}(½−p) = 0` because `p ↦ 1−p` is an involution of `P` pairing `½−p` with `−(½−p)` (it has no
fixed point unless `½ ∈ P`, and then that term is `0`). (ii) `C_px^{½−p} + C_{1−p}x^{−(½−p)} ≥
2\sqrt{C_pC_{1−p}}` termwise, and `inf(f+g) ≥ inf f + inf g`; `Σ2\sqrt{C_pC_{1−p}} ≥ \#P(∏C_p)^{1/\#P}`
by AM–GM on the `\#P/2` pairwise geometric means. (iii) is (ii) with one pair, with equality at the
stated `x`. (iv) `\cosh` is even and convex in its argument and minimal at `0`. ∎ `PROVED`

### D.37.4 Where the failure actually comes from

**Theorem D.37.4 (the true separating step; `PROVED`).** In Theorem D.34.10's proof for `ξ`, the three
ingredients are: the polar floor `A_n ≥ 2\sqrt{ab\,Φ_g(0)Φ_g(1)}` (Lemma D.37.2(iii)); the zero bound
`|Z_n| ≤ \sqrt{W(E)W(g)}`; and the gate `R(E) > 0 ⟹ W(E) < 2ab`. Transported to `Ξ_a`:

 (i) **the polar floor transports** (Lemma D.37.2(i),(ii),(iv)), and for the falsifier family it is
     even *attained at `n = 1`* and `r`-independent;
 (ii) **`|Z_n| ≤ \sqrt{W(E)W(g)}` does not**, for two independent reasons: `|n^{½−ρ}| = n^{½−\mathrm{Re}ρ}
      = n^{∓a}` is **unbounded** in `n` (the zeros of `Ξ_a` are `ρ ± a`); and
      `W_a(E) = Σ_ρΦ_E(ρ)Φ_E(1−ρ)` is **not** `Σ_ρ|Φ_E(ρ)|²`, because `1−ρ ≠ \barρ` for a zero of
      `Ξ_a` off `\mathrm{Re}\,s = ½` — so Cauchy–Schwarz against `W_a(E)` is unavailable;
 (iii) **the gate transports but is vacuous:** `R_a(E_r) > 0` holds for **every** `r` (Lemma
      D.37.1(iii)) precisely because `W_a` is indefinite, so it imposes **no bound whatever** on the
      zero data `(Φ_{E_r}(ρ))_ρ = r(Φ_{F_+}(ρ))_ρ`.

## D.40 — Closure of the trace-cone route

### D.40.1 The final form

**Restatement D.38.3a is the final form of the trace-cone route:**

    RH \quad⟺\quad I_∞(E,\,g_λ) ≥ 0 \quad\text{for every real } E ∈ D^W \text{ with }
    \deg E > 0 \text{ and } \tfrac12 I_∞(E,E) > \tfrac14\deg E ,

for `g_λ = K + λh` of Lemma D.34.9′ (`0 < λ ≤ (2W^{\mathrm{abs}}(h))^{−1/2}`). One bilinear
inequality, one test class, no index set, no cone, no `h⁰`, no transfer theorem. `PROVED` +
`RESTATEMENT`. **It is not progress towards a proof**, and phases 143–144 have now established that
nothing weaker survives: every weakening tried is equivalent to it.

### D.40.2 The single ray `E_t = tF + K` refutes every weakening

Let `F ∈ C_c^∞(R)` be real, primitive, with `q := I_∞(F,F) > 0` — an object that exists **iff RH is
false** (Paper 45 Theorem 10.4) — and let `E_t := tF + K`, `t ∈ R`. Then:

| invariant of the frame | value along the ray | |
|---|---|---|
| `a = Φ_{E_t}(1)` | `½` | **frozen** (`F` primitive) |
| `b = Φ_{E_t}(0)` | `½` | **frozen** |
| `\deg E_t = a+b` | `1` | **frozen** |
| `I_∞(E_t,K) = ½\deg E_t` | `½` | **frozen** |
| `R(E_t) = ½I_∞(E_t,E_t) − ¼\deg E_t` | `½t²q > 0` | **sign frozen** (`E_t ∈ S_0` for all `t ≠ 0`) |
| `W(E_t) = Σ_ρΦ_{E_t}(ρ)Φ_{E_t}(1−ρ)` | `−t²q → −∞` | **unbounded** |
| `Σ_ρ|Φ_{E_t}(ρ)|²` | `t²Σ_ρ|Φ_F(ρ)|² → +∞` | **unbounded** |
| `t_m(E_t)` (any fixed `m`) | `Φ_g(0)\cosh(½\log m) + t\,t_m(F) → −∞` | **unbounded below** |
| `|I_∞(E_t,F)|/\deg E_t` | `|t|q → ∞` | **unbounded** |

**Lemma D.40.2 (`PROVED`).** The ray exists iff RH is false; and if it exists, every row of the
second table holds. *Proof.* Existence: Paper 45 Theorem 10.4 and its converse (a primitive `F` with
`I_∞(F,F) > 0` contradicts RH by Paper 45 Theorem 10.4's "consequently" clause, which is Theorem
D.34.8's last step). The rows are Theorem D.38.3(i), P46-49, Prop. D.38.4(iii), Theorem D.38.5(i),
Cor. D.38.5a, Cor. D.38.5c, each proved above from the same three identities `\deg K = 1`,
`I_∞(F,K) = 0`, `I_∞(K,K) = ½` and from `Φ_K(ρ) = 0`. ∎ `PROVED`

> **The diagnosis.** The frame's unconditional data on a class are `(a,b,\deg E,R(E))` together with
> **upper** bounds on `W(E)`. Along the ray all of these are frozen (or frozen in sign) while the zero
> data is unbounded. A frame whose invariants are constant along a family on which the clause changes
> truth value cannot decide the clause. That is not an accident of the particular weakenings tried; it
> is a property of the invariants.

### D.40.3 What the frame cannot see, stated exactly

Two facts, and only two, are used by Theorem D.34.10 and are unavailable to the frame:

 (W1) **`W(E) = Σ_ρΦ_E(ρ)Φ_E(1−ρ)` is a norm iff RH.** Unconditionally it is a `ρ ↔ 1−ρ` **pairing**,
      indefinite; `W(E) = Σ_ρ|Φ_E(ρ)|²` requires `1−ρ = \barρ`. Consequently `R(E) > 0`, which reads
      `2ab − W(E) > ½(a+b)`, bounds `W` from **above** only (Prop. D.38.1), and Cor. D.38.5c shows no
      lower bound in the frame's invariants exists.
 (W2) **`|n^{½−ρ}| = 1` iff RH.** Unconditionally (E2) gives only `|n^{½−ρ}| = n^{½−\mathrm{Re}ρ} ∈
      (n^{−½},n^{½})`, and `\inf_ρ\mathrm{Re}\,ρ > 0` is a quasi-RH zero-free-strip statement, not
      supplied by any classical zero-free region (`R143_004` §5).

Both are **zero-location** statements. Neither is a Riemann–Roch fact, a Hodge-index fact, or a
statement about degrees, effectivity or the canonical class. The one genuinely frame-internal
ingredient that looked like a separator — the AM–GM polar floor — transports to the falsifier
verbatim (Lemma D.37.2, Remark D.37.5) and separates nothing. `PROVED` (each clause, at the
statements cited).

### D.40.4 The parting point with `Ξ_a`

`Ξ_a` satisfies, **verbatim and unconditionally**: (T1′) (`R143_001` §D.34.5 item 2); the transfer
theorem D.34.8 (item 4); the eventual half of Prop. D.34.16 (Remark D.34.16c); the polar AM–GM floor
and its `\cosh` normal form (Lemmas D.37.2, D.37.1(iv)); the degree and `R`-positivity of the ray
(Lemma D.37.1(iii)). The **only** point at which `ξ` and `Ξ_a` part company is:

> **there is a primitive compactly supported `F` with `I_∞(F,F) > 0`** — for `Ξ_a` a **theorem**
> (Proposition 12.4B, unconditional: its hypothesis "`Ξ_a` has an off-line zero" is automatic, since
> the zeros are `ρ ∓ a`), and for `ξ` **literally `¬RH`** (Paper 45 Theorem 10.4).

So the separation *is the statement itself*, not a step towards it: the falsifier is not a
counterexample that some finer argument might dodge, but a proof that every statement of the frame
tested so far is **model-uniform**, and that the one non-uniform statement is RH. `PROVED` (as a
reading of the cited theorems; cf. the scope caveat at D.37.4).

### D.40.5 Consequence

The trace-cone route (Horn B) is **closed**, with Restatement D.38.3a as its final form. **No new
positivity target inside the Riemann–Roch / Hodge-index frame is proposed by this round**, and the
two that round 1 proposed (D.39(a),(b)) are withdrawn as already decided. Continuations, if any,
must be outside the frame: Horn A (gated on O-19′/O-18′) or O-26/(T2) on `B4^{g,0}` — neither of
which is a positivity clause on `S_0`. `SYNTHESIS`
