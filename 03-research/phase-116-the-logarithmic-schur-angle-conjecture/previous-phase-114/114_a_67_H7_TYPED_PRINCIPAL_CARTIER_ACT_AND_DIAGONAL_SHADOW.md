# 114.a.67 — H7: typed principal Cartier act and ordinary diagonal shadow

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| DEFINITION  A regular central scalar cuts a quotient by E((s)).         |
| ACT         sO is a principal invertible right O-act, not an Ab-module. |
| BASE CHANGE Quotients commute with the pushout defining the square.     |
| PRIME       V_p is the generalized principal quotient cut out by p.     |
| DIAGONAL    Its ordinary layers are p^k Z / p^(k+1) Z = F_p.           |
```

## 1. A definition that is typed in Haran's category

Let `A` be a commutative generalized ring with involution.  Haran (4.2)
makes its symmetric unary scalars a central commutative monoid acting on
every operation set.  Sections 8.1--8.3 define equivalence ideals, the
equivalence ideal `E(a)` generated by an ideal `a`, and the quotient
generalized ring.

### Definition 1.1 (regular principal generalized Cartier datum)

A symmetric central scalar `s in A_[1],[1]` is **regular** if

\[
 sa=sa'\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=a'                                \tag{1.1}
\]

on every operation set under consideration.  Its regular principal
generalized Cartier datum is

\[
 D_A(s):=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(A/E((s))\bigr)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Spec}A,                                                \tag{1.2}
\]

together with the principal right `A`-act

\[
 sA=\{sa:a\in A\}.                                                     \tag{1.3}
\]

This is a definition inside the generalized-ring/act language.  It does not
call `A` an abelian module.

### Proposition 1.2

If `s` is regular, multiplication gives an isomorphism of right `A`-acts

\[
 A\xrightarrow{\sim}sA.                                               \tag{1.4}
\]

### Proof

It is surjective by the definition of `sA` and injective by (1.1).  Centrality
and associativity make it compatible with every right action.  The inverse
on the image is therefore action-compatible.  QED.

Thus (1.3) is the correctly typed analogue of an invertible principal ideal.
It is deliberately called an **act**, not a locally free object in Haran's
abelian module category.

## 2. Quotient base change

Let `C->A`, `C->B` be maps of commutative generalized rings and let
`q:A->A/E((s))` be the quotient.  Since the category is cocomplete and
`A tensor_C B` is the pushout, the universal properties give a canonical
isomorphism

\[
 (A/E((s)))\otimes_C B
 \simeq (A\otimes_C B)/E((\bar s)),                                    \tag{2.1}
\]

where `bar s` is the image of `s`.

Indeed, maps from either side to a target `R` are exactly pairs of compatible
maps `A->R`, `B->R` for which `s` maps to zero.  Yoneda proves (2.1).  This
argument uses only quotient and pushout universal properties; it does not
assume flatness.

## 3. Application to the prime ruling

Let `X=overline{Spec Z}`, `S=Spec F{+-1}`, and `Y=X x_S X`.  On the ordinary
finite-prime chart, `x_p` is the quotient of `X` by the equivalence ideal
generated by `p`.  Applying (2.1) gives

\[
 V_p=x_p\times_SX
 \simeq D_Y(p).                                                        \tag{3.1}
\]

This identifies the already literal ruling of `a_17` with a principal
generalized quotient, unconditionally.  If H7-PRIME-REG holds, `p` also
satisfies Definition 1.1 on every required square chart and pro-level.  Then
`pO_Y` is a principal invertible right act by Proposition 1.2.

Moreover H7-PRIME-REG puts `p` in the denominator system (11.1).  The local
multiplier `1/p` therefore defines the completed Section-11 lattice whose
unit torsor is `T_p`.  Hence, under H7-PRIME-REG, the three typed objects agree
at the level actually available:

1. the closed quotient `V_p=D_Y(p)`;
2. the principal ideal act `pO_Y`;
3. the completed inverse lattice generated by `1/p` and its torsor `T_p`.

No abelian conormal module is used in this comparison.

## 4. The ordinary diagonal-normal shadow

The diagonal satisfies `Delta^*p=p` and lands on the ordinary arithmetic
curve near `x_p`.  Define the zero-based diagonal layer by restricting the
principal filtration first and then using the ordinary ring chart:

\[
 \mathcal Q^{\Delta}_{p,k}
 :=p^k\mathbb Z_{(p)}/p^{k+1}\mathbb Z_{(p)},\qquad k\ge0.              \tag{4.1}
\]

This is a genuine ordinary module and

\[
 \mathcal Q^{\Delta}_{p,k}\simeq\mathbb F_p.                          \tag{4.2}
\]

Multiplication gives canonical graded maps

\[
 \mathcal Q^{\Delta}_{p,a}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}
 \mathcal Q^{\Delta}_{p,b}
 \xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal Q^{\Delta}_{p,a+b},                      \tag{4.3}
\]

with the distinguished class of `p^k` fixing the trivializations.  For
distinct primes,

\[
 \mathbb F_p\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb F_q=0.                         \tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently, for `n=product p^k`, the tensor of the selected ordinary
diagonal layers is `F_p` when `n` is a prime power and zero otherwise.  Its
logarithmic cardinality is exactly

\[
 \log\#\Gamma(\mathcal Q_n^\Delta)=\Lambda(n).                        \tag{4.5}
\]

This is a typed geometric origin for the contact shadow `M_n`: the ruling
and its powers live on the generalized square, while the additive normal
layer is formed only after restriction to the ordinary diagonal chart.

## 5. Exact scope

`a_67` closes, conditional on H7-PRIME-REG, the following weaker and fully
typed statement:

> **H7-CART-ACT-DELTA.** Prime rulings are regular principal generalized
> quotients with invertible principal right acts; their ordinary diagonal
> filtration has the contact algebra and mass `Lambda(n)`.

It does **not** construct:

1. a global abelian conormal sheaf `I/I^2` on the non-totally-commutative
   square;
2. a derived pullback functor or a `Tor` interpretation;
3. an intersection product away from the ordinary diagonal shadow;
4. dynamic kernels or their convolution.

Thus the extra type gate exposed by `a_66` is partially resolved without
undoing its retraction.  Full H7-CART-NORMAL remains stronger than
H7-CART-ACT-DELTA.
