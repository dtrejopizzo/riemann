# D.70 — Jet cyclicity and the Sturm--resolvent gate

## Status

This note investigates cyclicity of

\[
 u_e(t)=2\cosh(t/2),\qquad u_o(t)=2\sinh(t/2)              \tag{0.1}
\]

for the finite-window parity blocks of the coupled prime--Gamma operator.
Three conclusions are proved.

1. Each jet is cyclic for the complete source family of compressed
   symmetrized translations in its parity channel.
2. Cyclicity does not pass formally from that family to their single
   prime--Gamma aggregate.  In fact, the uncompressed aggregate in the
   first prime cell has spectral multiplicity at least two and admits no
   cyclic vector.
3. Even if finite-window cyclicity of the aggregate is granted, strict
   Sturm interlacing does not force primitive negativity or index one.  An
   exact cyclic finite-dimensional counterexample is given.  The surviving
   scalar condition is exclusion of positive zeros of the jet resolvent,
   which is exactly the D.56 Schur sign.

Thus a genuine finite-window uniqueness theorem for the actual aggregate
would remove persistent modes, but would not by itself close row D.  No RH,
zeta zero, Weil positivity or screw positivity is used.  The paper is not
modified.

## 1. Finite-window source cyclicity

Let `I_T=(-T,T)`, let `P_T` be restriction after zero extension, and write

\[
 v_{e,T}=1_{I_T}u_e,qquad v_{o,T}=1_{I_T}u_o.              \tag{1.1}
\]

Both are nonzero compactly supported functions.  Their Fourier transforms
are entire functions of exponential type and are not identically zero.
Consequently their real zero sets are discrete and

\[
 \widehat v_{\epsilon,T}(\tau)\ne0
 \quad\hbox{for almost every real }\tau.                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Suppose `f in L^2(I_T)` is orthogonal to every compressed translate

\[
 P_TS_av_{\epsilon,T},\qquad a\in\mathbb R.                \tag{1.3}
\]

After zero extension, the function

\[
 a\longmapsto\langle f,S_av_{\epsilon,T}\rangle           \tag{1.4}
\]

is their cross-correlation.  Its Fourier transform is, up to the fixed
Fourier convention,

\[
 \overline{\widehat f(\tau)}\,
 \widehat v_{\epsilon,T}(\tau).                            \tag{1.5}
\]

Equations (1.2)--(1.5) imply `widehat f=0` almost everywhere, hence `f=0`.
Therefore

\[
 \overline{\mathrm{span}}
 \{P_TS_av_{\epsilon,T}:a\in\mathbb R\}=L^2(I_T).         \tag{1.6}
\]

In a fixed parity channel it is enough to use the symmetrized translations

\[
 W_a=P_T(S_a+S_{-a})P_T.                                  \tag{1.7}
\]

Indeed, reflection gives

\[
 \langle f,S_{-a}v_{\epsilon,T}\rangle
 =\langle f,S_av_{\epsilon,T}\rangle                     \tag{1.8}
\]

when `f` and `v_(epsilon,T)` have the same parity.  Thus orthogonality to
all `W_av_(epsilon,T)` is orthogonality to every translate.

> **Theorem 1.1 (source-family cyclicity).**  In each parity channel, the
> corresponding Tate jet is cyclic for the family of all compressed
> symmetrized translations.  Equivalently, it is separating for the
> translation features before they are summed into the arithmetic
> operator.

This theorem includes every displacement as a source feature.  It does not
say that the jet is cyclic for one weighted sum of those features.

## 2. Aggregation can destroy cyclicity

For a self-adjoint family `A_alpha`, cyclicity of `u` for the von Neumann
algebra generated by the whole family does not imply cyclicity for one
element

\[
 A=\int A_\alpha\,d\nu(\alpha).                            \tag{2.1}
\]

The arithmetic aggregate exhibits this loss already before finite-window
compression.  In the first prime cell of D.69 its even Fourier multiplier
on the half-line is

\[
 b(\tau)=-\mathfrak a(\tau)
 +{2\log2\over\sqrt2}\cos(\tau\log2),qquad \tau\ge0.     \tag{2.2}
\]

D.69 proves, at

\[
 \tau_0={3\pi\over2\log2},                                \tag{2.3}
\]

that

\[
 b'(\tau_0)>0.                                             \tag{2.4}
\]

At

\[
 \tau_1={\pi\over2\log2},                                 \tag{2.5}
\]

one has `sin(tau_1 log2)=1`, hence

\[
 b'(\tau_1)=-\mathfrak a'(\tau_1)
             -\sqrt2(\log2)^2<0.                          \tag{2.6}
\]

The function `b` is real analytic.  It therefore has an interior local
minimum between regions where (2.6) and (2.4) hold.  On a nonempty interval
of values immediately above a strict such minimum, the map

\[
 \tau\longmapsto b(\tau)                                   \tag{2.7}
\]

has at least two inverse branches of positive measure.  If several critical
points share the minimum, the multiplicity can only increase; analyticity
excludes a constant interval.

The spectral representation of multiplication by `b` consequently has
fiber dimension at least two on a set of spectral values of positive
measure.  A single vector generates at most one scalar direction in each
fiber under polynomials or bounded Borel functions of `b`.  Hence:

> **Theorem 2.1 (aggregate multiplicity no-go).**  The uncompressed
> first-cell prime--Gamma multiplication operator has no cyclic vector in
> the even Fourier representation.  In particular Theorem 1.1 cannot be
> pushed through the arithmetic summation map as a formal cyclicity
> argument.

Finite-window compression is not multiplication by `b`; it can change
spectral multiplicity.  Theorem 2.1 therefore does not prove that the
finite-window jet is noncyclic.  It proves that such cyclicity, if true,
must come from a new boundary uniqueness theorem for the compressed
operator, not from scalar Fourier functional calculus.

## 3. What finite-window cyclicity would mean

Let `B_e` or `B_o` be a finite Galerkin compression, or a compact-resolvent
parity block with discrete spectrum.  A jet `u` is cyclic precisely when

1. every eigenvalue of `B_epsilon` is simple; and
2. the projection of `u` on every eigenvector is nonzero.

For finite matrices this follows from the spectral expansion of the Krylov
space

\[
 \mathrm{span}\,\{u,Bu,B^2u,\ldots\}.                \tag{3.1}
\]

For compact resolvent the same statement follows from the spectral theorem.
Thus proving cyclicity would indeed remove every persistent mode identified
in D.50 and D.69.

The available source properties do not prove these two assertions:

* the Gamma energy is positivity improving only for the unperturbed ground
  state, not for all excited parity states;
* the prime shifts have correlations of both signs;
* the aggregate scalar multiplier is nonmonotone and noncyclic before
  compression by Theorem 2.1.

No finite-window source proof of cyclicity is supplied by these facts.

## 4. Exact Sturm--resolvent formula under cyclicity

Assume now, solely for this section, that `u` is cyclic for a
finite-dimensional parity block `B`.  Let

\[
 m(z)=\langle u,(B-z)^{-1}u\rangle.                        \tag{4.1}
\]

If

\[
 \lambda_1>\lambda_2>\cdots>\lambda_N                    \tag{4.2}
\]

are the eigenvalues of `B`, then

\[
 m(z)=\sum_{j=1}^N{w_j\over\lambda_j-z},qquad w_j>0.      \tag{4.3}
\]

The derivative is positive between poles.  The eigenvalues

\[
 \mu_1>\mu_2>\cdots>\mu_{N-1}                             \tag{4.4}
\]

of the compression to `u^perp` are exactly the zeros of `m`, and strict
Sturm interlacing gives

\[
 \lambda_1>\mu_1>\lambda_2>\mu_2>
 \cdots>\mu_{N-1}>\lambda_N.                              \tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore the primitive sign in this channel is the scalar but still
nontrivial condition

\[
 \boxed{m(z)\ne0\quad(z>0).}                              \tag{4.6}
\]

Equivalently, the largest compression eigenvalue `mu_1` must be
nonpositive.  Cyclicity makes all inequalities in (4.5) strict; it does not
locate `mu_1` relative to zero.

## 5. Cyclicity plus Sturm interlacing is insufficient

Take

\[
 B=\mathrm{diag}(4,3,2,-1),\qquad
 u=(1,1,1,1)^T.                                           \tag{5.1}
\]

The Krylov determinant is a nonzero Vandermonde determinant:

\[
 \det[u,Bu,B^2u,B^3u]
 =\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)\ne0.                   \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus `u` is cyclic, all resolvent weights are positive, and strict
interlacing (4.5) holds.  Nevertheless the two-dimensional space spanned by

\[
 v_1=(1,-1,0,0)^T,qquad v_2=(1,0,-1,0)^T                 \tag{5.3}
\]

lies in `u^perp`, and the restricted Gram matrix of `B` is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}7&4\\4&6\end{pmatrix}>0.                 \tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore `B|_(u^perp)` has at least two positive directions.

> **Theorem 5.1 (cyclic Sturm no-go).**  Self-adjointness, simple spectrum,
> cyclicity of the exact jet and strict Sturm interlacing do not force the
> one-jet primitive compression to be nonpositive, nor do they force an
> index-one law for the full block.

The example can also be written `B=mI-D^*D` by choosing any `m>=4`; hence
the global mass-minus-feature-energy form does not repair the implication.

## 6. The remaining arithmetic statement

For the actual finite-window prime--Gamma blocks, cyclicity would be a
valuable theorem because it removes the persistent-mode clause from D.69.
It would reduce each parity channel to the scalar zero-exclusion (4.6).
But (4.6) is exactly the Schur/Feshbach sign already isolated in D.56:

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{cyclicity}&\text{all poles visible; strict interlacing}\\
 \text{row-D sign}&\text{no positive zero of the jet resolvent}.
 \end{array}                                               \tag{6.1}
\]

A complete Sturm route therefore needs two independent source theorems:

1. finite-window cyclicity or an exclusion of persistent modes;
2. a comparison locating the first resolvent zero at or below zero.

Theorem 1.1 proves the first statement only for the unsummed source family;
Theorem 2.1 prevents transferring it formally to the aggregate; Theorem 5.1
proves that even actual aggregate cyclicity would not supply the second.
No global index-one theorem is established here, so row D remains open.
