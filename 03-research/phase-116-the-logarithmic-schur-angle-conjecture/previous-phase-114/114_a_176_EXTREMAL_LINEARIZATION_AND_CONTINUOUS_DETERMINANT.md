# Extremal linearization and the continuous determinant of cohomology

## 1. The finite-depth exact category

For a periodic section module at a fixed Frobenius depth, let `Ext(E)` be
its finite set of extremal rays.  Work in the category `ExtPer` whose
objects are such filtered modules together with their canonical slope order
and whose isomorphisms are principal translations and periodic coordinate
isomorphisms.  These maps preserve slopes and hence induce order-preserving
bijections of extremal rays.

Define the extremal linearization

`T(E)=R[Ext(E)]`.

It is a finite-dimensional real vector space with a distinguished ordered
basis.  This is a functor on `ExtPer`.  It is not the free vector space on
all tropical sections; it is the tangent coefficient space of the canonical
extremal presentation.

Put the split exact structure on the finite-dimensional target category.
Then `T(E)[0]` is a perfect complex and

`Det R Gamma_ext(E):=det_R T(E)`

is an ordinary Knudsen--Mumford determinant line.  There are no hidden
higher groups in this definition: `R Gamma_ext(E)` is the displayed perfect
complex concentrated in degree zero.

## 2. Kunneth for extremal linearization

Let `E` and `F` be finite-depth periodic section modules with extremals
`phi_i` and `psi_j`.  In the functionally reduced external tensor, every
pair

`phi_i boxplus psi_j`

is extremal.  Indeed, choose points where `phi_i` and `psi_j` uniquely
dominate their respective extremal presentations.  Their product point is
a unique-dominance witness for the pair.  Conversely the external module is
generated by the pair functions, so it has no other extremal ray.
Therefore

`Ext(E widehat-boxtimes_red F)=Ext(E) x Ext(F)`.

It follows canonically, with lexicographic bases, that

`T(E widehat-boxtimes_red F) ~= T(E) tensor_R T(F)`.

This proves the finite-depth Kunneth theorem in the exact linearized
cohomology category.  In particular its dimension is the product of the
two dimensions, not an imposed numerical rule.

## 3. Realization inside the periodic coefficient topos

Let `O_per=y(0)` be the Day unit of the periodic coefficient topos.  At
depth `(r,s)`, attach to a line object `L(x)` the abelian coefficient object

`T_(r,s)(L(x))=R[Ext_(r,s)(Gamma_per L(x))] tensor_R R[O_per]`.

Here `R[O_per]` denotes objectwise real linearization of the representable
unit.  Evaluation at the unit is exact in a presheaf category and the
Yoneda lemma gives

`R Gamma(T_(r,s)(L(x))) ~=
 R[Ext_(r,s)(Gamma_per L(x))][0]`.

Thus the determinant in Section 1 is literally the determinant of derived
global sections of a finite locally free abelian coefficient object on the
same topos.  The functor `T` is canonical on the divisor-line subcategory
and on its principal isomorphisms.

## 4. Normalized continuous determinant

For the component `(p,q)`, let the two depth indices be `(r,s)` and let

`d_r=#Ext(E_r)`, `e_s=#Ext(F_s)`.

Give the distinguished wedge in

`det_R(T(E_r) tensor T(F_s))`

the trace-normalized norm

`exp(-p^(-r)q^(-s)d_r e_s)`.

The one-dimensional periodic Riemann--Roch theorem proves

`p^(-r)d_r -> max(delta,0)`,
`q^(-s)e_s -> max(eta,0)`.

All determinant lines are based, so the distinguished wedges give canonical
transition identifications with `R`.  Their norms have the unique limit

`exp(-max(delta,0)max(eta,0))`.

Define `Det_c R Gamma_ext` to be this based normed limit line.  Independent
cofinal changes of `r` and `s` give the same limit because each factor has a
separate limit.

For finite prime-pair support, determinant of a direct sum is the tensor
product of determinants.  Hence, for effective arithmetic `x`,

`-log ||1_x||=sum_(p,q)delta_p eta_q=d_1(x)d_2(x)`.

## 5. Functoriality and tensor law

Principal translations preserve the ordered extremal slope sets, so the
determinant line and its norm are representative-independent.  Symmetry of
the external product sends the lexicographic basis to the corresponding
Koszul-oriented basis; the standard determinant symmetry supplies the
sign.  Associativity follows from associativity of Cartesian products of
extremal sets and tensor products of vector spaces.

At finite depth, the ordinary determinant identity

`det(V tensor W)
 ~=det(V)^(tensor dim W) tensor det(W)^(tensor dim V)`

is compatible with the normalized trace weights.  Passing to the based
limit proves the continuous tensor law.

## 6. Finite contact and Green quotient

The reduced contact complex `[Z --p--> Z]` is already a perfect integral
complex.  Its determinant line has torsion norm `p^(-1)`.  Regard these
complexes as abelian coefficient objects supported on the diagonal
components of the periodic topos.  Finite-support tensor product gives the
contact determinant `lambda_C`.

The second difference of the continuous extremal determinant is

`B_RR(x,y)=d_1(x)d_2(y)+d_2(x)d_1(y)`.

Therefore

`lambda_G=delta Det_c R Gamma_ext tensor lambda_C^(-1)`

is a based normed line and

`delta Det_c R Gamma_ext ~= lambda_C tensor lambda_G`

is a canonical isometry.  This is an equality in an actual determinant
Picard groupoid: the Riemann--Roch factor comes from perfect finite-depth
global-section complexes and the finite factor from perfect torsion
contact complexes.

## 7. Scope

The construction is the determinant of the canonical extremal
linearization of periodic cohomology.  It is not the determinant of the raw
spherical smash; that object has exponential absolute dimension and cannot
produce surface Riemann--Roch.  No claim identifying these two inequivalent
cohomology functors is made.
