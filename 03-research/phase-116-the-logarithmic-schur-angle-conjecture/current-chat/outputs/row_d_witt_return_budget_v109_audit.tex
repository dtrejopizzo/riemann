\section{Fixed-depth asymptotics}

Write $\Lambda_k=\Lambda^{*k}$ and, as in row (d), put
\begin{equation}
 V_{N,k}=\sum_{m\le N}\frac{\Lambda_k(m)^2}{m},
 \qquad H_{N,k}=\frac{\Lambda_{2k}(N)}{\sqrt N}.                \tag{1}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact fixed-depth constant --- \PROVED]
For every fixed integer $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{V_{N,k}}{(\log N)^{2k}}
       =\frac{k!}{(2k)!}.}                                    \tag{2}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The leading stratum consists of squarefree integers
$m=p_1\cdots p_k$ with distinct primes.  On this stratum
\[
 \Lambda_k(m)=k!\prod_{j=1}^k\log p_j.
\]
The prime number theorem and partial summation give, for $0\le y\le1$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{(\log N)^2}\sum_{p\le N^y}\frac{(\log p)^2}{p}
       \longrightarrow\frac{y^2}{2}.                          \tag{3}
\end{equation}
Thus the normalized prime measures converge to $y\,dy$.  Product-measure
convergence on the simplex $y_1+\cdots+y_k\le1$, whose boundary has zero
limit measure, gives
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{(k!)^2}{(\log N)^{2k}}
 \sum_{p_1<\cdots<p_k\atop p_1\cdots p_k\le N}
       \prod_{j=1}^k\frac{(\log p_j)^2}{p_j}\\
 &\hspace{25mm}\longrightarrow
 \frac{(k!)^2}{k!}
 \int_{y_j\ge0,\,\sum y_j\le1}\prod_{j=1}^ky_j\,dy_1\cdots dy_k
 =\frac{k!}{(2k)!}.                                           \tag{4}
\end{align*}
The factor $1/k!$ removes the ordering of the distinct primes, and the last
integral is the Dirichlet simplex integral.

It remains to remove the leading stratum.  Expanding the two copies of
$\Lambda_k(m)$ classifies every remaining term by a common prime support.
If a prime occurs with total exponent at least two, its summation contains
$p^{-a}$ with $a\ge2$ and is convergent after any fixed logarithmic weight.
Otherwise the support has at most $k-1$ freely growing primes.  Repeated
use of (3) then gives $O_k((\log N)^{2k-2})$.  There are only finitely many
exponent and incidence patterns for fixed $k$.  Hence the complement of
the squarefree distinct-prime stratum is $o((\log N)^{2k})$, proving (2).
\end{proof}

\section{The complete all-depth budget}

The uniform Witt-moment proposition in row (d) supplies, for a fixed
$\varepsilon>0$, a summable majorant
\begin{equation}
 \frac{V_{N,k}}{(\log N)^{2k}}
 \le C_\varepsilon\sqrt k\,e^{\varepsilon k}\frac{k!}{(2k)!}
 \quad(1\le k\le\delta\log N),                                \tag{5}
\end{equation}
and says that the sum over $k\ge\delta\log N$ is $o(1)$.

\begin{theorem}[Strict arithmetic return budget --- \PROVED]
One has
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \lim_{N\to\infty}
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{V_{N,k}+H_{N,k}}{(\log N)^{2k}}
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{k!}{(2k)!}
 =\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}e^{1/4}\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac12\right)
 =0.5922965364\ldots<1.}                                      \tag{6}
\end{equation}
Consequently the asymptotic unused unit budget is
\begin{equation}
 1-\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}e^{1/4}\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac12\right)
 =0.4077034635\ldots.                                         \tag{7}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equations (2), (5), and the large-depth $o(1)$ estimate in row (d) allow
dominated convergence in the $V$-series.

For the boundary term, write $N=\prod_pp^{e_p}$.  An ordered
$j$-factorization of $N$ into prime powers gives
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_j(N)=j!\!\sum_{\sum r_p=j}
 \prod_{p:r_p>0}
 \frac{\binom{e_p-1}{r_p-1}}{r_p!}(\log p)^{r_p}.              \tag{8}
\end{equation}
Since $\binom{e-1}{r-1}\le e^r$, comparison term by term with the
multinomial expansion of
$(\sum_pe_p\log p)^j=(\log N)^j$ gives
\begin{equation}
                         0\le\Lambda_j(N)\le(\log N)^j.        \tag{9}
\end{equation}
Moreover $\Lambda_j(N)=0$ for $j>\Omega(N)\le\log N/\log2$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 0\le\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{H_{N,k}}{(\log N)^{2k}}
 \le\frac{\log N}{2\log2\,\sqrt N}\longrightarrow0.         \tag{10}
\end{equation}
This proves the first equality in (6).

Finally, using $k!=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^kdt$ and then $t=u^2$,
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{k!}{(2k)!}
 &=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}(\cosh\sqrt t-1)\,dt\\
 &=\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2}\sinh u\,du
 =\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}e^{1/4}\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac12\right).
\end{align*}
The numerical enclosure displayed in (6) is separated from one by more
than $0.4077$.  Independently of the decimal evaluation, if
$a_k=k!/(2k)!$, then
\[
 \frac{a_{k+1}}{a_k}=\frac1{2(2k+1)}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}a_k
 <\frac12+\frac1{12}+\frac{1/120}{1-1/14}<0.593<1,
\]
which proves the strict inequality using rational bounds only.
\end{proof}

\section{Interface with the physical Schur capacity}

Theorem 2 strengthens the earlier statement ``all Witt depths are
summable'': the complete normalized arithmetic return consumes strictly
less than the unit budget, with an explicit limiting margin.  It does not
by itself identify the physical return vector
$D_0^{\dagger/2}(D_0h_e-q_e)$ with the coefficient model (1).  To turn
(6) into the G40 shorted-capacity inequality one must prove the
source-normalized comparison
\begin{equation}
 \bigl\|D_0^{\dagger/2}(D_0h_e-q_e)\bigr\|^2
 \le
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{V_{N,k}+H_{N,k}}{(\log N)^{2k}}\,\|e\|^2
 +o(1)\|e\|^2,                                                \tag{11}
\end{equation}
uniformly at the threshold cells.  Equation (11) is a comparison between
the physical Gamma--Tate return and the already constructed Witt word Gram;
it is not implied by summability.  If (11) is derived from G39 with its
fixed normalization, (6)--(7) give a strict large-threshold capacity
margin; only finitely many remaining thresholds then require certified
verification.
