## 2. Blueprint claims refuted or repaired, each with its counterexample

### 2.1 `REFUTED` — D.1(i)'s `|ρ(1−ρ)|²`

The blueprint writes `Λ(g) = Σ_ρ|ρ(1−ρ)|²Φ_g(ρ)Φ_g(1−ρ)`.  Paper 45
Lemma 10.3a gives `[ρ(1−ρ)]²`.  These agree **iff** `ρ(1−ρ) ∈ R`, i.e. — for a
nontrivial zero — iff `ρ` lies on the critical line.

*Counterexample.*  At `ρ = 0.6+14i`: `ρ(1−ρ) = 196.24 − 2.8i`, so
`[ρ(1−ρ)]² = 38502.3 − 1098.9i` (not even real) while
`|ρ(1−ρ)|² = 38518.0`.

*Why it matters and is not cosmetic.*  With `|·|²`, and with the companion
slip `Φ_g(1−ρ) = \overline{Φ_g(ρ)}` (also true only on the line), every
summand becomes `|·|^2 ≥ 0` and the target becomes a triviality.  **The
blueprint's display is RH written as a definition.**  Corrected in Paper 46
Theorem 3.4, with Remark 3.5 stating the general form of the trap: *the sum is
manifestly nonnegative precisely when what is to be proved is already true.*

### 2.2 `REPAIRED` — D.1's "`Π` is a bijection (Cor 10.2)"

Paper 45 Corollary 10.2 proves that `Π` maps `C_c^∞(I_T)` **into** `P_T` and
is **injective**.  It does not prove surjectivity, and the blueprint cites it
for a bijection.  Without surjectivity, "`Λ ≥ 0` on `C_c^∞`" is formally
weaker than "`q_T ≥ 0` on `P_T`", and D.1 would be an implication, not the
advertised equivalence.

*Repair, with proof (Lemma 3.1).*  `e^{−|t|/2}` is the Green kernel of `Π`; for
`F ∈ P_T` and `t` right of `supp F`,
`(e^{−|·|/2}∗F)(t) = e^{−t/2}Φ_F(1) = 0`, and left of it,
`e^{t/2}Φ_F(0) = 0`.  **The two Tate conditions are exactly the two conditions
that make the Green potential compactly supported.**  So `Π` is onto `P_T`,
with `\mathrm{conv}\,supp` preserved.  This is a small new theorem, and it is
the one place where the primitive condition acquires an interpretation that is
not a linear constraint.

### 2.3 `REPAIRED` — D.1(ii)'s `μ := −\hat T_∞|_{prim} + polar`

Three errors of type, none fatal, all necessary to fix before the sentence is
usable: (a) the positive-definite object is a **distribution on `R`**, `𝔏`,
and `μ` is its **Fourier transform** — the blueprint puts the two in one slot;
(b) the factor `Π²` is not optional (without it the polar packet does not drop
and the functional is not the one of the primitive sector); (c)
"`\hat T_∞|_{prim}`" is not a defined object — restriction to the primitive
sector is a condition on the test class, not on the symbol.  Corrected form:
`𝔏 = Π²[ϖ − (W_{fin}+ω_∞)]`, `ϖ(t) = 2\cosh(t/2)`, Paper 46 (3.2)–(3.3).

### 2.4 `REFUTED` — D.2's "`B_T` is Paper 43's hyperbolic Tate plane"

Three different two-dimensional objects are conflated: the ruling plane
`B_T = C⊕C` with `β_T` (Paper 45 Thm 8.1); the border
`𝒯_T = span\{φ_+,φ_-\}`; and the Tate **block** of the Weil form, which
P124.013 (2.3) also calls `B_T`.

*Counterexample, exact part.*  `M(Γ_{φ_+}) = (2\sinh T, 2T)` and
`M(Γ_{φ_-}) = (2T, 2\sinh T)` are not the ruling basis vectors `e_∓`, so the
polar part of the block has matrix `[[8T\sinh T, 4T²+4\sinh²T],[·,·]]`, not
`[[0,1],[1,0]]`.  It is still hyperbolic as a form
(`\det = −16(T²−\sinh²T)² < 0`).

*Counterexample, decisive part.*  The block is that **plus** `⟨𝒜_Tφ_σ,φ_τ⟩`,
and the sum has no predetermined signature.  Computed at `T = 0.72`
(`refine = 2`):

```text
B_T = [[0.11264, 0.07670],
       [0.07670, 0.11264]]        det = +6.81e-03 > 0,  trace > 0
```

— **positive definite, signature `(2,0)`**, not `(1,1)`.  The blueprint's
identification would force `\det B_T < 0`.

### 2.5 `REFUTED` (as available) — D.2's monotonicity under `T ↦ T+ε`

The Schur complement is `Σ_T(z) = \inf\{𝒲(F+φ^{(T)}_z) : F ∈ P_T\}`, and
`P_T ⊆ P_{T'}` would give monotonicity **if the border were fixed**.  It is
not: `φ^{(T)}_σ = e^{σt/2}\mathbf 1_{I_T}` moves with the window.  What
survives is (4.4),
`Σ_{T'}(z) ≤ Σ_T(z) + 2𝒲(φ^{(T)}_z,δφ_z) + 𝒲(δφ_z,δφ_z)`, with correction
terms of no fixed sign.

*Counterexample, numerical.*  `λ_{\min}Σ(T)` for `ζ`, named basis,
`refine = 2`, `T = 0.72…2.2`: `2.58, 1.83, 2.75, 3.45, 3.02, 2.30, 2.43, 1.74`
(`×10^{−2}`) — falls, rises, falls, rises.  In the orthonormalized basis over
`T = 1.55…1.76`: `4.23, 3.88, 3.66, 2.96, 2.77, 2.91, 3.08, 3.14`
(`×10^{−3}`) — same qualitative behaviour.  P124.014's "decreasing from
`6.8·10^{−2}` to `9.0·10^{−4}`" reports two endpoints of a coarse scan and is
not a monotonicity statement.

### 2.6 `REFUTED`, twice — D.3(a)'s "Theorem 14.1 transported"

*First refutation (the mechanism).*  Theorem 14.1(a),(b) build their definite
subspaces by concentrating `\hatφ` in prescribed `τ`-windows, using
`φ_{a,δ}(t) = δ^{−1/2}e^{iat}φ_0(t/δ)` with `a → ∞` and `δ → ∞`.  Every
element of `span\{Γ_n^{rad,sm}\}_{n≤N}` has
`\hat F(τ) = \hatφ_ε(τ)Σ_nc_n n^{−iτ}`: the envelope `\hatφ_ε` is fixed by
`ε` and does not move, and the Dirichlet factor is almost periodic.  The
construction does not exist on this family, so 14.1 says nothing about it.
(Paper 43's own phase-133 amendment already replaced "supported in" by
"concentrate" for exactly the reason that the Fourier image of `D_A` is a
proper subspace of `S(R)`; the same Paley–Wiener fact blocks the transport a
second time.)

*Second refutation (the conclusion).*  Under `(C1)` — which (5.1) uses, and
must use, since the target is a colimit statement — a positive eigenvalue of
`G_N^{prim}(ε)` contradicts RH by Theorem 5.6.  So "indefinite for large `N`"
is not merely unproved: **proving it would prove `¬RH`**, and asserting it is
asserting a statement about the zeros that the rules of this campaign forbid.
Under the fixed-chart reading the assertion is consistent, but then Paper 44
Theorem 8bis.1e holds only inside `\{|t| < \log ℓ_{next}(S)\}` and the family
leaves that window as soon as `\log N > \log ℓ_{next}(S)`, so the kernel
formula that defines `G_N` is out of its own range of validity.

### 2.7 `REPAIRED` (attribution) — blueprint §1 item 3

"The Frobenii are partial isometries … In any positive functional their Gram
matrix would be positive semidefinite for free."  The second sentence is true
but holds for the Gram matrix of **any** family in any positive functional;
the partial-isometry theorem (3ter.6) contributes nothing to it and is used
nowhere in §3.  The substantive point that *does* need saying, and is said in
Remark 3.9, is the opposite one: the primitive sector is an **ideal**, not a
unital subalgebra containing `Δ`, so no GNS argument applies to
`−T_∞|_{prim}` directly, and Theorem 3.7(e) obtains its representation from
Bochner — i.e. from the conclusion.

## 3. Findings against the corpus (not blueprint claims)

1. **P124.013 Corollary 3.3 is basis-dependent as stated.**  `λ_{\min}Σ(T)`
   changes by an order of magnitude between the named border basis and the
   Gram-orthonormalized one at the same `T` and resolution, and the congruence
   between them has condition number `∼ e^{T}` (since `‖φ_σ‖_{L²} ∼ e^{T/2}`).
   `Σ(T) ≥ 0` is invariant; `\liminf λ_{\min}Σ(T) > −∞` is not.  Carry (3.2),
   not the `λ_{\min}` form.
2. **The phase-118/124 Galerkin eigenvalues are variational upper bounds, and
   are not converged.**  The piecewise-constant space is a **subspace** of the
   test space, so the computed `λ_{\min}` is `≥` the true infimum.  For `ζ` at
   `T = 1.7`: `2.93, 1.99, 1.23, 0.84, 0.61` (`×10^{−2}`) at
   `refine = 2,…,6`, decreasing at every step.  Consequence, stated sharply:
   **a negative computed eigenvalue certifies a genuine negative direction; a
   positive one certifies nothing.**  The `ζ` "control" of P124.014 §3 is
   therefore incapable *by construction* of supporting any conclusion about
   `ζ`.  Scoped to the double-precision scans; it does not touch P123.036's
   interval-arithmetic endpoint certificate.
3. **P124.014's first-contact brackets for `Ξ_a` are not resolution-stable.**
   At `a = 0.35`, `T = 1.68`: `λ_{\min}(L_T) = +1.31·10^{−1}` (`refine=2`),
   `−1.58·10^{−1}` (`refine=3`), `−4.33·10^{−1}` (`refine=4`).  By finding 2
   the refined values **certify** `T_q(Ξ_{0.35}) ≤ 1.68`, outside the printed
   bracket `[1.7413, 1.7425]`; and the `Σ(T)` sign flip reported near
   `T = 1.70` moves under refinement.  P124.014 §3 disclaims convergence
   explicitly, so this is within its declared scope — but its four printed
   decimals must not be cited as a bracket.  Its **qualitative** conclusions
   (a genuine first contact exists; Gate R fails there; case 2 of the
   dichotomy is realized) are unaffected, and Theorem 7.1 now supplies the
   structural reason.

## 4. The four places I would press, as the auditor

**(P1) D.1 may be a change of vocabulary, and the paper should be forced to
say whether its one non-classical ingredient does any work.**  In
Theorem 3.7, (a)⇔(b) is Weil plus Paper 45, and (c)⇔(d)⇔(e) is the
Bochner–Schwartz/SNAG/GNS dictionary applied to a distribution.  The only
row A–C content is Remark 3.8 item 2: the group is the **carrier's modulus
group** and the flow is `Frob_∞`.  **Press: name one statement in Paper 46
whose proof uses that identification.**  As the paper stands, none does — the
identification is a *typing* of the answer, not an ingredient.  I have
labelled §3 `CLASSICAL + PROVED-IN-REPOSITORY` and said in Remark 3.8 item 4
that constructing `(H,π,Ω)` is row D and is not done; but an auditor should
decide whether even that labelling over-credits the section, and whether D.1
should be graded `RESTATEMENT`.  I would not resist that regrade.

**(P2) Theorem 5.8 deflates D.3 over most of its parameter range, and the
paper should be forced to say how much is left.**  The archimedean diagonal
diverges like `−(c_φ/ε)\log(1/ε)`, one **logarithm** faster than the prime
atoms, which are `O(ε^{−1})`.  So below an explicit `ε_1(N)` the whole matrix
is negative definite **unconditionally, for every model**, and the finite
inertia is evidence of nothing.  The separation observed in §5.4 therefore
lives in a window of `ε` of order one — which is exactly where the
concentrated Frobenius reading (8bis.7) is **invalid** and only the integral
form (8bis.6) applies.  **Press: what does the inertia mean in that window?**
No theorem in this paper answers it.  My honest reading is that §5.4 separates
two **models** and says nothing new about `ζ`, and that D.3 as a programme
item should be regraded accordingly.  *(This replaces the construction's first
draft of Theorem 5.8, which asserted `εG_N(ε) → A_N` — the pure-atom limit —
and was **false**: the diagonal beats the atoms by `\log(1/ε)`.  The numerical
diagonal `−1.98, −33.94, −175.56` at `ε = 0.1,0.03,0.01` fits
`(2/3ε)(−\log(1/ε)+2.00)` to three digits and does not fit
`−\log(1/ε)+γ+\log4π` at all.  Recorded here because the review should see that
the construction's own first statement was refuted by its own numerics.)*

**(P3) The falsifier table's threshold behaviour is unexplained and could be
an artifact.**  `Ξ_a` shows no positive eigenvalue at `N = 10, ε ≤ 0.5` and
seven at `N = 30, ε = 2.0`.  Two competing explanations: (i) genuine — the
off-line quartet needs enough prime channels to resonate; (ii) artifact — at
`N = 10` the four-dimensional primitive compression removes `4` of `9`
dimensions, and the surviving `5` may simply be too few.  I did **not**
disentangle these.  Press: run `N = 15, 20, 25` and check whether the
transition is smooth in `N` at fixed `ε`, and run the `a → 0` degeneration,
where `Ξ_0 = ξ²` must reproduce the `ζ` inertia exactly (the script's model
data degenerate correctly, but the compression rank changes from `4` to `2`
discontinuously at `a = 0`, and that discontinuity has not been examined).
Until that is done, §5.4 item 2 is a statement about my grid, not about the
models.

**(P4) Finding 4.8 and finding §9.3(2) together may retire a line of the
corpus, and the review should decide deliberately rather than by silence.**  If
a positive Galerkin eigenvalue certifies nothing, then every "`ζ` control"
computation in phases 118–124 that reports positivity is evidence of nothing —
including the ones used to argue that the corpus's objects behave as expected.
The *falsification* results survive (negative eigenvalues are certificates),
which is why §5.4 and P124.014's qualitative conclusions stand.  Press: does
any statement currently carried at `PROVED` or `COMPUTATIONAL-CERTIFICATE` in
the corpus rest on a positive double-precision Galerkin eigenvalue?  If yes,
it must be relabelled; if no, finding §9.3(2) should be recorded as a standing
methodological rule for future numerics, in the same place the "numerics
illustrate and falsify" rule lives.
