## E1 — Proposition 3.6a(iii) withdrawn

### 1.1 I re-derived the auditor's refutation before acting on it

The auditor is right on both counts, and I verified each independently.

**(a) The proof was invalid.**  Round 2 ran
`⟨Π²W_{fin},φ⟩ = ⟨W_{fin},Π²φ⟩ = ⟨W_{fin},ψ⟩ = ∞` with `φ = Π^{−2}ψ`.
`φ ∈ S(R)` is right — `\hatφ = \hatψ/(¼+τ²)²` and `(¼+τ²)^{−2}` is an
`S`-multiplier — but `φ ∉ D(R)`, and transposing a differential operator
across the `D'×D` pairing needs a test function **in `D(R)`**.  Both sides of
the first equality presuppose that `Π²W_{fin}` is already tempered, i.e.
exactly what was being denied.  The argument is a formal manipulation with no
theorem under it.

**(b) The statement is false under RH, and I confirmed the mechanism
numerically.**  `Π²ϖ = 0` is my own Theorem 3.6, so with `ν := W_{fin} − ϖdt`,

    Π²W_{fin} = Π²ν   exactly,

and the divergence the round-2 argument exploited is carried **entirely** by
the summand `Π²` annihilates.  Writing
`M(t) = Σ_{2≤n≤e^t}Λ(n)n^{−1/2}` and `∫_0^tϖ = 4\sinh(t/2) = 2e^{t/2}+O(1)`:
under RH, `ψ_{Ch}(x) = x + O(\sqrt x\log²x)` and partial summation give
`M(t) = 2e^{t/2}+O(t³)`, so `ν` has a polynomially bounded distribution
function, hence `ν ∈ S'(R)` and `Π²W_{fin} = Π²ν ∈ S'(R)`.

*My own check of the cancellation* (illustration only, proving nothing): over
all prime powers `n ≤ 4·10^6`,


The difference is **bounded**, not growing, over four orders of magnitude in
`e^t`.  The cancellation of `Π²ϖ = 0` is total in practice, and the round-2
argument threw it away.

**(c) And it was forbidden regardless.**  By (b), a proof of 3.6a(iii) is a
proof of `¬RH`; and the paper as printed in round 2 *contained* that proof, in
three steps of its own: `Π²W_{fin} ∉ S'` ⟹ `𝔏 ∉ S'` (since `ω_∞` with its
(8.5∞) finite part is tempered) ⟹ by Theorem 3.7 (c)⇒(d), `𝔏` is not
positive-definite ⟹ ¬(b) ⟹ ¬(a).  **A statement about the location of the
zeros, inside the one paper forbidden to make one.**  That is the decisive
reason, independent of provability, and I record it as such.

## E2 — Definition 6.8 acquires (R5)

**The defect.**  (R4) is `h(F) = 0` for **every** primitive `F`.  If
`F ↦ F^∨` preserves primitivity then `h(F^∨) = 0` too, and (6.1) reads
`0 = ½I_∞(F,F) + χ` for every primitive `F`: `I_∞` constant on the primitive
sector.  False by homogeneity — `F ↦ λF` scales `I_∞` by `λ²` — unless
`I_∞ ≡ 0` there, which Paper 43 Theorem 14.1 forbids.  So the list was
satisfied by **no `h` whatever**: vacuous, not merely unfulfilled.

**Repair.**  **(R5) The duality must carry the primitive sector off itself**:
there must exist primitive `F ≠ 0` with `F^∨` not primitive.  Weil's duality
`D ↦ K − D` does move the class; the clause now says so.  Two consequences are
stated inside (R5):

* **`∨ = †` is excluded.**  The half-density dagger is the *only* duality rows
  A–C construct on `Corr_A^{tr}` (Paper 43 §§3–5, Lemma 8.4), and it
  **preserves** primitivity: `λ_∓(Γ_h^†) = \overline{λ_±(Γ_h)}` (Paper 43
  Theorem 9.1, (1.3)), so `M(Γ_h) = 0` iff `M(Γ_h^†) = 0`.
* **`NOT CONSTRUCTED`, named as an open requirement.**  No duality in
  Papers 43–44 moves the primitive sector off itself; producing one — a
  Serre-type duality with a `K`-twist, not an involution of the same sector —
  is a **prerequisite** for (R1)–(R4) to be satisfiable at all.  Added to
  §9.1 as its own item **3a**.
