# A125.002 — two-sided excision: the exact obstruction, and its vanishing on the localized sector (clause A.3)

Status: `PROVED-IN-REPOSITORY` for Theorem 2.1 (the obstruction object) and
Theorem 3.1 (its vanishing on the ideal-generated sector).  `OPEN` for the
profile objects of A125.001.  No RH-side input is used.

P123.125 §4 leaves one explicitly open item, in these words: "An intermediate
correspondence can pass through the removed region and return, and (4) alone
does not identify every such composition after two-sided projection."  This
note settles it.  The answer is that the failure is measured by a single
computable object, and that this object vanishes on exactly the sector where
A.2 and A.3 were constructed.

## 1. Setting

`U:=A_sm^u(P)`; `J_1,\dots,J_m\subseteq P`; `O=\bigcup_aU_{J_a}`;
`I_O=\sum_aI_{J_a}`; `B_O=U/I_O`, a closed, continuously linearly split
quotient (P123.125 (1)).  Two-sided induction is

\[
        \mathcal I(M)=B_O\hat\otimes^{L}_{U}M\hat\otimes^{L}_{U}B_O .   \tag{1.1}
\]

P123.125 (4) proves `B_O\hat\otimes^{L}_UB_O\cong B_O`, and (3) proves
`I_{J_T}\hat\otimes_UB_O=0` for every `T\neq\emptyset`, where
`J_T=\bigcup_{a\in T}J_a`.

## 2. The obstruction is one object

**Theorem 2.1.**  For `M,N` bounded-above `U`-bimodule complexes there is a
natural comparison map

\[
        c_{M,N}:\ \mathcal I(M\star N)\longrightarrow
        \mathcal I(M)\star_{B_O}\mathcal I(N),                          \tag{2.1}
\]

and a distinguished triangle in `D_rel^-(B_O\text{-}B_O)`

\[
\boxed{
 B_O\hat\otimes^{L}_UM\hat\otimes^{L}_UI_O\hat\otimes^{L}_UN
      \hat\otimes^{L}_UB_O
 \longrightarrow \mathcal I(M\star N)
 \xrightarrow{\ c_{M,N}\ } \mathcal I(M)\star_{B_O}\mathcal I(N)
 \longrightarrow[1].}                                                   \tag{2.2}
\]

In particular `c_{M,N}` is an isomorphism if and only if

\[
        B_O\hat\otimes^{L}_UM\hat\otimes^{L}_UI_O
        \hat\otimes^{L}_UN\hat\otimes^{L}_UB_O=0 .                      \tag{2.3}
\]

*Proof.*  Using associativity of the relative derived tensor (P123.122 §3) and
`B_O\hat\otimes^L_UB_O\cong B_O`,

\[
 \mathcal I(M)\star_{B_O}\mathcal I(N)
 \cong B_O\hat\otimes^{L}_UM\hat\otimes^{L}_UB_O
        \hat\otimes^{L}_UN\hat\otimes^{L}_UB_O .
\]

The sequence `0\to I_O\to U\to B_O\to0` is admissible, hence a distinguished
triangle `I_O\to U\to B_O\to[1]`.  Apply the exact functor
`B_O\hat\otimes^L_UM\hat\otimes^L_U(-)\hat\otimes^L_UN\hat\otimes^L_UB_O`,
which is exact because all four outer factors are K-flat on the relevant side
by P123.122 §3 and P123.124 §3.  The middle term becomes
`\mathcal I(M\star N)` and the right-hand term becomes
`\mathcal I(M)\star_{B_O}\mathcal I(N)`, giving (2.2); (2.1) is the induced
map.  QED

Theorem 2.1 is the precise form of the P123.122 §5 non-monoidality: the
comparison there was "not asserted invertible", and (2.2) says exactly what
its failure is.  It also explains the geometric picture in P123.125 §4: the
object in (2.3) is supported on correspondences that leave the closed stratum
through `I_O` and return, and it is the only such contribution.

## 3. Vanishing on the localized sector

**Lemma 3.1.**  Let `K\subseteq P` and let `T\neq\emptyset`.  Then

\[
        I_{K\cup J_T,\beta}\hat\otimes_UB_O=0 .                          \tag{3.1}
\]

*Proof.*  Pick `a\in T`.  Since `J_a\subseteq K\cup J_T`, (2.1) of P123.124
gives `(K\cup J_T)\cup J_a=K\cup J_T`, so multiplication

\[
 I_{K\cup J_T,\beta}\hat\otimes_UI_{J_a}\longrightarrow I_{K\cup J_T,\beta}
\]

is an isomorphism, in particular surjective, with the continuous product
splitting of P123.124 (4)–(5).  Tensoring on the right by `B_O` and using
`I_{J_a}\hat\otimes_UB_O=0` (P123.125 (3)) kills every balanced elementary
product, and the splitting extends this to the completed tensor.  Hence
(3.1).  QED

**Theorem 3.2 (two-sided excision on the ideal sector).**  Let `M,N` be
objects of the stable sector generated by the twisted localized ideals
`\{I_{K,\beta}\}` under finite sums, retracts, shifts and cones.  Then (2.3)
holds, so

\[
\boxed{
        \mathcal I(M\star N)\;\cong\;
        \mathcal I(M)\star_{B_O}\mathcal I(N)}                           \tag{3.2}
\]

naturally, and derived two-sided restriction is monoidal on that sector.

*Proof.*  Both sides of (2.3) are exact in `M` and in `N` and commute with
finite sums, shifts, cones and retracts, so it suffices to treat
`M=I_{K,\beta}`, `N=I_{L,\eta}`.  The Čech complex of P123.125 (2), truncated
below degree zero, is a finite resolution of `I_O` by the terms
`\bigoplus_{|T|=n}I_{J_T}`, `1\le n\le m`, admissible by the least-vertex
contraction proved there.  Tensoring it with `M` on the left and `N` on the
right and using P123.124 (7) twice gives, in each Čech degree, a finite sum of
objects `I_{K\cup J_T\cup L,\ \beta\eta}`.  Every such index set contains
`J_T` with `T\neq\emptyset`, so Lemma 3.1 makes each term vanish after
tensoring with `B_O`.  A finite complex with vanishing terms vanishes; hence
(2.3), and (3.2) follows from Theorem 2.1.  QED

**Corollary 3.3.**  Clause A.3 is satisfied on the localized sector together
with its zero-stratum restrictions: composition, the two-sided unit and the
restriction functors to every closed zero-count stratum `F_k` of P123.125 §5
now commute, coherently for nested strata by the composition of the quotient
maps recorded there.

## 4. Where it fails, stated exactly

Theorem 3.2 uses that the objects are **ideals**: Lemma 3.1 is exactly the
statement that an ideal indexed by a set containing some `J_a` is annihilated
by `B_O`.  The dual objects `M_{J,\beta}=[I_{J,\beta},U]` of A125.001 §3 are
profile objects, not ideals, and Theorem 3.1 of that note shows their elements
are families with **no decay at `\partial U_J`**.  There is therefore no
reason for `M_{J,\beta}\hat\otimes_UB_O` to vanish, and the argument above
does not apply to them.

Consequently the honest statement of A.3 after this note is:

* two-sided excision holds on the sector generated by the localized ideals:
  `PROVED`;
* it is `OPEN` on the dagger-closure of that sector, i.e. once the profile
  objects of A125.001 are admitted.

This is a genuine tension between clauses A.3 and A.4, and it is new
information: closing A.4 by adjoining the duals enlarges the category in
exactly the direction where the excision proof of Theorem 3.2 stops working.
Resolving it requires either a support calculus for the profile objects
showing their `B_O`-tensor vanishes, or a restriction of the trace-class
domain of A125.003 to objects for which both hold.  It is recorded here, not
concealed inside either clause.
