## 1. R185_008 — Lemma 1.1(b′): finite index ⟹ finitely many off-line zeros

### (i) Normalization
Paper 47 uses F̂(τ) = ∫F(t)e^{iτt}dt, Tate moments A(F) = F̂(−i/2), B(F) = F̂(i/2); the full form on the
primitive domain is, by the explicit formula over the autocorrelation c_F (support [−2T,2T], ĉ_F = |F̂|²),
W(F) = Σ_ρ F̂(γ_ρ)·conj F̂(conj γ_ρ), ρ = ½+iγ_ρ, with weight v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n from eq:symbol. It matches R183_031 (1.1)
up to, at most, a positive factor, irrelevant for n₋. The points ±i/2 of the primitive condition are the correct
ones in this convention. **No objection.**

MINOR 1.1. Step 0(ii) cites the explicit formula on class 𝒳 as CITED. It is not needed: the hypothesis lives on
C_c^∞, where the explicit formula is that of Paper 47; for the Gaussian tests only the **zero side** and its
continuity are used (below). Rewrite: "W is defined on 𝒳∩prim by the sum over zeros; it coincides with the form of
Paper 47 on C_c^∞∩prim". This removes the only citation.

### (ii) Admissibility and passage to the window
ĥ = e^{−aM}e^{−a(s−c)²}p(s) ⇒ h(t) = e^{−ict}·(polynomial)·e^{−t²/4a}: decays superexponentially, h ∈ 𝒳.
Continuity of the zero side: if ‖(1+t²)e^{|t|/2}(h−h_R)^{(j)}‖_{L¹} → 0 (j ≤ 2), then ĥ_R → ĥ uniformly with
weight (1+|s|)² on the strip |Im s| ≤ ½, and Σ_ρ(1+|γ_ρ|)^{−2} < ∞ gives continuity. The truncation χ(t/R)h converges
in that norm; so does the primitivity correction by two fixed functions of C_c^∞ with coefficients → 0.
The subspace argument (Step 0(iii)): a basis of an r-dimensional negative subspace of 𝒳∩prim is approximated in
C_c^∞(I_{T_R})∩prim; negative definiteness of an r×r Gram matrix is an open condition. So the approximating subspace
lives in a finite window I_T. **Use of "∀T":** the hypothesis is n₋(W|prim∩C_c^∞(ℝ)) ≤ N₀ = sup_T N(T) (nested
windows, every finite-dimensional subspace is supported in some I_T). The argument produces, for each k, a T_k with
N(T_k) ≥ k. Correct; no sign is lost: the form on the window is the **same** restricted form.

MINOR 1.2. Write out explicitly the norm of 𝒳 used and the continuity bound (two lines); it currently just says
"both sides are continuous in the topology of 𝒳" without specifying it.
MINOR 1.3. N(T) in R184_004 is n₋ on 𝒫_T ⊂ L²(I_T) (form domain), not on C_c^∞(I_T). Add: C_c^∞(I_T)∩𝒫_T is
dense in the form domain ∩𝒫_T under the form norm, so both indices agree.

### (iii) Domination
For γ = c+x+iβ: |e^{−a(γ−c)²}| = e^{a(β²−x²)} = e^{av}. Correct. The concentration in frequency that is of
concern **does not occur in the dangerous sense**: the relative weight of any zero (real or not) outside F_c is
e^{2a(v_ρ−M_c)} ≤ e^{−2(a−1)δ_c}e^{2(v_ρ−M_c)}, and Σ_ρ e^{2(v_ρ−M_c)}|p(γ_ρ)p(γ̄_ρ)| converges (v ≤ ¼ − x², log
density, p polynomial of degree ≤ 2r+1). Real zeros near c have v = −x² ≤ 0 < M_c − δ_c + …: indeed
M_c ≥ β_*² > 0 and v ≤ 0 for every real zero, so δ_c ≥ min(M_c, …) > 0. The gap δ_c is independent of a. The
Lagrange interpolation has fixed nodes (F_c ∪ {±i/2}, distinct because |Im γ| < ½) and data of modulus 1 that
depend on a only through a phase: uniformly bounded coefficients. Q_a = −2·Id on V_a (checked: the pair term with
u = c_j, w = −c_j e^{−4iaxβ} equals −2|c_j|²; distinct pairs vanish due to the zeros of the interpolant).
Multiplicities: multiply the term by m ≥ 1, do not change the sign. If c = 0, F_c contains ±γ, ±γ̄: these remain
fixed distinct nodes. **No objection.**

### (iv) Cross terms (Step 3)
Bracket (M_i − v_ρ(c_i)) + (M_j − v_ρ(c_j)) = M_i + M_j − 2β² + x_i² + x_j² ≥ −½ + ½(c_i−c_j)² > 3/2 for
|c_i − c_j| > 2 (M ≥ 0, β² ≤ ¼). The text's bound "≥ ½(c_i−c_j)² − 1" is weaker but sufficient. Summability:
the bracket grows like x², so Σ_ρ e^{−a·bracket}|p p| ≤ e^{−(a−1)κ}·C. The diagonal errors of each block
are O(e^{−2(a−1)δ_{c_i}}); with k fixed and a → ∞ the matrix is −2·Id + o(1). n₋ ≥ k. **Correct.**

MINOR 1.4. "Their real parts are unbounded" — correct (finitely many zeros in each rectangle), but c_i must be
chosen as Re γ of an off-line zero to guarantee M_{c_i} > 0 and F_{c_i} made of off-line zeros
(v > 0 ⇒ β ≠ 0). The text does this in Step 1; repeat it in Step 3.

### (v) Consistency with R184_004 Lemma 1 and R162_2602 Thm 6
A quartet {γ, γ̄, −γ, −γ̄} is two conjugate pairs; Step 1 gives (1,1) per pair, hence (2,2) per quartet: matches
R184_004 Lemma 1 and sup N = 2m (R162_2602 Thm 6, N → 2κ_off with κ_off = number of quartets). The lemma only
gives the lower bound n₋ ≥ k (blocks), not 2m; no contradiction.
MINOR 1.5. "sup N = 2m" still depends on R162_2602 Thm 6 (CITED in the corpus); the text says so, but the status
table should read: "m < ∞ ⟺ sup N < ∞: PROVED; sup N = 2m: CITED R162_2602". Clarify that m counts quartets
with multiplicity.

replaced. Theorem 1 and Cor 1.2 of R183_031 (under the uniform-index hypothesis) become PROVED. R184_004

## 2. R185_006 — Analytic positivity on ½log2 < T ≤ ½log3

### Normalization (risk flagged in R185_006) — DECIDED
Paper 47 eq:symbol: σ_T(τ) = m(τ) − Σ_{n∈N_T} v_n cos(τ log n), v_n = 2Λ(n)/√n, N_T = {log n < 2T}. For
½log2 < T ≤ ½log3, N_T = {2} (at T = ½log3, log 3 < 2T is false). v_2 = 2log2/√2 = √2·log2. So
θ = σ_T = m − √2 log2 cos(s log2) **exactly**, and the identity W_T = 𝔞_T on 𝒫_T is eq:form. **PROVED, correct
normalization.** θ(0) = ψ(¼) − logπ − √2 log2 = −5.3721 − 0.9803 = −6.3524 ✓.
