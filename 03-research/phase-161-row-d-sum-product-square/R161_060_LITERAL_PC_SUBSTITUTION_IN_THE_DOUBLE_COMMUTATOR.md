# R161_060 — Literal PC substitution in the double commutator

## 1. What PC-ren supplies

Write

    S A Fcal=R_PC                                           (1.1)

for the exact finite renormalized anomaly, suppressing M,N. Applied to Fcal f,

    S A f=R_PC Fcal f.                                     (1.2)

At first contact decompose

    Af=r+ell,
    r=PAf in Tate,
    ell=QAf.                                               (1.3)

For every admissible test g, (1.2) gives

    <Sr,g>+<S ell,g>=<R_PC Fcal f,g>.                     (1.4)

After splitting g into physical and exterior pieces, the Tate term and the exterior Weil term on the left recombine exactly as in R161_023. Equation (1.4) is an amplitude identity; its right side is not zero on a first-contact vector.

## 2. Substitution into the quadratic transport

In the block notation of R161_059, the radical double commutator is the sum

    Q_B(f)=<Bf,HBf>+<B*f,HB*f>>=0.                         (2.1)

Use (1.3)-(1.4) with the two tests generated by Bf and B*f. Every occurrence of an exterior product with Xf=QAf can be replaced by a PC anomaly pairing, plus the uniquely fixed Tate term. The result has the form

    Q_B(f)
      =Re<R_PC Fcal f,g_B^+>
       +Re<R_PC Fcal f,g_B^->
       +explicit polar/Tate terms.                         (2.2)

This is an exact re-expression of Q_B. First-contact radicality kills the primitive interior pairings used to reach (2.2), but supplies no vanishing of the two PC anomaly pairings. Adding the functional-equation exterior reconstructs the extremal correlation, exactly as in R161_023(3.1); it does not set it to zero.

Thus literal substitution of PC-ren into the positive double commutator does not yield

    0=-Q_B(f)-D_N^Gamma(F).                               (2.3)

It yields an identity defining the same exterior correlation in new coordinates. To obtain (2.3), one still needs an antisymmetric boundary law for the two pairings on the right of (2.2).
