# R161_065 — The support concomitant is the already-audited exterior commutator

## 1. Exact reduction

For the candidate introduced in R161_064,

    K_P(S;g,w)=<Pg,PSw>-<PS*g,Pw>,                            (1.1)

selfadjointness of `P` gives immediately

    <Pg,PSw>=<g,PSw>,
    <PS*g,Pw>=<S*g,Pw>=<g,SPw>.

Therefore

    boxed:
    K_P(S;g,w)=<g,[P,S]w>.                                   (1.2)

Inserting `I=P+Q` gives the equivalent off-diagonal expansion

    K_P(S;g,w)=<Pg,PSQw>-<Qg,SPw>.                            (1.3)

Thus the candidate is not an additional Poisson--Tate identity.  It is exactly the physical support commutator
already expanded in R161_057(4.1)--(4.2), and its primitive/Tate refinements are the terms audited in
R161_042--R161_044.

## 2. Consequence for the proposed positive expansion

The proposed equation R161_064(4.2) would assert that the already known indefinite commutator (1.2) equals a sum
of nonnegative scattering, Gamma and parity-Tate energies.  Neither projection algebra nor `(PC-ren)` as presently
proved supplies that assertion:

- the primitive part is the horizontal-modulation radical tautology (R161_044);
- the source-derivative Tate part has opposite parity and is zero, not the positive Tate square (R161_043);
- the two exterior blocks are the unresolved wrong-side reciprocity (R161_057);
- literal substitution of `(PC-ren)` reconstructs the extremal correlation and supplies no zero or sign
  (R161_060).

Consequently R161_064(4.2)--(4.3) are withdrawn as purported consequences.  They remain only a restatement of the
missing theorem and must not be cited as an identity.

## 3. Exhaustion of the new Lagrangian/power attempt

## 4. Precise missing input

What remains is not an unperformed algebraic expansion.  It is an additional arithmetic theorem excluding the
three-block countermodel of R161_050.  Any valid completion must prove, independently, one of the equivalent

    Re<g,P_R C_log g+epsilon g>=0,                            (4.1)

support concomitant, power balance, or Lagrangian boundary law does not derive it.
