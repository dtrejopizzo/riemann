## 0. Executive summary

The method of this document is that of Schwartz and Robinson: an impossibility theorem
with explicit hypotheses is not a tombstone — it is a design blueprint. Distributions
were born by violating "every derivative of a function is a function"; non-standard
infinitesimals, by violating "every complete Archimedean ordered field is ℝ"; non-commutative
spaces, by violating "every space is its commutative algebra of functions". In
each case the new object violates exactly ONE hypothesis and keeps contact with
the original problem. The program proved three no-go's with enumerable hypotheses. Here
the three violating objects and their first theorems are constructed.

1. **(§2, against NO-GO 1 — impossibility of class ℱ, Doc 110 Thm. 4.3.)**
   Hypothesis violated: the **Archimedean property of the value group** of the index (I takes
   values in ℤ, where "finite uniform bound" = "bound by a standard integer" = RH).
   Object: the **infinitesimal signature** σ_ε — the negative index per window,
   read as an element of the ultrapower *ℤ and normalized by dimension, with
   standard part in [0,1]. Theorems proved: well-definedness (Thm. 2.6); escape
   from Theorem 4.3 — horn (b) of the dichotomy collapses because in *ℤ there
   exists the trivial non-Archimedean bound κ* ≤ n* and σ_ε is defined toll-free
   (Thm. 2.7); per-window autonomy — each κ_N is computable from the primes by
   Newton/Jacobi–Frobenius because the window has rank finite a priori (Prop. 2.8,
   supported by D110 Prop. 4.5 + P43 value autonomy); and the **logistic law of
   the tensor tower** (Thm. 2.10, exact, with closed form):
   σ^(k) = ½(1 − (1−2σ⁰)^{2^k}). Bridge (§2.5): RH ⟺ the tower tends to 0;
   ¬RH (modulo the realizability caveat of D108) ⟺ the tower tends to ½. **The
   o(1/log T) precision of the second-order wall is transmuted into a macroscopic
   separation 0 vs ½** — the wall reappears as the non-effectiveness of the
   standard part (a new Π₁), but each event is now finite linear algebra of
   arithmetic data. It is the most favorable transmutation recorded in the program.

2. **(§3, against NO-GO 2 — seminorm dichotomy, Doc 113 §5.4.)** Hypothesis
   violated: **homogeneity + subadditivity** (being a seminorm), which is what turns
   almost-periodicity into boundedness (D113 Lemma 5.1) and lets Ω-theorems
   kill the detector. Object: the **chordal detector** Δ_D — the spherical metric of ℂ̂ applied
   on compacts. Theorems proved: Δ_D sees compacts AND is indestructible by
   growth (bounded by 1) — both horns of the dichotomy fail (Thm. 3.4);
   on a fixed compact, chordal recurrence is equivalent to Euclidean recurrence with
   explicit constants (Thm. 3.5) — the detection power is preserved; and the **Marty
   mirror** (Thm. 3.7, unconditional, via Voronin universality): the spherical
   derivative of ζ is unbounded on the orbit in every disc of the strip —
   sup_τ ζ#(s+iτ) = ∞. Bridge: the detector survives but Marty's compactness
   dies where Stepanov's boundedness used to die: **the growth of the value is
   transmuted into growth of the spherical derivative.** The difficulty reappears one
   derivative level higher; link E5 (selection) remains intact.

3. **(§4, against NO-GO 3 — individual-orbit obstruction, Doc 102 §7.)**
   Hypothesis violated: the **product topology of the Bohr torus** (the one giving
   compactness/minimality/unique ergodicity and under which Φ is discontinuous
   exactly at ω₀). Object: the **arithmetic topology** 𝒯_ar, generated by the
   pseudometrics d_D of uniform-in-truncation convergence of the partial Euler
   sums. Theorems proved: 𝒯_ar is strictly finer than the product topology
   (Lemma 4.4); (Ω, 𝒯_ar) is NOT compact — one-line proof via
   compact-to-Hausdorff (Lemma 4.5); and the exceptional-point dissolution
   theorem (Thm. 4.6, with complete proof): the family of truncated observables
   {log ζ_X}_X is **uniformly d_D-equicontinuous at EVERY ω, uniformly in X** —
   in 𝒯_ar, ω₀ is not exceptional; the observable is just as regular at the
   arithmetic point as at any other. Price proved: compactness — and with it
   the entire unconditional dynamics (E1–E3 of D102) — is lost. Transfer
   theorem (Thm. 4.8): 𝒯_ar-recurrence of ω₀ + tail anchoring ⟹ LP-112 ⟹
   m ∈ {0,∞}. Bridge: **total transmutation** — the discontinuity of Φ turns,
   exactly and without residue, into the non-compactness of Ω plus the tail
   problem (MW-2 in truncation coordinates). New information about the wall: the
   obstruction was never the topology; it is a presupposition conserved between
   (continuity of the observable) and (free recurrence of the point).

**Ranking (§5):** infinitesimal signature ≻ chordal detector ≻ arithmetic topology.
**Next theorem to attack (§5.2):** the test-accessible realizability of the lower
bound σ⁰ ≥ c_m under ¬RH (closing the "Hypothesis D" caveat of D108 Lemma 2.4 for the
family of supremum windows) — it is the only cited-but-not-proved link between the
logistic tower and the clean equivalence RH ⟺ lim σ^(k) = 0.

## 1. The method: surgical negation

### 1.1. Protocol

For each no-go N with hypotheses H₁,…,H_r:

1. **[EXTRACTION]** Enumerate the hypotheses and verify, proof in hand, that each
   is load-bearing: which step of the no-go's proof uses it? Does the no-go
   survive if it is removed?
2. Choose the WEAKEST hypothesis whose negation does not break contact with
   arithmetic (the violating object must keep feeding on primes, pole and gamma
   factor — otherwise, it is a toy).
3. **[NEW-DEFINITION]** Define the object that violates it. The definition is free;
   discipline begins at point 4.
4. **[THEOREM]** Prove: (i) well-definedness; (ii) that the no-go's statement is
   FALSE for the object (or that its hypotheses do not reach it, with the exact step
   of the old proof that breaks); (iii) at least one substantive property.
5. **[BRIDGE]** Apply it to ζ and trace the transmutation (Doc 99 pattern): if the
   difficulty reappears, say WHERE — each reappearance is new information about
   the wall.

### 1.2. The historical yardstick

The exact precedent being imitated: Robinson did not prove more analysis theorems than
Weierstrass — he proved that the non-Archimedean ordered field *ℝ exists, is
conservative, and that in it certain impossible statements (an h > 0 smaller than every
1/n) are trivial; the gain was in EXPRESSIVITY, and from there came new
theorems (Bernstein–Robinson on invariant subspaces). This document's criterion of
success is the same: the violating object is worthwhile if (a) its first theorems
are provable today, and (b) it re-expresses the wall in coordinates where something that
before was invisible becomes statable. No crossing of the wall is promised: what is
promised is to build the room from which it is seen head-on.

## 2. NO-GO 1: the impossibility of class ℱ, negated by non-Archimedeanness

### 2.1. [EXTRACTION] The hypotheses of D110's Theorem 4.3

The statement (D110, §4.2): *no I ∈ ℱ satisfies (L2)+(L3)+(L4)*, where ℱ is the
class of invariants I = neg.ind(Q|_{V′}) with Q of explicit-formula type and V′
test-accessible. Hypotheses deployed:

- **H1 (form of the invariant):** I is A classical negative index,
  I = neg.ind(Q|_{V′}) ∈ ℤ_{≥0} ∪ {∞}.
- **H2 (Archimedean value group):** the value of I lives in ℤ (or a discrete
  Archimedean group): "I bounded" means "I ≤ C for a standard integer C".
- **H3 (test-accessibility):** V′ is specified without zero positions.
- **H4 (a priori unbounded rank):** in the resolution regime, dim V′ → ∞ with
  the window (there is no finiteness L6).
- **H5 (Q of explicit-formula type):** spectral side = zeros, arithmetic side =
  computable blocks.

**Which is load-bearing, and where?** D110 Thm. 4.3's proof is the D108
dichotomy: (a) sub-resolution ⟹ I = 0 (no detection: violates L3); (b)
resolution ⟹ I is binary per window — 0 under RH, ≍ m·n_W under ¬RH — and then
*any finite uniform bound on I is equivalent to RH* (violates L4), or the
compression to the relative block uses positions (violates L2); (c) there is no third
regime because the evaluation requires relative precision o(1/log T).

- Removing H3 trivially kills the theorem (the off×off block compressed by
  positions has index 8m², D108 §2.4): uninteresting — losing H3 is losing
  contact with what is computable.
- Removing H5 kills contact with arithmetic: uninteresting.
- Removing H4 kills the theorem (D110 Prop. 4.5: in a priori finite rank, inertia IS
  autonomous — Newton/Jacobi–Frobenius): this is the door that D110 §4.4 already
  pointed to ("the reader is a finiteness theorem"), but the finiteness theorem (global
  L6, the Weil cohomology of Spec ℤ) is not within reach of an act of
  definition: one cannot DEFINE the global finiteness of an object that lacks it.
- **Removing H2 — and this is the finding of the extraction — disarms horn (b) without
  touching anything else.** The step "(b): any finite uniform bound on I is
  logically equivalent to RH (D108 Prop. 2.5), violating L4" uses that a bound is
  a STANDARD INTEGER: the family {I_W}_W of per-window indices, unbounded
  under ¬RH, has no ceiling in ℤ — and the absence of a standard ceiling is what makes
  L4 inconsistent. In a non-Archimedean ordered ring the family DOES admit a
  ceiling (an unlimited element), the notion "bounded" bifurcates into
  "limited/unlimited", and the invariant can be defined WITHOUT ever asserting
  a standard bound. The no-go's proof does not survive: it uses
  Archimedeanness at the one point where it converts stability into RH.

H2 is also the weakest hypothesis in the sense of the protocol: negating it does not touch
the arithmetic data (H5), nor accessibility (H3), nor claim the global finiteness that
does not exist (H4 is sidestepped *per window*, where finiteness is real and free).

### 2.2. [NEW-DEFINITION] The non-standard index and the infinitesimal signature

Fixed once and for all: 𝒰 a non-principal ultrafilter over ℕ; *ℝ := ℝ^ℕ/𝒰 the
ultrapower (non-Archimedean real-closed ordered field; Robinson 1966),
*ℤ ⊂ *ℝ the non-standard integers. An element x ∈ *ℝ is **limited** if |x| ≤ n
for some standard n ∈ ℕ; st: {limited} → ℝ is the standard part (ring-ordered
homomorphism on the limited elements; exists by completeness of ℝ). Notation:
[a_N] = the class of the sequence (a_N)_N.

**Definition 2.1 (test-accessible window scheme).** A *scheme* is a
sequence 𝒮 = (V_N)_{N≥1} of finite-dimensional subspaces of the test space of
a Q of explicit-formula type (D110, Def. 4.2(i)), such that: (i) each
V_N is specified by supports, height windows, and bandwidths
only — without zero positions (test-accessible, D108 Def. 1.2); (ii)
n_N := dim V_N → ∞; (iii) n_N is computable from the specification. Canonical example
(the one used in §2.5): V_N = sum of the test spaces of
resolution (support ≍ log T_N, band in window W) over ALL windows W
of a finite test-accessible grid of height ≤ T_N := 2^N, and
Q_N := Q|_{V_N}. For the index we take the maximum over the grid:
κ_N := max_{W ∈ grid(N)} neg.ind(Q|_{V_W}) and n_N := dim V_W (common by design
of the grid). The maximum over a finite family specified without zeros is
test-accessible: no window is chosen using a zero.

**Definition 2.2 (non-standard index).** The *non-standard index* of the scheme is
$$\kappa^*(\mathcal{S}) := [\,\kappa_N\,] \in {}^*\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},
\qquad n^* := [\,n_N\,] \in {}^*\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.$$

**Definition 2.3 (infinitesimal signature).** The *infinitesimal signature* of the
scheme is
$$\sigma_\varepsilon(\mathcal{S}) := \mathrm{st}\!\left(\frac{\kappa^*}{n^*}\right)
\in [0,1].$$
(The quotient is limited because 0 ≤ κ_N ≤ n_N for every N: the negative index of
a form on a space of dimension n_N is ≤ n_N. The standard part exists.)

**Definition 2.4 (tensor tower).** For each N let Q̄_N be the induced form on
V_N/rad(Q_N) (non-degenerate), of signature (p_N, q_N), p_N + q_N = n̄_N,
q_N = κ_N (the radical contributes no negative directions). The *tower* is the
sequence of forms Q̄_N^{⊗2^k} on (V_N/rad)^{⊗2^k}, k = 0, 1, 2, …, with
$$x_k(N) := \frac{\mathrm{neg.ind}\,\big(\bar Q_N^{\otimes 2^k}\big)}{\bar n_N^{\,2^k}},
\qquad
\sigma^{(k)} := \mathrm{st}\big([\,x_k(N)\,]\big).$$

**Remark 2.5 (what is new in the definition).** Three things, worth separating.
(1) The move to *ℤ is not decoration: lim sup_N κ_N/n_N is not
multiplicative and does not support the tensor calculus of §2.4; st along 𝒰 is a
ring homomorphism and does support it — Robinson's machinery is load-bearing.
(2) σ_ε depends a priori on 𝒰; when κ_N/n_N converges, it does not depend (st = the
limit); the theorems below hold for EVERY 𝒰, which is the correct form of
robustness. (3) The object is NOT in ℱ: it is not neg.ind(Q|_{V′}) for any single
V′ — it is a germ of per-window indices with values in a non-Archimedean ring.
H1 and H2 are left violated; H3, H4 (per window), H5 are preserved.

### 2.3. [THEOREM] First theorems of the object

**Theorem 2.6 (well-definedness and triviality of the toll).** For every scheme 𝒮 and
every non-principal ultrafilter 𝒰: (a) κ* ∈ *ℤ and σ_ε ∈ [0,1] are well
defined; (b) the non-Archimedean bound κ* ≤ n* holds in *ℤ, unconditionally; (c) if
the scheme is nested within a fixed window (V_N ⊆ V_{N+1} within the same
window), κ_N is non-decreasing and then σ_ε does not depend on 𝒰 on that
subfamily.

*Proof.* (a) Quotients of sequences well defined in the ultrapower; the
bound 0 ≤ κ_N ≤ n_N gives limitation; st exists on limited elements (completeness of ℝ;
Robinson 1966, ch. 2 — standard fact about ultrapowers, not reproduced here). (b) Coordinate
by coordinate: κ_N ≤ n_N; the inequality passes to the quotient by Łoś's theorem. (c) If
V ⊆ V′, every negative subspace of Q|_V is one of Q|_{V′}: neg.ind does not decrease; a
monotone bounded sequence in [0,1] converges after normalization, and st of a
convergent sequence is its limit, for every 𝒰. ∎

**Theorem 2.7 (violation of NO-GO 1: Theorem 4.3 does not reach σ_ε).** The
impossibility statement of D110 Thm. 4.3 is false for the infinitesimal
signature, in the following precise sense: σ_ε satisfies the analogues
(L2′) *autonomy*: each coordinate κ_N is computable from arithmetic data without
zero positions (Prop. 2.8); (L3′) *detection*: under RH σ_ε = 0 for every
scheme; under ¬RH there exists a test-accessible scheme with σ_ε > 0 [status of this
half: §2.5, modulo the D108 realizability caveat]; (L4′) *toll-free
stability*: σ_ε is defined for every scheme without ever asserting any standard
uniform bound on the κ_N — the step of the old proof that converted
stability into RH does not apply.

*Proof of (L4′), which is the point.* Horn (b) of D110 Thm. 4.3 derives the
inconsistency thus: "either I is defined as a per-window index — and any
finite uniform bound on I is logically equivalent to RH (D108 Prop. 2.5), violating
(L4)". The implicit premise: for the invariant family {κ_W} to be a SINGLE
object, a common ceiling is needed, and in ℤ a common ceiling is a standard integer, whose
existence is RH. In *ℤ the common ceiling exists unconditionally and for free:
κ* ≤ n* (Thm. 2.6(b)) — an unlimited element of *ℤ is a legitimate object of the
value ring, not a pathology. The definition of σ_ε consumes only that
non-Archimedean bound and the limitation of the quotient; at no step is
"κ_N ≤ standard C" asserted, so D108 Prop. 2.5's equivalence is never invoked.
Horn (a) (sub-resolution ⟹ index 0) does not obstruct: in resolution schemes
σ_ε is defined just the same. Horn (c) (evaluation with precision o(1/log T)) is
re-examined in §2.5: the statement there is about the evaluation of I ∈ ℱ per
window; σ_ε shifts the evaluation to a macroscopic separation (0 vs ½ in the
tower), which does not refute (c) — it dodges it by changing what must be evaluated. ∎

**Proposition 2.8 (coordinate-wise autonomy: the finite-rank counterexample,
activated).** Each κ_N is computable from the arithmetic data (primes, pole,
gamma factor) without zero positions, via an exact, finite algebraic
procedure.

*Proof.* Two inputs. (1) *Value autonomy* (P43, Thm. 4.2
"autonomous value"; D107): for tests f, g of the program's space, Q(f,g) is
computable from the arithmetic blocks of the explicit formula — the mixed
zeros×primes terms are reabsorbed and the primes×primes block is the almost-primes
functional; no zero position enters. (2) *A priori finite rank*: V_W
has dimension n_W fixed by the specification (supports and bands), not by
zeros. Then the Gram matrix G_W = (Q(f_i, f_j))_{i,j≤n_W} in any test-accessible
basis of V_W is a Hermitian matrix of computable entries; its number
of negative eigenvalues — κ(Q|_{V_W}) — is computed exactly by D110 Prop. 4.5's
method: Newton identities on the traces tr(G_W^j), j ≤ n_W, and
reading the signature via Jacobi–Frobenius ([Gan59, Vol. 2, Ch. X]); or,
directly, by the Lagrange–Sylvester congruence algorithm on G_W
(diagonalization by congruence, exact in the coefficients' arithmetic). The
maximum over the finite grid is computable. ∎

**Remark 2.9 (how this reconciles with "inertia is not autonomous").** There is no
contradiction with P43 Thm. autonomy nor with D108 §7.4: what is not autonomous is the
PARTITION of the index into off×off / off×on blocks — the data a Form-B-type
inertia reader needed. κ_N is the TOTAL index of the restriction, without
partition: that was always autonomous in finite rank (it is the D110
Prop. 4.5 counterexample, which the program had classified as "abstract"). Definition 2.2
makes it structural: we do not need the partition, because detection will not be done
by separating blocks but by iterating the tower (Thm. 2.10) — the off×on
contamination, which was the poison of the partition, is exactly what the lower
bound κ_W ≳ m·n_W of D108 exploits in our favor.

**Theorem 2.10 (logistic law of the tensor tower; exact).** Let (p, q)
be the signature of a non-degenerate Hermitian form Q̄ on a space of
dimension n̄ = p + q, and let x_k := neg.ind(Q̄^{⊗2^k})/n̄^{2^k}. Then, for
every k ≥ 0, as an identity of rational numbers:
$$x_{k+1} = 2x_k(1 - x_k), \qquad\text{and in closed form}\qquad
1 - 2x_k = (1 - 2x_0)^{2^k}.$$
Consequently, for every scheme 𝒮 and every 𝒰, with σ⁰ := st([x_0(N)]):
$$\sigma^{(k)} = \tfrac12\Big(1 - \big(1 - 2\sigma^0\big)^{2^k}\Big),$$
and the tower has exactly two possible limits:
$$\lim_{k\to\infty} \sigma^{(k)} =
\begin{cases} 0, & \sigma^0 \in \{0, 1\},\\[2pt] \tfrac12, & \sigma^0 \in (0,1).\end{cases}$$

*Proof.* Tensor identity of the index for non-degenerate finite-dimensional forms
(Doc 106; P43 ledger L3): neg.ind(Q₁⊗Q₂) = p₁q₂ + q₁p₂, and the
positive part is p₁p₂ + q₁q₂. For Q̄^{⊗2^{k+1}} = (Q̄^{⊗2^k}) ⊗ (Q̄^{⊗2^k}),
with signature (P_k, K_k), P_k + K_k = n̄^{2^k}:
$$K_{k+1} = 2 P_k K_k, \qquad P_{k+1} = P_k^2 + K_k^2.$$
Dividing by n̄^{2^{k+1}} = (n̄^{2^k})²: x_{k+1} = 2(P_k/n̄^{2^k})(K_k/n̄^{2^k})
= 2(1−x_k)x_k. Closed form: 1 − 2x_{k+1} = 1 − 4x_k + 4x_k² = (1 − 2x_k)²;
iterating, 1 − 2x_k = (1 − 2x_0)^{2^k}. Passing to σ: each x_k(N) ∈ [0,1] is limited;
st is a ring homomorphism on the limited elements, so the polynomial identity
1 − 2x_{k+1} = (1 − 2x_k)² passes to the standard parts for each fixed k (k is
standard; the identity is used finitely many times). Limits: if σ⁰ ∈ (0,1),
|1 − 2σ⁰| < 1 and (1−2σ⁰)^{2^k} → 0, so σ^(k) → ½. If σ⁰ = 0, σ^(k) ≡ 0. If
σ⁰ = 1 (asymptotically negative-definite form), 1 − 2σ⁰ = −1 and (−1)^{2^k} = 1
for k ≥ 1: σ^(k) = 0 — consistent: the tensor square of a negative-definite
form is positive definite. ∎

**Proposition 2.11 (substantive calibration example).** (a) Toy scheme with
κ_N = ⌊αn_N⌋, α ∈ (0,1) fixed: σ⁰ = α and σ^(k) = ½(1 − (1−2α)^{2^k}) → ½; with
α = α_N → 0 but α_N n_N → ∞: σ⁰ = 0 and the tower is identically 0 — the
infinitesimal signature does NOT see defects of zero relative density, which is exactly
the correct scale: it distinguishes "a positive proportion of negative directions" from
"o(n) directions", it does not aim to see o(1/log T) events per coordinate. (b) For
the Weil form under RH: Q ≥ 0 on C_c^∞ (Weil 1952, program normalization:
D110 §3.2(a)) ⟹ κ_N = 0 for every scheme ⟹ σ⁰ = 0 and the tower is 0:
the RH branch of the bridge is a theorem with no caveats.

*Proof.* (a) Direct computation using Thm. 2.10; the second part: x_0(N) =
⌊α_N n_N⌋/n_N → 0 ⟹ st = 0 for every 𝒰. (b) Restriction of a
positive semidefinite form: no negative eigenvalues in any finite Gram matrix. ∎

**[WISH 2.12].** That there exist a "local reason" (axiom L5 of D110) annihilating each
κ_N — a per-window positivity theorem with a uniform mechanism. The tower does not
give it; what it gives is that the ABSENCE of that reason would be measured in a
macroscopic constant, not an asymptotic precision.

### 2.4. [BRIDGE] What this gives on ζ, and where the wall reappears

**Bridge Theorem 2.13 (logistic dichotomy for ζ; mixed status, declared).**
Let 𝒮* be the canonical scheme of Def. 2.1 (supremum window grid, resolution
tests of D108). Then:

(a) **[THEOREM]** Under RH: σ⁰(𝒮*) = 0 and lim_k σ^(k) = 0. (Prop. 2.11(b).)

(b) **[CITED-CONDITIONAL — the inherited caveat]** Under ¬RH with m ≥ 1 off-critical
quadruple: D108 (Prop. 2.3 + Lemma 2.4 + Prop. 2.5) gives, for the ALIGNED
window at resolution scale, index ≥ c·m·n_W with c > 0 absolute; since the
grid of 𝒮* contains a window at distance o(1) from the aligned one at each scale,
κ_N ≥ c′·m·n_N along a cofinal subsequence for a suitable 𝒰, whence
σ⁰ ≥ c′m > 0 and lim_k σ^(k) = ½. **Status:** the lower bound by aligned
window is proved in D108 in the toy model under Hypothesis D and for the program's
realizations with m = 1; the extension to general m and the stability under the
o(1) shift of the grid are D108's declared GAP (its Lemma 2.4 on
realizability). No theorem is claimed; what is claimed is: *if* D108's realizability
holds, *then* the dichotomy is complete.

(c) **[COROLLARY of (a)+(b), with (b)'s status]**
$$\mathrm{RH} \iff \lim_{k\to\infty} \sigma^{(k)}(\mathcal{S}^*) = 0
\iff \lim_k \sigma^{(k)} \neq \tfrac12.$$

**Frank tracing of the transmutation.** Let us compare with the inventory of
the master quantifier (P43, Principle 3.1) coordinate by coordinate:

1. *What it used to be:* certifying RH via indices required either a standard
   uniform bound (= RH, circular) or evaluating Π with relative precision o(1/log T)
   per window (D108 eq. 4.1; D110 Lemma 2.4(b): the same o(1/log T) of the
   winding). The signal was second order against a leading term (log T)².
2. *What it is now:* certifying RH via the tower requires distinguishing
   lim_k σ^(k) = 0 from lim_k σ^(k) = ½ — two macroscopic constants separated
   by ¼ already at the first k with (1−2σ⁰)^{2^k} ≤ ½. The logistic amplification does
   what Form B could not (D110 Obs. 5.3: "Deligne minus finiteness"): since the
   tower is built PER WINDOW (finite rank per coordinate), the denominator is
   fixed — n̄^{2^k} is data, not estimate — and the iteration wins against a
   fixed bound. It is literally Rankin–Deligne squaring executed where
   finiteness is true: in the window, not globally.
3. *Where the wall reappears:* in two places, and naming them is the value of
   the exercise. (i) The standard part is **not effective**: σ⁰ = 0 is a
   Π₁ statement over the computable sequence (κ_N/n_N)_N — "for all N large, κ_N/n_N
   small" — D110 Obs. 2.5's Π₁ form reappears intact. But with a real
   improvement in coordinates: each individual event is now *finite linear algebra of

## 3. NO-GO 2: the seminorm dichotomy, negated by non-homogeneity

### 3.1. [EXTRACTION] The hypotheses of the dichotomy (D113 §5.4)

The statement: *every almost-periodicity seminorm that sees compacts is
destroyed by the growth of ζ in the strip; every seminorm under which ζ is
almost-periodic is blind to compacts.* Hypotheses deployed, with their place in
the proof:

- **H1 (homogeneity):** ‖λf‖ = |λ|‖f‖. Used in D113 Lemma 5.1 (a.p. ⟹
  boundedness): "f is within < ε of a trigonometric polynomial P, and ‖f‖ ≤ ε + ‖P‖ with
  ‖P‖ < ∞" — the finiteness of ‖P‖ for arbitrary P is homogeneity + subadditivity
  over the monomials.
- **H2 (subadditivity):** ‖f+g‖ ≤ ‖f‖+‖g‖. Used in the same step (triangle
  inequality) and in that the a.p. class be a closed vector space.
- **H3 (seeing compacts):** the seminorm assigns positive weight to fixed windows.
  Used so that Rouché can bite (Obs. 2.4 of D112): without H3 the detector is useless.
- **H4 (growth of ζ):** Ω-theorems + subharmonicity of |f|^p (D113 Thm. 5.2):
  large values inflate the fixed-window norm to ∞.

**Survival analysis.** Without H3 there is no detector: not negotiable. H4 is a
theorem about ζ: it cannot be negated. The lethal chain is H1+H2 ⟹ (a.p. ⟹
‖ζ‖ < ∞) + H3+H4 ⟹ (‖ζ‖ = ∞): contradiction. The ENTIRE proof of the
destructive side goes through "a.p. ⟹ boundedness in the norm" — which is a theorem
ABOUT seminorms. A detector bounded by construction makes that step vacuous: the
implication "a.p. ⟹ bounded" becomes trivially true and
contentless, and H4 has no norm to inflate. The weakest hypothesis to negate:
**H1+H2 — being a seminorm** — replacing the vector-space detection structure
with a bounded metric structure. Contact with arithmetic is untouched: the
detector is evaluated on the same shifts of ζ.

### 3.2. [NEW-DEFINITION] The chordal detector

On the Riemann sphere ℂ̂, the chordal metric:
$$\chi(a,b) := \frac{|a-b|}{\sqrt{1+|a|^2}\,\sqrt{1+|b|^2}}, \qquad
\chi(a,\infty) := \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|a|^2}}, \qquad \chi \le 1.$$

**Definition 3.1 (chordal window detector).** For D ⊂ {½ < σ < 1} compact and
f, g meromorphic in a neighborhood of D:
$$\Delta_D(f,g) := \sup_{s\in D}\ \chi\big(f(s),\, g(s)\big) \in [0,1].$$
The *chordal recurrence* of ζ in D: there exist τ_k → ∞ with
Δ_D(ζ(\cdot+iτ_k), ζ) → 0. The associated growth quantity is the **spherical
derivative** f^{\#}(s) := |f'(s)|/(1+|f(s)|²) (the derivative with respect to χ; Marty), and
we define the **orbit's Marty modulus**:
$$\mu^{\#}_D(T) := \sup_{0\le\tau\le T}\ \sup_{s\in D}\ \zeta^{\#}(s+i\tau).$$

**Remark 3.2.** Δ_D is not a seminorm nor does it come from one: there is no
homogeneity (χ(2a, 0) ≠ 2χ(a,0)), no scaling inequality, and Δ_D(f, 0) ≤ 1 for every
f. It is a complete metric on C(D, ℂ̂). H1 and H2 are left negated; H3 is
checked below.

**Remark 3.3 (variant registered and why discarded).** The objective
also suggested growth-modulated detection d(f) = liminf |f|/M(t). It was
explored and discarded in favor of χ for a structural reason: any
modulation M(t) chosen a priori is a function of the program, and Ω-theorems
are non-uniform in t (large values occur at uncontrolled heights):
the correct modulation would depend on the orbit itself. The chordal metric modulates
by the POINTWISE VALUE — 1+|f|² — which is the only modulation available at the
exact point and moment of the large value. It is the canonical solution of the same wish.

### 3.3. [THEOREM] First theorems

**Theorem 3.4 (violation of NO-GO 2: both horns fail for Δ_D).**
(a) Δ_D *sees compacts*: if f ≠ g at some point of D (as continuous functions
D → ℂ̂), then Δ_D(f,g) > 0; in particular Δ_D does not identify functions that
differ in the window — the blindness of B²/Weyl (D112 Obs. 2.4) does not affect it.
(b) Δ_D *survives growth*: sup_τ Δ_D(ζ(\cdot+iτ), g) ≤ 1 for any g;
D113 Thm. 5.2's destructive mechanism is inapplicable — its proof requires
(i) "a.p. ⟹ boundedness in the norm" (Lemma 5.1: uses H1+H2, which Δ_D lacks — and
for Δ_D boundedness is unconditional and vacuous) and (ii) subharmonicity of |f|^p
to inflate the norm with Ω-values — χ(f(s), g(s)) is not subharmonic in s and is
bounded by 1: there is no norm to inflate. (c) Hence the dichotomy's statement
("sees compacts ⟹ destroyed by growth") is false in the category
of bounded metric detection structures: Δ_D is a counterexample.

*Proof.* (a) χ is a metric on ℂ̂: f(s₀) ≠ g(s₀) ⟹ χ(f(s₀),g(s₀)) > 0
⟹ sup ≥ that value. (b) χ ≤ 1 by definition; for the inapplicability: D113's
Lemma 5.1 deduces ‖f‖ ≤ ε + ‖P‖ using the triangle inequality of the SEMInorm
on f = (f−P) + P and the finiteness of ‖P‖ which comes from homogeneity over the
monomials e^{iλt}; with Δ_D there is no ‖·‖: the quantity is bounded by 1 from the
start and the implication "almost-periodic ⟹ bounded" has no content to exploit. D113's
Theorem 5.2 needs a window seminorm whose value grows with
sup_D |ζ|; χ is built so that two large values close together in ℂ̂ give
small χ: V_n = |ζ(σ+it_n)| → ∞ gives χ(ζ, ∞) → 0, not ∞. (c) immediate from (a)+(b).
∎

**Theorem 3.5 (preservation of detection power: local equivalence).** Let
D ⊂ {½<σ<1} be compact, M := sup_D |ζ| < ∞, and
ε₀ := 1/(2\sqrt{1+M^2}). Then for every τ and every 0 < ε < ε₀:
$$\Delta_D(\zeta(\cdot+i\tau), \zeta) < \varepsilon \;\Longrightarrow\;
\sup_{s\in D} |\zeta(s+i\tau) - \zeta(s)| \;\le\; C(M)\,\varepsilon,
\qquad C(M) := \sqrt{1+(2M+2)^2}\,\sqrt{1+M^2},$$
and conversely sup_D |ζ(s+iτ)−ζ(s)| < ε ⟹ Δ_D < ε (since χ(a,b) ≤ |a−b|). Hence:
*chordal recurrence of ζ in D ⟺ LP-112 in D*, and chordal recurrence
feeds Rouché exactly the same way (D112 Thm. 2.3 applies verbatim:
chordal recurrence ⟹ m ∈ {0,∞}).

*Proof.* Let a := ζ(s+iτ), b := ζ(s), |b| ≤ M. First: χ(a,b) < ε₀
forces |a| ≤ 2M+2. Indeed, if |a| > 2M+2:
$$\chi(a,b) = \frac{|a-b|}{\sqrt{1+|a|^2}\sqrt{1+|b|^2}}
\ \ge\ \frac{|a|-M}{\sqrt{1+|a|^2}\,\sqrt{1+M^2}}
\ \ge\ \frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+M^2}} = \varepsilon_0,$$
where the last inequality uses (|a|−M)/√(1+|a|²) ≥ ½ for |a| ≥ 2M+2 (because
|a| − M ≥ (|a|+2)/2 ≥ ½√(1+|a|²) when |a| ≥ 2M+2 ≥ 2: it is verified that
(|a|+2)² ≥ 1+|a|² ⟺ 4|a|+3 ≥ 0, and |a|−M ≥ |a| − (|a|−2)/2 = (|a|+2)/2). With
|a| ≤ 2M+2 and |b| ≤ M: |a−b| = χ(a,b)·√(1+|a|²)√(1+|b|²) ≤ ε·C(M). Taking sup over
s ∈ D gives the conclusion. ∎

**Remark 3.6.** Theorem 3.5 is deliberately "disappointing" and is
information: the new detector does NOT change what recurrence on a fixed
compact means — it changes WHICH THEOREMS CAN EXIST about the detector. The
dichotomy of D113 showed that the detector with the required properties did not
exist in the category of seminorms; Δ_D exists, sees the zero's window, and
no Ω-theorem can kill it. The live question shifts to compactness.

**Theorem 3.7 (the Marty mirror; unconditional).** For every closed disc
D = D̄(s₀, r) ⊂ {½<σ<1}:
$$\sup_{\tau\ge 0}\ \sup_{s\in D}\ \zeta^{\#}(s+i\tau) = \infty,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \mu^{\#}_D(T) \to \infty.$$
More precisely: for every K > 0, the set
{τ : sup_{s∈D} ζ^{\#}(s+iτ) > K} has positive lower density. Consequently the
family {ζ(\cdot+iτ)}_τ is NOT normal in Marty's sense on any disc of the
strip: the extraction of chordal limits (the analogue of link E2 of D113's
chain §4.1) fails for the full orbit.

*Proof.* Fix K > 0. Let δ := 1 and the target
$$g(s) := \delta\,\exp\!\Big(\frac{2K}{\delta}(s-s_0)\Big),$$
entire and zero-free; in particular g ∈ H₀^c(D̄(s₀, r)) (continuous and nonzero on the
closed disc, holomorphic in the interior; the closed disc has connected
complement). By Voronin's universality theorem in its modern form (D102,
Theorem 2.1, verified there against Matsumoto Thm. 1), the set of τ with
$$\sup_{|s-s_0|\le r}\ |\zeta(s+i\tau) - g(s)| < \eta, \qquad
\eta := \min\!\Big(\frac{Kr}{2},\ \frac{\delta}{2}\Big)\cdot\frac{1}{2},$$
has positive lower density. For each such τ, we estimate ζ^{\#} at s₀+iτ:
(i) |ζ(s₀+iτ)| ≤ |g(s₀)| + η = δ + η ≤ 3δ/2;
(ii) by Cauchy's formula applied to ζ(\cdot+iτ) − g over |s−s₀| = r:
|ζ'(s₀+iτ) − g'(s₀)| ≤ η/r, and g'(s₀) = 2K. Hence
$$\zeta^{\#}(s_0+i\tau) = \frac{|\zeta'(s_0+i\tau)|}{1+|\zeta(s_0+i\tau)|^2}
\ \ge\ \frac{2K - \eta/r}{1 + (3\delta/2)^2}
\ \ge\ \frac{2K - K/4}{1 + 9/4} \ >\ \frac{K}{2}.$$
Since K is arbitrary, sup_τ sup_D ζ^{\#} = ∞ (replacing K by 2K gives the
density form at each level). Marty non-normality is Marty's criterion: a
family of meromorphic functions is normal in D if and only if the spherical
derivatives are uniformly bounded on compacts of D; here they are not
bounded on any disc. ∎

## 4. NO-GO 3: the individual-orbit obstruction, negated by re-topologization

### 4.1. [EXTRACTION] The hypotheses of the obstruction (D102 §7)

The statement: the transfer [unconditional recurrence of ω₀ in the torus] ⟹
[recurrence of ζ] fails because the Euler observable Φ is discontinuous
exactly on the arithmetic orbit, and that continuity is RH-equivalent
(D102 Prop. 7.2). Hypotheses deployed:

- **H1 (product topology on Ω):** gives compactness, and with it minimality and
  unique ergodicity of the Kronecker flow (D102 E1–E3) — the "free" side of the
  transfer: EVERY point, including ω₀, is uniformly recurrent.
- **H2 (continuity as the notion of regularity):** the transfer uses
  continuity of Φ at ω₀ with respect to THAT topology; unique ergodicity controls
  continuous observables and nothing else.
- **H3 (the fixed observable):** Φ(ω) = random Euler product, defined where
  it converges; its domain excludes the orbit of ω₀ under ¬RH
  (Prop. 7.2).

**Survival analysis.** H3 is a theorem (Prop. 7.2): it cannot be negated
without changing the observable, and D102 showed the observable is essentially
unique (it is the one connecting with ζ). H2 without H1 is vacuous: continuity is
relative to a topology. The negatable hypothesis is **H1**: nothing forces
the product topology to be used. The no-go does NOT survive its negation in the
following exact sense: the statement "Φ is discontinuous at ω₀" is relative to
the topology, and there exists a finer topology where the family of truncated
observables is equicontinuous at EVERY point (Thm. 4.6) — the "exceptional point"
is an artifact of the product topology. What must be audited is the price: which
theorems from H1 (compactness, minimality, free recurrence) survive the
refinement. Answer: none (Lemma 4.5) — and the exact accounting of that loss is
the content of the bridge.

### 4.2. [NEW-DEFINITION] The arithmetic topology

Notation: Ω = ∏_p 𝕋_p the Bohr torus; for ω ∈ Ω, X ≥ 2 and s in the strip,
$$S_X(s;\omega) := \sum_{p\le X} \omega(p)\,p^{-s}, \qquad
\zeta_X(s,\omega) := \prod_{p\le X}\big(1-\omega(p)p^{-s}\big)^{-1},$$
$$\log\zeta_X(s,\omega) = S_X(s;\omega) + R_X(s;\omega), \qquad
R_X(s;\omega) := \sum_{p\le X}\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\omega(p)^k}{k\,p^{ks}}$$
(branch given by the series; converges because |ω(p)p^{−s}| = p^{−σ} < 1).

**Definition 4.1 (uniform-truncation pseudometrics).** For D compact
⊂ {½<σ<1}:
$$d_D(\omega,\omega') := \sup_{X\ge2}\ \sup_{s\in D}\
\big|S_X(s;\omega) - S_X(s;\omega')\big| \ \in [0,\infty].$$

**Definition 4.2 (arithmetic topology).** 𝒯_ar is the topology on Ω generated
by the product topology together with the balls {ω′ : d_D(ω, ω′) < ε}, over
all compact D in the strip, ω ∈ Ω, ε > 0. The *arithmetic phase space* is
(Ω, 𝒯_ar) with the same flow T_τ(ω) = (p^{−iτ}ω(p))_p.

**Remark 4.3 (the idea in one line).** d_D measures closeness of phases WEIGHTED BY
ARITHMETIC and UNIFORM IN TRUNCATION: two points of the torus are close if
all their partial Dirichlet polynomials are close at once. It is the
minimal initial topology making the family of truncations equicontinuous — the set
of quasi-periods from the objective's suggestion is exactly the preimage under
τ ↦ T_τω₀ of the d_D-balls at ω₀.

### 4.3. [THEOREM] First theorems

**Lemma 4.4 (strictly finer).** 𝒯_ar is finer than the product
topology, and strictly: for every D containing a real point σ₀ ∈ (½,1), the
ball {ω : d_D(ω, ω₀) < 1} contains no product-basic neighborhood of ω₀.

*Proof.* Finer: by definition (open sets are added). Strict: a
product-basic neighborhood of ω₀ restricts finitely many coordinates, say p ≤ N.
Define ω′ ∈ Ω by ω′(p) := 1 for p ≤ N and ω′(p) := −1 for p > N. Then
ω′ is in the basic neighborhood, but for X > N:
$$\big|S_X(\sigma_0;\omega_0) - S_X(\sigma_0;\omega')\big|
= \Big|\sum_{N<p\le X} 2\,p^{-\sigma_0}\Big| = 2\!\!\sum_{N<p\le X}\!\! p^{-\sigma_0}
\ \xrightarrow[X\to\infty]{}\ \infty$$
because Σ_p p^{−σ₀} diverges for σ₀ < 1. So d_D(ω₀, ω′) = ∞ ≥ 1. ∎

**Lemma 4.5 (the price: compactness dies, and with it the free dynamics).**
(Ω, 𝒯_ar) is not compact. Consequently, theorems E1–E3 of D102 §7.1
(minimality, unique ergodicity, uniform recurrence of every point) lose their
hypotheses: none is available in 𝒯_ar by the classical route.

*Proof.* The identity map id: (Ω, 𝒯_ar) → (Ω, product) is continuous (Lemma 4.4)
and bijective, and the product topology is Hausdorff. If (Ω, 𝒯_ar) were
compact, id would be a homeomorphism (continuous bijection from a compact to a Hausdorff
space), so 𝒯_ar = product — contradicting Lemma 4.4's strictness. The second
statement: minimality/unique ergodicity of rotations are proved over compact
groups (D102 cites Walters ch. 6); without compactness there is no Birkhoff
recurrence theorem nor normalizable Haar measure to average over — the statements are
left unproved, and in fact the recurrence of ω₀ in 𝒯_ar is a nontrivial open
question (§4.4). ∎

**Theorem 4.6 (dissolution of the exceptional point: universal equicontinuity of the
truncated observable).** Let D′ ⊂ {½<σ<1} be compact and σ₁ := min{Re s : s ∈ D′}
> ½. There exists a function η: (0,1) → (0,∞) with η(d) → 0 as d → 0 (explicit in
the proof, η(d) ≤ C(σ₁)·d^{(2σ₁−1)/2} + 8d^{1/2}) such that for ALL
ω, ω′ ∈ Ω with d := d_{D′}(ω, ω′) < 1:
$$\sup_{X\ge2}\ \sup_{s\in D'}\
\big|\log\zeta_X(s,\omega) - \log\zeta_X(s,\omega')\big|\ \le\ d + \eta(d).$$
In particular the family {log ζ_X(\cdot, ·)}_{X≥2}, viewed as functions from
(Ω, 𝒯_ar) to (C(D′), ‖·‖_∞), is uniformly equicontinuous, uniformly in X,
**at every point of Ω** — including ω₀. The arithmetic point is not exceptional for
𝒯_ar: the regularity of the truncated observable at ω₀ is exactly the same as
at any other point.

*Proof.* log ζ_X = S_X + R_X. Part k=1: |S_X(s;ω) − S_X(s;ω′)| ≤ d
for every X and s ∈ D′, by definition of d. Part k≥2: with
δ_p := |ω(p) − ω′(p)| ∈ [0,2],
$$\big|R_X(s;\omega)-R_X(s;\omega')\big|
\ \le\ \sum_{p\le X}\sum_{k\ge2} \frac{|\omega(p)^k-\omega'(p)^k|}{k\,p^{k\sigma}}
\ \le\ \sum_{p}\sum_{k\ge2} \frac{\min(2,\,k\,\delta_p)}{k}\,p^{-k\sigma_1},$$
using |a^k − b^k| ≤ k|a−b| for |a| = |b| = 1. We bound the inner sum two
ways: (i) by δ_p Σ_{k≥2} p^{−kσ₁} and (ii) by 2Σ_{k≥2} p^{−kσ₁}/k ≤
Σ_{k≥2} p^{−kσ₁}; and Σ_{k≥2} p^{−kσ₁} = p^{−2σ₁}/(1−p^{−σ₁}) ≤ 4p^{−2σ₁} (since
p^{−σ₁} ≤ 2^{−1/2} gives 1/(1−p^{−σ₁}) ≤ 1/(1−2^{−1/2}) < 4). Hence
$$\big|R_X(\omega)-R_X(\omega')\big| \ \le\ 4\sum_p p^{-2\sigma_1}\,\min(1,\,\delta_p).$$
It remains to control δ_p with d. Taking in the definition of d the consecutive
truncations X = p and X = p^- (the previous prime), the difference of partial
sums isolates the term p:
$$\delta_p\,p^{-\sigma} = \big|(S_p - S_{p^-})(s;\omega) - (S_p - S_{p^-})(s;\omega')\big|
\ \le\ 2d \quad (s\in D'),$$
and choosing s ∈ D′ with Re s = σ₁ (if D′ does not contain such a point, σ₁ is
attained on the boundary; take s realizing the minimum of Re): δ_p ≤ 2d·p^{σ₁}. Split
at P ≥ 2 to be chosen:
$$4\sum_{p\le P} p^{-2\sigma_1}\cdot 2d\,p^{\sigma_1}
\ +\ 4\sum_{p>P} p^{-2\sigma_1}
\ \le\ 8d \sum_{n\le P} n^{-\sigma_1}\ +\ 4\sum_{n>P} n^{-2\sigma_1}
\ \le\ 8d\,P\ +\ \frac{4}{2\sigma_1-1}\,P^{1-2\sigma_1},$$
(using Σ_{n≤P} n^{−σ₁} ≤ P and the tail integral with 2σ₁ > 1). With P := ⌈d^{−1/2}⌉:
$$\eta(d) := 8\,d^{1/2}\cdot 2\ +\ \frac{4}{2\sigma_1-1}\, d^{(2\sigma_1-1)/2}
\ \xrightarrow[d\to0]{}\ 0.$$
(The factor 2 absorbs the ceiling on P.) Adding the k=1 and k≥2 parts gives
the bound d + η(d), uniform in X, in s ∈ D′ and in the pair (ω, ω′). ∎

**Corollary 4.7 (multiplicative form, ready for Rouché).** Under the hypotheses
of Theorem 4.6, for every X and s ∈ D′:
$$e^{-(d+\eta(d))}\ \le\ \Big|\frac{\zeta_X(s,\omega)}{\zeta_X(s,\omega')}\Big|
\ \le\ e^{\,d+\eta(d)},$$
i.e. the truncated products are multiplicatively close, uniformly in the
truncation. (Exponential of the logarithm's bound.) ∎

**Theorem 4.8 (transfer: what 𝒯_ar-recurrence would buy).** Suppose:
(i) ω₀ is 𝒯_ar-recurrent: there exist τ_k → ∞ with d_{D′}(T_{τ_k}ω₀, ω₀) → 0; and
(ii) *tail anchoring*: for the same τ_k, ζ_X(s+iτ_k) → ζ(s+iτ_k) as
X → ∞, uniformly in s ∈ D and in k (D ⊂ int D′ compact). Then LP-112 holds
in D and therefore m ∈ {0,∞} (D112, Thm. 2.3).

*Proof.* Note that S_X(s; T_τω₀) = Σ_{p≤X} p^{−iτ}p^{−s} = S_X(s+iτ; ω₀)
and similarly ζ_X(s, T_τω₀) = ζ_X(s+iτ, ω₀) =: ζ_X(s+iτ): the pseudometric
over the orbit IS the uniform-in-truncation self-distance of the Dirichlet
polynomials. By (i) and Corollary 4.7, with ε_k := d_k + η(d_k) → 0:
|ζ_X(s+iτ_k)| = |ζ_X(s)|·e^{O(ε_k)} and, from the bound on the logarithm,
|ζ_X(s+iτ_k) − ζ_X(s)| ≤ |ζ_X(s)|(e^{ε_k} − 1) uniformly in X and s ∈ D′. By
(ii) and the classical convergence ζ_X(s) → ζ(s) in D (same tails at τ = 0, a
particular case of (ii) with k fixed... — for τ = 0 the uniform convergence on
compacts of ζ_X to ζ is NOT a theorem in the strip; we use (ii), which includes it
by hypothesis when taking the limit at fixed k and continuity: precisely, (ii) with
k frozen plus the multiplicative bound give, letting X → ∞ first on both sides:
|ζ(s+iτ_k) − ζ(s)| ≤ limsup_X |ζ_X(s+iτ_k) − ζ_X(s)| ≤
(e^{ε_k} − 1)·limsup_X |ζ_X(s)|, and this last quantity is finite and uniform in s ∈ D by
(ii) applied to τ = 0 if 0 is in the closure of the hypotheses or, if not, by the
same anchoring applied to the base point — we declare (ii) including τ = 0). Hence
sup_D |ζ(s+iτ_k) − ζ(s)| → 0: LP-112. The conclusion m ∈ {0,∞} is D112 Thm. 2.3
(Rouché with frozen constants). ∎

**Remark 4.9 (frankness about Theorem 4.8).** The theorem is a pure
conditional and is stated to KEEP TRACK, not to celebrate: it shows that the
pair (i)+(ii) is exactly sufficient, and the bridge below shows that the old
wall splits exactly between (i) and (ii).

### 4.4. [BRIDGE] Total transmutation, with exact accounting

Applied to ζ: in 𝒯_ar the obstruction of NO-GO 3 LITERALLY disappears — the
truncated observable is equicontinuous at ω₀ (Thm. 4.6), the arithmetic point stops
being exceptional, and the transfer works (Thm. 4.8). So where is the
wall? Accounting:

1. **Free recurrence was lost.** In the product topology, ω₀ was
   uniformly recurrent for free (compactness + minimality, D102 E3). In 𝒯_ar
   there is no compactness (Lemma 4.5). The 𝒯_ar-recurrence of ω₀ — hypothesis (i) —
   is: for every ε there exists arbitrarily large τ with
   sup_X sup_D |S_X(s+iτ) − S_X(s)| < ε. For EACH fixed X, Kronecker–Weyl gives
   relatively dense quasi-periods (finite torus; D112 Lemma 2.2): that is
   unconditional. The sup_X — the uniformity in truncation — is the new part, and is
   exactly the control of the tails Σ_{X<p≤Y}(p^{−iτ}−1)p^{−s} at the CHOSEN
   τ: LP-113's non-generic selection problem (D113 §4.5), in
   truncation coordinates. GAP: not even under RH do we know how to prove (i) — the
   convergence of Σp^{−s} under RH is locally uniform but not uniform in
   height, so not even the easy branch is free. Declared.
2. **Hypothesis (ii) is MW-2 in disguise:** anchoring ζ_X → ζ in the window
   along the τ_k is the propagation of the Euler product into the strip over a
   specific sequence — D102 Prop. 7.2 in sequential form.
3. **The conservation theorem (synthesis of the bridge).** Combining: any
   topology 𝒯 that makes (a) the truncated observable equicontinuous at ω₀ with
   modulus uniform in X and (b) ω₀ recurrent, together with (c) tail anchoring,
   implies m ∈ {0,∞} (Thm. 4.8's proof only uses (a)+(b)+(c)). Since
   m ∈ {0,∞} is not a theorem of the program, NO topology can give (a)+(b)+(c)
   unconditionally today; and the product topology and 𝒯_ar are the two extremes of
   the trade-off: product = (b) free, (a) impossible (D102 Prop. 7.2); 𝒯_ar = (a)
   free (Thm. 4.6), (b) open and equivalent to the selection problem. **The
   obstruction was not topological: it is a presupposition conserved between
   the regularity of the observable and the recurrence of the point.** This is new
   information about the wall: D102 saw it as discontinuity (a fact about the
   observable); the re-topologization shows it is a CONSERVATION LAW between
   observable and dynamics — you refine the topology to regularize the observable and
   the dynamics gives you back exactly the same debt in recurrence.
4. [WISH 4.10] An INTERMEDIATE topology: finer than the product only in the
   "long-tail directions" (a d_D with sup over X ≤ X(ε) growing in a
   controlled way), where perhaps a fragment of compactness survives (sequential
   compactness on balls) and a fragment of equicontinuity. Not developed here;
   it remains registered as the only unexplored gap in the trade-off.

### 4.5. Verdict NO-GO 3

**Viable new mathematics: YES as an object, NO as a lever.** Theorems 4.4–4.6
are real and the universal equicontinuity (Thm. 4.6, with explicit modulus) is a
clean result that dissolves the "exceptional point" as an artifact. **Bridge:
total transmutation.** The wall reappears whole, split into (i) = non-generic
selection (LP-113) and (ii) = sequential MW-2; the residual value is the
conservation law of point 3 — the most structural formulation the program has
of why the ergodic framework cannot win: it is not that a theorem is missing in the
category; it is that the category charges in recurrence what it pays in
continuity.
