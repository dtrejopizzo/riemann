                                                                                       Abstract
                                               Weil’s criterion states that the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to the non-
                                           negativity of an explicit quadratic form Q(f ) on test functions. For f supported in a window
                                           [−L, L] we study the normalized infimum λ∗ (L) = inf f Q(f )/∥f ∥22 from both sides.
                                               Lower bounds (unconditional). Positivity on bounded support is classical for supp f ⊆
                                           [− log2 2 , log2 2 ] (Yoshida; Connes–Consani by a different method). We prove, by certified com-

                                           support 1.6, which is 2.3 times the classical range. The proof rests on a one-stroke reduction:
                                           a pointwise envelope for the Weil symbol, with a comb constant that Weyl equidistribution
                                           shows to be optimal, converts positivity on the whole window into positive semidefiniteness
                                           of a single finite matrix, whose entries converge super-exponentially and whose coupling to

1.1   Weil’s criterion and the window profile

positivity on larger and larger spaces of test functions.

studied. Positivity is a yes/no statement, but one may ask how small Q(f ) can actually get,

modes, working precision 50 digits) is described in Section 5; its error budget closes with 24
orders of magnitude to spare, and an independent run at T ♯ = 150 gives a second, internally
monotone certification. Its four ingredients are an envelope lemma proved by classical means,
a Bernstein-ellipse quadrature bound, a forbidden-region tail bound and a verified Cholesky
residual. All are elementary and fully explicit; there are no anonymous constants.
    Section 7 reports an exploratory computation at L = 1.19 (support 2.38). An earlier draft of
this work claimed a certified theorem at that support, on the basis of a per-prime “sharpening”
of the envelope constant which in fact bounds the prime comb in the wrong direction; Remark 3.3
explains the error, and Lemma 3.2 shows that no such sharpening can exist. We retract the claim.
We keep the computation, a 950 × 950 matrix at 70-digit precision verified positive definite with
λmin ∈ (10−48 , 10−46 ), as evidence consistent with positivity, and Section 7 gives the corrected
cost of a genuine support-2.38 certificate together with a route that may bring that cost back
down.
    Finally, the restriction to even test functions in Theorem 1.2 can be removed. The Weil form
decouples into parity sectors, with a sign reversal of the pole term in the odd sector, and the
one-stroke reduction applies to each sector separately. Section 6 certifies Q ≥ 8.2 × 10−15 on
the odd sector at support 1.6, whence positivity for arbitrary complex f (Corollary 6.3), and
resolves the parity structure of the window spectrum: the ground state is simple and even, with
certified margins (Theorem 6.2). This is the first of the two missing spectral hypotheses in the
operator program of Connes–Consani–Moscovici [9] and Connes–van Suijlekom [8], verified here
at a fixed scale with explicit constants.

theorem and the measured constant 2π 2 in (5) corroborate each other.

Theorem 1.4 (Barrier for pointwise-envelope certificates). Any application of Theorem 1.1
requires T ♯ > T1 (L) = 2πeAL , and AL = (4 + o(1))eL as L → ∞ by the prime number theorem;
hence the matrix size N ≍ LT1 and the number of quadrature nodes grow doubly exponentially
in L. Moreover supt of the prime comb equals AL exactly (Lemma 3.2), so within the class of
certificates that bound the comb pointwise the threshold T1 cannot be lowered.

    Theorem 1.4 is unconditional and is proved in Section 14. Two statements of a different
logical character belong beside it, and we keep them outside the theorem: the first is measured
rather than proved, the second is interpretation.

Remark 1.5 (The margin to be resolved; measured, not proved). The margin any certificate
must resolve collapses at the Landau–Widom rate: by the certified upper bounds of Section 12
and the law (5), − ln λ∗ (L) ≃ 2π 2 N (T ∗ )/ ln N (T ∗ ) with T ∗ = 2πe2L , so that the floor falls
doubly exponentially in L: from 10−17 at L = 0.8 through 10−48 at L = 1.2 to 10−283 at L = 2.
The status of this statement is that of (5) itself, namely a law fitted to certified upper bounds;
converting it into a proved lower bound on the cost of an arbitrary positivity certificate is open
(Section 14).

Remark 1.6 (What the threshold means). T1 is the last frequency at which the prime comb,
in the worst case of phase alignment, is still able to drive ΨL to zero. (The supremum AL is
attained only in the limit, so the actual last sign change of ΨL lies somewhat below T1 ; what
Lemma 3.2 excludes is any pointwise argument that lowers the threshold.) Certifying positivity
past support ≈ 3.2 (T1 ≈ 107 ) by any pointwise-envelope method is therefore computationally
void. To improve on pointwise envelopes one must prove that the phases (t log p)p cannot align,
an arithmetic statement about simultaneous Diophantine approximation of {log p}, of the same
nature as the information carried by the zeros themselves.

    Under RH, Q ≥ 0 at every support, so an optimist will find Theorem 1.2 unsurprising. Its
content lies elsewhere, in unconditionality, explicitness and cost: it locates how much certified
positivity a given amount of computation buys, on the one route to RH where partial progress is
even well defined. The reduction theorem makes the certificates easy to check, since checking one
means factoring one matrix. The barrier theorem shows that the route closes doubly exponentially
fast, which turns the folklore that “positivity gets exponentially hard” into measured constants
and a specific arithmetic obstruction. Certificate, law and barrier are what we take this program
to deliver: the frontier moves from log 2 to 1.6, the profile beyond it is measured out to L = 2,
and the wall behind both is mapped rather than left implicit.

1.5    Related work
The tradition of attacking RH-adjacent quantities numerically is best represented by the de
Bruijn–Newman constant ΛdBN , whose lower bounds were improved over decades of computation

                                                 6
before the proof of ΛdBN ≥ 0 by Rodgers and Tao [14] (see also [15] for the current upper
bound); our study is in the same spirit, applied to a different, and to our knowledge previously
unmeasured, RH-equivalent quantity. On the theoretical side, Weil positivity for restricted
classes of test functions has been established by Connes–Consani using prolate spheroidal wave
functions [6]; the appearance of the Landau–Widom constant in (5) gives a quantitative bridge
to that circle of ideas, and Theorem 1.2 extends the range of unconditional positivity itself. Very
recently, finite-rank truncations of the Weil form have become objects of study in their own
right: Connes–van Suijlekom [8] and Connes–Consani–Moscovici [9] construct explicit Galerkin
matrices at a prime cutoff, Suzuki [10] develops a parallel screw-function treatment, and Groskin
[11] proves two-sided certification rules for such truncations, including an explicit “inconclusive
band” of spurious negative eigenvalues caused by the archimedean cutoff, a phenomenon close
to the one we diagnose in Section 10. Those works study a fixed truncation family at fixed
cutoffs; the window-variational infimum (1), its unconditional certification, and its asymptotic
law in L appear not to have been considered before. The program of [9] moreover isolates two
missing spectral steps, the first of which (a simple, even ground state at a finite scale) is exactly a
two-sided eigenvalue-separation problem for the window form; Section 6 settles it at λ = e0.8 with
certified constants. The extremal-problem viewpoint on the explicit formula is developed in the
Fourier-optimization literature, e.g. Carneiro–Chandee–Milinovich [16]; the variational problem
(1) may be viewed as an L2 -normalized member of that family. The certified-computation
ingredients are standard: our PSD verification is the approximate-factorization-plus-residual
argument (cf. [18]), and the interval closure uses mpmath [23]. Odlyzko-style zero data enter only
as a proposal engine and as validation of the geometric certificates (Remark 11.3); they never
enter the unconditional proofs.

Remark 2.1 (Positivity vs. cancellation). The zeros side (7) is a sum of non-negative terms;
the geometric side (2) computes the same tiny number as a difference of large terms. When
Q(f ) ≈ 10−20 ∥f ∥2 and the individual blocks are of order ∥f ∥2 , double-precision evaluation
of the geometric side is meaningless. This elementary observation, quantified in Section 10,
once suggested that upper bounds had to be computed on the zeros side. Multiprecision interval
arithmetic removes the obstruction: Section 11 certifies geometric-side Rayleigh quotients down
to 10−283 , matching (and typically slightly sharpening) the zeros-side proposals. Lower bounds
continue to require one-sided, sign-definite estimates on the geometric side.

Remark 2.2 (Variational upper bounds). For any finite-dimensional subspace V of admissible
test functions, the minimal eigenvalue of the restriction of Q to V (with respect to the L2 Gram
matrix) is an upper bound for λ∗ (L). All numbers in Table 3 are of this form; no claim of
convergence to the true infimum is needed for the upper bounds to be rigorous.

6     Parity of the window ground state
Everything so far concerns even test functions. This section resolves the odd sector at support
1.6 and, with it, a spectral question recently isolated by Connes–Consani–Moscovici [9]. Their
construction realizes approximations to the zeta zeros from the ground state of the semilocal
Weil form at scale λ, and its theoretical foundation, the zero-localization theorem of Connes–van
Suijlekom [8], applies under the hypothesis that the bottom of the spectrum is a simple eigenvalue
whose eigenfunction is even (invariant under u 7→ u−1 ). They list this as the first missing step
of the program; an independent reproduction has verified it empirically at many scales, but
not rigorously at any. We certify it at λ = e0.8 , where the semilocal form has prime content

                                                  14
{2, 3, 4} and coincides, place by place, with the window form studied in this paper (up to the
normalization dictionary, whose line-by-line verification against [9] we have not carried out; the
statements below, being ordering statements, are invariant under positive rescaling of the form).

7    An exploratory computation at support 2.38, and a retraction
An earlier draft of this work claimed a certified positivity theorem at L = 1.19 (support 2.38). The

10     Three generations of float64 experiments and how they fail
We summarize the failure modes we encountered before moving to multiprecision; details and
scripts are in the repository (Section 17). All three generations discretize (1) by a basis (φk )
of functions supported in [−L, L] and solve the generalized eigenproblem M c = λGc with
Mjk = Q(φj , φk ) and G the L2 Gram matrix.
    Generation 1: hat functions, geometric side. Piecewise-linear hat functions (uniform
nodes, or arithmetic nodes at log pk ) assembled through the geometric side. Two artifacts
appeared. First, boundary half-hats make f (±L) ̸= 0, so |fˆ(r)|2 decays only like r−2 and the
truncated archimedean integral loses a positive mass, producing spurious negative eigenvalues
that are, at first sight, structurally indistinguishable from a counterexample to RH. Second, after
fixing the boundary condition, the sweep produced a convincing exponential law λ∗ (L) ∼ e−2.64L ,
which later turned out to be an artifact of the float64 noise floor: the apparent rate is the local
slope of the true (much steeper) curve at the point where it crosses 10−16 .
    Generation 2: cubic B-splines and Richardson extrapolation. Replacing hats by C 2
cubic splines improves the spectral decay of the basis from r−2 to r−4 per factor and demonstrably
lowers the variational bound at fixed dimension; a Richardson extrapolation in the knot spacing
then appeared to converge to a cleaner exponential law with rate 1.52. The extrapolation,
however, was converging to the noise floor of the ambient discretization, not to λ∗ (L): refining
the mesh at fixed L eventually increased the computed minimum, violating the variational
monotonicity that a Galerkin scheme must obey. We recommend this monotonicity check as a
cheap self-diagnostic for quadratic-form computations near the precision floor.
    Generation 3: zeros side in float64. Evaluating (7) term by term is cancellation-
free, and certified re-evaluation of the proposed minimizer gives genuine upper bounds (e.g.
λ∗ (0.7) ≤ 5.5×10−13 , a valid bound lying within 10% of the later multiprecision value 5.00×10−13 ).
But for L ≳ 0.9 the true values sink below 10−16 and float64 eigen-solvers return the noise floor:
certified bounds stall near 10−16 for all L ∈ [1.0, 2.2]. Figure 1 displays all three generations
against the multiprecision curve.

14.2    The measured margin (Remark 1.5)
The margin that any certificate must resolve is the floor itself, and its decay is now a measured
law rather than folklore: by Table 3 and (5), the window floor collapses at the Landau–Widom
rate exp(−2π 2 N (T ∗ )/ ln N (T ∗ )) with T ∗ = 2πe2L , falling through 17 orders of magnitude at
L = 0.8, 48 at L = 1.2 and 283 at L = 2. The reduced form R tracks the window floor
closely (Section 8). The gap between λmin (R) and λ∗ is governed by the artificial well set
WT ♯ = {t ≤ T ♯ : ΨL (t) < β ∗ } created by the splitting: band-limited functions can concentrate
                                       2L|W |
on WT ♯ , and each Shannon mode 2πT ♯ of the well set costs a definite fraction of a decade of
floor. At L = 0.8 we measure a Shannon load ∼ 5 against a gap of one decade, so about 0.2
decade per mode; the load is ∼ 20–25 in the exploratory L = 1.19 run of Section 7. The logical
status here is that the law (5) is established for certified upper bounds only. Converting it into a
proved lower bound on the cost of any positivity certificate would require the sampling theory of
low zeta zeros, the window floor being twice the squared smallest singular value of a low-zero
sampling operator, and that remains open.

14.3    Interpretation (Remark 1.6)
The two exponential scales meet head on. A certificate of the one-stroke type must reach the
                                             L
comb-alignment threshold T1 = 2πe(4+o(1))e , because at t slightly beyond T1 the symbol’s lower
envelope is barely positive and the certificate must verify that band-limited functions cannot
exploit near-alignments of (t log p)p . Below T1 such alignments do null the symbol; above T1
they cannot. But proving a sharper threshold than T1 , that is, proving quantitatively that
the comb phases avoid simultaneous alignment, is a statement about linear forms in {log p},
and that is the same species of information as zero-density estimates encode. Meanwhile the
quantity to be certified collapses at the Landau–Widom rate in the resolution height T ∗ = 2πe2L .
Certifying positivity past support ≈ 3.2 (T1 ≈ 107 ) by any pointwise-envelope method is therefore
computationally void. In this sense the positivity route, continued past the wall, runs back into
the arithmetic of the zeros it was trying to circumvent.


15     Failed routes (for the record)
Three natural alternatives to Theorem 1.1 were implemented and abandoned; we record them
because their failure modes motivate the frequency-space formulation. (They are the geometric-
side counterparts of the float64 pathologies of Section 10.) (1) Galerkin solve-lemmas (certify
Q ⪰ 0 on a window, lift by solving Gx = φ): the inverses are rough in L2 [−L, L], Galerkin
residuals decay only polynomially, and the required 10−80 tolerances are unreachable. (2) Symbol
capping (replace ΨL beyond Tδ by a constant minorant): provably fatal, since once the window
resolves the cap the form becomes indefinite (measured λmin ≈ −2.7). Capping the symbol
amounts to admitting fake zeros. (3) Order-space block certificates (exact window block + tail


                                                31
bounds + Schur coupling): the prime terms are shift operators, so any hard cut in Legendre-order
space leaves O(1) coupling across the interface; the collective edge directions lose ≈ −2.4 against
a supply that grows only logarithmically. (4) Per-prime envelope sharpening: grouping the
comb by primes sharpens its minimum but cannot lower its maximum, which equals AL exactly
(Lemma 3.2). An earlier draft used the sharpened constant in the envelope, which is the wrong
direction, and the resulting support-2.38 claim is retracted in Section 7. The error is an easy one
to make, since the well depths of ΨL , governed by Aeff , and its high-frequency floor, governed
by AL , are two different extremes of the same comb. The frequency split, by contrast, has no
interface and only super-exponentially small coupling. It appears to be the right formulation,
and we suspect, without proving, that it is essentially unique in this respect.


16     Discussion
Interpretation. The profile λ∗ (L) quantifies the difficulty of approaching RH through compactly
supported test functions, and this paper determines it from both sides. An unconditional
certificate pushes the positive-side frontier from log 2 to 1.6, while certified variational bounds
show that the margin does not merely shrink exponentially but collapses at the Landau–Widom
rate exp(−2π 2 N/ ln N ) in the number N of zeros resolved by the window. Any finite-dimensional
positivity argument that operates within a window [−L, L], in particular any truncation in the
Connes–Consani prolate program [6], has to certify a quantity this small. Conversely, a spectral
construction whose positivity margin decayed more slowly than (5) on windows would contradict
our variational upper bounds.

spectral theory of time–band limiting in a quantitative way that, to our knowledge, is new. It
is tempting to read (5) as saying that near the resolution height the zeros of ζ behave, as far
as band-limited suppression is concerned, like a generic saturated sampling set, the arithmetic
entering only through the density ν and hence through N (T ∗ ), with the constant universal.
    The two heights. The synthesis of the two halves isolates a structural asymmetry of the
Weil form on windows. Defeating positivity, that is, finding small Rayleigh quotients, is governed
by the resolution height T ∗ = 2πe2L , singly exponential, because the minimizer only needs to
interpolate the zeros it can resolve. Certifying positivity through the geometric side is governed

by the alignment threshold T1 = 2πe(4+o(1))e , doubly exponential, because the certificate must

and proving that the primes cannot conspire. Closing it is an arithmetic problem, concerning
simultaneous Diophantine properties of {log p}, and not a computational one.

17     Reproducibility
Both pipelines run on a single machine in Python 3 with mpmath [23] (with the gmpy2 backend
for the largest runs). Lower-bound certification is ≈ 40 CPU-hours in total, plus about two
CPU-hours for the parity-sector runs of Section 6. On the upper-bound side, the geometric
certificate at L ≤ 1.4 finishes in under two minutes per point; the L = 2.0 run (365-digit geometric
arithmetic, 549 odd modes) is the bottleneck.
    For the certified claims the accompanying files are part of the proof rather than a courtesy.
Source for both pipelines, software versions, zero data, certified matrices and Cholesky factors, run
logs, error budgets and SHA-256 hashes are provided as supplementary material, together with
an independent verifier (verify certificate.py, depending on mpmath alone) that re-factorises
an archived matrix, recomputes the Cholesky residual in doubled precision, and re-derives the
quadrature budget cerr and the tail constants εD , εB of (13). On the archived M200 it returns
Q(f ) ≥ 9.0 × 10−18 ∥f ∥22 (the 8.9 × 10−18 of Theorem 1.2 rounds the budgets down).
