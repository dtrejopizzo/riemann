Theorem 1.1 should be compared with (1.1): it replaces the term N 4/5 with N 19/24
(note that 4/5 = 19/24 + 1/120). The most important range for our result is when
q ≈ N 1/2 , which happens for ‘generic’ α. For q smaller than N 1/2 , Kumchev [5]
obtained a bound with B 1/2 N 11/20 in place of N 4/5 . This is stronger than the
bound of Theorem 1.1 whenever B ≤ N 1/2−δ with δ > 1/60 (the terms B 1/2 N 11/20
and N 19/24 cross at B = N 29/60 ), but such α form a set of measure shrinking to
zero as N → ∞. Moreover, there is a large literature obtaining bounds of the form
                                          N

                                         B
for B ≤ N δ with small δ > 0.
Morally one can think of the N/B 1/2 term as coming from possible exceptional
zeros with ℜ(s) close to 1; this term can be improved for all q outside of a thin
exceptional set. Under GRH we have the stronger estimate

Our proof combines the Heath-Brown identity with mean value theorems and fourth
moment estimates for Dirichlet polynomials and L-functions, together with recent
large values estimates for Dirichlet polynomials in the q-aspect [1], extending work
of Guth and the first author [2]. As sketched in Section 2, inserting these large
values estimates into the standard zero density framework already improves the
exponent 4/5 to 67/84; the stronger exponent 19/24 of Theorem 1.1 comes from
working with the Dirichlet polynomials directly, exploiting the fact that the critical
factorizations behind the zero density argument cannot actually occur.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we outline the argument. In Section 3
we prove our main technical result, Proposition 3.11, which bounds the exponential
sum over a dyadic range n ∼ x when B ∈ [x4/9 , x1/2 ]. In Section 4 we deduce
Theorem 1.1 from Proposition 3.11 via a dyadic decomposition, using an estimate
of Kumchev [5] for the ranges where B is small.
