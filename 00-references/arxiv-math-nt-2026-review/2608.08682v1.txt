                                          A real polynomial is called hyperbolic if all its zeros are real. Pólya’s Jensen criterion [7]
                                          says that the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the hyperbolicity of J d,n for all d, n.
                                             Our main result is the following unconditional region in the (d, n)-plane.
                                          Theorem 1.1 (Main theorem). There is an absolute constant K > 0 such that, for all
                                          integers d ≥ 1 and n ≥ 0 satisfying

                                                                                  n3 log2 (n + 2) ≥ Kd5 ,

                                          the Jensen polynomial J d,n has d distinct negative real zeros.

                                          d ≤ c n3/5 log2/5 (n + 2)

with a suitable absolute c > 0.
    There is also a consequence for the derivative-aspect random-matrix picture. Griffin, Ono,
Rolen, and Zagier proved that, for each fixed d, a centered and scaled Jensen polynomial
tends to a Hermite polynomial as n → ∞ [2, Theorem 3]. The semicircle law for Hermite
zeros then gives a global GUE statement after taking d → ∞ in a second limit. The uniform
comparison used here allows the two parameters to tend to infinity together.

    Theorem 1.2 concerns the global empirical measure. It does not assert local sine-kernel

    The proof separates the construction of a real-rooted comparison polynomial from the
stability of its zeros under the remaining coefficient perturbation. Section 2 introduces the
quotient coordinates that organize the construction, displays the three nested comparison
families, and proves the abstract stability principle used at the end. The subsequent sections
verify its hypotheses. The analytic input is the complex form of the saddle estimate in
[3, Section 3]; the algebraic input consists of Jacobi root estimates and the real-root and
logarithmic-mesh properties of finite-free multiplicative convolution [4, Propositions 2.7 and
2.17]. After the hyperbolicity proof is complete, Section 11 identifies the semicircle law for
the comparison model and transfers it to the Jensen zeros.

12      Discussion
The published theorem of Griffin, Ono, Rolen, Thorner, Tripp, and Wagner gives hyperbol-
icity for n ≥ ced ; arXiv v3 records the sharper threshold n ≥ ced/2 [3, Theorem 1.1]. Their
comparison is Hermite. Here the comparison stays in the positive-root class: the positive
error at R3 selects the first Jacobi deformation, and the negative error at R4 is absorbed by
the second finite-free factor.
     Theorem 1.2 improves the order of limits in the global    p derivative-aspect GUE statement
of [2]. In their   notation   δ(n) ∼ (2n)−1/2 , so the factor    n/d in Theorem 1.2 is the leading
             √
form of 1/( 2d δ(n)); centering at −1/R1 uses the exact first coefficient ratio. Their theorem
first fixes d and lets n → ∞, obtaining a Hermite polynomial, after which the semicircle law
is recovered as d → ∞. Here the two limits are simultaneous throughout the polynomial
wedge. No claim is made about local GUE correlations.
     After R1 , . . . , R4 have been matched, the logarithmic multiplier vanishes at five consecu-
tive indices and has fifth derivative O(1/(n4 log    √ n)). The root localization and perturbed
Jacobi equation give the derivative scale rF ≍ nd. Consequently the remaining error is
                                        √      !                    !
                                       ( nd)5              d5/2
                                   O              =O                  ,
                                      n4 log n           n3/2 log n

which yields the stated wedge. The constants in the Jacobi localization can be taken
explicitly; the proof above uses CJ = 8, and the localization itself requires only A ≥ B + d
rather than A ≥ 64(B + d). The later differential recurrence is where the modest condition
A ≥ 8B is used. No attempt is made to optimize the eventual absolute constant.
    This result controls an asymptotic region and does not provide a converse route from
partial Jensen hyperbolicity to the Riemann hypothesis; see Farmer [1]. O’Sullivan’s modified
Pólya–Jensen criterion gives a complementary use of Hermite combinations [6].
