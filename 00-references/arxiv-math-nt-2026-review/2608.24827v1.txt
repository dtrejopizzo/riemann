                                                                                             Abstract
                                                    Weil’s criterion equates the Riemann Hypothesis with the positivity of an explicit quadratic
                                                form Q(f ). For test functions supported in a window [−L, L] we study the profile λ∗ (L) =
                                                inf Q(f )/∥f ∥22 from both sides. A one-stroke reduction converts window positivity into positive
                                                semidefiniteness of a finite matrix; executing it at L = 0.8 yields the unconditional certificate
                                                Q(f ) ≥ 8.9 × 10−18 ∥f ∥22 for all (complex) f of support 1.6, extending the classical range
                                                log 2 of Yoshida and Connes–Consani by a factor 2.3. The same computation shows that
                                                the window ground state is simple and even. On the other side, interval-arithmetic Rayleigh
                                                quotients, evaluated on the geometric side of the explicit formula with no appeal to zeros
                                                or to RH, give unconditional upper bounds down to 3.2 × 10−283 at L = 2. They follow
                                                − ln λ∗ (L) ≃ 2π 2 N (T ∗ )/ ln N (T ∗ ) with T ∗ = 2πe2L , the Landau–Widom plunge rate for time–
                                                band limiting. At L = 0.8 the two halves enclose the profile, 8.9 × 10−18 ≤ λ∗ (0.8) ≤ 2.27 × 10−17 .
                                                Any pointwise-envelope certificate must resolve frequencies up to T1 = 2πeAL with AL ∼ 4eL , a
                                                doubly exponential barrier. Under RH we prove λ∗ (L) ≤ exp(−LeL ) for all large L.

Theorem 3 (Barrier). Any application of Theorem 7 requires T ♯ > T1 (L) = 2πeAL , and AL =
(4 + o(1))eL as L → ∞ by the prime number theorem. The matrix size N ≍ LT1 therefore grows
doubly exponentially in L. Moreover supt of the prime comb equals AL exactly, so within the class
of certificates that bound the comb pointwise the threshold T1 cannot be lowered.

    To improve on pointwise envelopes one must prove that the phases (t log p)p cannot align—an
arithmetic statement about {log p}, of the same nature as the information carried by the zeros
themselves.

Related work. The tradition of attacking RH-adjacent quantities numerically is represented by
the de Bruijn–Newman constant [14]. Weil positivity for restricted classes of test functions was
established by Connes–Consani using prolate spheroidal wave functions [6]; Theorem 1 extends
the range of unconditional positivity itself. Finite-rank truncations of the Weil form have recently
become objects of study [8, 9, 10, 11]. Those works study a fixed truncation family; the window-
variational infimum (1), its unconditional certification, and its asymptotic law in L appear not to
have been considered before.

5    The Landau–Widom law
A function supported in [−L, L] has Fourier transform of exponential type L; by Jensen, its real
                                                                       1
zeros have density at most L/π. The zeros of ζ have density ν(t) = 2π    ln(t/2π), which reaches the
                         ∗         2L           ∗
budget L/π exactly at T (L) = 2πe . Below T a test function can in principle interpolate every γj ;
above T ∗ the zeros outnumber the available oscillations. Killing N = N (T ∗ ) zeros with an exhausted
budget is the Landau–Widom regime [13]: eigenvalues of a time–band limiting operator plunge at
exp(−π 2 j/ ln K) past the Shannon number K. Modelling the suppression as an excursion of order
N beyond Shannon suggests (4) rather than the naive “one nat per zero” guess − ln λ∗ ≍ N (T ∗ ).

7    The barrier, and a closing remark
Theorem 3 is immediate from Theorem 7 and Lemma 6: β ∗ > 0 forces T ♯ > T1 = 2πeAL , the PNT
gives AL ∼ 4eL , and no pointwise bound on the comb can do better. At L = 1.6 one already has
T1 ≈ 107 ; past support ≈ 3.2 a pointwise-envelope certificate is computationally void.
    Two heights coexist. Defeating positivity—finding small Rayleigh quotients—is governed
by the resolution height T ∗ = 2πe2L , singly exponential, because the minimizer only needs to
interpolate the zeros it can resolve. Certifying positivity through the geometric side is governed
by T1 = 2π exp (4 + o(1))eL , doubly exponential, because the certificate must exclude worst-case
                                                                       L
phase alignment of the prime comb. The gap between e2L and e4e is the gap between knowing
where the zeros are and proving that the primes cannot conspire.
    Certificate, law and barrier are what we take this program to deliver: the frontier of unconditional
positivity moves from log 2 to 1.6, the profile beyond it is measured out to L = 2, and the wall
behind both is mapped rather than left implicit.
