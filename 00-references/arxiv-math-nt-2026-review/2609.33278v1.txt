                                                                                 1. Introduction
                                              Prime characterizations built from arithmetic functions are most useful when
                                           the functions involved share a structural origin. The identity studied here arises
                                           from two standard transforms of the completely multiplicative function Ik (n) = nk .
                                           Under Dirichlet convolution,
                                                                           σk = 1 ∗ I k ,        Jk = µ ∗ Ik ,                           (1)
                                           where 1(n) = 1 and µ is the Möbius function. Thus σk is the ordinary divisor

   The exceptional value 18 is not an isolated numerical accident. It is the only
composite that survives a divisibility condition by 3, and that condition becomes
impossible as soon as k ≥ 2. Before proving this classification, we place it among
the classical prime criteria and the literature on inverse arithmetic functions.

                       2. Related work and contribution
   Several classical arithmetic functions characterize primes by their values at a
single integer. For n > 1, one has
                           φ(n) = n − 1            ⇐⇒         n is prime,                         (5)
while Lehmer’s celebrated problem asks whether the stronger divisibility φ(n) | n − 1
can hold for a composite n [1]. The Jordan analogue is Jk (n) = nk − 1 at primes.
Subbarao and Siva Rama Prasad studied the corresponding divisibility condition
Jk (n) | nk − 1 and related analogues of Lehmer’s totient problem [2]. Likewise,
σk (n) = nk + 1 characterizes primes because a composite integer has a proper divisor
other than 1.
   These familiar criteria use the values of σk or Jk themselves. The present work
instead uses the inverse of σk in the ring of arithmetic functions. Cashwell and
Everett developed the ring-theoretic setting for arithmetic functions under Dirichlet
convolution [3]; McCarthy gives a systematic account of multiplicative functions and
convolution [4]. General explicit expressions for Dirichlet inverses were studied by
Haukkanen [5]. Brown gave an expository treatment of inverse arithmetic functions
and, for the ordinary divisor sum, recorded
         (−1)                           (−1)                        (−1)
       σ1       (p) = −(p + 1),        σ1        (p2 ) = p,       σ1       (pa ) = 0    (a ≥ 3)   (6)
[6]; the same sequence is catalogued as OEIS A046692 [7]. The corresponding
generalized formulas also occur in analytic work on L-functions [8, eqs. (4.7)–(4.8)],
and follow immediately from σk = 1 ∗ Ik .
   To the author’s knowledge, the literature on inverse arithmetic functions cited
above does not combine these formulas with µ and Jk to classify the zeros of (3).
The novelty claimed here is limited to this mixed zero-set classification and its
uniform extension in k; none of the component identities is new. The global converse
shows that the prime-level cancellation forced by the convolution identities has no
composite solutions for k ≥ 2, while at k = 1 it has exactly one, namely 18. This
simultaneously explains the constant 3, identifies the exceptional mechanism, and
shows why that mechanism disappears throughout the higher Jordan family.
   The result is structural rather than algorithmic. Direct evaluation of the multi-
plicative formulas generally presupposes a prime factorization, so the criterion is
not proposed as a faster primality test. Its significance is instead that it links three
operations on the same basic function Ik : divisor summation, Möbius transforma-
tion, and inversion in the Dirichlet ring. Theorem 1 supplies the nontrivial global
classification for Ik (n) = nk . We first derive the three functions from their Lambert-
series identities and then organize their local values according to the three possible
prime-exponent patterns. After proving the zero-set classification, we establish the
             PRIME-DETECTING IDENTITIES FROM DIRICHLET INVERSION                        3


companion criterion and the exact intermediate-divisor relation between the two
results.
