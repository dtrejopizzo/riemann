1.1. On the sixth and twelfth moments. The estimate M12 (T ) ≪ε T 2+ε was proved by Heath-
Brown via short second moments of zeta. The desire to improve on this bound has inspired many
developments in analytic number theory, and led to several different proofs of the same result. For
example, Iwaniec [Iwa80] deduced it from bounds for the fourth moment of zeta in short intervals,
obtained via spectral theory of automorphic forms. Jutila [Jut91] gave a different proof of Iwaniec’s
result, bypassing the appeal to spectral theory. A textbook proof is given in [Ivi03, Chapter 8].
   Both Iwaniec’s and Jutila’s methods give the same bounds for a fixed GL2 L-function in place
of ζ 2 . It is worth mentioning that significantly less is known for GL3 L-functions. The best known
bound for the second moment in the t-aspect (which should be compared to M6 (T )) is T 4/3+ε , due
to Dasgupta, Leung, and Young [DLY24]. Interestingly, the bound T 5/4+ε was recently obtained
by Aggarwal, Leung, and Munshi [ALM26] for a short second moment of length T 2/3 .
   As noted earlier, the sixth moment bound of Oε (T 5/4+ε ) follows by interpolating the fourth and
twelfth moment bounds. Expanding out with the approximate functional equation, this bound
roughly amounts to showing an asymptotic with remainder Oε (X 7/6+ε ) for a smoothed version of

One may interpret this 7/6 as square-root cancellation in the h-sum, over the n-sums minus their
expected main terms, and Theorem 1.1 should improve the exponent 7/6.
  Finally, let us mention that sufficiently large improvements on the sixth moment bound imply
improved zero density estimates for zeta; Iwaniec [Iwa24] noted that a bound M6 (T ) ≪ T 6/5−δ
implies better zero density estimates at σ = 3/4 than Huxley’s [Hux72] classical bound. Such an
improvement was recently obtained via different methods by Guth and Maynard [GM26].
