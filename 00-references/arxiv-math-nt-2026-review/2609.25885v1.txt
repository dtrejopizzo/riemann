                                                THE WEIGHTED LARGE SIEVE THROUGH PARSEVAL


                                                                                 OLIVIER RAMARÉ
arXiv:2609.25885v1 [math.NT] 22 Sep 2026




                                                    Abstract. We modify the approach to the arithmetical form of the large
                                                    sieve by relying on the Parseval identity rather than on an approximate Bessel
                                                    inequality and as a consequence, improve on the weighted large sieve inequality.
                                                    We compute in passing the L2 -norm of the Beurling-Selberg majorant of the
                                                    characteristic function of an interval.


                                                                       1. Introduction and results
                                              Since their discovery by V. Brun in [2, 3], sieve methods have proved very effi-
                                           cient to bound above quantities defined by the exclusion of some or several residue
                                           classes. Sieve approaches can be classified in three main categories: the combina-
                                           torial sieves, the Selberg (or Λ2 ) sieve and the large sieves. Gallagher’s larger sieve
                                           introduced in [5] remains an isolated tool, though a powerful one where it applies.
                                           We are concerned in this paper with the large sieve that arose from the work of
                                           Yu. V. Linnik in [11]; we refer the readers to the monographs [12] by H. L. Mont-
                                           gomery and [20] for more historical details. As explained by H. L. Montgomery
                                           in [13], the denomination large sieve or large sieve inequality usually refers to an
                                           inequality on a finite set of points. We proposed in [24] a different approach by
                                           relying on the Parseval identity on R{Z and recovered in this way a slightly weaker
                                           form of what is known as the arithmetical form of the large sieve or Montgomery’s
                                           sieve; we also proved that the trigonometric polynomial of a sifted sequence, if
                                           large, has to have a sharp peak around the origin. See Lemma 9.1 below.
                                              In the present paper, we use the Parseval identity in a different manner and
                                           obtain another arithmetical form of the weighted large sieve inequality, which, in
                                           some cases, is stronger than the earlier one contained in [14] by H. Montgomery &
                                           R. C. Vaughan. To do so, and as a sidedish, we compute in Theorem 4.1 the L2 -
                                           norm of the Beurling-Selberg majorant of the characteristic function of an?    interval.
                                           In many emblematic problems, this already enables us to sieve up to Q “ N . We
                                           next incorporate the sharp-peak property in our method, obtaining a quantitative
                                           improvement of our result.

On the method. As explained in detail in [24], the arithmetical usage of the large
sieve relies on a local (i.e. modulo q) lower bound coupled to a global upper bound.
We equally rely on an equality for the lower bound, but one that is stronger than the
usual L2 lower bound, though still very close to it. As for the upper bound, and as
in [24], we rely on the Farey dissection of the unit interval, as in the circle method.
Coupled with a result (Theorem 4.1) on a smoothing function, Theorem 1.3 (and
consequently Corollary 1.2) will follow.
   This first proof is rather self-contained, and exhibits the basic mechanism. In a
second step, and on using stronger regularity assumptions on our sieving conditions,
we improve on this first result by using the L2 -bound proved in the first paper [24]
of this series.
