# An sl₂ triple on the archimedean continuum and arithmetic progressions among the zeros of ζ: remarks on a hard-Lefschetz analogy

In Weil's proof of RH for curves over finite fields the positivity comes from a Hodge index theorem supplied by an ample class. This paper looks at what the archimedean place of ζ offers in that direction: the sign of the archimedean density and of the archimedean term of the Weil form on primitive tests, an sl₂ triple on L²(ℝ), and what an sl₂ structure graded by the ordinates of the zeros would force.

## Results
- Prop. 3.1 (proved): Ψ(r) = Re ψ(1/4 + ir/2) − log π is strictly increasing on r > 0, with a unique positive zero r₀ = 6.2898… (value computed numerically), below the first ordinate 14.13…
- Prop. 4.1 (proved, with numerical values): the archimedean term on the primitive Gaussians (r² + 1/4)e^{−r²/2σ²} tends to Ψ(0) < 0 (normalized) as σ → 0 and to +∞ as σ → ∞; numerically the sign changes between σ = 4 and σ = 5.
- Prop. 5.1 (proved): an sl₂-string of length ≥ 3 with grading proportional to multiplication by the ordinates forces a three-term arithmetic progression among the ordinates, excluded under LI; strings of length 2 are not excluded.
- Prop. 5.2 and 7.1 (proved): the standard sl₂ triple built from translation and multiplication on L²(ℝ) satisfies the stated relations on the Schwartz space and has no lowest-weight vector in L².
- Remark 6.2 (numerical): on the primitive Gaussians the explicit formula holds to 30-digit precision, with the archimedean and prime terms large, negative and nearly equal.

## Known and new
Weil's explicit formula, the Weyl commutation relation, the digamma facts and Weil's criterion are classical and cited. The observations on Ψ and on primitive Gaussians, the string statement under LI, and the numerical table are the content of this paper. No claim about RH is made.
