\title[Lefschetz-type structures and the zeros of zeta]{An $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple on the archimedean
continuum and arithmetic progressions among the zeros of $\zeta$: remarks on a
hard-Lefschetz analogy}
\author{David Alejandro Trejo Pizzo}
\thanks{Independent Researcher. \texttt{dtrejopizzo@gmail.com}}


\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
In Weil's proof of the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields the
positivity comes from the Hodge index theorem, supplied by an ample class
(equivalently a hard-Lefschetz $\sltwo$-action). We examine what the archimedean
place of $\zeta$ offers in this direction. We prove that the archimedean density
$\Psi(r)=\Real\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})-\log\pi$ is strictly increasing on
$r>0$ with a unique positive zero $r_0=6.2898\ldots$, below the first ordinate of
a zero. We prove that the archimedean term $\Ainf$ of the Weil form is negative
on the Gaussian tests $\widehat f_\sigma(r)=(r^2+\frac14)e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}$ (which
annihilate the pole term) for small $\sigma$ and positive for large $\sigma$;
numerically the sign changes between $\sigma=4$ and $\sigma=5$. We record the
standard $\sltwo$ triple built from translation and multiplication on
$L^2(\mathbb R)$, with grading proportional to the multiplication operator, and
prove that it has no lowest-weight vector in $L^2$. On the zero side we prove
that an $\sltwo$-string of length at least three, with grading proportional to
the operator of multiplication by the ordinates, would produce a three-term
arithmetic progression among the ordinates, which the linear independence
hypothesis (LI) excludes. A numerical check of the explicit formula on the
Gaussian tests illustrates the near cancellation of the archimedean and prime
terms. No claim about RH is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Weil's proof of $\RH$ for a curve $C/\mathbb F_q$~\cite{Weil48,bgWeil49} has two
parts: an intersection pairing on the surface $C\times C$, and the Hodge index
theorem, which makes the pairing negative definite on the primitive part. The
second part is supplied by an \emph{ample class} on the surface; in higher
dimension the corresponding structure is a hard-Lefschetz $\sltwo$-action whose
positivity is the Hodge--Riemann bilinear relation (the hard Lefschetz theorem
in positive characteristic is proved in~\cite{bgDeligne}). The Frobenius acts on $H^1$, its eigenvalues give the
zeros, and the ample class is independent geometric input.

Earlier papers of the series reformulate the zero side of the Weil form in a
Kre\u\i n space~\cite{RP8}, study its negative index~\cite{RP11}, and bound windowed zero sums of the
form~\cite{RP16}; Hodge-index reformulations of $\RH$ as positivity statements
are discussed in~\cite{RP14}. By Weil's criterion~\cite{Weil52} RH is equivalent
to nonnegativity of this form. In analogy with the function-field case, and with
the spectral interpretations of~\cite{bgConnes99,bgDeninger}, one may ask for an
$\sltwo$ action supplying that positivity.

This paper collects some observations on that question. Section~\ref{sec:filter}
explains, heuristically, why operators that are functions of the ordinates
cannot supply new positivity. Sections~\ref{sec:psi}--\ref{sec:indef} study the
archimedean density and the sign of the archimedean term on primitive Gaussian
tests. Section~\ref{sec:sl2} records the standard $\sltwo$ triple built from
translation and multiplication on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ and proves that, under LI (the
hypothesis that the positive ordinates of the zeros are $\mathbb Q$-linearly
independent), no $\sltwo$-string of length at least three with grading
proportional to the ordinates exists on the zero side.
Section~\ref{sec:transport} checks the explicit formula numerically on the
Gaussian tests, and Section~\ref{sec:dichotomy} proves that the continuum triple
has no lowest-weight vector. Nothing here proves or assumes $\RH$; the
computations are reproducible from Appendix~\ref{app:code}.

\section{Functions of the ordinates}\label{sec:filter}
Let $\Gamma=\{\gamma\in\mathbb R:\zeta(\tfrac12+i\gamma)=0\}$ be the set of
ordinates of the zeros on the critical line, let $\ell^2(\Gamma)$ have
orthonormal basis $(e_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}$ indexed by the distinct
ordinates, and let $\T$ be the self-adjoint operator $\T e_\gamma=\gamma e_\gamma$
(on its maximal domain). Then $\T$ has simple pure point spectrum, its eigenvalues
are exactly the elements of $\Gamma$, and the operators commuting with $\T$ are
the bounded functions $f(\T)$; let $W^*(\T)$ denote the von Neumann algebra they
form.

\begin{remark}[Heuristic]\label{prop:filter}
Suppose one tries to produce a positive form $\omega(x,y)=Q(Lx,y)$ from the Weil
form $Q$ and an operator $L$. If $L=f(\T)\in W^*(\T)$, then $L$ is diagonal in
the basis $(e_\gamma)$, and any such construction only reweights the zero side
eigenvector by eigenvector; asserting its positivity is then a reformulation of
the Weil positivity rather than a source of it. This suggests that a candidate
playing the role of an ample class should not lie in $W^*(\T)$. We do not make
this into a theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{The archimedean density}\label{sec:psi}
The archimedean place contributes to the Weil quadratic form, for a test function
$f$ with Fourier transform $\widehat f$, the term
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ainf}
\Ainf(f,f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\widehat f(r)|^2\,\Psi(r)\,dr,
\qquad
\Psi(r)=\Real\psi\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2}\right)-\log\pi,
\end{equation}
where $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$ and $\Psi$ is the density of the archimedean factor
$\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$ in the explicit
formula~\cite{Weil52},~\cite[Thm.~5.12]{bgIK}.

\begin{proposition}[Structure of $\Psi$]\label{prop:psi}
$\Psi$ is even and real-analytic, and:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(i)] $\Psi'(r)=\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(\tfrac14+n)\,r/2}
{\bigl((\tfrac14+n)^2+(r/2)^2\bigr)^2}>0$ for $r>0$ (strictly increasing);
\item[\rm(ii)] $\Psi$ has a unique positive zero $r_0=6.2898359888\ldots$, and
global minimum $\Psi(0)=\psi(\tfrac14)-\log\pi=-\gamma-3\log2-\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\log\pi
=-5.37218\ldots$;
\item[\rm(iii)] $\Psi(r)=\log\dfrac{|r|}{2\pi}+O(r^{-2})$ as $|r|\to\infty$, so
$\tfrac1{2\pi}\Psi(r)$ agrees to leading order with the derivative
$\frac1{2\pi}\log\frac{r}{2\pi}$ of the main term of the Riemann--von Mangoldt
formula~\cite[\S9.4]{bgTitchmarsh}.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $\Psi(r)>0\iff|r|>r_0$, and since $r_0<14.1347\ldots$, the
smallest positive ordinate of a nontrivial zero, $\Psi>0$ at every ordinate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Evenness and analyticity are clear. For (i), differentiate \eqref{eq:Ainf}:
$\Psi'(r)=\Real\bigl[\tfrac{i}{2}\psi'(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})\bigr]
=-\tfrac12\Im\psi'(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})$, and
$\psi'(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}(z+n)^{-2}$ with $\Im(a+ib)^{-2}=-2ab/(a^2+b^2)^2$ gives
$\Im\psi'(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})=-\sum_n\frac{2(\tfrac14+n)(r/2)}
{((\tfrac14+n)^2+(r/2)^2)^2}$; multiplying by $-\tfrac12$ gives the stated
series, which is positive for $r>0$. Hence $\Psi$ increases from $\Psi(0)<0$ to
$+\infty$, crossing zero once. The value $\psi(\frac14)=-\gamma-\frac\pi2-3\log2$
is classical, and (iii) follows from Stirling's expansion
$\psi(z)=\log z-\tfrac1{2z}+O(|z|^{-2})$ in $\Real z>0$: for
$z=\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2$ one has $\Real\log z=\log\tfrac{|r|}2+O(r^{-2})$ and
$\Real\tfrac1{2z}=O(r^{-2})$, so
$\Real\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})=\log\tfrac{|r|}{2}+O(r^{-2})$. The numerical
value of $r_0$ and the inequality $r_0<\gamma_1$ are checked to $30$ digits in
Appendix~\ref{app:code}.
\end{proof}

So the archimedean density is positive on the range $|r|\ge\gamma_1$ of the
ordinates and negative only on the band $|r|<r_0$.

\section{Sign of the archimedean term on primitive tests}\label{sec:indef}
Call a test $f$ \emph{primitive} if $\widehat f(\pm\tfrac i2)=0$, so that the
pole term of the explicit formula vanishes on it. We write
$\|f\|^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int|\widehat f|^2\,dr$.

\begin{proposition}[$\Ainf$ is indefinite on primitives]\label{prop:indef}
Let $\widehat f_\sigma(r)=(r^2+\tfrac14)e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}$. Each $f_\sigma$ is
primitive, and
\[
\lim_{\sigma\to0}\frac{\Ainf(f_\sigma,f_\sigma)}{\|f_\sigma\|^2}=\Psi(0)<0,
\qquad
\lim_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{\Ainf(f_\sigma,f_\sigma)}{\|f_\sigma\|^2}=+\infty .
\]
Hence $\Ainf$ is indefinite on primitive tests. Numerically the ratio is
$-1.82$ at $\sigma=1$, $-0.110$ at $\sigma=4$, $+0.117$ at $\sigma=5$ and
$+0.998$ at $\sigma=12$, so the sign changes between $\sigma=4$ and $\sigma=5$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$(\pm i/2)^2+\tfrac14=0$, so $f_\sigma$ is primitive. Substituting $r=\sigma t$,
\[
\frac{\Ainf(f_\sigma,f_\sigma)}{\|f_\sigma\|^2}
=\frac{\int(\sigma^2t^2+\frac14)^2e^{-t^2}\,\Psi(\sigma t)\,dt}
{\int(\sigma^2t^2+\frac14)^2e^{-t^2}\,dt}.
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:psi}, $|\Psi(r)|\le C(1+\log(1+|r|))$, so for
$\sigma\le1$ the numerator integrand is dominated by
$C(1+t^2)^2e^{-t^2}(1+\log(1+|t|))$; dominated convergence in numerator and denominator gives the limit $\Psi(0)$. For the second
limit, the probability measures
$\mu_\sigma\propto(\sigma^2t^2+\frac14)^2e^{-t^2}dt$ converge to the probability measure proportional to $t^4e^{-t^2}dt$, so for
fixed $\delta>0$ the mass $\epsilon_\sigma=\mu_\sigma(|t|\le\delta)$ tends to a
limit $\epsilon(\delta)<1$, while $\Psi\ge\Psi(0)$ everywhere and
$\Psi(\sigma t)\ge\log(\sigma\delta/2\pi)-C'$ for $|t|\ge\delta$; hence the
ratio is at least
\[(1-\epsilon_\sigma)\bigl(\log(\sigma\delta/2\pi)-C'\bigr)
+\epsilon_\sigma\Psi(0)\to+\infty,\]
since $1-\epsilon_\sigma\to1-\epsilon(\delta)>0$ and $\epsilon_\sigma\Psi(0)$ is
bounded (here $\sigma\delta\ge1$, and
$C'$ is such that $\Psi(r)\ge\log\frac{|r|}{2\pi}-C'$ for $|r|\ge1$). The numerical values are computed in
Appendix~\ref{app:code}.
\end{proof}

The signed form factors through a positive form and a sign: if $\M$ denotes
multiplication by $r$ on $L^2(\mathbb R,dr)$, then on the Fourier side
$\Ainf=g_\infty(S\,\cdot,\cdot)$ with $g_\infty=\frac1{2\pi}|\Psi|(\M)\ge0$ and
$S=\sgn(\M^2-r_0^2)$. This has the shape of a Hodge--Riemann form (a positive
form times a sign attached to a grading); the next section asks whether such a
grading can come from an $\sltwo$ action.

\section{\texorpdfstring{An $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple on the continuum and strings among the ordinates}{An sl2 triple on the continuum and strings among the ordinates}}\label{sec:sl2}
Since $\T$ has simple spectrum, any operator commuting with $\T$ lies in
$W^*(\T)$. In the function-field case the Lefschetz operator does not commute
with Frobenius but intertwines it up to a Tate twist. The analogue here is the
relation $[\T,L]=cL$.

\begin{proposition}[Strings among the ordinates]\label{prop:nogo}
Let $c\ne0$ and let $L$ be a linear operator on $\ell^2(\Gamma)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(i)] If $\gamma\in\Gamma$ and $[\T,L]x=cLx$ holds for
$x=e_\gamma,Le_\gamma,\dots,L^{k-1}e_\gamma$ (with these vectors and their images
in the relevant domains), and $L^ke_\gamma\neq0$, then
$\gamma,\gamma+c,\dots,\gamma+kc\in\Gamma$.
\item[\rm(ii)] In particular, if $(L,F,H)$ is an $\sltwo$-triple with
$H=\frac2c\T$ containing such a string of length $k+1\ge3$, then $\Gamma$
contains a three-term arithmetic progression $\gamma,\gamma+c,\gamma+2c$, which
yields a nontrivial $\mathbb Q$-linear relation among the positive ordinates.
Under $\mathrm{LI}$ no such string exists.
\item[\rm(iii)] Strings of length two are not excluded by $\mathrm{LI}$: for
$\gamma_1\ne\gamma_2$ in $\Gamma$ and $c=\gamma_2-\gamma_1$, the rank-one
operator $L=\langle\cdot,e_{\gamma_1}\rangle e_{\gamma_2}$ is bounded, nonzero,
and satisfies $[\T,L]=cL$ on the finite linear span of the basis.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) If $\T y=\lambda y$ and $[\T,L]y=cLy$, then $\T Ly=L\T y+cLy=(\lambda+c)Ly$.
Inductively $L^je_\gamma$ is an eigenvector of $\T$ with eigenvalue $\gamma+jc$
whenever it is nonzero, and $L^je_\gamma\ne0$ for $j\le k$ because
$L^ke_\gamma\neq0$. The eigenvalues of $\T$ are exactly the elements of $\Gamma$.

(ii) The progression gives $\gamma+(\gamma+2c)=2(\gamma+c)$. Write each of the
three ordinates as $\pm$ a positive ordinate ($0\notin\Gamma$ since
$\zeta(\frac12)\ne0$, and $\Gamma=-\Gamma$). If the three absolute values are
distinct, the relation has coefficients $\pm1,\pm2,\pm1$ on three distinct
positive ordinates. Two absolute values cannot coincide for $\gamma$ and
$\gamma+2c$ (that would force $\gamma+c=0$); if $|\gamma|=|\gamma+c|$ then
$\gamma=-c/2$ and, up to an overall sign, the progression is $-x,x,y$ with
$x=|c|/2$ and $y=3x$ both positive ordinates; the relation $-x+y=2x$ becomes
$3x-y=0$, a nontrivial relation between the distinct positive ordinates $x,y$;
the case $|\gamma+c|=|\gamma+2c|$ is
symmetric. In all cases one obtains a nontrivial $\mathbb Q$-linear relation,
which $\mathrm{LI}$ excludes.

(iii) $\T Le_{\gamma_1}=\gamma_2e_{\gamma_2}$ and $L\T e_{\gamma_1}=\gamma_1
e_{\gamma_2}$, so $[\T,L]e_{\gamma_1}=ce_{\gamma_2}=cLe_{\gamma_1}$; on
$e_\gamma$ with $\gamma\ne\gamma_1$ both sides vanish.
\end{proof}

On $L^2(\mathbb R,dr)$, by contrast, the multiplication operator $\M$ has
spectrum $\mathbb R$, which is translation invariant, and the standard $\sltwo$
triple built from translation and multiplication is available. Let
$(\tau_cf)(r)=f(r-c)$.

\begin{proposition}[The continuum $\sltwo$ triple]\label{prop:sl2}
On the Schwartz space $\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\subset L^2(\mathbb R,dr)$ put
$L=\tau_c$, $H=\tfrac2c\,\M$, and $F=\tau_{-c}\,m(\M)$ with
$m(r)=-\tfrac{r^2}{c^2}+\tfrac{r}{c}$. These operators preserve
$\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ and satisfy
\[
[\M,L]=cL,\qquad [H,L]=2L,\qquad [H,F]=-2F,\qquad [L,F]=H,
\]
so $(L,F,H)$ is an $\sltwo$-triple whose raising operator intertwines $\M$ up to
the shift $c$. The raising operator $L$ is not a multiplication operator, hence
$L\notin W^*(\M)$, and the grading $H$ has continuous spectrum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$([\M,\tau_c]f)(r)=rf(r-c)-(r-c)f(r-c)=c(\tau_cf)(r)$, which is the
Weyl commutation relation; then $[H,L]=\tfrac2c[\M,\tau_c]=2L$. Since
$\tau_cF=m(\M)$ and $(F\tau_cf)(r)=m(r+c)f(r)$,
$[L,F]=m(r)-m(r+c)=\tfrac2c r=H$, using
$m(r)-m(r+c)=-\tfrac{1}{c^2}(r^2-(r+c)^2)+\tfrac1c(r-(r+c))=\tfrac2c r$. Finally
$[\M,\tau_{-c}]=-c\tau_{-c}$ gives $[H,F]=\tfrac2c[\M,\tau_{-c}]m(\M)=-2F$. Since
$\tau_c\,e^{i\pi\M/c}=-e^{i\pi\M/c}\,\tau_c$, the translation $\tau_c$ does not
commute with the bounded function $e^{i\pi\M/c}$ of $\M$, so it is not in the
abelian algebra $W^*(\M)$. A finite-difference check of the relations is in
Appendix~\ref{app:code}.
\end{proof}

\section{The explicit formula on primitive Gaussian tests}\label{sec:transport}
The continuum structure connects to the zeros only through the explicit formula.

\begin{proposition}[Explicit formula]\label{prop:transport}
For primitive $f$ in the Schwartz class with $\widehat f$ even and $h=|\widehat f|^2$ extending to an
entire function of rapid decay in horizontal strips,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
Q(f,f):=\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)
=\Ainf(f,f)-P(f,f),\qquad
P(f,f)=2\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\vM(n)}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
\end{equation}
where $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma_\rho$ runs over the nontrivial zeros with
multiplicity, $h$ is extended analytically to complex $\gamma_\rho$, $\vM$ is
the von Mangoldt function and $g(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int h(r)e^{-iur}dr$ (the pole
term $h(\frac i2)+h(-\frac i2)$ vanishes since $\widehat f(\pm i/2)=0$). This is
Weil's explicit formula~\cite{Weil52}; see~\cite[Thm.~5.12]{bgIK}. Under $\RH$,
$Q(f,f)=\sum_\rho|\widehat f(\gamma_\rho)|^2\ge0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Numerical]\label{rem:transport}
Table~\ref{tab:transport} evaluates both sides of \eqref{eq:transport} for
$\widehat f_\sigma$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:indef}, using the first $300$ zeros
and prime powers $n\le2\cdot10^4$, at $30$-digit working precision. For
$\sigma\le4$ the omitted zero and prime terms are smaller than $10^{-36}$
(the zero tail is far smaller; the prime tail beyond $n=2\cdot10^4$, of size
$e^{-\sigma^2(\log n)^2/4}$ up to polynomial factors, is about $3\times10^{-37}$
for $\sigma=2$ and smaller for $\sigma=3,4$), so the residual measures only the working precision. On these
primitives $\Ainf$ and $P$ are both negative and nearly equal, and $Q$ is the
small residual of their difference.
\end{remark}

\section{No lowest-weight vector on the continuum}\label{sec:dichotomy}

\begin{proposition}[No lowest-weight vector]\label{prop:ps}
For the triple of Proposition~\ref{prop:sl2}: the closure of $H$ is
self-adjoint with $\spec(H)=\mathbb R$, unbounded below and above; and the
equation $Fv=0$ has no nonzero solution $v\in L^2(\mathbb R)$ (with $F$ defined
on its maximal domain). In particular the representation has no lowest-weight
vector in $L^2$ and no decomposition $\bigoplus_kL^k(\ker F)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$H$ is $\frac2c$ times multiplication by $r$, essentially self-adjoint on
$\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ with spectrum $\mathbb R$. If $Fv=0$ then
$m(r+c)v(r+c)=0$ for almost every $r$, so $v$ vanishes outside the zero set
$\{0,c\}$ of $m$, a null set; hence $v=0$ in $L^2$.
\end{proof}

The Hodge--Riemann bilinear relations are stated for $\sltwo$-representations
generated by lowest-weight vectors (for example finite-dimensional ones), through
the primitive decomposition; Proposition~\ref{prop:ps} shows that the continuum triple is not
of this kind, so those relations do not apply to it. Numerically, on the tests
of Table~\ref{tab:transport} the positive form
\[g_\infty(f,f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int|\widehat f|^2|\Psi|\,dr\]
exceeds $Q$ by factors of
about $4\times10^{17}$, $1.3\times10^{6}$ and $2.1\times10^{2}$ for $\sigma=2,3,4$
(Appendix~\ref{app:code}); this illustrates, but does not prove, that the
positivity of $g_\infty$ is unrelated to that of $Q$.

\begin{remark}\label{thm:dichotomy}
Under $\mathrm{LI}$, the zero spectrum contains no three-term arithmetic
progression, so no $\sltwo$-string of length $\ge3$ with grading proportional to
$\T$ exists on $\ell^2(\Gamma)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:nogo}). On
$L^2(\mathbb R)$ the explicit triple of Proposition~\ref{prop:sl2} has no
normalizable lowest-weight vector (Proposition~\ref{prop:ps}), so the
finite-dimensional Hodge--Riemann relations do not apply to it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
These observations concern only the specific structures considered here: an
$\sltwo$ action with grading proportional to the ordinates, and the triple of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sl2}. They do not assert that $\RH$ is false, nor exclude
other constructions of a positivity mechanism.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}
We proved the structure of the archimedean density $\Psi$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:psi}) and the indefiniteness of $\Ainf$ on primitive
Gaussian tests (Proposition~\ref{prop:indef}); recorded the standard $\sltwo$
triple built from translation and multiplication on $L^2(\mathbb R)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sl2}) and proved that it has no lowest-weight vector
(Proposition~\ref{prop:ps}); and proved that under $\mathrm{LI}$ no
$\sltwo$-string of length at least three with grading proportional to the
ordinates exists on the zeros (Proposition~\ref{prop:nogo}). Numerically, on the
primitive Gaussian tests the zero side of the explicit formula is the small
difference of two large negative quantities $\Ainf$ and $P$
(Remark~\ref{rem:transport}).
