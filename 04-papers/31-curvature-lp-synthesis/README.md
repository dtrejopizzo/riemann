# The second σ-derivative of log|ξ| on the critical line

The paper studies C(γ) = ∂²_σ log|ξ(σ+iγ)| at σ = 1/2, equivalently −(log|f|)'' with f(t) = ξ(1/2+it). It records the Hadamard-product formula for C, its sign under the Riemann Hypothesis, the explicit contributions of the polynomial and gamma factors of ξ, and the relation of positivity of C to known formulations of RH.

## Results

- Hadamard formula ∂²_σ log|ξ(s)| = Σ_ρ Re(−(s−ρ)⁻²) (Theorem 2.1; classical).
- RH implies C(γ) = Σ_ρ (γ−γ_ρ)⁻² > 0 off the ordinates; an off-line zero ½+b₀+iγ₀ contributes −2/b₀² to C(γ₀) together with its reflection (Theorem 3.4).
- Whether C > 0 implies RH is open; the analogue for general real entire functions of order one is false, e.g. (t²+1) sin πt (Remark 3.6).
- Λ = 0, κ(Q) = 0, f ∈ LP and RH are equivalent by known results and imply C > 0 (Theorem 4.4). The spectral-flow, K-theory and energy formulations are discussed only as remarks (heuristic).
- Sector decomposition of C into polynomial, gamma and ζ parts (Theorem 5.1); G_Γ(t) = −1/(4t²) + O(t⁻⁴), G_ana(0) ≈ −3.70, G_ana(t) = 7/(4t²) + O(t⁻⁴), so G_ana has a positive zero; with t_* the smallest one, G_ana < 0 on (−t_*, t_*) (Proposition 5.2). Its location t_* ≈ 0.4406 and its uniqueness on (0, 50] are numerical evidence only.

## Known and new

The Hadamard formula and RH ⟹ C > 0 are immediate; the related criterion RH ⟺ Re(ξ'/ξ) > 0 on Re s > 1/2 is due to Lagarias (1999), and the LP and de Bruijn–Newman equivalences are classical. The explicit polygamma computation of the analytic part and the discussion of the converse are what the paper adds.
