# Jacobi operators, a prime-sum potential and the logarithmic derivative of zeta on the critical line

The paper examines a proposed spectral reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis: take a limit Jacobi operator of the Connes–Consani–Moscovici construction and an explicit potential C_∞ = w − Ψ, built from the digamma function and a prime sum, and state RH as an "incidence inclusion": C_∞ vanishes at every real zero of Ξ. The paper shows that this reformulation does not hold and proves what is true.

What the paper proves:
- At the ordinate γ of a zero of multiplicity m on the critical line, C_∞(γ+iη) = 2m/η + O(1), so the Abel boundary value does not exist. The incidence inclusion fails at every such zero, and by Hardy's theorem it is false unconditionally.
- A Jacobi operator whose coefficients tend to zero is compact, so its spectrum cannot be the set of zero ordinates; its moment problem is determinate.
- The truncated potential C_λ takes no value of modulus ≤ 1 in a region far out in Im z ≥ 1/2 + ε. Near a non-degenerate real critical point, the zeros of C_λ − μ leave the real axis as a conjugate pair with an explicit local law, and their imaginary part is bounded below using sup|C_λ''| ≪ λ log²λ.
- The classical identity Re ζ'/ζ(1/2+it) = (1/2)log π − (1/2)Re ψ(1/4+it/2) = −w(t)/2 for Ξ(t) ≠ 0, derived from the Hadamard expansion. It holds for every configuration of zeros and does not involve RH (also checked numerically at four points).
- The only equivalence with RH in the paper is a trivial restatement: RH holds iff all zeros of Ξ are real.

False statements of the proposed reformulation are discussed in remarks, each with the reason it fails. The Hadamard expansion, the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula and the identity on the line are classical (Davenport); the determinacy of the m_∞ moment problem is due to Connes–Consani–Moscovici. The new material is the analysis of the potential and the elementary statements about C_λ.
