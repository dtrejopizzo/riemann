## A. The lattice sums are finite on the declared category (removes I-3)

The declared completion (`main.md` Def. 3.1) consists of compactly supported
scalar sections of the Fell bundle over the external class group.  A section
`h` is a function on classes `w`, compactly supported in the real orbit
coordinate `log|w|_∞`, with finitely many finite-place components.

**Proposition A.1.**  For compactly supported `h`, every lattice sum
`Σ_{γ∈Γ_S}` in `main.md` (8.2) evaluated against `h` is a **finite** sum.

*Proof.*  `Γ_S` is generated by the primes of `S_f`, `γ=∏p^{e_p}`, and
`γw` has real orbit coordinate `log|w|_∞ + Σ_p e_p log p`.  Compact support
of `h` in that coordinate bounds `Σ e_p log p`, and the `e_p` are integers,
so finitely many `γ` contribute.  ∎

**Proposition A.2 (the finite-place packet is a finite trace).**  For
`h∈C_c^∞(R)` supported in `[−R,R]`, with `w_n(h)=n^{−1/2}[h(log n)+h(−log n)]`
and the finite orbit lattices `V_n` of P123.038,

    lim_{r→1^-} Tr( ⊕_{2≤n≤e^R} w_n(h) log(1−rT_n) )
      = Σ_{p,k≥1} (log p) p^{−k/2} [h(k log p)+h(−k log p)],

all sums finite by support; "passage to the limit requires no unproved
uniform estimate across labels" (P123.039 §4, (4.1)).  The local factor
**[CORRECTED, phase 128 A10:]** the sentence below is wrong and is withdrawn.
In P123.039 (4.1) the summand is (weight `p^{−k/2}`) × (log-torsion `log p`) —
a product of a multiplicative weight with a logarithm — whereas
`log τ_p(p^k) = −(k/2)log p`, which is not `(log p)p^{−k/2}` for any `k ≥ 1`.
So Proposition A.2 does not exhibit `Σ_v log τ_v` and does not support the
local-factor reading of `T`; what it does support, and all that is used, is the
**finiteness** of the lattice sums on compactly supported sections.  Withdrawn
sentence: "`(log p)p^{−k/2}` is `log` of the half-density `|p^k|^{1/2}` times the fixed
determinant `det(1−T|V_{p^k})=p` of P123.039 §3, i.e. the local factor (8.1)
at `σ_p=p^k` where `|1−p^k|_p=1`.  Status: `PROVED-IN-REPOSITORY`.

**Consequence.**  **[REBUILT, phase 129.]**  The previous text here defined
`T(h) := Σ_w h(w) Σ_v log τ_v(w)`.  That is withdrawn: it is the wrong shape,
for the reason `main.md` §13.6 gives — the relative trace is a sum of local
*distributions whose densities are the `τ_v`*, not a sum of their logarithms.
The definition of `T` is now `main.md` Definition 8.6 and its local
decomposition is `main.md` Theorem 8.7,

    T(F) = Σ_{v∈S} W_v(F),
    W_p(F) = ∫_{v_p(x)≠0} a(x) τ_p(x) d^*x
             + (log p)∫_{Z_p^×}(a(u)−a(1))/|1−u|_p du,    a=F∘j_p,

which is P123.042 (3.2) verbatim, with the archimedean term P123.035 (3.1).
What Proposition A.1 contributes is unchanged and is still used: on the
declared completion every `Γ_S`-fold in these integrals is a **finite** sum,
so the local terms are finite sums of finite integrals and no convergence
question arises beyond the identity class.  The pairing `⟨F,G⟩ := T(F⋆G^†)` is

## B. Excision at the origin (removes I-2)

### B.1 Setting

Let `R = C·1 ⊕ I` be a unital augmented complete Fréchet algebra with
augmentation ideal `I`, and `σ` a continuous automorphism with `σ(1)=1`,
`σ(I)=I`.  Assume `I` is **admissibly H-unital**: its bar complex
`B'_n(I)=I^{⊗̂n}` with `b'(a_1⊗…⊗a_n)=Σ(−1)^{j−1}a_1⊗…⊗a_ja_{j+1}⊗…⊗a_n`
admits a continuous linear contraction `h` with `b'h+hb'=id` in degrees
`n≥1`.

**Lemma B.1 (H-unitality of the coefficient ideals).**  `E_P`, every `I_J`
and `N=I_∅` are admissibly H-unital.  *Proof.*  P123.124 (2)–(4): with the
adapted partition `{e_i,ρ_i}` (shell idempotents at `p∈J`; `χ_p` and
negative shells at `p∉J`), `h(a)=Σ_i e_i⊗ρ_i a` converges by the summability
estimate (3), and "inserting (4) at the first factor contracts the nonunital
multiplication bar … the ideal bar itself is linearly contractible in the
stated topology".  For `E_P` use the partition at identity label without the
lattice.  ∎

### B.2 The cone argument

**Theorem B.2.**  With the hypotheses of B.1, there is a continuous
homotopy equivalence of complexes

    C̄(R, R_σ) ≃ C[0] ⊕ C(I, I_σ),

where `C̄` is the normalized Hochschild complex and `C(I,I_σ)_n=I⊗̂I^{⊗̂n}`
with the twisted Hochschild boundary.  In particular
`HH(R,R_σ) ≅ C[0] ⊕ HH(I,I_σ)` admissibly.

*Proof.*  The normalized complex is `C̄_n = R_σ ⊗̂ (R/C1)^{⊗̂n} = R⊗̂I^{⊗̂n}
= (C1⊗̂I^{⊗̂n}) ⊕ (I⊗̂I^{⊗̂n}) =: P_n ⊕ Q_n`, a topological direct sum since
`R=C1⊕I` is.  Write the twisted boundary
`b(a_0⊗…⊗a_n) = Σ_{j<n}(−1)^j a_0⊗…⊗a_ja_{j+1}⊗… + (−1)^n σ(a_n)a_0⊗a_1⊗…⊗a_{n−1}`.

*On `Q`.*  All products lie in `I`, so `b(Q_n)⊆Q_{n−1}`: `Q=C(I,I_σ)`.

*On `P`.*  For `1⊗a_1⊗…⊗a_n`: the terms with `j≥1` are
`(−1)^j 1⊗…⊗a_ja_{j+1}⊗…∈P_{n−1}` and reproduce `b'` on `I^{⊗̂n}` (with sign
`−`); the `j=0` term is `a_1⊗a_2⊗…⊗a_n ∈ Q_{n−1}` and the cyclic term is
`(−1)^n σ(a_n)⊗a_1⊗…⊗a_{n−1}∈Q_{n−1}`.  Hence `P_n=I^{⊗̂n}` with differential
`−b'`, and a connecting map `φ:P_n→Q_{n−1}`,
`φ(a_1⊗…⊗a_n)=a_1⊗…⊗a_n + (−1)^n σ(a_n)⊗a_1⊗…⊗a_{n−1}`, so that
`C̄ = Cone(φ: B'(I)[?]→C(I,I_σ))` up to the standard sign convention; in
degree `0`, `P_0=C·1` with `b=0`, which splits off as `C[0]` because
`b(1⊗a_1)=a_1−σ(a_1)·1∈I` lands in `Q_0`, not in `P_0`.

*Contracting the cone.*  `B'(I)` is contractible by `h`.  For a cone
`Cone(φ:X→Y)` with `X` contractible via `h`, the inclusion `Y→Cone(φ)` is a
homotopy equivalence with inverse `(x,y) ↦ y − φ(h x)` and homotopy built
from `h`; every map is a finite composite of `φ`, `h` and the differentials,
hence continuous.  Therefore `C̄(R,R_σ) ≃ C[0] ⊕ C(I,I_σ)`.  ∎

Applied to `R=C=E_P⊕C1` (the unital coefficient algebra of the source) with
`I=E_P`: `HH(C,C_σ) ≅ C[0]⊕HH(E_P,(E_P)_σ)`, the `C[0]` being the class of
the adjoined unit.  Applied at a single finite place to the unital
`A_1=Cχ_p⊕F_p^{≥0}` (functions on `Z_p`, unit `χ_p`, augmentation
"evaluation at 0", ideal `F_p^{≥0}` H-unital by the shell homotopy on
`j≥0`): `HH(A_1,σ) ≅ C[0]⊕HH(F_p^{≥0},σ)`, the `C[0]` being the origin
class.  Import I-2 is discharged; excision in this **split augmented** case
needs nothing beyond B.2.

### B.3 The one cited theorem

For the non-split H-unital extension used in Appendix C (the ideal
`E_P ⊂ C^⊗`), we cite: **Wodzicki's excision theorem** — for an H-unital
ideal `I` in an algebra `A`, the sequence `HH(I)→HH(A)→HH(A/I)` is a
distinguished triangle — in its topological form for Fréchet algebras with
continuous H-unitality (Wodzicki, Ann. Math. 129 (1989); Fréchet/bornological
versions in Cuntz–Quillen and in R. Meyer, *Local and analytic cyclic
homology*, EMS 2007, Thm. on excision for H-unital bornological algebras),
with twisted coefficients handled identically since the proof uses only the
bar complex of `I`.  Hypothesis map: H-unitality of `E_P` with a continuous
contraction is Lemma B.1; all algebras are nuclear Fréchet, hence
bornological with the precompact bornology, so Meyer's hypotheses apply.
This is the only statement in the closure not proved inside the repository
or this appendix.

## C. Künneth for the coefficient algebra (removes I-1)

### C.1 Unital Eilenberg–Zilber, with continuity

Let `A,B` be unital complete Fréchet algebras, `M` an `A`-bimodule, `N` a
`B`-bimodule, all complete.  The Hochschild complex `C(A⊗̂B, M⊗̂N)` has
`n`-th term `(M⊗̂N)⊗̂(A⊗̂B)^{⊗̂n} ≅ (M⊗̂A^{⊗̂n})⊗̂(N⊗̂B^{⊗̂n})`, a continuous
isomorphism by associativity and commutativity of `⊗̂_π` on complete spaces,
and its face maps are `d_i⊗d_i`: it is the **diagonal** of the bisimplicial
module `C_{p,q}=C_p(A,M)⊗̂C_q(B,N)`.  The degeneracies `s_i` (insertion of
`1_A`, `1_B`) exist because the algebras are unital.

**Theorem C.1.**  The shuffle map `∇: Tot C_{•,•} → Diag C_{•,•}`,
`∇(x⊗y)=Σ_{(μ,ν)} sgn(μ,ν) s_{ν_q}…s_{ν_1}x ⊗ s_{μ_p}…s_{μ_1}y` over
`(p,q)`-shuffles, and the Alexander–Whitney map `AW: Diag→Tot`,
`AW(z)=Σ_{p+q=n} d_{p+1}…d_n z ⊗ d_0^{p} z` (front and back faces), are
continuous chain maps with `AW∘∇=id` and `∇∘AW ≃ id` by the Eilenberg–Mac Lane
homotopy, itself a finite signed sum of composites of faces and
degeneracies.  Hence `C(A⊗̂B,M⊗̂N) ≃ Tot(C(A,M)⊗̂C(B,N))` continuously and
admissibly.

*Proof.*  The formulas and identities are the classical ones for
bisimplicial modules (Eilenberg–Zilber; Mac Lane, *Homology*, VIII.8); they
are purely combinatorial identities among faces and degeneracies and hold in
any additive category, in particular for the simplicial objects in complete
locally convex spaces at hand.  Each face is a multiplication or the module
action, each degeneracy a unit insertion; all are continuous, and every map
above is a **finite** signed sum of finite composites of them in each degree,
hence continuous.  The homotopy is likewise finite in each degree.  ∎

Twisted coefficients: for `σ=σ_A⊗σ_B` acting coordinatewise, the twisted
bimodule `(A⊗̂B)_σ` is `A_{σ_A}⊗̂B_{σ_B}` as a bimodule, so C.1 applies with
`M=A_{σ_A}`, `N=B_{σ_B}`.  By induction, for finitely many unital factors.

### C.2 From the unital tensor to the coefficient algebra

Let `E_v^+ = E_v⊕C1` be the unitization at each place, `C^⊗ := ⊗̂_v E_v^+`
(unital), and `C = E_P⊕C1 ⊂ C^⊗` via `1↦⊗1`.  As a vector space
`C^⊗ = ⊕_{V⊆places} E_V` with `E_V=⊗_{v∈V}E_v` (units elsewhere), `E_{all}=E_P`.

**Step 1 (per place).**  `HH(E_v^+,σ_v) ≅ C[0]⊕HH(E_v,σ_v)` by Theorem B.2,
since `E_v` is admissibly H-unital (Lemma B.1 at one place).

**Step 2 (unital Künneth).**  By C.1,
`HH(C^⊗,σ) ≅ ⊗̂_v HH(E_v^+,σ_v) ≅ ⊕_{V} ⊗_{v∈V}HH(E_v,σ_v)`, where the
summand `V=∅` is `C[0]`.

**Step 3 (the top summand is `HH(E_P)`).**  `E_P⊂C^⊗` is an H-unital ideal
(Lemma B.1) with quotient `Q=C^⊗/E_P=⊕_{V≠all}E_V`.  By Wodzicki excision
(B.3) there is a triangle `HH(E_P,σ)→HH(C^⊗,σ)→HH(Q,σ)`.  Filter `C^⊗` by
`F_k = ⊕_{|V|≤k}E_V`; each `F_k/F_{k−1}` is an orthogonal sum of the `E_V`
with `|V|=k` (products of two such land in `|V∪V'|>k` or vanish), and the
same Step-2 computation applied to the algebras `⊗_{v∈V}E_v^+` (`|V|=k`)
together with B.2 identifies `HH(F_k/F_{k−1},σ) ≅ ⊕_{|V|=k}⊗_{v∈V}HH(E_v,σ_v)`.
By induction on `k` (excision at each step, the ideals `F_{k−1}⊂F_k` being
H-unital as finite orthogonal sums of tensors of H-unital algebras, with the
tensor of contractions), `HH(Q,σ) ≅ ⊕_{V≠all}⊗_{v∈V}HH(E_v,σ_v)`, and the map
`HH(C^⊗)→HH(Q)` is the projection killing the `V=all` summand, because it is
induced by the quotient map, which is the identity on `E_V` for `V≠all` and
zero on `E_P`.  Hence the fibre is the top summand:

    HH(E_P,(E_P)_σ) ≅ ⊗̂_v HH(E_v,(E_v)_{σ_v}).                        (C.1)

**Step 4 (the source coefficient algebra).**  By B.2 applied to `C=E_P⊕C1`,
`HH(C,C_σ) ≅ C[0]⊕⊗̂_v HH(E_v,σ_v)`.  This is `main.md` (7.2) in its correct
form: the adjoined unit contributes `C[0]`, and the coefficient part is the
tensor of the local twisted Hochschild complexes.  Import I-1 is discharged,
with B.3 as the only cited theorem.

## D. The local twisted computations, corrected

### D.1 Shells and the origin

At a finite place, `E_p = A_1 ⊕ A_2` as an orthogonal sum of ideals,
`A_1=Cχ_p⊕F_p^{≥0}` (functions on `Z_p`, unital with unit `χ_p`),
`A_2=F_p^{<0}=⊕̂_{j<0}F^{(j)}` (functions on `Q_p∖Z_p`).  Each shell algebra
`F^{(j)}` is the algebra of functions on `p^jZ_p^×≅Z_p^×` with rapid
conductor Fourier weights.

**Lemma D.1 (orthogonal sums).**  For orthogonal ideals `A_1,A_2` with
`A_1` unital and `A_2` admissibly H-unital, `HH(A_1⊕A_2,σ) ≅
HH(A_1,σ)⊕HH(A_2,σ)` when `σ` preserves each.  *Proof.*  Mixed words (with
factors from both) form a subcomplex, since products across summands vanish;
the homotopy `s(a_0⊗…)=a_0χ_p⊗χ_p⊗… + Σ_{j<0}a_0 1_j⊗1_j⊗…` (one term for
`A_1`, the shell family for `A_2`, convergent by rapid decay on `j<0`)
satisfies `bs+sb=id` on mixed words because at least one product in every
term vanishes, and preserves the pure subcomplexes; hence the mixed
subcomplex is contractible.  ∎

**Lemma D.2 (the origin).**  `HH(A_1,σ) ≅ C[0]⊕HH(F_p^{≥0},σ)`, the `C[0]`
being evaluation at `0`, untwisted because `σ(χ_p)−χ_p∈F_p^{≥0}` and
`σ(0)=0`.  *Proof.*  Theorem B.2 with the shell homotopy on `j≥0`.  ∎

### D.2 Non-unit twist: shells shifted

If `v_p(σ_p)=v≠0`, `σ` shifts shells `j↦j+v`.  The shell homotopy
`s(a_0⊗…)=Σ_j a_0 1_j⊗1_j⊗…` on the twisted complex of `F_p^{≥0}` (resp.
`F_p^{<0}`) satisfies `bs+sb=id` in **all** degrees including zero, because
the only term not of the untwisted form is the cyclic one, which contains
`1_j·σ(1_j)=1_j1_{j+v}=0`.  Hence `HH(F_p^{≥0},σ)=HH(F_p^{<0},σ)=0` and

    HH(E_p,(E_p)_σ) ≅ C[0]   (v_p(σ_p)≠0),                              (D.1)

the origin class.  This part of `main.md` §7.1(c) stands.  Note
`|1−σ_p|_p=1` here **when `v_p(σ_p) > 0`**, so the local factor (8.1) is then
the half-density `|σ_p|^{1/2}` alone.  **[CORRECTED, phase 128 A7:]** the first
version stated this for all `v_p(σ_p) ≠ 0`.  For `v_p(σ_p) < 0` one has
`|σ_p|_p > 1`, hence `|1−σ_p|_p = |σ_p|_p` and `τ_p = |σ_p|_p^{−1/2}`.  The
correct uniform statement is `τ_p(σ) = p^{−|v_p(σ)|/2}`, which is what P123.042
writes ("`x = p^k u`, `k≠0`, gives `p^{−|k|/2}`").  Negative valuations occur in
every lattice sum, `Γ_S` being a full lattice.

### D.3 Unit twist: the correction

Let `σ_p=u∈Z_p^×`, `u≠1`, `k:=v_p(1−u)≥1`.  `σ` preserves every shell and
acts on `F^{(j)}≅C^∞_{rapid}(Z_p^×)` by translation `x↦ux`.  In the character
basis `e_κ` of `Z_p^×`, `σ(e_κ)=κ(u)e_κ`.

**Lemma D.3 (`u ∈ 1+pZ_p` only).**  **[CORRECTED, phase 128 A8:]** the hypothesis
`k = v_p(1−u) ≥ 1`, i.e. `u ≡ 1 mod p`, is essential and was left implicit.  In
general `HH_0` is spanned by the characters trivial on the **closure of `⟨u⟩`**,
and that closure is `1+p^kZ_p` only when `k ≥ 1`; otherwise the count is
`[Z_p^× : closure⟨u⟩]`.  With that hypothesis:
`HH_0(F^{(j)},σ) ≅ C^{N_k}` with `N_k=(p−1)p^{k−1}
=|Z_p^×/(1+p^kZ_p)|`, and `HH_n(F^{(j)},σ)=0` for `n≥1`; the isomorphism is
continuous and the complex is admissibly equivalent to `C^{N_k}[0]`.

*Proof.*  `F^{(j)}` is unital (unit `1_j`), commutative, so
`HH_0(F,F_σ)=F/(1−σ)F`.  On `e_κ`, `(1−σ)e_κ=(1−κ(u))e_κ`.  `κ(u)=1` iff `κ`
is trivial on the closure of `⟨u⟩`, which is `1+p^kZ_p` (for `p` odd, or
with the usual modification at `p=2`); there are `N_k` such characters.  For
the others, `κ(u)` is a root of unity of order dividing `p^{m−k}(p−1)` where
`m=c(κ)` is the conductor, so `|1−κ(u)|≥c·p^{−(m−k)}`, and division by
`1−κ(u)` is continuous on the rapid-conductor space with a seminorm shift.
Hence `(1−σ)F` is closed, complemented by `span{e_κ:κ(u)=1}`, and
`HH_0≅C^{N_k}`.  For `n≥1`: the twisted normalized complex of `F` is
diagonalized by the character basis in each tensor factor; the summand
indexed by characters `(κ_0,…,κ_n)` has boundary a finite matrix with
entries `±1` and `±κ_n(u)`, and the untwisted contraction of P123.118 (which
proves `H_n(F)=0`, `n≥1`, for this algebra) is character-diagonal; twisting
multiplies the cyclic face by `κ_n(u)`, a scalar of modulus one, and the
same contraction works degreewise with the same seminorm control, except on
the summands where all `κ_i(u)=1`, i.e. the subalgebra `C(Z_p^×/(1+p^kZ_p))`
of `N_k` points on which `σ` acts trivially: a finite-dimensional semisimple
algebra, whose Hochschild homology is `C^{N_k}` in degree `0` and zero above.
∎

**Corollary D.4 (corrected local factor for unit twists).**

    HH(E_p,(E_p)_u) ≅ C[0] ⊕ ⊕̂_{j∈Z} C^{N_k}[0],   N_k=(p−1)p^{k−1},      (D.2)

**infinite-dimensional** in degree zero: one orbit-space copy per shell.

### D.4 What this corrects, and what it confirms

`main.md` §7.1(c) claimed `C[0]` for every `σ_p≠1`; that holds only for
`v_p(σ_p)≠0` (D.1).  For unit twists the shadow is (D.2).  Consequently:

* **Theorem 7.4 (isomorphic shadows) is withdrawn.**  Two unit classes with
  different `k` have shadows `C⊕⊕̂_jC^{N_k}` and `C⊕⊕̂_jC^{N_{k'}}`, not
  isomorphic as filtered objects.
* **Corollary 7.5 is restated correctly.**  Additive invariants of the
  shadow are either **constant** (non-unit twists: the origin class alone,
  independent of `σ_p`; so on the arithmetic family `Γ_n` no rank sees
  `p^{−k/2}`) or **divergent** (unit twists: infinitely many shells, so no
  finite rank, Euler characteristic or determinant is defined).  In no case
  is there a finite additive invariant separating classes.  The statement
  "no additive numerical theory is Weil-strength" stands, with this proof.
* **The analytic trace is the regularization that makes (D.2) finite, and
  it is exactly (8.1) — **on `1+pZ_p`.**  **[CORRECTED, phase 128 A8:]** the
  identification fails for units `u ≢ 1 mod p`: for `p=7`, `u=−1`, the closure
  of `⟨u⟩` is `{±1}`, of index `3`, while `|1−u|_7 = |2|_7 = 1`, so the
  "regularized orbit count" would have to be `1 ≠ 3`.  So the unification below
  holds on `1+pZ_p` and not on all units; `T` does not separate all unit
  classes.  Per shell the orbit count is `N_k=(p−1)p^{k−1}
  = c_p·p^k = c_p/|1−u|_p` with `c_p=1−p^{−1}=vol(Z_p^×)`; the Haar weight of
  shell `j` is `c_p p^{−j}` (P123.046 §1, `dk_p=dx/(c_p|x|_p)`), and the
  normal cutoff trace with identity subtraction of P123.042 sums the
  shell contributions with these weights against the test's compact support
  in the orbit coordinate, giving the finite value `1/|1−u|_p` per unit of
  test mass, i.e. (8.1) with `|u|^{1/2}=1`.  So the analytic factor is the
  Haar-regularized rank of the twisted shadow: the two halves of the insight,
  "rank at a unit twist" and "half-density at a non-unit twist", are one
  quantity, `|σ|^{1/2}/|1−σ|_p`.
* **The separating computation stands, in its rebuilt form.**
  **[REBUILT, phase 129:]** it is now `main.md` Theorem 9.5, stated for
  compactly supported sections supported away from the identity class, where
  by `main.md` Theorem 8.8 the trace is an absolutely convergent integral with
  no counterterm.  It uses only the local density `τ_p`, confirmed in both
