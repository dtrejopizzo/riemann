\title[The localized Weil form on a Hermite--Gauss basis]{The localized Weil form on a Hermite--Gauss basis:\\[3pt]
the effect of an off-line zero pair and the truncation of the prime sum}
\author{David Alejandro Trejo Pizzo}
\address{Independent researcher}
\email{dtrejopizzo@gmail.com}

\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We consider the Gram matrix of Weil's quadratic form on $J$ Hermite--Gauss
functions of width $\sigma$ centred at height $T_0$, written as the zero
side minus the arithmetic side of the explicit formula.
\textbf{(I)} Replacing a double point $\tfrac12+i\gamma$ of the zero configuration by the pair
$\tfrac12\pm\delta+i\gamma$ changes the zero-side matrix by
$\delta^2A(\gamma)+O(\delta^4)$, where $A=-(\Phi\Phi^{\top})''$ has rank at
most three and least eigenvalue $-a(\gamma)<0$, with an explicit lower bound
for $a(\gamma)$ and an explicit bound for the remainder.
\textbf{(II)} The operator norm of the tail of the prime sum beyond $X$ is at
most $\Atr\exp(-\alpha(\log X)^2)$ for every $\alpha<\sigma^2/4$, with an
explicit majorant; only a Chebyshev bound for $\psi(x)$ is used.
\textbf{(III)} For the model configuration obtained in (I), with the
arithmetic side of $\zeta$ truncated at $X$, the matrix has a negative
eigenvalue for all $X\ge X_{\min}$, given explicitly, provided $\delta$ is
below an explicit threshold. For $\zeta$ itself the
truncated matrix is the prime tail and tends to $0$ whether or not the
Riemann Hypothesis holds; nothing here bears on RH. For a fixed basis the
passage $X\to\infty$ is elementary; the bound is not uniform in $(\sigma,J)$,
and positivity for all bases, which is what Weil's criterion requires, is not
addressed. The expansion and the tail bound are checked numerically.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\subsection{Weil positivity and its localization}

Let $h$ be holomorphic in a strip $|\im r|\le\tfrac12+\epsilon$ with
$h(r)\ll(1+|r|)^{-2-\epsilon}$ there, and put
$g(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(r)e^{-iru}\,dr$. The
Riemann--Weil explicit formula (see \cite{Weil1952}, \cite{bgBombieriWeil} for this class of test functions; \cite[Thm.~5.12]{IK} for compactly supported ones, from which the present case follows by approximation, the functions $g_{jk}$ below being of Gaussian decay) reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit}
  \underbrace{\sum_{\rho} h(\gamma_\rho)}_{\text{zero side }Z(h)}
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{h(\tfrac i2)+h(-\tfrac i2)}_{\text{polar}}
  +\underbrace{\frac1{2\pi}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\! h(r)\,\Omega(r)\,dr}_{\text{archimedean}}
  -\underbrace{\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,\bigl(g(\log n)+g(-\log n)\bigr)}_{\text{prime side}},
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over the non-trivial zeros $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma_\rho$
(so $\gamma_\rho\in\mathbb C$, and both $\gamma_\rho$ and $-\gamma_\rho$
occur), counted with multiplicity, $\Lambda$ is the von Mangoldt function and
$\Omega(r)=\re\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}{2})-\log\pi$. For
even $h$ the prime side is $2\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}g(\log n)$. Weil's
criterion \cite{Weil1952,bgBombieriWeil} states that RH holds if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weil-criterion}
  B(f,f):=Z\bigl(\hat f\cdot\overline{\hat f(\bar{\,\cdot\,})}\bigr)\ \ge\ 0
\end{equation}
for all $f$ in a suitable class of test functions (for instance smooth and
compactly supported on $\mathbb R$; see \cite{bgBombieriWeil} for the precise
statement), where $\hat f$ is the transform in the normalisation above. The
functional $B$ is the Weil quadratic form.

We study the finite section of $B$ on a localized basis. Fix a centre $T_0$,
a width $\sigma>0$, a dimension $J$, and the functions $\phi_j$ of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}, Hermite functions of width $\sigma$ centred at $T_0$
in the spectral variable. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
  Q_{jk} \;=\; \bigl(M_{\mathrm{zeros}}\bigr)_{jk}-\bigl(M_{\mathrm{arith}}\bigr)_{jk},
\end{equation}
the zero side minus the arithmetic side of \eqref{eq:explicit} evaluated on
$h_{jk}(r)=\phi_j(r)\phi_k(r)$. For $\zeta$ the identity \eqref{eq:explicit}
makes $Q$ identically $0$ when both sides are complete; when the prime sum is
cut at $X$, $Q$ is the tail of the prime sum. A complete matrix
$Q_\infty\ne0$ arises only when the zero side and the arithmetic side come
from different data, as in the model configuration
studied below.

\subsection{Main results}

We work with a model configuration $\mathcal Z_\delta$, a formal signed
multiset. Let $\gamma>0$ be any real number (not necessarily an ordinate of
a zero of $\zeta$) and $\delta>0$. Then $\mathcal Z_\delta$ consists of the
zeros of $\zeta$ together with the four points $\tfrac12\pm\delta\pm i\gamma$
with multiplicity $+1$ and the two points $\tfrac12\pm i\gamma$ with
multiplicity $-2$; its zero-side matrix is by definition
$M_{\mathrm{zeros}}(\mathcal Z_\delta)=M_{\mathrm{zeros}}(\zeta)+P_\delta$,
with $P_\delta=P(\gamma,\delta)+P(-\gamma,\delta)$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:forced}. Equivalently, $P_\delta$ is the change produced
when a double point at $\tfrac12\pm i\gamma$ of an on-line configuration is
split into the off-line points. We do not claim that $\mathcal Z_\delta$ is
the zero set of a Dirichlet series, nor that $\zeta$ has a multiple zero; it
is used with the arithmetic side of $\zeta$, held fixed.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\Roman*)}]
\item (Lemma~\ref{lem:rank2}, Theorem~\ref{thm:forced}.) The zero-side matrix
changes by $P=\delta^2A(\gamma)+O(\delta^4)$, $A=-(\Phi\Phi^{\top})''$, of
rank $\le3$, with $\lmin(A)=-a(\gamma)<0$ (and indefinite in all computed cases); hence by Weyl's
inequality the least eigenvalue of the perturbed matrix is $-a(\gamma)\delta^2+O(\delta^4)$
when the unperturbed complete matrix is $0$.
\item (Theorem~\ref{thm:trunc}.) For every $\alpha<\sigma^2/4$ the tail of
the prime sum beyond $X$ has operator norm at most $\Atr\exp(-\alpha(\log X)^2)$; the proof uses only the Chebyshev bound
$\psi(x)<1.03883\,x$ of Rosser and Schoenfeld~\cite{RS62}.
\item (Theorem~\ref{thm:detector}.) For the model configuration, if $a_*>0$
and $C_*\delta^2\le\tfrac12(1-\kappa)a_*$, there is an explicit cutoff
$X_{\min}$ with $\lmin(Q_X)<0$ for $X\ge\max(2,X_{\min})$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:dichotomy} records what happens for a fixed basis as
$X\to\infty$, and what is not proved.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope}

These statements concern the response of the localized matrix to an
\emph{inserted} off-line pair. For $\zeta$ itself, $Q_X$ is the prime tail
and tends to $0$ independently of RH, so the results give no test of RH
from prime data. The existence direction of Weil's criterion (an off-line
zero gives some test function with $B<0$) is classical
\cite{Weil1952,bgBombieriWeil} and is not what a fixed finite basis gives.

\section{The localized form and its assembly}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Hermite--Gauss basis and the two sides}

Let $\psi_j(x)=(2^jj!\sqrt\pi)^{-1/2}H_j(x)e^{-x^2/2}$ be the
$L^2$-normalized Hermite functions and put
\[
  \phi_j(z)=\sigma^{-1/2}\psi_j\bigl((z-T_0)/\sigma\bigr),\qquad
  \Phi(z)=\bigl(\phi_0(z),\dots,\phi_{J-1}(z)\bigr)^{\!\top},
\]
entire functions of $z$, real on $\mathbb R$, so that
$\Phi(\bar z)=\overline{\Phi(z)}$. With $h_{jk}=\phi_j\phi_k$, which
satisfies the hypotheses of \eqref{eq:explicit}, the zero side is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mzero}
  M_{\mathrm{zeros}} = \sum_{\rho}\Phi(\gamma_\rho)\,\Phi(\overline{\gamma_\rho})^{*}
  =\sum_{\rho}\Phi(\gamma_\rho)\,\Phi(\gamma_\rho)^{\top},
\end{equation}
the sum running over all non-trivial zeros, including those with
$\re\gamma_\rho<0$. It converges absolutely, since
$\|\Phi(\gamma_\rho)\|$ decays like a Gaussian in $|\gamma_\rho|$ while
$N(T)\ll T\log T$. For real $\gamma_\rho$ the term is
$\Phi\Phi^{\top}\succeq0$. A pair of zeros $\rho$, $1-\bar\rho$ with
ordinates $\gamma\mp i\delta$ contributes
$2(aa^{\top}-bb^{\top})$, $a=\re\Phi(\gamma-i\delta)$,
$b=-\im\Phi(\gamma-i\delta)$, which is indefinite whenever $a$ and $b$ are
linearly independent (in particular for small $\delta>0$ when $J\ge2$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank2}).

The arithmetic side consists of the three remaining terms of
\eqref{eq:explicit}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Marith}
  M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{arith}} = M_{\mathrm{prime}}^{(X)} + M_{\mathrm{arch}} + M_{\mathrm{polar}},
\end{equation}
with
\[
M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{prime}}=-\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\bigl(g(\log n)+g(-\log n)\bigr),
\]
where $g=(g_{jk})$,
$g_{jk}(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int h_{jk}(r)e^{-iru}\,dr$;
$M_{\mathrm{arch}}=\bigl(\frac1{2\pi}\int h_{jk}\Omega\,dr\bigr)_{jk}$; and
$M_{\mathrm{polar}}=\bigl(h_{jk}(\tfrac i2)+h_{jk}(-\tfrac i2)\bigr)_{jk}$,
which is of size $e^{-T_0^2/\sigma^2}$ times a polynomial. We write
$M_{\mathrm{arith}}=M^{(\infty)}_{\mathrm{arith}}$,
$Q_X=M_{\mathrm{zeros}}-M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{arith}}$,
$Q_\infty=\lim_{X\to\infty}Q_X$, and
$E_X=M^{(\infty)}_{\mathrm{prime}}-M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{prime}}$. For $\zeta$,
\eqref{eq:explicit} gives $Q_\infty=0$ and $Q_X=E_X$.

\begin{remark}[Check of the implementation]\label{rem:engine}
For $\zeta$, $T_0=46.13$, $\sigma=1$, $J=6$, the first $30$ zeros, prime
powers up to $2\cdot10^7$, and the archimedean integral computed by the
trapezoidal rule, the scripts accompanying this paper give
$\|M_{\mathrm{zeros}}-M_{\mathrm{arith}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\approx2\cdot10^{-13}$,
against $\|M_{\mathrm{zeros}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\approx0.92$. This checks the
sign conventions of \eqref{eq:explicit}--\eqref{eq:Marith}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The on-line reference form}

If every $\gamma_\rho$ is real, $M_{\mathrm{zeros}}$ is a sum of positive
semidefinite rank-one terms. We write $\Qzero$ for the complete form
\eqref{eq:Q} of a configuration whose zero side and arithmetic side satisfy
\eqref{eq:explicit}; then $\Qzero=0$. A general real symmetric $\Qzero$ is
allowed in Theorem~\ref{thm:forced}(i).

\section{The effect of an off-line pair}\label{sec:forced}

\subsection{The perturbation}

Replace a double point $\tfrac12+i\gamma$ of the zero configuration, $\gamma\in\mathbb R$, by the
pair $\tfrac12\pm\delta+i\gamma$, $\delta>0$ (the mirror points at $-\gamma$
are treated in the same way, see \eqref{eq:taylor}). In the spectral
variable the ordinates $\gamma,\gamma$ become $\gamma-i\delta,\gamma+i\delta$.
Write $F(z)=\Phi(z)\Phi(z)^{\top}$, an entire matrix function.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rank2}
Let $J\ge2$, $\gamma\in\mathbb R$, $\delta>0$, and
\[
P(\gamma,\delta)=F(\gamma-i\delta)+F(\gamma+i\delta)-2F(\gamma).
\]
Then $P$ is real symmetric, $P=2(aa^{\top}-bb^{\top})-2\Phi\Phi^{\top}$ with
$a=\re\Phi(\gamma-i\delta)$, $b=-\im\Phi(\gamma-i\delta)$, $\Phi=\Phi(\gamma)$, and
\[
P=\delta^{2}A(\gamma)+R,\qquad
A(\gamma)=-\bigl(2\Phi'\Phi'^{\top}+\Phi\Phi''^{\top}+\Phi''\Phi^{\top}\bigr)=-F''(\gamma),
\qquad
\|R\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C(\gamma,\delta)\,\delta^4,
\]
with $C(\gamma,\delta)=\frac1{12}\max_{|s|\le\delta}\|F''''(\gamma+is)\|_{\mathrm{op}}$.
$A(\gamma)$ has rank at most $3$, is indefinite in general, and
\[
a(\gamma):=-\lmin(A(\gamma))\ \ge\ 2\Bigl(|\Phi'|^{2}-\frac{(\Phi\cdot\Phi')^{2}}{|\Phi|^{2}}\Bigr)>0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\Phi(\bar z)=\overline{\Phi(z)}$, $\Phi(\gamma+i\delta)=a+ib$ and
$\Phi(\gamma-i\delta)=a-ib$, so
$F(\gamma-i\delta)+F(\gamma+i\delta)=(a-ib)(a-ib)^{\top}+(a+ib)(a+ib)^{\top}=2(aa^{\top}-bb^{\top})$.
Let $G(s)=F(\gamma+is)+F(\gamma-is)$ for real $s$; $G$ is even and smooth,
$G(0)=2F(\gamma)$, $G''(0)=-2F''(\gamma)$, and
$\|G''''(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\max_{|s|\le\delta}\|F''''(\gamma+is)\|_{\mathrm{op}}$
for $|t|\le\delta$ (note $\|F''''(\gamma-is)\|=\|F''''(\gamma+is)\|$). Taylor's
formula with integral remainder gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:taylor}
  P(\gamma,\delta)=G(\delta)-G(0)=-\delta^2F''(\gamma)+R,\qquad
  \|R\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac{\delta^4}{24}\max_{|t|\le\delta}\|G''''(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C(\gamma,\delta)\delta^4 .
\end{equation}
The range of $A$ lies in $\operatorname{span}\{\Phi,\Phi',\Phi''\}$, so
$\operatorname{rank}A\le3$. If $\Phi$ and $\Phi'$ are linearly independent,
let $v$ be the unit vector in $\operatorname{span}\{\Phi,\Phi'\}$ orthogonal
to $\Phi$; then $v^{\top}Av=-2(v\cdot\Phi')^2$ and
$(v\cdot\Phi')^2=|\Phi'|^2-(\Phi\cdot\Phi')^2/|\Phi|^2>0$, which gives the
bound for $a(\gamma)$ by the Rayleigh quotient \cite[Thm.~4.2.2]{HJ}.
Independence holds for $J\ge2$: with $x=(\gamma-T_0)/\sigma$,
$\phi_0'/\phi_0=-x/\sigma$ and $\phi_1'/\phi_1=(1/x-x)/\sigma$ differ
wherever $\phi_1\ne0$, while at $x=0$ one has $\phi_1=0\ne\phi_1'$ and
$\phi_0\ne0$. That $A$ also has positive eigenvalues is not used; it holds in
all computed cases (Proposition~\ref{prop:numconf}).
\end{proof}

For the four points $\tfrac12\pm\delta\pm i\gamma$ replacing double points at
$\tfrac12\pm i\gamma$, the change of $M_{\mathrm{zeros}}$ is
$P_\delta:=P(\gamma,\delta)+P(-\gamma,\delta)$, and by \eqref{eq:taylor}
\[
\|P_\delta-\delta^2A_*\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C_*\delta^4,\qquad
A_*=A(\gamma)+A(-\gamma),\quad C_*=C(\gamma,\delta)+C(-\gamma,\delta).
\]
We put $a_*=-\lmin(A_*)$; by Weyl's inequality $a_*\ge a(\gamma)-\|A(-\gamma)\|_{\mathrm{op}}$,
and $\|A(-\gamma)\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ and $C(-\gamma,\delta)$ are exponentially small
in $(\gamma+T_0)^2/\sigma^2$ (Remark~\ref{rem:locality}).

\subsection{Least eigenvalue of the perturbed form}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:forced}
Let $Q=\Qzero+P$ with $P=P(\gamma,\delta)$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:rank2}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item For every real symmetric $\Qzero$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forced}
  \lmin(Q)\ \le\ \lmin(P)+\|\Qzero\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \ \le\ -a(\gamma)\,\delta^{2}+C(\gamma,\delta)\delta^{4}+\|\Qzero\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}
\item If $\Qzero=0$, then $|\lmin(Q)+a(\gamma)\delta^2|\le C(\gamma,\delta)\delta^4$.
\item For $m$ displaced pairs with ordinates $\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_m$ and the
same $\delta$, the change is exactly $P=\sum_kP(\gamma_k,\delta)$, so
$\|P-\delta^2\sum_kA(\gamma_k)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\delta^4\sum_kC(\gamma_k,\delta)$ and
$\lmin(P)=\delta^{2}\lmin\bigl(\sum_kA(\gamma_k)\bigr)+O(\delta^{4})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Weyl's inequality $|\lmin(X+Y)-\lmin(X)|\le\|Y\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ for real
symmetric $X,Y$ \cite[Thm.~4.3.1]{HJ}, applied with $X=P$, $Y=\Qzero$, and
then with $X=\delta^2A(\gamma)$, $Y=R$, together with Lemma~\ref{lem:rank2}.
For (iii), $M_{\mathrm{zeros}}$ is additive over zeros, so there are no
cross terms.
\end{proof}

In (iii), negativity of $P$ for small $\delta$ requires
$\lmin\bigl(\sum_kA(\gamma_k)\bigr)<0$. This holds if some $v$ with
$v\perp\Phi(\gamma_k)$ for all $k$ has $v\cdot\Phi'(\gamma_k)\ne0$ for some
$k$, since then $v^{\top}A(\gamma_k)v=-2(v\cdot\Phi'(\gamma_k))^2\le0$ for
all $k$ with strict inequality for one; such $v$ exists when
$\Phi(\gamma_1),\dots,\Phi(\gamma_m)$ do not span $\mathbb R^J$ and the
condition on $\Phi'$ is met, e.g.\ for $J>m$ outside a degenerate set.

\begin{remark}[Locality and monotonicity in $J$]\label{rem:locality}
Since $|\phi_j^{(\ell)}(\gamma)|\le C_{j,\ell}\,\sigma^{-1/2-\ell}(1+|x|)^{j+\ell}e^{-x^2/2}$,
$x=(\gamma-T_0)/\sigma$, one has
$\|A(\gamma)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C_J\sigma^{-3}(1+|x|)^{2J+2}e^{-x^{2}}$, and
similarly for $C(\gamma,\delta)$ when $\delta\le\sigma$: the effect of a pair
at distance $\gg\sigma$ from $T_0$ is exponentially small, though not zero.
Moreover $A_J(\gamma)$ is the leading principal $J\times J$ block of
$A_{J+1}(\gamma)$, so by Cauchy interlacing \cite[Thm.~4.3.28]{HJ}
$a_J(\gamma)\le a_{J+1}(\gamma)$. At $(\gamma-T_0)/\sigma=0.47$, $\sigma=1$,
the computed values are $a_J=1.30,\,3.76,\,12.19,\,24.35,\,38.97$ for
$J=2,4,8,12,16$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Numerical check of Lemma~\ref{lem:rank2}]\label{prop:numconf}
For $T_0=46.13$, $\gamma=T_0+0.47\sigma$, $\sigma\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}$,
$J\in\{4,8,12,16,20\}$ and $\delta/\sigma\in\{10^{-2},10^{-3},10^{-4},10^{-5}\}$
($80$ cases), the computed $\lmin(P)$ satisfies
$|\lmin(P)+a(\gamma)\delta^2|\le C(\gamma,\delta)\delta^4$ in every case,
with $C$ evaluated by sampling, and the relative deviation
$|\lmin(P)+a\delta^2|/(a\delta^2)$ is at most $8.2\cdot10^{-4}$, $8.2\cdot10^{-6}$,
$8.2\cdot10^{-8}$, $8.2\cdot10^{-10}$ for the four values of $\delta/\sigma$.
For $J=8$, $\sigma=1$, $\gamma=46.6$ the spectrum of $A(\gamma)$ is
$\{-12.188,\,-2.392,\,0^{(5)},\,16.752\}$, so $A$ has rank $3$ and is
indefinite, and the lower bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:rank2} gives
$a(\gamma)\ge12.185$. For three and five pairs in $[44.1,47.9]$ ($J=8$,
$\delta=10^{-3}$), $\lmin(P)/\delta^2$ agrees with
$\lmin(\sum_kA(\gamma_k))$ ($-17.028$ and $-21.982$) to $5\cdot10^{-6}$
relative error. These are checks of the expansion, computed with
$50$-digit arithmetic by the scripts accompanying this paper.
\end{proposition}

\section{Truncation of the prime sum}\label{sec:trunc}

The cutoff $X$ in the prime sum produces the error $E_X$; this section
bounds it.

\begin{definition}[Truncation error]
For the arithmetic side of $\zeta$ let
\[
\eta(X)=\|E_X\|_{\mathrm{op}},\qquad
E_X=M^{(\infty)}_{\mathrm{prime}}-M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{prime}} .
\]
\end{definition}

With $h_{jk}=\phi_j\phi_k$ one has
$g_{jk}(u)=\frac1{2\pi}e^{-iT_0u}K_{jk}(\sigma u)$,
$K_{jk}(w)=\int\psi_j(x)\psi_k(x)e^{-iwx}\,dx$, and for $j\ge k$, $d=j-k$,
\[
K_{jk}(w)=\sqrt{k!/j!}\,\bigl(-iw/\sqrt2\bigr)^{d}e^{-w^2/4}L_k^{(d)}(w^2/2),
\]
with $L_k^{(d)}$ the Laguerre polynomial. Since
\[
L_k^{(d)}(t)=\sum_{i\le k}\binom{k+d}{k-i}\frac{(-t)^i}{i!},
\]
one has
$|K_{jk}(w)|\le p_{jk}(|w|)e^{-w^2/4}$ with
\[
p_{jk}(w)=\sqrt{k!/j!}\,(w/\sqrt2)^{d}L_k^{(d)}(-w^2/2),
\]
a polynomial with
non-negative coefficients of degree $j+k$. Hence
\[
\|g(\pm u)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sum_{j,k}|g_{jk}(\pm u)|\le G(u):=\Pi(u)e^{-\sigma^2u^2/4},
\qquad \Pi(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{j,k<J}p_{jk}(\sigma u),
\]
for $u\ge0$, with $\Pi$ of degree $2J-2$ and non-negative coefficients.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:trunc}
Let $c_0=1.03883$. For $X\ge2$ and $L=\log X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-bound}
  \eta(X)\ \le\ 2\sum_{n>X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,G(\log n)
  \ \le\ 2c_0\int_{L}^{\infty}e^{u/2}\Bigl(\tfrac12G(u)+|G'(u)|\Bigr)\,du ,
\end{equation}
and the right side is $\exp\bigl(-\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{4}L^{2}+\tfrac12L+O_{\sigma,J}(\log L)\bigr)$.
In particular, for every $\alpha<\sigma^2/4$,
$\eta(X)\le\Atr\exp(-\alpha L^2)$ with
$\Atr=\Atr(\alpha,\sigma,J)=2c_0\int_0^\infty e^{\alpha u^2+u/2}\bigl(\tfrac12G+|G'|\bigr)\,du<\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first inequality is the triangle inequality with the bound for
$\|g(\pm u)\|$. Put $f(x)=x^{-1/2}G(\log x)\ge0$. Partial summation gives,
for $Y>X$,
$\sum_{X<n\le Y}\Lambda(n)f(n)=\psi(Y)f(Y)-\psi(X)f(X)-\int_X^Y\psi(x)f'(x)\,dx$;
letting $Y\to\infty$ (the first term tends to $0$) and dropping
$-\psi(X)f(X)\le0$,
\[
\sum_{n>X}\Lambda(n)f(n)\le\int_X^\infty\psi(x)|f'(x)|\,dx\le c_0\int_X^\infty x|f'(x)|\,dx,
\]
by $\psi(x)<c_0x$ for $x>0$ \cite[Thm.~12]{RS62}. With $x=e^u$,
$x|f'(x)|\,dx=e^{u/2}|G'(u)-\tfrac12G(u)|\,du$, which gives
\eqref{eq:eta-bound}. Since $|G'(u)|\le(|\Pi'(u)|+\tfrac{\sigma^2u}{2}\Pi(u))e^{-\sigma^2u^2/4}$,
the integrand is $e^{u/2-\sigma^2u^2/4}$ times a polynomial, and
$\int_L^\infty e^{u/2-\sigma^2u^2/4}u^m\,du=\exp(-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{4}L^2+\tfrac12L+O(\log L))$;
the lower bound of the same order follows since $\tfrac12G+|G'|\ge\tfrac{\sigma^2u}{2}\Pi(u)e^{-\sigma^2u^2/4}-|\Pi'(u)|e^{-\sigma^2u^2/4}$.
Finally $e^{\alpha u^2}\ge e^{\alpha L^2}$ for $u\ge L$, so the right side of
\eqref{eq:eta-bound} is at most $e^{-\alpha L^2}\Atr$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Numerical comparison]\label{prop:eta-emp}
For $T_0=46.13$, $\sigma=1$ and $J\in\{8,10\}$, the computed $\eta(X)$ (prime
powers up to $2\cdot10^7$ standing for the complete sum) and the right side
of \eqref{eq:eta-bound} at $X=10^2,\dots,10^6$ are listed in the output of
the accompanying scripts; the bound holds in every case and exceeds $\eta(X)$
by a factor between $4\cdot10^{2}$ and $5\cdot10^{5}$; the same holds for
$T_0=85.7$, $\sigma=2$, $J=10$ at $X=10^{1/2},\dots,10^{5/2}$. At large $L$ the quantity
$-L^{-2}\log$ of the right side increases towards $\sigma^2/4$, as
Theorem~\ref{thm:trunc} predicts (for $\sigma=1$, $J=8$ it is $0.155$, $0.214$,
$0.236$, $0.245$ at $L=20,40,80,160$); over $X\le10^6$ it is still far below
$\sigma^2/4$ because of the polynomial factor and the term $\tfrac12L$.
\end{proposition}

\section{The model configuration}\label{sec:detector}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:detector}
Fix $(T_0,\sigma,J)$, $J\ge2$, $\gamma>0$, $\delta>0$, $\kappa\in(0,1)$ and
$\alpha<\sigma^2/4$. Let
$Q_X=M_{\mathrm{zeros}}(\mathcal Z_\delta)-M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{arith}}(\zeta)$,
where $\mathcal Z_\delta$ is the configuration of the introduction, and let
$a_*,C_*$ be as after Lemma~\ref{lem:rank2}. Then $Q_X=P_\delta+E_X$ and
\[
\lmin(Q_X)\le\lmin(P_\delta)+\eta(X)\le-a_*\delta^{2}+C_*\delta^{4}+\Atr e^{-\alpha(\log X)^{2}}.
\]
Consequently, if $a_*>0$, $C_*\delta^{2}\le\tfrac12(1-\kappa)a_*$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Xmin}
  X\ \ge\ \max(2,X_{\min}),\qquad X_{\min}
  \;=\;\exp\Bigl(\sqrt{\alpha^{-1}\log^{+}\bigl(\Atr/(\kappa\,a_*\delta^{2})\bigr)}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
then $\lmin(Q_X)\le-\tfrac12(1-\kappa)a_*\delta^{2}<0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The explicit formula for $\zeta$ gives
$M_{\mathrm{zeros}}(\zeta)-M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{arith}}(\zeta)=E_X$, and
$M_{\mathrm{zeros}}(\mathcal Z_\delta)=M_{\mathrm{zeros}}(\zeta)+P_\delta$
by the definition of $\mathcal Z_\delta$. Weyl's inequality, the bound after
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank2}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:trunc} give the first display.
For $X\ge\max(2,X_{\min})$, $\Atr e^{-\alpha(\log X)^2}\le\kappa a_*\delta^2$, hence
$\lmin(Q_X)\le-(1-\kappa)a_*\delta^2+\tfrac12(1-\kappa)a_*\delta^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For $\zeta$ itself $Q_X=E_X\to0$ whether or not RH holds. The theorem
concerns the response of the matrix to an inserted off-line pair and gives
no test of RH from prime data. Moreover, a genuine off-line zero of $\zeta$
would not by itself force $\lmin(M_{\mathrm{zeros}})<0$ for a fixed finite
basis: the negative direction of $2(aa^{\top}-bb^{\top})$ would also have to
be nearly orthogonal to $\Phi(\gamma_\rho)$ for the other zeros.
\end{remark}

\section{Fixed bases and the limit \texorpdfstring{$X\to\infty$}{X to infinity}}\label{sec:dichotomy}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dichotomy}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item For fixed $(T_0,\sigma,J)$ and any zero configuration used with the
arithmetic side of $\zeta$, and every $\alpha<\sigma^2/4$ and $X_0\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform}
  \sup_{X\ge X_0}\bigl|\lmin(Q_X)-\lmin(Q_\infty)\bigr|\ \le\ \Atr(\alpha,\sigma,J)\,e^{-\alpha(\log X_0)^2}\xrightarrow[X_0\to\infty]{}0 .
\end{equation}
\item If RH holds, then $M_{\mathrm{zeros}}(T_0,\sigma,J)\succeq0$ for all
$(T_0,\sigma,J)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) $Q_X-Q_\infty=E_X$; apply Weyl's inequality and Theorem~\ref{thm:trunc}.
(b) Under RH every $\gamma_\rho$ is real and \eqref{eq:Mzero} is a sum of
terms $\Phi\Phi^{\top}\succeq0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:LB}
If RH holds, then for every $(T_0,\sigma,J)$, every $\alpha<\sigma^2/4$ and
every $X\ge2$,
\[
\lmin\bigl(M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{arith}}(\zeta)\bigr)\ge-\Atr(\alpha,\sigma,J)\,e^{-\alpha(\log X)^2}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$M^{(X)}_{\mathrm{arith}}=M_{\mathrm{zeros}}-E_X$; use
Proposition~\ref{prop:dichotomy}(b) and Weyl's inequality.
\end{proof}

The converse of Proposition~\ref{prop:dichotomy}(b) would follow from density
of the functions $\sum_jc_j\phi_j$ (over all $T_0,\sigma,J$) in a class for
which Weil's criterion is known, together with continuity of $B$ on that
class; we do not prove this. The constant $\Atr(\alpha,\sigma,J)$ is not
bounded as $J\to\infty$ or $\sigma\to0$, so the truncation estimate gives no
information uniform in the basis; this non-uniformity, and not the estimate
\eqref{eq:uniform} for a fixed basis, is where the difficulty of Weil
positivity lies. Structural approaches to positivity include the trace
formula of Connes \cite{Connes1999} and its archimedean analysis by Connes
and Consani \cite{bgCC21}, and de~Branges' theory of Hilbert spaces of entire
functions \cite{deBranges}.

\section{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{The model configuration.}
All quantitative statements concern $\mathcal Z_\delta$, where the off-line
pair is inserted by hand and the arithmetic side is that of $\zeta$, held
fixed.

\paragraph{Davenport--Heilbronn.}
The Davenport--Heilbronn function $L_{\mathrm{DH}}(s)=\sum a_nn^{-s}$, studied
in Paper~04 of this series \cite{P04}, has off-line zeros but no Euler
product. Since $a_1=1$, $-L_{\mathrm{DH}}'/L_{\mathrm{DH}}=\sum_n\Lambda_{\mathrm{DH}}(n)n^{-s}$
with $\Lambda_{\mathrm{DH}}=(a\log)*a^{-1}$ (Dirichlet convolution with the
Dirichlet inverse), uniquely determined but not supported on prime powers;
its arithmetic side is
$-\sum_n\Lambda_{\mathrm{DH}}(n)n^{-1/2}(g(\log n)+g(-\log n))$ plus the
archimedean term of its functional equation (there is no polar term,
$L_{\mathrm{DH}}$ being entire), and Theorem~\ref{thm:trunc} does not apply
without a bound for $\sum_{n\le x}|\Lambda_{\mathrm{DH}}(n)|$. We do not treat
it here.

\paragraph{No bearing on RH.}
See the remark after Theorem~\ref{thm:detector} and
Section~\ref{sec:dichotomy}.
