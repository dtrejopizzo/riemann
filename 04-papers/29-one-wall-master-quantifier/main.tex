\title[One Wall in Many Coordinates]{One wall in many coordinates:\\
a heuristic synthesis of the propagation trichotomy\\
and a master quantifier for the Riemann Hypothesis}

\author{David Alejandro Trejo Pizzo}
\thanks{Independent Researcher. \texttt{dtrejopizzo@gmail.com}}


\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
The papers \cite{P27,P28} discussed three argument-forms for the Riemann
Hypothesis (RH) that the heuristic survey of one-dimensional propagation in
\cite{P27} does not cover: two-dimensional propagation (Form~A), index
amplification (Form~B), and an effective self-approximation criterion
(Form~C). Heuristic arguments in \cite{P28} indicate that Forms~A and~C fail;
Form~B reduces to a bridge lemma that is open and, under the hypothesis of
finitely many off-line zeros, equivalent to RH. We collect these statements,
with their exact status, and propose a heuristic synthesis: in each case the
object that is controlled unconditionally is an \emph{averaged},
\emph{generic}, or \emph{interior} quantity, and RH is, heuristically or under
the finiteness hypothesis, equivalent to an
\emph{individual}, \emph{uniform}, or \emph{boundary} refinement of it, so
that the gap is the inversion of a single quantifier. We argue,
heuristically, that the same quantifier inversion underlies the parity
phenomenon of sieve theory, the failure of the Euler product to propagate
information into the critical strip, the Hadamard barrier, and the gap in
Bagchi's recurrence criterion. A formulation suggested by the amplification
analysis distinguishes two kinds of autonomy of a quadratic functional: its
\emph{value} can be computed from the pole, the primes and the archimedean
factor, without the zeros (a theorem of \cite{P28}),
while we argue heuristically that its \emph{inertia} cannot be determined
without the positions of the zeros. We also list the results available in
\cite{P24,P25,P27,P28} with their hypotheses: the single-scale trace
criterion (a restatement of $\dnu=0$), the classical tensor index identity
$\negind(Q_1\otimes Q_2)=p_1q_2+q_1p_2$ and the amplification it gives under
finitely many off-line zeros, and a two-variable explicit formula. No new
theorem toward RH is proved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Setting}
Papers \cite{P27} and \cite{P28} studied three argument-forms for RH in the
setting of the Weil form and the scaling operator of \cite{P25}, with the
Connes--Consani--Moscovici zeta spectral triples \cite{CCM25} as background.
The survey \cite{P27} discussed heuristically why the classical
one-dimensional propagation mechanisms do not pass from the interior of the
known one-parameter reformulations of RH to their boundary point, and
identified three argument-forms not covered by its discussion:
two-dimensional propagation (Form~A, \cite[Problem~6.1]{P27}), index
amplification (Form~B, \cite[Problem~7.1]{P27}), and an effective version of
Bagchi's self-approximation criterion (Form~C, \cite[Problem~8.1]{P27}).
The paper \cite{P28} gave heuristic arguments that Forms~A and~C fail
\cite[Heuristics~2.1, 2.2, 3.1]{P28}, proved the index amplification and a
two-variable explicit formula for Form~B under the hypothesis of finitely many
off-line zeros, and reduced Form~B to the sieve-to-index bridge
\cite[Missing Lemma~6.1]{P28}, which is open and which, under that hypothesis,
is equivalent to RH.

This paper does two things. First (\S\ref{sec:trichotomy}), it records the
status of the three forms. Second (\S\ref{sec:master}--\S\ref{sec:autonomy}),
it proposes a heuristic description of what the three have in common, as the
inversion of a single quantifier, and a formulation of it in terms of value
and inertia of a quadratic form. The remaining sections list the available
results with their hypotheses (\S\ref{sec:ledger}) and discuss what this
description suggests about the methods considered (\S\ref{sec:boundary}).

\subsection{What is claimed}
We claim no proof of RH and no conditional result. The central claim is
stated as a heuristic principle (Principle~\ref{prin:master}), not a theorem.
The proved inputs are taken from \cite{P24,P25,P27,P28} and are stated with
their hypotheses; the statements on Forms~A and~C, and the inertia part of
\S\ref{sec:autonomy}, are heuristic.

\subsection{Framework}
We use the notation of \cite[\S1.3]{P28}: $\hat h(s)=\int_0^\infty
h(x)x^{s-1}\,dx$, $\tilde h(x)=\overline{h(1/x)}\,x^{-1}$,
$(f*g)(x)=\int_0^\infty f(y)g(x/y)\,dy/y$ the multiplicative convolution,
$W_\infty$ the archimedean term, $Q(f,g)=\sum_\rho\widehat{f*\tilde g}(\rho)$
the Weil form on $C_c^\infty(\bbR_+^*)$, and $(H_Cf)(x)=i(xf'(x)+\tfrac12f(x))$
the scaling operator.
\emph{Hypothesis~D} \cite[Def.~2.4]{P25}: the zeros of $\zeta$ off the
critical line form exactly $m<\infty$ quartets
$\{\tfrac12\pm b_j\pm i\gamma_j\}$; we allow $m=0$, which is RH. Under
Hypothesis~D with $m\ge1$ the Weil form $Q$ has negative index $\kappa(Q)=2m$,
the completion of the test space is a Pontryagin space whose finite part
$\Koff\cong\bbC^{4m}$, indexed by the off-line zeros, carries a form
$Q|_{\Koff}$ of signature $(2m,2m)$ \cite[Prop.~4.7]{P25}, and the scaling
operator $H_C$ has a unique $Q$-self-adjoint extension, with
$\negind(H_C)=2m$ eigenvalues in the open upper half-plane \cite[Prop.~5.3]{P25}. Without Hypothesis~D none of this is
available. The discrepancy measure $\dnu=d m_{\mathrm{full}}-d m_{\mathrm{on}}$
compares $|\zeta|^2$ with the square of its on-line projection against the
archimedean spectral measure; it is non-negative and $\RH\iff\dnu=0$
\cite[Def.~2.1, Cor.~3.2]{P24}, a restatement of RH. For $\lambda>0$,
$N(\lambda)$ is the number of zero ordinates in $(0,\lambda]$, $W_\lambda$ is
the Christoffel-type kernel of \cite[(2)]{P27} built from the orthonormal
polynomials of the archimedean measure, and $\Tl=\int W_\lambda\,\dnu$. The
two-variable functional is
$\Wtwo(h)=\sum_{\rho_1,\rho_2}\hat h(\rho_1,\rho_2)$ on
$C_c^\infty((\bbR_+^*)^2)$, with quadratic form
$Q_2(h,h')=\Wtwo(h*\tilde h')$ \cite[\S5]{P28}; on the thickened diagonal
$h(x_1,x_2)=g(x_1x_2)\varphi(x_1/x_2)$, $g,\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\bbR_+^*)$
\cite[(1)]{P28}. We write $\La$ for the von Mangoldt function and $\LDBN$
for the de~Bruijn--Newman constant. Following \cite[\S1.3]{P28},
$\mathcal E(h)=\hat h(0)+\hat h(1)-\sum_n\La(n)[h(n)+n^{-1}h(1/n)]-W_\infty(h)$
is the arithmetic side of the Weil explicit formula,
$\sum_\rho\hat h(\rho)=\mathcal E(h)$. For a non-degenerate Hermitian form
$\psi$ on a finite-dimensional space, $\kappa(\psi)$ is its negative index and
$\psi^{\otimes k}$ its $k$-th tensor power. For $k\ge1$,
$Q_k(h,h')=\mathcal W_k(h*\tilde h')$ with
$\mathcal W_k(h)=\sum_{\rho_1,\dots,\rho_k}\hat h(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_k)$ on
$C_c^\infty((\bbR_+^*)^k)$, and under Hypothesis~D $Q_k^{\mathrm{rel}}$ is its
off-line block (the sum over $k$-tuples of off-line zeros), with
$\kappa(Q_k^{\mathrm{rel}})=\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})$ for
$\psi=(\Koff,Q|_{\Koff})$ \cite[\S6.1]{P28}.

\section{The three forms}\label{sec:trichotomy}

We restate the status of the three forms, citing their sources. The
statements on Forms~C and~A are heuristic in \cite{P28} and remain heuristic
here.

\begin{heuristic}[Form~C: the amplifier appears self-blocking {\cite[Heuristics~2.1, 2.2]{P28}}]
\label{thm:C}
Let $\delta(d,\varepsilon)$ be the lower density of admissible shifts in the
self-approximation property $\mathrm{SR}_d$ with accuracy $\varepsilon$
\cite[\S8]{P27}. One expects that $\delta(d,\varepsilon)$ stays bounded below
as $d\to0$ while the first admissible shift grows like $1/|d|$. The
Rouch\'e replication of one off-critical zero $\rho_0=\beta_0+i\gamma_0$
happens only in the window $J_d=\{\tau:d\tau\approx\gamma_0\}$, where the
statement required is of the same kind as strong recurrence $\mathrm{SR}_0$,
which is equivalent to RH \cite{Bagchi}; and a mobile choice $d=d(T)\to0$
either freezes the slow copy or yields only $o(T)$ zeros, which is compatible
with the zero-density bound $N(\sigma,T)=O(T^{A(\sigma)})$ \cite[Ch.~IX]{bgTitchmarsh}.
\end{heuristic}

\begin{heuristic}[Form~A: degeneration {\cite[Heuristic~3.1]{P28}}]
\label{thm:A}
Let $\mathcal{T}(t,x)$ be a joint quantity built from the finite Euler
approximant indexed by $x$ and evolved by the de~Bruijn--Newman heat flow to
time $t$ \cite[Problem~6.1]{P27}; it is not defined in \cite{P27}. Since the
forward flow preserves reality of zeros \cite{deBruijn50}, any $\mathcal{T}$
that measures only the non-reality of the zeros vanishes for $t\ge\LDBN$ and
gives no input from that edge to a coupling inequality; a useful $\mathcal T$
would have to measure something else, and no such quantity is known.
\end{heuristic}

\begin{proposition}[Form~B: what is proved {\cite{P28}}]
\label{thm:B}
Assume Hypothesis~D with $m\ge1$. Then $\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})=\tfrac12(4m)^k$
for $\psi=(\Koff,Q|_{\Koff})$ \cite[Thm~4.2]{P28}; the off-line block of
$Q_2$ on thickened diagonals realises $\psi^{\otimes2}$ with signature
$(8m^2,8m^2)$ \cite[Thm~5.1]{P28}; and $\Wtwo(h)=(\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal
E)(h)$ is computed from pole, prime and archimedean terms alone
\cite[Thms~5.3, 5.4]{P28}. Under Hypothesis~D with $m\ge0$ (finitely many
off-line quartets, possibly none), the sieve-to-index bridge
\cite[Missing Lemma~6.1]{P28}, an $m$-independent bound
$\kappa(Q_k^{\mathrm{rel}})\le f(k)$ with $f(k)=o(k)$ derived from the
arithmetic side, is open and its conclusion is equivalent to RH
\cite[\S6.1]{P28}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each assertion is the cited statement of \cite{P28}. The equivalence holds
because $\kappa(Q_k^{\mathrm{rel}})\in\{0\}\cup[\tfrac12 4^k,\infty)$ by
\cite[Thm~4.2 and \S6.1]{P28}, so a bound $f(k)=o(k)$ independent of $m$ holds if and
only if $m=0$.
\end{proof}

The bound required by the bridge concerns the relative negative index, which
under Hypothesis~D is $0$ or at least $\tfrac12 4^k$; so a bound $f(k)=o(k)$
forces $m=0$ as soon as $f(k)<\tfrac12 4^k$, that is, for all large $k$. The
difficulty is therefore entirely in deriving such a bound from the arithmetic
side. Whether an arithmetic derivation of such a bound needs only an upper
bound for the almost-prime functional of \cite[Thm~5.4]{P28} or its
asymptotic evaluation is left open in \cite[Remark~6.2]{P28}.

The three forms fail, or stall, at three apparently different places: a
matching of density exponents (C), an invariance of the heat flow (A), a
dichotomy of the relative index (B). The rest of the paper argues,
heuristically, that they are the same place.

\section{The master quantifier}\label{sec:master}

\subsection{The common shape}
In each form, separate the object that is unconditionally controlled from
the RH-type statement.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
\begin{tabular}{lp{5.5cm}p{5.5cm}}
\textbf{Form} & \textbf{Unconditionally controlled} & \textbf{RH-type statement}\\
\hline
C & zero density \emph{on average}, $N(\sigma,T)=O(T^{A(\sigma)})$ & replication in the \emph{individual} window $J_d$\\
A & the flow on the \emph{interior} / forward region $t\ge\LDBN$ & the corner $(0,\infty)$ at the \emph{boundary}\\
B & the functional value, computed without the zeros & the relative index \emph{uniformly} in $k$\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The identification with RH is heuristic in row C, depends on an undefined
quantity in row A, and holds under Hypothesis~D in row B.

In all three the controlled quantity is an average, a generic value, or an
interior value; the RH-type statement concerns the corresponding individual,
specific, or boundary refinement. The gap between the two columns is, in each
row, the inversion of one quantifier.

\begin{principle}[The master quantifier]\label{prin:master}
In the settings of \cite{P24,P27,P28}, RH is, heuristically or under Hypothesis~D, equivalent to a statement
obtained from an unconditional theorem by inverting a single quantifier:
\begin{gather*}
\text{``for almost all / on average / in the interior''}\\
\longrightarrow\;
\text{``for the specific / uniformly / at the boundary.''}
\end{gather*}
The unconditional methods considered there certify the averaged side and not
the refined side. Heuristically, this is because the favourable direction of
RH is an exact-zero event for the specific arithmetic object, which averaged
information does not detect.
\end{principle}

Principle~\ref{prin:master} is not a theorem; it is a description suggested
by the three forms.

\subsection{Other instances}
The same quantifier inversion can be seen, heuristically, in several known
obstructions.

\emph{Sieve parity.} Sieve methods control counts of integers with given
congruence data \emph{on average over residue classes}; asymptotics for
sequences weighted by $(-1)^{\Omega(n)}$ require distinguishing the parity of
the number of prime factors for the individual sequence. Selberg's parity
phenomenon is the statement that sieve data alone cannot do this
\cite{bgFI}.

\emph{Euler product region.} The Euler product controls $\zeta$ in the
half-plane $\Real(s)>1$; the zeros are individual global objects in the
strip, attached to no single prime. Heuristically, the information does not
propagate because it is averaged information about a region, not pointwise
information about a locus.

\emph{The Hadamard barrier.} In the sense of \cite[Def.~7.1]{P24}: the
non-negativity $\dnu\ge0$ is available unconditionally, while its vanishing,
the \emph{individual} statement, is RH; the reformulations of
\cite[Thm~4.1]{P24} are restatements of $\dnu=0$ \cite[Rem.~7.2]{P24}.

\emph{Bagchi's criterion.} The Kronecker flow on the Bohr torus is uniquely
ergodic, so the arithmetic point is uniformly recurrent unconditionally
\cite[\S5.5]{P27}; RH is equivalent to strong recurrence of $\zeta$ itself
\cite{Bagchi}, a statement about one specific function rather than about the
generic behaviour of the flow.

\begin{remark}\label{obs:collapse}
Heuristically, these four obstructions and the three forms of
\S\ref{sec:trichotomy} are instances of Principle~\ref{prin:master}. We do
not claim that they are equivalent in any precise sense.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Why the inversion is hard in this way}
An averaged statement and its individual refinement can differ by the
behaviour on a null set, and the favourable direction of RH asks that the
off-critical count be not merely small but exactly zero. Exact vanishing can
of course be proved from inequalities: the single-scale criterion
\cite[Thm~4.4]{P27} deduces $\dnu=0$ from the vanishing of the integral of a
non-negative integrand. What is missing in the cases above is an inequality
of this kind whose input is unconditional. We know of no method among those
considered here, majorisation, equidistribution, or density estimates, that
certifies the exact-zero event from averaged data alone; the bounds they
give are compatible with both RH and its negation.

\section{Autonomy of value versus autonomy of inertia}\label{sec:autonomy}

The amplification analysis of \cite{P28} suggests a sharper formulation, which
we isolate here.

\begin{definition}
Let $Q_2$ be the quadratic form of $\Wtwo$ on the thickened-diagonal test
space. We say the \emph{value} of $Q_2$ is autonomous if $Q_2(h,h)$ is
computable from arithmetic data (primes, pole, gamma factor) without the
positions of the zeros of $\zeta$. Under Hypothesis~D, we say the
\emph{inertia} of $Q_2$ is autonomous if the partition of its negative
directions into the off-line block (the pull-back of
$(Q|_{\Koff})^{\otimes2}$) and the remainder is so computable.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Autonomy of value {\cite[Thms~5.3, 5.4]{P28}}]
\label{thm:autonomy}
For $h\in C_c^\infty((\bbR_+^*)^2)$, $\Wtwo(h)=(\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal
E)(h)$, a finite combination of pole, prime and archimedean terms in each
variable with no zeros of $\zeta$; for the thickened diagonal the first of
the four primes$\times$primes sums is
\[
\sum_{N\ge1} g(N)\sum_{d\mid N}\La(d)\La(N/d)\varphi(d^2/N).
\]
Hence the value of $Q_2$ is autonomous.
\end{proposition}

\begin{heuristic}[Inertia is not autonomous]\label{rem:inertia}
Under Hypothesis~D with $m\ge1$, the tensor square of the completed space
$(\mathcal K,Q)$ has infinite negative index (an infinite positive part
tensored with a negative direction), so only the relative block, of index
$\tfrac12(4m)^2$, is finite \cite[after Thm~4.2]{P28}. Isolating that block
from the off-line$\times$on-line part requires knowing which zeros are off
the line, and we know no test-function construction that does it from the
arithmetic side alone. In this sense the inertia of $Q_2$ does not appear to
be autonomous. This is the Hadamard barrier in a finer form: one can write
down a position-blind functional whose value encodes the zeros (Proposition
\ref{thm:autonomy}), but reading its index, the invariant that detects $m$,
seems to require the partition into blocks, which depends on the positions of
the zeros. The amplification route needs the index, not the value.
\end{heuristic}

\begin{remark}\label{obs:value-inertia}
Heuristically, value/inertia is the master quantifier in operator-theoretic
terms: the value is a trace-like, averaged datum; the inertia is a
spectral-resolution, individual datum. A single off-line quartet contributes
a full set of negative directions to the inertia, while its effect on the
value is mixed with the contribution of all other zeros.
\end{remark}

\section{Available results}\label{sec:ledger}

We list the results, with sources and hypotheses, that the discussion uses.

\begin{enumerate}[\rm(L1)]
\item \emph{Non-negativity of the discrepancy:} $\dnu\ge0$ as a measure, and
$\dnu=0\iff\RH$ \cite[Cor.~3.2]{P24} (a reformulation, not a step toward RH).
\item \emph{Single-scale trace criterion:} for $N(\lambda)\ge1$ the Christoffel-type
kernel $W_\lambda$ is strictly positive at every real point \cite[Thm~4.2]{P27},
whence $\RH\iff T_{\lambda_0}=0$ for a single $\lambda_0$ with
$N(\lambda_0)\ge1$ \cite[Thm~4.4]{P27}; by \cite{P27} this is a trivial
restatement of $\dnu=0$.
\item \emph{Tensor index identity:} for non-degenerate Hermitian forms of
signatures $(p_i,q_i)$, $\negind(Q_1\otimes Q_2)=p_1q_2+q_1p_2$ (the
classical multiplicativity of signature, see \cite[Ch.~II]{Lam}, and
\cite[Thm~4.1]{P28}); under Hypothesis~D with $m\ge1$ it gives
$\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})=\tfrac12(4m)^k$ \cite[Thm~4.2]{P28}.
\item \emph{Realisation of the tensor square:} under Hypothesis~D the
off-line block of the two-variable form on the thickened diagonal realises
$(\Koff,Q|_{\Koff})^{\otimes2}$ with signature $(8m^2,8m^2)$, i.e.\ $(8,8)$
for one quartet \cite[Thm~5.1]{P28}.
\item \emph{Sub-resolution blindness (model statement):} in the model of
window forms of \cite[Def.~4.3]{P26} (whose assumptions (S1)--(S3) are not
proved for $\zeta$), if every quadruple is sub-resolution and blind
($\theta_j=0$), the symbol of the field of window forms is strictly positive although
$m=\infty$ \cite[Thm~4.10]{P26}. Whether the two-variable form $Q_2$ itself
is positive on tests of fixed support in high windows is open.
\item \emph{A boundary example:} for
$B(r)=\tfrac18\operatorname{sech}^{1/2}(r)(105\operatorname{sech}^4
r-78\operatorname{sech}^2 r+5)$ and $h_B(x)=x^{-1/2}B(\log x)$, the prime
series diverges like $(5\sqrt2/8)\log X$, so $h_B$ sits on the boundary of
the Weil class \cite[\S3.3]{P28}.
\item \emph{Reformulations:} the equivalent reformulations of RH as
functionals of $\dnu$ \cite[Thm~4.1]{P24}, all restatements of $\dnu=0$
\cite[Rem.~7.2]{P24}; and, under Hypothesis~D, the index dictionary
$\kappa(Q)=\negind(H_C)=2m$ \cite[Prop.~5.3]{P25}.
\item \emph{An open target weaker than RH:} a bound, from the arithmetic side,
on the relative negative index that is independent of $m$ would bound $m$
under Hypothesis~D: for instance $\kappa(Q_2^{\mathrm{rel}})\le C$ would give
$8m^2\le C$ by (L4). No such bound is known.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The boundary of the argument-forms considered}\label{sec:boundary}

\subsection{What the three forms suggest jointly}
The argument-forms considered here, propagation (one-dimensional in \cite{P27},
two-dimensional in Form~A), amplification (Form~B) and recurrence (Form~C and
Bagchi's criterion), all stall, in the discussion above, at an inversion of
the kind described in Principle~\ref{prin:master}. This is a heuristic
observation about these forms, not a theorem about all possible methods.

\begin{principle}[The boundary]\label{prin:boundary}
Heuristically, a proof of RH must certify an individual, uniform, or boundary
statement that its averaged, generic, or interior counterpart leaves
undetermined. None of the argument-forms discussed in \cite{P27,P28}
(propagation, amplification, recurrence) and in \S\ref{sec:master} (sieve,
positivity of $\dnu$) is known to do this.
\end{principle}

\subsection{Where such a mechanism might live}
We do not know the mechanism. Heuristic~\ref{rem:inertia} suggests a property
it should have: it should read the \emph{inertia} of an arithmetic functional,
not merely its value. In the function-field case, where the analogue of RH is
a theorem, the mechanism is cohomological: the finite-dimensionality of
$\ell$-adic cohomology locates the poles of the $L$-functions of the tensor
powers uniformly in $k$ \cite{bgDeligne}, and for curves the Hodge index
theorem supplies a signature directly. A cohomological invariant over
$\Spec\bbZ$ of this kind would supply the bound, or the inertia, without passing through averaged analytic data;
whether such an invariant exists is an open question of the
Connes--Consani programme \cite{bgConnes99,CCM25}, outside the analytic
methods considered here.

\section{Assessment}\label{sec:assessment}

Forms~A and~C of \cite{P27} are, by the heuristic arguments of \cite{P28},
unlikely to succeed as stated (Heuristics~\ref{thm:C} and~\ref{thm:A}); Form~B
is reduced to a bridge lemma that is open and, under Hypothesis~D, equivalent
to RH (Proposition~\ref{thm:B}). We have proposed a heuristic description of
a common obstruction, the inversion of a single quantifier from averaged to
individual (Principle~\ref{prin:master}), and a formulation of it as the
contrast between the autonomous value (Proposition~\ref{thm:autonomy}) and
the apparently non-autonomous inertia (Heuristic~\ref{rem:inertia}) of a
quadratic functional. No progress toward RH is claimed.
