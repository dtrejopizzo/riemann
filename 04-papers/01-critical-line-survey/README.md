# Paper 01 — The Critical Line: A Survey of Recent Progress on the Riemann Hypothesis

A survey of recent work on the Riemann hypothesis along three directions: the analytic
approach (including the Guth–Maynard large-value estimate for Dirichlet polynomials), the
spectral approach, and the algebro-geometric approach. It gives technical accounts of these
developments, assesses how much arithmetic content the proposed geometric models carry, and
lists the main conceptual obstacles. Its conclusion is that in each direction the problem
reduces to establishing a foundational positivity statement. The paper frames the programme
of the later papers in this folder.

## Main results

- Survey content throughout; the results reported are those of the cited literature
  (e.g. `thm:gm`, the Guth–Maynard bound) — CITED.
- Conditional statements and open problems from the literature are reported with their
  original status (CONDITIONAL / OPEN); the survey proves no new theorems.
- Synthesis: across the analytic, spectral and geometric approaches the remaining step is
  a positivity statement — ARGUED (an expository assessment, not a theorem).
