# Paper 01 — The Critical Line: A Survey of Recent Progress on the Riemann Hypothesis

An expository survey of recent work on the Riemann Hypothesis in three directions: analytic
(zero-free regions, zero density, zeros on the critical line), spectral (Hilbert–Pólya,
Connes, prolate operators, the modular bootstrap) and algebro-geometric (the Connes–Consani
programme). It contains no new theorems and no computations of its own; every result stated
is quoted from the literature with a reference.

## Contents

- Analytic results: explicit zero-free regions (Mossinghoff–Trudgian–Yang); the Guth–Maynard
  large value estimate for Dirichlet polynomials and the zero density bound
  N(σ,T) ≤ T^{30(1−σ)/13+o(1)} (`thm:gm`), with its consequences for primes in short
  intervals; the proportion 5/12 of zeros on the critical line; extreme values of ζ.
- Computation and statistics: verification of RH up to height 3·10^12 (Platt–Trudgian), pair
  correlation, random matrix statistics and their arithmetic lower-order terms
  (Bogomolny–Keating, Conrey–Snaith).
- Spectral approaches: Hilbert–Pólya, Berry–Keating, Connes's semilocal trace formula, the
  prolate operators of Connes–Consani–Moscovici, the modular bootstrap reformulation of
  Benjamin–Chang.
- The function-field model (Weil's proof through the Castelnuovo–Severi inequality) and the
  Connes–Consani programme over the sphere spectrum, with the steps that remain open.
- Criteria proved equivalent to RH: Weil, Li (and the Bombieri–Lagarias relation between
  them), de Bruijn–Newman (Λ ≥ 0 by Rodgers–Tao), Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte.
- A closing comparison, which is the author's assessment and not a theorem: two of the
  equivalent criteria are positivity statements, and the geometric and bootstrap programmes
  aim at positivity statements that are not known to imply RH.

## What is known and what is new

Everything in the paper is known and attributed. The selection, the comparison of the
approaches and the list of open problems are the author's.
