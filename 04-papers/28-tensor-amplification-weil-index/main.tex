\title[Tensor amplification of the Weil negative index]{Tensor amplification of the Weil
negative index\\ and a two-variable explicit formula\\ on thickened diagonals}

\author{David Alejandro Trejo Pizzo}
\thanks{Independent Researcher. \texttt{dtrejopizzo@gmail.com}}


\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
In a heuristic survey \cite{P27} we listed three open directions towards the Riemann
Hypothesis (RH) in the framework of the Weil quadratic form: two-dimensional propagation
(Form~A), index amplification (Form~B), and an effective version of Bagchi's self-approximation
criterion (Form~C). We give heuristic reasons why Forms~A and~C are unpromising, and we prove
the following statements about Form~B. The negative index of a tensor product of Hermitian forms
of signatures $(p_i,q_i)$ is $p_1q_2+q_1p_2$ (classical). Assuming that the zeros off the
critical line form $m<\infty$ quartets, the off-line block of the Weil form has signature
$(2m,2m)$ and its $k$-th tensor power has negative index $(4m)^k/2$. For the thickened diagonal
kernels $h(x_1,x_2)=g(x_1x_2)\varphi(x_1/x_2)$ the two-variable Mellin transform factors as
$\tfrac12\hat g(\tfrac{s_1+s_2}{2})\hat\varphi(\tfrac{s_1-s_2}{2})$, the off-line block of the
two-variable form on these kernels has signature $(8m^2,8m^2)$, the two-variable zero sum equals
the iterated one-variable explicit formula, and its primes$\times$primes term is the
$\varphi$-weighted convolution
$\sum_N g(N)\sum_{d\mid N}\Lambda(d)\Lambda(N/d)\varphi(d^2/N)$ plus three explicit finite
terms. The bound on the index of the tensor powers that would be needed to conclude (a
sieve-to-index bridge) is equivalent, under the finiteness assumption, to RH; we state it and discuss what it would require.
No proof of RH is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Where we stand}
The survey \cite{P27} discussed, without proving any no-go theorem, why several
one-dimensional propagation mechanisms (limit, analyticity, parabolic backward uniqueness,
monotonicity, ergodic recurrence) do not carry known unconditional statements to RH, and listed
three open directions: two-dimensional propagation (Form~A, \cite[Problem~6.1]{P27}), index
amplification in the manner of Deligne (Form~B, \cite[Problem~7.1]{P27}), and an effective
study of Bagchi's self-approximation family as the comparison parameter tends to zero (Form~C,
\cite[Problem~8.1]{P27}).

This paper examines the three directions. For Forms~A and~C we give heuristic reasons why they
are unpromising (Sections~\ref{sec:formC} and~\ref{sec:formA}); these sections contain no
theorems. For Form~B we prove the index computations and a two-variable explicit formula that a
realisation of the amplification would use (Sections~\ref{sec:formB}
and~\ref{sec:experiment}), and we state the remaining bridge, which under Hypothesis~D is equivalent to RH
(Section~\ref{sec:bridge}).

\subsection{What this paper proves, and does not}
Proved: the classical tensor signature formula (Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor-index}); the
amplification $\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})=(4m)^k/2$ of the off-line block, assuming finitely many
off-line zeros (Theorem~\ref{thm:amplify}); the closed formula for the index of tensor powers
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sterility}); the Mellin factorisation and the signature of the off-line
block on thickened diagonals (Theorem~\ref{thm:signature}); the two-variable explicit formula
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed}) and its primes$\times$primes term (Theorem~\ref{thm:almostprime}).
Heuristic: Heuristics~\ref{thm:formC-selfblock}, \ref{cor:mobile}
and~\ref{thm:formA-degeneration}. Open: Missing Lemma~\ref{ml:bridge}, which, as shown after
its statement, is, under Hypothesis~D, a reformulation of RH and not a weakening of it. We do not prove RH.

\subsection{Framework}\label{ssec:framework}
We follow the notation of \cite{P25}. Let $\Scal=C_c^\infty(\Rp)$, $\hat h(s)=\int_0^\infty
h(x)x^{s-1}\,dx$, $\tilde h(x)=\overline{h(1/x)}\,x^{-1}$, and $(f*g)(x)=\int_0^\infty
f(y)g(x/y)\,dy/y$. The explicit formula of Weil \cite{Weil52}, in the form of
Bombieri \cite{Bombieri00}, reads
\[
\Wcal(h):=\sum_\rho\hat h(\rho)=\Ecal(h),\qquad
\Ecal(h):=\hat h(0)+\hat h(1)-\sum_{n\ge1}\La(n)\bigl[h(n)+n^{-1}h(n^{-1})\bigr]-W_\infty(h),
\]
with $W_\infty$ the archimedean term of \cite[Def.~2.1]{P25}; the zero sum converges
absolutely for $h\in\Scal$. The Weil form is $Q(f,g)=\Wcal(f*\tilde g)$, and $\kappa(Q)$ denotes
its negative index; $\RH\iff\kappa(Q)=0$ \cite{Weil52}, \cite[Def.~2.3]{P25}. The scaling
operator is $(H_Cf)(x)=i(xf'(x)+\tfrac12f(x))$ \cite[Def.~3.1]{P25}; it acts on Mellin
transforms as multiplication by $i(\tfrac12-s)$, so that it is realised on zero coordinates
with eigenvalues $i(\tfrac12-\rho)$; this is the sense in which the spectral data of $H_C$ are
the zeros.

\emph{Hypothesis D} (\cite[Def.~2.4]{P25} for $m\ge1$; we also allow $m=0$, which is RH) says that the zeros off the critical line form exactly
$m<\infty$ quartets $\{\tfrac12\pm b_j\pm i\gamma_j\}$. Under it, \cite[Prop.~4.7]{P25}
shows that the completion $(\Kcal,Q)$ of $\Scal/\ker Q$ is the Pontryagin space
$\Kcal=\ell^2(Z_0;n_\rho)\oplus\bbC^{4m}$, through $Jf=(\hat f(\rho))_\rho$, where the finite part
$\Koff:=\bbC^{4m}$, indexed by the off-line zeros, carries the form
$\sum n_\rho x_\rho\overline{y_{1-\bar\rho}}$; it is an orthogonal sum of $2m$ hyperbolic planes,
so $Q|_{\Koff}$ has signature $(2m,2m)$ and $\kappa(Q)=2m$. Moreover $H_C$ has a unique
$Q$-self-adjoint extension in $\Kcal$, with $\negind(H_C)=2m$ \cite[Prop.~5.3]{P25}. Without
Hypothesis~D none of this is available. For the localized Weil form on even test functions the
off-line part has signature $(m,m)$ and the negative index is at most $m$
\cite[Thm.~3.1, Cor.~6.5]{P18}; in this paper we use only the full form of \cite{P25}.
We also recall that the discrepancy measure $d\nu\ge0$ of \cite[Cor.~3.2]{P24} satisfies
$\RH\iff d\nu=0$, and that the single-scale trace criterion \cite[Thm.~4.4]{P27} is a trivial
restatement of $d\nu=0$; neither is used below. The Connes--Consani--Moscovici zeta spectral
triples \cite{CCM25} are the background for the scaling operator but are not used.

\section{Form C: a heuristic discussion of self-approximation}\label{sec:formC}

\subsection{The proposal}
Bagchi showed that RH is equivalent to the strong recurrence of $\zeta$ \cite{Bagchi}. The
self-approximation statement $\mathrm{SR}_d$---$\zeta(s+i\tau)$ approximates $\zeta(s+id\tau)$
uniformly on a compact disk in the strip $\tfrac12<\Real s<1$ for a set of $\tau$ of positive
lower density---is proved unconditionally for irrational $d$ \cite{Nak09,Pan10}, while
$\mathrm{SR}_0$ is equivalent to RH \cite{Bagchi}. Problem~8.1 of \cite{P27} asked how the
constants of $\mathrm{SR}_d$ degrade as $d\to0$ through irrational values. We do not prove
anything about this question; we record a heuristic picture, in which the relevant quantities
are only described informally.

\begin{heuristic}[The amplifier appears self-blocking]\label{thm:formC-selfblock}
Let $\delta(d,\varepsilon)$ be the lower density of admissible shifts $\tau$ in
$\mathrm{SR}_d$ with accuracy $\varepsilon$, and let $T_0(d,\varepsilon)$ be the size of the
first admissible shift. One expects that $\delta(d,\varepsilon)$ stays bounded below as $d\to0$
while $T_0(d,\varepsilon)$ grows like $1/|d|$. In Bagchi's argument, strong recurrence and
Rouch\'e's theorem replicate one off-critical zero $\rho_0=\beta_0+i\gamma_0$ into a family of
zeros, which is then compared with a zero-density estimate $N(\sigma,T)=O(T^{A(\sigma)})$,
$\sigma>\tfrac12$. For $\mathrm{SR}_d$ with small $d$ the replication happens only in the window
$J_d=\{\tau:d\tau\approx\gamma_0\}$, where the slowly moving copy $\zeta(s+id\tau)$ is essentially
frozen; there the statement asks for approximation of a function with a zero in the disk by
$\zeta$ itself, which by Hurwitz's theorem is the same kind of statement as $\mathrm{SR}_0$.
We do not prove this.
\end{heuristic}

\begin{heuristic}\label{cor:mobile}
A choice $d=d(T)\to0$ does not seem to help: if $|d|T=O(1)$ the slow copy is frozen and
$\mathrm{SR}_{d(T)}$ behaves like $\mathrm{SR}_0$; if $|d|T\to\infty$ the anchor is lost and the
replication gives only $o(T)$ zeros, which is compatible with the zero-density bound.
\end{heuristic}

\begin{observation}
In this picture the statement that would make Form~C work (universality, in short intervals,
towards a target with a zero in the disk) is itself a form of strong recurrence, hence of RH
\cite{Bagchi}. We do not pursue it.
\end{observation}

\section{Form A: a heuristic discussion of two-dimensional propagation}\label{sec:formA}

\subsection{The proposal}
Form~A \cite[\S6]{P27} asks for a joint quantity $\mathcal{T}(t,x)$, built from the
finite-Euler approximant indexed by $x$ and evolved by the de~Bruijn--Newman heat flow to time
$t$, with control on the edge $t\ge\LDBN$ ($\LDBN$ the de~Bruijn--Newman constant) and on the
edge of finite $x$, and a coupling inequality between $\partial_t\mathcal{T}$ and
$\partial_x\mathcal{T}$ that would propagate control to the corner $(t,x)=(0,\infty)$
\cite[Problem~6.1]{P27}. As stated in \cite{P27}, $\mathcal{T}$ is not defined; so there is no
theorem to prove about it.

\subsection{A limitation}

\begin{heuristic}[Degeneration]\label{thm:formA-degeneration}
de~Bruijn \cite[Thm.~13]{deBruijn50} shows that if $\Xi_{t_0}$ has only real zeros then so
does $\Xi_t$ for every $t\ge t_0$. Hence any trace that measures only the non-reality of the
zeros of $\Xi_t$ vanishes for $t\ge\LDBN$ and carries no information in the region $t\ge\LDBN$. A
quantity $\mathcal T$ of this kind gives no input from the edge $t\ge\LDBN$ to a coupling
inequality; a useful $\mathcal T$ would have to measure something other than the reality of
zeros, and we do not know such a quantity.
\end{heuristic}

The edge of finite $x$ concerns finite Euler approximants, a different family from $\Xi_t$; we
know no control on it either \cite[\S6]{P27}. The corner $(0,\infty)$ is then reached only
through the passage to the limit discussed in \cite[\S5.1]{P27}.

\subsection{A boundary example for the Weil class}
The following computation illustrates how a natural test kernel can sit on the boundary of
Weil's class. Let
\[
B(r)=\tfrac18\operatorname{sech}^{1/2}(r)\bigl(105\operatorname{sech}^4 r-78\operatorname{sech}^2
r+5\bigr),\qquad h_B(x)=x^{-1/2}B(\log x).
\]
Since $B$ is even, the prime term of $\Ecal(h_B)$ is $-2\sum_n\La(n)n^{-1/2}B(\log n)$. With
$s=\operatorname{sech}(\log n)=2n/(n^2+1)$, the polynomial $105s^4-78s^2+5$ has roots
$s^2\approx0.6720$ and $s^2\approx0.0709$, which correspond to $n\approx1.92$ and
$n\approx7.38$; hence $B(\log n)<0$ for $2\le n\le7$ and $B(\log n)>0$ for $n\ge8$. Because the
prime term enters with a minus sign, its increments are positive for $n\le7$ and negative for
$n\ge8$. Moreover
\[
\sum_{n\le X}\La(n)n^{-1/2}B(\log n)=\tfrac{5\sqrt2}{8}\log X+O(1).
\]
Indeed $\operatorname{sech}(\log n)=2n^{-1}(1+n^{-2})^{-1}$, so
$B(\log n)=\tfrac{5\sqrt2}{8}n^{-1/2}\bigl(1+O(n^{-2})\bigr)$, and the claim follows from
Mertens' estimate $\sum_{n\le X}\La(n)/n=\log X+O(1)$ \cite{bgMV} together with the
convergence of $\sum_n\La(n)n^{-3}$. Thus the prime series of $h_B$ diverges: $h_B$ decays like
$x^{-1}$ at infinity, the borderline rate (its Mellin transform converges only in
$0<\Real s<1$, with poles at $s=0$ and $s=1$), and any trace identity for $h_B$ can only hold formally.

\section{Form B: amplification of the negative index}\label{sec:formB}

\subsection{The Deligne skeleton}
A central step of Deligne's proof over finite fields \cite{Del74} is not a positivity argument:
an invariant (the weight), an amplification (tensor powers multiply the defect), a bound
growing more slowly than the amplification (rationality: the poles do not move), and the
conclusion that the defect vanishes \cite[\S7]{P27}. Under Hypothesis~D the Weil form supplies an
integer invariant, $\kappa(Q)=2m$, carried by $\Koff$ (\S\ref{ssec:framework}). Form~B asks for
the amplification and for the bound.

\subsection{The negative index under tensor products}
The falsifier of \cite[\S7]{P27} (``$\kappa$ is exactly additive under every available
product'') does not apply to the tensor product of Hermitian forms.

\begin{theorem}[Tensor index]\label{thm:tensor-index}
For Hermitian forms $Q_1,Q_2$ on finite-dimensional spaces, non-degenerate of signatures
$(p_1,q_1)$ and $(p_2,q_2)$,
\[
\negind(Q_1\otimes Q_2)=p_1q_2+q_1p_2.
\]
This exceeds $q_1+q_2$ if and only if $(p_1-1)q_2+(p_2-1)q_1>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In bases diagonalising each $Q_i$ to $\pm1$, the product basis diagonalises $Q_1\otimes Q_2$,
and a product vector is negative if and only if exactly one factor is negative, which gives
$p_1q_2+q_1p_2$ negative directions. The last assertion is
$p_1q_2+q_1p_2-(q_1+q_2)=(p_1-1)q_2+(p_2-1)q_1$.
\end{proof}

This is the classical multiplicativity of signature under tensor products (see e.g.\
\cite[Ch.~II]{Lam}).

\begin{theorem}[Exponential amplification on $\Koff$]\label{thm:amplify}
Assume Hypothesis~D with $m\ge1$. Then $\psi=(\Koff,Q|_{\Koff})$ has signature $(2m,2m)$, and
\[
\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})=\tfrac12(4m)^k\;\ge\;k\cdot2m=k\,\kappa(\psi)
\qquad(k\ge1),
\]
with equality only at $k=1$. If there are no off-line zeros, $\Koff=0$ and
$\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})=0$ for all $k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The signature is \cite[Prop.~4.7]{P25} (\S\ref{ssec:framework}). In a basis diagonalising
$Q|_{\Koff}$ to $2m$ entries $+1$ and $2m$ entries $-1$, the product basis of $\Koff^{\otimes k}$
diagonalises the tensor form, and a product vector is negative if and only if an odd number of
its factors are negative; by the symmetry $+1\leftrightarrow-1$ of the multiset of signs, this
is exactly half of the $(4m)^k$ product vectors. Finally $\tfrac12(4m)^k\ge 2mk$, with equality
only at $k=1$, since $\tfrac12(4m)^k/(2m)=(4m)^{k-1}\ge4^{k-1}>k$ for $k\ge2$.
\end{proof}

The tensor product of the whole of $\Kcal$ with itself has infinite negative index already at
$k=2$ (an infinite positive part tensored with a negative direction); the finite invariant is
the index of the off-line block $\Koff^{\otimes k}$, which we call the relative index.

\subsection{The sterility lemma}
Amplification alone gives nothing, because the index of the powers is determined by the
signature.

\begin{proposition}[Sterility]\label{prop:sterility}
Let $\psi$ be a non-degenerate Hermitian form of signature $(p,q)$ on a finite-dimensional space.
Then
\[
\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})=\tfrac12\bigl((p+q)^k-(p-q)^k\bigr).
\]
If $p,q\ge1$ then $\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})\ge kq$ for all $k$. Consequently a bound
$\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})\le f(k)$ with $f(k)=o(k)$, valid for all $k$, holds for a form with
$p\ge1$ if and only if $q=0$; such a bound carries exactly the information $q=0$ and must
come from a source other than the signature to give anything new.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A product vector of a diagonal basis is negative iff it has an odd number of negative
factors; the number of such sign patterns is the odd part of $(p+q)^k$ in the expansion of
$(p+(-1)q)^k$, i.e.\ $\tfrac12((p+q)^k-(p-q)^k)$. Writing $\kappa_k$, $\pi_k$ for the negative and
positive indices of $\psi^{\otimes k}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor-index} gives
$\kappa_{k+1}=p\kappa_k+q\pi_k\ge\kappa_k+q$ when $p\ge1$ (since $\pi_k\ge p^k\ge1$), whence
$\kappa_k\ge kq$. If $q=0$ then $\kappa_k=0$.
\end{proof}

In Deligne's proof the independent source is cohomological: the poles of the $L$-functions of
the tensor powers are located with an error uniform in $k$, by the finite-dimensionality of
$\ell$-adic cohomology. For the Weil form, a candidate independent source must come from the
arithmetic side of an explicit formula, so the tensor product must first be realised as a
functional with its own explicit formula. This is done in the next section.

\section{A two-variable explicit formula with a diagonal kernel}\label{sec:experiment}

\subsection{Setup}
For $h\in C_c^\infty((\Rp)^2)$ write
$\hat h(s_1,s_2)=\int_0^\infty\!\int_0^\infty h(x_1,x_2)x_1^{s_1-1}x_2^{s_2-1}\,dx_1\,dx_2$ and
define
\[
\Wtwo(h)=\sum_{\rho_1,\rho_2}\hat h(\rho_1,\rho_2),
\]
the sum over pairs of non-trivial zeros with multiplicity. Integrating by parts in each variable,
$|\hat h(\sigma_1+it_1,\sigma_2+it_2)|\le C_A(1+|t_1|)^{-A}(1+|t_2|)^{-A}$ uniformly for
$\sigma_1,\sigma_2\in[0,1]$, so the double series converges absolutely. On a product kernel
$h=f_1\otimes f_2$ one has $\Wtwo(h)=\Wcal(f_1)\Wcal(f_2)$; to see sums of zeros the variables
must be coupled.

\subsection{The thickened diagonal}
The naive diagonal $h(x_1,x_2)=g(x_1x_2)$ is not admissible: with $u=x_1x_2$, $v=x_1$, the inner
integral $\int_0^\infty v^{\rho_1-\rho_2-1}\,dv$ diverges. We use the thickened multiplicative
diagonal
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thick}
h(x_1,x_2)=g(x_1x_2)\,\varphi(x_1/x_2),\qquad
g,\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\bbR_+^*),
\end{equation}
which lies in $C_c^\infty((\Rp)^2)$. Let $\mathcal D$ be the linear span of such kernels. Under
Hypothesis~D, the off-line block of the two-variable form is
\[
Q_2^{\mathrm{off}}(h,h')=\sum_{\rho_1,\rho_2\ \text{off-line}}n_{\rho_1}n_{\rho_2}\,
\hat h(\rho_1,\rho_2)\,\overline{\hat h'(1-\bar\rho_1,1-\bar\rho_2)},
\]
i.e.\ the pull-back of the tensor square of $Q|_{\Koff}$ by $J_2h=(\hat h(\rho_1,\rho_2))$.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral side; the Minkowski sum]\label{thm:signature}
For $h$ as in \eqref{eq:thick},
\[
\hat h(s_1,s_2)=\tfrac12\,\hat g\!\Bigl(\tfrac{s_1+s_2}{2}\Bigr)\,
\hat\varphi\!\Bigl(\tfrac{s_1-s_2}{2}\Bigr),
\]
so that $\Wtwo(h)=\tfrac12\sum_{\rho_1,\rho_2}\hat g(\tfrac{\rho_1+\rho_2}{2})
\hat\varphi(\tfrac{\rho_1-\rho_2}{2})$: the zero term sees the Minkowski sum of zero pairs,
weighted in their difference. Under Hypothesis~D, $Q_2^{\mathrm{off}}$ restricted to
$\mathcal D$ has signature $(8m^2,8m^2)$; for one quartet ($m=1$) the $16$-dimensional block has
signature $(8,8)$, in agreement with Theorem~\ref{thm:amplify} for $k=2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $u=x_1x_2$, $v=x_1/x_2$, so $x_1=\sqrt{uv}$, $x_2=\sqrt{u/v}$,
$x_1^{s_1}x_2^{s_2}=u^{(s_1+s_2)/2}v^{(s_1-s_2)/2}$ and
$dx_1\,dx_2/(x_1x_2)=du\,dv/(2uv)$; this gives the factorisation.

For the signature, let $Z$ be the set of the $4m$ off-line zeros. The map $J_2:\mathcal
D\to\bbC^{Z\times Z}$ is surjective. Indeed, the pairs $(\rho_1,\rho_2)\in Z\times Z$ give points
$P=(a,b)=(\tfrac{\rho_1+\rho_2}{2},\tfrac{\rho_1-\rho_2}{2})$, distinct for distinct pairs since
$(\rho_1,\rho_2)\mapsto(a,b)$ is invertible. Let $A$, $B$ be the finite sets of first and second
coordinates. The functionals $g\mapsto\hat g(a)$, $a\in A$, are linearly independent on
$C_c^\infty(\Rp)$ (if $\sum c_a\hat g(a)=0$ for all $g$ then $\sum c_ax^{a-1}=0$ on $\Rp$, so
all $c_a=0$, the exponents being distinct); so there are $g_a$ with $\hat g_{a}(a')=\delta_{aa'}$
on $A$, and likewise $\varphi_b$ with $\hat\varphi_b(b')=\delta_{bb'}$ on $B$. Then
$h=2g_a(x_1x_2)\varphi_b(x_1/x_2)$ has $J_2h=e_{P}$. Now $Q_2^{\mathrm{off}}(h,h')=\langle
J_2h,J_2h'\rangle$, where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the tensor square of the form of
$\Koff$, which has signature $(2m,2m)$; by Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor-index} it has signature
$(8m^2,8m^2)$ on $\bbC^{Z\times Z}$ and is non-degenerate. A Hermitian form pulled back by a
surjective linear map has the same positive and negative indices (preimages of definite
subspaces contain definite subspaces of the same dimension, and a definite subspace upstairs
meets $\ker J_2$ trivially). Equivalently: the involution
$(\rho_1,\rho_2)\mapsto(1-\bar\rho_1,1-\bar\rho_2)$ has no fixed point on $Z\times Z$, so the
$(4m)^2$ coordinates split into $8m^2$ hyperbolic planes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The role of $\varphi$]
For one quartet $\rho\in\{\tfrac12\pm\delta\pm i\gamma_0\}$, the sums $\rho_i+\rho_j-1$ take the
values $\pm2\delta$, $0$, $\pm2i\gamma_0$ and $\pm2\delta\pm2i\gamma_0$. For the eight pairs
with $\rho_i+\rho_j-1\in i\bbR$, the off-criticality enters $\hat h$ only through $\hat\varphi$,
at $\Real\tfrac{\rho_i-\rho_j}{2}=\pm\delta$; so the difference variable is necessary to separate
those pairs.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The arithmetic side}
The two-variable zero sum does not involve mixed zeros$\times$primes terms: it is the iterated
one-variable explicit formula. Write $\Ecal_1$ and $\Ecal_2$ for the functional $\Ecal$ of
\S\ref{ssec:framework} applied in the variable $x_1$ or $x_2$.

\begin{theorem}[Two-variable explicit formula]\label{thm:mixed}
Let $h\in C_c^\infty((\Rp)^2)$. The function $F(x_1)=\Ecal_2[h(x_1,\cdot)]$ lies
in $C_c^\infty(\Rp)$, and
\[
\Wtwo(h)=\Ecal_1[F]=(\Ecal\otimes\Ecal)(h).
\]
Hence $\Wtwo(h)$ is a finite combination of pole, prime and archimedean terms in each variable,
and no zero of $\zeta$ appears on the right.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $x_1$, $x_2\mapsto h(x_1,x_2)$ lies in $C_c^\infty(\Rp)$, so the explicit formula
\cite{Weil52}, \cite[Def.~2.1]{P25} gives $\sum_{\rho_2}\hat h_2(x_1,\rho_2)=F(x_1)$, where
$\hat h_2(x_1,s)=\int_0^\infty h(x_1,x_2)x_2^{s-1}dx_2$. Each term of $\Ecal_2[h(x_1,\cdot)]$
vanishes when $x_1$ is outside the projection of $\operatorname{supp}h$; the prime sum is finite
and the archimedean integral may be differentiated in $x_1$ under the integral sign, so
$F\in C_c^\infty(\Rp)$. Integration by parts in $x_2$ gives
$|\hat h_2(x_1,\tfrac12+\beta+it)|\le C_A(1+|t|)^{-A}$ uniformly in $x_1$ and $|\beta|\le\tfrac12$,
so by dominated convergence
$\sum_{\rho_2}\hat h(\rho_1,\rho_2)=\int_0^\infty F(x_1)x_1^{\rho_1-1}dx_1=\hat F(\rho_1)$. Summing
over $\rho_1$ (the double series converges absolutely) and applying the explicit formula to
$F$ gives $\Wtwo(h)=\Ecal_1[F]$. By linearity of each term in $h$ this is
$(\Ecal\otimes\Ecal)(h)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Primes$\times$primes term]\label{thm:almostprime}
The primes$\times$primes part of $(\Ecal\otimes\Ecal)(h)$ is
\[
\sum_{n_1,n_2\ge1}\La(n_1)\La(n_2)\Bigl[h(n_1,n_2)+\tfrac{1}{n_2}h(n_1,\tfrac1{n_2})
+\tfrac1{n_1}h(\tfrac1{n_1},n_2)+\tfrac{1}{n_1n_2}h(\tfrac1{n_1},\tfrac1{n_2})\Bigr],
\]
with a plus sign. For the thickened diagonal \eqref{eq:thick} the first of the four sums equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:almostprime}
\sum_{N\ge1} g(N)\sum_{d\mid N}\La(d)\,\La(N/d)\,\varphi\!\bigl(d^2/N\bigr),
\end{equation}
a $\varphi$-weighted transform of the Dirichlet convolution $\La\star\La$ (the second part of
Selberg's $\La_2(N)=\La(N)\log N+(\La\star\La)(N)$), and the other three are
\[
\sum_{n_1,n_2}\tfrac{\La(n_1)\La(n_2)}{n_2}\,g\bigl(\tfrac{n_1}{n_2}\bigr)\varphi(n_1n_2),\quad
\sum_{n_1,n_2}\tfrac{\La(n_1)\La(n_2)}{n_1}\,g\bigl(\tfrac{n_2}{n_1}\bigr)\varphi\bigl(\tfrac1{n_1n_2}\bigr),\quad
\sum_{n_1,n_2}\tfrac{\La(n_1)\La(n_2)}{n_1n_2}\,g\bigl(\tfrac1{n_1n_2}\bigr)\varphi\bigl(\tfrac{n_2}{n_1}\bigr),
\]
in the first two of which $g$ is evaluated on a quotient and $\varphi$ on a product, while the
third is the image of \eqref{eq:almostprime} under $(n_1,n_2)\mapsto(1/n_1,1/n_2)$ with weight
$1/(n_1n_2)$. All four sums are finite, and they
are computed from the primes alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The prime part of $\Ecal$ is $-\sum_n\La(n)[f(n)+n^{-1}f(1/n)]$; its tensor square is the
displayed sum. For \eqref{eq:thick}, $h(n_1,n_2)=g(n_1n_2)\varphi(n_1/n_2)$; grouping by
$N=n_1n_2$, $d=n_1$, so that $n_1/n_2=d^2/N$, gives \eqref{eq:almostprime}. The other three
follow from $h(n_1,1/n_2)=g(n_1/n_2)\varphi(n_1n_2)$, $h(1/n_1,n_2)=g(n_2/n_1)\varphi(1/(n_1n_2))$
and $h(1/n_1,1/n_2)=g(1/(n_1n_2))\varphi(n_2/n_1)$. Finiteness follows from the compact supports
of $g$ and $\varphi$.
\end{proof}

The convolution $\La\star\La$ is supported on integers $p^aq^b$ with at most two distinct prime
factors, prime powers included. Theorems~\ref{thm:signature}--\ref{thm:almostprime} show that
$\Wtwo$ on thickened diagonals has a spectral side containing the tensor square of the
off-line block and an arithmetic side computed without the zeros; with the involution
$\tilde h(x_1,x_2)=\overline{h(1/x_1,1/x_2)}(x_1x_2)^{-1}$ and two-variable convolution,
$Q_2(h,h')=\Wtwo(h*\tilde h')$ has $Q_2^{\mathrm{off}}$ as its off-line block.

\section{The sieve-to-index bridge}\label{sec:bridge}

\subsection{The bridge statement}
For $k\ge1$ let $\mathcal W_k(h)=\sum_{\rho_1,\dots,\rho_k}\hat h(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_k)$ on
$C_c^\infty((\Rp)^k)$, whose arithmetic side is $\Ecal^{\otimes k}(h)$ by the argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed}, and let $Q_k(h,h')=\mathcal W_k(h*\tilde h')$. Under Hypothesis~D let
$Q_k^{\mathrm{rel}}$ be its off-line block (the sum over $k$-tuples of off-line zeros); by the
argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:signature}, its negative index on $C_c^\infty((\Rp)^k)$ is
$\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})=\tfrac12(4m)^k$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:sterility} an
independent bound on this index is needed, and the arithmetic side $\Ecal^{\otimes k}$, whose
primes$\times$primes part for $k=2$ is \eqref{eq:almostprime}, is the candidate source.

\begin{missinglemma}[The sieve-to-index bridge]\label{ml:bridge}
Prove, using only the arithmetic side $\Ecal^{\otimes k}$ (in particular sieve upper bounds on
the order-$k$ analogues of \eqref{eq:almostprime}), a bound
\[
\kappa\bigl(Q_k^{\mathrm{rel}}\bigr)\;\le\;f(k),\qquad f(k)/k\to0,
\]
with $f$ independent of $m$.
\end{missinglemma}

If proved, Missing Lemma~\ref{ml:bridge}, combined with the lower bound in the form
$\kappa(\psi^{\otimes k})\ge 2mk$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:amplify}), forces $2mk\le f(k)$
for all $k$, hence excludes $1\le m<\infty$: the off-line zeros would be absent or infinite
in number. It would not by itself give RH. We do not prove it.

\emph{Reformulation.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:amplify},
$\kappa(Q_k^{\mathrm{rel}})\in\{0\}\cup[\tfrac12 4^k,\infty)$, so an $m$-independent bound
$f(k)=o(k)$ holds if and only if $m=0$. Thus, within Hypothesis~D, Missing
Lemma~\ref{ml:bridge} is equivalent to RH; unconditionally it is equivalent to the statement
that the number of off-line zeros is zero or infinite, which RH implies. It is no easier than
RH restricted to Hypothesis~D. What it would add is
the route: the bound would have to be derived from the arithmetic side alone.

\subsection{Parity}
The parity phenomenon of sieve theory forbids sieves from distinguishing integers with an even
or odd number of prime factors, and hence from producing asymptotics for sequences detected by
such a sign \cite{bgFI}. Missing Lemma~\ref{ml:bridge}, as stated, asks for upper bounds on
almost-prime sums, which the Selberg sieve provides \cite{bgFI}, and for which combinatorial
decompositions of $\La$ (Vaughan, Heath-Brown) are available \cite{bgIK}; so parity is not an evident obstruction to the input. Whether such
bounds can control the negative index at all is the open question, and by the reformulation
above any control of the required strength is, under Hypothesis~D, equivalent to RH.

\begin{remark}[A possible obstruction]
If the index growth were governed by the main term of the almost-prime functional rather than
by a majorant, the bridge would require asymptotic evaluation of correlations of the type
$\sum\La(n)\La(n+h)$, the problem studied by Montgomery \cite{Mont73} and Goldston--Montgomery
\cite{GM87}. This is a problem of evaluation, not of sign. Whether the bridge needs a bound or
an asymptotic is an analytic question about \eqref{eq:almostprime} that we leave open.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The shape of a proof, and its falsifiers}
One possible shape of a proof of Missing Lemma~\ref{ml:bridge} has three steps, each of which
could fail:
\begin{enumerate}[\rm(1)]
\item \emph{Index from the functional.} Express $\kappa(Q_k^{\mathrm{rel}})$ through the
arithmetic side and bound it by the number of sign changes of the arithmetic kernel
\eqref{eq:almostprime}. If the index is not controlled by these sign changes (for instance if the
archimedean part dominates), the arithmetic side is irrelevant and the route fails.
\item \emph{Sieve majorant.} Bound the almost-prime functional by a Selberg majorant uniformly
in $k$. If the only available bound grows at least linearly in $k$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:sterility} is not overcome.
\item \emph{Uniformity.} Obtain $f(k)/k\to0$ with constants independent of $m$; otherwise the
bound cannot exclude all $m\ge1$.
\end{enumerate}
Step~(1) is the natural first target.

\section{Summary}\label{sec:assessment}

For Forms~A and~C we gave heuristic reasons, not theorems: Form~C appears to reduce to a
localised strong recurrence, itself equivalent to RH (Heuristic~\ref{thm:formC-selfblock}), and
a trace that only measures the non-reality of the zeros of $\Xi_t$ carries no information for
$t\ge\LDBN$ (Heuristic~\ref{thm:formA-degeneration}). For Form~B we proved that, under
Hypothesis~D, the negative index of the off-line block grows like $(4m)^k/2$ under tensor powers
(Theorem~\ref{thm:amplify}), that the tensor square is realised on thickened diagonals by a
two-variable functional of signature $(8m^2,8m^2)$ on its off-line block
(Theorem~\ref{thm:signature}), and that this functional obeys the iterated explicit formula,
whose primes$\times$primes term is the $\varphi$-weighted convolution \eqref{eq:almostprime}
(Theorems~\ref{thm:mixed}--\ref{thm:almostprime}). The bridge from the arithmetic side to a
sublinear bound on the index (Missing Lemma~\ref{ml:bridge}) is equivalent to RH under Hypothesis~D (unconditionally it
would only exclude a finite non-zero number of off-line zeros), and we make
no claim about its provability.
