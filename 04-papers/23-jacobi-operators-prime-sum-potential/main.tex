\title[Jacobi operators, prime potentials and the critical line]{Jacobi operators, a prime-sum
potential and the logarithmic derivative of zeta on the critical line}
\author{David Alejandro Trejo Pizzo}
\thanks{Independent Researcher. \texttt{dtrejopizzo@gmail.com}}


\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
Motivated by the Jacobi matrices that Connes, Consani and Moscovici attach to the Riemann zeta
function, we examine a proposed reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis in terms of a limit
Jacobi operator and an explicit potential $\Cinf=w-\Psi$, built from the archimedean symbol $w$
and the prime sum $\Psi$, together with an ``incidence inclusion'' $\incInv$ asserting that
$\Cinf$ vanishes at every real zero of $\Xi(t)=\xi(\half+it)$. We show that this reformulation
does not hold. The Abel boundary value of $\Cinf$ at the ordinate $\gamma$ of a zero of
multiplicity $m$ on the critical line diverges like $2m/\eta$, so $\incInv$ is false at every
such zero; by Hardy's theorem it is therefore false unconditionally. A Jacobi operator with
off-diagonal coefficients tending to zero is compact, so its spectrum cannot be an unbounded
zero set. What survives consists of elementary or classical facts, which we state with proofs:
the boundary divergence just described; a non-vanishing region for the truncated potential far
out in the half-plane $\Im z\ge\half+\varepsilon$; a local description of how pairs of zeros of
$\Clam-\mu$ leave the real axis at a non-degenerate critical point, with a lower bound for their
imaginary part; and the classical, unconditional identity
$\Re\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\half+it)=\half\log\pi-\half\Re\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2)=-\half w(t)$
for $\Xi(t)\neq0$, which holds for every configuration of zeros and does not involve the Riemann
Hypothesis. The only equivalence with the Riemann Hypothesis that remains is the trivial
restatement that all zeros of $\Xi$ are real.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A persistent strategy toward the Riemann Hypothesis ($\RH$) is to realize the ordinates of the
nontrivial zeros as the spectrum of a self-adjoint operator. Connes, Consani and Moscovici
\cite{CCM} construct self-adjoint operators, as rank-one perturbations of the spectral triple of
the scaling operator on $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$, that involve only the Euler factors of primes
up to $\lambda^2$. A natural attempt is to pass to the limit in this family,
obtain a limit Jacobi operator $\Jinf$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)$, identify its spectrum with the
real zero set of an explicit potential $\Cinf=w-\Psi$, and then express $\RH$ as the
``incidence inclusion'' $\incInv$: every real zero of $\Xi$ is a zero of $\Cinf$.

This paper examines that attempt and records what is actually true. The main points are the
following.
\begin{itemize}
\item The potential $\Cinf$ is defined on $\mathbb R$ only as an Abel boundary value. At the
ordinate of a zero of $\zeta$ of multiplicity $m$ on the critical line, this boundary value
diverges like $2m/\eta$ (Proposition~\ref{thm:B}). Hence $\incInv$ is false at every real zero of
$\Xi$, and, since $\Xi$ has infinitely many real zeros (Hardy), $\incInv$ is false
unconditionally (Remark~\ref{prop:three}).
\item If the off-diagonal coefficients of a Jacobi operator tend to zero, the operator is
compact (Proposition~\ref{thm:C}); its spectrum is bounded and cannot be the unbounded set of
ordinates of zeros. Its moment problem is determinate (Proposition~\ref{prop:moment}).
\item For the truncated potential $\Clam$ we prove a non-vanishing statement far out in the
half-plane $\Im z\ge\half+\varepsilon$ (Proposition~\ref{thm:zero-free}), a local description of
complex zeros of $\Clam-\mu$ near a non-degenerate real critical point
(Proposition~\ref{prop:geom}), and a lower bound for their imaginary part
(Proposition~\ref{prop:approach}).
\item The Hadamard expansion of $\zeta'/\zeta$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:had})
gives, for every $t$ with $\Xi(t)\ne0$, the classical identity
\[
\Re\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\big(\tfrac12+it\big)=\tfrac12\log\pi-\tfrac12\Re\psi\big(\tfrac14+
\tfrac{it}2\big)=-\tfrac12w(t)
\]
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-implies-ii}). It holds for every configuration of zeros; the real part of
the zero sum cancels by the symmetry $\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho$, not because of $\RH$. Thus the
relation $\Re[-\zeta'/\zeta(\half+it)]=w(t)/2$ holds at every $t$ with $\Xi(t)\ne0$, which is
why it carries no information about $\RH$.
\end{itemize}
Several natural statements of such a reformulation are false or unproved (the spectral
identification, the inclusion $\{\Cinf=0\}\subseteq\{\Xi=0\}$, the claim that $\incInv$ reformulates $\RH$, and a derivation of $\RH$ from linear independence and simplicity); each is
discussed in a remark with the reason it fails. The only equivalence with $\RH$ in this paper is the trivial
one: $\RH$ holds if and only if all zeros of $\Xi$ are real, which is the definition.

\section{The CCM construction and Jacobi operators}\label{sec:ccm}

\subsection{The finite-dimensional setting}
Fix $\lambda>1$ and an integer $N\ge1$, and set $L=2\log\lambda$. Let
$\mathcal H_\lambda=L^2([\lambda^{-1},\lambda],d^\ast u)$ with $d^\ast u=du/u$, and let
$V_n(u)=L^{-1/2}e^{2\pi in\log(\lambda u)/L}$, $-(N-1)\le n\le N-1$; these $2N-1$ functions are
orthonormal in $\mathcal H_\lambda$ and span a subspace $E_N$. Connes, Consani and Moscovici
\cite{CCM} construct self-adjoint operators, as rank-one perturbations of the spectral triple of
the scaling operator on $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$, that involve only the Euler factors of primes
up to $\lambda^2$; we refer to \cite{CCM} for the definitions, which are not used below.

\begin{proposition}\label{thm:ccm}
Let $Q$ be a Hermitian form on $E_N$ and let $D=(Q(V_m,V_n))_{m,n}$ be its matrix in the
orthonormal basis $\{V_n\}$. Then $D$ is a Hermitian matrix of size $2N-1$, its eigenvalues are
real, and $D$ is unitarily equivalent to a Jacobi (real symmetric tridiagonal) matrix whose
off-diagonal entries are non-negative.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$D^\ast=D$ because $Q$ is Hermitian, so the spectrum is real. Householder tridiagonalisation of a Hermitian matrix produces a unitarily equivalent Hermitian tridiagonal matrix; conjugating by a diagonal unitary matrix makes the off-diagonal entries non-negative, hence the matrix real symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}
The relation between the eigenvalues of the CCM operators and the zeros of $\Xi$ is the subject
of \cite{CCM}; nothing below depends on it. In particular we do not use any statement that the
eigenvalues are the zeros of a specific entire approximant of $\Xi$.
\end{remark*}

\subsection{Classical inputs}
\begin{remark}\label{prop:kpos}
No positivity property of the Weil kernel of \cite{CCM} is used or claimed in this paper.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Riemann--von Mangoldt]\label{prop:count}
Let $N_\Xi(T)$ be the number of zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ of $\zeta$ with $0<\gamma\le T$,
counted with multiplicity. Then $N_\Xi(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi e}+O(\log T)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is classical; see \cite[Ch.~15]{Davenport}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:weak}
Let $0<\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le\cdots$ be the positive ordinates of zeros of $\zeta$ on the
critical line, with multiplicity, and $\mu_T=\frac1{N_\Xi(T)}\sum_{\gamn\le T}\delta_{\gamn}$.
Then $\mu_T\to0$ vaguely as $T\to\infty$. In particular the normalised counting measures of the
zeros have no limit probability measure.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a continuous $f$ supported in $[-A,A]$, $|\int f\,d\mu_T|\le\|f\|_\infty N_\Xi(A)/N_\Xi(T)
\to0$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:count}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Jacobi operators with coefficients tending to zero}

\begin{definition}[Jacobi operator]\label{def:jinf}
For a bounded sequence $a=(a_n)_{n\ge0}$ of positive numbers, $J_a$ is the bounded self-adjoint
operator on $\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)$ given by
\[
(J_a f)_n=a_{n-1}f_{n-1}+a_nf_{n+1}\qquad(a_{-1}f_{-1}:=0),
\]
with spectral measure $\mu_a$ at $e_0$ and Weyl function
$m_a(z)=\langle e_0,(J_a-z)^{-1}e_0\rangle$, $z\in\mathbb C^+$. In the attempted reformulation
$\Jinf=J_{a^\infty}$ was to be the limit of the CCM matrices, with $a_n^\infty\to0$; neither the
existence of the limit coefficients nor their asymptotics is established here or in the
literature known to us.
\end{definition}

\section{The prime-sum potential}\label{sec:potential}

\begin{definition}[Potential]\label{def:cinf}
Let $\psidig=\Gamma'/\Gamma$ and
\[
w(z)=\tfrac12\Big[\psidig\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{iz}2\big)+\psidig\big(\tfrac14-\tfrac{iz}2\big)\Big]
-\log\pi,
\]
a meromorphic function, real on $\mathbb R$. For $\Im z>\half$ let
\[
\Psi(z)=2\sum_p\log p\;p^{-1/2}e^{iz\log p}=2P\big(\tfrac12-iz\big),\qquad
P(s)=\sum_p\log p\;p^{-s},
\]
and continue $\Psi$ meromorphically to $\Im z>0$ through $P(s)=-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)
-\sum_{k\ge2}\sum_p\log p\;p^{-ks}$, the last double series being holomorphic for
$\Re s>\half$. Put $\Cinf=w-\Psi$, and for $t\in\mathbb R$ consider the Abel boundary value
$\lim_{\eta\to0^+}\Cinf(t+i\eta)$ whenever it exists. For $\lambda>1$ let
$\Psil(z)=2\sum_{n\le\lambda^2}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}e^{iz\log n}$ and $\Clam=w-\Psil$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark*}
The expansion $\Cinf(t)=2\sum_\rho\cos(t\log|\rho|)/|\rho|+c_0$ does not follow from the explicit
formula: there the zeros enter through $\rho-\half$ and a test function, not through
$\log|\rho|$, and the series does not converge absolutely since $\sum_\rho1/|\rho|=\infty$. Note
also that $\Psi$ sums over primes only, while $\Psil$ (and $-\zeta'/\zeta$) sum over prime
powers. Consequently $\Psil(z)\to-2\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\half-iz)$ for $\Im z>\half$ as
$\lambda\to\infty$, which differs from $\Psi(z)$ by the prime-power series
$2\sum_{k\ge2}\sum_p\log p\;p^{-k(1/2-iz)}$, holomorphic on $\Im z>0$;
Proposition~\ref{thm:B} holds verbatim for either limit.
\end{remark*}

\begin{proposition}[Compactness]\label{thm:C}
If $a_n\to0$, then $J_a$ is compact. Its spectrum is a bounded subset of $\mathbb R$ whose only
possible accumulation point is $0$. In particular $\spec(J_a)$ is not equal to the set of
ordinates of zeros of $\zeta$, nor to any unbounded subset of $\mathbb R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $J^{(M)}$ be $J_a$ with $a_n$ replaced by $0$ for $n\ge M$; it has finite rank, and
$\|J_a-J^{(M)}\|\le2\sup_{n\ge M}a_n\to0$. A norm limit of finite-rank operators is compact, and
the spectrum of a compact self-adjoint operator consists of eigenvalues accumulating only at $0$,
together with $0$ (when the space is infinite-dimensional). Its spectrum is contained in
$[-\|J_a\|,\|J_a\|]$, while there are infinitely many ordinates of zeros of $\zeta$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:count}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}
The statement $\spec(\Jinf)=\{t\in\mathbb R:\Cinf(t)=0\}$, together with a spectral functional
equation
\[
\textstyle c\cdot m_{a^\infty}(z)=\Cinf'(z)/\Cinf(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb C^+,
\tag{EF2}
\]
with a constant $c$ is not available: no such constant survives the limit, and by Proposition~\ref{thm:C} the
spectrum of $\Jinf$ (if $a_n^\infty\to0$) is bounded and cannot coincide with an unbounded set
of zeros.
\end{remark*}

\begin{proposition}[Boundary divergence at zeros on the line]\label{thm:B}
Let $\rho=\half+i\gamma$, $\gamma\ne0$, be a zero of $\zeta$ of multiplicity $m\ge1$. Then, as
$\eta\to0^+$,
\[
\Psi(\gamma+i\eta)=-\frac{2m}{\eta}+O(1),\qquad
\Cinf(\gamma+i\eta)=\frac{2m}{\eta}+O(1).
\]
In particular $|\Cinf(\gamma+i\eta)|\to\infty$ (indeed $\Re\Cinf(\gamma+i\eta)\to+\infty$), so
the Abel boundary value of $\Cinf$ at $\gamma$ does not exist.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:cinf}, $\Psi(\gamma+i\eta)=2P(s)$ with $s=\half+\eta-i\gamma$. The point
$\bar\rho=\half-i\gamma$ is a zero of $\zeta$ of multiplicity $m$, so
$-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=-\frac m{s-\bar\rho}+O(1)=-\frac m\eta+O(1)$. The term $k=2$ in
$P(s)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)$ is $-\sum_p\log p\;p^{-2s}=\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(2s)+H(2s)$ with
$H$ holomorphic on $\Re u>\half$; as $2s\to1-2i\gamma$, which lies on $\Re u=1$ where $\zeta$
is holomorphic and non-zero ($\gamma\ne0$), it stays bounded. The terms $k\ge3$ converge
absolutely and uniformly for $\Re s\ge\frac25$. Hence $P(s)=-m/\eta+O(1)$. Finally $w$ is
holomorphic near $\gamma$, since its poles are at $\pm i(\half+2k)$, $k\ge0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}
Numerically, $\Re\big(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\big)(\half+\eta+i\gamma_1)=-99.5953\ldots,\ -999.5947\ldots$ for $\eta=10^{-2},10^{-3}$ ($\gamma_1=14.1347\ldots$), in accordance with the
proof.
\end{remark*}

\begin{remark}\label{cor:few}
No bound $O(1)$ for the number of off-critical zeros up to height $T$ follows from the present
methods. A derivation through the inclusion $\{\Cinf=0\}\subseteq\{\Xi=0\}$ fails, since that
inclusion is unproved, a count of zeros of $\Cinf$ is not
available, and the series $\sum_\rho1/|\rho|$ diverges; moreover a count of order $T\log T$
compared with $N_\Xi(T)\asymp T\log T$ yields no bound.
\end{remark}

\section{The truncated potential off the real axis}\label{sec:geom}

\begin{proposition}[Non-vanishing far out in the half-plane]\label{thm:zero-free}
Let $\varepsilon>0$ and $S_\varepsilon=\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-1-\varepsilon}$. There is
$R_\varepsilon$, independent of $\lambda$, such that for every $\lambda>1$ and every
$c\in\mathbb C$ with $|c|\le1$, $\Clam(z)\ne c$ whenever
\[
\Im z\ge\half+\varepsilon,\qquad|\Re z|\ge\Im z,\qquad|z|\ge R_\varepsilon.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $y=\Im z\ge\half+\varepsilon$, $|n^{-1/2}e^{iz\log n}|=n^{-1/2-y}\le n^{-1-\varepsilon}$, so
$|\Psil(z)|\le2S_\varepsilon$ for all $\lambda$. If $|\Re z|\ge\Im z>0$, the points
$u_\pm=\frac14\pm\frac{iz}2$ satisfy $|\arg u_\pm|\le\frac{3\pi}4$, and Stirling's formula
$\psidig(u)=\log u+O(1/|u|)$, uniform in $|\arg u|\le\frac{3\pi}4$ \cite[Ch.~8, \S4]{Olver} (the expansion of $\log\Gamma$
differentiated, which is legitimate in a smaller sector),
gives $\Re w(z)=\half\log|u_+u_-|-\log\pi+O(1/|z|)=\log\frac{|z|}{2\pi}+O(1/|z|)$. Choose
$R_\varepsilon$ so that $\Re w(z)>2S_\varepsilon+1$ for $|z|\ge R_\varepsilon$ in this region;
then $\Re(\Clam(z)-c)>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}
Non-vanishing of $\Clam-\mu$ on the whole half-plane $\Im z\ge\half+\varepsilon$ for large
$\lambda$ does not follow from Hurwitz's theorem and the maximum principle, which do not give
non-vanishing on an unbounded region. Note that $w$ itself has poles at
$z=i(\half+2k)$, so near the imaginary axis no such statement can hold without further
restrictions; Proposition~\ref{thm:zero-free} is what is proved.
\end{remark*}

\begin{proposition}[Zeros leaving the axis at a critical point]\label{prop:geom}
Let $f$ be holomorphic near $x_c\in\mathbb R$, real on $\mathbb R$, with $f'(x_c)=0$ and
$f''(x_c)\ne0$. There are $r,\delta_0>0$ such that for every real $\mu$ with
$0<|f(x_c)-\mu|<\delta_0$, the function $f-\mu$ has exactly two zeros in $|z-x_c|<r$, and
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(a)] they are real if $(f(x_c)-\mu)f''(x_c)<0$;
\item[\rm(b)] they form a non-real conjugate pair if $(f(x_c)-\mu)f''(x_c)>0$.
\end{enumerate}
In both cases they satisfy
$|z-x_c|^2=\frac{2|f(x_c)-\mu|}{|f''(x_c)|}\big(1+O(|f(x_c)-\mu|^{1/2})\big)$, and in case
{\rm(b)} also
$|\Im z|=\big(\frac{2|f(x_c)-\mu|}{|f''(x_c)|}\big)^{1/2}\big(1+O(|f(x_c)-\mu|^{1/2})\big)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $f(z)-f(x_c)=(z-x_c)^2g(z)$ with $g$ holomorphic, real on $\mathbb R$, and
$g(x_c)=f''(x_c)/2\ne0$. Near $x_c$ put $h(z)=(z-x_c)\sqrt{g(z)/g(x_c)}$, with the branch equal
to $1$ at $x_c$; $h$ is holomorphic, real on $\mathbb R$, $h(x_c)=0$, $h'(x_c)=1$, so it maps a
disc around $x_c$ biholomorphically onto a neighbourhood $U$ of $0$, maps real points to real
points, and $h(\bar z)=\overline{h(z)}$. The equation $f=\mu$ becomes $h(z)^2=\kappa$ with
$\kappa=(\mu-f(x_c))/g(x_c)\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$. For $|\kappa|$ small it has the two
solutions $h=\pm\sqrt\kappa$ in $U$. If $\kappa>0$ these are real, and their preimages are real
since $h$ restricted to a real interval is a real increasing bijection onto $U\cap\mathbb R$.
If $\kappa<0$ they are $\pm i\sqrt{|\kappa|}$; their preimages are conjugate by the symmetry of
$h$, and non-real because $h^{-1}(U\cap\mathbb R)\subset\mathbb R$ and $h$ is injective.
The sign of $\kappa$ is that of $(\mu-f(x_c))f''(x_c)$. Finally $h^{-1}(v)=x_c+v+O(v^2)$
gives $|z-x_c|=|\kappa|^{1/2}(1+O(|\kappa|^{1/2}))$; in case (b),
$h^{-1}(\pm i\sqrt{|\kappa|})=x_c\pm i\sqrt{|\kappa|}+O(|\kappa|)$, so
$|\Im z|=|\kappa|^{1/2}(1+O(|\kappa|^{1/2}))$. Here $|\kappa|=2|f(x_c)-\mu|/|f''(x_c)|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Height of the pair]\label{prop:approach}
There is an absolute constant $A$ such that
$\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|\Clam''(x)|\le A\,\lambda\log^2\lambda$ for $\lambda\ge2$. Consequently, in case {\rm(b)} of
Proposition~\ref{prop:geom} applied to $f=\Clam$, the conjugate pair satisfies
\[
|\Im z|^2\ge\frac{2|\Clam(x_c)-\mu|}{A\,\lambda\log^2\lambda}\big(1+O(|\Clam(x_c)-\mu|^{1/2})\big),
\]
where the implied constant depends on $\lambda$ and $x_c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$w''$ is bounded on $\mathbb R$ ($w$ is real-analytic there and $w''(x)=O(x^{-2})$ by
Stirling's formula). Next $|\Psil''(x)|\le2(\log\lambda^2)^2\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}$ with
$X=\lambda^2$. Write $\psi_0(u)=\sum_{n\le u}\Lambda(n)$ for
Chebyshev's function. By partial summation and Chebyshev's bound $\psi_0(u)\le c\,u$
\cite[Ch.~7]{Davenport},
\[
\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}=\psi_0(X)X^{-1/2}+\half\int_1^X\psi_0(u)u^{-3/2}\,du
\le2c\,X^{1/2}=2c\lambda.
\]
The lower bound follows from the last assertion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:geom}, case (b).
\end{proof}

\section{The incidence inclusion}\label{sec:inf}

\begin{definition}[Incidence inclusion]\label{def:ii}
$\incInv$ is the statement that, for every real zero $\gamn$ of $\Xi$, the Abel boundary value
of $\Cinf$ at $\gamn$ exists and equals $0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{prop:three}
$\incInv$ is not equivalent to a ``conditional-minimum'' condition on $\Clam$, to an identity
$2\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}\Lambda(p)p^{-k/2}\cos(\gamn\log p^k)=w(\gamn)$, or to
$\Re[-\zeta'/\zeta(\half+i\gamn)]=w(\gamn)/2$ in a regularised sense: the first would need the
spectral statements discussed in \S\ref{sec:potential}, which fail; in the second the sum runs
over prime powers while $\Psi$ sums over primes, and the series diverges at $\gamn$; the third
ignores the residue (see Proposition~\ref{prop:reg}), and its regularised form holds at every
$t$ with $\Xi(t)\ne0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-implies-ii}). What is true is the following.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Corollary.} \emph{$\incInv$ is false. More
precisely, it fails at every real zero of $\Xi$, and $\Xi$ has infinitely many real zeros.}

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Proof.} By Proposition~\ref{thm:B} the boundary value of $\Cinf$ at a
real zero of $\Xi$ does not exist. Hardy's theorem \cite[Ch.~10]{Titchmarsh} gives infinitely
many real zeros. \qed
\end{remark}

\section{The Hadamard expansion and the real part of \texorpdfstring{$\zeta'/\zeta$}{zeta'/zeta} on the line}
\label{sec:hadamard}

\begin{theorem}[Hadamard expansion {\cite[Ch.~12]{Davenport}}]\label{thm:had}
\[
\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=B-\frac1{s-1}+\tfrac12\log\pi-\tfrac12\psidig\!\big(\tfrac s2+1\big)
+\sum_\rho\Big(\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\Big),\qquad B=-\sum_\rho\Re\frac1\rho,
\]
the sum over nontrivial zeros counted with multiplicity; it converges absolutely, since
$|\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho|=\frac{|s|}{|\rho||s-\rho|}\ll_s|\rho|^{-2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Regular part]\label{prop:reg}
Let $\rhon$ be a nontrivial zero of multiplicity $m_n$. Then
$-\zeta'/\zeta(s)=-m_n/(s-\rhon)+g_n(s)$ with $g_n$ holomorphic near $\rhon$ and
\[
g_n(\rhon)=-B+\frac1{\rhon-1}-\tfrac12\log\pi+\tfrac12\psidig\!\big(\tfrac{\rhon}2+1\big)
-\frac{m_n}{\rhon}-\sum_{\rho\ne\rhon}\Big(\frac1{\rhon-\rho}+\frac1\rho\Big),
\]
the last sum over the zeros different from $\rhon$, with multiplicity.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Separate the $m_n$ terms with $\rho=\rhon$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:had}, change sign, and evaluate
the remainder, which is holomorphic at $\rhon$, at $s=\rhon$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cancellation on the line, unconditional]\label{lem:cancel}
For $s=\half+it$ not a zero of $\zeta$,
$\Re\sum_\rho\big(\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\big)=\sum_\rho\Re\frac1\rho=-B$, whatever the
location of the zeros.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The map $\rho\mapsto\rho'=1-\bar\rho$ permutes the nontrivial zeros and preserves
multiplicities (functional equation and $\zeta(\bar s)=\overline{\zeta(s)}$). If
$\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ then $s-\rho=(\half-\beta)+i(t-\gamma)$ and $s-\rho'=(\beta-\half)+
i(t-\gamma)$, so $\Re\frac1{s-\rho}+\Re\frac1{s-\rho'}=0$, and $\Re\frac1{s-\rho}=0$ when
$\rho=\rho'$. Since $\sum_\rho\Re(\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho)$ and $\sum_\rho\Re\frac1\rho
=\sum_\rho\beta/|\rho|^2$ converge absolutely, we may group the terms in orbits of the
involution, and the claim follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Classical identity; independent of $\RH$]\label{thm:rh-implies-ii}
For every real $t$ with $\Xi(t)\ne0$, and $s=\half+it$,
\[
\Re\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)=\tfrac12\log\pi-\tfrac12\Re\psidig\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2\big)
=-\tfrac12w(t).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take real parts in Theorem~\ref{thm:had} and use Lemma~\ref{lem:cancel}; the terms $B$ and
$-B$ cancel, $\psidig(\frac s2+1)=\psidig(\frac s2)+\frac2s$, and
$\Re\frac1s+\Re\frac1{s-1}=0$ on $\Re s=\half$; finally $-\half w(t)=\half\log\pi-\half\Re
\psidig(\frac14+\frac{it}2)$ by Definition~\ref{def:cinf}. Alternatively: with
$\xi(s)=\half s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(\frac s2)\zeta(s)$ and $\Xi$ real on $\mathbb R$,
$\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\half+it)=-i\,\Xi'(t)/\Xi(t)$ is purely imaginary, and
$\frac{\xi'}{\xi}=\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}-\half\log\pi+\half\psidig(\frac s2)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}
The identity holds for every configuration of zeros and does not involve $\RH$; the
cancellation of the real part of the zero sum comes from the symmetry $\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho$.
A numerical check at $t=5,10,13.5,20.3$ gives agreement to $30$ digits. The conclusion $\Cinf(\gamn)=0$ fails for every $n$, whether or not $\RH$ holds (Corollary in Remark~\ref{prop:three}); the cancellation above is unconditional.
\end{remark*}

\section{What remains of the equivalence}\label{sec:equiv}

\begin{remark}\label{thm:D}
The implication $\incInv\Rightarrow\RH$ is vacuous, since $\incInv$ is false
(Remark~\ref{prop:three}). A derivation of the reality of all zeros of $\Xi$ from the reality of
the eigenvalues of $\Jinf$, via a measure built from real zeros only, says nothing about non-real
zeros.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Trivial restatement]\label{thm:equiv}
$\RH$ holds if and only if every zero of $\Xi$ is real. This is a restatement of the definition,
via $\Xi(t)=\xi(\half+it)$; it is the only equivalence with $\RH$ in this paper.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The zeros of $\xi$ are exactly the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$, and $\xi(\half+it)=0$ with $t$
real means $\Re\rho=\half$.
\end{proof}
Since $\incInv$ is false (Remark~\ref{prop:three}), the statement $\RH\iff\incInv$ is equivalent to the negation of $\RH$; it is not a reformulation of $\RH$.

\begin{remark}\label{thm:E}
It is not known that $\mathbb Q$-linear independence of $\{\log|\rho|\}$ together with simplicity
of the zeros implies $\RH$. An argument that infers equality of measures from equality of
weights, without using simplicity, derives nothing about non-real zeros.
\end{remark}

\section{Determinacy}\label{sec:gap}

\begin{proposition}[Determinacy]\label{prop:moment}
For a bounded sequence $a$ of positive numbers, the moment problem of the spectral measure
$\mu_a$ of $J_a$ (Definition~\ref{def:jinf}) is determinate, and $\mu_a$ has compact support.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mu_a$ is supported in $[-\|J_a\|,\|J_a\|]$, and a measure with compact support is determined
by its moments \cite[Ch.~2]{Akhiezer}. Alternatively, Carleman's condition
$\sum_n a_n^{-1}=\infty$ holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}
$\RH$ cannot be reduced to the determinacy of a moment problem attached to $\Jinf$: by
Proposition~\ref{prop:moment} the determinacy is automatic, and $\sum_n(a_n^\infty)^{-1}$
diverges when $a_n^\infty\to0$. The moment problem relevant to the measure
$dm_\infty(s)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}|\Gamma(\frac14+\frac{is}2)|^2ds$ is determinate
(Connes--Consani--Moscovici \cite[Prop.~3.3, Thm.~5.2]{CCMprolate},
\cite[Prop.~3.1]{CCMq}). Paper~\cite{MomentRigidity} shows that $\RH\iff\Delta_n=0$ for a single
$n$ is trivial, and uses this determinacy, due to Connes--Consani--Moscovici, to prove that
constancy of $\Delta_n$ in $n$ is equivalent to $\RH$.
\end{remark*}

\section{Conclusion}

The attempted spectral reformulation of $\RH$ through a limit Jacobi operator and the incidence
inclusion $\incInv$ does not hold: $\incInv$ is false at every zero on the critical line
(Proposition~\ref{thm:B} and Remark~\ref{prop:three}), and a Jacobi operator with coefficients
tending to zero is compact (Proposition~\ref{thm:C}). What is proved consists of elementary
statements about the truncated potential (Propositions~\ref{thm:zero-free}, \ref{prop:geom},
\ref{prop:approach}) and the classical identity for $\Re\zeta'/\zeta$ on the critical line
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-implies-ii}), which holds independently of $\RH$. The only equivalence with
$\RH$ stated here is the trivial one (Proposition~\ref{thm:equiv}).
