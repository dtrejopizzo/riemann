# Obstructions to Weil Positivity: A Catalogue of No-Go Theorems, Falsifiers and Countermodels in the Prime–Gamma Doob Coordinate

Paper 40 reduces the remaining sign on every finite row of the Connes–Consani–Moscovici and Suzuki route to one strict inequality, the source surplus g > δ♭. Assuming a ground-state identity, which Paper 39 (Theorem 23.1) supplies, the surplus on a cofinal family of rows gives Weil positivity on even tests, and that is equivalent to RH by Paper 39, Corollary 23.2. This paper does not attempt the surplus. It collects 38 mechanisms in eight families that do not give it, working in the Doob coordinate, where even Weil positivity is the sharp Poincaré inequality E_K(r) ≥ ½ Var_{μ_K}(r). Each entry is marked as proved, as an explicit countermodel, or as an observation.

## What the paper proves or records

- `thm:doob`: the Doob–variance identity and its equivalence with RH. Both come from Paper 39, Theorem 23.1 and Corollary 23.2.
- `thm:margin`: the pointwise bound 2K(u) < (1501/2000) r_Γ(u), proved with exact rational arithmetic. `thm:absorption`: an algebraic Gamma–connection identity. `prop:pnt`: the common PNT Stieltjes primitive. All three are proved here.
- `thm:ess` (essential threshold) and `thm:bounded` (bounded prime generator): stated as expected, not proved. Both are typeset as remarks.
- `thm:heat`: Paper 40, Theorem 9.18. Paper 40 proves it under the ground-state identity, which Paper 39 supplies.
- `thm:antisurplus`: if RH fails, the scalar-row surplus G_X − δ_X equals A_∞(q,q) < 0 for some real even centered compactly supported q and every head X. This is proved from Papers 39 and 40.
- Entries A1, A2, B3, C1, C5 and E5 are proved here, and H2 is cited from Paper 39, Theorem 16.1. Entry G3 is an explicit countermodel. The remaining 30 entries are observations recorded without proof, with references where they exist.
- Principle P1 (radical saturation) is proved; it is Entry C1. P2 and P3 are heuristic.
- The paper contains no numerical computations.

Known results used here: Weil's criterion, the Beurling–Deny criterion, Douglas's factorization lemma, Haynsworth's inertia additivity and the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula. The new contributions are the explicit theta–Gamma margin, the absorption identity, the anti-surplus theorem and the organization of the obstructions.
