\title[Obstructions to Weil positivity]
{Obstructions to Weil Positivity:\\
A Catalogue of No-Go Theorems, Falsifiers\\
and Countermodels in the Prime--Gamma Doob Coordinate}

\author{David Alejandro Trejo Pizzo}
\thanks{Independent Researcher. \texttt{dtrejopizzo@gmail.com}}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Paper~40 of this series reduces the remaining sign on every finite row of
the Connes--Consani--Moscovici and Suzuki route
\cite{Connes1999,CCM2025,Suzuki2025} to one strict inequality, the source
surplus \(\mathfrak g>\delta^\flat\); assuming a ground-state identity, which is
supplied by the Doob--variance identity of Paper~39 (Theorem~23.1), the
surplus on a cofinal family of rows gives Weil positivity on even tests,
which is equivalent to RH by Paper~39, Corollary~23.2.  This paper does
not attempt that inequality.  It records in one place mechanisms which
cannot prove it, and states which of these obstructions are proved and
which are only observed.

We work throughout in the full-kernel Doob coordinate, in which Weil
positivity is the single sharp Poincar\'e inequality
\(\EK(r)\ge\frac12\Var_{\mu_K}(r)\), with \(\EK\) the positive jump energy
retaining every ordinary von Mangoldt atom and the complete Gamma jump
measure.  The catalogue contains thirty-eight entries in eight families:
order and total positivity; coupling, transport and martingale routings;
local squares, sums of squares and Hodge--Korn completions; determinants
and minor charging; finite heads and adaptive filtering; spectral and
operator-theoretic detectors; realization and factorization rigidity; and
the semilocal branch-selection coordinate.  Each entry is classified as a
no-go theorem inside the literal Riemann system (proved here or cited with
its exact source), a countermodel given by an explicit model, or an
observation, that is, a heuristic obstruction recorded without proof in
this paper.  Proved or cited no-go statements are Entries A1, A2, B3, C1,
C5, E5 and H2; Entry G3 is an explicit countermodel; the remaining entries
are observations.

Three heuristic principles organize the entries; the first is proved.
\emph{Radical
saturation}: the infinite-dimensional family \(K^{(2j)}/K\) attains
equality, and its first member is unbounded, so no decomposition into
nonnegative summands may contain a local edge square.  \emph{Measure
capacity}: the source carries singular mass on the lines
\(t\pm s=\log p^k\) while the polar pair law is absolutely continuous, so
one expects that no endpoint-preserving positive coupling exists.
\emph{Counterfactual invariance}: if RH fails, the positive data of the
system persist while the surplus is negative on a compactly supported vector
(Theorem~4.1), so an implication that does not use the location of the
zeros cannot give the surplus.

We also prove an explicit pointwise inequality
\(2K(u)<\frac{1501}{2000}r_\Gamma(u)\) and the resulting algebraic Gamma
absorption identity, which absorbs the Hermitian part of a delay-bank
connection into a Gamma form with a fixed margin, and record two rigidity
observations (Entries G1, G2) indicating that the remaining contraction
threshold does not change under the changes of signed realization
considered here.  We conclude by describing the mechanisms that the
entries leave open.  No statement in this paper proves the Riemann
Hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose}

This paper is a companion to Paper~40 \cite{TrejoPizzo40} and uses the
Doob--variance coordinate of Paper~39 \cite{TrejoPizzo39}.  Paper~40
reduces the remaining sign on each finite row of the
Connes--Consani--Moscovici and Suzuki route
\cite{Connes1999,CCM2025,Suzuki2025} to a strict source surplus.  The
subject of this paper is the collection of mechanisms which do not give
that surplus.  As Section~\ref{sec:circularity} shows, the surplus at a
scalar row is the complete Weil form evaluated at a fixed vector
(Theorem~\ref{thm:antisurplus}); it is not an estimate weaker than Weil
positivity.  A list of obstructions is therefore independent of whether
the surplus is ever proved.

Entries are of three kinds.  Some are theorems, proved here or cited with
their exact source; one is an explicit countermodel; the others are
observations, recorded without proof, which describe why a mechanism is
not expected to work.  The tag of each entry says which kind it is.

\subsection{What is not claimed}

We do not claim that the Riemann Hypothesis is false, nor that the
Connes--CCM route is unviable.  Every entry below rules out a
\emph{method}, not the conclusion.  We also do not claim that the
catalogue is exhaustive.

\section{The canonical coordinate}
\label{sec:coordinate}

Most obstructions are stated in one coordinate, so that they can be
compared; Entry~A1 and Family~H use the additive semilocal coordinate of
\cite{TrejoPizzo39}.  We recall its construction.

Let \(\xi(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) and
\(\XiR(z)=\xi(\frac12+iz)\).  Let \(K\) be Riemann's even kernel,
normalized by
\[
 \widehat K=\XiR .
\tag{2.1}
\]
The theta representation gives, for \(x\ge0\),
\[
 K(x)=2\pi e^{5x/2}\sum_{m\ge1}m^2\bigl(2\pi m^2e^{2x}-3\bigr)
 e^{-\pi m^2e^{2x}},
\tag{2.2}
\]
in which every summand is positive because \(2\pi m^2e^{2x}>3\).  Hence
\(K\) is smooth, even, strictly positive (this positivity goes back to
P\'olya \cite{Polya1926}) and double-exponentially decreasing.  Put
\[
 h(x)=\cosh(x/2),
 \qquad
 c_K=\int_\R h(x)K(x)\dd x=\widehat K(i/2)=\xi(0)=\tfrac12 .
\tag{2.3}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Full-kernel Doob--variance identity]
\label{thm:doob}
For every even compactly supported smooth multiplier \(r\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \QW(Kr,Kr)=\EK(r)-\tfrac12\Var_{\mu_K}(r),}
\tag{2.4}
\]
where
\[
 \EK(r)=\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_\R
 K(x)K(x-u)|r(x)-r(x-u)|^2\dd x\dd\nu_\zeta(u),
\tag{2.5}
\]
\[
 \dd\nu_\zeta(u)=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\delta_{\log n}(\dd u)
 +\frac{e^{-u/2}}{1-e^{-2u}}\dd u,
 \qquad
 \dd\mu_K=\frac{hK\dd x}{c_K}.
\tag{2.6}
\]
The identity is unconditional.  Consequently the Riemann Hypothesis is
equivalent to the single sharp Poincar\'e inequality
\[
 \boxed{\EK(r)\ge\tfrac12\Var_{\mu_K}(r)}
\tag{2.7}
\]
for all even compactly supported smooth \(r\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity is \cite[Theorem~23.1]{TrejoPizzo39}, with the same
normalizations (2.3), (2.5), (2.6).  Since \(K>0\), every even
\(f\in C_c^\infty(\R)\) is \(Kr\) with \(r=f/K\) even in
\(C_c^\infty(\R)\).  By \cite[Theorem~13.2]{TrejoPizzo39}, failure of RH
yields a negative even Weil test, while RH gives \(\QW\ge0\) by Weil's
criterion \cite{Weil1952}; hence (2.7) is equivalent to RH
\cite[Corollary~23.2]{TrejoPizzo39}.
\end{proof}

The virtue of (2.4) is that it is an identity, not an estimate: no prime,
Gamma or polar term has been separated, bounded, or discarded.  Every
obstruction below is therefore an obstruction to proving (2.7), and can
be compared with every other.  We write \(\mathcal J_u(r)\) for the inner
displacement form in (2.5),
\[
 \mathcal J_u(r)=\int_\R K(x)K(x-u)|r(x)-r(x-u)|^2\dd x\ \ge0 ,
\tag{2.8}
\]
so that \(\EK(r)=\int_0^\infty\mathcal J_u(r)\dd\nu_\zeta(u)\).

\subsection{The radical}

The form (2.5) is closable on \(L^2_{\rm even}(\mu_K)\)
\cite[Section~23]{TrejoPizzo39}, \cite{FukushimaOshimaTakeda}; we keep the
notation \(\EK\) for its closure and let \(L\ge0\) be the associated
self-adjoint operator.  Set
\[
 \mathcal R=\overline{\operatorname{span}}
 \Bigl\{\,\tfrac{K^{(2j)}}{K}\ :\ j\ge1\,\Bigr\}_{\rm centered},
 \qquad
 \mathscr C=(\mathbf 1\oplus\mathcal R)^\perp .
\tag{2.9}
\]
Then \(L\mathbf 1=0\) and \(Lr=\frac12r\) for \(r\in\mathcal R\)
\cite[Proposition~23.3]{TrejoPizzo39}: the radical is contained in the
threshold eigenspace.  More precisely, \cite[(23.11)]{TrejoPizzo39} and
its extension to the form domain give
\[
 \EK(r_j,s)=\tfrac12\langle r_j,s\rangle_{L^2(\mu_K)}
 \qquad(s\in D(\EK)),\qquad r_j=\tfrac{K^{(2j)}}{K}-4^{-j}.
\tag{2.9a}
\]
In multiplier coordinates, orthogonality to \(\mathbf 1\oplus\mathcal R\)
is related to the convolution equation
\[
 (hq)*K=0 .
\tag{2.10}
\]
Indeed, for even \(q\) of at most exponential growth \(e^{c|x|}\),
\(c<\infty\), the function \(G=(hq)*K\) is even and real analytic
(\(K\) extends holomorphically to a strip with double-exponential decay),
and \(\int hq\,K^{(2j)}\dd x=G^{(2j)}(0)\); hence \(q\perp\mathbf 1\) and
\(q\perp K^{(2j)}/K\) for all \(j\ge1\) if and only if every derivative of
\(G\) vanishes at \(0\), that is, \(G\equiv0\).  Solutions of (2.10)
include the modes \(\chi_z(x)=\cos(zx)/h(x)\) for zeros \(z\) of
\(\XiR\), since \(K\) is even and
\(\int\cos(z(x-y))K(y)\dd y=\cos(zx)\,\XiR(z)=0\) (for
\(|\operatorname{Im}z|<\frac12\) the mode lies in \(L^2(\mu_K)\)).  We
write
\(\NK=\{F:F*K=0\}\).

\section{Unconditional structure that survives}
\label{sec:positive}

Before the catalogue we record what is proved, and two statements that are
expected but not proved here.

\begin{remark}[Expected essential threshold]
\label{thm:ess}
One expects \(\sigma_{\rm ess}(L|_{\mathbf 1^\perp})\subset[1/2,\infty)\);
this is the structural statement \cite[(23.12)]{TrejoPizzo39}, which is
not proved there and is not proved here.  If it holds, then every spectral
point of \(L|_{\mathscr C}\) in \((0,1/2)\) is an isolated eigenvalue of
finite multiplicity.  A natural route combines local compactness of the
form domain, from a Gamma small-jump estimate
\(\mathscr E_\Gamma+C\|f\|^2\gtrsim\int\log(2+|\xi|)|\widehat f(\xi)|^2\dd\xi\),
with a prime-number-theorem quadrature for the tail mass
\(2c_K^2=1/2\).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Heat-core Galerkin exhaustion]
\label{thm:heat}
Let \(\EK\) be the closure of the jump form (2.5) from
\(C_{c,\rm even}^\infty(\R)\).  With
\(S_{\mathscr C}=L|_{\mathscr C}+\frac12I\ge\frac12I\) and any
\((g_j)\subset\mathscr C\) with Hilbert-dense span, the nested spaces
\(V_M^{\rm heat}=\operatorname{span}\{e^{-S_{\mathscr C}/k}g_j:1\le j,k\le M\}\)
have union dense in \(D(\EK)\cap\mathscr C\) for the form norm.  On every
fixed finite-dimensional \(E\subset D(\EK)\) the omitted Euler tail
satisfies \(\|N_E^{-1/2}(\mathcal T_X)_EN_E^{-1/2}\|\to0\).  A finite
omitted block containing two distinct primes has positive definite Gram
form on every finite centered space \(E\oplus\mathfrak R_J\) with
\(E\subset\mathscr C\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is \cite[Theorem~9.18]{TrejoPizzo40}.  Here \(\mathcal T_X\) is the
omitted-tail form of Section~\ref{sec:circularity},
\(\mathfrak R_J=\operatorname{span}\{r_1,\dots,r_J\}\) with \(r_j\) as in
(2.9a), and, for a basis \(e_1,\dots,e_d\) of \(E\),
\(N_E=(\langle e_i,e_j\rangle_{\mu_K})\) and
\((\mathcal T_X)_E=(\mathcal T_X(e_i,e_j))\); the proof in
\cite{TrejoPizzo40} shows that every entry of \((\mathcal T_X)_E\) tends
to \(0\).  It is stated there under the
ground-state identity (the agreement of the jump form with
\(\QW(Kr,Kr)+\frac12\Var_{\mu_K}(r)\) on even \(r\in C_c^\infty\)), with
archimedean weight \(g_\Gamma(u)=e^{u/2}/(e^u-e^{-u})\).  Since
\(g_\Gamma(u)=e^{-u/2}/(1-e^{-2u})\) is the Gamma density of (2.6) and the
prime weights are the same, the jump form of \cite{TrejoPizzo40} is (2.5)
and the ground-state identity is Theorem~\ref{thm:doob}
\cite[Theorem~23.1]{TrejoPizzo39}.
\end{proof}

The last assertion uses only that \(\log p/\log r\notin\Q\), so the group
generated by the two displacements is dense; a vector killed by both is
invariant under a dense subgroup, hence constant, hence zero after
centering.  Theorem~\ref{thm:heat} removes weighted spectral synthesis of
the zero modes as a separate hypothesis.

\begin{remark}[Expected boundedness of the prime generator]
\label{thm:bounded}
One expects that the ordinary-prime jump generator is bounded on
\(L^2(\mu_K)\); its multiplication part
\(c_Kh(x)^{-1}\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\{K(x-\log n)+K(x+\log n)\}\)
receives contributions only from \(n\) with \(\log n\) near \(|x|\).  This
is not proved here.  If it holds, every normalized subthreshold eigenstate
of the complete prime--Gamma operator lies in the Gamma operator domain.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Gamma absorption theorem}
\label{subsec:absorption}

The following unconditional inequality and identity are used in
Entries~C5 and G2 below.  Set
\[
 r_\Gamma(u)=\frac{e^{-5u/2}}{1-e^{-2u}}\quad(u>0),
 \qquad
 a=\frac Kh,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C=T_{K'+K/2}M_a ,
\tag{3.1}
\]
where \(T_bf=b*f\) is convolution by \(b\) and \(M_a\) is multiplication
by \(a\); \(\mathcal C\) is the Abel connection of the delay-bank
realization.  We use the weighted inner product
\[
 \langle F,G\rangle_{\omega_K}=\int_\R\overline{F}\,G\,\dd\omega_K,
 \qquad
 \dd\omega_K=\frac{a(x)}{c_K}\dd x=\frac{2K(x)}{h(x)}\dd x .
\]
For a nonnegative displacement density \(v\) on \((0,\infty)\), extended
evenly, write
\[
 \mathfrak b_v(q)=\tfrac12\iint_{\R^2}v(x-y)K(x)K(y)|q(x)-q(y)|^2\dd x\dd y
 =\int_0^\infty v(u)\mathcal J_u(q)\dd u .
\tag{3.2}
\]
We put \(\mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}:=\mathfrak b_{r_\Gamma}\).  Since
\(r_\Gamma(u)=e^{-2u}\,e^{-u/2}/(1-e^{-2u})\), the form
\(\mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}\) is the Gamma part of (2.5) with its density
multiplied by \(e^{-2u}\le1\); in particular
\(\mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}\le\mathscr E_\Gamma\), where
\(\mathscr E_\Gamma\) is the Gamma part of \(\EK\).  All forms below are
evaluated on \(q\in C_c^\infty(\R)\), where they are finite: the densities
are \(O(u^{-1})\) at \(0\) and \(|q(x)-q(y)|^2=O(|x-y|^2)\).

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise theta--Gamma margin]
\label{thm:margin}
For all \(u>0\),
\[
 \boxed{2K(u)<\frac{1501}{2000}\,r_\Gamma(u)},
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
 w_\Gamma:=r_\Gamma-2K\ \ge\ \frac{499}{2000}r_\Gamma>0 .
\tag{3.3}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(t=e^{2u}\ge1\).  From (2.2) and (3.1),
\[
 \frac{2K(u)}{r_\Gamma(u)}
 =4\pi t^{3/2}(t-1)\sum_{m\ge1}m^2(2\pi m^2t-3)e^{-\pi m^2t}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Write \(R_1(t)=4\pi t^{3/2}(t-1)(2\pi t-3)e^{-\pi t}\) for the first
atom.  Its logarithmic derivative
\(d(t)=\frac3{2t}+\frac1{t-1}+\frac{2\pi}{2\pi t-3}-\pi\) satisfies
\(d'(t)<0\), so \(R_1\) has a single global maximum.  Using
\(\frac{333}{106}<\pi<\frac{355}{113}\) and monotonicity of \(d\) in
\(\pi\), rational arithmetic gives \(d(169/100)>0>d(17/10)\), so the
maximizer lies in \((169/100,17/10)\).  On that interval
\(t^{3/2}<\frac{17}{10}\cdot\frac{131}{100}\) since
\((131/100)^2>17/10\); also \(t-1<7/10\) and
\(e^{-\pi t}<e^{-56277/10600}<1/202\).  Hence
\[
 R_1(t)<4\cdot\tfrac{355}{113}\cdot\tfrac{17}{10}\cdot\tfrac{131}{100}
 \cdot\tfrac7{10}
 \Bigl(2\cdot\tfrac{355}{113}\cdot\tfrac{17}{10}-3\Bigr)\tfrac1{202}
 =\tfrac{240179723}{322417250}<\tfrac34 .
\tag{3.5}
\]
For \(m\ge2\), discard \(-3\) and use
\(\frac{(m+1)^4e^{-\pi(m+1)^2t}}{m^4e^{-\pi m^2t}}\le(\tfrac32)^4e^{-5\pi}<10^{-5}\)
to get \(\sum_{m\ge2}m^4e^{-\pi m^2t}<17e^{-4\pi t}\).  With \(s=t-1\) and
\((1+s)^{5/2}\le e^{5s/2}\),
\[
 t^{5/2}(t-1)e^{-4\pi t}\le\frac{e^{-4\pi}}{e(4\pi-5/2)} .
\tag{3.6}
\]
Using only \(\pi^2<10\), \(\pi>3\), \(e>2\) and \(e^{4\pi}>e^{12}>160000\),
the tail contributes less than \(1360/(19\cdot160000)<1/2000\).  Adding
(3.5) proves (3.3).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof uses no floating-point input.  The true maximum of (3.4) is
\(\approx0.71779\), attained at \(t\approx1.69610\), so the stated
constant has genuine slack; the tightest step is
\(e^{-\pi\cdot169/100}<1/202\), where the true value is \(1/202.24\).
Any application needing only a constant below \(1\) may replace \(3/4\)
by \(4/5\) throughout and avoid that step.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Gamma--connection factorization]
\label{thm:absorption}
Let \(q\in C_c^\infty(\R)\) and put \(F=hq\), \(f=Kq\).  Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}(q)+2\operatorname{Re}\langle F,\mathcal CF\rangle_{\omega_K}
 =\mathfrak b_{w_\Gamma}(q)+2\int_\R(K*K)(x)K(x)|q(x)|^2\dd x .}
\tag{3.7}
\]
Both terms on the right are nonnegative, and consequently
\[
 \mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}(q)+2\operatorname{Re}\langle F,\mathcal CF\rangle_{\omega_K}
 \ \ge\ \frac{499}{2000}\,\mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}(q).
\tag{3.8}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(r_\Gamma=w_\Gamma+2K\) we have
\(\mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}=\mathfrak b_{w_\Gamma}+2\mathfrak b_K\).  Next,
\(\overline F\,a=\overline q\,K=\overline f\) and \(aF=f\), so
\[
 \langle F,\mathcal CF\rangle_{\omega_K}
 =\frac1{c_K}\int_\R\overline{f}\,\bigl((K'+\tfrac12K)*f\bigr)\dd x
 =2\langle f,K'*f\rangle_2+\langle f,K*f\rangle_2 .
\]
Since \(K'\) is real and odd, \(T_{K'}\) is skew-adjoint on \(L^2(\R)\),
so \(\langle f,K'*f\rangle_2\) is purely imaginary; since \(K\) is real and
even, \(\langle f,K*f\rangle_2\) is real.  Hence
\(\operatorname{Re}\langle F,\mathcal CF\rangle_{\omega_K}=\langle f,T_Kf\rangle_2\).
Expanding the difference square in \(\mathfrak b_K\), using
\(\int K(x-y)K(y)\dd y=(K*K)(x)\) and
\(\iint K(x-y)K(x)K(y)\overline{q(x)}q(y)\dd x\dd y=\langle f,K*f\rangle_2\),
yields the exact identity
\[
 \mathfrak b_K(q)+\langle f,T_Kf\rangle_2
 =\int_\R(K*K)(x)K(x)|q(x)|^2\dd x .
\tag{3.9}
\]
Combining the three displays proves (3.7); Theorem~\ref{thm:margin} and
monotonicity of \(v\mapsto\mathfrak b_v\) give (3.8).
\end{proof}

The argument is performed entirely at the difference-form level and never
splits the singular Gamma kernel into separately divergent diagonal and
convolution pieces.  Its consequence is that the Hermitian part of the
connection is no longer an open sign: it is absorbed by the completed
Gamma remainder with the explicit margin \(499/2000\), for every
\(q\in C_c^\infty(\R)\) (and, by continuity, on any space where both sides
of (3.7) extend continuously).  The \(K'\) part of \(\mathcal C\) is
exactly skew-adjoint and therefore lossless.

\subsection{The common PNT Stieltjes form}

Let
\[
 \mathcal D(u)=\sum_{\log n\le u}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}-2(e^{u/2}-1),
 \qquad
 k_D(u)=e^{-u/2}\{\psi(e^u)-e^u+1\} .
\tag{3.10}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Common primitive]
\label{prop:pnt}
As distributions on \((0,\infty)\),
\(\dd\mathcal D=\dd k_D+\frac12k_D(u)\dd u\).  Consequently the outgoing
prime-number-theorem quadrature and the incoming Abel flux are the two
halves of a single Stieltjes form,
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak P_{\rm PNT}(q)
 =\int_{[0,\infty)}\mathcal J_u(q)\dd\mathcal D(u)
 =-\int_0^\infty k_D(u)\{\mathcal J_u'(q)-\tfrac12\mathcal J_u(q)\}\dd u .}
\tag{3.11}
\]
Here \(q\in C_c^\infty(\R)\), \(\mathcal J_u(q)\) is (2.8) and
\(\mathcal J_u'=\partial_u\mathcal J_u\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On \((0,\infty)\),
\[
 \dd k_D=-\tfrac12k_D\dd u+e^{-u/2}\{\dd\psi(e^u)-e^u\dd u\},
 \qquad
 e^{-u/2}\dd\psi(e^u)=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\delta_{\log n};
\]
hence \(\dd k_D+\frac12k_D\dd u=\dd\mathcal D\).  Integrating
\(\mathcal J_u(q)\) against \(\dd k_D\) by parts gives (3.11); the
boundary terms vanish because \(k_D(0)=0\), \(\mathcal J_0(q)=0\) and
\(\mathcal J_u(q)\) decays double-exponentially as \(u\to\infty\), while
\(k_D(u)=O(ue^{u/2})\) by the prime number theorem.
\end{proof}

Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:absorption}, the inequality studied in the
delay-bank realization is
\[
 \mathfrak P_{\rm PNT}(q)+\mathfrak b_{w_\Gamma}(q)
 +2\int_\R(K*K)K|q|^2\dd x\ \ge\ 0,
\tag{3.12}
\]
with the simpler sufficient version
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak P_{\rm PNT}(q)+\tfrac{499}{2000}\mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}(q)\ \ge\ 0 .}
\tag{3.13}
\]
We do not derive (3.12) from (2.7) in this paper, and we do not claim
that the two are equivalent; (3.12) and (3.13) are recorded as targets.
Entry~C5 shows that their displacement densities are negative below
\(\log2\), so they cannot be proved by positivity of the density.

\section{The circularity boundary}
\label{sec:circularity}

This section states the organizing theorem of the paper.  We use the
forms of \cite[(9.2)--(9.4)]{TrejoPizzo40}: on the centered real-even
space, for \(X\ge2\),
\[
 \mathcal A_X(q,s)=\mathscr E_\Gamma(q,s)
 +\sum_{p^k\le X}\frac{\Lambda(p^k)}{p^{k/2}}\mathcal J_{k\log p}(q,s)
 -\tfrac12\langle q,s\rangle_{\mu_K},
 \qquad
 \mathcal T_X(q,s)=\sum_{p^k>X}\frac{\Lambda(p^k)}{p^{k/2}}
 \mathcal J_{k\log p}(q,s),
\]
where \(\mathcal J_u(q,s)\) is the polarization of (2.8) and
\(\mathscr E_\Gamma\) the Gamma part of (2.5); thus
\(\mathcal A_\infty=\EK-\frac12\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mu_K}\),
\(\mathcal A_X=\mathcal A_\infty-\mathcal T_X\) and
\(\mathcal T_X\succeq0\).  These definitions are unconditional; the
ground-state identity under which \cite{TrejoPizzo40} relates them to
\(\QW\) is Theorem~\ref{thm:doob}.  For a single vector \(q\) (the case \(M=1\), \(J=0\) of
\cite[(9.25x), Theorem~9.11]{TrejoPizzo40}, where there is no old-mode
regression and no radical shorting, so that
\(\delta^\flat=-\mathcal A_X(q,q)\) and \(\mathfrak g=\mathcal T_X(q,q)\)) the finite-head deficit, the adaptive gain and the
finite tail gain are
\[
 \delta_X:=-\mathcal A_X(q,q),\qquad
 G_X:=\mathcal T_X(q,q),\qquad
 \mathfrak C_X(Y):=\mathcal T_X(q,q)-\mathcal T_Y(q,q)\quad(Y>X).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Off-line anti-surplus]
\label{thm:antisurplus}
Assume RH fails.  Then there is a real even centered
\(q\in C_c^\infty(\R)\) such that for every finite prime-power head
\(X\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item the finite-head pivot is negative,
 \(\mathcal A_X(q,q)=-\delta_X<0\);
\item the scalar adaptive gain is the complete omitted-prime response,
 \(G_X=\mathcal T_X(q,q)\);
\item the source surplus is strictly negative,
\[
 \boxed{G_X-\delta_X=\mathcal A_\infty(q,q)<0 ;}
\tag{4.1}
\]
\item for every finite tail cutoff \(Y>X\),
 \(\mathfrak C_X(Y)-\delta_X<0\).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover \(q\) may be replaced by \(q-p\) for any \(p\) in the span of
finitely many \(r_j\) of (2.9a) without changing \(\mathcal A_\infty(q,q)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:doob} (that is, \cite[Theorem~13.2 and
Corollary~23.2]{TrejoPizzo39}) there is an even
\(r\in C_c^\infty(\R)\) with \(\EK(r)<\frac12\Var_{\mu_K}(r)\).  Since
\(\EK\) and \(\Var_{\mu_K}\) are real quadratic forms evaluated on
\(r=\operatorname{Re}r+i\operatorname{Im}r\) as sums of their values on
the real and imaginary parts, one of these is a real even test with the
same strict inequality; call it \(r_0\) and let \(m=\int r_0\dd\mu_K\).
Fix an even \(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\R)\) with \(0\le\chi\le1\) and
\(\chi=1\) near \(0\), put \(\chi_a=\chi(\cdot/a)\), and
\(q_a=r_0-m_a\chi_a\) with \(m_a=m/\int\chi_a\dd\mu_K\) (the denominator
is positive).  Then \(q_a\) is real, even, in \(C_c^\infty(\R)\) and
centered.  As \(a\to\infty\), \(\chi_a\to\mathbf 1\) pointwise with
\(0\le\chi_a\le1\), and \(\mu_K\) is a probability measure (the case
\(j=0\) of \(\int K^{(2j)}/K\dd\mu_K=4^{-j}\)), so \(\chi_a\to\mathbf 1\)
in \(L^2(\mu_K)\) and \(m_a\to m\).  Moreover \(\EK(\chi_a)\to0\): since
\(|\chi_a(x)-\chi_a(x-u)|^2\le\min(1,u^2\|\chi'\|_\infty^2/a^2)\), the
integrand of (2.5) for \(\chi_a\) tends to \(0\) pointwise and, for
\(a\ge1\), is dominated by \(K(x)K(x-u)\min(1,\|\chi'\|_\infty^2u^2)\),
which is integrable for \(\dd x\dd\nu_\zeta(u)\) because
\(\int K(x)K(x-u)\dd x=(K*K)(u)\) is bounded and decays
double-exponentially, the Gamma density of (2.6) is
\(O(u^{-1})\) at \(0\) and \(O(e^{-u/2})\) at \(\infty\), and
\(\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}(K*K)(\log n)<\infty\); dominated convergence
applies.  (This is the approximation of constants by \(\chi_a\) in the
form norm invoked in the proof of \cite[Corollary~23.2]{TrejoPizzo39}.)
The same bound with \(\chi_a\) replaced by \(r_0\) gives
\(\EK(r_0)<\infty\), and by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for the
nonnegative form \(\EK\),
\(|\EK(q_a)-\EK(r_0)|\le2|m_a|\EK(r_0)^{1/2}\EK(\chi_a)^{1/2}
+m_a^2\EK(\chi_a)\to0\).  Also
\(\|q_a\|_{\mu_K}^2=\|r_0\|_{\mu_K}^2-2m_a\int r_0\chi_a\dd\mu_K
+m_a^2\|\chi_a\|_{\mu_K}^2\to\|r_0\|_{\mu_K}^2-m^2=\Var_{\mu_K}(r_0)\).
Since \(q_a\) is centered,
\(\mathcal A_\infty(q_a,q_a)=\EK(q_a)-\frac12\|q_a\|_{\mu_K}^2\to
\EK(r_0)-\frac12\Var_{\mu_K}(r_0)<0\), and we take \(q=q_a\) with \(a\)
large, so that \(\mathcal A_\infty(q,q)<0\).  Since
\(\mathcal T_X\succeq0\), \(\mathcal A_X(q,q)\le\mathcal A_\infty(q,q)<0\),
which is (1); (2) is the definition; adding gives
\(G_X-\delta_X=\mathcal T_X(q,q)+\mathcal A_X(q,q)=\mathcal A_\infty(q,q)\),
which is (3).  Each omitted atom is nonnegative, so
\(\mathfrak C_X(Y)\le G_X\), and (4) follows from (3).  Finally, by (2.9a)
\(\mathcal A_\infty(r_j,s)=0\) for every centered \(s\) in the form domain,
so \(\mathcal A_\infty(q-p,q-p)=\mathcal A_\infty(q,q)\) for \(p\) in a
finite span of the \(r_j\) (the \(\mu_K\)-means of the \(r_j\) are zero).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Counterfactual invariance]
\label{cor:invariance}
The positive data
\[
 \Lambda(p^k)>0,\quad K>0,\quad \mathcal J_{\log p^k}(q,q)\ge0
\]
hold for every vector, in particular for the vector of
Theorem~\ref{thm:antisurplus}, on which the strict surplus
\(G_X>\delta_X\) fails if RH fails.  An argument that uses only these
data, and not the location of the zeros of \(\XiR\), therefore cannot
prove the surplus.  This is a heuristic guide, not a theorem: it does not
specify which arguments ``use only these data''.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The surplus is not a lemma]
\label{rem:notlemma}
Identity (4.1) says that, at a scalar row, the desired surplus is not a
preliminary estimate from which completed positivity follows: it is the
completed form \(\mathcal A_\infty\) evaluated at a single vector.  In a
proposed proof of the surplus, the step at which (4.1) is given a positive
sign carries the whole content of even Weil positivity.
\end{remark}

\section{Taxonomy}

Each entry below carries one of the following tags.

\begin{description}
\item[\NOGO] A precise statement in the literal Riemann system, proved here
 or cited with its exact source, showing that a class of arguments cannot
 produce (2.7).
\item[\FALS] A construction, valid if RH fails and inside the literal
 system, on which the proposed inequality is false.  The only statement
 of this kind in the paper is Theorem~\ref{thm:antisurplus}.
\item[\CTMD] An explicit, checkable model satisfying the structural
 hypotheses of a proposed mechanism and violating its conclusion.  Rules
 out proofs using only the listed hypotheses; does not itself concern
 \(\zeta\).
\item[\SCAL] A quantitative envelope mismatch: the mechanism is valid but
 loses more than the quantity it must control.  No entry of this kind is
 proved here; the scale entries below are observations.
\item[\OBS] A claim recorded without proof in this paper: a heuristic
 obstruction, with a reference where one exists.
\end{description}

We emphasize the difference between \NOGO{} and \CTMD.  A countermodel
rules out an \emph{axiomatization}: it shows that the properties invoked
are insufficient.  It leaves open that the actual Riemann system has
additional properties which close the argument.  A no-go rules out the
mechanism in the actual system.  An observation rules out nothing by
itself; it records where a mechanism is expected to fail.  For an entry
tagged \OBS, the paragraph headed \emph{Consequence} describes what the
observation would exclude if it were established.

Displayed equations are numbered consecutively by group, and the first
digit need not be the section number.

\section{Family A. Order, positivity preservation, total positivity}
\stepcounter{family}

\begin{entry}[\NOGO{} Failure of the first Beurling--Deny criterion]
\label{ent:A1}
Let \(\varphi_1,\varphi_2\ge0\) be smooth bumps, not identically zero, with
\(\operatorname{supp}\varphi_1-\operatorname{supp}\varphi_2\subset[a,b]\),
where \([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\) contains no \(\log p^k\) and
\(2\cosh(u/2)>c_\infty(u):=e^{-u/2}/(1-e^{-2u})\) on \([a,b]\) (for
example \([a,b]=[0.9,1.05]\)).  Then the Weil cross form is
\[
 \QW(\varphi_1,\varphi_2)
 =\iint\varphi_1(x)\varphi_2(y)
 \Bigl\{2\cosh\tfrac{x-y}2-c_\infty(|x-y|)\Bigr\}\dd x\dd y>0 .
\]
Hence the first Beurling--Deny criterion \(\QW(f,g)\le0\) for disjoint
nonnegative \(f,g\) \cite{BeurlingDeny,FukushimaOshimaTakeda} fails for
every semilocal form \(\QW|_{I_L}\) of \cite[Section~16]{TrejoPizzo39}
whose window contains both supports, and the corresponding semigroups are
not positivity preserving.  The same holds in the even sector: take
\(\varphi_2\) even with support in \([-\delta,\delta]\),
\(\operatorname{supp}\varphi_1\subset[0.975-\delta,0.975+\delta]\),
\(0<\delta\le0.0375\), and \(f=\varphi_1+\varphi_1(-\,\cdot)\); then
\(f,\varphi_2\) are even, nonnegative and disjointly supported, and
\(\QW(f,\varphi_2)=2\QW(\varphi_1,\varphi_2)>0\) by reflection invariance.
\end{entry}

\begin{proof}
In the additive coordinate the off-diagonal part of \(\QW\) between
disjointly supported tests consists of the polar kernel
\(2\cosh\frac{x-y}2\), the Gamma kernel \(-c_\infty(|x-y|)\) and prime
atoms supported on \(|x-y|=\log p^k\) \cite[Section~16]{TrejoPizzo39}.
Since \([a,b]\) contains no \(\log p^k\), the atoms do not contribute, and
the integrand is positive on the support.  For \([0.9,1.05]\): it lies
strictly between \(\log2\approx0.693\) and \(\log3\approx1.099\), and
\(2\cosh(u/2)-c_\infty(u)>1.43\) on it (both functions are monotone
there: \(2\cosh(u/2)\ge2\cosh(0.45)>2.2\) and
\(c_\infty(u)\le c_\infty(0.9)<0.77\)).
The condition is not automatic for small separations: at \(u=0.1\),
\(2\cosh(0.05)<2.01\) while \(c_\infty(0.1)>5.2\).
\end{proof}

\emph{Consequence.} No Perron--Frobenius, Harnack, or ground-state
domination argument based on the pointwise cone of the additive
coordinate applies to the semilocal Weil operators \(A_L\) of
\cite{TrejoPizzo39}.  The heuristic ``the bottom eigenvector should be
positive, hence the radical vector should be the bottom'' is therefore
unavailable there.  In the Doob coordinate the situation is different:
\(\EK\) is a jump form, its semigroup is positivity preserving with
ground state \(\mathbf 1\), and the open question is the gap \(\tfrac12\)
above it, not positivity of the ground state.

\begin{entry}[\NOGO{} The Gamma jump kernel is not \(\mathrm{TP}_2\)]
\label{ent:A2}
The Gamma density \(g(u)=e^{-u/2}/(1-e^{-2u})\) of (2.6) is strictly
log-convex on \((0,\infty)\), so \(g(t)^2-g(t-\eps)g(t+\eps)<0\) for
\(0<\eps<t\).  Hence the translation kernel \((x,y)\mapsto g(x-y)\) on
\(\{x>y\}\) is not totally positive of order two \cite{Karlin}.
\end{entry}

\begin{proof}
\(\log g(u)=-\frac u2-\log(1-e^{-2u})\), and
\((\log g)''(u)=4e^{2u}/(e^{2u}-1)^2>0\).  Strict convexity of \(\log g\)
gives \(2\log g(t)<\log g(t-\eps)+\log g(t+\eps)\).  With
\(x_1=t<x_2=t+\eps\) and \(y_1=0<y_2=\eps\), the \(2\times2\) minor
\(g(x_1-y_1)g(x_2-y_2)-g(x_1-y_2)g(x_2-y_1)=g(t)^2-g(t-\eps)g(t+\eps)\)
is negative.  Whether the short-time semigroup of the full form is
\(\mathrm{TP}_2\) is not decided by this computation; we do not use it.
\end{proof}

\emph{Consequence.} Total positivity of the Gamma jump kernel cannot be
used as the input to a variation-diminishing ordering argument; in
particular the single sign change of \(K''/K\) does not, by this route,
identify it as the first centered mode.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Failure of stochastic monotonicity]
\label{ent:A3}
The folded ordinary-prime--Gamma Doob process has, for each displacement
\(u\), the reflected branch \(t\mapsto|t-u|\).  With the literal atom
\(p=2\), at \(t_0=\frac14\log2\) and \(z=\frac34\log2\), the upper-tail
jump rate has a downward discontinuity of exactly
\(c_K\Lambda(2)K(z)/(\sqrt2\,h(t_0))>0\); the Gamma continuum and every
other prime-power atom form a continuous remainder because no other
displacement meets that boundary.  This violates the necessary tail-rate
order.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} If A3 holds, the short-time semigroup and the
large-parameter resolvent do not preserve the increasing cone, and
\(K''/K\) cannot be ordered as the first centered mode by stochastic
monotonicity.  Together with A1 and A2 this suggests that order-based
arguments are unavailable.

\section{Family B. Coupling, transport, martingale routing}
\stepcounter{family}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} No radical-sharp first-chaos coupling]
\label{ent:B1}
Restore the continuous theta coordinate and the integer and rational
theta marks in all four channels (Gamma, divisible, nondivisible
fractional, central crossing), giving an exact positive latent Dirichlet
form with the literal coefficients.  No positive conditional-expectation
or first-chaos projection maps this lift to the polar pair gradient while
preserving the full equality family
\(\{K^{(2j)}/K\}\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Mechanism.} Equality in conditional Jensen makes every radical edge
signature deterministic; the signatures separate oriented endpoints and
therefore force endpoint preservation.  But the literal prime edges carry
singular source mass on the lines \(t-s=\log n\) and \(t+s=\log n\), while
the polar pair law is absolutely continuous.  This is an exact
measure-capacity contradiction.  A second, independent capacity
obstruction holds in the Gamma channel alone: its density lacks polar
capacity on remote fixed-displacement rectangles.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} No positive martingale routing]
\label{ent:B2}
In the folded polar theta law with dilation variable \(S=Be^T\), choosing
a prime-power divisor \(N\mid b\) with probability \(\Lambda(N)/\log b\)
identifies the divisor current and the cross-divisor dispersion as the
conditional mean and variance of one increment, and the resulting ANOVA
transform is unitary.  Nevertheless every finite or countable positive
martingale routing retaining all currents, dispersions, fractional and
central fibres, and Gamma, obeys an exact nonnegative defect identity;
radical equality forces every martingale difference and every unused
source square to vanish, hence endpoint preservation, hence the
contradiction of B1.  The natural separated assignment already fails on
the two-state fibre \(2<S<3\).
\end{entry}

\begin{entry}[\NOGO{} Shorting is not a weakening]
\label{ent:B3}
For every centered \(q\) in the form domain and every \(p\) in the span
of finitely many \(r_j\) of (2.9a),
\(\mathcal A_\infty(q-p,q-p)=\mathcal A_\infty(q,q)\).  Hence shorting a
finite part of the radical leaves the sign of the completed form
unchanged: the complementary inequality is equivalent to, not weaker
than, the original one on such vectors.  Moreover the radical is not
complete: for every zero \(z\) of \(\XiR\), the mode
\(\chi_z(x)=\cos(zx)/\cosh(x/2)\) is a nonzero element of
\(L^2(\mu_K)\) orthogonal to \(\mathbf 1\) and to every \(K^{(2j)}/K\),
\(j\ge1\).
\end{entry}

\begin{proof}
The first claim is the last assertion of Theorem~\ref{thm:antisurplus}.
For the second, zeros satisfy \(|\operatorname{Im}z|<\frac12\), so
\(\int|\chi_z|^2\dd\mu_K=2\int|\cos zx|^2K/h\,\dd x<\infty\), and
\(\chi_z\ne0\).  Further
\(\langle K^{(2j)}/K,\chi_z\rangle_{\mu_K}
=2\int K^{(2j)}(x)\cos(zx)\dd x=2(-z^2)^j\XiR(z)=0\) for \(j\ge0\), using
\(\widehat{K^{(2j)}}(z)=(-z^2)^j\XiR(z)\), evenness of \(K\), and the
double-exponential decay of \(K^{(2j)}\) in the strip.
\end{proof}

\emph{Consequence.} Passing to the complement of a finite part of the
radical gives no room: the completed form takes the same values there.
This entry is used repeatedly below.

\section{Family C. Local squares, sums of squares, Hodge--Korn}
\stepcounter{family}

This family contains the master obstruction of the paper.

\begin{entry}[\NOGO{} Radical saturation: no local positive remainder]
\label{ent:C1}
The functions \(\rho_j=K^{(2j)}/K\), \(j\ge1\) (equivalently the centered
\(r_j\) of (2.9a)), satisfy
\[
 \EK(\rho_j)=\tfrac12\Var_{\mu_K}(\rho_j)\qquad(j\ge1),
\tag{7.1}
\]
so the sharp inequality (2.7) is saturated on an infinite-dimensional
space.  Suppose a proposed proof has the form
\[
 \EK(r)-\tfrac12\Var_{\mu_K}(r)=\sum_\alpha\mathcal S_\alpha(r),
 \qquad \mathcal S_\alpha\ge0,
\tag{7.2}
\]
and that one summand dominates a local edge square,
\[
 \mathcal S_{\alpha_0}(r)\ \ge\
 \int_0^\infty\!\!\int_\R w(x,u)|r(x)-r(x-u)|^2\dd x\dd u
\tag{7.3}
\]
with \(w\ge0\) positive on a set of positive measure, where (7.2) and
(7.3) are required to hold on the form domain of \(\EK\) (or at least at
\(r=K''/K\)).  Then (7.2) is impossible.
\end{entry}

\begin{proof}
By \cite[Proposition~23.3]{TrejoPizzo39}, \(K^{(2j)}/K-4^{-j}\) lies in
the form domain and satisfies (2.9a); taking \(s\) equal to it gives
(7.1), since \(\EK\) and \(\Var_{\mu_K}\) do not see constants.  (The
input is that \(\widehat{K^{(2j)}}=(-z^2)^j\XiR\) vanishes at every zero,
so that \(\QW(K^{(2j)},\cdot)=0\) by the explicit formula, together with
the extension of (2.4) from compactly supported multipliers to
\(K^{(2j)}/K\) by the cutoffs \(\chi(\cdot/R)K^{(2j)}/K\); both sides are
continuous under this approximation by dominated convergence, using the
double-exponential decay of \(K\); see the proof of
\cite[Proposition~23.3]{TrejoPizzo39}.)

Take \(\rho_1=K''/K\).  By (7.1) the left side of (7.2) vanishes, so every
nonnegative summand vanishes; (7.3) then forces \(\rho_1(x)=\rho_1(x-u)\) for
almost every \((x,u)\) in the set \(\{w>0\}\), which has positive
measure.  By Fubini there is \(u_0>0\) such that \(\rho_1(x)=\rho_1(x-u_0)\) for
\(x\) in a set of positive measure.  Since \(K>0\) is real analytic,
\(\rho_1\) is real analytic on \(\R\), so \(\rho_1(x)=\rho_1(x-u_0)\) for all
\(x\), and \(\rho_1\) is continuous and periodic, hence bounded.  But the
first term of (2.2) dominates with all its derivatives, and
\(\partial_x^2\exp(-\pi e^{2x})=(4\pi^2e^{4x}-4\pi e^{2x})\exp(-\pi e^{2x})\),
so \(K''(x)/K(x)=4\pi^2e^{4x}(1+o(1))\) as \(x\to+\infty\), and \(\rho_1\) is
unbounded.  This contradiction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\emph{Consequence.} The following are excluded whenever they produce a
decomposition (7.2) with a summand of type (7.3):
\begin{enumerate}
\item canonical-path comparison using only divisible theta indices;
\item Efron--Stein decomposition over individual prime towers;
\item any Cheeger estimate with a strict edge surplus;
\item any sum-of-squares proof whose individual squares are local
 translation differences.
\end{enumerate}
What survives is a \emph{signed} global decomposition with genuine
cancellation between different prime channels.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Finite-incidence Hodge--Korn rigidity]
\label{ent:C2}
The vertex signatures \((K^{(2j)}(x)/K(x))_{j\ge0}\) are finitely linearly
independent.  Hence a local linear combination of edge increments which
vanishes on the complete radical is divergence free; it is an incidence
cycle and therefore vanishes on every gradient.  Since the theta-divisor
fibres are stars, every radical-sharp local amplitude on their current,
dispersion or residue-character coordinates is zero.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} No nontrivial integral of finite-local squares can
complete the signed Gamma--polar channel.  After the exact global radical
anti-short one obtains the sharp identity
\(\QW=\|G_\perp r\|^2-\|D_\perp r\|^2\), whose remaining Korn contraction
is globally nonlocal and is violated by a hypothetical subthreshold mode
by the factor \((2\alpha)^{-1/2}>1\).

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} No infinite boundary escape]
\label{ent:C3}
In the literal source norm, with every von Mangoldt and theta weight
retained, the conductance of the folded spatial cut at \(R\) is
\(O(\exp\{-ce^R\})\).  Compact smooth even multipliers form a core of the
closed prime--Gamma form, so every bounded locally divergence-free current
is orthogonal to all form-domain gradients and has zero flux at the folded
end.  A nonzero cofinal boundary flux would require squared source norm at
least \(\exp\{ce^R\}\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} C2 cannot be evaded by moving the missing amplitude to
infinity.  There is no extra bounded boundary-homology sector.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Primitive and Volterra squares are indefinite]
\label{ent:C4}
Pushing the identities \(k_y=\frac12(D^2-\frac14)f_y\) and
\(\phi(y)=-\pi(y^3e^{-\pi y^2})'\) through the complete source, the
multiplicative pushforward is the single literal signed measure
\[
 \dd\eta=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)\delta_n
 +\Bigl\{-1+\frac1{z(z^2-1)}\Bigr\}\dd z .
\tag{7.4}
\]
Fibrewise positivity is false: before the first prime, \(\eta\) is
strictly negative on \((4/3,2)\), and even the quadratic increment
satisfies \(A_{(\log z)^2}(2^-)<-\frac{43}{4500}\).  Summing every theta
and character fibre gives exactly \(F_r(z)=z^{-1/2}J_{\log z}(r)\), whose
cumulative rises to the plastic root, then has strictly negative
continuous drift between prime powers with upward jumps
\(\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}J_{\log n}\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Neither the fibrewise nor the fully aggregated
coordinate is a local positive square.  The endpoint terms vanish, so
Gamma and the pole cannot be appended afterwards as a boundary
correction: they are already inside the signed cumulative.

\begin{entry}[\NOGO{} No centered-path or local Stieltjes completion]
\label{ent:C5}
Let \(C_uq=\{K(x)K(x-u)\}^{1/2}\{q(x)-q(x-u)\}\) and
\(Z_q(u)=e^{-u/4}C_uq\), so \(\|Z_q(u)\|^2=e^{-u/2}\mathcal J_u(q)\).  For
\(0<\theta\le1\) set \(a_\theta(u)=\theta e^{u/2}r_\Gamma(u)>0\).  Then
\[
 \mathfrak P_{\rm PNT}+\theta\mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}
 =\int_0^\infty a_\theta\Bigl\|Z_q-\frac{E_1(e^u)}{a_\theta}Z_q'\Bigr\|^2\dd u
 -\int_0^\infty\frac{E_1(e^u)^2}{a_\theta(u)}\|Z_q'(u)\|^2\dd u ,
\tag{7.5}
\]
with \(E_1(T)=\psi(T)-T+1\).  The second line has the wrong sign and is
not an omitted Gamma term: the entire Gamma budget is already inside
\(a_\theta\).  Moreover, at the available margin \(\theta_*=499/2000\),
the scalar density of the sufficient form (3.13) is
\(w_*(u)=-e^{u/2}+\theta_*r_\Gamma(u)\), and with \(z=e^u\)
\[
 \frac{\theta_*r_\Gamma(u)}{e^{u/2}}=\frac{\theta_*}{z^3-z},
 \qquad
 z\ge\tfrac98\ \Longrightarrow\ z^3-z\ge\tfrac{153}{512}>\tfrac{499}{2000},
\tag{7.6}
\]
since \(306000>255488\).  Hence
\[
 \boxed{w_*(u)<0\qquad\bigl(\log\tfrac98\le u<\log2\bigr).}
\tag{7.7}
\]
In the exact residual (3.12) the density \(-e^{u/2}+r_\Gamma-2K\) is
likewise strictly negative on \([\log\frac32,\log2)\), because
\(z^3-z\ge\frac{15}8>1\) and \(K>0\).
\end{entry}

\begin{proof}
Here \(q\in C_c^\infty(\R)\).  Expanding the square,
\(\int a_\theta\|Z_q-a_\theta^{-1}E_1Z_q'\|^2-\int a_\theta^{-1}E_1^2\|Z_q'\|^2
=\int a_\theta\|Z_q\|^2-\int E_1(e^u)\,\partial_u\|Z_q\|^2\).  The first
term is \(\theta\int r_\Gamma\mathcal J_u\dd u=\theta\mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}(q)\)
by (3.2), and since \(\|Z_q\|^2=e^{-u/2}\mathcal J_u\) and
\(e^{-u/2}E_1(e^u)=k_D(u)\), the second is
\(-\int k_D(\mathcal J_u'-\frac12\mathcal J_u)\dd u=\mathfrak P_{\rm PNT}(q)\)
by (3.11).  This proves (7.5).  The displacement density of
\(\mathfrak P_{\rm PNT}\) on \((0,\log2)\) is \(-e^{u/2}\), since no prime
power lies there, which with (7.6) gives (7.7).
\end{proof}

\emph{Quantitative version (not proved here).}  Let \(q_N=\chi\cos(Nx)\) with
\(0\ne\chi\in C_c^\infty\) chosen so that its support overlaps its
translates on a fixed \(I\Subset(0,\log2)\).  Then
\(\int_I\|Z_{q_N}'(u)\|^2\dd u\ge c_IN^2\) for large \(N\), whereas
one expects
\[
 \mathfrak b_{\Gamma,*}(q_N)=O(\log N),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak P_{\rm PNT}(q_N)=O(1).
\tag{7.8}
\]
If so, the two \(N^2\) terms in (7.5) cancel each other, and estimating
either separately loses a factor larger than any fixed Gamma margin.

\emph{Consequence.} By (7.7), positivity of the common displacement
density is not available.  By (7.5), and if (7.8) holds, the following are
not available either: a pointwise completion of the centered Abel
derivative, a bound of \(\mathcal J_u'\) or \(Z_q'\) by the Gamma form,
and a finite-radical version of either.  Note that (7.7)
locates the failure \emph{below the first prime}, where the arithmetic is
entirely explicit --- the obstruction is not an artifact of prime
irregularity.

\section{Family D. Determinants, minors, charging}
\stepcounter{family}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Cauchy--Binet charging is circular]
\label{ent:D1}
In exterior-power coordinates, an abstract contractive charge exists
exactly when \(\tau_{d+1}\ge\delta_J\tau_d\), i.e. exactly when the
inequality to be proved holds.  Consequently the squared-minor expansion
supplies no sign.  Moreover a universal positive endpoint-local
weight-preserving charge would saturate Jensen on every radical signature,
hence preserve endpoints, and then contradict B1.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} The only surviving possibility is a row-dependent,
globally nonlocal charge after the complete radical anti-short, whose
strict norm bound is precisely the projected Pl\"ucker-frame inequality.
That is the surplus again, not a route to it.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Network positivity does not force crossing]
\label{ent:D2}
There is an exact four-state rational model with a connected full-rank
tail, nontrivial adaptation, an exact threshold radical, positive Gamma
and head graph channels, literal weights \(\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n\), and a
theta-sized envelope, for which the bordered determinant remains negative.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Effective resistance, matrix-tree expansions, generic
network positivity and summability do not force the crossing.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Exact saturation with the literal theta data]
\label{ent:D3}
A one-dimensional model using the actual theta displacement forms, the
actual lengths \(\log p^k\), and the ordinary weights
\(\Lambda(p^k)=\log p\), with the free source diagonal set equal to the
complete positive tail, has every proper determinant strictly below the
deficit, converging to it.  Permanent non-crossing is exactly
\(\sigma_\infty\le0\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} This rules out any proof from source algebra, positive
ordinary-prime increments, inertia and summability alone.  Total
positivity supplies no missing input either: the Gamma kernel is not
\(\mathrm{TP}_2\) (A2), the literal prime tower has a \(\mathrm{PF}_2\)
failure, and the polarized jet \(j_2\) is already indefinite on
\(\{p,p^2\}\).

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} The observation-only determinant is strictly lossy]
\label{ent:D4}
The Gram ratio
\(\det G_{\mathcal B}^{(M)}/\det G_{\mathcal B}^{(M-1)}\) of the
observation vectors is a valid but in general weaker certificate than the
adaptive gain (8.1), since it omits the component parallel to the old
observation range, which (8.1) prices by the positive preceding
prime--Gamma block \(A\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} The force-bearing object is the augmented determinant
\[
 \Delta_{\mathcal B}
 =\frac{\det\begin{pmatrix}A+U^*U&U^*r\\ r^*U&\|r\|^2\end{pmatrix}}
        {\det(A+U^*U)},
\tag{8.1}
\]
not the observation Gram ratio.  We record this because the weaker
certificate is the one that suggests itself first.

\section{Family E. Finite heads, staircases, adaptive filtering}
\stepcounter{family}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} No finite-head telescope]
\label{ent:E1}
Every proper finite prime-power head is strictly negative on each
nonconstant exact Riemann-radical direction.  Hence its signed short over
the radical is unbounded below, and the full infinite prime--Gamma--polar
limit must be assembled \emph{before} radical shorting.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} A finite-head Cholesky or Pick telescope is
impossible.  This is the finite-level shadow of B3.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Discrete levels and full-rank sensors are not enough]
\label{ent:E2}
There is an exact three-state reversible graph generator with a constant
ground state, an exact completed \(1/2\)-radical, discrete levels, and an
infinite norm-convergent bank of strictly positive full-rank sensors
carrying the literal weights \(\Lambda(p^k)/p^{k/2}\), which nevertheless
retains a complementary eigenvalue \(1/4\).
\end{entry}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Adaptation loss defeats tail energy]
\label{ent:E3}
There is an injective two-mode theta-scale model whose residual tail energy
strictly exceeds its deficit, but which loses enough energy to adaptive
regression that its completed pivot remains negative.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Schur adaptivity, positive literal weights, strict
observability and summability do not force crossing.  The missing input is
an estimate of the adaptation-loss term itself.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Exact equality saturation over the prime powers]
\label{ent:E4}
There is a saturation model indexed by the ordinary prime powers, with the
literal weights \(\Lambda(m)/\sqrt m\), an \(e^{-2\pi m}\) theta envelope,
injective atom features and zero adaptive leakage, whose cumulative gain
equals \(\delta\) exactly: every finite pivot is negative and the
completed pivot is zero.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Stieltjes positivity and theta summability perform the
finite selection only \emph{after} a strict surplus has been proved.
Equality does not suffice.  This entry is the reason the target inequality
must be strict, and the reason a compensated vanishing-tolerance form is
needed for the limit.

\begin{entry}[\NOGO{} Response--inertia bound]
\label{ent:E5}
For a Hermitian \(H\) and any \(T\),
\(\nu_-(H|_{\ker T})\ge\nu_-(H)-\operatorname{rank}T\) (finite
dimensions).  Hence whenever a signed finite spectral block has more
negative directions than available prime-midpoint rows, some vector on
which the block is negative annihilates every one of those rows.
\end{entry}

\begin{proof}
Let \(N\) be the span of the eigenvectors of \(H\) with negative
eigenvalues, so \(\dim N=\nu_-(H)\) and \(\langle Hv,v\rangle<0\) for
\(0\ne v\in N\).  Then
\(\dim(N\cap\ker T)\ge\dim N-\operatorname{codim}\ker T
=\nu_-(H)-\operatorname{rank}T\), and \(H\) is negative definite on
\(N\cap\ker T\), so \(\nu_-(H|_{\ker T})\) is at least this dimension by
the min--max principle.
\end{proof}

\emph{Consequence.} No arbitrary-vector midpoint frame floor may be used.
For the actual staircase the dimension obstruction disappears, because
\(A\succ0\) with a negative Schur pivot forces negative index exactly one
by Haynsworth's inertia additivity \cite{Haynsworth};
but no inertia identity then bounds the regression residual from below.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Local observability does not pay the deficit]
\label{ent:E6}
On every finite analytic even mode space the literal displacement feature
restricted to any nonempty midpoint aperture is injective, giving the
computable Christoffel bound
\(\mathcal J_{u,W}(q^*)\ge\kappa_M(u,W)>0\) (compare the local Christoffel
detection of \cite[Proposition~9.9]{TrejoPizzo40}).  However, for a nonzero
completed radical vector the finite-head deficit equals the sum of
\emph{all} omitted positive atom energies.  Hence every proper finite
omitted block gains strictly less than the deficit, and its
moving-midpoint mass can tend to zero.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Any surviving estimate must use the completed
anti-short \(q^*\in\mathcal R^\perp\) quantitatively; local observability,
the adaptive source equation, and tail localization are individually
insufficient.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Prime-sampling envelope mismatch]
\label{ent:E7}
On every fixed spectral block \(|\operatorname{Im}z|\le b<1/2\) with jets
of order at most \(J\), and every fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
 \sum_{X<p\le(1+\delta)X}\beta_p|A_p(q)+\rho_p(q)|^2
 \ \ge\ c'_{\mathcal Z,\delta}X^{3-b}(1+\log X)^{-2J}
 e^{-2\pi(1+\delta)X}\|q\|_{\rm coeff}^2 .
\tag{9.1}
\]
Here \(\beta_p\), \(A_p\), \(\rho_p\) and \(c'_{\mathcal Z,\delta}\)
refer to the prime-sampling coordinates of the finite rows; we do not
prove (9.1) here.  Even if (9.1) holds, compared with the natural tail
scale \(e^{-2\pi X}\) it loses \(e^{-2\pi\delta X}\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} A relative-interval attack cannot place the response
inside the \(O(1)\) prime window where the theta weight is comparable to
its leading tail value.  Remez and Tur\'an localization on the same
relative interval do not repair this.  Any successful estimate must be a
short-window statement.

\section{Family F. Spectral and operator-theoretic detectors}
\stepcounter{family}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Birman--Schwinger supplies no sign]
\label{ent:F1}
A compactly supported positive boost produces a compact
Birman--Schwinger operator \(\mathcal K_{\lambda,R,M}\) whose eigenvalue
\(1\) detects each isolated subthreshold bound state.  But the exact
Rayleigh quotient shows that \(\|\mathcal K_{\lambda,R,M}\|\le1\) is,
term for term, equivalent to \(\EK\ge\lambda\|\cdot\|^2\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Localization proves Fredholm compactness and supplies
no sign.  This is the usual Birman--Schwinger reformulation in the
nonlocal setting; it is not a new mechanism.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Mourre and dilation commutators]
\label{ent:F2}
After ground-state conjugation, the translation commutator is parity odd
and has identically zero quadratic form on the physical even sector.
Dilation is parity even, but every prime power at \(a=\log n\) contributes
the isolated order-one distribution \(a(\delta_a'-\delta_{-a}')\), and the
complete raw dilation commutator is unbounded in both signs on compact
oscillatory even rows.  In the exact complement, translation is tangent to
\(F*K=0\) but changes parity, while dilation preserves parity and has
transverse leakage \(t\XiR'(t)\widehat F(t)\) at every simple zero.  Below
the essential threshold a strict projected Mourre estimate holds exactly
when the corresponding spectral projection is zero, and the normalized
commutator vanishes on heat or hybrid rows concentrating on a bound
cluster.
\end{entry}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Heat moments and operator-monotone transforms]
\label{ent:F3}
Every raw heat Hankel matrix is automatically positive, whereas eventual
positivity of any one odd moment shifted to the threshold is already
equivalent to the physical surplus.  The same equivalence persists after every
strictly increasing operator-monotone transform.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Heat summability and scalar moment positivity supply
no hidden fraction of the missing bound.  Relatedly, the relative-heat
observable has logarithmic decay rate exactly
\(-\{\inf\sigma(L|_{\mathscr C})+\frac12\}\), so the desired surplus is
equivalent to rate at most \(-1\) while the unconditional energy gives
only \(-1/2\).

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Positive source atoms tolerate a subthreshold mode]
\label{ent:F4}
If a subthreshold ground cluster \(\alpha<1/2\) of multiplicity
\(m_\alpha\) exists, the relative spectral measure of the pair
\cite{BirmanSolomyak} has exact local
density \(m_\alpha\dd\lambda\), so
\(\Theta_V(t)\sim m_\alpha e^{-t(\alpha+1/2)}\) for every positive
injective trace-class boost, and the heat-weighted source satisfies
\(\frac12A_V-E_V\sim(\frac12-\alpha)m_\alpha t^{-1}e^{-t(\alpha+1/2)}>0\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Positive source atoms and all unshifted heat moments
remain compatible with a subthreshold mode.  A closure must estimate the
\emph{compensated} source itself.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Non-uniformity as the deficit vanishes]
\label{ent:F5}
Assuming the essential threshold of Remark~\ref{thm:ess}, for every fixed
\(\delta>0\) the subthreshold spectral projection below
\(1/2-\delta\) is finite rank, and one finite heat-core compression detects
every such eigenstate below \(1/2-\delta/2\).  The reduction is not
uniform as \(\delta\downarrow0\): packets of amplitude \((\log N)^{-1/2}\)
have vanishing Hilbert mass at frequency \(N\) but fixed Gamma form
energy, and Hilbert accuracy \(\eta^2\asymp\delta\) already requires
\(\Omega\gtrsim\exp(C_R/\delta)\).
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Fixed-gap violations genuinely reduce to finite
arithmetic; near-threshold violations do not.  Any finite-dimensional
computational strategy must therefore come with a proof that the deficit
is bounded away from zero, which is itself part of the problem.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} No central tightness from mean periodicity]
\label{ent:F6}
Multiplicity jets are essential to local \(\XiR\)-universality: a
compactly supported annihilator would have an entire Fourier--Laplace
transform with \(O(T)\) zeros in \(|z|\le T\), while the Riemann--von
Mangoldt formula \cite[Theorem~9.4]{Titchmarsh} gives
\(\asymp T\log T\) zeros with multiplicity.  Hence exact mean-periodic
zero modes approximate zero on any central window and an arbitrary profile
on a disjoint window.  A diagonal construction gives normalized real-even
graph-domain vectors in the exact radical complement whose mass escapes
equally to both tails while every localized second-log norm tends to zero.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Mean periodicity, evenness, and the regularity
expected in Remark~\ref{thm:bounded} would not yield central tightness.  The remaining
input must be a global threshold-resolvent or eigenstate-observability
estimate using the literal operator equation.

\section{Family G. Realization and factorization rigidity}
\stepcounter{family}

The entries of this family indicate that the missing constant does not
change under the repackagings of the problem considered here.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Canonical signed transfer has a universal threshold]
\label{ent:G1}
After constants and the complete radical have been shorted, the unique
exact signed transfer is
\(C_0=2^{-1/2}U_DA^{-1/2}U_A^*\), with
\[
 \boxed{\|C_0\|=(2\inf\sigma(A))^{-1/2}.}
\tag{10.1}
\]
Hence every exact signed kernel has the \emph{same} contraction threshold,
and a subthreshold eigenmode is amplified by precisely
\((2\alpha)^{-1/2}>1\).  A virial identity further shows that a square
plus a trace-zero commutator cannot conceal such a mode.
\end{entry}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Compact all-atom factorization is unique-minimal]
\label{ent:G2}
Let \(\widetilde{\mathcal G}\) be the complete literal von
Mangoldt--Gamma displacement gradient and
\(\widetilde A=\widetilde{\mathcal G}^*\widetilde{\mathcal G}\) after exact
anti-shorting.  Both \(\mathcal K_a=[T_K,M_a]\) and
\(\mathcal C=T_{K'+K/2}M_a\) are Hilbert--Schmidt on the physical
mean-periodic space, with
\(\|T_bM_a\|_{\rm HS}^2=c_K^2\iint|b(x-y)|^2\dd\omega_K(x)\dd\omega_K(y)\) on \(L^2(\omega_K)\).
Moreover \(\widetilde A\succeq\alpha_0I\) for some \(\alpha_0>0\); this
is conditional on the essential threshold of Remark~\ref{thm:ess},
together with triviality of the kernel after removing constants.  Hence \(\mathcal C=H_C\widetilde{\mathcal G}\) with
\(H_C=\mathcal C\widetilde A^{-1/2}U_A^*\) compact and of minimal norm,
\[
 \inf_{\mathcal C=H\widetilde{\mathcal G}}\|H\|^2
 =\|\mathcal C\widetilde A^{-1}\mathcal C^\sharp\|,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\|H_C\|\le1\iff\mathcal C^\sharp\mathcal C\preceq\widetilde A .}
\tag{10.2}
\]
These gains are operator-norm limits of common-cutoff regularized
factorizations containing every prime power cofinally.  Of these claims,
the boxed equivalence in (10.2) is Douglas's factorization lemma
\cite{Douglas}: \(\mathcal C=H\widetilde{\mathcal G}\) with \(\|H\|\le1\)
if and only if
\(\mathcal C^\sharp\mathcal C\preceq\widetilde{\mathcal G}^*\widetilde{\mathcal G}=\widetilde A\);
the other claims are not proved here.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} If G1 and G2 hold, a change of realization within
the class considered here does not produce the strict gain: the
remaining constant is the same threshold in each realization.

\begin{entry}[\CTMD{} Local passivity cells cannot be multiplied]
\label{ent:G3}
On \(\R/L\Z\), for \(0<\eps<1/2\), the kernel
\(K_L=1+\eps\cos(4\pi x/L)+\eps\cos(6\pi x/L)\) is strictly positive and
\(F_L*K_L=0\) for \(F_L=\cos(2\pi x/L)\), because
\(\widehat{K_L}(\pm1)=0\).  Thus \(K_L\) is a positive kernel with a
nonzero mean-periodic row, the two structural properties of \(K\) used
by a passivity argument.
\end{entry}

\begin{proof}
\(K_L\ge1-2\eps>0\).  The Fourier coefficients of \(K_L\) on
\(\R/L\Z\) are supported on \(\{0,\pm2,\pm3\}\) and those of \(F_L\) on
\(\{\pm1\}\), so the coefficients of \(F_L*K_L\), which are products, all
vanish.
\end{proof}

\emph{Consequence.} A certificate that uses only positivity of the kernel
and the mean-periodic equation does not give a sign, since \(K_L\) has
both properties and a nonzero row in its annihilator.  This is an
abstract positive-kernel countermodel, not the theta kernel.  One expects
in addition that the Hermitian connection power of \(F_L\) stays fixed
while the Gamma channel and any fixed finite bank of delays
\((\log n,\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n)\) contribute \(O(L^{-2})\); we do not prove
these estimates here.  If they hold, a certificate must use the cofinal
prime \emph{placement} jointly with Gamma, theta, and the polar channel,
rather than Gamma or a fixed prime head.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Nonlinear amplification is unavailable]
\label{ent:G4}
On the exact complement \((hq)*K=0\): translation, M\"obius and Jordan
multipliers preserve each frequency and cannot amplify its transverse
exponent.  Pointwise powers do amplify it, but an off-axis mode
\(\cos(zx)^k\) necessarily leaves the zero divisor once
\(k|\operatorname{Im}z|>1/2\); tensor powers retain the equations only
before the non-intertwining diagonal restriction.  The spatial M\"obius
inverse cannot be passed through the zero-mode convolution: the required
squarefree absolute series diverges, and the regularized symbol is
\(\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+\eps)\), which has a pole at \(s=\rho-\eps\) for each
zero \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\), so it is unbounded near \(\rho\) as
\(\eps\downarrow0\).
\end{entry}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} The bar homotopy has no critical contraction]
\label{ent:G5}
The normalized multiplicative bar homotopy
\(Z_\eps^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}(-X_\eps)^k\) recovers every literal von
Mangoldt weight from \(Z_\eps^{-1}\delta Z_\eps\).  If ordered
factorization chains remain orthogonal, the signed collapse on an
\(r\)-prime squarefree fibre has exact norm
\((\sum_kk!S(r,k))^{1/2}\ge\sqrt{r!}\); if they are cancelled before the
norm, the result is the M\"obius inverse itself.  On
\(q_\gamma=\cos(\gamma x)/\cosh(x/2)\), a critical zero of multiplicity
\(m\) forces homotopy gain \(1/|\zeta(\bar\rho+\eps)|\asymp\eps^{-m}\) and
connection gain \(m/\eps+O(1)\).  The specialized bar complex has nonzero
zeroth homology.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Gamma and the pole are analytic and nonzero at the
zero, so completion preserves the residue.  Supplying the zero-fibre map
separately returns exactly to the canonical transfer of G1.

Doob coordinate.  Notation follows \cite{TrejoPizzo39}, with the window \(L\) written as a subscript: \(L>0\),
\(\lambda=e^{L/2}\), \(N=\lambda^2=e^L\); \(A_L\) is the semilocal Weil

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Fixed derivative models miss off-line channels]
\label{ent:H1}
Every fixed span of cutoff derivatives of Riemann's full kernel is
asymptotically orthogonal to a hypothetical off-line evaluation channel,

\end{entry}

\begin{entry}[\NOGO{} Many-vector co-Poisson frames do not improve the quotient]
\label{ent:H2}
Let \(A_L\) be lower-semibounded, self-adjoint and of compact resolvent,
\(P_-\) its negative spectral projection, and \(a_-(Q_L)\) the lower frame
bound on \(\Ran P_-\) of the low-mode co-Poisson/prolate family
\((q_{j,L})\).  Then \cite[Theorem~16.1, (16.2)]{TrejoPizzo39}
\[
 \sum_j\|P_-A_Lq_{j,L}\|^2\ \ge\ a_-(Q_L)
 \sum_{\epsilon_{\ell,L}<0}\epsilon_{\ell,L}^2 ,
\tag{11.1}
\]
so a fixed negative branch forces the aggregate overlap and residual to
vanish at the same rate.  The scale comparison is also adverse: Gamma
coercivity and the absolute \(O(\lambda)\) prime--pole norm control at most

\(O(\lambda^2)\) modes.
\end{entry}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Unquantified cyclicity diverges]
\label{ent:H3}
Although translates and derivatives of \(K\) are cyclic in ordinary
\(L^2\), an off-line zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) forces every truncated
radical approximant of a detecting test to have squared error
\(\gg\lambda^{-2(\beta-1/2)}\).  The semilocal lower-bound cost is
therefore \(\gg\lambda^{2(1-\beta)}\), which diverges rather than
vanishes.  This is \cite[(22.9)]{TrejoPizzo39}, where it is stated
without a detailed proof.
\end{entry}

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} No \(-O(d_8)\) floor from a divisor covariance]
\label{ent:H4}
In the exact centered square
\(Q_L=\mathcal D_N-\kappa_N+(\text{polar})\) of
\cite[Theorem~19.1]{TrejoPizzo39}, with
\(\kappa_N=4\lambda+o(\lambda)\) \cite[(19.5)]{TrejoPizzo39} and
\(d_8\asymp\lambda^{17}e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}\) \cite[Section~15]{TrejoPizzo39},
a divisor-type covariance with Fourier symbol of order \(N\log N\) at
quadratic scale is not comparable with the von Mangoldt jump, of order
\(\sqrt N(\log N)^2\).  One expects that a fixed compact even core
orthogonal to \(q_L^+\) and to the pole has value
\(-\kappa_N+O(1)=-4\lambda+o(\lambda)\) under such a covariance, so that
no lower bound \(-O(d_8)\) holds on that space.  We do not prove this
here.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} A lower bound of size \(-O(d_8)\) on the complement
would have to come from the complete coupled prime--Gamma--pole
complement inequality, that is, from the surplus.

\begin{entry}[\OBS{} Qualitative positivity improvement is insufficient]
\label{ent:H5}
There is an explicit two-dimensional positivity-improving family with a
fixed negative ground state, a strictly positive near-radical model, and
overlap tending to zero at exactly the residual rate.
\end{entry}

\emph{Consequence.} Endpoint normalization is angle-blind and qualitative
positivity improvement cannot supply the denominator in the
anti-orthogonality quotient.  A viable positivity route must prove the
quantitative Harnack--tightness estimate for the actual ordinary-prime
operator.

\section{Three heuristic principles}
\label{sec:principles}

Three heuristic principles organize the entries.  The first is a
proposition; the other two are heuristic guides.

\begin{proposition}[P1. Radical saturation]
The equality set of (2.7) contains the infinite-dimensional family
\(\{K^{(2j)}/K\}_{j\ge1}\), whose first member is real analytic and
unbounded.  Therefore no decomposition of
\(\EK-\frac12\Var_{\mu_K}\) into nonnegative summands may contain a
summand dominating a local edge square, in the sense of (7.3).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is Entry~C1.
\end{proof}

P1 organizes C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, D1 and D3.  \emph{Diagnostic:} if a
proposed argument produces a nonnegative leftover which dominates a local
edge square as in (7.3), it is refuted by P1.

\begin{remark}[Heuristic principle P2: measure capacity]
The source carries positive singular mass on the lines
\(t\pm s=\log p^k\), while the polar pair law is absolutely continuous;
and the Gamma density alone lacks polar capacity on remote
fixed-displacement rectangles.  Therefore no endpoint-preserving positive
coupling, conditional expectation, martingale routing, or weight-preserving
charge from the source to the polar gradient is expected to exist.  This
is not proved here (see Entries B1, B2).
\end{remark}

P2 organizes B1, B2, D1 and the endpoint clause of D2.
\emph{Diagnostic:} an argument that routes source energy to target energy
through a positive kernel that respects endpoints is expected to fail.

\begin{remark}[Heuristic principle P3: counterfactual invariance]
If RH fails, the data \(\Lambda(p^k)>0\), \(K>0\) and
\(\mathcal J_{\log p^k}\ge0\) persist, while the strict surplus fails on
the vector of Theorem~\ref{thm:antisurplus}, at which the surplus equals
the completed form.  Hence an argument that does not use the location of
the zeros is not expected to prove the surplus (Remark~\ref{cor:invariance}).
\end{remark}

P3 organizes A1--A3, E1--E7, F1--F6, G1--G5 and H1--H5.
\emph{Diagnostic:} if a proposed argument would remain valid verbatim in
a system with an off-line zero, it cannot prove the surplus; this is the
first check to apply.

\section{The admissible corridor}
\label{sec:corridor}

We state what the three principles leave open.  The list is heuristic.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The decomposition must be \emph{signed}, with genuine cancellation
 between distinct prime channels (P1).  Nonnegative summands dominating a
 local edge square (7.3) are excluded; signed differences of squares are
 not.
\item The transfer must be \emph{nonlocal} in the displacement variable,
 and must not preserve endpoints (P2).  It may be row dependent.
\item It must be applied \emph{after} the complete mean-periodic
 anti-short, and must use the actual prime-power locations \emph{before}
 any norm is taken (P3).  A mechanism that takes norms atom by atom has
 already discarded the placement information which is the only thing that
 would distinguish \(\zeta\) from the models described, but not
 exhibited, in Entries D2, D3, E2, E3 and E4.
\end{enumerate}

Concretely, the target is recorded in two forms.  The first is the
displacement form
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak P_{\rm PNT}(q)+\mathfrak b_{w_\Gamma}(q)
 +2\int_\R(K*K)K|q|^2\dd x\ \ge\ 0 ,}
\tag{13.1}
\]
with the sufficient uniform-margin version (3.13).  The second is the
relative operator form
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal C^\sharp\mathcal C\ \preceq\ \widetilde A ,}
\tag{13.2}
\]
equivalently \(\|H_C\|\le1\) for the minimal-norm compact factor of
Entry~G2.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:absorption} the Hermitian connection power
in (13.1) has already been absorbed with margin \(499/2000\); what remains
is the common outgoing and incoming ordinary-prime power, which by
Entry~C5 cannot be handled by positivity of its density, and by
Entry~E7 is not expected to be estimable on a relative interval.

\begin{remark}[Status of the target]
We expect (13.1) and (13.2) to be equivalent to even Weil positivity; we
do not prove this.  The list above describes where a proof would have to
take place, not evidence that one exists.  The recurrence of the same
threshold constant across reformulations (Entries G1, G2) should not be
read as progress toward it.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

Paper~40 \cite{TrejoPizzo40} reduces the remaining sign on every finite
row of the Connes--Consani--Moscovici and Suzuki route to one strict
inequality, the source surplus \(\mathfrak g>\delta^\flat\); assuming a
ground-state identity, which is supplied by the Doob--variance identity of
Paper~39 \cite[Theorem~23.1]{TrejoPizzo39}, the surplus on a cofinal
family of rows gives Weil positivity on even tests, which is equivalent
to RH by \cite[Corollary~23.2]{TrejoPizzo39}.  This paper establishes the
following about that surplus.

First, at a scalar row it is not a lemma.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:antisurplus} it equals the completed form
\(\mathcal A_\infty\) evaluated at a single vector, and if RH fails it is
negative on a compactly supported vector for every head
\(X\).  In a proposed proof, the step at which (4.1) is given a positive
sign carries the whole content of even Weil positivity
(Remark~\ref{rem:notlemma}).

Second, several mechanisms are excluded by proved statements: positivity
preservation of the semilocal Weil semigroups in the additive coordinate
(A1), total positivity of the Gamma kernel (A2), shorting a
finite part of the radical (B3), sums of nonnegative summands containing
a local edge square (C1), positivity of the common displacement density
(C5), and arbitrary-vector midpoint floors (E5); Paper~39's frame
inequality gives H2, and G3 is an explicit countermodel.  The other
entries are observations; three heuristic principles --- radical
saturation (proved as P1), measure capacity, and counterfactual
invariance --- organize them.

Third, Entries G1 and G2 indicate that the contraction threshold does not
change under the changes of signed realization considered here.  These
are observations, apart from the Douglas-lemma equivalence in (10.2).

What remains open is described by the forms (13.1) and (13.2), which we
expect, but do not prove, to be equivalent to even Weil positivity.  The
unconditional identity of Theorem~\ref{thm:absorption} removes the
Hermitian connection power from (13.1) with the explicit margin
\(499/2000\), leaving the common outgoing and incoming ordinary-prime
power.
