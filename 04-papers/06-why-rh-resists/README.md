# Paper 06 — Why the Riemann Hypothesis resists: a classifier for zero-absence mechanisms, the finite-to-full positivity gap, and a finitization obstruction under linear independence

The paper asks why formal routes to the Riemann hypothesis stall at the same point. It does not
attempt a proof. It introduces a four-condition classifier $D_0$ for mechanisms that could force
the absence of off-line zeros and calibrates it on about thirty historical programmes. It then
studies four positivity families attached to ζ (intersection/Hodge, moment/Hankel, total
positivity, combinatorial Hodge). In each family every finite-order positivity is unconditional,
while full-order positivity is the Riemann hypothesis. The missing ingredient is called a
finitization. Under the linear independence conjecture for the zeros, no Frobenius-type
periodicity, and hence no finite-dimensional algebraic finitization, can exist.

## Main results

- `prop:cal`: the classifier $D_0$ reproduces the known outcome of each calibration programme — ARGUED
  (calibration against the literature, which is CITED).
- `thm:tp`: total-positivity (Pólya frequency) form of the Riemann hypothesis — PROVED, from CITED
  classical results.
- `prop:orders`, `thm:gap`: finite-order positivity is unconditional in each family; full order is the
  Riemann hypothesis; the four hierarchies have the same gap — PROVED.
- `prop:finit`: over $\mathbb F_q$ full positivity is a finite condition via Hodge index on
  $\mathrm{NS}(C\times C)$ — PROVED (classical input CITED).
- `thm:LI`, `cor:degen`: under the linear independence conjecture no finite-dimensional algebraic
  finitization exists; the $\mathbb F_1$ degeneration — CONDITIONAL (on LI).
