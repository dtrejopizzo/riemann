# Positivity formulations of the Riemann hypothesis and the function-field analogy: a heuristic checklist and some remarks

An expository note. It compares programmes aimed at the Riemann hypothesis (RH) with the one
setting where an analogue of RH is a theorem, zeta functions over finite fields. It proposes a
four-clause heuristic checklist $D_0$ for mechanisms that could exclude zeros off the critical
line, recalls positivity formulations of RH in which finitely many conditions are known and the
full family is equivalent to RH, and discusses the periodicity of the zeros over finite fields.

## What the paper contains

- Criterion 2.1 and Table 1: the checklist $D_0$ and how eighteen known programmes fare against
  it. This is a judgment, not a theorem. In this assessment only the proofs of RH over finite
  fields (Weil; Stepanov–Bombieri; Deligne) meet all four clauses; for the other programmes
  clause $I_2$ is not met or is open, or clause $I_3$ is not met.
- Theorem 3.1 (Schoenberg's criterion): RH holds if and only if the Fourier transform $\Lambda$
  of $1/\xi(\tfrac12+y)$ is a Pólya frequency function. Pólya's kernel $\Phi$ itself is not a
  Pólya frequency function (its Fourier transform has zeros); a 5×5 determinant
  $\det[\Phi(0.1(i-j))]$ is negative, which is numerical evidence that $\Phi$ fails $\mathrm{TP}_5$.
- Proposition 3.2: the known unconditional facts — log-concavity of $\Phi$ (Csordas–Varga), the
  Turán inequalities (Csordas–Norfolk–Varga), hyperbolicity of the Jensen polynomials $J^{d,n}$
  for $d\le 8$ and for large shift $n$ at each degree $d$ (Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier) — and the
  Pólya–Jensen criterion (RH if and only if all $J^{d,n}$ are hyperbolic).
- Remark 3.3: what is missing is uniformity in $(d,n)$; no order-by-order equivalence between
  different positivity hierarchies is claimed.
- Proposition 4.1: for a curve over $\mathbb F_q$ the zeros are periodic with period
  $2\pi i/\log q$ (the ordinates with period $2\pi/\log q$), so RH is a condition on $2g$
  numbers, supplied by the Hodge index theorem on $C\times C$.
- Proposition 4.2: the ordinates of the zeros of $\zeta$ on the critical line do not lie in
  finitely many arithmetic progressions with a common difference, because the number of distinct
  ordinates up to $T$ is $\gg T\log T$. Remark 4.4 notes that the Linear Independence
  conjecture would give the same conclusion but is not needed.

## Known and new

All theorems quoted are known; Proposition 4.2 is an immediate consequence of known results on
zeros on the critical line. What the note adds is the checklist and the arrangement of the
material. Nothing here is progress towards RH.
