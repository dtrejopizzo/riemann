\begin{abstract}
This expository note relates three descriptions of the size of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line
--- the moment exponents $(\log T)^{k^2}$, the exponents $\beta^2$ attached to the powers
$|\zeta(\half+it)|^{2\beta}$, and the Fyodorov--Hiary--Keating law for the maximum of $\log|\zeta|$ on short
intervals --- to the weight $z^{\omega(n)}$, where $\omega(n)$ is the number of distinct prime factors of $n$. We
recall that $\dk(n)=k^{\omega(n)}$ on squarefree $n$, so that the diagonal sum $\sum_{n\le N}\dk(n)^2/n$ has the
Selberg--Delange exponent $k^2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:weight}); we prove that under the weight $z^{\omega(n)}/n$ on
$n\le N$ the function $\omega(n)$ concentrates at $z\loglog N$ with Gaussian fluctuations, for every $z>0$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ld}); and we record that grouping the primes by $\lfloor\loglog p\rfloor=j$ gives levels of
variance $\half+O(e^{-j})$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:levels}). Sections 3, 5 and 6 and Proposition~\ref{prop:dict} are proved and classical in
substance. The link with the powers $|\zeta|^{2\beta}$ in Section 4 and the whole of Section 7 are heuristic and
restate the known branching picture: under the substitution
$z=\beta^2$ the critical value $\beta_c=1$ of the short-interval chaos corresponds formally to the trivial weight
$z=1$, and a branching random walk with $\loglog T$ generations, $\log T$ leaves and increment variance $\half$
has maximum $\loglog T-\tfrac34\llll T+O_{\Pp}(1)$, the form of the maximum of $\log|\zeta|$ for which the upper
bound and the tightness are theorems of Arguin, Bourgade and Radziwi{\l}{\l}. Section 8 contains small-scale
numerical illustrations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\subsection{Large values of \texorpdfstring{$\zeta$}{zeta} on the critical line}
Let $\zeta$ denote the Riemann zeta function and write
\[
F(t):=\log|\zeta(\half+it)| .
\]
Three quantitative theories describe the size of $F$ as $T\to\infty$.

\emph{(i) Typical size.} Selberg's central limit theorem \cite{Selberg} states that $F(t)/\sqrt{\half\loglog T}$
converges in distribution, for $t$ chosen uniformly in $[T,2T]$, to a standard Gaussian, and Selberg's moment
computation gives the second moment of $F$ on $[T,2T]$ as $\sim\half\loglog T$.

\emph{(ii) Moments.} For $k\in\NN$ one expects
\[
\frac1T\int_T^{2T}\bigl|\zeta(\half+it)\bigr|^{2k}\,dt\ \sim\ \frac{a_k\,g_k}{(k^2)!}\,(\log T)^{k^2},
\]
with $a_k$ an arithmetic (Euler-product) factor and $g_k$ an integer; this is a theorem for $k=1,2$
\cite{HL,Ingham} (with $g_1=1$, $g_2=2$) and, with $g_k/(k^2)!=G(k+1)^2/G(2k+1)$ in terms of the Barnes
$G$-function, the conjecture of Keating and Snaith \cite{KS} for general $k$ (see \cite{CFKRS} for the full main
term). Under the Riemann Hypothesis the upper bound $\ll_k(\log T)^{k^2}$ is due to Harper \cite{HarperMoments},
sharpening Soundararajan's $(\log T)^{k^2+\varepsilon}$ \cite{Sound}. The exponent $k^2$ is the object of interest
below.

\emph{(iii) The maximum.} Fyodorov, Hiary and Keating \cite{FHK,FK} conjectured, on the basis of a
log-correlated/branching heuristic, that for $t$ chosen uniformly in $[T,2T]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fhk}
\max_{|h|\le1}\log\bigl|\zeta(\half+i(t+h))\bigr|\ =\ \loglog T\ -\ \tfrac34\,\llll T\ +\ O_{\Pp}(1).
\end{equation}
The leading term is a theorem of Arguin--Belius--Bourgade--Radziwi{\l}{\l}--Soundararajan \cite{ABBRS} (and, with
the lower bound conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis, of Najnudel \cite{Najnudel}; see the survey
\cite{HarperBourbaki}); the upper bound with the correction $-\tfrac34\llll T$ and the tightness of the recentred
maximum are due to Arguin--Bourgade--Radziwi{\l}{\l} \cite{ABR1,ABR2}, after Harper \cite{HarperFHK}. Thus $F$
behaves, in its extreme values, like a log-correlated field, and \eqref{eq:fhk} has the form of the maximum of a
branching random walk: a leading term $c\,n$ and a logarithmic correction $-\frac{3}{2\theta}\log n$ in the number
$n=\loglog T$ of generations (\S\ref{sec:max}).

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\omega$}{omega}-viewpoint}
For $n\in\NN$ write $\omega(n)$ for the number of distinct primes dividing $n$ and $\Omega(n)$ for the number
with multiplicity. Sums weighted by $z^{\omega(n)}$ are governed by the Selberg--Delange method
\cite{SelbergSathe,Delange}, \cite[Ch.~II.5]{Tenenbaum}, and produce powers of $\log N$ whose exponent is the
weight $z$; the distribution of $\omega(n)$ itself is the content of the Erd\H{o}s--Kac theorem \cite{ErdosKac}
and, for the local laws, of the work of Sathe and Selberg \cite{Sathe,SelbergSathe}. Since
$\dk(n)=k^{\omega(n)}$ on squarefree $n$, the exponent $k^2$ of the diagonal of the $2k$-th moment is the
Selberg--Delange exponent of the weight $z=k^2$. The purpose of this note is to set out this elementary link, to
describe the measure $z^{\omega(n)}/n$ on $n\le N$ for every $z>0$, and to display the level structure of the
primes behind the branching heuristic for \eqref{eq:fhk}.

\subsection{Scope}\label{ssec:scope}
The statements of \S\ref{sec:weight}, \S\ref{sec:ld} and \S\ref{sec:branch}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:dict}, are
proved; they are classical in substance or routine consequences of the Selberg--Delange method and of Mertens'
theorem. The relation between the weight $z=\beta^2$ and the powers $|\zeta|^{2\beta}$ in \S\ref{sec:dict}, and
the passage from the branching model to the maximum of $\log|\zeta|$ in \S\ref{sec:max}, are heuristic: nothing
about the maximum is proved here, the theorems being those of \cite{ABBRS,Najnudel,ABR1,ABR2,HarperFHK}. The
computations of \S\ref{sec:numerics} are at small scale ($\loglog X<3$) and confirm no asymptotic statement.

\section{Notation and analytic background}\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Arithmetic functions}
For $n=\prod_i p_i^{a_i}$ we write $\omega(n)=\#\{i\}$, $\Omega(n)=\sum_i a_i$, and call $n$ squarefree if all
$a_i=1$. The $k$-fold divisor function is
\[
\dk(n)=\#\Bigl\{(m_1,\dots,m_k)\in\NN^k:\ m_1\cdots m_k=n\Bigr\}=\sum_{m_1\cdots m_k=n}1,
\]
multiplicative, with $\dk(p^a)=\binom{a+k-1}{k-1}$ and Dirichlet series $\sum_n\dk(n)n^{-s}=\zeta(s)^k$
($\re s>1$). More generally, for $z\in\mathbb C$ we write $d_z$ for the multiplicative function with
$d_z(p^a)=\binom{a+z-1}{a}$, so that $\sum_n d_z(n)n^{-s}=\zeta(s)^z$ for $\re s>1$ (principal branch).

\subsection{The Selberg--Delange method}
We use the following consequence of the Selberg--Delange method \cite[Ch.~II.5]{Tenenbaum}.

\begin{lemma}[Selberg--Delange]\label{lem:lsd}
Let $A>0$ and $0<\delta\le1$. Let $z\in\mathbb C$ with $|z|\le A$, and let $g$ be an arithmetic function with
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{g(n)}{n^{s}}=\zeta(s)^z H(s)\quad(\re s>1),\qquad
H(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{h(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|h(n)|}{n^{1-\delta}}\le A .
\]
Then, uniformly in such $z$ and $g$, for $N\ge3$,
\[
\sum_{n\le N}\frac{g(n)}{n}\ =\ \frac{H(1)}{\Gamma(z+1)}\,(\log N)^{z}
+O_{A,\delta}\Bigl(\bigl(1+(\log N)^{\re z-1}\bigr)\loglog N\Bigr).
\]
In particular, if $z>0$ is real and $H(1)\ne0$, then
$\sum_{n\le N}g(n)/n\sim H(1)\,\Gamma(z+1)^{-1}(\log N)^{z}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The function $H$ is holomorphic for $\re s>1-\delta$ and bounded by $A$ for $\re s\ge1-\delta$. By
\cite[Thm.~II.5.2]{Tenenbaum} (with one term of the expansion), uniformly for $|z|\le A$ and $t\ge3$,
\[
D(t):=\sum_{n\le t}g(n)=t(\log t)^{z-1}\Bigl(\frac{H(1)}{\Gamma(z)}+O_{A,\delta}\Bigl(\frac1{\log t}\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
For $t<3$ one has $D(t)=O_A(1)$, since $g=d_z*h$, $|d_z(n)|\le A$ for $n=2,3$ and $|h(n)|\le3A$ for $n\le3$.
By partial summation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n\le N}\frac{g(n)}{n}&=\frac{D(N)}{N}+\int_1^N\frac{D(t)}{t^2}\,dt\\
&=\frac{H(1)}{\Gamma(z)}\int_3^N\frac{(\log t)^{z-1}}{t}\,dt
+O_{A,\delta}\Bigl(1+(\log N)^{\re z-1}+\int_3^N\frac{(\log t)^{\re z-2}}{t}\,dt\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first integral equals $\bigl((\log N)^z-(\log3)^z\bigr)/z$ (and $\loglog N-\loglog3$ for $z=0$, where
$1/\Gamma(z)=0$), and $z\,\Gamma(z)=\Gamma(z+1)$, so the main term is
$H(1)\Gamma(z+1)^{-1}(\log N)^z+O_A(1)$. In the last integral
$(\log t)^{\re z-2}\le\bigl(1+(\log N)^{\re z-1}\bigr)(\log t)^{-1}$ for $3\le t\le N$, which gives the bound
$\bigl(1+(\log N)^{\re z-1}\bigr)\loglog N$.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis on $H$ is verified in the same way in all the applications below. There $g$ is multiplicative
with $g(p)=z$ and $|g(p^a)|\le K(a+1)^K$ for a constant $K$ depending only on $A$; then $h$ is multiplicative,
its local factor is $(1-x)^z\sum_{a\ge0}g(p^a)x^a=1+O(x^2)$ with $x=p^{-s}$, so that $h(p)=0$ and
$|h(p^a)|\le K'(a+1)^{K'}$ with $K'=K'(A)$, and
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|h(n)|}{n^{3/4}}\le\prod_p\Bigl(1+\sum_{a\ge2}\frac{K'(a+1)^{K'}}{p^{3a/4}}\Bigr)<\infty .
\]
Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:lsd} applies with $\delta=\tfrac14$, and $H$ is an Euler product absolutely convergent for
$\re s>\half$.

In particular, for $g(n)=z^{\omega(n)}$ one has
$\sum_n g(n)n^{-s}=\prod_p\bigl(1+z(p^s-1)^{-1}\bigr)=\zeta(s)^zH_z(s)$ with
$H_z(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{z}\bigl(1+z(p^s-1)^{-1}\bigr)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:lsd} gives, for every real $z>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zomega}
\sum_{n\le N}\frac{z^{\omega(n)}}{n}\ \sim\ C(z)\,(\log N)^{z},\qquad
C(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z+1)}\prod_p\Bigl(1-\tfrac1p\Bigr)^{z}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{z}{p-1}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The function $C$ is entire, positive on $(0,\infty)$, and $C(1)=1$. More precisely, writing
$S_N(z)=\sum_{n\le N}z^{\omega(n)}/n$, the lemma gives, uniformly for complex $z$ with $|z|\le A$ and $N\ge3$,
\[
S_N(z)=C(z)(\log N)^z+O_A\Bigl(\bigl(1+(\log N)^{\re z-1}\bigr)\loglog N\Bigr).
\]

\subsection{The prime model of \texorpdfstring{$\log|\zeta|$}{log|zeta|}}
On the critical line one has, in the sense made precise by Selberg's method \cite{Selberg} and its descendants,
the approximation
\[
\log\bigl|\zeta(\half+it)\bigr|\ \approx\ \re\sum_{p\le X}\frac{1}{p^{\,1/2+it}}\ =\ \sum_{p\le X}\frac{\cos(t\log p)}{\sqrt p}\ =:\ G(t),
\]
with prime-power and remainder terms of lower order, for a suitable truncation $X=X(T)$. We use $G=G_X$ as a
\emph{model}. Expectations and variances of $G$ are taken with respect to $t$ uniform on $[T,2T]$ in the limit
$T\to\infty$ with $X$ fixed; equivalently, the numbers $p^{-it}$ are replaced by independent random variables
uniform on the unit circle. Then $\Ee G=0$ and, by Mertens' theorem \cite{Mertens},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
V:=\Var G=\half\sum_{p\le X}\frac1p\ \sim\ \half\loglog X,
\end{equation}
matching Selberg's variance when $\loglog X\sim\loglog T$. The covariance is
\[
\Ee\bigl[G(t)G(t+h)\bigr]=\half\sum_{p\le X}\frac{\cos(h\log p)}{p}=\half\log\frac1{|h|}+O(1)
\qquad\bigl((\log X)^{-1}\le|h|\le1\bigr),
\]
by the prime number theorem with remainder and partial summation: the primes with $\log p\le1/|h|$ contribute
$\half\loglog e^{1/|h|}+O(1)$ and the remaining ones $O(1)$. This logarithmic correlation is the source of the
branching structure.

\subsection{Multiplicative chaos}
For $\beta>0$ consider the random measures $|\zeta(\half+i(t+h))|^{2\beta}\,dh$ on $|h|\le1$, with $t$ uniform
on $[T,2T]$. Saksman and Webb \cite{SaksmanWebb} relate $\zeta$ on such intervals to Gaussian multiplicative
chaos. For a field of covariance $\half\log(1/|h|)$ the chaos of parameter $\beta$ is subcritical for $\beta<1$
and $\beta_c=1$ is its critical value: for $\beta>1$ the mass of the measure is expected to be dominated by the
neighbourhood of the maximum (freezing; see \cite{FHK,FK,HarperFHK}). This transition concerns the short-interval
partition function $\int_{-1}^{1}|\zeta(\half+i(t+h))|^{2\beta}\,dh$ for typical $t$. For the average
$\frac1T\int_T^{2T}|\zeta(\half+it)|^{2\beta}\,dt$ the conjectured order of magnitude is $(\log T)^{\beta^2}$ for
every $\beta>0$, with no transition; the exponent $\beta^2$ is the subject of \S\ref{sec:dict}.

\section{The per-prime weight and the moment exponent}\label{sec:weight}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dk}
For squarefree $n$ one has $\dk(n)=k^{\omega(n)}$, and consequently $\dk(n)^2=(k^2)^{\omega(n)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\dk$ is multiplicative and $\dk(p)=\binom{k}{k-1}=k$. For squarefree $n=p_1\cdots p_r$ with $r=\omega(n)$,
$\dk(n)=\prod_{i=1}^r\dk(p_i)=k^r=k^{\omega(n)}$. Squaring gives $\dk(n)^2=k^{2\omega(n)}=(k^2)^{\omega(n)}$.
\end{proof}

Combinatorially, writing a squarefree $n$ as an ordered product of $k$ factors amounts to assigning each of its
$\omega(n)$ prime factors to one of the $k$ slots, whence $k^{\omega(n)}$ choices. The identity is of course
standard.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:weight}
Let $k\ge1$ be an integer.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item For every $N\ge1$,
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{n\le N}\frac{\dk(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\Bigr|^2dt=\sum_{n\le N}\frac{\dk(n)^2}{n},
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_{n\le N}\frac{\dk(n)^2}{n}\ \sim\ A_k\,(\log N)^{k^2}
\]
as $N\to\infty$, with $A_k=B_k(1)/\Gamma(k^2+1)$ and $B_k$ as in the proof.
\item For every real $z>0$, the sum restricted to squarefree $n$ (denoted by $\flat$) satisfies
\[
\sideset{}{^\flat}\sum_{n\le N}\frac{z^{\omega(n)}}{n}\ \sim\ C^{\flat}(z)\,(\log N)^{z},\qquad
C^{\flat}(z)=\frac1{\Gamma(z+1)}\prod_p\Bigl(1-\tfrac1p\Bigr)^{z}\Bigl(1+\tfrac zp\Bigr).
\]
In particular $\sum^{\flat}_{n\le N}\dk(n)^2/n=\sum^{\flat}_{n\le N}(k^2)^{\omega(n)}/n\sim
C^{\flat}(k^2)(\log N)^{k^2}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) Expanding the square, the mean value of $(m/n)^{it}$ over $[0,T]$ tends to $1$ if $m=n$ and to $0$
otherwise, which gives the first identity. One has $\sum_n\dk(n)^2n^{-s}=\zeta(s)^{k^2}B_k(s)$, where
\[
B_k(s)=\prod_p\,(1-p^{-s})^{k^2}\sum_{a\ge0}\binom{a+k-1}{k-1}^2p^{-as}
\]
is an Euler product absolutely convergent for $\re s>\half$, with $B_k(1)>0$ since every factor is positive at
$s=1$ (for $k=2$, $B_2(s)=1/\zeta(2s)$). Lemma~\ref{lem:lsd} applied to $g=\dk^2$, $z=k^2$, $H=B_k$ gives the
asymptotic.

(b) Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:lsd} to $g=\mu^2z^{\omega}$, for which
$\sum_ng(n)n^{-s}=\prod_p(1+zp^{-s})=\zeta(s)^z\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^z(1+zp^{-s})$. The last assertion follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:dk}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
We call $\sum_{n\le N}\dk(n)^2/n$ the \emph{diagonal} of the $2k$-th moment: it is the mean square of the
Dirichlet polynomial approximating $\zeta^k$. Theorem~\ref{thm:weight} places its exponent $k^2$ among the
exponents of the $\omega$-weighted sums \eqref{eq:zomega}, at $z=k^2$; the asymptotic itself is classical. The
value $k^2=k\cdot k$ records that in $|\zeta|^{2k}=\zeta^k\overline{\zeta}^{\,k}$ each factor contributes one
divisor weight $\dk$, with $\dk(p)=k$, and that on the diagonal the two are multiplied prime by prime. The
diagonal sum is not the moment: for $k=1$ it gives the correct asymptotic $\log T$ with $N=T$, but for $k=2$
the diagonal constant $A_2=1/(4\pi^2)$ is half of Ingham's constant $1/(2\pi^2)$, and for $k\ge3$ the factor
$g_k$ has no counterpart in the diagonal.
\end{remark}

\section{The substitution \texorpdfstring{$z=\beta^2$}{z=beta\textasciicircum 2} and the critical value}\label{sec:dict}

Replacing the integer $k$ by a real parameter $\beta>0$, the generalized divisor function $d_\beta$ has
$d_\beta(p)=\beta$, hence $d_\beta(p)^2=\beta^2$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dict}
Let $\beta>0$ and let $d_\beta$ be the multiplicative function with $d_\beta(p^a)=\binom{a+\beta-1}{a}$, so
that $\sum_nd_\beta(n)n^{-s}=\zeta(s)^\beta$. Then $d_\beta(n)=\beta^{\omega(n)}$ for squarefree $n$, and
\[
\sum_{n\le N}\frac{d_\beta(n)^2}{n}\ \sim\ A_\beta\,(\log N)^{\beta^2},\qquad
A_\beta=\frac1{\Gamma(\beta^2+1)}\prod_p\Bigl(1-\tfrac1p\Bigr)^{\beta^2}\sum_{a\ge0}\binom{a+\beta-1}{a}^2p^{-a}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows from multiplicativity and $d_\beta(p)=\beta$. For the second, the local factor of
$\sum_nd_\beta(n)^2n^{-s}$ is $1+\beta^2p^{-s}+\cdots$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:lsd} applies with $z=\beta^2$ and
$H(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{\beta^2}\sum_{a\ge0}\binom{a+\beta-1}{a}^2p^{-as}$, and $H(1)>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:dict}
The following is heuristic. The diagonal of the $2\beta$-th moment of $\zeta$ is the sum of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dict} with $N=T$, which suggests
$\frac1T\int_T^{2T}|\zeta(\half+it)|^{2\beta}\,dt\asymp(\log T)^{\beta^2}$. The lower bound
$\gg_\beta(\log T)^{\beta^2}$ is known for all $\beta>0$ \cite{HS}; the upper bound is known for $0<\beta\le2$
\cite{HRS} and, under the Riemann Hypothesis, for all $\beta>0$ \cite{HarperMoments}. Under the
substitution $z=\beta^2$ the critical value $\beta_c=1$ of the short-interval chaos corresponds to $z=1$, where
the weight $z^{\omega(n)}$ is trivial and $\sum_{n\le N}1/n=\log N+O(1)$. This correspondence is formal: the
$\omega$-weighted sum itself shows no transition at $z=1$, since \eqref{eq:zomega} and Theorem~\ref{thm:ld} hold
in the same form for all $z>0$. The values $0<\beta<1$, $\beta=1$, $\beta>1$ correspond to $0<z<1$, $z=1$, $z>1$,
and for every $z\ne1$, below as well as above $1$, the weight moves the mass away from the integers with
$\omega(n)\approx\loglog N$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:ld}).
\end{remark}

\section{The distribution of \texorpdfstring{$\omega(n)$}{omega(n)} under the weight
\texorpdfstring{$z^{\omega(n)}/n$}{z\textasciicircum omega(n)/n}}\label{sec:ld}

Put $\lambda=\loglog N$. By \eqref{eq:zomega}, $S_N(z)/S_N(1)\sim C(z)e^{(z-1)\lambda}$, which is $C(z)$ times
the generating function $\Ee[z^{P}]$ of a Poisson variable $P$ of mean $\lambda$; the factor $C(z)$ is the
analogue, for the harmonic weight $1/n$, of the Sathe--Selberg correction \cite{Sathe,SelbergSathe},
\cite[Ch.~II.6]{Tenenbaum}. Tilting by $z^{\omega(n)}$ moves the mean of $\omega$ from $\lambda$ to $z\lambda$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ld}
Fix $z>0$, put $\lambda=\loglog N$ and $S_N(z)=\sum_{n\le N}z^{\omega(n)}/n$, and let $\mu_{N,z}$ be the
probability measure on $\{1,\dots,N\}$ with
\[
\mu_{N,z}(\{n\})=\frac{z^{\omega(n)}}{n\,S_N(z)} .
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item For every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is $c=c(z,\varepsilon)>0$ such that
\[
\mu_{N,z}\bigl(\,|\omega(n)-z\lambda|>\varepsilon z\lambda\,\bigr)\ \ll_{z,\varepsilon}\ (\log N)^{-c}.
\]
One may take $c=z\bigl((1+\varepsilon)\log(1+\varepsilon)-\varepsilon\bigr)$.
\item Under $\mu_{N,z}$, the variable $(\omega(n)-z\lambda)/\sqrt{z\lambda}$ converges in distribution to a
standard Gaussian as $N\to\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $w\in\mathbb C$ one has $\Ee_{\mu_{N,z}}[w^{\omega}]=S_N(zw)/S_N(z)$. By the uniform form of
\eqref{eq:zomega} stated after it, for $|w|\le2$,
\begin{align*}
S_N(zw)&=C(zw)(\log N)^{zw}+O_z\Bigl(\bigl(1+(\log N)^{z\re w-1}\bigr)\lambda\Bigr),\\
S_N(z)&=C(z)(\log N)^z\bigl(1+O_z(\lambda(\log N)^{-\min(1,z)})\bigr),
\end{align*}
and $C(z)>0$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\Ee_{\mu_{N,z}}[w^{\omega}]=\frac{C(zw)}{C(z)}\,e^{z\lambda(w-1)}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)
+O_z\Bigl(\lambda\,(\log N)^{-z}+\lambda\,(\log N)^{z\re w-z-1}\Bigr).
\end{equation*}

(i) Let $0<w\le2$ be real. Both error terms are
$\ll_z\lambda(\log N)^{-\min(1,zw)}e^{z\lambda(w-1)}$, so that
$\Ee_{\mu_{N,z}}[w^{\omega}]\ll_{z,w}e^{z\lambda(w-1)}$. By Markov's inequality with $w=1+\varepsilon$,
\[
\mu_{N,z}\bigl(\omega\ge(1+\varepsilon)z\lambda\bigr)\le w^{-(1+\varepsilon)z\lambda}\,\Ee_{\mu_{N,z}}[w^{\omega}]
\ll\exp\bigl(-z\lambda\,((1+\varepsilon)\log(1+\varepsilon)-\varepsilon)\bigr),
\]
and with $w=1-\varepsilon$,
\[
\mu_{N,z}\bigl(\omega\le(1-\varepsilon)z\lambda\bigr)\le w^{-(1-\varepsilon)z\lambda}\,\Ee_{\mu_{N,z}}[w^{\omega}]
\ll\exp\bigl(-z\lambda\,((1-\varepsilon)\log(1-\varepsilon)+\varepsilon)\bigr).
\]
Both exponents are positive multiples of $\lambda=\loglog N$, and
$(1-\varepsilon)\log(1-\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\ge(1+\varepsilon)\log(1+\varepsilon)-\varepsilon>0$ for
$0<\varepsilon<1$ (the difference of the two sides vanishes at $\varepsilon=0$ and has derivative
$-\log(1-\varepsilon^2)\ge0$).

(ii) Fix $u\in\mathbb R$ and take $w=e^{i\theta}$ with $\theta=u/\sqrt{z\lambda}$. Then
$z\lambda(w-1)=iu\sqrt{z\lambda}-u^2/2+O_u(\lambda^{-1/2})$; in particular
$|e^{z\lambda(w-1)}|\asymp_u1$, the two error terms above are $O(\lambda(\log N)^{-\min(1,z)})=o(1)$ (note
$z\re w\le z$), and $C(zw)/C(z)\to1$ by continuity of $C$. Therefore
\[
\Ee_{\mu_{N,z}}\Bigl[\exp\Bigl(iu\,\frac{\omega-z\lambda}{\sqrt{z\lambda}}\Bigr)\Bigr]
=e^{-iu\sqrt{z\lambda}}\ \Ee_{\mu_{N,z}}[w^{\omega}]\ \longrightarrow\ e^{-u^2/2},
\]
and the claim follows from L\'evy's continuity theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For $z=1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:ld} is the Erd\H{o}s--Kac theorem \cite{ErdosKac} for the harmonic weight $1/n$ on
$n\le N$; for general $z$ it is the corresponding statement for the tilted weight and belongs to the
Sathe--Selberg circle of results \cite{Sathe,SelbergSathe}. The concentration is slow, the relative width being
$(z\loglog N)^{-1/2}$ and the factor $C(zw)/C(z)$ being far from $1$ at accessible $N$ (see
\S\ref{sec:numerics}). The following interpretation is heuristic. For $z=\beta^2$ the measure
$\mu_{N,\beta^2}$ restricted to squarefree $n$ is proportional to the diagonal weight $d_\beta(n)^2/n$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:dict}), so in the diagonal approximation the $2\beta$-th moment of $\zeta$ is carried by
integers with about $\beta^2\loglog N$ prime factors. In this sense the distributional theory of $\omega(n)$ and
the moment theory of $\zeta$ are linked, at the level of the diagonal exponent, by $z=\beta^2$; no statement
about the chaos measures of \S\ref{sec:prelim} or about the maximum of $\zeta$ follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:ld}.
\end{remark}

\section{The branching hierarchy}\label{sec:branch}

We organize the primes of the model $G$ by a doubly logarithmic scale.

\begin{definition}
For an integer $j\ge0$ the \emph{$j$-th level} is the set of primes
$\mathcal L_j=\{p:\ \lfloor\loglog p\rfloor=j\}=\{p:\ e^{e^{j}}\le p<e^{e^{j+1}}\}$, and
$\mathcal L_{-1}=\{2\}$ (the only prime with $\loglog p<0$).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[levels of asymptotically equal variance]\label{prop:levels}
The variance of the model contributed by level $\mathcal L_j$ is
\[
V_j:=\half\sum_{p\in\mathcal L_j}\frac1p\ =\ \half+O(e^{-j})\qquad(j\ge0),
\]
by Mertens' theorem in the form $\sum_{p\le x}1/p=\loglog x+M+O(1/\log x)$. Consequently, for the truncation
$X=T$ and $n=\lfloor\loglog T\rfloor$, the model $G$ is the sum of the contributions of the single prime $2$
(of variance $V_{-1}=\tfrac14$), of the full levels $\mathcal L_0,\dots,\mathcal L_{n-1}$ and of the partial
level $\mathcal L_n\cap[2,T]$, and
\[
V=\half\sum_{p\le T}\frac1p=\half\loglog T+\frac M2+O\Bigl(\frac1{\log T}\Bigr)=\frac n2+O(1),
\]
in agreement with \eqref{eq:variance}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Mertens' theorem with remainder \cite{Mertens}, \cite[Ch.~I.1]{Tenenbaum},
\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal L_j}\frac1p=\loglog e^{e^{j+1}}-\loglog e^{e^{j}}+O(e^{-j})=1+O(e^{-j}),
\]
since $\log e^{e^{j}}=e^{j}$. The decomposition of the primes $p\le T$ is the definition of the levels, and the
formula for $V$ is Mertens' theorem at $x=T$.
\end{proof}

Thus the primes $p\le T$, organized by the doubly logarithmic scale of their size, form about $\loglog T$ levels
whose variances tend to $\half$ (numerically $V_0=0.422$, $V_1=0.461$; see \S\ref{sec:numerics}). The partial sums
\[
G_j(t)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal L_{-1}\cup\mathcal L_0\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal L_j}\frac{\cos(t\log p)}{\sqrt p}
\]
play the role of a walk after $j$ generations: by the covariance formula of \S\ref{sec:prelim}, the values of
$G_j$ at two points $t$, $t+h$ are strongly correlated when $|h|\le e^{-j}$ and the later increments at these
two points decorrelate. This is the approximate branching structure used in \cite{ABBRS}; the level sums are
not exactly the independent increments of a tree-indexed walk.

\section{The two terms of the maximum}\label{sec:max}

We recall how \eqref{eq:fhk} is read off the branching model. As stated in \S\ref{ssec:scope}, the passage from
the model to the maximum of $\log|\zeta|$ is the content of the cited works and is not reproved here.

\begin{heuristic}[the maximum from the hierarchy]\label{heur:max}
For a Gaussian branching random walk with $n$ generations, $e^{\kappa n}$ leaves and increment variance
$\sigma^2$, the maximum over the leaves is
\[
c\,n\ -\ \frac{3}{2\theta}\log n\ +\ O_{\Pp}(1),\qquad c=\sigma\sqrt{2\kappa},\quad\theta=\frac{c}{\sigma^2}
\]
\textup{(}Bramson \cite{Bramson} for branching Brownian motion; Addario-Berry--Reed \cite{ABReed} and A\"{\i}d\'ekon
\cite{Aidekon} for branching random walks\textup{)}. In the model $G$, level $j$ consists of the primes with
$\log p\in[e^{j},e^{j+1})$, so $G_j$ varies on the scale $e^{-j}$ in $t$ and an interval of length $1$ carries
about $e^{j}$ distinct values: $\kappa=1$. With $\sigma^2=\half$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:levels}) and
$n=\loglog T$ this gives $c=1$, $\theta=2$, hence, for $t$ uniform on $[T,2T]$,
\[
\max_{|h|\le1}G(t+h)\ =\ \loglog T\ -\ \tfrac34\,\llll T\ +\ O_{\Pp}(1),
\]
since $\log n=\llll T$. The coefficient $\tfrac34$ is $3/(2\theta)$ with $\theta=2$, and the argument
$\log n=\llll T$ is the logarithm of the number $n=\loglog T$ of levels.
\end{heuristic}

Thus the leading term $\loglog T$ of \eqref{eq:fhk} is $c\,n$ with $c=\sqrt{2\kappa\sigma^2}=1$; it equals
twice the total variance $V\sim\half\loglog T$ because $\kappa=2\sigma^2$ here (about $\log T=e^{2V}$
effectively independent values on an interval of length $1$). The subleading term $-\tfrac34\llll T$ is the
logarithmic correction of the branching random walk with these parameters. For $\log|\zeta|$ in place of $G$,
the leading term is the theorem of \cite{ABBRS} (see also \cite{Najnudel}), and the upper bound with the
correction and the tightness are the theorems of \cite{ABR1,ABR2}, after \cite{HarperFHK}.

\section{Numerical illustration}\label{sec:numerics}

The computations below are produced by the script \texttt{numerics.py} accompanying this note (sieving in
double precision). They are at small scale, $\loglog N<3$, and illustrate the statements above without
confirming any asymptotic.

\paragraph{The per-prime weight (Proposition~\ref{prop:dk}).} For $k=3$: $d_3(6)=9=3^{\omega(6)}$ and
$d_3(30)=27=3^{\omega(30)}$.

\paragraph{The \texorpdfstring{$\omega$}{omega}-weighted sum \eqref{eq:zomega}.} A least-squares fit of $\log S_N(z)$ against
$\loglog N$ on $30$ logarithmically spaced values of $N$ in $[3\cdot10^4,2\cdot10^6]$ gives slopes
$0.25$, $0.96$, $2.84$, $4.61$ for $z=\tfrac14,1,4,9$. At $N=2\cdot10^6$ the ratio $S_N(z)/(\log N)^z$ equals
$1.189$ for $z=\tfrac14$ (against $C(\tfrac14)=1.191$) and $1.040$ for $z=1$ (against $C(1)=1$; here
$S_N(1)=\log N+\gamma+O(1/N)$), while for $z=4$ and $z=9$ it is still about $4$ and $10^3$ times $C(z)$. For the
squarefree sum of Theorem~\ref{thm:weight}(b) the ratio is $1.013$ for $z=\tfrac14$ (against
$C^\flat(\tfrac14)=1.006$) and $0.680$ for $z=1$ (against $C^\flat(1)=6/\pi^2=0.608$).

\paragraph{The tilted mean (Theorem~\ref{thm:ld}).} At $N=2\cdot10^6$, where $\loglog N=2.675$, the mean of
$\omega$ under $\mu_{N,z}$ is $0.77$, $2.06$, $3.76$, $4.61$ for $z=\tfrac14,1,4,9$, against
$z\loglog N=0.67$, $2.67$, $10.70$, $24.07$: the mean increases with $z$, but for $z=4$ and $z=9$ it is far
below its asymptotic value at this height.

\paragraph{Levels (Proposition~\ref{prop:levels}).} Sieving to $X=10^7$: $V_{-1}=0.25$ (the prime $2$),
$V_0=0.422$, $V_1=0.461$, and the part of $\mathcal L_2$ below $10^7$ (the level ends at
$e^{e^3}\approx5.3\cdot10^8$) contributes $0.388$. The total is $V=1.521$, against
$\half(\loglog X+M)=1.521$.

\paragraph{The maximum of the model (\S\ref{sec:max}).} For $X=2\cdot10^3,\,2\cdot10^4,\,2\cdot10^5$ we
computed $\max_{0\le h\le1}G_X(t+h)$, sampled with step $0.002$ in $h$, for the $200$ values
$t=10^7+10i$, $0\le i<200$.
The mean of these $200$ maxima is $0.98$, $1.16$, $1.32$ (with standard deviation about $0.9$ in each
case), that is $0.43$, $0.45$, $0.48$ times $2V=2.29,\,2.55,\,2.76$; the corresponding values of
$(\loglog X-\tfrac34\llll X)/(2V)$ are $0.65$, $0.65$, $0.66$ (this expression is for the window $|h|\le1$ of \eqref{eq:fhk}; the change of window
length alters the maximum by $O(1)$). The maximum over a unit window is thus well below
$2V$ at these heights and increases with $X$, as the model predicts; the difference from the two-term
expression is of the size of the $O_{\Pp}(1)$ term, which is not negligible when $\loglog X<3$.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Summary}
The substitution $z=\beta^2$ places side by side objects usually treated by different techniques:
\begin{multline*}
\underbrace{\text{exponent $k^2$ of the diagonal}}_{\text{Thm.~\ref{thm:weight}}}
\ \longleftrightarrow\
\underbrace{\text{exponent $\beta^2$}}_{\text{Prop.~\ref{prop:dict}}}
\ \longleftrightarrow\
\underbrace{z\loglog N\text{ prime factors}}_{\text{Thm.~\ref{thm:ld}}}\\
\longleftrightarrow\
\underbrace{\loglog T\text{ branching levels}}_{\text{Prop.~\ref{prop:levels}}}
\ \longrightarrow\
\underbrace{\eqref{eq:fhk}}_{\S\ref{sec:max}}.
\end{multline*}
The exponent $k^2$ of the diagonal sum is the square of the value $\dk(p)=k$; the critical value $\beta_c=1$
corresponds formally to the trivial weight $z=1$; under the weight $z^{\omega(n)}/n$ the integers that matter
have about $z\loglog N$ prime factors; and the two constants of the maximum are those of a branching random walk
with $\loglog T$ levels, branching rate $\kappa=1$ and increment variance $\half$. The statements under the
braces (Theorem~\ref{thm:weight}, Proposition~\ref{prop:dict}, Theorem~\ref{thm:ld},
Proposition~\ref{prop:levels}) are proved. The arrows between them are not theorems: the first two are the
substitutions $z=k^2$ and $z=\beta^2$; the third is an analogy (by Mertens' theorem each level carries
$\sum_{p\in\mathcal L_j}1/p=1+O(e^{-j})$, so that under $\mu_{N,z}$ an integer has on average about $z$ prime
factors in each level below $N$); the last is heuristic (\S\ref{sec:max}).

\subsection{Proved and heuristic statements}
Lemma~\ref{lem:lsd}, Propositions~\ref{prop:dk}, \ref{prop:dict}, \ref{prop:levels} and
Theorems~\ref{thm:weight}, \ref{thm:ld} are unconditional. The identification of the diagonal sums with moments
of $\zeta$ is heuristic except in the following cases: for $k=1,2$ the moment asymptotics are theorems
\cite{HL,Ingham} (with a constant that for $k=2$ is twice the diagonal one), and for real $\beta$ the order of
magnitude $(\log T)^{\beta^2}$ is known in the ranges recalled in Remark~\ref{rem:dict}. The identification of
the model $G$ with $\log|\zeta|$ at the level of the maximum (\S\ref{sec:max}) is the content of
\cite{ABBRS,Najnudel,ABR1,ABR2,HarperFHK}.

\subsection{Relation to the Riemann Hypothesis}
No statement of this note assumes or bears on the Riemann Hypothesis.

\subsection{Open directions}
Three questions seem natural. First, to follow the level decomposition of \S\ref{sec:branch} through the moment
method of \cite{ABBRS}, so as to state its upper bound in terms of the levels $\mathcal L_j$. Second, to
describe the factor $g_k$ of the moment conjecture, which is absent from Theorem~\ref{thm:weight}, in terms of
correlations between levels (the additive divisor problem). Third, to relate the finer features of the
Fyodorov--Hiary--Keating law beyond \eqref{eq:fhk}, such as the limiting distribution of the recentred maximum,
to the level structure.
