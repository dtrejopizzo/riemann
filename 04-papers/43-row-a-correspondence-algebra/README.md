# A correspondence algebra and the Weil distribution on semilocal idele class groups

`S` is a finite set of places of `Q` containing the real place and at least one prime, and `C_S` is
the quotient of the semilocal idele group by the positive `S`-units, a twofold cover of the
`S`-idele class group of Connes and Meyer. The paper has two independent parts.

**Graph bimodules (Sections 2–4, Appendix B).** Each idele class defines a bimodule over a crossed
product by the positive `S`-units; these bimodules compose as graphs of dilations do and carry an
involution. Their Hochschild complex splits according to the elements of the group, and each
summand is determined up to homotopy: an exterior algebra on `|S_f|` generators, plus two copies of
a Koszul complex when the twist is trivial at the real place. The proof uses a twisted form of
excision in Hochschild homology. Appendix B proves the corresponding composition and duality
statements for graphs of automorphisms of a curve.

**The functional `T` (Sections 5–8, Appendix A).** On `C_c^∞(C_S)`, `T` is the sum over `S` of the
local terms of Weil's explicit formula, in Connes's form, plus a free constant `c` at the identity.
The paper

- computes the Fourier transform of the local terms, its exact supremum `m` and its poles;
- proves that the Hermitian form `T(F ⋆ G†)` is nondegenerate, and that it has infinite positive
  index if and only if `c > −m`;
- builds a completion containing the two ruling classes, and shows by a counterexample that the
  form does not extend continuously to them;
- shows that the closure of the cone of non-negative functions is proper;
- defines, on a Banach completion, a determinant relative to `T` and a Riemann–Roch pairing;
- describes the behaviour of `T` and of the form when `S` grows.

What is classical: `T` itself (Weil; Connes), the closed forms of its Fourier transform, and the
archimedean inequality for small support (Yoshida; Connes–Consani). The explicit formula as an
identity with a sum over zeros is not used.
