\title[The Defect of Off-Line Zeros]{The arithmetic defect of off-line zeros:\\
the modified explicit formula and two-scale structure}
\author{David Alejandro Trejo Pizzo}
\thanks{Independent Researcher. \texttt{dtrejopizzo@gmail.com}}


\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We study the arithmetic footprint that a \emph{finite} set of off-critical-line zeros of the
Riemann zeta function would leave. The off-line zeros form Klein four-orbits
$\{\half\pm b\pm i\gamma\}$. Assuming Hypothesis~D (there are $m$ quartets counted with
multiplicity, $0<m<\infty$, which is the negation of the Riemann Hypothesis
together with finiteness of the off-line set), the completed zeta function factors as
$\xi=\xi_0\,P_{4m}$ with $P_{4m}$ the polynomial of the off-line zeros and $\xi_0$ entire,
sharing the functional equation and having all zeros on the line. This gives a modified
explicit formula $\psi(x)=\psi_0(x)-D_m(x)$ whose defect $D_m$ is a finite sum of $4m$ explicit
oscillations; a single orbit contributes a term of exact order $x^{1/2+b}$ and logarithmic
energy $\asymp X^{2b}$ on $[X,2X]$. The companion $\zeta_0=s(s-1)\zeta(s)/(2P_{4m}(s))$ is
entire and is not a Dirichlet series on any half-plane, for an elementary growth reason that
does not distinguish off-line from on-line zeros. We compute the exact contribution of a
quartet to $\log|\xi(\half+it)|$, its excess over a double zero on the line, the mean square of
its localized part, and the size of the structure function of the normalized Chebyshev error, and
check that all of this is compatible with the unconditional prime number theorem. We list two
inputs that would suffice to exclude Hypothesis~D; neither is known. Nothing here assumes or
proves the Riemann Hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Riemann Hypothesis ($\RH$) is the assertion that every nontrivial zero
$\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ of $\zeta$ has $\beta=\half$. Suppose it fails. What, precisely, would a
finite collection of off-line zeros do to the distribution of primes, and where exactly does
the assumption that they exist collide with the arithmetic of $\zeta$? This paper describes the
first exactly and gives a reformulation of the second, under the working hypothesis that the
off-line set is finite.

We write $\xi(s)=\half s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$ for the completed zeta function,
$\Zoff=\{\rho:\beta_\rho\ne\half\}$ for the off-line zeros and $\Zcl=\{\rho:\beta_\rho=\half\}$
for the on-line ones. The functional equation $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and the reality
$\overline{\xi(\bar s)}=\xi(s)$ make $\Zoff$ invariant under the Klein four-group
$V=\{1,\sigma,\iota,j\}$, $\sigma(s)=\bar s$, $\iota(s)=1-s$, $j=\sigma\iota$, so the off-line
zeros come in quartets
\[
\mathcal O_j=\{\sigma_j\pm i\gamma_j,\ (1-\sigma_j)\pm i\gamma_j\},\qquad
\sigma_j=\half+b_j>\half,\ \ \gamma_j>0 .
\]
The companion paper \cite{Index} studies the zero side $Q$ of the Weil form on even real test
functions: each off-line quartet contributes one Lorentzian block, and if there are finitely many
quartets the negative index of $Q$ on that space is at most their number
\cite[Thm.~3.1, Cor.~6.5]{Index}. The present paper develops the \emph{arithmetic} content of a
finite off-line set.

The standing hypothesis throughout the conditional parts is:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Hypothesis D.}\ $0<m<\infty$: there are finitely many off-line zeros, and at least
one; they form $m\ge1$ Klein quartets counted with multiplicity.
\end{quote}

Hypothesis~D is stronger than $\neg\RH$: it is $\neg\RH$ together with finiteness of the
off-line set. Finiteness alone is implied by $\RH$ and is unconditionally open (it is not
delivered by the positive-proportion or zero-density theorems \cite[Prop.~7.3]{Index}); we assume it only to make the defect a finite, fully explicit object, and we
are careful to mark every statement that uses it.

\S\ref{sec:geometry} records the number of off-line zeros in a half-plane and its relation to
the count in \cite{Index}, together with a heuristic picture of the negative directions. \S\ref{sec:mef}--\S\ref{sec:wall} derive, under Hypothesis~D, the factorization
$\xi=\xi_0P_{4m}$ and the modified explicit formula $\psi=\psi_0-D_m$, analyze the defect
$D_m$, and show that the companion $\zeta_0$ is not a Dirichlet series. \S\ref{sec:twoscale}
computes the signature of an orbit on the critical line and in the Chebyshev variable, and
\S\ref{sec:barrier} compares it with known results and lists inputs that would suffice to
exclude Hypothesis~D. None of this assumes or proves $\RH$.

\section{The count of off-line zeros and the Weil form}\label{sec:geometry}

Let $Q$ be the form of \cite{Index} on the space $\mathcal V$ of even real test functions and
$\kappa_{\mathcal V}$ its negative index, the maximal dimension of a subspace of $\mathcal V$ on
which $Q$ is negative definite. For $f\in\mathcal V$ we write $\fhat(\rho)$ for the transform $\int f(u)e^{(\rho-1/2)u}\,du$ of \cite{Index}. For an
off-line orbit $\mathcal O_j$ write $\rho_j^+=\sigma_j+i\gamma_j$, $\rho_j^-=(1-\sigma_j)+
i\gamma_j$, and split the orbit by height into the upper sub-orbit $\mathcal O_j^+=\{\rho_j^+,
1-\bar\rho_j^+\}$ (both at height $+\gamma_j$) and the lower sub-orbit $\mathcal O_j^-=\{
\bar\rho_j^+,1-\rho_j^+\}$ (both at height $-\gamma_j$).

\begin{remark}[Heuristic: the form near one sub-orbit]\label{prop:restricted}
Formally, for test functions whose transform is concentrated near $+\gamma_j$, the zeros of
$\mathcal O_j^+$ contribute $\fhat(\rho_j^+)^2+\fhat(1-\bar\rho_j^+)^2$ to the zero sum of the
Weil form. If one had $\fhat(1-\bar\rho_j^+)=\overline{\fhat(\rho_j^+)}$, this would equal
$2[(\Re\fhat(\rho_j^+))^2-(\Im\fhat(\rho_j^+))^2]$, a form of signature $(1,1)$. That identity
holds for real even $f$, but for such $f$ the heights $\pm\gamma_j$ are identified
(on $\mathcal V$ the transform takes equal values at $\rho$ and $1-\rho$ and conjugate values at $\rho$ and $\bar\rho$ \cite[Lemma~2.3]{Index}), so the splitting by sub-orbit is not well defined there. The correct
local statement is the localization theorem of \cite[Thm.~3.1]{Index}, in which a whole
quartet contributes an indefinite block. We use this picture only as motivation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Heuristic: separation of heights]\label{prop:orth}
One expects test functions localized at distinct heights to be nearly $Q$-orthogonal. A proof
would require an explicit family of entire transforms, for instance
$\fhat(z)=e^{-(z-\gamma_j)^2/(2\delta^2)}$, an explicit bound for $|\fhat^+(\rho)\fhat^-(\rho)|$
at every zero, the sum of these bounds against the zero-counting function, and the analogous
estimates for the archimedean and prime terms. We do not carry this out, and nothing below
depends on it.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Count in a half-plane]\label{thm:parity}
If $\Zoff$ is finite, consisting of $m$ Klein quartets counted with multiplicity, then $\xi$ has
exactly $2m$ zeros, with multiplicity, in $\Re s<\half$; equivalently $E_\xi(z)=\xi(\half+iz)$
has exactly $2m$ zeros, with multiplicity, in the open upper half-plane.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A zero $\rho$ of $\xi$ corresponds to the zero $z=-i(\rho-\half)$ of $E_\xi$, with
$\Im z=\half-\Re\rho$, so the zeros of $E_\xi$ in $\Im z>0$ are those of $\xi$ with
$\Re\rho<\half$; all of them are off-line. The $V$-symmetry puts exactly two members of each
quartet, $1-\sigma_j\pm i\gamma_j$, in $\Re s<\half$, and since $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and
$\overline{\xi(\bar s)}=\xi(s)$, the four members of a quartet have the same order $\ell_j$. A
quartet of multiplicity $\ell_j$ thus contributes $2\ell_j$, and summing gives $2m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation with the Weil form]\label{rem:two}
By \cite[Thm.~3.1, Cor.~6.5]{Index}, on $\mathcal V$ each off-line quartet of multiplicity $\ell$
contributes one Lorentzian block $4\ell(u^2-v^2)$, where $u,v$ are the real and imaginary parts of
the evaluation of $\fhat$ at the corresponding off-axis point; with $m'\le m$ distinct quartets the
off-line part of $Q$ has signature $(m',m')$ and $\kappa_{\mathcal V}\le m'$, equality being open.
The number $2m$ of Proposition~\ref{thm:parity} is not shown to be the negative index of any
space: $E_\xi^\#=E_\xi$, where $E^\#(z)=\overline{E(\bar z)}$, so the de Branges space $H(E_\xi)$ of \cite{deBranges} is $\{0\}$
\cite[Rem.~6.6]{Index}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Heuristic: Lorentz planes]\label{thm:lorentz}
By \cite[Thm.~3.1]{Index}, on $\mathcal V$ the form $Q$ is the sum of the non-negative on-line part and $m'$ Lorentzian blocks, one per distinct quartet, each with one negative direction. One would expect $\kappa_{\mathcal V}=m'$; a proof would require test functions on which the on-line part and the positive parts of the blocks are small compared with the negative parts, in the spirit of Remark~\ref{prop:orth}, and this is not known. This is a picture, not a theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Regimes]\label{cor:trichotomy}
By \cite[Prop.~7.2]{Index}: if $\RH$ holds then $\kappa_{\mathcal V}=0$; if there are $m'$
distinct off-line quartets with $0<m'<\infty$ then $\kappa_{\mathcal V}\le m'$; if there are
infinitely many, \cite{Index} gives no finite bound on $\kappa_{\mathcal V}$. Whether the finite non-zero regime
can occur is open \cite[Prop.~7.3]{Index}.
\end{remark}

\section{The modified explicit formula}\label{sec:mef}

We now assume Hypothesis~D and read off the arithmetic consequences. The finiteness of $\Zoff$
makes the off-line part of the zero sum a genuine finite object.

\begin{proposition}[Factorization]\label{prop:factor}
Under Hypothesis~D,
\[
\xi(s)=\xi_0(s)\,P_{4m}(s),\qquad P_{4m}(s)=\prod_{\rho\in\Zoff}(s-\rho),
\]
where $P_{4m}$ is a degree-$4m$ polynomial with conjugate-symmetric roots and $\xi_0$ is
entire, shares the functional equation $\xi_0(s)=\xi_0(1-s)$, and has all its zeros on
$\Re s=\half$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Divide the Hadamard product of $\xi$ by the finite product over $\Zoff$; the quotient $\xi_0$
is entire of order one with zero set $\Zcl$. The set $\Zoff$ is $V$-invariant, so $P_{4m}$ is
invariant under $s\mapsto1-s$ and under conjugation, whence $\xi_0=\xi/P_{4m}$ inherits the
functional equation and reality.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Arithmetic defect and virtual Chebyshev function]\label{def:defect}
Under Hypothesis~D set
\[
D_m(x):=\sum_{\rho\in\Zoff}\frac{x^\rho}{\rho},\qquad
\psi_0(x):=x-\sum_{\rho\in\Zcl}\frac{x^\rho}{\rho}-\log(2\pi)-\half\log(1-x^{-2}).
\]
$D_m$ is real-valued ($\Zoff$ is conjugation-symmetric) and is a finite sum of $4m$ terms.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Modified explicit formula]\label{thm:mef}
Under Hypothesis~D, for every non-integer $x>1$,
\[
\psi(x)=\psi_0(x)-D_m(x),
\]
an exact identity. Writing $E(x)=\psi(x)-x$ and $E_0(x)=\psi_0(x)-x$, one has
$E(x)=E_0(x)-D_m(x)$ with $E_0(x)=O(x^{1/2}\log^2x)$; hence the growth of $E$ beyond
$x^{1/2}\log^2x$ is carried entirely by the defect $D_m$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Insert $\sum_\rho=\sum_{\Zcl}+\sum_{\Zoff}$ into the classical explicit formula
$\psi(x)=x-\sum_\rho x^\rho/\rho-\log(2\pi)-\half\log(1-x^{-2})$. Under Hypothesis~D the
off-line sum is finite, needing no regularization; rearranging gives the identity. For the
bound, use the truncated explicit formula $\psi(x)=x-\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}x^\rho/\rho+
O(x\log^2(xT)/T+\log x)$ \cite[Ch.~17]{Davenport}. For $T$ larger than every ordinate in
$\Zoff$ this gives $E_0(x)=E(x)+D_m(x)=-\sum_{\rho\in\Zcl,|\gamma|\le T}x^\rho/\rho+
O(x\log^2(xT)/T+\log x)$, and $\sum_{\rho\in\Zcl,|\gamma|\le T}|x^\rho/\rho|\le
x^{1/2}\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}|\rho|^{-1}\ll x^{1/2}\log^2T$. Take $T=x$.
\end{proof}

\section{Anatomy of the defect}\label{sec:anatomy}

Fix an orbit $\mathcal O_j$ and write $\theta_j^\pm=\arg\rho_j^\pm$.

\begin{proposition}[Single-orbit defect]\label{prop:Dj}
\[
D_j(x)=\underbrace{\frac{2x^{1/2+b_j}}{|\rho_j^+|}\cos(\gamma_j\log x-\theta_j^+)}_{\text{dominant}}
+\underbrace{\frac{2x^{1/2-b_j}}{|\rho_j^-|}\cos(\gamma_j\log x-\theta_j^-)}_{\text{subdominant}} .
\]
Consequently $D_m(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{2x^{1/2+b_j}}{|\rho_j^+|}\cos(\gamma_j\log x-\theta_j^+)
+O(x^{1/2-b_{\min}})$ as $x\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pair conjugate zeros: $\frac{x^{\sigma_j+i\gamma_j}}{\sigma_j+i\gamma_j}+\frac{x^{\sigma_j-
i\gamma_j}}{\sigma_j-i\gamma_j}=\frac{2x^{\sigma_j}}{|\rho_j^+|}\cos(\gamma_j\log x-\theta_j^+)$,
and similarly for the $(1-\sigma_j)$ pair with exponent $1-\sigma_j=\half-b_j$. Collecting the
subdominant terms over all orbits gives the asymptotic.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Defect energy]\label{prop:energy}
For the logarithmic energy $\mathcal E_j(X)=\int_X^{2X}|D_j(t)|^2t^{-2}\,dt$,
\[
\mathcal E_j(X)=\frac{2X^{2b_j}}{|\rho_j^+|^2}\Big(\frac{2^{2b_j}-1}{2b_j}
+\Re\Big[e^{2i(\gamma_j\log X-\theta_j^+)}\frac{2^{2b_j+2i\gamma_j}-1}{2b_j+2i\gamma_j}\Big]\Big)+O(1),
\]
(for a simple orbit; for multiplicity $\ell_j$ the leading term is multiplied by $\ell_j^2$),
and the second coefficient has modulus strictly smaller than the first, so
$\mathcal E_j(X)\asymp X^{2b_j}$. For the full defect,
$\int_X^{2X}|D_m(t)|^2t^{-2}\,dt=O(X^{2b_{\max}})$, and $\asymp X^{2b_{\max}}$ when a single
orbit attains $b_{\max}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $D_j=M_j+N_j$ with $M_j$ the dominant and $N_j$ the subdominant term of
Proposition~\ref{prop:Dj}. Then $|N_j|^2t^{-2}\ll t^{-1-2b_j}$ and $|M_jN_j|t^{-2}\ll t^{-1}$
integrate to $O(1)$ over $[X,2X]$. Using $\cos^2u=\half(1+\cos2u)$,
\[
\int_X^{2X}M_j^2t^{-2}dt=\frac{2}{|\rho_j^+|^2}\int_X^{2X}t^{2b_j-1}\big(1+\Re\,
e^{2i(\gamma_j\log t-\theta_j^+)}\big)dt,
\]
and $\int_X^{2X}t^{2b_j-1+2i\gamma_j}dt=X^{2b_j+2i\gamma_j}(2^{2b_j+2i\gamma_j}-1)/(2b_j+2i\gamma_j)$,
which gives the formula. With $w=2b_j+2i\gamma_j$ one has
$|(2^w-1)/w|=|\int_0^{\log2}e^{wu}du|<\int_0^{\log2}e^{2b_ju}du=(2^{2b_j}-1)/(2b_j)$, the
inequality being strict because $\gamma_j\ne0$. For $D_m$, the cross terms between orbits are
bounded by Cauchy--Schwarz, which gives the upper bound; if one orbit attains $b_{\max}$, all
other terms are $O(X^{2b_{\max}-\eta})$ for some $\eta>0$ and the lower bound follows from the
single-orbit case.
\end{proof}

\section{The companion function and the Euler product}\label{sec:wall}

The factorization $\xi=\xi_0P_{4m}$ is analytic. Set
$\zeta_0(s)=\xi_0(s)/(\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2))=s(s-1)\zeta(s)/(2P_{4m}(s))$. It is entire: the
zeros of $P_{4m}$ are zeros of $\zeta$ of at least the same order, and $\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$
has no zeros. From $\xi'/\xi=\xi_0'/\xi_0+P_{4m}'/P_{4m}$ one gets
\[
-\frac{\zeta_0'(s)}{\zeta_0(s)}=-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}-\frac1s-\frac1{s-1}
+\sum_{\rho\in\Zoff}\frac{1}{s-\rho}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[$\zeta_0$ is not a Dirichlet series]\label{lem:noEuler}
Under Hypothesis~D, $\zeta_0$ is entire and is not equal, on any right half-plane, to a
Dirichlet series $\sum_na_nn^{-s}$ convergent somewhere; in particular it has no Euler
product.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
As $\sigma\to+\infty$ along the reals, $\zeta(\sigma)\to1$, so
$\zeta_0(\sigma)\sim\sigma^{2-4m}/2$. If $F(s)=\sum_na_nn^{-s}$ converges at some $s_0$, it
converges absolutely for $\Re s>\Re s_0+1$; put $\sigma_1:=\Re s_0+2$. For $\sigma>\sigma_1$,
$|F(\sigma)-a_1|\le2^{\sigma_1-\sigma}\sum_{n\ge2}|a_n|n^{-\sigma_1}$, so
$F(\sigma)=a_1+O(2^{-\sigma})$. If $a_1\ne0$ this contradicts $\zeta_0(\sigma)\to0$ (as
$m\ge1$); if $a_1=0$, then $F(\sigma)=O(2^{-\sigma})$ contradicts the polynomial decay
$\sigma^{2-4m}/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The conclusion holds after dividing $\xi$ by any non-constant polynomial $P$ dividing it,
including one built from on-line zeros: if $s(s-1)\zeta(s)/(2P(s))=F(s)$ were a Dirichlet series
convergent somewhere, then $s(s-1)/(2P(s))=F(s)/\zeta(s)$ would be one too, hence of the form
$c+O(2^{-\sigma})$ as $\sigma\to+\infty$, which a non-constant rational function is not (and
$s(s-1)/(2P)$ is non-constant because $0$ and $1$ are not zeros of $\xi$). So
Lemma~\ref{lem:noEuler} does not detect off-line zeros and gives no contradiction with
Hypothesis~D.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The statement (RSRP)]\label{def:wall}
Consider entire functions $f$ of order one satisfying $f(s)=f(1-s)$ and of the form
$f(s)=\tfrac{s(s-1)}2\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)L(s)$, where $L(s)=\sum_na_nn^{-s}$ is absolutely
convergent for $\Re s>1$. For such $f$ let $\kappa(f)$ be the number of zeros of $f$, with
multiplicity, in $\Re s<\half$. (RSRP) is the statement that no such $f$ has
$0<\kappa(f)<\infty$; the letters (RSRP) are only a label.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{thm:wall}
By Hamburger's theorem \cite{Hamburger}, every $f$ in the class of
Definition~\ref{def:wall} is a constant multiple of $\xi$. Hence (RSRP) is equivalent to the
statement that $\zeta$ does not have finitely many, and at least one, off-line zeros, that is,
to the negation of Hypothesis~D. It follows from $\RH$ and does not imply $\RH$, since it says
nothing about the case of infinitely many off-line zeros.
\end{remark}

\section{Two-scale structure of a single orbit}\label{sec:twoscale}

A single off-line orbit manifests in the Chebyshev variable $x$ (equivalently $t=\log x$) and in
$\log|\zeta(\half+it)|$ near the ordinate $t=\gamma_j$. Since $\xi$ has order one and
$\sum_\rho\Re\rho/|\rho|^2<\infty$, one has $\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_\rho(1-s/\rho)$ with $\rho$
and $\bar\rho$ grouped together \cite[Ch.~12]{Davenport}; in particular the factor of a quartet
needs no exponential convergence factors.

\begin{theorem}[Contribution of a quartet on the line]\label{thm:depression}
Under Hypothesis~D, the contribution
$\delta_j(t)=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal O_j}\log|1-(\half+it)/\rho|$ of $\mathcal O_j$ to
$\log|\xi(\half+it)|$, and hence to $\log|\zeta(\half+it)|$, is
\[
\delta_j(t)=\log\!\big(b_j^2+(t-\gamma_j)^2\big)+\log\!\big(b_j^2+
(t+\gamma_j)^2\big)-\log|\rho_j^+|^2-\log|\rho_j^-|^2 .
\]
At $t=\gamma_j$ it equals $2\log b_j-2\log\gamma_j+\log4+O(\gamma_j^{-2})$. Relative to a double
zero at each of $\half\pm i\gamma_j$, the difference is
\[
\Delta_j(t)=\log\Big(1+\frac{b_j^2}{(t-\gamma_j)^2}\Big)+\log\Big(1+\frac{b_j^2}{(t+\gamma_j)^2}\Big)
+c_j,\qquad c_j=O(b_j^2/\gamma_j^2),
\]
with $c_j$ independent of $t$; the $t$-dependent part is $O(b_j^2/(t-\gamma_j)^2)$ for
$|t-\gamma_j|\gg b_j$, $t>0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $s=\half+it$ and $\rho=\half\pm b_j\pm i\gamma_j$ one has
$|1-s/\rho|^2=(b_j^2+(t\mp\gamma_j)^2)/|\rho|^2$, and $|\rho|=|\rho_j^+|$ or $|\rho_j^-|$;
summing the four logarithms gives the formula. At $t=\gamma_j$ it equals
$\log b_j^2+\log(b_j^2+4\gamma_j^2)-\log(|\rho_j^+|^2|\rho_j^-|^2)$, and
$|\rho_j^\pm|^2=\gamma_j^2+O(1)$. For a double zero at each of $\half\pm i\gamma_j$ the same
computation gives $\log(t-\gamma_j)^2+\log(t+\gamma_j)^2-2\log(\tfrac14+\gamma_j^2)$;
subtracting yields $\Delta_j$ with
$c_j=\log\frac{(1/4+\gamma_j^2)^2}{|\rho_j^+|^2|\rho_j^-|^2}=O(b_j^2/\gamma_j^2)$, since
$|\rho_j^+|^2|\rho_j^-|^2=(\tfrac14+\gamma_j^2+b_j^2)^2-b_j^2=(\tfrac14+\gamma_j^2)^2+
2b_j^2\gamma_j^2+O(b_j^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Mean square of the localized term]\label{prop:cesaro}
For $T\ge1$, uniformly in $\gamma_j>0$,
\[
\frac1T\int_T^{2T}\log^2\Big(1+\frac{b_j^2}{(t-\gamma_j)^2}\Big)\,dt\le\frac{8\pi\log2\;b_j}{T}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substituting $t-\gamma_j=b_ju$, the integral is at most
$b_j\int_{\mathbb R}\log^2(1+u^{-2})\,du$. Integrating by parts,
$\int_{\mathbb R}\log^2(1+u^{-2})du=4\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\log(1+u^{-2})}{1+u^2}du
=8\pi\log2$, using $\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\log(1+u^2)}{1+u^2}du=2\pi\log2$ and
$\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\log u^2}{1+u^2}du=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Structure-function bounds and their compatibility]\label{thm:struct}
Let $X(t)=(\psi(e^t)-e^t)e^{-t/2}$ and $S_2(\tau,T)=\frac1T\int_0^T|X(t+\tau)-X(t)|^2\,dt$, for
fixed $\tau>0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(Under Hypothesis~D.)} If $b_{\max}$ is attained by exactly one orbit $\mathcal O_j$, of
multiplicity $\ell_j$, then with $A_j=2\ell_j/|\rho_j^+|$ and $w_j=e^{(b_j+i\gamma_j)\tau}-1$,
\[
S_2(\tau,T)=\frac{A_j^2|w_j|^2}{4T}\Big(\frac{e^{2b_jT}}{b_j}+\Re\Big[\eta_j
\frac{e^{(2b_j+2i\gamma_j)T}}{b_j+i\gamma_j}\Big]\Big)+o\Big(\frac{e^{2b_jT}}T\Big),
\]
for a constant $|\eta_j|=1$; since $|b_j+i\gamma_j|>b_j$, $S_2(\tau,T)\asymp e^{2b_jT}/T$.
\item \emph{(Unconditional.)} By the prime number theorem with the error term of de la
Vall\'ee Poussin, $|\psi(x)-x|\le Cx\exp(-c_1\sqrt{\log x})$ \cite[Ch.~18]{Davenport},
$S_2(\tau,T)\le C'e^{T}e^{-2c_1\sqrt T}$.
\item \emph{(Compatibility.)} The two bounds never contradict:
$e^{2b_jT}/T\le Ce^Te^{-2c_1\sqrt T}$ for large $T$ because $2b_j<1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) Under Hypothesis~D, $X=X_0-Y$ with $X_0(t)=(\psi_0(e^t)-e^t)e^{-t/2}$ and
$Y(t)=D_m(e^t)e^{-t/2}$. The function $X_0$ is the Chebyshev error with only on-line zeros;
Cram\'er's argument \cite{Cramer}, which uses only $\sum_{\rho\in\Zcl}|\rho|^{-2}<\infty$, the
truncated explicit formula and $\Re\rho=\half$ for the zeros involved, gives
$\frac1T\int_0^T|X_0(t)|^2dt=O(1)$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:Dj}, the term of $Y$ from orbit $j$
is $A_je^{b_jt}\cos(\gamma_jt-\theta_j^+)$, and all other terms are $O(e^{(b_j-\eta)t})$ for
some $\eta>0$. Now
\[
A_je^{b_j(t+\tau)}\cos(\gamma_j(t+\tau)-\theta_j^+)-A_je^{b_jt}\cos(\gamma_jt-\theta_j^+)
=A_je^{b_jt}\Re[w_je^{i(\gamma_jt-\theta_j^+)}],
\]
whose square is
$\half A_j^2e^{2b_jt}(|w_j|^2+\Re[w_j^2e^{2i(\gamma_jt-\theta_j^+)}])$. Integrating over $[0,T]$
gives the main term with $\eta_j=w_j^2e^{-2i\theta_j^+}/|w_j|^2$, up to $O(1)$. The
remaining terms, and the cross terms with $X_0$ by Cauchy--Schwarz, are
$o(e^{2b_jT}/T)$. The lower bound uses $|\Re[\eta_je^{2i\gamma_jT}/(b_j+i\gamma_j)]|\le
1/|b_j+i\gamma_j|<1/b_j$. (2) From $|X(t)|\le Ce^{t/2}\exp(-c_1\sqrt t)$, by
square-integrating. (3) is the displayed inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{Comparison with known results; sufficient inputs}\label{sec:barrier}

\begin{remark}[Value distribution]\label{thm:chaos}
There is no lower bound on $\log|\zeta(\half+it)|$ on the line, which equals $-\infty$ at every
on-line zero. The negative value of $\delta_j(\gamma_j)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:depression} is
therefore not in tension with known value-distribution results, which concern maxima over short
intervals: the conjecture of Fyodorov, Hiary and Keating \cite{FHK}, and the results of
Arguin, Belius, Bourgade, Radziwi\l\l\ and Soundararajan \cite{ABBRS} and Najnudel
\cite{Najnudel}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[No lower bound on $b_j$]\label{rem:correction}
The zero-free region of Korobov and Vinogradov bounds $b_j$ from above only,
$b_j\le\half-c_0(\log\gamma_j)^{-2/3}(\log\log\gamma_j)^{-1/3}$ \cite[Ch.~6]{Ivic}; it gives no
lower bound on $b_j$, hence no upper bound on $|\log b_j|$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Inputs that would suffice]\label{thm:barrier}
\begin{itemize}
\item[\emph{(F1)}] An unconditional bound $\psi(x)-x=O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon_0})$ with
$\varepsilon_0<b_{\max}$ would contradict Proposition~\ref{prop:Dj}, since $x^{-1/2-b_{\max}}D_m(x)$ is, up to $o(1)$, a non-zero trigonometric polynomial in
$\log x$ (the orbits with $b_j=b_{\max}$ have distinct ordinates), hence does not tend to $0$,
and $E_0(x)=O(x^{1/2}\log^2x)$.
\item[\emph{(F2)}] The statement (RSRP) is, by Remark~\ref{thm:wall}, the negation of
Hypothesis~D.
\end{itemize}
Neither is known; (F1) for every $\varepsilon_0>0$ is equivalent to $\RH$.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

If the off-line set is finite, a Klein quartet of off-line zeros has an explicit footprint:
two zeros in each open half-plane (Proposition~\ref{thm:parity}) and one Lorentzian block of
the Weil form on even real test functions \cite{Index}, a defect term in the explicit formula (Theorem~\ref{thm:mef}) of exact order
$x^{1/2+b}$ with logarithmic energy $\asymp X^{2b}$ (Propositions~\ref{prop:Dj}
and~\ref{prop:energy}), and a contribution to $\log|\xi(\half+it)|$ whose excess over a double
zero on the line is, up to a constant $O(b^2/\gamma^2)$, localized near $t=\pm\gamma$, the
localized term $\log(1+b^2/(t-\gamma)^2)$ having mean square $O(b/T)$ on $[T,2T]$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:depression}, Proposition~\ref{prop:cesaro}). All of this is compatible with
the unconditional prime number theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:struct}). The companion
$\zeta_0=\xi_0/(\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2))$ is entire and is not a Dirichlet series
(Lemma~\ref{lem:noEuler}), but for a reason that does not see off-line zeros, and the statement
(RSRP) reduces, by Hamburger's theorem, to the negation of Hypothesis~D
(Remark~\ref{thm:wall}). Nothing here assumes or proves the Riemann Hypothesis.
