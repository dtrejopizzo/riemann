# Finiteness of off-line zeros and sequential self-approximation of zeta: a conditional reduction of the Riemann Hypothesis

Let m be the number of quadruples of non-trivial zeros of ζ off the critical line, so that RH is the statement m = 0. The paper studies how RH splits into a finiteness statement (m < ∞) and a dichotomy (m < ∞ ⇒ m = 0), and shows that the dichotomy follows from a sequential self-approximation hypothesis on ζ.

## What the paper proves or observes

- Theorem 2.3: if ζ satisfies sequential self-approximation on every compact disk of 1/2 < Re s < 1 (Hypothesis (SA)), then m ∈ {0, ∞}. The proof is Bagchi's Rouché argument; under m < ∞ one replica suffices.
- Corollary 5.1: m < ∞ together with (SA) implies RH. Both hypotheses are open; both are implied by RH; neither is known to be equivalent to RH.
- Theorem 4.1: for every [σ1, σ2] ⊂ (1/2, 1) and p ≥ 1, t ↦ ζ(·+it) is not Stepanov almost periodic as an L^p(σ1, σ2)-valued function. This is an elementary consequence of the unboundedness of ζ on vertical lines.
- An explicit even, real, order-one entire function with exactly m off-line quartets (no Euler product), and the remark that (SA) holds unconditionally on zero-free disks by Voronin universality (in its form for compact sets with connected complement). No numerical computations are used.
- Section 3, the opening paragraph of §4.2, §4.3 (including a Carlson-type count showing that good shifts near an off-line zero form a density-zero set), Observations 4.2 and 4.3, and the remarks on the Euler product are heuristic discussion.

## Known and new

Strong recurrence is equivalent to RH (Bagchi) and the Rouché replication step is his. What is new is the observation that, under finiteness of off-line zeros, a single replica (a sequence of good shifts rather than a positive-density set) suffices.
