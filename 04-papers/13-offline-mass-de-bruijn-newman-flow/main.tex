\begin{abstract}
We record the identity $\dot\Lyap=-2\sum_{j\ne k}(y_j-y_k)^2/|z_j-z_k|^2$ for
$\Lyap=\sum_j(\im z_j)^2$ along the zero dynamics of the backward heat flow $\partial_tp=-\partial_{zz}p$,
note that it holds for every conjugate-symmetric configuration of simple zeros, recall de Bruijn's strip
bound in this language, and compute backward-collision times for three windows of zeta zeros.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
The de Bruijn--Newman constant $\Lambda$ is defined through the one-parameter deformation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ht}
H_t(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\,\Phi(u)\,\cos(zu)\,du,\qquad H_0(z)\propto\xi(\half+\tfrac{iz}{2}),
\end{equation}
where $\xi$ is the Riemann $\xi$-function and $\Phi$ its (super-exponentially decaying) kernel; $H_t$ satisfies
the backward heat equation $\partial_t H_t=-\partial_{zz}H_t$. By the theorems of de Bruijn \cite{dB} and
Newman \cite{Newman}, there is a constant $\Lambda\in\mathbb R$ such that $H_t$ has only real zeros iff
$t\ge\Lambda$. Newman conjectured $\Lambda\ge0$; this was proved by Rodgers and Tao \cite{RT}, while
Polymath15 \cite{P15} established $\Lambda\le0.22$, improved to $\Lambda\le0.2$ by Platt and Trudgian \cite{PT}. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RHL}
\mathrm{RH}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \Lambda\le 0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \Lambda=0.
\end{equation}
In the variable $z$, a zero $\beta+i\gamma$ of $\zeta$ corresponds to $z=2\gamma-i(2\beta-1)$, so
$|\im z|=2|\beta-\half|$.

This note records an elementary identity for the zero dynamics (\S\ref{sec:lyap}), remarks on its
limitations (\S\ref{sec:nogo}), and a small computation of backward-collision times
(\S\ref{sec:extremal}). The companion paper \cite{P12} compares five reformulations of the Weil positivity; the heat-flow reformulation treated here is not among them.

\paragraph{The zero dynamics.} Where a real entire function or polynomial $p_t$ with
$\partial_tp_t=-\partial_{zz}p_t$ has simple zeros $z_j(t)$, differentiating $p_t(z_j(t))=0$ gives
$\dot z_j=p_t''(z_j)/p_t'(z_j)$, and for a polynomial this is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow}
\dot z_j\;=\;2\sum_{k\ne j}\frac{1}{z_j-z_k},
\end{equation}
the classical zero dynamics of the heat flow \cite{CSV}. The constant $2$ is forced by the equation
$\partial_tp=-\partial_{zz}p$. For $H_t$ the zero set is infinite and the sum in \eqref{eq:flow} converges
only when taken symmetrically, as in \cite{CSV,RT}. The flow is undefined at multiple zeros. Since $p_t$ has
real coefficients, non-real zeros occur in conjugate pairs.

\section{The off-line mass and its monotonicity}\label{sec:lyap}

Write $z_j=x_j+iy_j$ and define the \emph{off-line mass}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
\Lyap\;:=\;\sum_j (\im z_j)^2\;=\;\sum_j y_j^2\;\ge0,\qquad \Lyap=0\iff\text{all zeros real.}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity]\label{thm:lyap}
Let $p_t$ be a real polynomial with $\partial_tp_t=-\partial_{zz}p_t$, and suppose that on a time interval its
zeros $z_1(t),\dots,z_n(t)$ are simple. Then, along \eqref{eq:flow},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ldot}
\dot\Lyap\;=\;-2\sum_{j\ne k}\frac{(y_j-y_k)^2}{|z_j-z_k|^2}\;\le\;0,
\end{equation}
with equality iff all $y_j$ are equal, which for a conjugate-symmetric configuration forces all $y_j=0$.
Thus $\Lyap$ is non-increasing, and strictly decreasing while some zero is non-real.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\dot\Lyap=2\sum_j y_j\dot y_j=2\sum_j y_j\,\im\dot z_j$. By \eqref{eq:flow},
$\im\dot z_j=2\sum_{k\ne j}\im(z_j-z_k)^{-1}=-2\sum_{k\ne j}(y_j-y_k)/|z_j-z_k|^2$, so
$\dot\Lyap=-4\sum_{j\ne k}y_j(y_j-y_k)/|z_j-z_k|^2$. Symmetrizing under $j\leftrightarrow k$ replaces
$y_j(y_j-y_k)$ by $\half(y_j-y_k)^2$, giving \eqref{eq:Ldot}. Each summand is $\ge0$; equality needs $y_j=y_k$
for all $j,k$, and conjugate symmetry then forces $y_j\equiv0$.
\end{proof}

For $H_t$ with $t\ge\Lambda$ all zeros are real, and for every $t>0$ only finitely many zeros are
non-real \cite{KKL}, so $\Lyap$ is finite; we do not prove \eqref{eq:Ldot} for $H_t$. The identity does not
by itself force $\Lyap\to0$: that zeros confined to a horizontal strip become real in finite time is de Bruijn's
theorem \cite{dB} (Corollary~\ref{cor:height}), not a consequence of \eqref{eq:Ldot}.

\begin{corollary}[de Bruijn's strip bound]\label{cor:height}
If every zero of $H_{t_0}$ satisfies $|\im z|\le\Delta$, then every zero of $H_t$, $t\ge t_0$, satisfies
$|\im z|^2\le\max(\Delta^2-2(t-t_0),0)$; in particular $\Lambda\le t_0+\Delta^2/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
We give the computation for a polynomial $p_t$ with simple zeros; the case of $H_t$ is due to de Bruijn
\cite{dB}. Let $y_j=\max_k y_k>0$. Every term of $\im\dot z_j=-2\sum_{k\ne j}(y_j-y_k)/|z_j-z_k|^2$ is
$\le0$, and the conjugate partner $\bar z_j$ contributes $-2\cdot 2y_j/(2y_j)^2=-1/y_j$. Hence
$\dot y_j\le-1/y_j$, i.e.\ $\frac{d}{dt}y_{\max}^2\le-2$ (in the sense of upper Dini derivatives), and
$y_{\max}(t)^2\le\Delta^2-2(t-t_0)$ while positive.
\end{proof}

This is an upper bound on $\Lambda$, and it gives no lower bound on the time needed to heal. For example,
for $p_t=e^{-t\partial_z^2}p_0$, $p_0=(z^2+\frac14)(z^2-a^2)$, so $\Delta^2/2=0.125$, the zeros become all real
at $t\approx0.0586$, $0.0948$, $0.1189$ for $a=0.3,1,3$ (file \texttt{output.txt}).

\section{Limitations of the monotonicity}\label{sec:nogo}

Forward in $t$, $\Lyap$ decreases. RH concerns $t=0\le\Lambda$, reached by flowing backward, where $\Lyap$
increases; monotonicity then yields only $\Lyap(0)\ge\Lyap(\Lambda)=0$, which is trivially true.

\begin{remark}\label{thm:nogo}
The identity \eqref{eq:Ldot} holds for every finite conjugate-symmetric configuration of simple zeros. For
instance, a real polynomial with a non-real zero satisfies it as well. So the monotonicity of $\Lyap$ carries
no information distinguishing $\xi$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The information about RH is contained in the initial condition $H_0$, i.e.\ in where the zeros start;
the vector field \eqref{eq:flow} is the same for all initial data.
\end{remark}

\section{Backward collisions}\label{sec:extremal}

\begin{remark}\label{prop:collision}
For an isolated pair of real zeros with gap $g$, \eqref{eq:flow} gives $\frac{d}{dt}g^2=8$, so
$g^2=g_0^2+8t$ and the backward collision occurs at $t=-g_0^2/8$. Neighbouring zeros change this time (see the
table below). If $H_0$ has a non-real zero then $\Lambda>0$ by the definition of $\Lambda$. If all zeros of $H_0$ are
real, then $\Lambda=0$ by \cite{RT}, so $H_t$ has non-real zeros for every $t<0$. For a polynomial, a conjugate
pair can reach the real axis only through a real double zero, that is, through a collision of two real zeros in
backward time; we do not prove the corresponding statement for $H_t$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Numerical computation.} We take windows of $N=20$ consecutive zeros $\half+i\gamma_n$ of $\zeta$
(computed with \texttt{mpmath.zetazero}) and integrate $\dot\gamma_j=2\sum_{k\ne j}(\gamma_j-\gamma_k)^{-1}$
backward in $t$ (classical Runge--Kutta, step $10^{-3}$ times the squared minimal gap) until the minimal gap
falls below $10^{-3}$, the remaining time being added from the two-body law $-g^2/8$, recording the collision time $t_c$ and the mean spacing $s=2\pi/\log(T/2\pi)$ at the height $T$ of
that pair. The times refer to the flow in the variable $\gamma$; for $H_t$, in the variable $z=2\gamma$, the
corresponding time is $4t_c$. The ratio $t_c/s^2$ is scale-invariant. Script \texttt{collision\_times.py},
output \texttt{output.txt}.

The computation uses three windows and omits the influence of zeros outside the window. The values of
$t_c/s^2$ depend on the closest gap in the window and do not indicate a constant.

\begin{remark}\label{obs:target}
Assuming RH, the backward-collision margins of the zeros tend to $0^-$ in absolute units. Under the GUE model
the smallest normalized gap among the first $N$ zeros tends to $0$, so the normalized closest-pair margin also
tends to $0$. The computation above therefore yields no criterion for RH in terms of gap statistics. (Montgomery's
pair-correlation results \cite{Mont} assume RH.)
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

For real polynomials with simple zeros, the off-line mass $\Lyap$ is non-increasing along the heat-flow zero
dynamics (Theorem~\ref{thm:lyap}); for $H_t$ the largest imaginary part obeys de Bruijn's strip bound
(Corollary~\ref{cor:height}), and the identity holds for every conjugate-symmetric configuration
(Remark~\ref{thm:nogo}), so it does not distinguish $\xi$. Backward-collision times for three windows of zeta
zeros are reported in \S\ref{sec:extremal} as numerical data.
