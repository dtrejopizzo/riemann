# Tensor amplification of the Weil negative index and a two-variable explicit formula on thickened diagonals

The paper examines three open directions listed in the heuristic survey of Paper 27: two-dimensional propagation (Form A), index amplification in the manner of Deligne (Form B) and an effective version of Bagchi's self-approximation criterion (Form C). For Forms A and C it gives only heuristic discussions. For Form B it computes how the negative index of the off-line block of the Weil form behaves under tensor powers and realises the tensor square by a two-variable Weil functional on thickened multiplicative diagonals.

## Results
- Proved: the tensor signature formula neg.ind(Q₁⊗Q₂) = p₁q₂ + q₁p₂ (classical) and the index of tensor powers ((p+q)^k − (p−q)^k)/2 (Theorem 4.1, Proposition 4.3).
- Proved, assuming finitely many off-line zeros (Paper 25, Prop. 4.7): the off-line block has signature (2m,2m) and its k-th tensor power has negative index (4m)^k/2 (Theorem 4.2).
- Proved: the Mellin factorisation of g(x₁x₂)φ(x₁/x₂), and, under the same hypothesis, signature (8m², 8m²) of the two-variable off-line block on these kernels (Theorem 5.1).
- Proved: the two-variable zero sum equals the iterated one-variable explicit formula (Theorem 5.3); its primes×primes term is Σ_N g(N) Σ_{d|N} Λ(d)Λ(N/d) φ(d²/N) plus three explicit finite terms (Theorem 5.4).
- Proved: an elementary computation of a boundary kernel B for the Weil class (sign pattern of its prime increments, divergence (5√2/8) log X) (§3.3).
- Heuristic: the Form A and Form C discussions (Heuristics 2.1, 2.2, 3.1).
- Open: the sieve-to-index bridge (Missing Lemma 6.1), which is equivalent to RH under the finiteness hypothesis; unconditionally it would only exclude a finite non-zero number of off-line zeros.

The tensor signature formula, the explicit formula and the sieve facts are classical; the amplification count on the off-line block, the two-variable formula on thickened diagonals and its primes×primes term are new here. No proof of RH is claimed.
