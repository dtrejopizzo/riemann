\begin{abstract}
For the $\omega$-class decomposition $D_F=\sum_kS_k$ of a Dirichlet
partial sum $D_F(t;N)=\sum_{n\le N}a_n n^{-1/2-it}$, where $S_k$
collects the terms with $\omega(n)=k$, the \emph{inter-class energy
ratio}
$r(t;N)=\big(|D_F|^2-\sum_k|S_k|^2\big)/\sum_k|S_k|^2$
measures whether the classes add constructively ($r>0$) or destructively
($r<0$). We point out that $r$ has no characteristic sign. By the
Montgomery--Vaughan mean value theorem the average of the cross-term
$X=|D_F|^2-\sum_k|S_k|^2$ over an interval of length $H$ is $O(N/H)$, so
over long ranges neither sign of $X$ dominates. For the ratio itself we
find numerically, for $\zeta$ at $N=10^5$ on $5498$ points of
$[10^5,1.03\times10^5]$, that the mean of $r$ over the deciles of
$|D_\zeta|$ increases from $-0.998$ on the lowest decile (where every
point has $r<0$) to $+1.544$ on the highest decile (where $92\%$ of the
points have $r>0$), changing sign near the $69$th percentile; the mean of
$r$ over the whole sample is $-0.26$, and $73\%$ of the points have
$r<0$. A random completely multiplicative model behaves in the same way.
In particular the value $r=-0.78$ at the single point $t=150000$ and the
positive values of $r$ at the largest values of $|D_\zeta|$ are
consistent with each other: the former reflects the proximity of a point
where $D_\zeta$ nearly vanishes, not a general property of $\zeta$. Statements about the
sign of $r$ should therefore specify the set of $t$ over which it is
taken.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The $\omega$-class decomposition of a Dirichlet partial sum~\cite{P02}
groups the terms by the number of distinct prime factors of the index.
Writing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sk}
D_F(t;N)=\sum_{n\le N}\frac{a_n}{n^{1/2+it}}
=\sum_{k\ge0}S_k(t;N),\qquad
S_k(t;N)=\sum_{\substack{n\le N\\\omega(n)=k}}\frac{a_n}{n^{1/2+it}},
\end{equation}
one asks how the class-sums $S_k$ combine: do their phases reinforce
(constructive interference) or cancel (destructive interference)? The
natural scalar summarizing this is the inter-class energy ratio
$r(t;N)$ of Definition~\ref{def:r} below.

Two computations of $r$ for $F=\zeta$ point in opposite directions.
Evaluated at the single point $t=150000$ the ratio is negative
($r=-0.78$ at $N=10^5$), which suggests that the interference between
classes is destructive. Averaged over the largest values of $|D_\zeta|$
it is positive (mean $+1.54$ over the top decile of a sample, with $92\%$
of the points positive), which suggests that it is constructive.

The purpose of this note is to explain why the two readings are
consistent. The ratio $r$ takes both signs, and which sign one sees
depends on the set of $t$ over which $r$ is evaluated or averaged. In
Section~\ref{sec:conditional} we record what the Montgomery--Vaughan mean
value theorem gives: the average of the cross-term $X=|D_F|^2-\sum_k|S_k|^2$
over an interval of length $H$ is $O(N/H)$. This concerns $X$ and long
intervals; it does not give the mean of $r$. In Section~\ref{sec:main}
we find numerically, on one window at $N=10^5$, that the mean of $r$ over
the deciles of $|D_\zeta|$ increases with the decile, from negative
values on the low deciles to positive values on the top deciles. In
Section~\ref{sec:150000} we examine the point $t=150000$: it lies next
to a point where $D_\zeta$ nearly vanishes, in the lower half of the local distribution of
$|D_\zeta|$, and its negative value of $r$ is the one expected there.
Section~\ref{sec:implications} lists the consequences, and
Appendix~\ref{app:repro} describes the computations.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\begin{definition}[$\omega$-class sum]\label{def:Sk}
For $F(s)=\sum a_n n^{-s}$ and $k\ge0$, the $\omega$-class partial sum is
$S_k(t;N)$ as in~\eqref{eq:Sk}, where $\omega(n)$ is the number of
distinct prime factors of $n$ (with $\omega(1)=0$). Thus
$D_F=\sum_k S_k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Inter-class energy ratio]\label{def:r}
Assume $a_1\neq0$. Define the \emph{class energy}
$E(t)=\sum_k|S_k(t)|^2$, the \emph{cross-term}
$X(t)=|D_F(t)|^2-E(t)=\sum_{j\ne k}\re[S_j\overline{S_k}]$,
and the \emph{inter-class energy ratio}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r}
r(t;N)=\frac{X(t)}{E(t)}.
\end{equation}
Thus $|D_F|^2=E(1+r)$. We call the interference between classes
\emph{constructive} at $t$ if $r>0$ and \emph{destructive} if $r<0$.
\end{definition}

Here and below $N$ is fixed and is omitted from the notation $E(t)$,
$X(t)$, $S_k(t)$. Since $S_0=a_1$, one has $E\ge|a_1|^2>0$, so $r$ is
well defined. By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
$|D_F|^2\le K_N E$, where $K_N$ is the number of non-empty classes
($K_N=7$ for $N=10^5$), and therefore
\[
-1\le r(t;N)\le K_N-1 ,
\]
with $r=-1$ exactly at the zeros of $D_F$.

We take $F=\zeta$ ($a_n=1$) throughout, with a random multiplicative
control (coefficients $a_p=e^{i\theta_p}$, $\theta_p$ i.i.d.\ uniform on
$[0,2\pi)$, extended completely multiplicatively) for comparison; in both
cases $a_1=1$ and $E\ge1$. All sums are evaluated in double precision.
The sample consists of the points
\begin{equation*}
t_j=10^5+j\,\frac{2\pi}{\log N},\qquad 0\le j<5498,\qquad N=10^5,
\end{equation*}
which fill the window $[10^5,1.03\times10^5]$. For a subset $\mathcal E$
of the sample we write $\langle r\rangle_{\mathcal E}$ for the mean of
$r$ over $\mathcal E$ (Definition~\ref{cor:signfree}).

\section{The mean of the cross-term}
\label{sec:conditional}

The one general fact available is a bound for the mean of the cross-term
over long intervals.

\begin{proposition}[Mean of the cross-term]\label{prop:mvt}
Let $|a_n|\le1$ for all $n$. For all $N\ge1$, $T_0\in\mathbb{R}$ and
$H>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mvt}
\bigg|\frac{1}{H}\int_{T_0}^{T_0+H} X(t)\,dt\bigg|
\le\frac{12\pi N}{H},
\end{equation}
and the same bound holds with $X$ replaced by
$2\re\big[S_j\overline{S_k}\big]$ for any $j\ne k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal N\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}$ and let $b_n$, $n\in\mathcal N$,
be complex numbers. The numbers $\log n$, $n\in\mathcal N$, are distinct,
and the distance from $\log n$ to the nearest other one is at least
$\log\frac{n+1}{n}\ge\frac1{n+1}$. The mean value theorem of Montgomery
and Vaughan~\cite{MV74}, which follows from their weighted
form of Hilbert's inequality, therefore gives (trivially so if
$\mathcal N$ has a single element)
\begin{equation*}
\bigg|\int_{0}^{H}\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}b_n n^{-it}\Big|^2dt
-H\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}|b_n|^2\bigg|
\le 3\pi\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}(n+1)\,|b_n|^2 .
\end{equation*}
Replacing $b_n$ by $b_nn^{-iT_0}$ gives the same inequality for the
integral over $[T_0,T_0+H]$. Apply it with $b_n=a_nn^{-1/2}$, first to
$\mathcal N=\{1,\dots,N\}$, which gives $D_F$, and then to each class
$\mathcal N_k=\{n\le N:\omega(n)=k\}$, which gives $S_k$. The classes
partition $\{1,\dots,N\}$, so the main terms $H\sum_{n}|a_n|^2/n$ cancel
in $\int(|D_F|^2-\sum_k|S_k|^2)\,dt$, and
\[
\bigg|\int_{T_0}^{T_0+H}X(t)\,dt\bigg|
\le 2\cdot3\pi\sum_{n\le N}\frac{n+1}{n}\,|a_n|^2
\le 6\pi\,(N+1+\log N)\le 12\pi N .
\]
For a single pair $j\ne k$ use
$2\re\big[S_j\overline{S_k}\big]=|S_j+S_k|^2-|S_j|^2-|S_k|^2$ and apply
the inequality to $\mathcal N_j\cup\mathcal N_k$, $\mathcal N_j$ and
$\mathcal N_k$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The bound~\eqref{eq:mvt} is informative only for $H$ large compared with
$N$: over such ranges neither sign of the cross-term dominates on
average. It says nothing about the mean of $r=X/E$, because $E$ is not
constant. It is also vacuous in the regime of our computations
($N=10^5$, $H=3000$), where the mean of $X$ over the sample is $-0.92$
against a mean of $E$ of $11.75$.

\begin{definition}\label{cor:signfree}
For a finite sample $\mathcal T$ of abscissae and a non-empty subset
$\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal T$, write
$\langle r\rangle_{\mathcal E}=|\mathcal E|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}r(t)$.
\end{definition}

Since $r(t)$ takes both signs (Section~\ref{sec:main}), a statement about
``the sign of $r$'' is meaningful only once $\mathcal E$ is specified;
different choices of $\mathcal E$ can give opposite signs, and do.
On our sample the distribution of $r$ is two-sided and skewed:
$r\in[-1.00,3.90]$, with mean $-0.26$, median $-0.73$, and $r<0$ at $73\%$
of the points. Selecting the points where $|D_F|$ is large samples the
positive side; selecting the points where $|D_F|$ is small samples the
negative side. The next section makes this quantitative.

\section{Numerical observation: the decile means of \texorpdfstring{$r$}{r}}
\label{sec:main}

Order the sample of Section~\ref{sec:setup} by increasing $|D_F|$ and
split it into ten consecutive bands $B_1,\dots,B_{10}$ of $550$ or $549$
points (the deciles of $|D_F|$). Write $\bar r(q)=\langle r\rangle_{B_i}$, $q=(i-\tfrac12)/10$,
for the mean of $r$ over $B_i$. We call \emph{troughs} the points of the
lowest decile $B_1$ and \emph{peaks} those of the highest decile
$B_{10}$; these are not the local extrema of $|D_F|$.

\begin{observation}[Sign reversal between low and high deciles; numerical]\label{thm:main}
Let $F=\zeta$ and $N=10^5$, on the sample of $5498$ points of
$[10^5,1.03\times10^5]$. The band means $\bar r(q)$
(Table~\ref{tab:rbar}, Figure~\ref{fig:rbar}) increase from each band to
the next:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \textbf{Troughs} (lowest decile of $|D_\zeta|$): every point has
$r<0$, and $\bar r=-0.998$.
\item \textbf{Middle}: the band means change sign between the seventh and
eighth bands (linear interpolation: $q\approx0.69$).
\item \textbf{Peaks} (top decile): $\bar r=+1.544$, with $92\%$ of the
points having $r>0$; for the $50$ largest values of $|D_\zeta|$ the mean
of $r$ is $2.30$ and all $50$ have $r>0$.
\end{enumerate}
The random multiplicative control (five seeds
$\{42,137,271,314,577\}$) has the same profile, with top-decile mean
$\bar r=+1.41\pm0.04$ (mean and standard deviation over the seeds) and
sign change at $q\approx0.68$.
\end{observation}

These are statements about one window and one value of $N$; we make no
claim about other ranges.

The direction of the effect is forced by the identity
$|D_F|^2=E(1+r)$ together with the bounds $-1\le r\le K_N-1$ and $E\ge1$
of Section~\ref{sec:setup}: $|D_F|$ can be small only if $r$ is close to
$-1$, which explains the lowest deciles. At the top, a large value of
$|D_F|^2$ is produced jointly by $E$ and by $1+r$. On our sample $E$ is
far from constant (it ranges over $[1.1,230]$, with standard deviation
$1.43$ times its mean); its mean over the top decile is $34.6$, against
about $7$ in the middle deciles, and the mean of $r$ there is $1.54$.
The mean of $E$ over the lowest decile is $13.9$, so troughs are not
low-energy events but events of nearly complete cancellation between
classes.

\begin{remark}
The mean of $r$ over a set of points thus depends on how the set meets
the deciles of $|D_F|$, and on the weighting. On the whole sample the
unweighted mean of $r$ is $-0.26$, while the mean weighted by $E$, which
is the ratio of the mean of $X$ to the mean of $E$, is $-0.08$. A single
point, or a small or selected set of points, can give either sign.
\end{remark}

\section{The point \texorpdfstring{$t=150000$}{t=150000}}
\label{sec:150000}

We now look at the single point $t=150000$ mentioned in the
introduction, where $r$ is negative.

\begin{remark}[The point $t=150000$]\label{prop:150000}
At $N=10^5$ one has $|D_\zeta(150000;N)|=0.741$, $E=2.49$ and
$r(150000;N)=-0.779$ (for $N=1.5\times10^5$: $r=-0.787$). The point lies
$0.26$ to the left of a near-zero of $D_\zeta$ (at $t\approx150000.26$,
where $|D_\zeta|$ falls to $0.002$ and $r=-1.000$). Among the $184$ points of
spacing $2\pi/\log N$ in $[149950,150050]$, $35\%$ have smaller
$|D_\zeta|$. The point therefore lies in the lower half of the local
distribution of $|D_\zeta|$, next to a near-zero, and its value of $r$ is
close to the mean $\bar r=-0.86$ of the band $0.3<q<0.4$ of
Table~\ref{tab:rbar}. On the $18$ points with the largest $|D_\zeta|$ in the same
neighbourhood (its top decile) the mean of $r$ is $+1.42$, in line with Observation~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{remark}

Thus the negative value at $t=150000$ is the value of $r$ at a point of
the lower part of the distribution of $|D_\zeta|$. It is not
representative of $\zeta$ in general, and no single value is. The
negative value at this point and the positive mean over the top decile
both fit the increasing profile $\bar r(q)$ of
Observation~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Consequences}
\label{sec:implications}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \textbf{Specify the set of $t$.} A statement of the form ``$\zeta$
exhibits destructive (resp.\ constructive) interference between classes''
is incomplete. What the computations support is ``$r$ is negative on the
low deciles of $|D_\zeta|$'' and ``$r$ is positive on the top decile of
$|D_\zeta|$'', on the window and at the value of $N$ considered.
\item \textbf{Single points.} The value of $r$ at one point mostly
records where the point sits in the distribution of $|D_F|$, in
particular how close it is to a zero of $D_F$, where $r=-1$. It should
not be read as a property of $F$.
\item \textbf{Large values.} Questions about the size of
$|\zeta(1/2+it)|$ (the Lindel\"of hypothesis, which follows from the
Riemann hypothesis) concern the top of the distribution of $|D_\zeta|$;
comparisons with random models aimed at such questions should be made on
the top quantiles. The present computations do not bear on either
hypothesis: at $N=10^5$ the profile of $\zeta$ and that of the random
multiplicative control are close throughout (Table~\ref{tab:rbar}).
\item \textbf{The profile $\bar r(q)$ is the object of interest.} Rather
than a single number, the full profile $\bar r(q)$ (negative on the low
deciles, positive on the top ones) is the natural object to compare
across functions, through its slope and the location of its sign change.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Computations}
\label{app:repro}

All numerical values in Sections~\ref{sec:conditional}, \ref{sec:main}
and~\ref{sec:150000} are produced by the Python script
\texttt{reproduce\_colab.py} distributed with this note, which needs no
external data. The script:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item builds $\omega(n)$ for $n\le N$ from a smallest-prime-factor sieve;
\item computes the class-sums $S_k(t;N)$, $D_F=\sum_kS_k$, $E$ and
$r=X/E$ in double precision at the sample points
$t_j=10^5+2\pi j/\log N$, $0\le j<5498$, with $N=10^5$;
\item sorts the sample by $|D_F|$, splits it into ten consecutive bands of
$550$ or $549$ points and prints the band means of $r$ and of $E$, together with the
statistics of $r$, $X$ and $E$ over the whole sample quoted in the text
(Table~\ref{tab:rbar}, Observation~\ref{thm:main});
\item evaluates $|D_\zeta|$, $E$ and $r$ at $t=150000$ for $N=10^5$ and
$N=1.5\times10^5$, locates the minimum of $|D_\zeta|$ on $[149999,150001]$, and ranks
the value $|D_\zeta(150000)|$ among those at the $184$ points of spacing
$2\pi/\log N$ in $[149950,150050]$ (Remark~\ref{prop:150000});
\item repeats steps (2)--(3) for the random multiplicative control with
seeds $\{42,137,271,314,577\}$ and draws Figure~\ref{fig:rbar}.
\end{enumerate}
The random seeds are fixed. The run takes between ten and thirty minutes on a
desktop machine.
