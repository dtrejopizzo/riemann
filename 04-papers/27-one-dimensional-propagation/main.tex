\title[One-dimensional propagation and RH]{One-dimensional propagation and the Riemann
Hypothesis:\\ a heuristic survey of obstructions and two open directions}

\author{David Alejandro Trejo Pizzo}
\thanks{Independent Researcher. \texttt{dtrejopizzo@gmail.com}}


\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to the vanishing of the non-negative measure
$d\nu=\dmfull-\dmon$ comparing $|\zeta|^2$ with the modulus squared of its on-line Hadamard
projection against the archimedean measure. Several known reformulations of RH share a common
shape: RH is the \emph{boundary point} of a one-parameter family whose other members are
unconditional theorems or are controlled unconditionally (the de~Bruijn--Newman time $t$, the
self-approximation parameter $d$, the set of places, the abscissa of convergence of
$\sum\mu(n)n^{-s}$). We suggest, as an informal heuristic, that this shape explains why five
classical one-dimensional mechanisms --- limit/continuity, analyticity, parabolic backward
uniqueness, monotonicity/integrality, ergodic recurrence --- do not reach RH, and we discuss for
each where it fails or reduces to RH. Two elementary facts are proved: a real-analytic faithful
detector of reality of the zeros of $\Xi_t$ would already prove RH (Proposition~5.1), and the
Christoffel-type polynomial kernel $\Wl$ built from the orthogonal polynomials of the
archimedean measure is strictly positive, so that, as for the Poisson kernel in the trace
criterion, a single scale suffices; this is a restatement of $d\nu=0$. Finally we formulate two directions not covered by the heuristic,
\emph{two-dimensional propagation} and \emph{index amplification} in the spirit of Deligne, as
open problems with stated falsifiers, together with a third, more modest problem on the
self-approximation family. Nothing about RH is proved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{What this paper is, and is not}
This paper does not prove the Riemann Hypothesis, nor does it claim partial progress toward a
proof. It is a discussion of the \emph{shape} of several known reformulations of RH and of why
the classical one-dimensional mechanisms for passing from an interior statement to a boundary
statement do not apply to them. The discussion is heuristic: the list of mechanisms is not
proved exhaustive, and the conclusions are suggestions, not theorems. Its purpose is to isolate
two logical forms of argument that the discussion does not rule out and to state them as precise
open problems.

We use the comparison of $\zeta$ with its on-line projection and the reformulations of RH in
terms of the measure $\dnu$ from \cite{P24}, and the localized Weil form and its negative index
from \cite{P18,P25}. We recall the minimum needed in \S\ref{sec:framework}. Sections
\ref{sec:metapattern}--\ref{sec:nogo} contain the heuristic discussion, with two elementary
results (\S\ref{sec:wlambda} and Proposition~\ref{prop:analytic}); Sections
\ref{sec:formA}--\ref{sec:formC} state open problems.

\subsection{The elementary results}
Theorem~\ref{thm:wlambda-strict} shows that the Christoffel-type kernel $\Wl$ of
\eqref{eq:wlambda} is strictly positive at every real point; its proof is a three-term recurrence
argument. For the Christoffel-type kernel \eqref{eq:wlambda} the same single-scale statement
holds as for the Poisson kernel of \cite[Thm.~5.3]{P24}; like that statement it is a trivial
consequence of $d\nu\ge0$ \cite[Cor.~3.2]{P24} and of the strict positivity of the kernel
(Theorem~\ref{thm:wlambda-strict}). Proposition~\ref{prop:analytic} records that a real-analytic
faithful detector of reality of the zeros along the de~Bruijn--Newman flow would already prove
RH.

\section{The framework, recalled}\label{sec:framework}

We fix notation following \cite{P24}. Let $0<\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le\cdots$ denote the positive
ordinates of the non-trivial zeros of $\zeta$, listed with multiplicity.

\begin{itemize}
\item The archimedean spectral measure is
$\dm(s)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\,|\Gamma(1/4+is/2)|^2\,ds$ on $\bbR$, a probability measure whose
density decays like $|s|^{-1/2}e^{-\pi|s|/2}$ \cite[Def.~2.1]{P24}.
\item $\{\mathcal{P}_n\}_{n\geq0}$ are the orthonormal polynomials for
$\dm$, with $\mathcal{P}_0\equiv1$ and Jacobi data $b_n=0$ (parity),
$a_n^\infty=\tfrac12\sqrt{(2n+1)(2n+2)}$. They obey the three-term
recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ttr}
s\,\mathcal{P}_n(s)=a_n^\infty\,\mathcal{P}_{n+1}(s)+a_{n-1}^\infty\,\mathcal{P}_{n-1}(s),
\qquad a_k^\infty>0,
\end{equation}
with the convention $\mathcal P_{-1}\equiv0$.
\item $\dmfull=|\zeta(1/2+is)|^2\,\dm$ and $\dmon=|\zeta_{\mathrm{on}}(1/2+is)|^2\,\dm$,
where $\zeta_{\mathrm{on}}$ is obtained from $\zeta$ by moving each off-line zero quartet to the
critical line, as in \cite[Def.~2.1]{P24}.
\item The central object is the difference measure $\dnu=\dmfull-\dmon$. It is unconditionally
non-negative, $\dnu=(R-1)\,\dmon$ with $R=|\zeta/\zeta_{\mathrm{on}}|^2\geq1$, it has a
continuous density with respect to Lebesgue measure, and $\dnu=0$ if and only if RH holds
\cite[Thm.~3.1, Cor.~3.2]{P24}; the dominance $R\ge1$ is taken in \cite{P24} from the
moment-rigidity paper cited there.
\item Following \cite{P13}, write $\Phi$ for the Riemann--P\'olya kernel and
$\Xi_t(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du$ for $t\in\bbR$, so that
$\Xi_0(z)$ is a constant multiple of $\xi(1/2+iz/2)$. The de~Bruijn--Newman constant $\Lambda$ is
the real number such that $\Xi_t$ has only real zeros exactly when $t\ge\Lambda$ (de~Bruijn
\cite{dB}, Newman \cite{New76}; see \cite{P13}); RH is equivalent to the reality of the zeros of $\Xi_0$.
\item For $\lambda>0$ with $N=N(\lambda):=\#\{n:\gamma_n\leq\lambda\}$, the Christoffel-type
kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wlambda}
\Wl(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,|\mathcal{P}_n(s)|^2+(a_N^\infty)^2\,|\mathcal{P}_{N+1}(s)|^2\;\geq0,
\end{equation}
and we put $\Tl=\int_\bbR \Wl\,\dnu$.
\item The trace criterion of \cite[Thm.~5.3]{P24} is stated there for the Poisson kernel
$\lambda^2/(\lambda^2+s^2)$ in place of \eqref{eq:wlambda}: with that kernel,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p39}
\RH\iff \Tl=0\ \text{for one (equivalently, every) }\lambda>0,
\end{equation}
and \cite{P24} states that this is a trivial restatement of $\dnu=0$.
\item \cite[Thm.~4.1]{P24} lists seven reformulations of RH, six of them positive functionals of $\dnu$ and one a sum of positive terms over the off-line zeros ($\dnu=0$;
the vanishing of a Nevanlinna transform of $\dnu$; the trace; the Jensen excess; an $L^2$
distance; a relative entropy; and $\sup_\lambda\Tl=0$), all trivially equivalent to $\dnu=0$
\cite[Rem.~7.2]{P24}.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[The Hadamard barrier {\cite[Def.~7.1]{P24}}]\label{def:hadamard}
By the \emph{Hadamard barrier} we mean the gap between two facts: (a) the comparison of $\xi$
with its on-line projection yields, unconditionally, $R\ge1$, i.e.\ $\dnu\ge0$; (b) $\dnu=0$ is
equivalent to RH.
\end{definition}

A non-negative measure need not vanish, so (a) alone carries no information on whether
$\dnu=0$. Other well-known obstructions of the same flavour are: Weil positivity of the explicit
formula is itself equivalent to RH; the Euler product converges absolutely only for $\Real s>1$
and its local information does not by itself control the critical strip; unconditional
estimates typically give positivity bounds of the wrong sign. The heuristic of
\S\ref{sec:metapattern}--\S\ref{sec:nogo} is that these obstructions have a common shape.

\section{The meta-pattern: RH as a boundary point}\label{sec:metapattern}

\subsection{The recurring shape}
Several known reformulations of RH instantiate one template. There is a one-parameter family of
statements; for the parameter values away from one boundary value the statement is an
unconditional theorem, or is controlled unconditionally; and the boundary value is RH.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\textbf{Family} & \textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Unconditional control} & \textbf{Boundary $\iff$ RH}\\
\hline
Self-approximation $\mathrm{SR}_d$ & $d$ & proved for all irrational $d$ & $d=0$\\
de Bruijn--Newman $\Xi_t$ & $t$ & real zeros for $t\geq\Lambda$ & $t=0$\\
Semilocal Weil positivity & support & archimedean place, & all places,\\
 & & support in $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$, $\lambda\le\sqrt2$ & all supports\\
$\sum\mu(n)n^{-s}$ & $\sigma$ & converges for $\sigma\ge1$ & converges\\
 & & & for every $\sigma>1/2$\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The sources are as follows. $\mathrm{SR}_d$ is the statement that for every compact
$K\subset\{1/2<\Real s<1\}$ and $\varepsilon>0$ the set of $\tau$ with
$\max_K|\zeta(s+id\tau)-\zeta(s+i\tau)|<\varepsilon$ has positive lower density; for $d=0$ this
is strong recurrence, equivalent to RH by Bagchi's theorem \cite{Bagchi}; it was proved for
algebraic irrational $d$ by Nakamura \cite{Nak09} and for all irrational $d$ by Pa\'nkowski
\cite{Pan10}. For the de Bruijn--Newman family, $\Xi_t$ has only real zeros for $t\ge\Lambda$,
with $\Lambda\le1/2$ (de~Bruijn \cite{dB}) and $\Lambda\ge0$ (Rodgers--Tao \cite{RT}), and RH
holds if and only if $\Lambda=0$; see \cite{P13} for this setting. The semilocal row is the
archimedean positivity of Connes--Consani \cite{CC21}. The series $\sum_n\mu(n)n^{-s}$ of
$1/\zeta(s)$ converges for $\Real s\ge1$ (the case $\Real s=1$ being equivalent to the prime
number theorem), and it converges for every $\Real s>1/2$ if and only if RH holds (Littlewood;
\cite[Thm.~14.25(A)]{Tit}). The finite-Euler spectral triples of
\cite{CCM25} give a further family of the same shape, indexed by the set of primes used; we do
not state any unconditional control for it here.

\begin{principle}[The boundary-point pattern]\label{prin:boundary}\sloppy
In each of the families above, RH is the boundary member of a one-parameter family whose
interior is unconditionally controlled. The obstructions recalled in \S\ref{sec:framework} may
be read as the assertion that no one-dimensional propagation principle carries the interior
control to the boundary.
\end{principle}

Heuristic principle~\ref{prin:boundary} is not a theorem, and we do not know how to make it one.
Section~\ref{sec:nogo} discusses the propagation mechanisms one by one.

\section{Strict positivity of the Christoffel-type kernel}\label{sec:wlambda}

Before the discussion we record an elementary fact about the kernel \eqref{eq:wlambda}.

\begin{lemma}[No shared zeros of consecutive orthogonal polynomials]\label{lem:noshared}
For every $n\geq0$, the polynomials $\mathcal{P}_n$ and $\mathcal{P}_{n+1}$
have no common zero in $\bbC$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $n=0$ this is clear since $\mathcal P_0\equiv1$. Suppose $n\ge1$ and
$\mathcal{P}_{n+1}(s_0)=\mathcal{P}_n(s_0)=0$. Equation \eqref{eq:ttr} at index $n$ gives
$0=a_n^\infty\mathcal{P}_{n+1}(s_0)+a_{n-1}^\infty\mathcal{P}_{n-1}(s_0)
=a_{n-1}^\infty\mathcal{P}_{n-1}(s_0)$, so $\mathcal{P}_{n-1}(s_0)=0$ since
$a_{n-1}^\infty>0$. Descending induction yields
$\mathcal{P}_0(s_0)=0$, contradicting $\mathcal{P}_0\equiv1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Strict pointwise positivity of $\Wl$]\label{thm:wlambda-strict}
For every $\lambda$ with $N(\lambda)\geq1$, $\Wl(s)>0$ for all $s\in\bbR$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:wlambda}, $\Wl$ is a sum of non-negative terms with strictly
positive coefficients. If $\Wl(s_0)=0$ then every term vanishes; in
particular the term $n=N$ (present because $N\geq1$) forces
$\mathcal{P}_N(s_0)=0$, and the last term forces
$\mathcal{P}_{N+1}(s_0)=0$. This contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:noshared}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Any continuous kernel that is strictly positive everywhere gives the same conclusion as
Theorem~\ref{thm:single-scale} below; this is the case of the Poisson kernel in
\cite[Thm.~5.3]{P24}. For the degenerate range $N(\lambda)=0$ one has
$\Wl=(a_0^\infty)^2|\mathcal{P}_1|^2$, which vanishes only at $s=0$; since $\dnu$ has a continuous
density with respect to Lebesgue measure \cite[Cor.~3.2]{P24}, a single point is $\dnu$-null and
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:single-scale} applies unchanged.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[A single scale suffices]\label{thm:single-scale}
Fix any $\lambda_0>0$ with $N(\lambda_0)\geq1$. Then $\RH\iff\Tl[\lambda_0]=0$. Like
\eqref{eq:p39}, this is a trivial restatement of $\dnu=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) Under RH, $\dnu=0$, so $\Tl[\lambda_0]=0$. ($\Leftarrow$)
Write $\Tl[\lambda_0]=\int \Wl[\lambda_0]\,(R-1)\,|\zeta_{\mathrm{on}}|^2\,\dm$
with $R-1\geq0$ \cite[Cor.~3.2]{P24} and $\Wl[\lambda_0]>0$ everywhere
(Theorem~\ref{thm:wlambda-strict}). The integrand is non-negative; its
integral vanishing forces $(R-1)|\zeta_{\mathrm{on}}|^2=0$ $\dm$-a.e., i.e.\ $\dnu=0$,
i.e.\ RH \cite[Cor.~3.2]{P24}.
\end{proof}

For this family the boundary is thus attached to the interior in a trivial way. For the other
families in the table no such argument is known, and \S\ref{sec:nogo} discusses why the
classical one-dimensional mechanisms do not supply one.

\section{One-dimensional propagation mechanisms}\label{sec:nogo}

We discuss the classical mechanisms by which an unconditional interior statement could be
propagated to a boundary value, and indicate for each where it fails or reduces to RH. The
list is not claimed to be exhaustive, and the discussion is heuristic except where a statement
is proved.

\subsection{Limit / continuity}\label{ssec:limit}
If a family $f(\theta)$ were continuous at the boundary and identically zero on the interior, the
boundary value would vanish by continuity. For the finite-Euler spectral triples of
\cite{CCM25} one would like to apply Hurwitz's theorem to the regularised determinants and
transfer reality of zeros to the limit; but Hurwitz's theorem requires locally uniform
convergence to a function that does not vanish identically on the region in question, and
establishing this in the critical strip is not available. In a related setting it is noted in
\cite[\S4]{P23} that Hurwitz's theorem and the maximum principle do not give non-vanishing on an
unbounded region. Heuristically, continuity up to the boundary is of the same strength as the
conclusion.

\subsection{Analyticity}\label{ssec:analyticity}
\begin{proposition}[No faithful analytic detector]\label{prop:analytic}
Suppose $F:[0,\infty)\to\bbR$ is real-analytic and satisfies $F(t)=0$ if
and only if the zeros of $\Xi_t$ are all real. Then RH holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The de~Bruijn--Newman constant $\Lambda$ is finite (de~Bruijn \cite{dB}: $\Lambda\le1/2$;
Rodgers--Tao \cite{RT}: $\Lambda\ge0$), and $\Xi_t$ has only real zeros for all $t\geq\Lambda$.
Hence $F\equiv0$ on $[\Lambda,\infty)$. A real-analytic function on the connected interval
$[0,\infty)$ vanishing on a set with a limit point vanishes identically, so $F(0)=0$, i.e.\ the
zeros of $\Xi_0$ are all real, which is RH (\S\ref{sec:framework}).
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:analytic} shows that constructing a real-analytic faithful detector of
zero-reality is already a proof of RH. Any candidate ``analytic monotone quantity'' must be
checked against this proposition: if it is faithful and analytic, the work is already done.

\subsection{Parabolic backward uniqueness}\label{ssec:backward}
The de~Bruijn--Newman flow is a backward heat flow, and backward uniqueness for parabolic
equations would propagate $u(\Lambda)=0\Rightarrow u\equiv0$. This works for quantities
\emph{linear} in $\Xi_t$. The reality-of-zeros detector is nonlinear (it sees the imaginary parts
of zeros, not a linear functional of $\Xi_t$), so backward uniqueness does not apply to it
directly.

\subsection{Monotonicity / integrality}\label{ssec:integrality}
The negative index $\kappa$ of the localized Weil form on the even class $\mathcal V$ is an
integer. When the off-line zeros form $m<\infty$ quartets it satisfies $\kappa\le m$
\cite[Prop.~2.5]{P25}, \cite[Cor.~6.5]{P18}; whether equality holds is open \cite{P25}. Its
negative directions come from the off-line part $Q_{\mathrm{off}}$ of the decomposition
$Q=Q_{\mathrm{on}}+Q_{\mathrm{off}}$ \cite[Thm.~3.1]{P18}, and $Q_{\mathrm{off}}$ has signature
$(m,m)$ \cite[Cor.~6.5]{P18}. The negative index of a continuous family of Hermitian forms is
lower semicontinuous and is locally constant wherever the form stays non-degenerate
\cite[Thm.~5.3]{P18}. If a local principle forbade its jumps along a deformation to the on-line
configuration, integrality would force $\kappa$ to keep its on-line value $0$. But jumps occur
where the form degenerates, and nothing local prohibits them. Integrality constrains the value
but not its constancy.

\subsection{Ergodic recurrence}\label{ssec:ergodic}
The vertical flow $\tau\mapsto\zeta(\cdot+i\tau)$ is modelled on the Bohr torus
$\Omega=\prod_p\mathbb{T}_p$, on which the Kronecker flow is uniquely ergodic, so the arithmetic
point is uniformly recurrent unconditionally. Thus the obstruction is not recurrence of the flow
but strong recurrence of $\zeta$ itself in the sense of Bagchi: by Bagchi's theorem
\cite{Bagchi}, $\RH$ holds if and only if for every compact $K\subset\{1/2<\Real s<1\}$ and every
$\varepsilon>0$ the set of $\tau$ with $\max_K|\zeta(s+i\tau)-\zeta(s)|<\varepsilon$ has positive
lower density. The ergodic route therefore reduces to a known equivalent of RH.

\subsection{Summary of the discussion}\label{ssec:summary}
\begin{observation}\label{obs:nogo}
Each of the five one-dimensional propagation mechanisms above, applied to the boundary-point
families of \S\ref{sec:metapattern}, either reduces to a statement of the strength of RH
(continuity, \S\ref{ssec:limit}; analyticity, \S\ref{ssec:analyticity}; ergodic recurrence,
\S\ref{ssec:ergodic}) or does not apply for a structural reason (backward uniqueness by
nonlinearity, \S\ref{ssec:backward}; integrality by jumps at degeneracies,
\S\ref{ssec:integrality}).
\end{observation}
This suggests, but does not prove, that an argument reaching RH is not a one-dimensional
propagation principle. Two logical forms are not covered by the discussion: \emph{(A)}
two-dimensional propagation, where information flows across a region from its sides to a corner
without passing continuously through any single boundary point; and \emph{(B)} amplification,
where a defect is multiplied until it collides with an unconditional sublinear bound. The rest of
the paper formulates these as open problems.

\section{Form A: two-dimensional propagation}\label{sec:formA}

\subsection{Why a second dimension is not excluded}
The discussion of \S\ref{sec:nogo} concerns one-parameter families. The Euler-product
filtration alone does not reach the boundary, and the heat time alone does not; we are not aware
of results addressing their coupling. Two-dimensional propagation principles---the maximum
principle, Almgren-type monotonicity, logarithmic convexity along diagonals---move information
from the \emph{edges} of a region into its interior and corners, and do not require continuity
through any single point.

\subsection{The candidate object}
Heuristically, consider a joint trace
\begin{multline*}
\mathcal{T}(t,x)=\text{(trace of the finite-Euler approximant indexed by }x\text{,}\\
\text{evolved by the de Bruijn--Newman heat flow to time }t).
\end{multline*}
We do not define $\mathcal T$ rigorously here; making it precise is part of the problem. The
features one would want are:
\begin{itemize}
\item Edge $\{t\geq\Lambda\}$: control coming from the reality of the zeros of $\Xi_t$ for
$t\ge\Lambda$ \cite{dB,RT,P13}.
\item Edge $\{x\text{ finite}\}$: control at each finite level of the approximation; we do not
claim such control is known.
\item Corner $(t,x)=(0,\infty)$: RH.
\end{itemize}

\begin{problem}[The coupling inequality]\label{prob:coupling}
Can $\mathcal{T}(t,x)$ be defined so that it satisfies a differential inequality coupling
$\partial_t$ and $\partial_x$---parabolic, monotone, or logarithmically
convex along diagonals of the quadrant---under which positivity propagates
from the two edges to the corner $(0,\infty)$, even though it fails along
each axis separately?
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}[What would falsify Form A]
If $\partial_t\mathcal{T}$ and $\partial_x\mathcal{T}$ have no fixed sign relation on the
quadrant, for a suitable discrete difference in $x$, then no maximum or monotonicity
principle applies and Form~A fails. A first finite test is to compute $\partial_t\mathcal{T}$ for
a two-prime approximant and compare its sign to $\partial_x\mathcal{T}$.
\end{remark}

\section{Form B: index amplification (the Deligne mechanism)}\label{sec:formB}

\subsection{The amplification step in Deligne's proof}
RH is a theorem over finite fields \cite{Del74}. A central step of Deligne's argument is not a
positivity argument: for every integer $k\ge1$ Deligne applies Rankin's method to the $2k$-th
tensor power of the sheaf: its $L$-function is rational, its local factors have non-negative
coefficients, and its poles are located through the monodromy coinvariants; this gives
$|\alpha|^{2k}\le q^{k\beta+1}$ for an eigenvalue $\alpha$ and reference weight $\beta$, and
letting $k\to\infty$ gives $|\alpha|\le q^{\beta/2}$ \cite[\S3]{Del74}. The structure we wish to
isolate is: an invariant (the weight of a Frobenius eigenvalue), an amplification (even tensor
powers multiply the
deviation from the expected weight), and a bound that grows more slowly than the amplification,
so that the deviation must vanish.

\subsection{What is available}
The negative index $\kappa$ of the localized Weil form is an integer, it vanishes under RH, and
when the off-line zeros form $m<\infty$ quartets it satisfies $\kappa\le m$ while
$Q_{\mathrm{off}}$ has signature $(m,m)$ \cite[Prop.~2.5]{P25}, \cite[Thm.~3.1, Cor.~6.5]{P18}.
Whether $\kappa\ge1$ on $\mathcal V$ when off-line zeros exist is open \cite{P25}; for the full
form on its whole test space, $\kappa(Q)=2m$ in that case \cite[Prop.~4.7]{P25}. The missing ingredient is the
amplification.

\begin{problem}[The amplifying functor]\label{prob:amplify}
Does there exist a class of objects $\psi$ (Hermitian forms such as the Weil form $Q$ on its
test space, or spectral triples), each with a negative index $\kappa(\psi)\in\{0,1,\dots\}$, and
an operation $\boxtimes$ on this class such that
\begin{enumerate}[\rm(a)]
\item $\kappa(\psi_1\boxtimes\psi_2)\geq\kappa(\psi_1)+\kappa(\psi_2)$
(superadditivity of the negative index), and
\item there is a bound $\kappa(\psi^{\boxtimes k})\leq f(k)$ with
$f(k)/k\to0$, provable by a finiteness argument?
\end{enumerate}
If both hold, then $k\,\kappa(\psi)\leq f(k)$ for all $k$ forces $\kappa(\psi)=0$. For the full
Weil form $\kappa(Q)=0$ is equivalent to RH by Weil's criterion \cite[Def.~2.3]{P25}; for the
localized form on $\mathcal V$ one would in addition need $\kappa_{\mathcal V}\ge1$ whenever
off-line zeros exist, which is not known \cite[Cor.~6.5]{P18}.
\end{problem}

\subsection{Where to look}
For $\zeta$---a $\mathrm{GL}(1)$ object---the \emph{grouplike} tensor theory
is trivial: $\mathrm{sym}^k$ of the trivial character is trivial, which is
why the Langlands route to Ramanujan-type bounds works for modular forms
but says nothing about $\zeta$. The operation $\boxtimes$ must therefore be
\emph{non-grouplike}: a candidate is the $\lambda$-ring / Frobenius-lift
structure on the Connes--Consani scaling site, or a Rankin--Selberg-type
convolution realised directly on the test space with the form $Q$ rather than on automorphic data; for spectral and dynamical analogies of this kind see \cite{bgConnes99,bgDeninger}.

\begin{remark}[What would falsify Form B]
If $\kappa$ is exactly additive under every available $\boxtimes$ (not
strictly superadditive) and the bound $f(k)$ is genuinely linear, there is
no contradiction to extract and Form~B fails. The decisive sub-question
is whether \emph{any} natural product on the test space with the form $Q$ makes the negative index
strictly superadditive.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation with Bagchi's argument]
Bagchi's proof that strong recurrence implies RH \cite{Bagchi} has the same amplification
shape: one off-critical zero, replicated by strong recurrence and Rouch\'e's theorem to a family
of zeros of positive density, contradicts the unconditional zero-density bound
$N(\sigma,T)=o(T)$ for $\sigma>1/2$. Form~B is that skeleton with an algebraic amplifier in place
of the ergodic one. The ergodic version does not prove RH because its premise (strong
recurrence) is equivalent to RH (\S\ref{ssec:ergodic}); the skeleton itself is sound.
\end{remark}

\section{Form C: the boundary point as a quantitative singularity}\label{sec:formC}

A more modest line. The self-approximation statement $\mathrm{SR}_d$ is proved unconditionally
for all irrational $d$ \cite{Nak09,Pan10}, while $\mathrm{SR}_0$ is equivalent to RH
\cite{Bagchi}. We are not aware of an effective study of the degeneration as $d\to0$.

\begin{problem}[Effective degeneration of $\mathrm{SR}_d$]\label{prob:effective}
As $d\to0$ through irrational values, how do the constants (the lower density, as a function of
$K$ and $\varepsilon$) in the proof of $\mathrm{SR}_d$ degrade? If the degradation is like
$e^{-c/|d|}$, this quantifies the failure of the Euler product to propagate into the strip; if it
is polynomial in $1/|d|$, there is a tension with the Rouch\'e argument linking recurrence to zero
density which would deserve a dedicated analysis.
\end{problem}

Form~C is finite, falsifiable, and independent of Forms A and B.

\section{Summary}\label{sec:assessment}

We claim no proof and no partial proof of RH. The paper contains: a heuristic discussion
(Heuristic principle~\ref{prin:boundary} and Observation~\ref{obs:nogo}) of why five classical
one-dimensional propagation mechanisms do not carry interior control to the boundary in several
reformulations of RH, which suggests but does not prove that an argument reaching RH is not of
this type; two elementary results, the strict positivity of the Christoffel-type kernel
(Theorem~\ref{thm:wlambda-strict}, with the trivial single-scale restatement
Theorem~\ref{thm:single-scale}) and Proposition~\ref{prop:analytic}; and three open problems
(Problems~\ref{prob:coupling},~\ref{prob:amplify},~\ref{prob:effective}), each with a stated
falsifier, corresponding to forms of argument not covered by the discussion.
